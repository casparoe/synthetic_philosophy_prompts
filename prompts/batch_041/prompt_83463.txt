I am drafting the scientific annex to a sentience statute, which will sort animals into three tiers — no protection, basic protection, full protection — with each tier's trigger supplied by a criterion from a theory of the distribution of consciousness across animals. Before we entertain any particular criterion, I want the specification. What must a theory in this area be able to do to count as adequate for that use? Give me the desiderata, each with the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are merely preferences of one camp.

At a minimum, work through the following and tell me which are genuine demands on the theory and which are not:

- The cases it must classify correctly, both those where our pre-theoretic verdicts are firm and those where they are not: a dog, a honeybee, a shrimp, an oyster, a sea sponge.
- The distinctions it must not collapse: phenomenal consciousness versus mere responsiveness or nociception; the capacity to suffer versus the capacity for rich experience; being a subject of experience versus being an agent; and gradedness versus a threshold — if consciousness comes in degrees, the statute still needs tier boundaries, and the theory has to say what those boundaries are boundaries in.
- The empirical results it must not contradict, including how to treat taxa that lack a layered cortex yet display flexible, integrated behaviour, and how to handle taxa on which the decisive experiments cannot be run at all.
- The practical constraints of the use: the theory must yield a verdict from evidence a legislative advisory body can actually obtain without invasive experiments; it must deliver verdicts about individual animals rather than idealized species members; and it must specify a default for taxa on which it returns no verdict.
- Stability constraints: the tiers must not flip when a population is split or lumped by taxonomists, and must not depend on measurements that cannot be taken on juveniles, on sleeping animals, or on damaged ones.

Then flag any pair or triple of these desiderata that cannot be jointly satisfied. If such a set exists, that is the result I most want, since it would show the schedule is not a neutral application of the science but a choice among trade-offs. Finally, tell me which single requirement does the most work in excluding the theories currently on offer, and which requirement is most likely a parochial demand of one camp rather than a real constraint. Leave the scorecard of particular theories to me.
