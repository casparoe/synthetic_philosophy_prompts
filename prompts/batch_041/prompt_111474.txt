A friend of mine likes to close arguments with the line that circulates as "God does not play dice with the universe" — Einstein's remark, in his 1926 letter to Max Born, that God "is not playing at dice." In his hands the sentence does two jobs at once: it is supposed to show that Einstein's resistance to quantum mechanics was religious rather than scientific, and it is supposed to show that Einstein must have regarded free will as an illusion, since nothing in nature is left to chance. I suspect the sentence is being asked to carry more than it can. Please lay out, as a menu of readings rather than a single interpretation, the distinct precise claims that line could be making: what exactly is being denied (irreducible chance in nature, mere unpredictability, lawlessness, the completeness of quantum mechanics), what work "God" is doing in the sentence (a personal creator, Spinoza's order of nature, a figure for the intelligibility of the world), and what, if anything, follows from any of these readings about human freedom. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, or something Einstein himself would have rejected, and say which readings would actually license each of my friend's two conclusions and which one he most likely has in mind. Please write your answer in American English.
