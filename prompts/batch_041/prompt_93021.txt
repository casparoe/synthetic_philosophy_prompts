You are an intellectual historian of the Annales school and a philosopher of the social sciences who is also fluent, at a professional level, in time-series econometrics. Work carefully rather than quickly, and begin by brainstorming candidate correspondences in a list before you commit to an argument or a structure.

I am a quantitative macroeconomist. I am comfortable with trend-cycle decompositions, unit roots and near-unit roots, fractional integration and long memory, structural break estimation, regime-switching models, cointegration, structural VARs and impulse response functions, spectral and band-pass methods, and the standard worries about ergodicity, mixing, and time aggregation. I have read almost no history. What I want from you is a translation exercise, and I want the failures of the translation to be as prominent as the successes.

The position to translate is Fernand Braudel's, as set out in his 1958 Annales essay "Histoire et Sciences sociales : La longue durée": the division of historical time into three tiers — structure (the near-immobile), conjuncture (the slowly rhythmic), and event (the fast, surface, and on his account largely illusory) — together with his polemic against event-centred history and his claim that the deepest layer is where the real causal action is. Restate this in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can. Then give me the residue report.

The residue report is the part I care about. For each element of Braudel's position, tell me whether it maps cleanly onto something I already have, maps only with a distortion that you should name, or has no counterpart at all in my vocabulary. Some things I would like you to address directly, though do not let this list confine you:

- Is a structure best modelled as a unit-root or near-unit-root process, as a very slow regime, or as something my formalism cannot represent because it is not a stochastic process at all?
- Is conjuncture just the business cycle, and if so, does Braudel's use of the term carry commitments that the business-cycle concept does not?
- Is the event an innovation in an impulse response function? If so, does Braudel's claim that events are causally shallow amount to a claim about variance shares across frequencies, about explanatory priority, or about something neither can express?
- Braudel seems to treat the choice of time scale as a substantive commitment rather than a free parameter of the analyst. I treat scale as a modelling choice. Say what is lost when I do that, and whether anything is gained.
- Does my reliance on ergodicity or on stationary-ish asymptotics presuppose exactly what Braudel denies about deep structures?
- When the two frameworks disagree about whether slow processes explain fast ones or merely co-occur with them, is the disagreement empirical, conceptual, or rhetorical?

Close with a verdict on the residue: where it remains, say whether it indicates a defect in my vocabulary — something a quantitative social scientist ought to be able to represent and cannot — or whether it shows that Braudel's position was less determinate than its rhetoric suggests. Be honest if the answer is a mix.

Throughout, distinguish what is settled among specialists from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus. Where your translation depends on a particular interpretation of Braudel — of what he meant by structure, or of how strongly he meant the explanatory priority claim — flag that dependence explicitly, note the main rival readings, and say which you are adopting and why. Where you are uncertain about the econometrics, say so rather than papering over it.
