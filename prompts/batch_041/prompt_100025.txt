I teach a philosophy of mind seminar, upper-level undergrads, about 15 of them, one 90-minute session, and I want to do the session on eliminative materialism -- the Churchland-style line that beliefs and desires are posits of a folk theory that a mature neuroscience will just throw out, the way we threw out phlogiston and caloric. My problem is that when I explain it, everyone nods politely and then says "but I obviously believe things," and we're done in ten minutes. I want them to actually feel the force of it before they get to object. So I want a runnable activity, not a lecture.

Here's a rough sketch I'd like you to turn into something that actually works, or replace it if you've got a better structure. I'm imagining a prediction tournament with banned vocabularies, in rounds. I want the rules, the materials, the timing, a scoring scheme (no real money, obviously), and a debrief.

The sketch: tables of 3-4 get a stack of short case cards -- little vignettes of a person doing something, plus a held-back "answer" about what they actually do next. Task each round is the same: write one line explaining the behavior and one line predicting what happens next. Score the predictions that match.

Round 1, no restrictions, baseline. Round 2, same task but you're banned from the vocabulary of some dead theory -- no "humor," no "vital force," no "caloric," that kind of thing. I expect this round to be trivially easy and I want the facilitator to count how many times anyone slips (should be near zero). Round 3, same task, new cards, but now the banned list is the mentalistic one -- no believe, want, intend, desire, feel, think, know, hope, fear, prefer, decide, remember, expect, understand, mean -- and they can only use observable behavior, dispositions to behave, and a "neuro sheet" I hand them with a page of plausible mechanism talk on it. The facilitator tallies slips again. My hunch is round 2 costs nothing and round 3 is agony, and that contrast is the whole point, but I need the cases chosen so that round 3 really bites -- I want the cards to lean on the cases where folk psychology is at its worst (weakness of will, self-deception, confabulation, people whose stated beliefs and behavior come apart, the whole zoo), not easy everyday stuff where the mental vocabulary wins in a walk.

Then I want one more twist before the debrief: hand them the neuro sheet *again* and let them retry, and ask out loud whether it actually gives them any prediction they couldn't already guess, or whether it just relabels. I want them to feel the difference between re-describing a behavior and predicting a new one.

For the debrief, please map the moments onto the actual philosophy: round 2 onto the claim that folk psychology is a theory like phlogiston and so should be costless to drop; round 3 onto the felt indispensability and then the honest concession that eliminativism doesn't say folk psychology is useless *now*, only that it gets superseded later, which the activity can't stage; the cheat-sheet twist onto the reduction-vs-elimination distinction and onto the fact that when we "predict" people we're usually simulating or pattern-matching, not applying a theory at all -- so the premise that folk psychology *is* a theory is carrying a lot of weight, and there's a live fight about that (simulation vs theory-theory). Also work in two things the activity will make them feel but won't resolve: the self-refutation worry (the eliminativist has to *argue*, which needs beliefs to exist to be argued *with*), and the possibility that we can't drop the vocabulary not because it tracks something real but because our institutions and self-images have been built around it and it reshapes the people it classifies.

Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it -- I can already imagine teams gaming the ban with paraphrase ("her brain is disposed such that..."), the round-3 silence and giggling eating my time, the neuro sheet being too vague to do anything, and one student refusing to play along because beliefs are obviously real. How do I handle each? And be straight with me about what the activity genuinely can't reproduce and what I'll have to just tell them.
