I've spent the better part of this year on a qualifying paper about imperial decline, and a running argument with two friends in the department has pushed me toward a pattern I can describe but not name. Here is the shape of it. Someone says that the power we are watching is an empire, or a hegemon, or the center of a unipolar order, and that empires decline, and therefore we should expect this one to decline too. The evidence offered is a list: Rome, the Ottomans, the Spanish, the British, the Soviets. The length of the list is doing the work. When you object that a fair number of polities that sat at the top of the heap are still with us, or quietly stopped being at the top without any dramatic fall, the reply is that those were not really empires, or were not really at the top, or that the interesting question concerns only the ones that did fall. I have started noticing the identical shape in smaller arguments. A colleague will predict that a particular firm is doomed because every firm that reached this size with this much debt has failed, and when I ask how many firms of that description did not fail, it turns out the sample contains only the failures, because the ones that survived are not filed under that description — or are filed under it only in retrospect, once their survival has been reinterpreted as a long, slow, disguised decline.

What I want to know is whether this has a name, and if it does, which name fits best, because I keep reaching for ones that are close but wrong. It is near the business of choosing a base rate from a class of cases whose outcomes you already know; it is near the general puzzle that any event belongs to indefinitely many reference classes that yield different probabilities; and it is near the warning about drawing conclusions from a set that was filtered by the very outcome you are trying to predict. But none of those quite catches the part that bothers me, which is that here the category itself appears to have the outcome folded into it, so that whether a given case belongs to the reference class is not settled until the future resolves, and the comforting sample is assembled after the fact. Is there an established term for that specific circularity, or for the family it belongs to? If there is not, please coin one: give it a definition, say plainly how it differs from survivorship bias and from the ordinary reference-class problem and from the more technical error of conditioning on a common effect, and mark its boundaries — for instance, whether it stops being a mistake when the person is scrupulous about how the category is defined, or when the sample is very large, or when the category names a real kind rather than a retrospective label. I am not after reading recommendations or citations; I want the concept itself pinned down, and a bare label will not satisfy me, since what I actually need is the carving: what this thing is, what it is not, and where its edges are. Please answer in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — just paragraphs, the way you would write a long reply to a colleague.
