So on my reading you can deny animals thought and still leave them a kind of feeling, whereas her reading has him deny the feeling too. Her comeback is that for Descartes thought just *is* conscious experience — cogitatio covers everything that's present to the mind — so if you deny animals thought you've denied them any inner life at all, and my "sensation without thought" is a distinction he never actually drew; I'm reading my own modern category of phenomenal feeling back into a 17th-century author. Then a third person in the group said the whole fight is anachronstic, because "consciousness" in our sense — there being something it's like — simply wasn't a category Descartes was working with. He filed sensation under body and thought under soul and never had the intermediate box we're both rummaging around in. So now we've got three people and three completely different stories about what the disagreement even *is*, and that's the part I actually want sorted out.
