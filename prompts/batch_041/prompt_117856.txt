ok so I need help writing a speech and I've left it late. Thursday, 7pm, the rec center: my neighborhood association is voting on a motion about the 6-story building proposed for the old gas station lot on Vernon. The motion says the neighborhood does not consent to the project. I'm on the wrong side of the room — I'm going to argue against that motion, in front of 60 of my neighbors, probly 40 of whom I've known for years, and the councilmember who'll actually decide this will be sitting in the back row. Four minutes, hard limit, no slides, the vote happens right after I sit down.

I don't want the standard housing-crisis speech. Two reasons. One, the supply argument in that room is a dead horse — everyone there has heard it, everyone has a reply ready, and the reply is always "then build it somewhere else," and around we go again. Two, I don't actually believe the standard version of it, and I'd rather not stand up and say something I think is only half true.

Here's what I want the speech built around. When somebody in that room says 'this is my neighborhood and I get a say,' they might mean one of two completely different things, and my side keeps pretending it's only ever the first one:

- a claim about burden — I'm being made to eat a cost (the parking, the crane for two years, the school that's already full) that the people who profit from this don't eat. Fine. That's a distributive complaint and it deserves a real answer, not a sigh.
- a claim about standing — this is where my life happens, so I have a say over what it becomes, and not because I can itemize what I lose. I have it just by living here.

The second one is real. And it's the one my side violates every time we tell people the building won't really change anything, nothing to see here. It will change things. Saying otherwise is exactly how you end up with a room that's stopped trusting you. So I want to say that to them straight: you're right that this changes the place, and you don't get to veto it anyway, and here's why. The "here's why" can't be "because there's a crisis and you're being selfish."

The other thing I want in there, and this is the hard part. The motion is about consent, and I think consent is actually the right frame — I just think the room has drawn the boundary of who counts in the wrong place. The people who'd live in that building aren't here and don't get to be. Neither are the people who get pushed out of the neighborhood when nothing gets built, and some of them used to be in this room and got priced out already. A room can't consent on behalf of people it won't let in the room. I want to land that without it sounding like a seminar or like I'm calling my neighbors small.

Constraints. It has to sound like me talking, not like an op-ed. Don't have me cite studies, papers, or anybody's book — I'd get the numbers wrong and it would land as condescending anyway. Definately don't use the word NIMBY; that word ends the conversation in that room. Give people something concrete to vote for at the end, since I'm asking them to give up the big symbolic vote. And I have to admit one thing I personally stand to lose without pretending I don't — I rent, my lease is up in June, and if nothing gets built around here I'm the one moving. Don't make that a punchline or a sob story, just let it sit there.

Write the speech.
