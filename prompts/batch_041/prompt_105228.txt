I am a doctoral candidate in formal epistemology, and I am convening an adversarial collaboration between two philosophers of probability who have spent the past year talking past one another. The first maintains that Dutch book arguments — the synchronic one for the probability axioms and the diachronic one for conditionalization — genuinely justify probabilistic coherence as a requirement of practical rationality: an agent whose credences are incoherent is committed to a package of bets she must regard as individually fair and that guarantees her a loss, and avoiding sure loss is a constitutive demand on rational partial belief. The second maintains that such arguments establish at most a constraint on an agent's fair betting odds, not on her credences as doxastic states; because the argument must route through betting dispositions and utilities, it delivers a pragmatic consistency condition rather than an epistemic norm, and any genuine justification of probabilism has to be built instead from considerations of accuracy or dominance.

Please draft the joint statement that both could sign. I want a numbered section of claims they agree on — including what the Dutch book theorem does and does not assert, and what it presupposes — followed by the residual disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would accept, and then the cruxes, with each side's prior prediction recorded. I take two cruxes to be whether credence is constitutively tied to betting dispositions at all, and whether a failure of countable additivity is a genuine incoherence or merely a finitely-additive non-issue; for each crux, supply a concrete case or argument that both sides would accept as bearing on it. Two cases I want both to address are an agent who is incoherent but has no betting dispositions whatsoever, and an agent whose credences are merely imprecise rather than incoherent. Finally, tell me whether any part of their dispute is verbal, and which.
