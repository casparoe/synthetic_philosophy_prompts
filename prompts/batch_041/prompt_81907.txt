For a piece I'm writing on political polarisation, I've been working from what seemed like an obvious premise: that an "echo chamber" and an "epistemic bubble" name the same phenomenon — a person sealed off from opposing views — and that the remedy is therefore the same for both, namely to flood the zone with the other side's best arguments until the better case wins. A colleague who studies this tells me I've collapsed two quite different pathologies into one, and that for at least one of them exposure to countervailing argument is close to useless — it can even entrench the position it was meant to dislodge. I'd like to get this right before I file, and I can't consult anything online.

Assume the reader is intelligent but has no background in this area. Set out, in this order and roughly these lengths:

1. My premise, restated fairly, and precisely where it goes wrong (~100 words).
2. What the two phenomena actually are, and why the difference between them is not merely terminological (~250 words).
3. What each does to a person's beliefs and, separately, to their trust in the sources of those beliefs — and why that second effect is the one my proposed fix ignores (~250 words).
4. What follows for the intervention I was going to recommend, and what would have to be true for it to work at all (~200 words).
5. What I still cannot honestly claim in the piece (~100 words).

Keep it non-technical, avoid jargon where plain words will do, and be blunt about the parts where I am simply mistaken.
