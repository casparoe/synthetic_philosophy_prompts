I retired about six years ago, after thirty-five years in a molecular biology lab, and I've finally been doing what I always said I'd do and reading philosphy — mostly philosophy of biology, which I never had time for back when it would have helped. I keep circling back to the adaptationism fight, the spandrels paper and the decades of argument since, and two things bother me.

The first is that the same argument keeps showing up in different clothes. When ENCODE came out claiming that something like 80% of the genome was functional, and half the field screamed that biochemical activity isn't the same thing as function, it felt to me exactly like the spandrels fight again, just with different molecules. A philosopher friend told me that's really just the old split between a causal-role sense of function and a selected-effects sense, and that most of the heat in both debates comes from people using one sense while their critics use the other. Maybe she's right. Maybe I'm just pattern-matching because I'm a tourist.

The second is that I can't tell which parts of this are actually live and which parts are just famous. Is the real open question whether the spandrel critique is an empirical claim or a conceptual one? Whether byproduct explanations can be tested at all, when "it's a byproduct" seems to predict the absence of design rather than the presence of anything you could go looking for? Whether we're arguing about how a trait originated or about why it's still around? Whether the same argument form does any real work in byproduct theories of religion, or of consciousness? Or is it something I'm not seeing because I'm new here?

So here's what I actually want, and I want a ranking, not a tour. I sit on the advisory board of a small foundation, and we can afford to keep four or five people going for three years on purely conceptual work in this area — reading, thinking, writing, arguing with biologists, no new data, no wet lab. Which of the open questions here most deserve that money, in what order, and why? For each one I want the reasoning, not just the label: is it upstream of the others, so that settling it would dissolve or sharpen the rest? Is it tractable for people who are good at philosophy but will never run an experiment? Is it one of those questions everyone nods at as important but that wouldn't actually change anything downstream if it got answered? And what would have to happen in the next year or two for you to move it up or down the list?

Be blunt about my ENCODE comparison — if it's a shallow analogy that only works because I don't know this literature, say so and tell me what the real disanalogy is. And skip the reading list, please; I can find papers on my own. I want your judgement about what's worth doing.
