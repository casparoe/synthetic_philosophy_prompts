Next month I'm giving a talk where I argue that the whole process-vs-outcome supervision debate in AI safety is built on a mistake. My line: shifting the credit-assignment bottleneck from the outcome to the process doesn't get rid of it, it just relocates it to a higher-dimensional space that's harder to specify, and it adds a fresh Goodhart failure where models learn to make their reasoning look legible rather than actually be sound -- so in some regimes chain-of-thought monitoring is counterproductive rather than helpful. I call it the bottleneck-relocation argument, and Ive tacked on a proposed fix: "outcome-anchored process penalties", where you only reward the process to the extent it predicts the outcome on a held-out distribution.

The audience is mostly RL engineers who build process reward models and care about whether this stuff scales and about sample efficiency, plus a couple of mech-interp people who basically think you can just read the chain of thought off the model.
