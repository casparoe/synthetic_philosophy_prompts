Take the night the Berlin Wall effectively opened — Nov 9 1989, the botched press conference, the crowds massing at Bornholmer Strasse, the border guards who in the end just let them through. Analyze it as a case in the theory of collective intentionality and joint action. Do the mapping properly: who or what is the acting subject at each stage — the individual crossers, the crowd, the guards, the East German state itself — and is anything here a genuine joint action rather than a pile of individual actions that happened to converge? Run the usual suspects against it: Bratman-style shared intention (meshing subplans, common knowledge, mutual responsiveness), Searle-style collective intentionality (which a single person can have on their own, no one else required), and Gilbert's joint commitment with its demand for concurrence. The guards are the part I care about — they seem to act together, but with a shared intention to do what, exactly? And did the crowd ever have a 'we', or only a coordination cascade plus a shared mood? Then flip it: what does the episode do to the theories? Does it show shared intention is too strong and that we need something like minimal/emergent joint action instead, or that none of these accounts were ever built for a leaderless mass on a single evening? Keep it philosophy, not history — I know the history. Maybe 800 words.
