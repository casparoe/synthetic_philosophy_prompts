I'm a third-year philosophy student presenting my term paper at my department's work-in-progress session next week, and I need a Q&A prep sheet. The paper defends a graded account of moral status built on two axes I distinguish as felt presence — whether there is anything it is like to be the creature at all — and hedonic integration, the degree to which a creature's affective states are bound into a single evaluative perspective that holds across modalities and over time. My claims are: status comes in degrees; the axes are not interchangeable, since felt presence functions close to a gate while hedonic integration is a dial fixing how much status a creature that clears the gate has; the behavioural and neurobiological markers people actually rely on — nociceptors, opioid-responsive analgesia, trade-off behaviour — are evidence for the first axis and nearly silent about the second; and the assumption embedded in most welfare legislation, that a creature either is or is not a bearer of interests, is a category error rather than a harmless simplification. My worked cases are an insect, a fish, a dog, a human infant, and, as a limiting case, a thermostat, which I say scores zero on the first axis and nonzero on the second — which is how I show the second axis cannot be doing the work on its own.

The room will be about a dozen people: two comparative psychologists who work on nociception in fish and insects and doubt that graded status is measurable at all; one Kantian who insists status must be all-or-nothing if it is to ground any duty; three consequentialists who will ask what the view implies for insect farming and pest control; and two functionalist philosophers of mind who will press me on what integration is supposed to be.

Give me the objections I will actually get, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, each phrased the way that audience member would phrase it, with a two-minute reply and a candid note on where the reply is weakest. Tell me what to concede at the outset and what to hold, and include the question I am hoping nobody asks. I don't need citations or a reading list.
