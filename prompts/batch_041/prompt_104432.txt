I'm a backend engineer with no real philosophy training past one ethics elective, and I've been reading way too much about prisons lately, mostly because a coworker keeps posting photos of Halden, that Norwegian prison that looks like a nice college dorm with a forest and a recording studio. The take everyone repeats is punishment vs rehabilitation: the US locks people up to hurt them, Norway locks people up to help them. My half-formed suspicion is that this framing hides the actually interesting split, and I want to turn the suspicion into a proper proposal for a one-semester independent study — roughly 20 pages, no lab, just reading and writing.

Here's the idea I'm trying to sharpen. Rehabilitation seems to mean at least two different things and people slide between them. On one reading, the offender has some defect (bad judgment, bad habits, whatever) and the point of the prison is to correct it, which is basically a kind of therapy aimed at a broken person. On the other reading, the point isn't that the person is broken at all; it's that a sentence shouldn't strip someone of the ordinary standing to come back into normal life, so the prison gets built so ordinary life keeps going inside it — work, cooking, visits, guards treating you like a person. The first reading treats the prisoner as a patient, the second treats the prisoner as a citizen who's been paused, not deleted. What I can't tell is which reading the Norwegian example actually supports, and whether it even matters — because if it's the second, then the usual retributive reply (but they did the crime, they deserve the punishment) might not even be in conflict with it, since the second reading never denies responsibility in the first place.

So: write me a short research proposal. I want the central question stated precisely enough that it has a definite answer — something like, is Halden-style rehabilitation better understood as correcting a defect in the offender or as preserving the offender's ordinary standing, and does the choice change what the Norwegian example shows about American mass incarceration? Tell me why the existing ways of framing this (punishment vs rehab, retributive vs consequentialist, the whole standard menu) leave my question open rather than already answered. Then the method: what I'd actualy do month by month, which features I'd compare (the role of the guards, the normalcy principle, the stuff American prisons call reentry programs), and why that approach beats just arguing from first principles. Then say what a finished result looks like, and be honest about what would count as the project failing — e.g. if the two readings turn out to be the same thing in disguise, or if the distinction makes no difference to any real disagreement. Don't bother with citations or a reading list, I'll add those myself once I know what I'm looking for. And tell me if this is too big for a semester.
