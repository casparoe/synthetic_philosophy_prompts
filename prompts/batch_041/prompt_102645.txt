I write about science and ideas for a general-reader mag, and I keep bumping into two camps in the math-and-physics philosophy crowd who've been talking passed each other for years about Wigner's old puzzle — why the math we cook up purely for its own sake keeps turning out to fit the physical world. Camp one, the realists: the fit is a genuine fact about the world and it cries out for an explanation. Camp two, the pragmatists/anti-realists: the fit is mostly a fact about us — how we build, prune and relabel theories; we brand something "math" once it works and quietly bury the bits that didn't. Write me the joint statement these two could both actually put their names to after a serious good-faith collaboration.

I want: (1) the claims they can both sign, numbered; (2) the disagreements, each stated in wording its own side would accept as fair to itself; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling things would actually move somebody; (4) for each crux, the case, argument or historical episode both sides agree is relevant, with each side's prediction written down in advance. And before any of that, make them do the unglamorous part: break "effectiveness" into the distinct things people lump together — fitting data we already had, predicting something nobody was looking for, tying two unrelated domains together, and just being computationally usable — and say, for each, whether both sides agree it needs explaining at all. If a chunk of their fight is really just verbal, say which chunk and why.

Then hand me the disagreements as a table, columns: disputed question | realist wording they'd sign | pragmatist wording they'd sign | the crux it comes down to | the shared case/argument both accept | what the realist predicts about it | what the pragmatist predicts.
