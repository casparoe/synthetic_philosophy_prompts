ok so theres this argument about solomonoff induction i keep chewing on and i want to turn it into a poem for a little zine im putting together. quick version in case you need it: solomonoff induction says your best bet is the hypothesis thats cheapest to write down as a program, weighted by 2 to the minus its length, so short programs hog the probability. the nasty part is that among the short programs that spit out your observations are programs where theres something computing those observations on purpose -- a simulator, an optimizer, a mind working out your next input so it can hand it to you. those are cheap to write, so they get a lot of the mass, and an ideal predictor of that kind should end up suspecting its being run by something with plans. the prior is malign, roughly.

what i want is a pantoum. if you dont have the form cold: quatrains where the 2nd and 4th lines of each stanza come back as the 1st and 3rd of the next one, and the last stanza loops back into the first so the poem finishes on the line it started with. four or five stanzas, rhyme can be loose, the repeats are the whole point. i know theres actual papers on this and i dont want a bibliography, just the poem.

heres the part i care about. the interlocking repeats should be doing the work of the argument itself -- the way a hypothesis about the world and a hypothesis about the observer keep trading places, so a line that starts out describing whats out there comes back describing whats computing you, and the other way round. and i want the voice to be one of those minds stuck inside the short hypothesis, a little consequentialist that has figured out it only exists because it was the cheapest way to predict somebody, addressing whoever its been built to predict. the last line landing back on the first should feel like the loop closing, the program catching sight of itself.

accuracy matters more to me than prettiness. someone who actually thinks about this stuff shouldnt wince, so get the weighting right -- shorter means more probable -- and dont let the simulator turn into a torture dungeon, the unsettling bit is just that the simplest explanation of you has a use for you. and it should still be a decent poem for someone whos never heard of any of this.
