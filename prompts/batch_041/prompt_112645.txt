I'm an undergraduate in a political theory seminar and I sit as the student representative on my student union's clubs and societies committee, which votes next Thursday on whether to recognize a campus chapter of a national political party. The chapter's constitution requires its officers to vote as the membership directs, forbids members from publicly opposing the party's platform, and asks for the right to run candidates in student elections under the party's name; in exchange it wants a budget line, a room, and a stall at the fair. Two members want to refuse recognition outright, and the chair wants to recognize it on the single condition that the binding-mandate clause be struck.

Our seminar has been reading Simone Weil's late note on abolishing political parties, and I want her used rather than gestured at. The definitions I want you to work with are hers: a party as a machine for manufacturing collective passion; an organization built to exert collective pressure on the thought of each of its members; a body whose first and, in the last analysis, only end is its own unlimited growth. So too her claim that belonging to a party always constrains one to untruth, which is why she calls the existence of parties an unconditional evil, and her proposed substitute: proximity to a journal one reads or writes for, rather than membership in an organization that fields candidates.

Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete disposition -- recognize, recognize with stated conditions, or refuse -- with any conditions spelled out and a timetable for review, and it should give the strongest case for whichever side I come out against, including the freedom-of-association argument and the objection that a 200-member student club is not the mass party of a European nation-state, which is the setting Weil was writing in. I also want it to settle one question I can't. If the binding mandate and the pledge of loyalty to the platform are the heart of her objection, is the objection severable from them? Would a chapter that dropped both have escaped what she was describing, or does she think the party form does the damage regardless of which clauses it happens to contain? Argue that through rather than asserting it. No citations needed.
