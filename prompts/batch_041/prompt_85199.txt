William James died in 1910, before the DSM, before the concept of an interactive kind, and before online communities in which people adopt and circulate psychiatric labels. Suppose he could observe a present-day case: a diagnostic category for a condition characterised by pervasive low mood, impulsive self-harm and unstable self-image, heavily discussed on social media; people who fit the label begin to experience themselves in its terms, their symptoms shift accordingly, clinicians revise the criteria in response, and the revised criteria shape how the next cohort presents. Ian Hacking called this a looping effect and treated such categories as interactive kinds, in contrast to indifferent kinds whose members do not alter under classification. James, for his part, warned against the psychologist's fallacy, the observer's confusion of his own standpoint with that of the mental fact he reports, and in his study of religious experience he attacked medical materialism, the dismissal of an experience by appeal to its organic causes. Predict James's likely position. Would he treat the looping effect as a vindication of his pragmatism and radical empiricism, or as a reductio of any would-be science of mind? How would he distinguish the looping effect from the psychologist's fallacy, if at all? Would he accept Hacking's distinction between interactive and indifferent kinds, or collapse it? What would he say about the practical authority of a diagnostic label that is at once a clinical instrument and a form of self-understanding? And what stance would he recommend to a clinician deciding whether to diagnose, and to a patient deciding whether to identify with the diagnosis?

Answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. Each item should make one distinct predictive claim about James's view and tie it to a feature of his philosophy. Write in British English.
