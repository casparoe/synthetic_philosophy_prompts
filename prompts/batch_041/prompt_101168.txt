The essay question was: "Scientific realists hold that the empirical success of our best theories gives us reason to believe those theories are at least approximately true. State the pessimistic meta-induction, and assess whether it defeats the realist's claim." One student's answer is pasted below.

"Scientific realists like Putnam argue that realism is the only philosophy that doesn't make the success of science a miracle: our theories make novel predictions that keep coming true, and the best explanation of that is that their central terms really refer and their claims are at least approximately true. The pessimistic meta-induction, first put forward by Larry Laudan, turns this reasoning against the realist. Laudan gives a long list of theories that were once accepted and empirically successful but are now regarded as false — the crystalline spheres, phlogiston, caloric, the electromagnetic ether, and so on. Run an induction over that record and the conclusion is that our own successful theories are probably false too. So empirical success is not a reliable sign of truth, and the no-miracles argument collapses. Really the meta-induction is just a version of the underdetermination of theory by evidence that Duhem and Quine talked about: for any body of evidence there are always rival theories that fit it, so success can never pick out the true one. Realists have replied in a few ways. Worrall's divide et impera strategy says we should be selective: the parts of a theory that do the predictive work are usually retained by the successor theory while the idle metaphysical baggage gets thrown out, so we can be realists about the former. Psillos's structural realism says we should be realists about the mathematical structure of theories and agnostic about the nature of the entities. Kitcher makes a similar point with his distinction between working and presuppositional posits. On these views the meta-induction fails, because it is only ever the discarded parts that turn out to be false and the parts we are asked to believe in survive theory change. Since the responses show that success does track truth after all, realism comes out of the exchange vindicated."
