Ok, longtime lurker here — I've been reading the rationalist blogs and forums since the old Overcoming Bias days, and there's one fight I've watched go around at least four times now without it ever actually resolving, just people talking past each other harder each round. The fight is about whether religion makes people better, and whether a secular society can hold its trust together without it. I want to finally force the two camps to write the document they should've written years ago.

So picture two people. Person A: thinks religion is doing real load-bearing work for prosociality and social trust — that it's not just in-group niceness, that the moral vocabulary and the community enforcement and the shared rituals are all propping something up, and that if you dissolve it you get a trust deficit that nothing else has convincingly replaced yet. Person B: thinks secular societies demonstrably sustain trust fine, that the apparent religiosity effect is mostly confounded (in-group vs out-group generosity, selection effects, people claiming to be nicer than they are on surveys), and that more moral was never a well-defined target anyway so the question is partly malformed.

What I want is the joint statement these two could both put their names to after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Specifically:

The claims they can actually both sign — about what the data does and doesn't show, about what would even count as evidence one way or the other, and about what more moral could plausibly mean (better treatment of out-group strangers? lower crime? more charitable giving? more honest? self-reported vs behavioral?). Don't let either side smuggle in a definition the other wouldn't accept.

Then the real disagreements, each one stated in wording that its own side would nod at — I don't want the neutral-sounding formulation that quietly favors B.

Then the cruxes: the sub-questions where settling one would actually move a side. I have a hunch one crux is whether the trust religion produces is general or parochial, and another is whether the secular trust we see now is riding on a religious inheritance that's still decaying, but you tell me if those are the real ones or just the ones that are easy to argue about.

For each crux, give me a concrete case or piece of evidence that both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. And if part of this dispute is just verbal — two people using trust or moral to mean different things and then fighting about the conclusions — say which part and why.

Format: numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table with columns for the claim, A's version, B's version, and the crux it hangs on. Keep the whole thing under 900 words, table included, cause I want to actually print it out and hand it to the two people in my reading group who've been going at this since like 2019.
