A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about farmed shrimp and crabs and reached an impasse I would like help getting past. Her position is that the arithmetic settles the matter: the numbers of individuals are so vast, and the killing practices so lightly regulated, that aquatic animals should absorb a large share of animal-welfare effort however uncertain the underlying science; she wanted, more specifically, to assign a shrimp some fixed fraction of the moral weight of a chicken and then let the sums run. Mine is that the uncertainty is itself the crux — that we do not know whether a decapod's nociception amounts to anything it is like to be, and that a program which presumes the question will resolve favorably is neither good science nor good advocacy — and I wanted to know whether such a fraction is even the kind of quantity one could estimate rather than stipulate. Neither of us could say what a serious effort should take up first. Suppose a foundation has roughly three years and funding for a small team, and means to support conceptual work and inexpensive empirical work rather than costly neuroscience, on the welfare of farmed fish and crustaceans. Which open questions should it prioritize, and why? Please defend the ordering rather than survey the field: say what makes one question upstream of others, which questions the field treats as central are in fact dead ends because no plausible answer would change anyone's practice, and what would have to be learned, or fail to be learned, for the ranking to change.
