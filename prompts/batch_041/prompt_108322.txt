I am assembling my position on the demandingness objection to consequentialism for a seminar and I want a consistency audit of the commitments below before I present them. Here is where I have landed.

(1) The criterion of rightness is act-consequentialist: an act is right just in case it produces at least as much impartial good as any alternative available to the agent.

(2) That criterion is a standard of rightness, not a deliberative procedure. Since it was never offered as an algorithm for deciding what to do, the fact that trying to maximize impartially would be ruinous as a way of life is no objection to it.

(3) All the same, a standard of rightness is defective if it is not livable. A requirement that no human being could satisfy over the course of a whole life is not a requirement. Ought implies can, and here can means psychologically sustainable across a lifetime, not merely logically possible.

(4) There is no fundamental distinction between duty and supererogation. If an act is optimal, it is required; talk of going beyond the call of duty is at best a pragmatic label rather than a moral category.

(5) The demandingness objection, properly stated, is a fairness objection rather than a cost objection. The complaint is not that morality is expensive but that the burden of compliance is distributed unfairly, so that some people are asked to give nearly everything while others give nothing.

(6) In conditions where others are not complying, what one is required to do changes: one is not obliged to shoulder the whole of a project that others are free-riding on.

As a verdict on a case: someone who spends a large discretionary income on a private passion while giving only a modest amount to effective charity acts wrongly, though I would not call them blameworthy.

Which of these actually conflict, which only appear to, and which single revision would resolve the most? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and for each one say what would have to be given up and what that concession costs. Be explicit about any commitment I am treating as free that is not, and about which apparent conflicts dissolve once the commitments are read charitably. Write in American English.
