I have an argument that I assembled for a seminar on levels of description, and I can no longer tell whether it is a genuine reductio or a piece of sleight of hand. It is set in the careenium, Hofstadter's image of a billiard table covered with countless tiny magnetic marbles, the simms, which careen about and occasionally clump magnetically into larger, slower clusters, the simmballs. The simmballs are not mere visual noise; their trajectories are partly shaped by forces from outside the table, so that they begin to track conditions beyond it, which is why the pun on simmballs and symbols is tempting. The question I care about is Hofstadter's own: inside the careenium, who shoves whom around? Do the simmballs push the simms, or is the appearance of the simmballs pushing the simms merely an overlay on the simms pushing one another?

The argument runs as follows.

(1) Each simm is a small magnetic marble; the force on it at any moment is the vector sum of the magnetic and contact forces exerted by the other simms in its neighborhood, together with the walls.
(2) Hence the force on any simm can be computed from the positions of the other simms alone. No reference to simmballs is needed, and adding one changes no prediction.
(3) So every motion of every simm is fully determined by simm-level facts together with the laws.
(4) A simmball is nothing over and above a cluster of simms bound together magnetically; there is no further entity in the arena.
(5) Therefore the simmball level introduces no causal power beyond what the simm level already supplies. Whatever a simmball seems to do to a simm is nothing but the simms doing it to one another, described at a coarser grain.
(6) So the simmballs never push the simms around. Symbols, in whatever sense the careenium has them, are along for the ride.
(7) Nothing above turned on the peculiarities of the careenium. Wherever one level is constituted by another, the same reasoning runs: a hurricane and its molecules, a market crash and its transactions, a chess strategy and its moves.
(8) Therefore nothing at any higher level is ever a cause; all causation occurs at the bottom.
(9) But (8) is intolerable. It implies that a physician who removes a tumor because it is malignant, a trader who sells because a bubble is bursting, and a person who acts for a reason are all naming non-causes; that raising a price or offering an argument cannot bring anything about; and that no description above the bottom can be right or wrong about what caused what.
(10) Since (9) is unacceptable and the steps look valid, one of (1) through (6) must be false.

Tell me which step to give up, why it looked compelling in the first place, and what giving it up costs. In particular, tell me what becomes of the idea that there is a single privileged level at which causes are located, and whether whatever replaces it still lets simmballs, and reasons, make a difference rather than merely accompany one. I would most like to keep (1) through (4); if I have to surrender one of those, tell me what I am buying with the loss. Then say what would change your mind: which consideration would lead you to reject a different step, and where your own reasoning is most likely to fail.
