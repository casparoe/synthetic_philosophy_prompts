ok so I've been reading Szasz and I think I've talked myself into something close to his view: that 'mental illness' is a metaphor rather than a disease, because 'disease' in medicine means some kind of demonstrable bodily lesion or malfunction, and psychiatry cant produce anything like that for its main categories — so the label ends up doing moral and political work, marking deviance or unhappiness or inconvenient behaviour as a medical problem, and sometimes as grounds for locking someone up. I want to hold this line. Tell me what would actually count against it, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and be explicit about which of the defeaters I should concede rather than dodge.

Things I specifically want you to go at:

- My criterion of disease. Is 'disease means demonstrable lesion' something ordinary medicine would actually sign up to, or am I holding psychiatry to a bar that half of internal medicine fails (essential hypertension, migraine, chronic pain — things treated as diseases without anyone being able to point at the thing)? If the criterion is too strong, what's left? Does the argument collapse, or can I retreat to something weaker and keep the interesting part?

- The vindication pattern. General paresis is the classic case where a condition that looked purely psychiatric turned out to have an identifiable organic cause, and I gather there's now a family of autoimmune encephalitis cases where florid psychosis is the first presentation. Does the history of medicine being a run of these count against me? Or can I say those were never mental illnesses in my sense — that what happened is psychiatry lost custody of a neurological disease? And is that reply honest, or is it just immunising the view?

- Here's a case I keep chewing on. Suppose someone develops a reliable test — a blood marker, say — that predicts which first-episode patients will respond to antipsychotics and which will recover without them, but that tells you nothing at all about a cause or a mechanism. Not a lesion, so not a disease marker by my lights. Does that count for me or against me? It feels like the kind of thing that would make 'its just a metaphor' sound pretty thin.

- There's a reading of Szasz where the real target isnt the ontology at all but the coercion — the disease label is what licenses involuntary commitment, and 'is it really a disease' is a distraction from the liberty question. If that's the right reading, does my position have any empirical defeaters at all, or am I defending a purely conceptual thesis? And is that a bug or a feature?

- What would I actually have to see to give the view up? I'm worried that between 'the criterion is a bit loose' and 'the categories are socially shaped' and 'the label does political work', I've built something nothing could falsify. If that's what's happened, say so, and say whether it matters for a claim of this kind. Also: is the 'proves too much' objection fatal? If my criterion would rule out a bunch of things everyone calls diseases, isn't the honest move to drop the criterion rather than the diseases — and if I drop it, is there a version of the point that survives, something like: the category does normative work that its medical veneer hides?
