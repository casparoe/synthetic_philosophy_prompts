I'm not a historian, but I got roped into organizing my town's 250th anniversary last month and it's thrown me a bit. We're reviving the old May Day procession — except it turns out the procession was cooked up in 1972 by the parish council, and everyone on the committee knows this, and they still talk about restoring it and get all misty-eyed. Fine. But here's the part that's actually bugging me: I know it's made up too, and when the band started up I still got a lump in my throat. Now I see the same shape everywhere. A national holiday that's twenty years old but already gets called timeless. A company guarding its centuries-old ritual, which on inspection starts in the 1990s. And pointing out the dates never seems to defuse any of it — people just absorb the debunking and carry on, and so do I.

Is there a name for this? I've got a vague sense that historians have written about traditions being invented, and something about nations being imagined communities, but those seem to be about the making — not about this odd bit where everyone, me included, knows it's recent and it still works on us. Does this specific thing have an established name, and how would you mark it off from plain invented tradition, or from just calling it nostalgia? If there's no name, I'd want a proper definition with the edges drawn: what makes it different from a tradition people sincerely believe is old, and from a cynical elite foisting something on a gullible public. A bare label won't help me — I want the carving.
