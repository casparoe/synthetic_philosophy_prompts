I chair the governance committee of an interdisciplinary research institute, and I need to circulate a written opinion before our next meeting. The committee must decide how to allocate this year's pool of discretionary research funds, and one member has proposed dispensing with our usual deliberation in favor of a decision market. Under the proposal, the committee would first settle by majority vote the outcome we care about -- for instance, whether a funded project should be judged by its peer-reviewed output, by the external funding it subsequently attracts, or by some weighted combination of the two -- and would then let any member of the institute trade a contract that pays out according to whether a given proposal reaches that threshold within two years. The prevailing market price would determine what we fund, on the principle that we should vote on our values and bet on our beliefs. The member offering this has described it as an application of what is sometimes called futarchy.

I want the opinion to confront two features of our situation rather than pass over them. First, the people best placed to trade are exactly the people whose proposals are at stake, together with their collaborators and their competitors, so the pool of well-informed traders and the pool of interested parties are one and the same. Second, we do not agree on the metric, and I suspect that any metric we adopt will be gamed in proportion to how much rides on it; moreover, the choice of metric is itself partly an empirical question, which threatens the tidy division between voting on values and betting on beliefs.

Draft the opinion I will circulate. It should recommend a definite course of action -- adopt the market, reject it, or pilot it under conditions I can defend -- with a time frame, and, if a pilot, the safeguards it must carry and the conditions under which we would call it off. Give the strongest case for the side you come down against its due; I would rather the memo lose an argument fairly than win one by caricature. Write for readers who know the general idea of decision markets but nothing about our institute, and do not refer me to any literature.
