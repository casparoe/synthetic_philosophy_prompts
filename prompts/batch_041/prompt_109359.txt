What I want to get at is a kind of case I've been calling a "distributed omission" in my notes — where nobody actually lets someone die in the moment. Instead, some person, committee, or bit of software sets a default at some earlier point, and then whoever's on the scene just doesn't override it. The ventilator with the auto-wean setting, the triage tool that quietly deprioritizes a patient, the formulary that never covers the drug. The reason it's a mess is that you can pin the "doing" on whoever wrote the default and the "allowing" on whoever stood there and did nothing, and neither of them really owns the outcome — and sometimes its the same person at two different times, or nobody at all, because the default drifted out of some optimization process nobody signed off on. Give me five examples of conceptual issues this kind of case raises — exactly five, numbered, a sentence or two each, no intro and no wrap-up paragraph.
