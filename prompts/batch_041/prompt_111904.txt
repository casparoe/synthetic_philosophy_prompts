I'm the treasurer on my schools student council and I got kind of obsessed with this idea called futarchy -- where instead of arguing about what to do, you first vote on a number you want to go up (like average attendance or how many people say they're happy at school), then people bet on which proposal makes that number go up, and you just do whatever the bets say. I want to actually try it next semester. We've got about $4,000 in the activities fund, ~25 people on council, everything has to get decided in one 45 minute meeting, we can't gamble real money so it'd be points on a shared spreadsheet, and the principal can veto us. Also the annoying part: the people who'd be betting are mostly the same people proposing the proposals, and we won't see whether most decisions worked until next year when half of us have graduated and nobody who lost the bet is around anymore.

Can you turn futarchy into a procedure I could actually run in a meeting? I mean the steps in order, what counts as a good enough answer at each step, where it should just stop and say no, and the points where I have to stop pretending its a procedure and let a person decide. Then run it on two proposals: (1) spend $3,000 of the fund on new laptops for the computer lab, and (2) move the spirit week budget over to a peer tutoring program -- and for that second one I honestly don't even know what number we'd be trying to make go up. Include at least one where the procedure tells me something I won't like, and be honest about the parts where it's basically me making a judgement call and calling it a rule.
