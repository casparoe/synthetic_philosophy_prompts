I chair the ethics advisory board of a national quantum research institute. We must decide whether to approve a proposed decoherence-suppression campaign that will trap, cool, and repeatedly measure a large ion array, together with a companion study that annihilates small numbers of particles. A petition from staff, written from a panpsychist standpoint -- the view that experience is fundamental and ubiquitous -- argues that these procedures may inflict morally relevant suffering on countless micro-subjects, and asks us to suspend the campaign pending a suffering-impact review. The petitioners concede that no current experiment distinguishes panpsychism from its rivals, but invoke a precautionary-parity principle: we already extend protection to animals whose sentience is uncertain, so uncertainty about micro-subjects should not be treated as a license to proceed. The physics division's counter-memo replies that moral standing requires a unified subject whose experience can be good or bad, that the combination problem -- how micro-experiences would combine into a unified subject -- leaves micro-experiences with no locus of suffering, and that accepting the petition would authorize indefinite obstruction of fundamental research. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. Recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame; engage the petitioners' position seriously rather than dismissing it as metaphysics; and give an honest treatment of the strongest point on the losing side. The board needs something it can act on, not a general essay on panpsychism.
