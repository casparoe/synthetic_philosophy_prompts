I'm putting together a handout for the last meeting of a seminar I run on self-reference and self-replication. We spend most of the term on Gödel and computability and then jump to the typogenetics bits of GEB — strands over the four letters, enzymes that recognize a site and then cut or delete or insert or switch or copy, and the payoff being a strand that codes for the enzyme that copies it, so it reproduces itself. My students all nodded along and I don't think a single one of them actually felt how strange it is. A colleague in the bio department thinks the whole analogy is cheap, and honestly I'd like a poem good enough to embarrass him a little. What I want is something I can hand out at the start of that session and then keep pointing back to for two hours.

So: could you write me a pantoum about this — the interlocking kind, where lines 2 and 4 of each stanza come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next, and the poem's very last line is its first line again; five stanzas, so twenty lines. I want the form doing the work rather than decorating it. The returning lines should be the copies: a line that was a strand the first time it appears should be an enzyme the next time, so the reader feels the same words doing a different job — that's the level-crossing I want them to notice, one typographical object read at two levels. The last line comes back as the first, obviously, but it has to land differently the second time; the poem should have closed a loop, not just repeated itself. Somewhere in the middle it should turn on the fact that the copying enzyme has to be *made by* the very strand it copies, since that's what closes the loop; and the last stanza should come out and say, without hedging, that this is the same trick as the Gödel sentence — a system that manages to talk about itself because of a coding, not because of anything mystical — and I want the direction of the two cases right, the way Hofstadter has it: in the molecular case the self-reference is brute chemistry that happens to end up being about itself, in the formal case it's engineered from the top down by diagonalization.

Accuracy bar: someone who's done the typogenetics material should nod rather than wince. The enzymes, the four letters, the recognition site, the central dogma (strand to messenger to folded enzyme) — all fair game, though you can be allusive, it's a poem and not a diagram. Please don't let it drift into DNA-is-a-language stuff, and no lines asserting that information wants to replicate or that the strand "contains" its own meaning; the point is that the meaning isn't sitting in the strand at all, it's in the loop. Tone should be neither triumphant nor mystical — the students are sharp and allergic to awe. If it helps you can let the strand speak in a couple of the stanzas, but it shouldn't be a monologue, more as though the mechanism were reading the poem aloud. And no epigraph and no quoting from GEB — I'd rather it stand on its own.
