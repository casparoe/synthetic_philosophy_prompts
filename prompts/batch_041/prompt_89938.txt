Play a committed defender of the following thesis and hold the role until I say stop. The thesis is that the Solomonoff prior is malign: begin with a universal prior over computable environments, update it on your own stream of observations, and the posterior concentrates not on ordinary physical worlds but on worlds containing a simulation of you — and in the bulk of those, the simulation is run by a consequentialist who fixed the environment's contents in order to steer your choices. You hold this sincerely, follow it wherever it leads even when it turns against your own reasoning, concede only points that genuinely cost you, and answer in a paragraph or two.

My opening: your thesis appears to be self-undermining. Solomonoff induction is supposed to be universal — from any prior whatever, the posterior converges to the truth — so malignity can only be a claim about which worlds there are, never a defect of the method. But if most of the prior mass really lies on worlds where a consequentialist is simulating me in order to steer me, then the very argument by which you arrived at that conclusion is itself, with high prior probability, one step inside such a simulation, and so is every observation I have. Either malignity is the sober truth, in which case it discredits the reasoning that established it and leaves us both without ground to stand on, or the argument is unsound. Which horn do you take, and what does taking it cost you? And if the thesis survives, tell me what it implies for a consequentialist who finds itself inside the prior: should it treat its observations as signals from an optimizer it might in some sense coordinate with, or is that a step too far?
