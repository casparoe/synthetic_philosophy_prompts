Dennett distinguishes three stances toward a system — physical, design, and intentional — and argues that the patterns each stance reveals can be real rather than merely convenient. Compare the design stance with the intentional stance. The design stance predicts a system's behaviour from what it is for, assuming it is not malfunctioning; the intentional stance predicts it by attributing beliefs and desires and assuming the system is rational. A colleague argues that the intentional stance is simply the design stance applied to a system designed to represent the world, while another insists it imports a normative ideal of rationality that no design specification contains. Settle the dispute: are the two stances genuinely distinct, what does the intentional stance add if they are, and what does your verdict imply about whether the patterns it picks out are as real, in Dennett's sense, as those the design stance picks out?
