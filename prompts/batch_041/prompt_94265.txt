I'm in this weird reading group that's half ML interpretability people and half philosophy of science people, and we keep getting stuck on mechanistic anomaly detection. The question isn't just whether an output is anomalous, but when two detected anomalies count as the same mechanism. One person says it's when the same causal pathway is active -- same nodes, same edges, same intervention profile. Another says that's way too strict: two different circuits can realize the same mechanism if they're equivalent under the interventions we care about, so sameness is relative to a coarse-graining you chose in advance. A third person says both are chasing a fiction, because 'same mechanism' is just a pragmatic label we slap on for whatever purpose is at hand. I want to close our next session with a poem that stages this, not just name-drops it, because the disagreement is definately not going to resolve in discussion.

Could you write a villanelle, 19 lines, from the point of view of a detector that has flagged two anomalies and can't decide whether to file them as one mechanism or two? The two refrains should be the strict causal-pathway criterion and the coarse-grained intervention-equivalence criterion, and the way they keep returning and finally lock together in the closing quatrain should make the reader feel why the question won't settle -- each criterion should be right about something the other misses. Maybe let the pragmatist voice intrude in the tercets, but don't let it win. It should be accurate enough that somone who works on causal abstraction or mechanistic interpretability would nod, and someone who doesn't would still get the stakes. No citations, obviously. Start with the poem itself, no preface or commentary.
