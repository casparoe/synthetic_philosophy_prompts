A few of us have been going round in circles for months on the dust-specks thing — the case where you either let one person be tortured for fifty years, or you inflict one barely-noticeable speck of dust in the eye on some ludicrously huge number of people, and the question is which outcome is worse. Nobody's budged an inch. The aggregators say the total settles it; the other lot say a single torture is a different *kind* of bad and no pile of annoyances, however tall, can add up to it; and then everyone repeats themselves louder. I don't want a verdict on who's right. I want to know what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it verbal — are 'harm', 'worse' and 'welfare' quietly doing different jobs on the two sides? Is it a disagreement about what counts as a reason, one side treating each tiny thing as a reason and the other refusing to grant it the status at all? Is it bedrock — a clash of intuitions about the separateness of persons that no argument is ever going to reach? My own hunch, offered as a hunch and not a conclusion, is that the sheer size of the number is doing work that nobody has owned up to: 3^^^3 is not a population any universe could hold, so half the fight may be about an outcome that can't even happen, and that might be why it feels like arguing about nothing.

I want that hunch tested rather than agreed with, so here's a probe I invented. Shrink the case until it's plainly realisable: a big but ordinary cosmos, say a hundred billion billion people, each getting the single speck, weighed against the one torture. If the two camps still lock horns there, the disagreement isn't about uninstantiable numbers and my hunch is dead. If instead it softens into an ordinary utilitarian-versus-deontologist scrap the moment the numbers could actually exist, then the infinity-talk was load-bearing all along and the real dispute is somewhere else. Use the probe.

Write it up as four sections, in this order. 'The two sides' — restate what each camp is actually committed to, in their own terms, about 150 words. 'What the disagreement really is' — your diagnosis, with reasons, engaging what each side says rather than surveying the literature, about 500 words; be willing to say it's a mix, or that the question is ill-posed, if that's where the reasons lead. 'Running my probe' — say what happens to the disagreement when the numbers are realisable, and what that shows, about 250 words. 'What would count as progress' — a distinction, a formal result, a reframed question, or an argument that nothing should move it, about 150 words. Don't give me a reading list or citations, I can't look anything up from here anyway.
