I'm a third-year student in a formal epistemology seminar whose second half has been devoted to anthropics, and I now have to propose a topic for a substantial independent paper by the end of term — so the question of what is actually worth working on has become pressing rather than idle. I've come to think this whole cluster of problems hangs on a choice between two ways of letting one's own existence bear on hypotheses: the assumption that I should reason as though I were a random draw from the observers who actually exist, and the assumption that my existence is itself evidence favoring hypotheses on which more observers exist. The first seems to hand us a doomsday-style conclusion, together with a nagging problem about how the relevant class of observers is even to be drawn; the second appears to buy its way out of that at the price of the presumptuous philosopher, who is asked to prefer a theory predicting a vastly larger cosmos for no reason beyond the counting. A friend in the seminar insists the two assumptions are merely different bookkeeping conventions and that the whole disagreement is verbal; someone else proposed doing without either by conditioning on a complete non-indexical description of the world, which at different hours strikes me as obviously correct and obviously impossible. I suspect my friend is wrong, but I can't yet say what turns on the choice rather than on how we describe it.

What I want from you is an agenda: among the open questions in this area, which most deserve a student's sustained attention over the coming year, ranked and defended? I care far less about a comprehensive list than about the reasoning behind the ordering — for each item, whether it is upstream or downstream of the others, what would have to be settled first for it to become tractable, whether progress is more likely to come from formal work or from conceptual clarification, and which questions look central only because everyone repeats them. Please also say what would move a question up or down the ranking: an impossibility result, a decisive intuition pump, a resolution of the Sleeping Beauty dispute, a principled account of how the reference class is fixed, or something else entirely. Finally, tell me plainly whether the choice between the two assumptions is the right thing to organize a paper around, or whether it is a distraction from the questions that actually matter. Open with your bottom line in the first sentence — the ranking itself, stated flatly — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
