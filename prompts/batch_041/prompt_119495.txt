Ok so I've been going round in circles (ha) with two friends about Bertrand's paradox, the chord one. You say "pick a chord of a circle at random" and depending on how you do the picking you get 1/3, or 1/2, or 1/4 for the chance that the chord comes out longer than the side of the inscribed equilateral triangle. One friend says that just shows "at random" is meaningless until you say which physical process is doing the picking, so the puzzle dissolves and there's nothing left to argue about. The other says no, there's a right answer, 1/2, because there's essentially only one way to put a measure on the set of lines in the plane that stays the same under rotations and translations, and that's what picking a line at random has to mean; the other answers secretly assume some odd way of generating chords. They've been at this for months and neither moves.

I want a diagnosis, not a verdict. Is this a real disagreement, or are they answering different questions under one name, one about what a physical setup does and one about what a rational credence should be given only the words "at random"? And is the invariance argument an actual resolution, or does it smuggle in an assumption, like that the line is thrown from far away rather than pinned down by a point on the circle? I also keep thinking this is the same disease as the Borel–Kolmogorov thing where conditioning on a probability-zero event gives different answers depending on which limiting procedure you use. Am I right that those are one problem wearing two hats? And given whatever diagnosis you give, what would count as progress here: a theorem, a distinction, or just admitting the question was ill-posed from the start?
