So I work on reinforcement learning — I build agents, I think in states, policies, rewards, model-free vs model-based, exploration vs exploitation, that whole stack. I keep bumping into this old philosophy thing about the sphex wasp and "sphexishness" and free will, and I want to see it done properly in my own voccabulary instead of the hand-wavy version I keep getting.

Heres the gist as I understand it. The female sphex wasp paralyzes a cricket, drags it to her burrow, leaves it at the entrance, goes inside to check that everything is in order, comes back out, and then drags the cricket in. If you nudge the cricket a few inches away while shes inside, she comes out, finds it, drags it back to the entrance, and runs the check again. Do it again and she does it again — the story is that you can loop her indefinitely. The point people draw is that this looks like a fixed program: she can't clock that the burrow is already fine, she just re-invokes the subroutine. And the part that gets philosophers excited is that human behavior sometimes looks sphexish too — we say we're flexible, we can step back and notice we're looping, but do we actually, or do we just have a bigger, more nested program that feels flexible from the inside? Then free will gets bolted onto this: is the wasp-vs-us difference just a difference in the richness of the policy, or is there something categorically different — the ability to have done otherwise, to look at your own policy and rewrite it?

What I want from you: translate this whole thing — the wasp, the rigidity/flexibility axis, and the free-will payoff — into the vocabulary I actually use. States and observations, fixed vs learned policies, deterministic vs stochastic policies, model-free vs model-based control, hierarchical policies and options, meta-learning and self-modification, exploration, distribution shift, reward and the fact that a policy is always a function from states to actions, that kind of thing. Do it as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report: which parts of the philosophers' argument map cleanly onto something in my stack, which parts I can only get by fudging the terms, and which parts have no counterpart at all — no state, no policy, nothing that computes.

And the honest question at the end. Is my suspicion right that 'free will' here is basically just the gap between a fixed policy and a policy that can update itself — so a meta-learner is 'freer' than a tabular agent, an agent that rewrites its own objective is freer still, and there's no cliff, just a gradient? Or is there something in the philosophers' notion of acting freely that my vocabulary genuinely cannot express — and if so, is that a defect in my vocabulary or a sign that their notion was never clear to begin with? Also, side question: is the wasp really as sphexish as the story makes her out to be, or is this one of those cases where the vignette is a lot cleaner than the actual animal?
