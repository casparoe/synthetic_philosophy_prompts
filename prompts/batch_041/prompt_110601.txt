I teach a second-year seminar called Concepts and Reasoning — 16 students, one 80-minute session, no maths beyond school level. I want to run something in the style of those old Bongard puzzles: two piles of little drawings, you're told one pile is in and the other is out, and you have to work out what makes the difference. I want to use the format as a trap rather than a puzzle.

What I want them to feel in their bones is that fitting the examples is cheap. Every rule they come up with will fit every example I've shown them, and nothing in the examples picks out the one I had in mind. The way I want them to feel it is by getting confident, commiting in writing, and then being wrong in public on cases they never saw.

Two things I want built into the deck, and they should be doing the work, not the debrief:

1. The real rule should be relational, not a property of individual cards — what makes a card "in" is something about how the two piles relate, so that anyone hunting for a shared feature of the in-pile cards finds a perfectly good feature rule that fits every training card and then falls over on the held-out ones.

2. At least one of the spurious rules that fits all the training cards should be gerrymandered in the grue-ish way: a disjunction of two unrelated conditions (the stock illustration is the predicate that means green if you check it before some date and blue otherwise). Nobody would propose it after seeing more data, but it is exactly as consistent with what they've seen as the real rule.

Set it up so they commit in writing to a rule and to predictions about four held-out cards before I reveal anything. Then a second round where pairs swap envelopes, so they get to try again knowing what just happened to them. Then the reveal, then the debrief.

Practical constraints: pairs, not groups of four (16 students, 8 envelopes); everything on paper, cards printed in advance, no projector except for one slide at the end; I want it to fit in 80 minutes including the debrief, so tell me where to cut if I'm running late. The cards have to be fully describable in plain words — simple shapes, dots, arrows, numbers, positions, sizes — because I'm printing them from your description, not drawing from your images.

Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (I've had activities die because one person in each pair does all the thinking, and because students decide the answer is "any rule is as good as any other" and get smug about it instead of uncomfortable). And be honest about what the activity can't do: I know there's a limit to how much of the point you can stage with a made-up deck, and I'd rather know where the seams are.

Give it back to me as a single JSON object and nothing outside it — no preamble, no commentary after. Use exactly these fields:

- "title": string.
- "one_line_summary": string.
- "setting": object with "audience", "group_size", "duration_minutes", "room_and_materials".
- "learning_goal": string — what they should come away seeing, one sentence.
- "hidden_rule": object with "statement", "why_it_is_relational", "spurious_property_rule_that_also_fits", "gerrymandered_rule_that_also_fits".
- "deck": array of objects, each with "card_id", "pile" ("A" or "B"), "features" (array of short plain-word descriptions of what is drawn), "role" ("training" or "held_out"), "what_it_does" (why this card is in the deck).
- "opening_script": string — exactly what I say before handing out envelopes, phrased so it gives nothing away.
- "run_sheet": array of objects with "stage", "minutes", "cumulative_minutes", "what_happens", "facilitator_lines", "what_to_watch_for".
- "reveal_script": string.
- "debrief_map": array of objects with "moment_in_activity", "idea_it_maps_to", "how_to_put_it", "one_sentence_they_should_be_able_to_say".
- "predictable_failures": array of objects with "what_goes_wrong", "how_likely" ("high"/"medium"/"low"), "how_to_handle".
- "cannot_reproduce": array of objects with "feature_of_the_idea", "why_the_activity_cant_show_it", "what_the_facilitator_must_say".
- "if_running_short": string, and "if_running_long": string.
- "anything_else": string.

Write it in British English.
