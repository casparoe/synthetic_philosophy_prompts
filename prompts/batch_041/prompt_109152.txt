I run a fortnightly discussion group for about a dozen people, most of them out of the rationalist blogosphere, and I have one ninety-minute session for the multipolar-trap material: races to the bottom that nobody chose and that everyone can see. I don't want another prisoner's-dilemma demo — they have all played one and it produces no vertigo. I want the trap to assemble itself in the room. Three things matter to me. It must ratchet: each round's outcome should move the baseline for the next, so the group ends somewhere no one would have agreed to at the start and can point to the round where turning back stopped being available. Each player must know privately how much they can afford to keep going and be unable to check anyone else's capacity, so that the race runs on each player's belief that they might be the one who can outlast the rest rather than on any shared appetite for ruin. And all three of the usual exits — a binding agreement among the players, an enforcer who can punish defection, and a move to a fresh domain where the race has not started — should be available, with each failing for a different reason. Give me the rules, the materials, the scoring (nothing that requires real money), a timeline, and a debrief that maps each phase onto the structure it illustrates. Flag the ways it is likely to misfire on a first outing and what to do about them, and say plainly which feature of the real thing the game cannot reproduce and I will have to supply in words. British English, please.
