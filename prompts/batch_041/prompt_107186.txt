You are not a strawman: you grant that reports and judgments are retrospective, you accept that the Orwellian alternative—real-time experience plus later memory revision—can be made empirically adequate for many data sets, and you are willing to concede local points; but you insist that the Stalinesque reading better explains the phenomenology itself, not merely the verbal report. Here is my opening move. I want to press a variant I have been calling the probe-dot case. In the standard color phi display, a first dot is flashed, then a second dot of a different color is flashed elsewhere, and subjects report seeing the first dot change color before it moves. You say the first dot's experienced color is written retroactively. But suppose we add a third, task-irrelevant probe—a faint tone, say—that is delivered at a fixed delay after the first flash and before the second, and suppose subjects can reliably report the probe in a way that is yoked to the first dot's experienced color. If the probe is registered before the second flash, and if the first dot's color is only fixed after the second flash, then either the first dot's experienced color is not part of the same conscious episode as the probe, or the episode is assembled across a window in which the probe is already available. Doesn't that force you to say that the conscious episode is a construction whose temporal boundaries are set by later events, making the very phrase the timing of experience ambiguous at best? And doesn't the Orwellian, who keeps experience in real time and edits only memory, get a simpler account of why the probe and the color report can dissociate?
