Ok so this might be more of a "is there a word for this" question than a real philosophy question, but its been bugging me for months and everyone at work just says thats the hard problem and moves on, which I don't think is right.

Background, I do RL, reward shaping mostly, robot sims. Two things keep happening and I'm now pretty sure there the same thing.

Thing one. Any time a run is mostly negative reward, someone on the team says the agent is suffering, or in pain, or hates this level. But the reward numbers are mine. The same task can be written with a penalty on the bad outcome and zero on the good one, or with zero on bad and a bonus on good. I've shipped both. They're the same task with a constant added, the agent ends up with the same policy either way. So whether the agent's world contains negative numbers at all is a choice I made at my desk, and I can't see how a choice I made at my desk is where the suffering lives. People say yeah but it's the actual signal in the actual agent, sure, but the signal has no fixed zero point, so below zero isn't a fact about the agent, it's a fact about my spreadsheet.

Thing two, this is the one that really gets me. Take a trained policy and I can describe it two ways. It heads for the green tile because green pays, or it stays off the red tile because red costs. Identical trajectories. If I train it with only a penalty on red, I say its avoiding something. If a colleague trains the exact same behavior with only a bonus on green, she says its pursuing something. And there's no test that settles which of us is right, because nothing in the trajectories or the weights distinguishes the two stories. So whether we call the thing suffering or just optimizing seems to ride on which description we happened to pick, and the pick isn't forced by anything real.

Is there a name for this? I really don't think hard problem of consciousness is it, because I'm not asking whether the agent is conscious. I'm asking whether the claim that the reward signal is the agent's pleasure or pain is even well formed, given that the signal has no zero and the approach/avoid reading is underdetermined by the behavior. Nearest things I can come up with are indeterminacy of translation and the inverted spectrum thing, but those both seem to be about cases where there IS a fact of the matter and we just can't get at it, whereas here I suspect there might be no fact of the matter at all, which feels like a different problem. Is that distinction already named? If it isn't, coin me something, and then tell me where the edges are. Does it only bite for scalar signals? Does it go away if you add a homeostatic drive, or an internal critic, or a separate pain module? Does anyone actually think it means the whole reward-as-valence framing is broken, rather than just incomplete? I don't need a reading list, I need the carving, a name by itself won't help me.
