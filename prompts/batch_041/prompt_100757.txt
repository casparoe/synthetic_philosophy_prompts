Alright — I need help writing the final for a seminar I'm TAing on Dewey and democracy, and I want to build the essay question around something that actually happened to me this spring, because every practice question I've tried just comes back as a summary of Democracy and Education and that's not the point of the exam.

So I got roped onto my neighborhood's participatory-budgeting steering committee. In theory it's democracy-as-inquiry in the flesh: we meet, frame the problem, hear from residents, deliberate, then vote on which projects get the money. And it sort of worked, except for three things I can't stop chewing on.

First, the deliberation stage got captured. The most confident, most fluent people set the frame (there's one guy who just loves a microphone), and by the time we voted, the vote was basically ratifying whatever the loudest framing had been. Nobody moved. The "inquiry" felt like theater and the vote was the real thing.

Second, when our process picked a project, the group that lost kept saying "fine, we'll see" — and it hit me that we never actually will. There's no control group. We can't run the counterfactual where we funded the other project and watch what happens. So in what sense was any of it an experiment?

Third, and this is the one that bugs me: the whole problem situation was handed to us. The city council set the budget envelope and the eligible categories. We never got to inquire into whether those were even the right questions.

So here's the question I want to ask, and I want it to be something you can't answer by reciting Dewey's definition of inquiry or the line about democracy being a way of life. Roughly: take the strongest of my three worries, and tell me whether Dewey's experimentalism about democracy actually has the resources to answer it, or whether it needs patching — and if it needs patching, what the patch costs him. I want them to say which piece of the machinery (the indeterminate problem situation, the hypothesis, the test, the public) is doing the load-bearing work and which piece is expendable, and I want them to take a stand on whether "democracy is experimental" survives a case where there's genuinely no controlled test to be had.

Table columns: Move / What earns full credit / Marks / Common way students miss it. Total 20 marks, and make the A-versus-B line a specific move, not just "more detail."
