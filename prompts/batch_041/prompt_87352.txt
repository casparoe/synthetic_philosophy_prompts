In a graduate seminar on the philosophy of physics, a question about why the past is settled while the future remains open was closed off with the remark that entropy is disorder and that the arrow of time simply is the increase of disorder, so that there is nothing left to explain. I suspect that remark is doing several distinct jobs at once, and before I decide whether the dismissal was earned I would like a careful inventory of the claims it could be making.

Please lay the readings out as a menu, ordered from the nearly trivial to the genuinely radical. For each reading, state what it commits one to, and then give a verdict: platitude, defensible but contested, or false. I am particularly interested in how the following distinctions sort the menu. First, entropy as a count of the microstates compatible with a given macrostate versus entropy as intuitive messiness, and whether the latter survives the observation that the macrostate and the coarse-graining are chosen rather than found. Second, thermodynamic entropy versus the information-theoretic quantity that shares its name. Third, the second law understood as a strict law versus as a claim about overwhelming likelihood. Fourth, the role of the low-entropy initial condition, which appears to be doing the real explanatory work whenever the increase of entropy is asked to account for the direction of time, and which seems to make the near time-reversal symmetry of the underlying dynamics a problem for some readings and not others.

I would also like the "just is" in the remark separated into its possible strengths — identity, one-way explanation, or bare correlation between the thermodynamic asymmetry and the psychological asymmetry of memory and deliberation — since the dismissal presumably requires the strongest of these.

Finally, tell me which of the readings, if any, actually licenses the dismissal I encountered, which leave the original question standing (perhaps relocated to the initial condition rather than answered), and which reading the sort of person who says such things most likely has in mind.
