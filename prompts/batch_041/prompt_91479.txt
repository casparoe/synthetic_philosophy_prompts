Two people in my reading group got into it over the doomsday argument and I couldnt referee it. Marco: the doomsday argument is basically sound -- take yourself as a random sample from all humans who will ever live and you should expect to land somewhere in the middle of the pack, not at the very start, so we're probably fairly close to the end. Priya: that's a scam; the bare fact that you exist cant be evidence about how many people there will ever be, because you'd be having exactly this thought under any hypothesis. Marco: fine, but the only way to block it is to treat your own existence as evidence favoring hypotheses with more people in them, and then look what you've bought -- a philosopher in a world of a few thousand people who thinks the world might instead contain a googol should conclude the googol theory is a googol times likelier, purely because he's here. That's absurd, so your fix is worse than the disease and doomsday stands. Priya: it's not absurd, just counterintuitive, and the same charge bounces right back -- under your rule, a hypothesis on which humanity dies out in 200 years beats one on which we last a million, for no reason except that you happen to be alive now; you're taking a fact about where you happen to sit and laundering it into evidence about the size of the whole. Marco: no, I'm taking a random draw from a set and updating on it, which is just probability -- if there are more people, there are more slots I could have been in, and I shouldnt be surprised to be in a big set. Priya: then why does it stop being probability when the slots are people? Neither would move, and honestly they both sounded sane to me.
