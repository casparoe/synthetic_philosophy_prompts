ive been trying to wrap my head around Skolem's paradox and I think I've found a version of it that's an actual contradiction rather than just a spooky-sounding one. Löwenheim–Skolem gives you a countable model of ZFC, and then you can Mostowski-collapse it into a countable transitive model M — I'm pretty sure that step is free once you've got the countable model. But if M is transitive then M's ∈ is my ∈ and its members are real sets, so there's no non-standard membership to hide behind. M satisfies "there are uncountable sets", so take one, call it X. X ∈ M, so X is a real set. M is countable, so X is countable. And M thinks X is uncountable, and since M's membership is the real one, uncountable-in-M just is uncountable. So X is countable and uncountable, flat out — not "countable from outside, uncountable from inside", which always sounded like a dodge to me anyway. So where's the escape hatch? And if there isn't one, doesn't that show cardinality was never a fact about a set, only about which model you happen to be standing in?
