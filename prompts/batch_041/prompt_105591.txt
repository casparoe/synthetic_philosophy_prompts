I have a set of verdicts about when a system's internal representations are genuinely about something, and I would like to know what principle, if any, unifies them. A pocket calculator's '2' after '1+1' is not about two. A chess engine's internal queen representation is about the queen, despite the engine having no body. A parrot trained to say 'red' only in the presence of red objects is not yet using 'red' to mean red, because it never uses the word to guide action or inference. A robot that learns to avoid red objects because red predicts a shock does use its red detector to mean red, even if it cannot report the color. A language model that has only read text and can answer questions about red objects does not have a representation that means red in the way the robot's does, even when its answers are correct. A blind person who has only heard others talk about red can still mean red by 'red' when she uses the word in inference and action. Is there one account of what makes a representation about something that delivers all six verdicts? If not, which verdict should I give up, and what does your preferred account predict about a system that has a red sensor but is trained only to press a button labeled 'red' for a reward, never using the signal for anything else? Please state the principle precisely, say what it predicts about the new case, and tell me which of my six verdicts it cannot accommodate.
