I'm a third-year PhD in philosophy — Kant and ethics, so adjacent to this but definately not my home turf — and my supervisor has handed me one session of his upper-level undergrad seminar while he's away: 75 minutes, about 14 students, they've done the usual weeks on utilitarianism and deontology, and this one is listed as incommensurable values and hard choices. Don't explain the topic to me, plan how I explain it. Concretely:

First, the order. I have a hunch the whole thing dies on the table if I say the word incommensurable before they've got two other ideas in place — that there can be more than one kind of value, and that comparing two options is something you can do well or badly rather than something that just happens — but I might have the dependency backwards, or be missing a step entirely. Tell me what has to come before what and why, not just a list. A rough minute-by-minute would help, I always over plan.

Second, the carrying example. I've been using this one with friends and it keeps failing: someone I know has to choose between staying in the city where her entire support network is, taking a job she's overqualified for, and moving alone for the job she actually wants. It fails because everyone says well she just doesn't know herself well enough yet, which turns it into an information problem and then the session is dead before it starts. Fix it or replace it, and tell me what the case has to have and what it must not have — it can't be a case where the two options are equally good, and it can't be one where there's an obvious number I'm refusing to compute.

Third, and this is the real reason I'm asking: the thing I want them to leave with, which I suspect is not the same thing as incommensurability, and I don't know what to do about that. Some choices aren't hard because the goods are hard to compare, they're hard because the goods only exist from inside the option — there's no standpoint from which both lives are available to you, so there's nothing to weigh them on. If that's a different animal from incommensurability, the session has to get there without pretending it's the same claim, and I need to know where in the sequence it can even be raised without being nonsense to them.

Fourth, misconceptions, in the order they'll actually turn up, and exactly where in the sequence I should head each one off. Ones I'm expecting: incommensurable means equally good; incommensurable means you can't compare at all, so it's all subjective and anything goes; I don't know which is better so they must be incommensurable; and the money one — no amount of money would make me give up X, therefore X and money are incommensurable. That last one I think is just wrong, since a ranking where one thing trumps another however large is still a ranking, so they're comparing, and I want to be able to say that without doing the well-actually face.

Fifth, one question to ask in the last ten minutes that tells me whether they've got that a choice can be hard with no better option, as opposed to them having quietly decided it's all vibes.

And tell me what to cut, plus what I should say out loud that I'm deliberately skipping. 75 minutes is nothing and I will try to fit all of it in. Also keep everything to claims I can stand behind without putting anything on a slide that needs a citation — I'm not going to gesture at names I half-remember, and half of them will have looked things up anyway.
