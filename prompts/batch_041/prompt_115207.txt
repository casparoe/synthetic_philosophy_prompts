Two people in my reading group went at each other for about an hour last week over the colour phi thing and I never did figure out who was talking sense, so I'm outsourcing this one.

Quick reminder of the setup, in case it matters: you flash a blue dot near the top of the screen, then a fraction of a second later a red dot lower down. People report a single dot moving down the screen that switches colour abruptly about halfway — and if you ask them when the colour changed, they put the change before the second dot was even on the screen.

Ravi's line: so your experience of the first dot must have been rewritten before you ever got it. There was no moment where you saw blue and then later learned better; the film got cut before it was shown. Which means questions like "what did you actually experience at 150ms" have no answer at all.

Nadia's line: no, you really did see blue first, and then the memory got doctored afterwards. That's a claim about memory, not about experience, and it's a much cheaper claim.

Ravi: cheaper how? Every test you could run is a report, and the report comes after the doctoring. If two stories predict exactly the same thing about every possible measurement, there's nothing to choose between them. And the story where the brain edits before you see it should cost time — the visual system would have to wait around for the second flash before handing you the first — but when people measure how long it takes the first dot to reach awareness (masking, reaction times), the phi case comes out the same as a lone dot, with no extra delay. So there's no editing step to pay for.

Nadia: that's verificationism and you don't want it. Also the timing argument is weak — the editing could be cheap, or run in parallel. And anyway we already know from masking and blindsight that states can sit in the system and drive behaviour without ever being conscious, so "was there a state" and "was it conscious" come apart empirically. That's all the Orwellian story needs.

Ravi: blindsight is states-without-experience, not experience-then-erased. And nobody in the phi experiment reports seeing blue and then misremembering it; the movement is the primary thing they report. So your version posits a conscious episode that leaves no trace anywhere, including in the one trace it's supposed to leave. Also there's a third option neither of you has mentioned: maybe nothing got revised at all, and the "change halfway" is just what the visual system does with two adjacent stimuli interacting — lateral inhibition and so on — one state, no editing, no memory trick.

Nadia: then say what "seeing blue at t" is supposed to mean if nothing in you was ever blue.
