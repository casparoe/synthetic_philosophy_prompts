For the past two years I have been writing a novel whose central character never lies — not once, in four hundred pages — and by the last chapter she has done more harm than anyone else in the book, and I have found that I do not have the vocabulary for what she actually does. I would like to know whether that vocabulary exists.

Three of her scenes, roughly.

In the first, her husband asks whether she has been seeing someone. She answers, "I have been at the studio every afternoon this week." That is true; the studio is where the meetings happen. Of the many true sentences available to her, she chose the one that would leave a particular picture in his mind, and she chose it for that reason.

In the second, a journalist asks whether the foundation's money was ever diverted. She answers, "Every payment to the family was approved by the board." Also true. What she leaves out is that there were payments to other people, which the board never saw; she lets the journalist assume she has answered the question he asked rather than a narrower one.

In the third, she is simply tired of being oblique and says, "I have never stopped loving him." She says it because it is true, and she watches her sister take it as a confession of something it does not mean, and she does not correct her. She did not set out to deceive anyone; she saw the misreading coming and let it stand.

I am aware of the long-standing quarrel over whether lying and merely misleading are morally different, and I do not need that quarrel rehearsed for me. What I need is finer than that, and I keep running into three boundaries I cannot draw. First: between the case where the speaker's own words carry the false suggestion and the case where her words carry nothing false at all and the listener supplies the error herself. Second: between the speaker who wants the false belief and the speaker who merely foresees it and does not mind. Third: between any of this and a plain refusal to answer, which seems to me a different animal altogether.

So: does this family of moves have an established name, or several names for its several species? Which of my three scenes fall under which, and does any established concept reach the third at all, or is the third simply not deception? If some existing concept covers part of what I have described, tell me precisely where it stops covering it. If nothing covers it, coin the term yourself, define it, and give me the boundary cases — the edges are what I need, not the label. Please answer in American English.
