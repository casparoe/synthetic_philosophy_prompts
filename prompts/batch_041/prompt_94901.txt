I am a novelist at work on a book set largely in Brussels, and I have come to you with a problem of characterization. My protagonist is a mid-level official who has given thirty years to the treaties' undertaking to draw the peoples of Europe ever closer together, and I have given her the image that everyone in the memoir and commentary literature seems to reach for: Europe as a bicycle, which stays upright only for as long as it keeps moving forward — so that a pause for consolidation, a halt to enlargement, a moratorium on treaty change, is not rest but a fall. She uses it against a younger colleague who wants to stop and repair the democratic deficit first, on the grounds that further integration without a democratic warrant is indefensible. I have never found a first source for the image, and I suspect that is because it is less an argument than a mood; but before I put it in her mouth, I want to know how much weight it will bear.

So give me the audit. What corresponds to what — rider, pedals, momentum, balance, steering, road, destination, the surrounding traffic? Where does the mapping hold, and where does it break? Which of the breaks bear on the work the image is doing in her mouth, namely licensing the claim that halting is inherently destabilizing, and which are mere decoration? What does the bicycle quietly import that the European Union lacks — a single rider, a single heading, a destination, a physics in which standing still and falling are the same event? And once the disanalogies are on the table, does her conclusion survive, or is there a stable third option between pedaling and toppling? If the image can be improved, tell me what a better one would be.

Throughout, keep the distinction between what is settled and what is genuinely contested among people who study European integration, and among people who study how analogies argue; do not hand me a contested reading as though it were the consensus. A table of correspondences would be welcome. I would rather my character make a defensible argument that some scholars would reject than an indefensible one that all of them would.
