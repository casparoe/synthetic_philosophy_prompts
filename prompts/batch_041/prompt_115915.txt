I am a secondary-school student who edits our school's philosophy journal; we publish three times a year, a bad justification would embarrass us, and I have about ten minutes to screen each submission before our teacher signs off. A recurring problem is submissions that claim to justify a rule of inference by adding a further premise, and I want a short decision procedure based on the puzzle in which the Tortoise accepts the premises of a deductive argument but will not draw the conclusion until Achilles grants another conditional, and then will not draw it again. Set out, as a numbered checklist, what I should check in order, what distinguishes a genuine justification from a premise that merely restates the rule, where the regress may legitimately stop, whether the regress is vicious or benign, and where I must hand the decision to our teacher because it is a judgement call rather than a mechanical test. Then run the procedure on two submissions: one that tries to justify modus ponens by adding a conditional premise, and one that tries to justify inference to the best explanation by adding a premise that the best explanation is likely to be true. Make one outcome one the submitter will not like, and state what the procedure cannot settle. Please write in British English.
