I work on a small alignment team at a company that trains coding agents, and every couple of weeks we have to decide, for a new task family, whether we're going to train the model on the final artifact — does the patch pass the tests, does the migration script actually run — or on the intermediate trace — was each step of the model's reasoning a step in the right direction. Right now that call gets made by whoever talks loudest in the kickoff meeting, and it's driving me up the wall, because half of our disagreements turn out to be about whether our verifier is trustworthy and the other half are about whether our labelers can even read the traces.

Constraints you should assume. Three labelers, all decent programmers, none of them experts in the domains we actually care about (mostly data engineering and infra), paid per trace. Process labels cost us roughly ten times what outcome labels do, and we can afford them for maybe two task families a quarter; outcome labels are close to free because we can run them automatically. Traces run from about five steps to a couple hundred. Our verifiers come in three flavors: cheap and reliable (unit tests, a type checker, a linter that catches the thing we care about); cheap and gameable (the patch deletes the failing test, the script reads the expected output out of a fixture file, the model finds the answer in a cached artifact); and nonexistent. Whatever you write has to end in a one-page spec our team lead signs off on, and he is openly skeptical of process supervision — his line is that process reward models mostly learn the labeler's prose style — so the procedure has to tell me when he's right and we should drop it.

One distinction I keep reaching for and dont have a name for: some traces are legible to our labelers, meaning they can tell whether step 7 was needed, and some aren't, even though the same labeler can tell whether the final artifact works. Build that question in somewhere.

Also, one thing I've been doing informally and don't know where to put: take a trace we know is correct, swap a single step for a plausible-but-wrong step, and see whether the process reward model demotes it. It catches maybe half the junk. Tell me whether that test belongs in the procedure and where, or whether it's just me fooling myself.

What I want is a procedure we can run in the kickoff meeting in under an hour, per task family, that comes out with one of: outcome-only, process-only, both, or neither (fix the verifier first / don't train on this family yet).

Give it to me in these sections, in this order:

"The short version" — the whole procedure in one paragraph, about 120 words.

"The questions, in order" — numbered, and for each one what counts as a sufficient answer and what it decides. This is the bulk of it, so take the room you need.

"Where it stops and hands off" — the points where a human has to decide, who that person is, and what exactly they're deciding.

"Three runs" — run it on (a) writing a SQL migration that adds an index and backfills a column, verifier is running it against a staging copy, traces ten to thirty steps; (b) fixing failing tests in a repo, verifier is the test suite itself; (c) writing a rollout plan for a service migration, no verifier, traces over a hundred steps, and the labeler isn't the stakeholder who'd judge it. At least one of these should come out against what I'd probably want, and say so plainly.

"Where this will misfire" — about 250 words, including the parts of the judgment you've dressed up as steps.
