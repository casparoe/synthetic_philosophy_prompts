I'm writing a short essay that applies Dennett's ladder of creature kinds — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian — to large language models, and I'd like detailed comments on it before I circulate it to anyone. Here is the draft, near enough as it stands:

The Tower of Generate-and-Test is usually told as a story about tests being moved inward. A Darwinian creature is tested only by selection across generations. A Skinnerian creature is tested within its own lifetime, but only by actually emitting the behaviour and paying for the outcome; it cannot run the trial off-line. A Popperian creature has an inner environment in which candidate actions can be tried and killed before anything is done in the world. A Gregorian creature adds the thinking tools it inherits from its culture, and so does its testing with borrowed instruments. Each rung buys cheaper trials, and the top of the tower is where trial is cheapest.

An LLM looks as though it were assembled from the top down. It has the products of the Gregorian rung in abundance: it handles notation, code, and argumentative form with real fluency. And with extended deliberation it can look as though it runs candidates past an internal critic. But the testing, on inspection, is not internal. It is a reward model fitted to human ratings, or a user, or the world. Generation is nearly free; evaluation is the scarce resource. That is the tower run in reverse, and I take that inversion to be the interesting fact about these systems, rather than any particular placement of them on the ladder.

Still, I keep wanting to place them, and I can argue for three of the four. Darwinian: training is selection over parameters, with no learning at inference time. Skinnerian: reinforcement learning from human feedback is operant conditioning over token sequences, reward shaping and all. Gregorian: they traffic in mind-tools at a scale no individual human manages. What I cannot make work is Popperian, since a Popperian creature is defined by the test being internal, and here the test is precisely what has been outsourced. I have been treating the rungs as cumulative — a Gregorian creature must already be a Popperian one — and that is what makes the placement question sharp for a system I want to call Gregorian at all.

What I want from you: (1) Is this a faithful use of the distinction or a stretch? Specifically, is my cumulative reading — which I lean on to rule out "Gregorian without Popper" — actually licensed by the account, or am I smuggling in necessary conditions where the taxonomy only records a sequence of additions? (2) Does the "tower in reverse" claim do real work, or is it a metaphor that collapses once I say what counts as a test? (3) Which of the three placements is weakest, and what would break it? (4) Flag anything I have got wrong about Dennett, and anything I assert about LLMs that you would want evidence for. Be blunt. I would rather cut the thesis than patch it.
