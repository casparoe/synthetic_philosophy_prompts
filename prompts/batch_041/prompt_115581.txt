ok so i want a poem about deflationism about truth -- the view that "true" isnt the name of some deep property propositions have, its more like a piece of logical kit: the thing that lets you sign off on a whole pile of claims at once, or agree with a claim you cant even state out loud. i want the form to carry it. use a refrain thats a plain truth-ascription ("it's true that ..."), and over the stanzas let the word "true" drop out of the refrain until the bare claim is standing there on its own and nothing is missing -- thats the redundancy idea made visible, we should be able to watch the word do no descriptive work. then in the last stanza bring it back, and make it plain we need it there: the case where you can only say "and all of that is true" because the list is open-ended or the claim cant be repeated. if you can, let one line nod at the objection that this leaves truth with no job as the thing inquiry is aiming at, but dont settle it -- the poem should end on needing the word, not on a verdict. 16-24 lines, no set form, dont rhyme unless it earns it. someone whos read a bit of the deflationist literature should find it right; someone who hasnt should still feel the word evaporate and then suddenly start working.
