I hold that an utterance of "there will be a sea battle tomorrow" is neither true nor false when made, and I take this to be a point about truth-making rather than about determinism: a future-tense claim needs something in existence to make it true, and the battle does not exist yet even if it is already causally determined. Tell me what would count against this view — the arguments, intuitions, cases, and formal results that should move me, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and distinguishing what a defender of the view would concede from what they would dismiss. In particular, I want to know whether it survives the disjunction (there either will or will not be a sea battle) remaining true while neither disjunct is, the fact that we assert, bet on, and assign credences to such claims, and the retrospective assessment of the prediction once the battle has been fought. Finally, give me an honest verdict on whether the position risks anything at all or has instead been insulated by its own qualifications, and whether that insulation is a defect. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of my question.
