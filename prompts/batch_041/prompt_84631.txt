My old research group is being wound up at the end of the month -- twelve years of technology-forecasting work, first half mostly nuclear risk, back half mostly AI. I'm giving the toast at the farewell dinner. The room splits in two: about a third are people who worked on this with me and will know what I'm gesturing at, and the rest are spouses, kids, two retired physicists, my brother-in-law the dentist, people who have heard me go on about this for a decade and have been very patient about it. So it has to land for both halfs.

I want to build it around von Neumann's late position on whether we can survive our own technology -- the thing he arrived at in the fifties, after the bomb work, where the interesting question stops being whether technology is good or bad and becomes that the rate of change has outrun the institutions that would have to steer it, and that this isn't a problem any one generation gets to hand off to the next. The reason I want that and not something warmer is that I don't want to give the standard farewell-dinner ending -- "the work continues, the next generation will figure it out" -- because if he's right, that ending is the one thing you're not allowed to say. But I also don't want to stand up and tell a room full of my friends' families that we're doomed, and I don't think he thought that either, as far as I can tell.

Write the toast, five minutes or so. Paraphrase his views instead of quoting him: nothing in quotation marks unless you're certain of the exact wording, and honestly I'd rather have no quotes at all than a made-up one. Keep the philosophy load-bearing -- the timescale mismatch and the no-handoff point should be what shapes the speech, so that it can't just dissolve into either reassurance or doom. Flag anything about his actual views you're unsure of rather than stating it as fact.

And tell me which parts of this lean on a contested reading of von Neumann, and what would change your mind about how to pitch it -- if the honest reading of him is more hawkish, or more resigned, or less of a "position" at all than I'm making him sound, say so, and say what that would do to the ending.
