I'm second year in a phil of science PhD and my thesis keeps drifting into Jeffrey conditionalization and updating on uncertain evidence, which is fine except I've realized I've been collecting objections and puzzle cases for a year without ever writing down what a theory here is supposed to do. Before I pick a side I want the spec. So: give me the desiderata list for a theory of how an agent should update its credences when what comes in isn't a proposition it's certain of, but something weaker — a shift in confidence, a noisy readout, a soft signal. Purpose I care about: I want a rule I can both defend in a phil paper and actually implement in a pipeline that ingests soft signals, so adequacy = gets the right answers on the cases below and doesn't need machinery I can't state out loud.

Requirements I want, each with the case that motivates it, and tell me which are non-negotiable and which are just what one camp happens to want:

- the classic case: I look at a swatch in bad light and come away 0.7 on green, 0.3 on blue. Any adequate rule gives an answer here, and it should be the standard-looking one — the old conditional probabilities of other hypotheses given green / given blue stay put, the new numbers on the partition are just the input. But say what that stay-put requirement (rigidity) is actually doing, and whether it's a genuine requirement or a substantive assumption in disguise.
- must reduce to plain conditionalization when the input is certainty. Non-negotiable, I assume.
- the input problem, which I think is the real one: on the version where the input isn't a proposition at all, just a change in credence, what stops any change of mind from counting as an observation? I want a requirement that seperates learning from wishful thinking, and I want to know if it can be met without smuggling propositions back in.
- partition choice. The rule only fires once you've said which partition the evidence lives on. If all I learn is that my credence in H should now be 0.8, what fixes the partition? Is there a requirement here or is this just a hole.
- order effects. Successive updates by the rule generally don't commute — I worked a two-hypothesis example and the two orders gave me different posteriors. So do I demand commutativity for evidence that's intuitively independent (two different soft classifiers on the same image; the same net run twice with dropout), or is the order-dependence a feature I should embrace?
- related, and I haven't seen anyone put it this way: the rule is invertible. Any input that takes me from P to P' can be undone by a later input, because rigidity is preserved either way. Should I require no-unlearning — evidence, once in, can't be washed out by a later soft input — and if so does that collide with rigidity or with the previous bullet?
- auditability: I want to be able to read the input back off the before/after pair. Contrast the graphical-models move of encoding the uncertainty as a likelihood ratio and conditioning on a dummy node, where the posterior doesn't tell you what likelihood you took in. Is auditability a real desideratum or an engineering prejudice of mine?
- the case where the evidence bears on the conditional structure itself and not just the marginal — I learn my colour vision is off, so P(green | it looks green) moves. Any rule that fixes the conditionals in advance seems to have nothing to say here. Requirement or counterexample?

Then flag any subset of these you think can't be jointly satisfied. Thats the part I most want. A short scorecard at the end is fine — plain conditioning, Jeffrey's rule, the dummy-node likelihood trick, and the option of insisting that all evidence is propositional after all and just conditioning on the experience. Keep the weight on the criteria though, I'll fight about the contenders in my chapter.
