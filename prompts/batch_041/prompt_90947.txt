I'm a novelist, not a biologist, and I've got a character who's an evolutionary biologist and spends most of a chapter arguing with a colleague about what natural selection actually selects. I need one good worked example to put in her mouth, and I want it to be airtight, so I'm asking you to build it to spec instead of handing me the usual one. Heres the purpose first, since it tells you what counts as cheating: the example has to make the disagreement real on the page, so it cant just stipulate that gene's-eye talk and group-selection talk are two names for the same thing — the two views have to be made to say different things about the same case.

So, the spec. Give me one single case, a real kind of organism, realistic ecology (no infinite populations, no hand-waving, and please stay away from the examples everyone already knows — sterile ants, blood-sharing bats, the myxoma rabbits), where some trait spreads through a population, and:

- a gene-level / inclusive-fitness account and a multilevel or group-selection account both get the spread right,
- but they come apart on the counterfactual — there's one parameter (say how much migration there is between groups, or how the groups get formed in the first place) such that if you nudge it, the two accounts predict different outcomes, or agree on this trait but disagree about what happens to a different trait in the same species,
- and the disagreement isnt about whether group selection exists in general, its about this case specifically.

Keep the scenario itself to a paragraph. Then walk it through: state the trait, the selection pressure, and for each account say what it claims is being selected and why. Then do the counterfactual and show exactly where the two peel apart. Tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work — thats the bit my character can lean on — and what happens to the whole thing if I remove that feature. And since she's arguing with someone who will push back, give me the best line the losing side has: how does a defender of that account attack the case and try to show the disagreement is merely verbal?

Last thing: at the end, tell me how confident you actually are in the verdicts you're giving, and single out the one claim you're least sure about. She cant afford to be wrong in front of a reader who knows this stuff.
