I've fallen down a rabbit hole on the old parapsychology work and I want to use it as a case study for a bit of metaphysics that I don't think gets pointed at episodes like this often enough. The shape of it, as I've got it: Rhine's card-guessing at Duke back in the thirties, the ganzfeld era, the precognition experiments that got alot of press around 2011, and then the failures to replicate, the decline effect (effects shrinking as samples grow), the sheep-goat or experimenter effect (results apparently better when the experimenter believes), psi-missing (the effect turning up as below-chance scoring), and the odd case of outright fraud. The believers' line is basically "the effect is real but fragile — it definately hides when you look at it wrong, it needs the right experimenter, it can invert." The sceptics' line is "there's nothing there, and you're describing noise, bias and selective reporting." I don't need a verdict on which is right; what I want is a conceptual mapping using the dispositions/finks/masks toolkit. As I've got that toolkit: a disposition is a real property whose causal basis, given the right triggering circumstances, produces its characteristic manifestation (a glass is fragile: struck, it breaks). A fink is a condition that removes the disposition or its basis at just the moment the trigger arrives, so the thing is genuinely fragile yet never breaks. A mask is something that blocks the manifestation while leaving the disposition untouched — the glass packed in foam doesn't break, but it was fragile all along. The standing puzzles are how to tell a fink from a mask from the mere absence of the disposition, and whether a "disposition" that is finked or masked in every test we've ever run deserves the name at all.

What I want from you: take the episode and run it through the toolkit properly. First get clear on the relata — who or what is the bearer of the disposition here (the subject, the supposed faculty, the experimental set-up as a whole?), what counts as the stimulus and what as the manifestation, and does the choice change the analysis. Then take the candidate explanations one at a time — psi-missing, the experimenter effect, the decline effect, selective reporting, fabrication, plain chance — and say for each whether the framework would class it as a mask, a fink, a failure of the disposition to exist at all, or none of those, being honest where the fit is bad. I'm especially interested in the cases that look like masks (sceptical experimenter in the room) but might really be finks, or might be neither, and in whether "psi hides when observed" is a coherent dispositional claim or just an unfalsifiable rescue dressed up in dispositional clothing. Then run it the other way: what does the episode expose about the framework itself? Does the fink/mask distinction actually do any work here, or does it quietly presuppose that we already know what the causal basis is — which is precisely what's in dispute? If the episode needs a category the toolkit doesn't have (say, something that removes the stimulus before it ever reaches the bearer, or something that flips the manifestation into its opposite rather than blocking it), then coin it, define it, and give me a couple of boundary cases where it would and wouldn't apply.

I'm not after a reading list, and please don't just tell me that dispositions are "about" counterfactuals and leave it there — I want the mapping to be specific to this episode, saying which features it explains and which it distorts, and where the theory is simply silent rather than making it speak. British spelling please, and don't worry about being tactful towards either the parapsychologists or the sceptics — I've read both sides and I want the concepts, not the tribal score-keeping.
