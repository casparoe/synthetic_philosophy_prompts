Write a joke about consequentialist cluelessness — a short stand-up bit, in American English, of maybe 150 to 250 words. The target is the predicament of an agent who accepts that the morally relevant consequences of an action run indefinitely far into the future and through causal pathways nobody can trace, so that any attempt to rank acts by their expected consequences gets swamped by effects no one could have foreseen; the familiar illustration is that a small kindness or a small rudeness might redirect a life, a marriage, or a political movement in ways that dwarf the immediate effect. The humor should turn on the actual structure of that problem rather than on generic jokes about utilitarians doing arithmetic. Let the audience see the specific bind — that cluelessness either leaves the theory useless for choosing among ordinary options, or pushes the agent back onto rules and habits that the theory was supposed to justify — and let them laugh at a character who has noticed this and is trying to live with it. Put the character in an ordinary moment: yielding a seat on the subway, picking a charity, deciding on a second cup of coffee. The punchline should follow from the logic of the problem, not from the character being dim, and it should not be resolved by invoking a philosopher, a book, or a technical term. A knowledgeable listener should come away thinking the joke understands cluelessness, not merely that it has heard of it.
