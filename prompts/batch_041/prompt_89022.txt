Ok so I've been going back over the LK-99 mess from last summer and it got me thinking about cold fusion, wich is the one everyone actually remembers. What I want: take Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes and run it over cold fusion properly, the real mapping and not the hand-wave. What's the hard core exactly, what's sitting in the protective belt, what did the positive heuristic actually tell people to go do next, and which of the rescue moves were genuinely ad hoc versus which were real novel predictions that just never got confirmed. I know the lazy version is just degenerating problemshift, case closed, so argue it through instead of assuming it. Did the programme ever have a progressive phase? And were the critics running a research programme of their own, with a belt made of calorimetry errors and contamination and bad statistics that they had just as much of an interest in defending?

Then the part I actually care about. Lakatos's whole thing is that you can't judge a programme in real time, that one failed experiment kills nothing and anomalies just get swallowed by the belt. But LK-99 was a claimed room-temperature ambient-pressure superconductor announced in preprints, a global replication race inside of about a week, and by mid-August basically everyone had settled on a copper-sulfide impurity doing the work and the whole thing was dead — a research programme judged and buried in a single summer, largely by people who never touched a sample. Cold fusion took years to reach the same verdict, and arguably never fully died, there's still a small LENR scene holding the line on the Fleischmann-Pons palladium-deuterium cell. So does the LK-99 speedrun refute Lakatos, or is he just describing a slower world? What's actually doing the work in the fast case — replication norms, statistics, the internet — and is any of that inside his methodology or is it all outside it? And if the framework can't tell us how long to wait, how long should we have waited on cold fusion, and who was supposed to be paying for that?

Last bit: what do the two cases do to the framework itself. Is progressive-versus-degenerating a real-time judgment or only a retrospective one, and if it's only retrospective, what is the methodology even for? Be honest about where Lakatos just has nothing to say instead of stretching him over it.
