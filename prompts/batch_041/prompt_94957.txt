I host a small metaphysics podcast, and next week's guest defends a view she calls network realism. On her account, universals are immanent — wholly present in each of their instances — and a universal's identity is exhausted by its position in a single network of determinable–determinate and similarity–difference relations; there is no intrinsic quiddity marking one universal off from another, only place in the network. The network itself, she says, is neither a further universal nor a set of universals but a primitive structure, and she holds that once the one-over-many argument is properly regimented it establishes less than its proponents suppose. Draft eight to ten interview questions that press the view rather than let her restate it. I want at least one question each on: whether her position can distinguish a world from its mirror image in which two universals swap every intrinsic character they have, and whether that scenario is even intelligible on her terms; what individuates the primitive structure itself, given that she cannot call it a universal without a fresh one-over-many; what does the work of instantiation if the tie between a particular and a universal is neither itself a universal nor a brute relation; and how sparse her realism is meant to be, i.e. what excludes disjunctive and negative properties once intrinsic character has been given up. For each of the first three, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give — she will probably deny that the mirror-image scenario is coherent, so make that follow-up cost her something. Close with one question about what she would accept as evidence against the view.
