Ok so I'm a third-year PhD in political theory and I have this argument with two friends -- one in PPE, one doing public finance -- that we've now had in basically the same form for two years, and I'd like you to diagnose it rather than settle it.

The shape of it: friend A says inherited wealth is the cleanest possible case of unearned advantage, you did nothing to deserve being born to the person who owned the firm, so it should be taxed away, and an inheritance tax is the least intrusive instrument for doing that. Friend B says the money is the parents' to dispose of as they like, and that a tax triggered only by death is aimed at a transfer that was never competed for in the first place, so 'equal opportunity' is the wrong frame to begin with; and anyway the thing raises almost nothing once trusts, valuation discounts and family firms are in play, so the whole fight is symbolic. They're both pretty sharp and neither has budged in alot of time.

My hunch -- tell me if it's wrong -- is that a big part of the impasse is two questions wearing one name. One is a fairness-to-individuals question: is the heir's starting point fair? The other is closer to the neo-republican worry about domination: not that some people have less, but that a handful of dynasties can capture parties, papers and the state, which is an oligarchy problem rather than an opportunity problem and would call for different remedies. There's also something bugging me about 'equal opportunity' itself. As a term it seems to belong to competitions (no rigged races), and inheritance isn't a race anyone lost, it's a transfer outside the market, so maybe the phrase is being stretched to cover two different ideas -- fair starting gates vs. fair play inside the gate.

And a smaller thing I cant tell the size of: does it matter whether we tax the estate or the recipient? Friend B says that's a draftsman's distinction; I suspect it's doing most of the moral work, because 'the parent's property' and 'the child's windfall' name different owners and different claims on them.

So: is this a verbal dispute, a substantive moral one, a hidden empirical one (I'd want to know which parts are empirical in principle and what measurement would actually move anyone), or two questions under one heading? Given your diagnosis, what would count as progress here -- a distinction, a piece of evidence, a change of question -- and if the answer is that nothing should be expected to move it, say that too.
