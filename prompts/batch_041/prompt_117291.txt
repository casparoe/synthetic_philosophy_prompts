I'm a science journalist — biotech and AI mostly, but I keep getting dragooned into pieces about speech: platform moderation, hate-speech law, the whole mess — and last month I sat through a two-day workshop on speech acts because I wanted my own head straight before writing a long feature. So here's where I've landed, and I'd like an audit rather than a lecture: (1) there's no such thing as a purely descriptive utterance — saying something is doing something, and 'that's just a fact' is itself a move in a game; (2) what act you've performed is fixed by the conventions in play in that context, not by what's going on in your head, so a promise made with no intention of keeping it is still a promise, just a rotten one, and sincerity is a norm you can violate without the act failing; (3) some acts only come off if they land — you haven't warned me, or refused, or resigned, unless it reaches someone in a position to receive it, so uptake isn't optional; (4) meaning is a matter of what the speaker intends to communicate, and hearers work that intention out from context, with conventions as raw material; (5) I've started thinking of a conversation as a running public ledger, where every move writes an entry — who's now committed to what, who's licensed to say what next — and the entries, not anybody's private intent, are what later moves answer to; and (6) the reason I care: some people's moves don't get entered. Not that they're ignored — there's no line in the ledger for the act they're trying to perform, so the refusal or the testimony never happens at all, however clearly they speak.

Go through these and tell me which are actually pulling against each other, which only look like they are, and how badly — rank them by severity instead of listing everything as equally bad. For each real tension, say which commitment has to give, what the cheapest fix is, and what that fix costs me elsewhere; if two of my tensions are the same underlying clash wearing different hats, say so and name the underlying one. I'd also like you to say how confident you are in your read overall, and to single out the one or two judgments you're least sure about — especially if any pair I'm treating as a tension is really just a verbal difference I've talked myself into.
