I've gotten weirdly obsessed with type design over the last few months — I bought a cheap drawing tablet and I've been redrawing the lowercase alphabet over and over, and it turns out to be way more philosophically slippery than I bargained for.

The thing thats been bugging me: when I draw an 'a' in some half-invented style, I can usually tell when its wrong — it looks like a 'd' that fell over, or like a squashed 'o', or like nothing on earth — and I can often tell when its right, but I can't for the life of me say what the 'a'-ness is. It's obviously not any particular shape. I've seen a's that are a bowl plus a stem, a's that are a circle with a stick on the right, a's that are basically a little hook and a blob, and the two-story a in the font I'm typing in versus the one-story a I write by hand look about as different as two shapes can look, yet nobody hesitates over either. So could you give me an example of a conceptual issue that comes up when you try to say what makes something an 'a'? Not a definition — an actual worked example. Pick some specific weird shape (make it up, the odder the better) and argue both sides of whether it's an 'a', or a botched 'a', or a different letter, or just a blob. I want to feel the tension, not have it resolved for me.

The wrinkle that makes it worse is that I can't design the letters one at a time. An 'a' I'm in love with on its own turns out to be wrong the moment I draw the 'b' and the 'c', becuase now it doesn't look like it belongs to the same alphabet as they do — but if I force it to fit them, it stops being a good 'a'. So there's a second question tangled up with the first: not just what makes this shape an 'a', but what makes it *this alphabet's* 'a', and when I look at a shape and say no, I genuinely can't tell which of the two standards is doing the work. Can you pick an example that shows those two pressures pulling against each other — a shape that's clearly an 'a' by one standard and clearly not by the other — and lay out what's actually at stake in each? And while you're at it: is there a coherent sense in which a shape could be a great 'a' that belongs to no style at all, or a shape that fits a style perfectly but isn't really an 'a'? I keep flip-flopping, so show me the cases rather than giving me a verdict.
