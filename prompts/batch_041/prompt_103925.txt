I am drafting a short essay on safe Pareto improvements and would like your comments. The essay’s central claim is that the usual definition of a safe Pareto improvement conflates two different robustness requirements. On that definition, a modification to a strategic interaction is a safe Pareto improvement when it leaves every agent at least as well off as the baseline across every preference profile in some uncertainty set. I want to argue that this captures only payoff safety. There is a second requirement, procedural safety: the modification must also not make it rational for any agent to adopt a decision procedure whose equilibrium consequences leave another agent worse off, even when the final payoffs at the original preference profile remain Pareto superior. My working example is a two-player game with a weakly dominated cooperative action and a baseline equilibrium. The modification adds an outside option that is individually rational for one player under some beliefs; its presence makes a threat credible and shifts play to an equilibrium that is worse for the other player. I claim the modification is payoff-safe if we quantify only over preference profiles and hold decision procedures fixed, but not procedurally safe if we quantify over admissible decision procedures as well. The proposed fix is to define safe Pareto improvements relative to a set of admissible decision procedures, perhaps restricted by a no-blackmail or no-irrelevant-counterfactual-sensitivity condition. Please comment on the following: (1) Is the payoff-safety/procedural-safety distinction real, or is it just a relabeling of equilibrium selection? (2) What is the weakest point in the example, and how would you repair it? (3) Is the proposed fix too strong, in the sense that it would rule out all non-trivial safe improvements? (4) Suggest one operationalization of procedural safety that a mechanism designer could actually use.
