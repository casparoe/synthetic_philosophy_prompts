Ok so I keep going back and forth about how much insects actually matter and I want to pin down what my real view is. Here are my gut calls. Swatting a mosquito: nothing, doesn't even register. Accidentally stepping on a beetle: a tiny pang, gone in a second. Boiling a lobster: that ones actually bad, I'd pay real money to avoid doing it. A whole ant colony wiped out by pesticide: I feel a twinge but I still spray. A billion chickens on factory farms: way, way worse than any number of ants, no question. But then I read there are something like 10^18 insects alive at any moment, and even if each one has like a 0.1% chance of being sentient, that's still 10^15 sentient beings, and I think 'huh, that might be the biggest moral fact there is' -- and then I go right back to swatting mosquitos.

The tentative principle I've been mulling: moral weight = number of individuals x probability of sentience x some rough capacity-for-suffering factor, with no discounting just because the probability is tiny. Does that get all my verdicts? Because it looks like it makes insects totally dominate everything (10^15 is not small), which is NOT how I feel when I spray the ants. But if I add a discount so that small probabilities get rounded toward zero, then the 10^18 stops mattering either, and I lose the 'biggest moral fact' feeling too. Is there a principle that keeps both, or do I have to give up one of my verdicts? And what does your answer say about a case I haven't mentioned: a lab that breeds ten million fruit flies and kills them for a study I think is basically pointless.
