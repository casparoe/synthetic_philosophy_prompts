Write a parody of a records-retention and disposal schedule issued by the National Records Office of an unnamed country: roughly 600 words, formatted as numbered series, each with a retention period and a one-line justification, and signed off at the end by a fictional officer with a plausible job title. The joke is that a document whose whole business is deciding which documents get destroyed must also decide its own fate, so make that self-reference exact rather than whimsical. Two invented mechanisms should carry the satire: a Non-Accessions Register, in which the Office lists the records it declined to take, and which is itself closed to the public; and a category of provisional permanence, under which records are held indefinitely pending a review that the schedule never schedules. Let the appraisal logic be recognisable to an archivist — the routine destruction of the records of ordinary people, the permanent retention of the records of institutions, duplicate and ephemeral doing quiet work as cover terms, a finding aid that can describe what is held but never what is missing — and let the standard objections land without ever being stated. Include a digitisation clause that makes the un-digitised disappear. The humour should come from getting the archival theory right, so that an archivist laughs rather than winces; stay deadpan, and name no real archive, statute, or archivist.
