I'm a first-year PhD student and I've been voluntold to do the ten-minute warm-up at our evolutionary psych reading group next week, before the actual discussion kicks off, and the room is a nightmare to open to: half of them think every human quirk is an adaptation that got hammered into us on the Pleistocene savanna, and the other half are the sort of people who bring up Gould and Lewontin's spandrels paper every single week and basically think the whole field is a machine for turning hunches into origin stories. I'd rather open with a joke then pick a side in the first thirty seconds, and I'm also just tired of the coffee in that building.

The trouble is every joke I can think of is some version of "evolutionary psychology explains why men don't put the toilet seat down," which is neither funny nor even a real example of the field. What I actually want is a joke where the punchline turns on the substance of the science-versus-just-so-stories fight: that you can cook up a plausible Pleistocene survival-or-mating function for pretty much any behavior, real or invented, that these stories are nearly impossible to falsify because nobody can go back and audit the ancestral environment, and that lots of traits are probably just side-effects of other machinery rather than adaptations at all — the way those little triangular panels under the dome of St Mark's look like they were put there on purpose but are really just what you're stuck with once you rest a dome on arches. If it helps, you can hang the joke on something modern and silly with no obvious caveman analog, like why we say "bless you" when someone sneezes, but the version I'd find funniest — and the one I'm really after — is a joke where the thing getting explained is evolutionary psychologists themselves, i.e. why they churn out so many of these unfalsifiable origin stories, with the punchline being that the explanation you just gave is itself another unfalsifiable origin story. Four or five lines with a real setup and a real punchline would be perfect; a slightly longer deadpan lab-meeting scene is fine too, just don't let it turn into an essay. It should be funny to someone who's actually read the adaptationism debates and not just a generic "scientists, am I right" joke, and it shouldn't need any names or citations to land.
