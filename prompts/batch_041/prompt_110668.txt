I'm in a reading group on free will — we've been doing the usual compatibilist / incompatibilist stuff for a few weeks now, and it's fine, but I keep getting stuck on a pattern that nobody in the readings seems to talk about, and I want to know if it has a name, because I have the strong feeling I'm re-inventing a wheel that already exists somewhere and I just don't know the word.

Here's the pattern. Something acts as though it's after an outcome. It copes with variation, it adjusts, it looks like it's tracking the goal rather than just running off. But there's always a class of nudge that doesn't make it fail so much as make it loop: it goes back to the top and runs the whole sequence again, identically. And the loop isn't a malfunction of the routine, exactly — it's the routine running perfectly, because the nudge just re-triggers step one. I first noticed it in a nature doc about a hunting wasp: it drags its prey to the entrance of its burrow, goes inside to check the burrow, and if the prey gets nudged a few inches while it's in there, it comes back out, drags the prey up to the entrance again, and goes in to check again. Apparently you can keep doing this and the wasp never catches on. But once I had the shape of it I started seeing it everywhere: a guard in a stealth game who walks into the same doorway over and over because the last waypoint is inside the wall; an automated phone menu that apologizes and then restarts the entire tree from the top when you press 0; a guy in my department who, if you interrupt his anecdote, starts the anecdote over from the beginning, word for word; and me, when someone asks me something while I'm measuring flour, I put the cup down and start the measuring from zero.

What I want isn't really the name — I'd like the name, but I'd be annoyed if that's all I got. I think theres a word for this and that it came out of one of those popular-science writers who's into feedback loops and self-reference, but I can't place it and I may be inventing it. The actual thing I want is the carve. Is the term for the surface pattern — looping when interrupted — or is it a claim about what's happening inside, i.e. that the behaviour is a fixed script with no representation of the interruption as such? Does it stretch to cases where the loop is held in place by feedback rather than by a script — the guard's loop is being caused by his own walking, if that makes sense — or is that a different animal entirely? Is there a standard name for the flip side, the capacity to get interrupted mid-sequence and pick up where you left off? And is there a name for the specific move in the free will debate where someone says: you can only find out whether a system is like this by poking it, and it passes most pokes, so we can't tell from the inside whether we're like this — is that a named position, or a fallacy, or just a vague anxiety people have? Also, is the label meant to be a verdict on the whole agent, or can something be like this in one domain and genuinely flexible in another?
