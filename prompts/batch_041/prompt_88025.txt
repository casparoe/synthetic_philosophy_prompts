Two of my friends got into an argument at a barbecue last weekend and I've been turning it over all week. I honestly couldnt referee it, so I'm outsourcing the job to you.

Here's the setup. A mutual friend of ours, Rob, has for the third time in two years quit drinking, made it about five weeks, and then talked himself back into it with basically the same speech every time. Same excuses, same order, even the same little joke at the end. It's uncanny.

Priya said that's not a decision, that's a loop. She brought up the digger wasp -- you know, the one that drags a paralyzed cricket to its burrow, leaves the cricket at the door, goes inside to check the tunnel, and if you nudge the cricket an inch while it's in there, it comes back out, drags the cricket back to the door, and checks again. You can do that all afternoon; it never once just carries the cricket in. Priya's read: the wasp looks like its supervising a process, but there's nobody home, just a fixed routine that keeps restarting. And she says Rob's relapse speech has the same shape -- the words look like deliberation, but it's a subroutine that fires whenever the trigger shows up. Calling that a choice, she said, is just flattering the mechanism, and if it's a loop then the guilt is misplaced, like blaming a thermostat. She said there's even a word for this, sphexishness, and that we're probably all a little bit of it.

Tom pushed back hard. He said she's conflating two different things. The wasp fails because it can't register the thing that actually matters -- nudge the cricket and the routine doesn't even notice. That's what makes it a loop: not that it's caused, but that it's blind to the very feature the situation turns on. Rob, by contrast, is exquisitely sensitive to reasons -- that's what the speech is, a machine for manufacturing a reason. A routine that responds to reasons isn't the same as a routine that can't. He also said even the wasp would be a little less sphexish if it noticed the cricket, which just shows sphexishness is a matter of degree rather than a binary, and that's a feature of the concept, not a bug. And he said her version proves too much: if sensitivity to reasons doesn't count as freedom, nothing does, because sensitivity is all there ever was.

Priya came back with: a thermostat is sensitive in the thin sense, and if the answer is just "more sensitivity," you never get a line, you just get a dial. She said the only real difference would be that a person can revise the routine itself and not just run it -- and then she immediately talked herself out of it, because a routine that edits itself is still a routine, a loop that loops over loops. She said she genuinely can't tell whether "flexibility" names a real joint in nature or is just what we say when the routine is too tangled to predict.

Tom said the self-editing thing is the whole ballgame, and she's treating the answer as if it were a problem. A system that can feed its own outputs back in as inputs, and can ask whether the routine it's running is the one it wants to be running, is doing something categorically different from the wasp, even if you can always redescribe it in mechanism-talk. He admitted he can't say exactly where on the dial that difference kicks in.
