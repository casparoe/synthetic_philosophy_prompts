I am a secondary-school student preparing a short position paper for my philosophy club on the question of who is entitled to work and who is entitled to leisure, and before I present it I would like an audit of whether my own commitments actually fit together. Here they are.

(1) Work is more than a way of being paid. For most people it is one of the main places where a life gets its shape and where self-respect is earned, so a society that treats work only as a cost to be minimised has lost something important.

(2) A legal ceiling on the working week — the kind of thing the 35-hour week was supposed to be — is justified, and not merely permitted.

(3) When someone dies from overwork, in the cases people call karoshi, the employer is morally responsible even though the employee agreed to the hours, because that agreement was made under social and economic pressure the employer helped to create.

(4) Leisure is not simply recovery time for work. It has a value of its own, and a decent society protects it for its own sake.

(5) It is not morally deficient to live without paid work if you can support yourself, and nobody should be shamed for it.

(6) The state has no business ranking the ways people spend their time as worthwhile or worthless.

My own guess is that (2) and (6) cannot both be true, since a state that caps my hours is surely ranking my time for me. But I would rather be told where the real trouble is than have my guess confirmed.

Please sort the list: which pairs genuinely pull against each other, which pairs only appear to, and for each real conflict say how severe it is, what the cheapest repair would be, and what that repair costs me elsewhere in the list. Then tell me which single change would clean up the most.

Answer in no more than 700 words, leading with the most severe tension.
