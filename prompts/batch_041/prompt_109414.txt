I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on rational choice and I am drafting the final exam. I want a single essay question on the independence axiom, built around the two pairs of prospects below rather than the textbook Allais pair, which too many students arrive having memorized. The exam runs two hours and the answer should come to roughly 600 words; no reading or citation should be required of the students.

Pair 1: A pays $500 for certain. B pays $500 with probability 0.90, $3,000 with probability 0.09, and $0 with probability 0.01.

Pair 2: C pays $500 with probability 0.10 and $0 otherwise. D pays $3,000 with probability 0.09 and $0 otherwise.

Many people prefer A to B and also prefer D to C.

Please write the question, a model answer, and a marking scheme. The question must not be answerable by reciting that the pattern violates independence and is therefore irrational. It should force candidates to (i) reconstruct the argument that ties the two pairs together, (ii) say what the pattern does and does not show, in particular whether it amounts to a logical inconsistency, a violation of an axiom, or something else, and (iii) take a stand on whether the preference can be defended, and on what becomes of that defense when the same choices are made in sequence with an opportunity to commit in advance.

Build the marking scheme around five must-hit points totalling 20 marks, give a one-line note for each on how candidates typically lose marks there, and state plainly what separates an A answer from a B one. Answers that name a non-expected-utility theory without saying what it gives up should not earn the top band.
