OK so I've been going back and fourth on this for weeks and I'd like someone to just lay the principle out for me.

Here's what I think about trading one person's skin for other people's welfare, going by gut. Breaking a stranger's finger (no warning, no consent) to save a different stranger's life: fine. Giving a million people a bad headache for a day to save one stranger's life: also fine. Taking $100 from each of a million people to pay for a life-saving operation for one stranger: fine. Killing one innocent stranger to save five: not fine. Killing one to save a hundred: still not fine, and honestly I want to say not fine at any number. But killing one to save a million and my gut wobbles — I think I'd say fine, and I hate that, because it makes the number matter at exactly the point where I said it can't. Blinding one stranger to save a million people from a day-long headache: not fine, no question, even though it's a million people.

The rule I've been telling myself is what I call the one-way street: you can pile up lots of small burdens on lots of people to buy one big benefit for somebody, but you can never put a big burden on one person to buy benefits for any number of others. That gets the headaches, the $100, the five and the blinding right, I think. It doesn't obviously get the finger case, since that's one small burden on one person rather than lots of people, and it tells me to drop the save-a-million verdict, which I'm not sure I can do. It's hard to seperate my gut here from the rule, which is sort of the problem.

I take it this is a contractualist sort of question — not whether the world comes out better on net, but whether every person affected could reasonably accept the rule that lets it happen — and I want to know whether that framing helps or whether it just moves the bump under the rug.

So what's the principle? Is there a version of the one-way street that lets me aggregate the small stuff without letting the big stuff get aggregated away? Test it against my cases, tell me what it says about ones I haven't listed, and be honest about which of my verdicts it has to overrule.

Give it to me as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no summing up at the end.
