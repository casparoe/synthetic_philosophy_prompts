Compare two ways of cashing out the claim that a being is omnipotent. On the first, omnipotence is the power to bring about anything that is not logically impossible; since a stone too heavy for an omnipotent being to lift is not a specification of any possible object, the challenge never gets off the ground, and nothing the being subsequently does or suffers can subtract from the attribute. On the second, omnipotence is a maximal power that includes the power of self-restriction: such a being could genuinely render itself unable to do something — bind itself to a promise it can no longer break, or surrender a capacity for good — and would thereafter simply not be omnipotent, without this counting as a defect, since the loss would be the residue of an earlier exercise of power rather than a limit imposed from outside.

I want to know how far these two conceptions can be kept apart, and which of them does the better work. (i) Is the second conception coherent, or is the power to become non-omnipotent not a power at all — more like the power to cease to exist than like the power to lift a stone? (ii) If it is coherent, does it force omnipotence to be indexed to a time or to a phase of the being's career, so that what the being has is not a perfection possessed simpliciter but a status that can be spent? (iii) Suppose the self-limitation is meant to be irrevocable and is invoked to make a promise absolutely secure — a promise never again to bring about a certain kind of catastrophe. Does the first conception have any resources here beyond the being's goodness and truthfulness, and if its appeal to logical impossibility does no independent work in this case, does that show that the first conception is doing less than it appeared to? (iv) Conversely, if a self-defeating description specifies no task, why should self-limitation be the exception — or is self-limitation precisely the case where a perfectly coherent task and a genuine loss of power coincide?

Give me a verdict rather than a survey: say which conception you take to be the more defensible, and where the other one goes wrong.
