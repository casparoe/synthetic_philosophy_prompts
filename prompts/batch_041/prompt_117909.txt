I'm in a very slow-moving decision theory reading group — we spent most of last month on Newcomb, again — and last week we finally got to the absent-minded driver problem. You're driving home along a road with two exits; when you arrive at an exit you can't tell whether it's the first one or the second one; pulling off at the first is worth nothing, pulling off at the second is worth a lot, and sailing past both leaves you mildly annoyed. The answer is that the best plan is to keep driving most of the time and only occasionally pull off, which means that at the moment the driver is actually sitting at an exit, the plan is telling him to do something that isn't the best thing to do given where he is. Everyone in the group said "right, dynamic inconsistency," and moved on, and I didnt want to be the guy who asks what that's supposed to mean.
