The idea is that whenever you do something to try to make the world better, the effects ripple out forever, and after a few steps they depend on so many things you can't possibly know that you have no idea whether your good deed actually made things better or worse in the end. Like, giving money to a charity might change some kid's life in a great way, but it might also set off some chain of events nobody could of predicted that ends in a war in eighty years. If that's right, then how is "do what has the best consequences" even a usable rule?

3. Draw a line around the near future and the people close to you and say only those consequences really count.

6. Say the whole mess shows that judging acts by their consequences is broken as a way of deciding anything.
