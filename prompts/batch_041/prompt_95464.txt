My friend and I spent about two and a half hours arguing about the absent-minded driver problem last night and now I want to turn it into a proposal before the shape of it goes stale.

The setup, quickly, in case it isn't at your fingertips: you're driving down a road with two exits. Pull off at the first and you get 0. Pull off at the second and you get 4. Sail past both and you get 1. You're "absent-minded" — you can't tell the exits apart, and you can't remember whether you've already been through one, so at the moment you're deciding whether to pull off you don't know if this is the first exit or the second. The standard answer is to randomize: keep going with probability 2/3, which is worth 4/3 on average, better than always continuing (1) or always pulling off (0). Fine, whatever.

My friend thinks that's the whole thing — it's a puzzle about imperfect information, you optimize over policies instead of acts, the end, no philosophy left over. I think that's way to fast. My hunch is that the actual engine here is that "continue" at the first exit and "continue" at the second exit aren't two similar acts, they're *the same act* — one thing the driver does, turning up twice. And if that's right, then what he does now is evidence about what he does at the other node, sort of the way your choice in Newcomb's problem is evidence about what's already in the boxes. Which would make this a cleaner Newcomb case than Newcomb's problem, and a problem for any theory that evaluates acts one at a time. My friend says that's a category error and I'm dressing up a memory glitch as a deep fact about causation.

I need a two-page proposal out of this — it's for the little workshop fund my department runs, due on the 20th. So: state the question in a way that actually has a determinate answer (right now "is it about memory or about act-individuation" is mush, I know), say why the standard treatments leave it open, say what I'd actually do and in what order and why that way rather than just grinding through the formal decision theory, what a real result would look like versus what would count as the project failing, and be straight with me about whether this is a two-pager or a dissertation.

One thing I want baked into the design: a version where the driver is only *partly* absent-minded — he has a hazy read on where he is, say a signal that's right 60% of the time. My bet is that's the case that seperates the two stories, since the act-identity is still fully there but you can't write it off as a pure memory failure anymore. If my bet is dumb, say so and hand me a better test case.
