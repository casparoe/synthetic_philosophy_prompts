I took early retirement from a career in condensed-matter theory — four decades of statistical mechanics, Monte Carlo simulation, and a great deal of informal Bayesian data analysis — and for the last two years I have been reading philosophy of mathematics and epistemology in the evenings with a small reading group. One topic has me stuck, and I would like you to translate it for me rather than explain it to me. The topic goes under the heading of logical uncertainty and the associated project, sometimes called logical induction: the idea that an agent may hold graded degrees of belief about mathematical and logical claims — whether a long computation halts, whether a very large number is prime, whether a sentence follows from a given set of axioms — and that these degrees of belief are answerable to a coherence requirement roughly of this shape. First, no computable betting strategy, given access to the agent's prices, can be guaranteed to extract money from it. Second, the agent must eventually learn the patterns that are genuinely there to be learned — the digits of a constant, the truth values of statements in some simple formal system — while never contradicting what it can already prove. Third, it must not be dogmatic: it must not assign probability exactly zero or one to a sentence that the theory it reasons with can neither prove nor refute, even when that sentence is the consistency of that very theory.

Please restate this position as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use: priors and posteriors, likelihoods, exchangeability, parameter uncertainty versus model uncertainty, proper scoring rules, Kelly betting and no-arbitrage arguments, martingales, estimators and their consistency, computability and complexity, idealization versus approximation. Take the translation seriously enough to be systematic; do not simply reproduce the position with a few probability words sprinkled over it. Then give me the residue report, which is the part I care about most. Which components translate cleanly into my vocabulary? Which translate only with distortion, and precisely what gets bent in the process? And which have no counterpart at all? I want your view, in particular, on whether the residue marks a defect in my vocabulary — something I am missing — or instead a sign that the original claim is less substantive than it appears, or perhaps that it is really two claims, one of which is ordinary statistics in disguise and the other of which is genuinely new. Where the translation fails, say what the failure consists in rather than gesturing at holism or normativity. Keep the whole reply under 800 words and organize it as the translation followed by the residue report; no citations are needed, and please do not pad it with history.
