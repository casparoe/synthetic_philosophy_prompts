Two people in my reading group have been going at it for months and I'm tired of it. One's a pragmatic-encroachment person (practical stakes can change whether someone knows), the other's an epistemic contextualist (what 'knows' picks out shifts with the conversational standards in play). They both think the invariantist is wrong, and after that they agree on basically nothing -- and I suspect they're half argueing past each other.

Write the document they should've produced: a numbered list of claims both can actually sign (about the data any anti-invariantist view has to explain, about what each of their views is and isn't a rival to, about what would count as evidence against their own side), then a disagreements section where each disagreement is stated in wording its own side would put its name to, then the cruxes, and for each crux a case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down before the fact. Put these two cases in, or improve on them: (1) a hiker alone on a trail, no conversation, ambiguous map, drowning if she's wrong -- does she know the trail is fordable? (2) an idle trivia-level question about whether a shop opens on Sundays, asked over a drink vs. chewed over in a seminar where skeptical scenarios are on the table. If part of their fight is really about which question each view is answering rather than a live disagreement, say which part.

And be careful to keep what's settled apart from what's genuinely contested -- don't let anything that's actually up for grabs among people who work on this come out sounding like the consensus.
