ok so I have to write up my own view for a seminar handout on disagreement and higher-order evidence, and I want a stress test first, because I have a feeling the set doesnt hang together and I keep going in circles trying to find where. Heres what I think:

(i) Conciliationism about peer disagreement. If someone I have good reason to think is as well informed and as competent as me ends up on the other side of a question, I should move a long way toward them, usually all the way to suspending judgment.

(ii) Higher-order evidence is real evidence. Evidence about how I formed a belief, or about whether my faculties are working properly right now, can make it irrational to keep believing something even when the first-order evidence hasn't changed one bit. Say I find out I did the reasoning while on a drug that's known to make people confident and wrong on exactly this sort of problem. Then I should drop the belief, even though the proof on the page still looks airtight to me.

(iii) There's no such thing as rational epistemic akrasia. Believing p while also believing that believing p is unjustified or irresponsible can't be the rational state to be in — it's not that it's rare, it's that the two attitudes can't both be the ones my evidence licenses. I know some people say you can be a "level splitter" here, with each level responding correctly to its own evidence, but I don't buy it.

(iv) Evidentialism. What's justified for me depends only on the evidence I have — not on who I am, where the belief came from, or what's causally upstream of it.

(v) Permissivism. The evidence doesn't always determine one unique rational attitude; sometimes two people with exactly the same evidence can both be fully rational and disagree.

The bits that bug me: in the drug case, and in the case where I'm dead certain about a simple logic problem and a peer tells me the opposite, I want to say I should suspend, and I also want to say nobody can rationally be akratic. But in those cases the proof is right there in front of me and I can see it's valid, so I'm not sure which of the five is actually pulling the rug out. Someone also told me (ii) and (iv) can't both be true, which I really don't see — as far as I can tell H.O. evidence is just evidence.
