Write a sustained philosophical argument, in American English, rather than a survey of what various parties have said. Do not cite or refer to published literature; the argument should stand or fall on the considerations set out here.

Take the following as your starting point. For finite collections, three claims about how many members a collection has seem to hold together:

1. Correspondence: two collections have the same number of members just in case their members can be paired off one-to-one, with nothing left over on either side.
2. Part-whole: if one collection is a proper part of another — every member of the first is a member of the second, and the second has at least one member the first lacks — then the first has strictly fewer members than the second.
3. Determinacy: for any two collections, there is a fact of the matter about whether the first has more members than the second, fewer, or the same number.

For infinite collections these claims come apart. The natural numbers can be paired off one-to-one with the even numbers, with the multiples of 3, and with the squares, and each of those is a proper part of the natural numbers. The standard set-theoretic treatment keeps Correspondence and gives up Part-whole; it therefore treats the remark that there are half as many even numbers as natural numbers as, at best, an informal observation about density rather than a statement about how many members there are. Call this the correspondence package. A rival keeps Part-whole and gives up Correspondence, assigning sizes from a richer ordered scale than the cardinal numbers — sizes that can be added and compared, at least for collections of natural numbers, on which the even numbers are strictly fewer than the natural numbers, and the natural numbers are strictly more numerous than the multiples of 3.

Argue for this position: the correspondence package is not uniquely correct. The question how many members an infinite collection has admits no answer that is correct independently of which package we adopt, and the part-whole package is a fully legitimate way of making precise what we mean by how many members a collection has, not a confused or merely figurative alternative. Your argument must do all of the following.

(a) Explain what is wrong with the natural objection that Correspondence simply is what same number means for collections, so that the part-whole package changes the subject rather than offering a rival answer.

(b) Explain why the part-whole package's reliance on sizes drawn from a richer scale, and its likely inability to assign a size to every collection one might ask about, is an acceptable cost rather than a fatal defect.

(c) Give a verdict on Hilbert's hotel — the hotel with a room for each natural number, all of them occupied, which nonetheless takes in one more guest by moving the guest in room n to room n + 1 — and defend that verdict as a reductio of completed infinity, a harmless curiosity, or something else. Then consider a variant: a hotel with a room for each integer, in which every guest moves from room n to room 2n, so that every guest ends up in a room, no two guests share a room, and every odd-numbered room is empty. Say what the part-whole package must say about this, given that on that package the number of guests is unchanged while the number of occupied rooms has strictly decreased even though guests and occupied rooms remain pairable one-to-one, and say whether the case counts against your position or illustrates it.

(d) Address the objection that if how many members a collection has is answerable only relative to a package, then arithmetic is answerable only relative to a package as well, so that 2 + 2 = 4 becomes optional. Either locate the disanalogy or accept the consequence and defend it.

(e) Say what your position implies for a finitist who denies that there are any completed infinite totalities at all: whether your argument helps or hinders that view, and whether the choice between the two packages even arises on it.

End by naming the single strongest consideration against your position that your argument does not fully neutralize, and by saying how much it should worry a reader who begins sympathetic.

Roughly 1500 to 2000 words.
