I'm a second year student in a course on historical sociology and we just finished a unit on path dependence, and I'm honestly still confused because everyone in seminar means something different by history being locked in. Sometimes they mean the outcome was contingent at first but then became self-reinforcing, sometimes they mean it was inevitable from the start, and sometimes they just mean it happened earlier so it got to call the shots. Our lecturer has this distinction she calls hot path dependence versus cold path dependence: hot is when a mechanism actually generates increasing returns and makes reversal costly, cold is when we only see a path in hindsight and the same endpoint could have been reached by a different route. I want to make a study tier list out of this, so please rank the following mechanisms or accounts from most hot to most cold, i.e. from those that genuinely make an outcome hard to reverse to those that are mostly retrospective storytelling. For each one give me a tier (S through F), a one or two sentence reason, and then a separate number from 0 to 1 for how much it supports the stronger claim that the same outcome would have arrived anyway even if the initial accident had not happened, with 0 meaning it makes convergence seem like an illusion and 1 meaning it makes convergence seem almost guaranteed. Also flag any that are really just the same mechanism wearing a different hat, because I suspect a few of these overlap.

Here's my list, with the glosses I have so far: 1. increasing returns to adoption, where more users make a technology more valuable so an early lead compounds. 2. sunk cost entrenchment and switching costs, where retooling is expensive so people stay put. 3. critical juncture plus reactive sequence, where a founding choice triggers a chain of reactions that closes off alternatives. 4. institutional complementarity, where one institution makes others more valuable so changing one alone is costly. 5. path creation or institutional entrepreneurship, where actors actively build a path rather than just follow one. 6. technological momentum, where a large technical system gains mass from sunk capital, vested interests, and embedded skills. 7. gene culture coevolution and niche construction, where organisms modify their environments and thereby inherit modified selection pressures. 8. structural determination in the last instance, where deeper economic or social structures set the path and accidents are surface noise. 9. punctuated equilibrium with exogenous shocks, where long stasis is broken by sudden branching and lock in only holds between shocks. 10. retrospective narrative construction, where historians or theorists impose a path on what was actually a mess. 11. first mover advantage, where being early gives a durable edge even without strong increasing returns. 12. multiple equilibria selected by small historical accidents, where the same underlying system could settle in different stable states depending on tiny initial perturbations. I know some of these are from different fields, but thats kind of the point, I want to see where they actually differ and where they collapse into each other.
