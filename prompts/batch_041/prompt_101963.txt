For some months now I have been keeping a record of a colleague's judgments about confirmation. She is a philosopher of science of evident good sense who has nonetheless never committed herself to any worked-out theory, and my aim is to be able to predict what she will say about cases that neither of us has yet considered. What I have logged is the following. She holds that a black raven, encountered in the course of an ordinary survey of ravens, confirms the hypothesis that all ravens are black. She holds that a green apple, encountered while cataloguing fruit, also confirms that hypothesis, but, she insists, only to a vanishingly small degree. She holds that a white shoe, encountered while cataloguing shoes, likewise confirms it, again only infinitesimally, and she concedes this with visible reluctance. She holds that a black shoe, encountered while cataloguing shoes, does not confirm it at all. And she holds that a green apple confirms the hypothesis that all non-black things are non-ravens to exactly the same degree that it confirms the hypothesis that all ravens are black.

Next week I intend to put to her a case that I believe is new to her. A sealed bag has been certified to contain only black objects. From it she draws one object at random, and it proves to be a raven. Does this observation confirm the hypothesis that all ravens are black?

I want you to tell me what she will say, and, more to the point, why. Set out the competing rules of confirmation that fit her recorded verdicts equally well; state what each of them predicts about the bag case; give me your best guess as to which rule she is actually running, with your reasons for preferring it; and then name the further case — ideally one I have not yet put to her — that would most cleanly separate the surviving rules. I should add that I teach this material in a graduate seminar, and I would like to use the exchange to show my students how far a body of verdicts can be explained by more than one theory, and how much work it takes to tell those theories apart.
