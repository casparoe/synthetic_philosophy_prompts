I'm a fourth-year bioethics PhD and I do consults for the ethics service at my university hospital. A friend of mine is a medical policy analyst at a mid-size insurer, and her team is buried in prior-auth appeals because their current guideline for "is this treatment or enhancement?" is basically just "does the patient have a diagnosis code." She asked me to help her rewrite it. I said I'd ask someone smarter.

So: please build a decision procedure her reviewers can actually run. Context you need: the reviewers have zero philosophy training, they see 30-40 of these a month, they get about twenty minutes per case, and whatever they write has to survive an external appeal, so every step needs to be defensible in a sentence or two of plain English. She signs off on the guideline but the medical director can override individual cases.

Ground it in the real debate over the therapy/enhancement distiction -- the biostatistical / normal-functioning view and the welfarist objections to it, the disability-rights critique that "normal" is doing covert normative work -- but do not write me a lit review. I want a procede. I want it to run on these five axes, in this order:

A. Reference class / baseline: what population and what counterfactual fix "normal" here, and who gets to pick them?
B. Positional vs non-positional benefit (the Hirsch point): does the benefit consist mostly in rank relative to others, i.e. is it a zero-sum arms race, or in absolute function?
C. Reversibility and identity: is the change reversible, does it alter the person's self-narrative or a community's shared form of life, and does that actually change the verdict or just the vibes?
D. Third-party and distributive effects: does paying for this pull resources from a claimant who comes out stronger on A-C?
E. Consent and coercion: would declining impose a serious cost (job, social standing, access), i.e. is the choice free?

For each axis, say what counts as a sufficient answer, where a no stops the process cold, and which axes are value calls that have to be kicked to a human rather than settled by the procedure. Mark the escalation triggers explicitly. Then run it on two cases: growth hormone for a healthy 11-year-old at the 1st percentile with no endocrine abnormality, and modafinil for a shift worker with no sleep diagnosis. Then add a third case of your own choosing where the procedure returns a verdict I won't like -- pick something genuinely uncomfortable, say which step produced it, and don't soften it. Finish by telling me where the procedure will misfire and what it can't decide at all.

Format: the procedure itself as a table with columns Step number | Question to ask | What counts as a sufficient answer | Outcome (stop / continue / escalate) | Why this step earns its place. Then the three cases as a JSON array, each object with fields case_id, step_verdicts (array of {step, answer, outcome}), final_verdict (one of treatment, enhancement, undecidable), confidence (0-1), hard_judgment_calls (array of strings), who_must_sign_off, and predicted_failure_mode. American English throughout, and please don't hedge every step into mush -- I need actual stops.
