I work in programming languages — dependently typed proof assistants by day, self-hosting interpreters and quines as a hobby. Some years ago I read the strange-loop literature (Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach and its descendants) and came away with a picture I have never managed to state in my own vocabulary: a tangled hierarchy, in which levels that ought to be ordered instead fold back into one another, as in Escher's Drawing Hands, where each hand draws the other; and a level that is supposed to stand outside the tangle and keep it from collapsing — the inviolate level.

Please translate that picture into the vocabulary I actually use. Restate it in terms of universes and stratification; object language versus metalanguage; predicativity and impredicativity; fixed-point constructions and the recursion theorem; quines, self-hosting compilers, and bootstrapping; and the treatment of stratified negation in logic programming. Be faithful: do not silently read the strange-loop claims as claims about type theory, and do not import type-theoretic assumptions and then present them as part of the original. Where a distinction in my vocabulary has no analogue in the picture, or vice versa, keep them separate rather than merging them.

Then give me the residue report, which is what I am really after. Which parts of the picture translate cleanly; which translate only with distortion, and in which direction the distortion runs; and which have no expression in my vocabulary at all — including whatever the picture says about first-person experience or the sense of self, if that turns out to be inexpressible here.

Finally, adjudicate the disagreement the translation exposes. My vocabulary appears to predict that self-reference requires an inviolate level outside the system, and I do not believe it: I can write a quine in a language with no levels, and in untyped settings self-reference emerges from a fixed-point construction rather than from a hierarchy. So is the inviolate level doing real work, or is it an artifact of the vocabulary you translated into? If it is doing real work, say precisely what it buys — consistency, well-foundedness, the ability to state the rules of the tangle from somewhere — and what the level-free constructions fail to deliver. If the two frameworks genuinely disagree about whether the level is necessary, say which one you think is right and what the other is missing.
