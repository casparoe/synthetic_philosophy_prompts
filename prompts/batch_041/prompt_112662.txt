Two friends of mine got into this at poker night and I honestly can't referee it, so I'm outsourcing. Both of them are pretty sharp and neither is definately a crank, which is why its bugging me.

The setup they agree on: you're offered a fair coin at 2:1 (you stake, you win twice the stake on heads, you lose the stake on tails), you can stake any fraction of your roll, and you only get the offer once. They also agree that if you got that same offer over and over, the growth-maximizing stake is a quarter of the roll, and that staking more than a quarter is "overbetting" in a real sense even though every extra dollar is +EV.

Sam: the quarter is the answer, full stop, even in the one-shot case. Your roll isn't a number, it's you, and there's no second copy of you hanging around to collect the ensemble average. Expected wealth is a fiction dominated by paths where you got astronomically lucky; the time average is what you actually live through. So "but it's +EV" isn't a reply to anything.

Alex: a quarter is a fine rule if you're a log-utility maximizer, but that's a preference, not a theorem. Kelly is literally just expected utility with log wealth, and the long-run/median stuff is a restatement of that, not a proof of it. A risk-neutral agent facing a genuine one-shot positive-EV bet with bounded downside should shove, and you don't get to call that irrational by pointing at a limit that never arrives. And the time-average move doesn't escape it either — "the typical path" is just another thing you can care about, and you chose it.

Sam: fine, but then tell me what you're doing when you say bet half of the quarter because my edge estimate might be off. That's not log-utility-plus-noise, that's you admitting the right fraction depends on your beliefs about the model, which is exactly the expected-utility picture you were just mocking.

Alex: no, estimation error is a different thing — it's shrinking toward a prior because your input is bad, not changing your objective. But sure, notice that in one case you correct for it and in the other you treat the number as sacred, and tell me which is which.

Is Sam right that overbetting is a mistake even in a truly one-shot bet, or is that question-begging?
