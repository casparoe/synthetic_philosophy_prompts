I am assembling materials for a graduate seminar on probability in no-collapse quantum mechanics, and I need a satirical piece for the opening meeting — something that will make the room laugh while making clear that the joke is on a real difficulty rather than on a caricature of one.

Write roughly 800–900 words from an internal underwriting manual belonging to a fictional insurance company that operates in an Everettian universe: the wave function never collapses, every outcome of nonzero amplitude occurs in some branch, and branches are individuated by decoherence. The manual should read as entirely routine — numbered sections, cross-references to other sections, at least one footnote from the general counsel, one dissenting marginal note from an underwriter, and a short customer-facing paragraph at the end. No winking at the reader and no throat-clearing about how strange quantum mechanics is; the comedy should come from a bureaucracy applying ordinary actuarial practice to a world in which all outcomes happen.

The piece has to be right about the actual problem, which is not that probability is mysterious in general but that this theory seems to leave no room for it. Build the jokes on the following, and let them collide with one another:

- The manual prices policies by amplitude-squared weighting and presents this as obviously correct. A rival product line weights every branch equally, and its failure is traced to the fact that the number of branches changes when the office changes its claims categories — a single branch can be subdivided by describing more of the environment — and depends on how finely the decoherence basis is drawn.
- The manual justifies its weighting with an axiom about the policyholder's preferences, roughly that a rational policyholder's preferences satisfy ordinary ordering and sure-thing conditions, concluding that only squared-amplitude weighting is consistent with them. The dissenting marginal note should observe that this establishes what a rational policyholder prefers, not what the world does, and that the equal-weighting policyholder is being charged with irrationality rather than shown to be mistaken.
- A clause on self-location: a policyholder filing a claim after an event does not know which branch they are in, is instructed to assign a uniform prior across branch descriptions and update on their evidence, and is assured that this reproduces the weights the manual already uses. The general counsel's footnote should note that the uniform prior is doing all the work, and that the procedure presupposes a canonical description of branches, which decoherence supplies only approximately.
- A section on the measure of existence, in which thinly populated branches are said to be less real and claims are paid pro rata to reality, alongside an auditor's objection that paying in proportion to a branch's reality is paying in proportion to the number the office chose at the outset.
- A solvency section that quietly notices the company is certain to remain solvent and certain to fail, in different branches, so that expected loss cannot mean what it means at other firms.
- The frequency defense: across many claims the office invokes the fact that branches whose observed frequencies depart from the amplitude-squared weights have vanishing total weight, without noticing that this turns on the very measure in dispute.

Invent the officers, divisions, product names, and a regulator; do not name real physicists or cite real papers. The piece should stand on its own for a reader who has not read the literature while being recognizable to one who has. Close with the regulator's letter demanding the physical basis of the office's loss distribution and the office's reply, and let the last line sit on the unanswered question rather than snap shut with a punchline.

Then add a separate note of roughly 150 words, clearly not part of the manual: where the piece takes liberties with the physics or the philosophy, which of the objections it leans on are contested and in what general terms (no names or citations needed), and which one you would defend if pressed. Be committed throughout — make definite choices about which objection the piece treats as decisive rather than laying out the options and leaving the choice to me. American English.
