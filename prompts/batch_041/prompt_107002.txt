Okay, so I'm a novelist, and next month I've somehow agreed to give the speech at the launch of our town's local currency — a scrip thing, paper notes you can only spend in the shops on the high street, run out of the old Co-op hall, everyone from the chamber of commerce to my neighbour's kid is going to be there. Half the room will be people who actually know how money works (there's a retired central bank economist on the steering comittee and she is not convinced) and half will be shopkeepers and families who just want the high street to survive. I got into this because a friend handed me Berkeley's Querist, the Irish one from 1735, and I got a bit obsessed: it's all questions, and the money ones are roughly — money is a "ticket or counter" and not a treasure; the real source of wealth is labour and industry; money is only useful insofar as it "stirreth up industry"; less money circulating fast is worth as much as more money sitting still; and he was pushing paper credit and a national bank for Ireland. So I want the speech built on that, not decorated with it. When people walk out I want them to understand that the notes we're launching are tickets, that hoarding them is what kills them, and that the town's real wealth is the people who'll take them — and I don't want the sceptics feeling talked down to. What worries me: Berkeley's line that money's value is basically conventional slides very easily into "so it's all made up, print whatever you like", and there's a nastier thread in the Querist where he blames Irish poverty partly on the poor being too content in dirt and beggary, which I do not want smuggled into a launch night. Don't cite anything or quote anything at me, just write it.

Give me: "The speech" (700–800 words, no jargon, no philosophy-speak), then "The Berkeley machinery" (150–200 words — which bit of Berkeley is holding up which part of the speech, and where I've bent him), then "The trap" (100–150 words — the strongest objection the retired economist will make on the night, and how the speech survives it).
