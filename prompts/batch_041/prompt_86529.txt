I am assembling a graduate seminar on de re modality, and before we look at any particular account I want the adequacy conditions laid out. Write the desiderata: what any theory of how a particular individual figures in claims about how things might have been — what that individual could or could not have been, done, or undergone — must deliver in order to count as adequate. For each requirement, state the case or consideration that motivates it. Then separate the genuine requirements from the demands that are really commitments of one camp in the counterpart-theory-versus-transworld-identity dispute wearing the costume of neutral adequacy conditions. Flag any pair or subset of requirements you think cannot be jointly satisfied; a list with an impossibility result buried in it is a result in its own right, and it is the part I most want to see.

Build the list around cases, and make sure these are among them:

- an individual and the claim that they could have had different parents — and, more generally, whether an adequate account owes us a determinate verdict on which of an individual's properties are essential to them, or may leave that verdict relative to something else;
- abstract objects such as numbers and sets: whether a number has any counterpart other than itself, and what licenses a candidate-for-being-a-counterpart relation that collapses to identity for abstracta while remaining looser for people;
- an individual that exists in every possible world and one that exists in exactly one, and what each account must say about whether that individual could have failed to exist;
- a world containing two individuals that stand in equally good candidate-for-being-a-counterpart relations to me: whether the claim that I could have been one of those two comes out true, false, indeterminate, or ill-formed;
- cases in which the similarity that grounds the candidate-for-being-a-counterpart relation is non-symmetric or non-transitive, and whether the account must then surrender principles of modal logic (the S5 axioms among them) or may retain them by stipulation;
- individuals of kinds whose modal properties seem to turn on convention rather than intrinsic nature — artifacts, contracts, words;
- a person's de re attitudes, such as wanting, fearing, or regretting something concerning a particular individual, and what the theory must say about which individual such an attitude is about.

Say also whether an adequate account must treat cross-world and cross-time predication uniformly, with counterparts doing the work that temporal parts or stages do, or whether a principled disanalogy is available; and whether the account owes us a single canonical counterpart relation per individual or may let the relation shift with the respect of similarity in play.

Finally, in no more than a page, score counterpart theory and the transworld-identity picture against the list and name the single requirement that does the most to exclude candidates. Lead with the bottom line in your first sentence — the requirement that does the most work, and whether you take the whole list to be jointly satisfiable — then give the reasoning. No preamble, no restating of the question.
