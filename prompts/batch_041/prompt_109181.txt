I have worked out a position on my own and I want to know what would count against it. The position: suffering is the only thing that is bad in itself, and it outweighs everything good not merely in degree but in kind, so that no amount of happiness, love, or achievement could justify adding one more instance of serious agony to the world; the good things are at most the relief of a lack. I also think it makes no difference whether the person who suffered later judges that their life was worth living.

Tell me what should move me, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, rather than a survey of everything ever written against pessimism. In particular: which parts of the view are load-bearing? Does holding it commit me to welcoming the destruction of all life if that were the only way to end suffering, or can I refuse that consequence without quietly giving the position up? Does it predict anything about the world, or about how I should treat the people around me, that would show it false or unlivable? And is there anything its defenders, or I, could be forced to concede, or is it built so that every counterexample is absorbed as fresh evidence of how bad things are? If it is insulated in that way, say plainly whether that is a flaw here.

What would move me most is a case where I am made to admit that something good genuinely outweighed suffering, or a demonstration that the view demands of me something I already know I will not do.
