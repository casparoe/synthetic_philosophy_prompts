A friend and I have just spent a long evening arguing about whether moral status comes in degrees, and we ended up sketching a distinction on a napkin: sentience as a capacity for valenced states versus sentience as the occurrent integration of those states into a self-model, plus a further question whether moral status attaches to tokens, types, or whole lineages. We could not agree which of those distinctions does the real work, or whether the whole scalar picture is a mistake. Suppose you are advising a funder who can support five researchers for three years on the conceptual foundations of graded moral status, from insects to thermostats. Which open questions should they prioritize, and in what order? Defend the ranking: for each, why it is upstream of other work, how tractable it is for philosophers and theorists rather than empirical scientists, and whether settling it would change what we owe to a bee, a fish, or a thermostat. Name one question in this area you would deprioritize and say what would move it back up. Give me a defended agenda, not a survey, and no reading list. Answer in under 700 words.
