I'm in a little reading group that's working through the agent-foundations / AI-safety-theory stuff — we're all philosophy people, nobody in the group actually builds anything — and last week was the session on "embedded agency," which I thought I followed at the time. The idea, as I wrote it down in my notes, is that you shouldn't picture the agent as a box standing outside the world with the world laid out in front of it; the agent is one more thing inside the world, so it has to reason about a situation that includes itself and its own reasoning, its own code can be one of the options it's choosing between, and any line you draw between "agent" and "environment" is a decision you made rather than something you discovered, which then shows up as an artifact somewhere in the formalism. Then at the end somebody said "isn't this just the frame problem with new vocabulary," everybody nodded, and we moved on. It's been bugging me since, partly because I'm the one who has to write up the notes. I don't think they're the same thing at all and I can't articulate why.

So compare them for me. The frame problem as it's normally told: the agent has a big pile of beliefs about the world, and when it acts, almost nothing in the pile tells it which few of the millions of facts are relevant to what it's doing next, and there's no general procedure for picking them out — which is suppose to be bad news for the whole idea of one general-purpose reasoner. Does the embedded-agent business add anything to that, or is it the same complaint relocated? Specifically: what does the relevance problem look like once you grant that the agent is inside the world rather than outside it, and what does the embedded-agency picture lose if you just delete the relevance problem from it entirely? I have a hunch that a genuine solution to one of these would hand you the other for free, but I can't tell which way the dependence runs, so I'd like your verdict on that, plus a verdict on the "same problem" diagnosis — right, wrong, or true but useless. And what I'd most like is a case where they come apart cleanly, a system that has one problem and not the other, because I think that would settle it; my own attempts were bad (a thermostat, which I think has both, and a proof checker, which I think has neither, but I'm not confident about either). Please don't give me a survey of what other people have written about the two of them — I've read plenty of that — it's the comparison itself I want.
