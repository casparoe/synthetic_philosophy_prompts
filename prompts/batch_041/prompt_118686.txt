I am a philosophy PhD student writing a chapter on the wrong-kind-of-reasons problem, and I need a case built to order rather than yet another visit to the usual scenario in which a threat of harm gives one a reason to admire something worthless. My chapter argues that fitting-attitude accounts need an extra clause for directed attitudes — attitudes addressed to a particular target, whose fittingness seems to turn on something about that target rather than merely on the attitude's content. I want a thought experiment that isolates whether the standard two-level diagnosis (one consideration may bear on whether an attitude is fitting; another may bear only on whether having the attitude is good for the agent or others) can accommodate a reason that is wrong-kind for an attitude toward one target and right-kind for the same attitude type toward a different target, with everything else held fixed.

Please construct the case to these specifications. (i) The attitude must be one of gratitude, resentment, trust, forgiveness, or admiration of a person — not belief or desire. (ii) The two targets must both be ordinary individual human beings who are fully agents and equally responsible for their conduct; the asymmetry must not come from one target being a non-agent, a group, an institution, a fiction, or someone excused by ignorance or duress. (iii) The consideration must be an ordinary reason of the sort people actually weigh — social, emotional, prudential — and must not involve demons, threats of torture, pills, brainwashing, hypnosis, infinite rewards, or any device that causes the attitude directly. (iv) The consideration must not differ in its evidential bearing on the two targets: it must not be evidence that one target acted in a way the other did not. (v) The scenario itself is one paragraph, present tense, contemporary, in a single ordinary setting. (vi) Do not stipulate the asymmetry in the setup; it must fall out of the structure of the case.

Then do the demonstration. State the verdict that each of the following gives and why: the plain two-level fitting-attitude diagnosis; a target-sensitive view on which fittingness for a directed attitude requires the reason to bear on the target's agency or standing toward the agent; and the deflationary view that treats every such reason as a reason to want to have the attitude, so that the asymmetry dissolves. Identify the single feature of the case doing the work, and say what happens if that feature is removed — in particular, whether the case collapses back into the familiar shape. Finally, say how a defender of the plain diagnosis would attack the case, and whether that defence costs them anything they should care about. The case is for a chapter, so it fails if it stipulates its verdicts instead of generating them: I will check whether each verdict follows from the theory as stated.

Do not give me citations, a reading list, or attributions; I want the structure and the reasoning only. Take a definite position: tell me whether the case succeeds, which view it favours, and which side you would bet on, giving your reasons. If you think it fails, say so plainly and say where it breaks, rather than laying out the options and leaving the choice to me.
