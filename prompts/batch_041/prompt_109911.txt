I am a third-year undergraduate in a philosophy of mind course, and for the final project I need to build a working classification instrument for the four grades of mind we have been using all term: Darwinian creatures, whose competence is installed by selection across generations and is largely acted out rather than revised; Skinnerian creatures, which adjust what they do through reinforcement within a single lifetime; Popperian creatures, which can run a model of the world inside themselves and let the hypothesis die in their place; and Gregorian creatures, whose inner testing is scaffolded by culturally transmitted thinking tools and external artifacts, such as notation, written language, instruments, and other people's stored work.

What I want is a decision tree that sorts creatures rather than restating the ladder. Every node should be a question about a capacity — what the creature can do, what happens when it is wrong, what has to be in place for the capacity to work at all — and should be phrased so that someone can answer it without knowing which grade it leads to. No node may simply ask whether the creature has a brain, uses language, or is conscious; those are the labels in disguise. Where a capacity is distributed across a colony, an institution, or a person plus her notebook, the question must say explicitly which entity it is about.

Please then run at least twelve cases through the tree and report each verdict together with the node that decided it. Include: a bacterium doing chemotaxis; a sea slug whose gill-withdrawal reflex first sensitizes and later habituates; a slime mold finding a short path through a maze; a termite colony building a mound; a rat learning a maze route for food; a bird that caches food and recovers it months later; a crow bending a wire to reach a reward; a person doing long multiplication on paper; that same person doing it in her head after years of practice; a reinforcement-learning agent trained by a designer rather than by selection; a chess engine searching a game tree; a language model that produces a chain of reasoning before it answers; a vertebrate immune system; and a bureaucracy with institutional memory.

Finally, three structural questions I need answered rather than dodged. First, which pairs of grades can this tree not separate, and why — is the distinction unavailable in principle, or only under the cases I supplied? Second, which combinations of answers are empty, and in each case is nobody there because the view is incoherent, because it is unattractive, or merely because it has not been explored? Third, does the tree treat the grades as nested, with each presupposing the one below, or as exclusive — and if nested, at what point does a tool stop being external scaffolding and become part of the inner environment, as in the case of the internalized notation? To close, say which reading of the taxonomy my tree encodes, mechanism or functional grade, and identify at least two cases where the two readings give different verdicts.
