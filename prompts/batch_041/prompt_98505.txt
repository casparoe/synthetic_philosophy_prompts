Something's been niggling me about how we actually run organ donation, and I can't work out what the rules are even trying to say. Here's the pattern as I see it. In most places it's a crime to pay someone for a kidney — serious jail time — but its perfectly normal to pay for blood plasma, sperm, eggs, and hair, and to pay a healthy person good money to swallow an experimental drug in a phase-one trial that might put them in hospital. Reimbursing a donor's travel and lost wages is fine; handing them cash is not. Donating to a stranger is fine, and so is a paired swap, where two incompatible couples each give to the other's patient, and so are the long chains where one stranger's donation kicks off a cascade of transplants down the line. Some transplant systems even nudge you by bumping you up the waiting list if you'd signed up to donate yourself — you get a kidney sooner because you'd have given one. Meanwhile you can pay a stranger to carry your child in some jurisdictions, and the same body that can't be sold can be rented out for boxing, mining or stunt work. There's also a scheme someone floated at a hospital near me: donors can't take cash, but they can nominate a charity to receive a payment on their behalf. And a handful of countries just let you sell a kidney outright.

What I want to know is what view of the person and the body all this presupposes. Is the ban on selling a kidney really resting on the idea that body parts aren't property and can't be alienated at all — in which case the eggs, the plasma, the surrogacy and the paid drug trial all look like holes in the theory — or is it resting on something narrower, like the fear that cash would coerce the poor or crowd out the warm glow of giving? Those two stories make very different predictions about the charity-naming scheme and about the paid trial, so I'd like them pulled apart. Tell me which parts of the practice are genuinely presupposed by some theory and which merely happen to fit several, where the whole thing is philosophically underdetermined so that incompatible views would licence the same rules, and whether the pieces — the ban, the exchanges, the priority nudge, the expense refunds — are actually consistent with one another or quietly presuppose different pictures. And if you swapped the implicit view for the rival one, which specific rules would have to go?
