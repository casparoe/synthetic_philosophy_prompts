I run a small reading group on self-reference and paradox — mostly philosophy graduate students — and I am writing up a chapter section on regress arguments. Last session we were on Escher's Drawing Hands, the lithograph in which two hands appear to draw each other, and I offered the standard defusing move: the picture is not self-contained, since the hands are drawn on a plane the picture itself never depicts, so there is a level at which the drawing is made that the drawing does not reach. Someone answered, "It's turtles all the way down," meaning, I took it, that no such level needs to be posited at all. Someone else said the slogan merely restates the problem. I suspect the three of us meant three different things, and I want the differences made explicit.

Take the role of a philosopher who works on regress arguments and self-reference, and think carefully before you answer. Begin by brainstorming: enumerate the genuinely distinct precise theses that "it's turtles all the way down" could be expressing. I expect the readings to vary along at least these axes — what the "it" is that is supposedly turtles (causes, grounds, justifications, explanations, semantic values, levels of description, definitions); what "all the way down" asserts (an actually infinite descent, a descent with no bottom, a circle, a self-supporting loop, an open-ended schema with no terminus); and whether the sentence is offered as a description of how things are, as a reductio of the demand for a bottom, or as a refusal to answer the question. Some readings will be trivially true, some substantive and defensible, some false; say which is which, and what would have to be the case for each to hold.

Then, for each of the following uses, tell me which reading is actually doing the work and whether it supports the conclusion it was enlisted for: (a) the use in my group, to deny that Drawing Hands requires any level outside itself; (b) the use to deny that any chain of justification, grounding, or explanation needs a foundational term; (c) the use as an expression of resignation, that the question of what stops the regress is ill-formed or unanswerable. Flag the places where the readings get conflated, especially between an infinite descent and a circle, and between "there is no bottom" and "there is a bottom at every level" — and say whether the inviolate level, as it figures in the Drawing Hands case, is a term outside the hierarchy or merely a distinguished term within it.

Do not bother with citations or a bibliography; I know the literature well enough to attach names to positions myself, and I would rather have the distinctions sharp than the references complete. Finally, be candid about the limits of your answer: say which consideration would change your verdict on any of the readings, and identify the point in your reasoning where you are most likely to be wrong — in particular, whether any of the distinctions I have asked you to draw is one you are drawing by stipulation rather than by argument.
