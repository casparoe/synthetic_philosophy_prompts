**Two Loops, One Loop, or No Loop: A Deflationary Reading of Interactive Kinds**

The received picture runs roughly as follows. Some classifications of people are *interactive*: the label alters the people labelled, and their altered behaviour in turn alters the label, so that the kind and its classification stand in a feedback relation that indifferent kinds such as electrons do not. Psychiatric categories are the paradigm case, and this is supposed to underwrite a distinctive worry about psychiatric science — that its apparent regularities are partly artefacts of its own classifications, and that the categories are therefore self-validating in a way that the categories of chemistry are not.

I want to separate two things that this picture runs together. Call the first *constructive looping*: the label brings into existence a way of being that would not otherwise have existed, such that the classified come to experience, describe and organise themselves in the label's terms, and there is no pre-label kind that the label is tracking. Call the second *recursive looping*: the label alters the measured distribution of a kind that exists independently of it — much as publicising a diagnostic threshold changes how many people present at clinics without changing whether they are ill. Both are feedback; only constructive looping threatens the claim that a category tracks anything. Recursive looping is ordinary measurement feedback, with the same structure as a thermometer whose readings are used to adjust the room.

My claim is that most psychiatric looping is recursive rather than constructive. Recently formalised or still-candidate categories — prolonged grief disorder, internet-gaming disorder — pick out something people already did and felt before the category existed; what changes is the rate at which it is noticed, named and presented, not the existence of the phenomenon. The genuinely constructive cases look rarer, and mostly historical — certain dissociative presentations in the twentieth century, perhaps. If that is right, the anxiety about psychiatric categories being self-validating is largely misplaced.

But the distinction needs a counterfactual baseline: the kind as it would have been had the label never been introduced. That baseline is unavailable. Any assertion that a category is merely recursive is a claim about an unobservable counterfactual, and any assertion that it is constructive is the denial of one. So the distinction may be undecidable in exactly the cases that matter.

The deflationary moral: drop the question whether the label constructs the kind, and ask a causal question instead — what is the label doing in the process that produces the observed distribution, and can we intervene on it? On this reading the looping effect is real but loses its metaphysical sting; it becomes a topic in the methodology of a science whose instruments talk back.
