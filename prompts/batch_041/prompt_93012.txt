I'm a retired seismologist — spent 35 years on earthquake hazard and coastal risk — and since I stoped working I've been reading philosophy, mostly ethics, slowly and badly. Something from my old job keeps nagging at me and I think it's a philosophy question but I don't have the words for it.

When we costed out a flood defence or a retrofit programme, the analysts always discounted the future benefits. Not even at one rate — the schedule had the rate falling over time, so a benefit in 50 years got knocked down at something like 3% but one in 300 years at 1%, or whatever the numbers were that year. I understand the mechanics, roughly: the rate is a pure time preference term plus a term that depends on how fast incomes are growing and how much we say we dislike inequality. Fine. But two things bother me. First, the same parameter that says how much we should care about a poor person today versus a rich person today also says how much someone living in 2200 counts relative to me — and I've never once seen anyone defend that those two things should be welded into a single number. Second, the institutions doing this are the same ones whose charters say every citizen counts equally and who get rightly upset about discrimination by race or sex or place of birth — but apparently discrimination by date of birth is just, well, policy. And nobody discounts the welfare of people in Australia, only people in the future.

So what does the practice of discounting the future actually presuppose? Does it commit you to a view about the moral status of future people, or only to something about how to make trade-offs under growth and uncertainty? Which theories of value does it quietly rule out, and which rival theories would also license the same spreadsheet? Are the parts consistent — the pure time preference bit, the inequality bit, and the falling-rate schedule? And if I decided the presupposed view was wrong, what would actually have to change: would I just be arguing about the size of the number, or about something sitting underneath the number? I don't need citations or a reading list, I just want to understand what I've been staring at all these years.
