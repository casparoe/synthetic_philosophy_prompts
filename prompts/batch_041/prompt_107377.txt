Right, so me and my mate have been going round in circles about cause prioritisation for weeks and I just want to see it written down properly. She's a strict ITN person — score importance, tractability and neglectedness, multiply them together, rank the causes, fund the top one, move on. She's also got this line I can never quite knock down, that neglectedness is really just a cheap proxy for the marginal value of an extra pound or an extra hour, and if you could estimate that counterfactual impact directly you wouldn't need the proxy at all.

I think the framework needs a fourth factor bolted on. I've been calling it deference-resistance — roughly, how much a cause's ranking depends on contested philosophical assumptions you might just be wrong about (population ethics, whether insects are sentient, how heavily to weight the far future against the present). If a cause only tops the list because you've swallowed a controversial axiology, I reckon that's a fragility plain ITN simply can't see. She reckons that's either already smuggled into importance or not a seperate measurable thing in the first place.

Write the two of us talking it out, her defending plain ITN and me defending ITN-plus-deference-resistance. Neither of us should be a strawman — we're both reasonably sharp — and we should actually answer each other's points rather than talk past each other. Have us fight over a concrete case at some point, like a cause that only wins on ITN because you've accepted a strong longtermist axiology.

Format: exactly five exchanges, back and forth, a couple of sentences per speaker each time, no introduction and no summing-up at the end. British English.
