I'm a novelist, about two-thirds through a book, and I need help with one of the in-universe artifacts. One of my characters is an ex-grantmaker from the effective-altruism world who now can't stop running his own life through the same spreadsheet he used at work. In the novel he anonymously writes a tiny advice newsletter, and I want the actual text of one column — that's the deliverable.

So: write the column. Readers send in there ordinary life problems — should I have a second kid, should I take the job in Ohio, my dad's sick and I can't decide how much of my savings to spend keeping him comfortable — and he answers every one by putting the problem through a cause-prioritization framework. Please get the machinery right, because the humor has to come from accuracy: the importance / tractability / neglectedness breakdown, the funnel from a big cause area down to a specific intervention, the way neglectedness is really a proxy for how much your marginal contribution moves the needle (so a popular, urgent problem scores LOW on it no matter how awful it is), and the fact that the three factors get multiplied even though there obviously not independent. Let him be a consistent true believer — if the framework spits out an answer that's technically correct and morally appalling, let it, and don't have him suddenly notice and soften.

Deadpan the whole way, no winking, no "isn't this absurd" asides. Around 700 words, three or four letters with his replies. Somebody who's never heard of any of this should still get the joke; somebody who's read the real cause-prioritization literature should laugh because it's dead-on rather than a caricature. And don't have him cite any real people or papers — he's fictional and I don't want to put words in anyones mouth.
