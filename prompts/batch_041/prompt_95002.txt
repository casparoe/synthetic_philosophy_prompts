A friend of mine and I have been arguing for about two years about whether we're just fancy wasps, and I want to write up the thing we should have produced instead of another shouting match. The setup: there's a wasp that provisions its nest in a fixed order — drags a paralyzed cricket to the burrow mouth, goes inside to check the burrow, and if you nudge the cricket a few inches while its in there, it comes back out, drags it over, and checks again, all afternoon. My friend's view (call it the longer-loop view): thats us. Deliberation is a longer and more conditional loop, reasons-talk is the noise the loop makes, and nothing in you is doing the deciding in any deeper sense than the wasp is. My view (the different-kind view): the wasp can't notice its loop and we can — we can represent our own routine, ask whether to keep running it, and change what we're aiming at, and that isn't more of the same, it's a different kind of thing. Write the joint statement the two of us could both sign after a real adversarial collaboration. I want numbered agreed claims first — about what the wasp case does and doesn't show, about what flexibility would have to mean for either of us to be right, and about whether any of this even bears on moral responsibility — then the remaining disagreements in a table, each phrased the way that side would want it phrased. Then the cruxes, including whether the wasp story is even empirically accurate and whether representing your own loop gets you out of it or is just another loop. For each crux, give a case or experiment we'd both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. And if part of this fight is just us meaning different things by flexible, say which part and why.
