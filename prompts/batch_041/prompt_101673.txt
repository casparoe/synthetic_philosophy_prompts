My sister and I each took one of those direct-to-consumer ancestry-and-traits tests, and the report came back with a "genetic score" for educational attainment — a percentile, alongside a note that genes account for a substantial share of the differences between people in how much schooling they complete. Hers is high, mine is average, and our parents have already turned it into a running joke: she has the doctorate, I dropped out after a year, so the spit sample has spoken. I don't believe my life was written into my DNA, but I can't put my finger on what is actually wrong with the inference, and it has begun to matter to me in a concrete way, because I have two young children and I keep catching myself wondering whether their scores should change how hard I push them at school. The fine print also says the score was validated mostly on people of European ancestry, and my partner is not, which I suspect is important but cannot say how much. What does this number actually license me to conclude — about my sister and me, about my children, and about differences between groups of people?
