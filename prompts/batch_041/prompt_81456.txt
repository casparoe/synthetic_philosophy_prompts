I am preparing a handout for a cognitive science reading group on Dennett's four grades of creatures, and I want a classification instrument rather than a summary. The four grades, as I understand them, are: Darwinian creatures, whose competences are fixed by natural selection and who cannot learn within a lifetime; Skinnerian creatures, whose competences are shaped by reinforcement and trial-and-error learning; Popperian creatures, who can run a candidate action through an internal model and discard it before it is executed, so that their mistakes are discounted in advance; and Gregorian creatures, who augment their minds with culturally transmitted thinking tools, especially language, and whose competences depend on artifacts and practices that are not encoded in their genes. I do not need citations or a reading list. What I need is a decision tree or flowchart that takes a described behavior and sorts it by the lowest grade sufficient to explain it, while also indicating whether a higher grade is implicated.

The distinction that matters for my purpose is not which label is flattering but whether the grade is being used as a claim about the mechanism that generates the competence or about the history that installed it. Please build the tree so that every branch node is a question a reader could answer about a concrete case without already knowing which grade it leads to. Each leaf should carry the name of the grade and one sentence on what the case would have to be like for that grade to be the wrong diagnosis. Where two grades cannot be separated by any question in your tree, say so explicitly rather than forcing a leaf. Where the tree implies a combination of grades that is empty — for example, a creature that is Gregorian but not Popperian, or Skinnerian but not Darwinian — note the gap and briefly diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

The tree must handle at least the following cases, and I would like the cases themselves to be the branch nodes wherever possible: a spider whose web-building improves after repeated failures; a rat that learns to press a lever for food; a crow that drops stones into a tube of water to raise a floating reward; a human child who learns long multiplication with pencil and paper; a person who uses a mnemonic technique taught by someone else; a large language model that produces fluent text but does not update its weights from the conversation; a eusocial insect colony whose foraging decisions look intelligent at the colony level; a human expert whose skill has become so automatic that it no longer feels like simulation; and a person who solves a novel problem by talking aloud to themselves. For each case, the tree should say which grade is the lowest that could explain it, and which higher grade, if any, is also implicated.

The final page should include a short scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the four grades plus two mixed outcomes: scaffolded Popperian, for a Popperian creature whose simulations depend on Gregorian tools, and cached Skinnerian, for a Skinnerian creature whose reinforcement history has been offloaded into an external artifact. I want the instrument to be usable by someone who has read only the handout, so please define each grade in one sentence at the top without assuming prior familiarity with Dennett's vocabulary. One more constraint: the tree should not treat the four grades as a strict ladder in which every creature occupies exactly one rung. If a case requires two grades at once, the leaf should say so. And if the tree cannot distinguish a genuine internal simulation from a very fast conditioned response, flag that as a limitation rather than pretending the distinction is available.
