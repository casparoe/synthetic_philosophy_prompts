I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am applying for a twelve-month fellowship that would buy out part of my reporting time and support a substantial piece of conceptual work. I need a two-page proposal, and I would like it built around a half-formed idea of mine rather than around the standard debate.

The idea is this. In arguments about contested markets — organs, votes, disaster pricing, carbon offsets, paid surrogacy, paying a friend for help — both sides usually treat commodification as one thing. I think two things are being run together. Putting a price on something can merely commensurate it, placing it on a common scale with other goods and making it comparable; or it can substitute it, making it actually exchangeable for those goods, so that it can be had for a sum. A damages award commensurates a limb without making it tradeable; a functioning organ market substitutes. My suspicion is that repugnance tracks substitution rather than commensuration, which would explain why some priced goods provoke outrage and others do not, and why some of the outrage looks misplaced. I do not know whether the distinction survives the hard cases, or whether it collapses once you press on it.

Please write the proposal: a statement of the question sharp enough to have a determinate answer; why the existing positions — those who locate the wrong in the meaning a market expresses, those who locate it in consequences, and those who deny there is a wrong at all — leave this question open; the method, meaning what I would actually do month by month and why this approach rather than a survey of cases or an opinion piece; what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and a realistic scope for twelve months, including an honest assessment of where the project could become too large. Do not include citations or a reading list; I will supply those.
