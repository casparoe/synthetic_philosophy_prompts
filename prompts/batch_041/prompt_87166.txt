Last night I spent several hours arguing with a friend about the modal ontological argument, and I would like from you a credence rather than a further round of the argument itself.

The proposition I want priced is this: that the argument is sound — that its modal logic is the correct logic of metaphysical necessity, that each of its substantive premises is true, and hence that there exists a being which possesses every positive property essentially and of necessity. Treat 'positive' as the argument's own axioms treat it: a property is positive just in case its negation is not, positivity is inherited by whatever a property entails, and necessary existence is positive. Please regiment the proposition into something with a determinate truth value before you price it, and say briefly what would count as its being settled rather than merely widely accepted.

Then decompose the number. At a minimum I want your probabilities for: (i) that the modal system the argument requires — in effect S5 — is correct as a description of metaphysical modality rather than a convenient stipulation; (ii) that 'positive property' picks out a determinate and non-arbitrary class, given that the axioms constrain it only up to a choice we make; (iii) that a being with every positive property is possible, which is where the real weight of the argument seems to sit; (iv) that the collapse objection is fatal — my friend's version of it was that the premises entail that nothing whatsoever is contingent, which he regards as a reductio of the premises and I regard as a reductio of the argument; (v) that the standard repair to the essence axiom rescues the argument without either reintroducing the collapse or trivialising the conclusion; and (vi) that the form of argument is not too permissive to be probative, given that structurally similar arguments can be run for a necessary being with a very different, even repugnant, set of properties. Add whatever further terms you think belong, and show me how the terms combine.

Finish with three things: which term the final number is most sensitive to; which argument or consideration would move that term most, and by roughly how much; and where the disagreement most likely lives between me, who puts the soundness of the argument at about 0.05, and my friend, who puts it at about 0.4. I am not asking for a reading list or a survey of the literature; I want the numbers, the derivation, and the weak points marked.
