I've been trying to get straight on this "am I a random sample of all the people (or all the times) that could've been me" business, and I keep contradicting myself. Here's what I actually think, case by case. I don't know wich of these is supposed to map onto which of the two assumptions people keep mentioning, so just tell me.

Sleeping Beauty: coin flip, heads I wake once, tails I wake twice with my memory wiped in between. I'm a thirder, 1/3 on heads. Not budging on that one.

Same setup, except tails makes a thousand copies of me all awake at once in identical rooms, each with my memories, and heads makes just the one. I wake up in a room. I want to say 50/50. A coin is a coin; other mes existing somewhere else doesn't make it land differently. But I can feel that this is the exact move I refused to make in the Beauty case and I can't say why.

Somebody flips a coin in secret before any world exists. Heads: a hundred billion people get created. Tails: a hundred billion times a hundred billion get created. I'm one of them and I'm told nothing else. I say 50/50, maybe the tiniest nudge toward the big one. I'm definitely not going to say "tails, obviously, because there'd be so many more people" — that strikes me as nuts, and I'm told that's what one of the two assumptions actually says. Is this the presumptuous philosopher thing?

The one I can't shake: I'm told I'm roughly the hundred-billionth human to have lived. I do feel that nudges me a little toward us dying out soon, since if we last ages I'd be weirdly early. Just a little.

So is there one principle that gets all four, or do I have to give some up? My guess at my own rule is: count the ways I could be me at a later time, but don't count extra simultaneous copies or extra strangers, because those aren't me in the relevant sense. I suspect that's just gerrymandering. If it is, tell me which verdict to drop and what the repaired principle says. And tell me what it predicts for a case I left out: a coin decides whether the world contains one person (me) or two people (me plus one other) — and also for the version where there's no coin at all and the number of people just falls out of a physical constant.
