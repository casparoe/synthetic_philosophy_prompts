I'm a third-year philosophy student taking a seminar on the philosophy of mind and cognitive science, and for the last few weeks we've been working through teleosemantic attempts to naturalize mental content — the rough idea being that a state's content is fixed not by what it happens to be caused by or what it happens to cause on any given occasion, but by the biological function it was selected to perform. Class discussion kept stalling on two familiar headaches. First, misrepresentation: if a state's content just is its selected function, it looks as though it can never misfire, which is surely wrong. Second, artifacts: if natural selection is doing all the work, then nothing built by humans — a thermostat, a rover, a language model — can have content, which most of us find too quick to accept. To get us off the standard examples, our professor floated a variant she called the recruitment view, and asked us to take it seriously on its own terms. On this view, a state's content is fixed by the role it was *recruited* to play in the causal economy of the system that actually uses it, whether or not that role was ever favored by selection and whether or not the system is biological; she further stipulated that a recruitment can be a one-off event, a novel use that persists only for a single system's lifetime, rather than a population-level trend.

Please describe the most important implications of this recruitment view. I'm especially concerned with three things: what it does to the line between genuine representation and mere causal covariation in very simple systems (a bacterium tracking a nutrient gradient, a plant's stomatal response to light, a thermostat's bimetallic strip); what it implies about machines like language models, which are designed rather than evolved and whose internal states arguably get recruited to all sorts of uses; and whether it can still accommodate misrepresentation — and if it can, whether it does so at the cost of making content uncomfortably indeterminate or use-relative. Please be explicit about which implications follow from the view as she stated it and which would require further stipulations, since I have to write a short paper defending one of them and I don't want to overclaim.
