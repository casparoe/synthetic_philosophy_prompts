Thirty-five years of isotope geochemistry and then I retire and get interested in philosophy, which has been humbling in ways I didn't expect. Anyway, I'm in a small reading group and one of the members, Dana, is an actual decision theorist who hands down verdicts instantly and never revises them, so I've been keeping score. She says the classic fellow with intransitive preferences — A over B, B over C, C over A — who gets walked round the loop and pays a penny a lap is irrational. She says the woman who is perfectly consistent at any one moment but buys a lockbox for her credit card in January and then pays a locksmith forty dollars to open it in February is irrational too. She says paying a waiter to take the dessert menu away is fine; a man who would honestly rather keep his grandmother's chipped teapot than a new car is fine; and someone who takes a bet that was good at the time and loses is fine.

The case I want to put to her is Mira, a PhD student who lives downstairs. Mira's preferences run in a loop as well — three flats, A over B, B over C, C over A — and definately always have. But she has made herself an absolute rule: she never swaps one thing for another, ever, and never pays anyone a fee for anything. So nobody has ever pumped her, she's out nothing, and she says she's content. Is Dana going to call Mira irrational? Whats the rule Dana is actually running, what other rules fit her verdicts just as well, and what case would I have to bring her to make her pick between them?
