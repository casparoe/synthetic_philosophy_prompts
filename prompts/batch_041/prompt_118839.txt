ok so im in a seminar on divine foreknowledge this term and we've spent like three weeks on Molinism and honestly nobody in the room has moved an inch, including the professor. i dont want a verdict on who's right, i want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is, because im starting to suspect the two sides arent even answering the same question.

heres the shape of it, as concretely as i can put it. the Molinist side says God knows, prior to any decision of his own will, what each possible free creature would freely do in each set of circumstances — middle knowledge, counterfactuals of freedom. thats the whole engine: it lets God providentially arrange a world down to the details without determining anyones choices, so you get real freedom and real providence at once. fine.

the critics (i think the standard name for their move is the grounding objection, correct me if thats wrong) say: if those counterfactuals are true, then something has to make them true. it cant be God, because then the creatures choice would be settled by God and not free. it cant be the creature, because the creature doesnt exist yet and its freedom is exactly whats in question. so theyd have to be true without anything making them true — brute truths — and the critics say thats just not allowed, truths need truthmakers.

the Molinist replies that the truthmaker principle is a controversial metaphysical doctrine and not something you get to assume, and that a proposition being true about a free act doesnt determine the act anyway (the old sea battle thing). and then someone always brings up the fatalism worry: if it was true from eternity that i'd do X, how is my doing X still up to me? and the reply is that truth isnt causation, which satisfies half the room and infuriates the other half.

my hypothesis, and i want you to tell me if its right or if im missing the real fault line: i think the impasse might not be about God at all. the critic wants a worldly item that makes the proposition true; the Molinist is content with an explanatory stopping point and thinks demanding a further maker is just a demand for an infinite regress. thats a disagreement about when explanation may stop and about whether grounding is a worldly relation or a conceptual one — which is basically a live fight in secular metaphysics about grounding, just wearing a 16th century hat. my professor made an offhand remark that the whole thing turns on the ordering of Gods knowledge into natural, middle and free, and whether that ordering is something real in God or just a way we have to represent it, and i cant tell if thats a throwaway or the actual crux.

so: is this a substantive metaphysical disagreement, a methodological one about what counts as a good explanation, two groups answering different questions under one word, or a verbal dispute about ground? does it secretly reduce to whether libertarian freedom is coherent at all? and given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress here — a distinction, a formal result, a change of question — or is this one of those things where we should just stop expecting the debate to end? engage with what each side actually says, not with a survey of who said it.
