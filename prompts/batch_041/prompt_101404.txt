I teach a graduate seminar on methods in the science of consciousness, and I am writing the final exam. I want one essay question on heterophenomenology that cannot be answered by reciting the familiar summary of the method or by contrasting it with introspectionism and behaviorism. The question should force students to confront a tension I have been trying to make concrete in class with a case I invented: a subject who, after a focal brain injury, reliably reports that he has no visual experience of objects in one half of his field, yet can reach for and identify those objects when asked to guess. I call him the silent seer. The method says the theorist should record the subject's reports as data and neither endorse nor dispute their accuracy, but it also says the theorist may treat some reports as confabulation or as evidence of a failure of self-knowledge. A student last term claimed the method handles the case cleanly: the report is just another datum about what the subject believes, and the reaching behavior is explained separately. I think that answer misses the real difficulty, which is to say what principled constraint keeps heterophenomenology from becoming a license to override first-person reports whenever they conflict with third-person findings, and whether that constraint is methodological or metaphysical. I want the exam question to make students state exactly where the method's neutrality ends, what the theorist is entitled to do with a report that is sincere, stable, and systematically at odds with behavior, and whether the resulting position can still count as a science of consciousness rather than a science of reports.

Please write the exam question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme worth 25 points, broken into must-hit moves, with a one-line note on the most common way students lose points on each move. The exam is closed-book and ninety minutes; the students are philosophy PhD candidates who know Dennett's method but have little clinical background, so the question should be demanding yet answerable from the concepts I have described here. Write in American English.
