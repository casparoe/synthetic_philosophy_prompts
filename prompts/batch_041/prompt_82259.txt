ok so I'm writing up my take on On Certainty for a seminar and I want a consistancy check before I commit to it in writing. here's where I've landed:

1. Moore is right that his claim to know he has two hands is a genuine piece of knowledge, not just some grammatical move or a refusal to play the game.
2. Wittgenstein is also right that there are hinge propositions — e.g. that the earth existed long before I was born — which we hold fast, which are exempt from doubt, and which we can't be said to know.
3. Knowledge requires being able to give some justification when you're challenged; if nothing at all can be said in support, then claiming to know is out of place.
4. Inquiry is a social and historical practice, so what counts as a hinge is what some community holds fast, and that shifts over time (physicists treating the light-speed limit as beyond question, a legal culture treating a procedural presumption as untouchable).
5. When a hinge collides with a solid empirical result, the hinge is what should give — the finding revises the hinge, not the other way round.

I suspect at least one of these has to go but I honestly can't see which. do they actually hang together? be specific about which pairs pull against each other, which ones only look like they conflict, and rank the genuine tensions by how much they cost me. then tell me which single one I should give up.
