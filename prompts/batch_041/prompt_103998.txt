A colleague insists that imprecise credences are never fundamental: any agent whose evidence leaves a range of admissible probability functions is, on his view, really a sharp agent with a single probability function over an enriched space — say, a probability over which member of the range is the correct one. My credence that this reduction is correct is about 0.2. I want your number, but mostly the decomposition. Fix the case sharply: the agent's evidence constrains a probability function only by finitely many inequalities, so the admissible set S is a continuum with no member singled out; the agent's sole doxastic commitment is that S contains exactly the credences its evidence does not rule out. Give me your credence that this agent's state is fully captured — behaviorally and justificatorily — by a single sharp probability function over an enriched space, decomposed into at least these terms, each with a rough probability and one line of justification: (a) the probability that some sharp distribution over S can reproduce the agent's betting behavior, given that the agent need not bet as any single member of S would; (b) the probability that the reduction can reproduce the agent's epistemic justification rather than relocating the imprecision one level up, since it is unclear what justifies any particular distribution over S; (c) the probability that the reduction preserves the intuitive difference between ambiguous and risky prospects, or else explains why that difference is illusory; and (d) any further term you think belongs. Then combine the terms, state the resulting number, and tell me which term my 0.2 is most sensitive to and what argument or piece of evidence would move it most.
