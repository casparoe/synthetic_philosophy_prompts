I did a biology degree and then drifted into philosophy, and there's a pattern in how people explain living things that I dont have a word for. We say the flower is red in order to attract pollinators, the bird calls in order to warn its kin, the fever is there to cook the pathogen -- and these explanations can be right or wrong, can be argued over, can be improved. But there's no one whose reason it is: the flower wants nothing, the bird isn't doing fitness math, the immune system isn't trying. And it isn't just a dressed-up way of saying that's how it happens to be, because if the pollinator story is false the explanation is false, and a metaphor can't be false that way. I've also seen the same move outside biology -- a market price 'signals' scarcity, a sorting routine 'wants' the list in order -- and I can't tell if that's the same thing or a looser cousin. It gets stranger when the reason I want to cite is one that couldn't have been selected for, since the trait is doing something useful now that had nothing to do with why it spread. Is there an established name for a reason that's no one's reason? But I want the joints, not the label: how does this differ from flatly saying the thing merely behaves as if it were aiming at something, from a designer's intention, and from saying the thing has a function? When is attributing such a reason legitimate, and what makes one attribution better than a rival one? Does it carry over to the market and algorithm cases or break somewhere along the way? And if there's no name for exactly this, tell me that and draw the boundaries yourself rather than forcing it onto the nearest term.
