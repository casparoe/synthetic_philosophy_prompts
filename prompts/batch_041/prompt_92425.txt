Give an example of a conceptual issue that arises in the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, which infers from the relativity of simultaneity that all events — past, present, and future — are equally real. Exhibit three events, A, B, and C, such that A and B are simultaneous in one inertial frame, B and C are simultaneous in another, and A and C are timelike related, so that no inertial frame makes A and C simultaneous; give explicit coordinates for the events in each of the two frames. Then explain what the example shows about the step from the reality of A and of B, together with the simultaneity of B and C, to the reality of C, and whether the construction can be iterated so as to make arbitrarily late events real. State how confident you are in your claims and single out the point you are least sure of.
