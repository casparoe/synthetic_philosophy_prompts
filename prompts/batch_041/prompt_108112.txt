Give an example of a conceptual issue that arises when pain asymbolia is used to pry apart the sensory dimension of pain from its badness. Construct a concrete case—clinical or fictional—in which a subject can localize and discriminate an intense nociceptive sensation and has intact nocifensive reflexes, yet reports no aversion to the sensation and no desire for it to stop. Then explain what this case does to three views: that pain is bad in virtue of its intrinsic phenomenal character; that pain is bad only because it is unwanted or disliked; and that pain is bad because it signals bodily damage. The example should isolate exactly which feature is removed, why that removal is conceptually coherent rather than merely verbal, and what residual question about the badness of pain remains once the case is described. Do not rely on empirical citations; the point is the conceptual structure of the example.
