Ok so I have to bring a position sheet to my free will seminar next week and I'd rather find the cracks now than in the room, so heres roughly where I've landed:

1. Compatibilism. Determinism (or indeterminism, for that matter) has nothing to do with whether an action is free. The only free will worth wanting is the kind that survives a deterministic universe, and if that's all there is then fine — the libertarian kind is either incoherent or not something I'd actually want anyway.

2. What makes an action mine in the responsibility-relevant sense is that it issues from a mechanism that's responsive to reasons and that represents my values. Nothing bypasses me, the mechanism is me. I don't think the causal history of how I came to have that mechanism matters for whether the action is free.

3. Test case I keep using: S was raised from birth in a closed community that shaped her values down to the ground. She's fully reasons-responsive, nothing is bypassed, she'd endorse her values on reflection, and she can revise them in light of argument — she just never encounters an argument she finds compelling. I think S is responsible for what she does.

4. Same case, one change: a neurosurgeon implanted those exact values in her last Tuesday, everything downstream identical. I don't think she's responsible for what she does after. So something about the history does matter to me, apparently.

5. The unwilling addict — hates the drug, takes it anyway, wishes she didn't — is responsible for using. She's not free in the flowery sense but she's answerable for it.

6. Resentment, gratitude, forgiveness, all of it: those are the primary data. Strawson-ish, I guess. The metaphysics of free will is downstream of the fact that we're already in the participant stance with each other and can't climb out of it to audit it from nowhere.

7. Free will comes in degrees — more or less of it, thinning out with addiction, coercion, immaturity — but responsibility is threshold-y: below some line you aren't answerable at all, above it you're in.

So: where's the friction? I want the audit, not the tour. Which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they conflict, and for the real ones what has to give and what it costs. Rank them by severity rather than handing me a list where everything is equally bad, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. If I've set this up badly — say I'm using "responsibility" in two different senses across the items and that's doing all the work — tell me that instead of smoothing it over. I'd rather be told the list is confused than get a tidy answer that pretends it isn't.
