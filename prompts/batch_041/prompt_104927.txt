So there's a move I keep noticing in arguments and I finally want to ask somebody about it.

Here's the move. Somebody explains why something happened by pointing at a big-picture feature of the situation. Then somebody else says: look, you can tell the whole story one level down, in terms of the small stuff, and the small stuff already accounts for everything that happened. So the big-picture feature can't be doing any of the work, its just along for the ride. And the funny thing is that it works the same way no matter what the two levels are:

- My friend says his depression is why he couldn't get out of bed for a month. His brother says no, the brain chemistry did that, "depression" is just what we call the pattern, so it isn't the thing causing anything.
- In a seminar about hiring algorithms, a guy said the model isn't what discriminated against women, the individual weights are, and the model is just a name for the weights.
- My sister, who's a nurse, got told she shouldn't feel bad about following a bad protocol, because the protocol didn't make her do anything, the specific written instructions did.

So: does this have a name? Someone told me it's epiphenomenalism, but that can't be right, because the people making the move usually insist the big-picture thing is perfectly real, they just say it isn't a cause. Someone else said it's the problem of mental causation, but two of my three cases have nothing to do with minds. And I don't think it's plain reductionism either, for the same reason.

What I actually want is the carving, not a label. Is this one argument, or three arguments that happen to look alike? In the depression case the two levels look like two ways of describing the same thing; in the protocol case the instructions are literally parts of the protocol, which feels like a different shape. Does the move only go through if you add a premise that every event has exactly one cause, or is that smuggled in? And is it even valid — because it seems like you could run it against the higher level every single time and never lose, which makes me suspicious. If there's no established name for the general thing, coin one, but tell me exactly where its edges are: which cases it covers and which it doesnt.
