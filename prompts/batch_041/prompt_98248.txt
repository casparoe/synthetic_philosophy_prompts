I am assembling materials for an adult-education unit on comparative civilizations, and before I commit to a framing I want a ranking I can defend rather than the usual litany. Below is a list of candidate items that have at various times been offered as "Western values." Please sort them into the following four tiers, and give one or two sentences of justification for each placement:

- Tier 1: load-bearing — remove it and the category of "Western values" stops meaning what it means.
- Tier 2: genuinely part of the tradition, but not definitional.
- Tier 3: associated with the tradition by history or connotation, yet derivative or contested.
- Tier 4: not distinctively Western at all — either a human universal or an artifact of modernity that gets mislabeled.

Where an item seems to you a human universal rather than anything specifically Western, say so plainly and place it in Tier 4; do not treat "Western" as a term of praise and do not inflate a placement to be generous. Conversely, if you think an item is distinctively Western but morally unattractive, that should not lower its tier.

The items:

1. The presumption that a claim must survive adversarial challenge before it is credited — the norm underlying the courtroom, the referee report, and the dissertation defense.
2. The idea that boredom is a defect in the world to be cured rather than a condition to be endured.
3. The practice of double-entry bookkeeping, in which every credit must be matched by a debit.
4. The expectation that marriage should be founded on romantic love between the two parties themselves.
5. The distinction between the descriptive and the normative — that an "is" and an "ought" are different kinds of claim.
6. The conviction that moral progress is real and has a direction.
7. The ideal of the self-made individual who owes their standing to effort rather than to birth.
8. The norm of charitable interpretation — reading an opponent's position in its strongest form before attacking it.
9. The separation of religious from political authority.
10. The notion of a career as a central component of personal identity.
11. Privacy valued as a good in itself rather than merely as a means to something else.
12. The expectation that one's own inherited customs are one option among several and therefore open to argument.

After the tier list, give me a single number between 0 and 1 for how confident you are that "Western values" names a coherent category at all, and one sentence on what that number is tracking. Then state which two items in the list you found hardest to place and why.
