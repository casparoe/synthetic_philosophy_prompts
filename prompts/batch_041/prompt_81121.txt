My reading group has two people who've been going at each other about the centipede game for about three months now and it's become a running joke. One of them is basically "the backward induction is correct, deal with it"; the other thinks the whole argument is broken and keeps saying stuff like "you can't reason about what I'd do at a node we'd never reach" and "common knowledge of rationality is a self-destructing assumption." Both are smart, neither will move, and I'd rather hand them a document than listen to round 47.

Here's the tree I keep putting on the whiteboard. Four decision nodes, P1 moves at 1 and 3, P2 at 2 and 4, payoffs written (P1, P2):

- P1 takes at node 1 → (3, 1), game over
- P1 passes, P2 takes at node 2 → (2, 4)
- both pass, P1 takes at node 3 → (5, 3)
- all pass, P2 takes at node 4 → (4, 6)
- everyone passes at node 4 → (7, 5)

So the pot grows on every pass, the taker always grabs the bigger share, and yet if they both keep passing they both do better than the node-1 take. Standard setup.

Draft the joint statement these two could actually both sign. Sections, in this order:

1. **Stuff we both accept** — 8 to 10 bullets. Include what the backward induction argument actually is when stated charitably, and what it does and doesn't claim.
2. **Our disagreement, in our own words** — two short paragraphs, one per side, each worded so that side could sign it without wincing. No strawmen.
3. **Cruxes** — 3 or 4. For each one: the sub-question, the concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and what each side predicts about that case. One of them should be the variant where I flip the last node so the last mover prefers passing instead of taking — I want to know whether the whole paradox hangs on that one preference or not.
4. **What would actually move us** — a few lines each. If the honest answer is nothing, say that.
5. **Where we were just talking past each other** — one short paragraph, if any of the fight turns out to be verbal.

Keep it tight, no literature review, don't cite papers, just the substance.
