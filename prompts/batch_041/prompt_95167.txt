I share a flat with two friends, and one of them — Priya — does all the grocery shopping because she's the only one with a car. Two months ago she quietly stopped buying beer for the flat and started buying the alcohol-free version of the same brand instead; same bottles, same spot on the fridge shelf. She insists she isn't stopping me: I can buy whatever I like with my own money, and if I ask, she'll put real beer on the list. When she first floated it I was half asleep and said "yeah sure, do whatever", which she now treats as me having agreed to the whole arrangement. I do drink less and I sleep better, so I can't really claim its hurting me. What gets me is that having a beer now means a detour to the shop past the roundabout, and that her interest isn't only in my liver — she's said she hates being around me after I've had a few, and when I asked her straight out whether she'd keep doing it if it made me miserable, she said yes but that she'd feel bad about it. She also keeps a tally on her phone and will show me the numbers if I ask, which somehow makes it worse.

Our other flatmate says I should just say thanks. My brother says it's creepy that someone else is arranging my life and I didn't even notice. I'm not after a lecture about willpower, and please don't tell me to have a conversation with her — I can do that myself. I want to know whether Priya has actually overstepped, and I'd like you to take seriously three things that seem to point in different directions: that I did sort of agree once, that opting out is easy in principle but costs me a trip every single time, and that a decent chunk of the benefit lands on her rather than on me. Also tell me whether your answer changes if the same thing is done to me by a supermarket app or by a government rather than by someone I live with, or whether the flat is just a small version of the same problem.

Give me your verdict in the first sentence, then the reasoning, and don't waste space restating what I've told you.
