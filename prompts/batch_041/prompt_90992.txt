I recently became convenor of a fortnightly discussion group in my department, and it has dawned on me that it is what I have started calling a closed loop: a group in which any challenge to the prevailing consensus can be raised only through the very members whose consensus is in question, so that dissent arrives pre-framed as incompetence or bad faith rather than as information. I have also come across the distinction between epistemic bubbles, in which relevant information and outside testimony are simply missing, and echo chambers, in which outside voices have been actively discredited, and I suspect my group is closer to the second than I would like. Please set out the most important implications of these ideas for how I should run the group from here. In particular, does the remedy lie in exposing members to more contrary evidence, or in repairing their standing trust in outsiders? Would appointing an outside referee for contested matters dissolve the loop or merely relocate it? And what follows for my own position, given that the loop may run through my authority as convenor?
