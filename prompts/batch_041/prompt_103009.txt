Me and a couple friends have been running a reading group on Wittgenstein's On Certainty for about a year and we're completely stuck on basically one thing: what are the "hinges" actually? One camp (the framework reading) says the whole point is that things like "I have two hands" or "the world has existed for more than five minutes" aren't propositions at all -- they're non-epistemic, animal certainties, not true or false, and asking whether we know them is a category mistake. The other camp thinks that's overblown: hinges are propositions, we're just entitled to accept them without evidence, so they can be true and might even count as a special kind of knowledge. A year in, nobody's budged, and I've started to suspect the fight isn't about On Certainty at all. My hypothesis is that it's two things tangled together: partly a verbal dispute about how to use the words "proposition" and "know", and partly two camps answering different questions under one heading -- one asking what certainty is like (a descriptive/psychological question) and one asking what we're rationally permitted to do (a normative one). There's also this pattern where each side keeps reaching for different example hinges -- mathematical ones for one camp, perceptual ones for the other -- and I can't tell if that's driving the whole thing. Diagnose what kind of disagreement this really is, using what each side actually says, and tell me what if anything would count as progress. Keep it under 350 words.
