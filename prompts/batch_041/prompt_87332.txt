I run model-welfare triage at an RL lab: before any training run that uses a large negative reward, a punishment-shaped penalty, or an explicit internal "distress" head goes to compute, my office has to clear it, we do about a dozen a month, and the clearance is counter-signed by the safety lead rather than by me. What I need is a procedure I can actually run in under an hour — the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it terminates in a clear no, and where it has to hand the decision to a person because the question is evaluative rather than empirical. Take the following as given, since it is what our internal review settled on and I don't want it re-litigated in the write-up: a scalar used for credit assignment is not yet valence; for a signal to count as valenced in the sense we care about, it should be globally broadcast rather than local to one module, capable of shaping future behaviour non-instrumentally (the system avoids it even at a net cost in other reward), represented in the system's own model of its states as something to be avoided for its own sake, and not fully re-describable as an ordinary preference ordering. Assume also that self-report is worthless as evidence on its own, because we train these systems on human approval, and that a system can represent a state as evaluatively loaded without anything being felt.

Then run it on three proposals: a simulated robot arm with a steep penalty for joint-limit violations; a long-horizon agent whose penalty is broadcast to every module and which has a learned self-model predicting its own future reward; and a run I am confident is trivially safe, a small gridworld agent that learns to avoid a "shock" tile, which I expect to sail through. If it clears the third, fine. If it doesn't, that is a result I want to see rather than have smoothed over, and I want the write-up to say what the criteria cannot distinguish, where the false-positive and false-negative costs fall asymmetrically, and which step is a value judgement dressed as a test. And if the framing itself is confused — if "reward signal as valence" is quietly assuming that functional role settles the question of experience, or if my four criteria are just a restatement of the thing to be decided — say so plainly rather than handing me a procedure that pretends to settle it.
