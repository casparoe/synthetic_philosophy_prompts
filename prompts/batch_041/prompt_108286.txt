I'm putting together a "which view do you actually hold?" quiz for the first meeting of a reading group on virtual worlds — specifically whether virtual objects and virtual harms are real. It's going up on a blog so people can fill it in at home, which means it has to stand on its own: no citations, no reading list, no names of philosophers, and no jargon that would need a footnote. But I don't want it dumbed down either. I want the cases in the weeds enough that you can't get through it by reciting the standard positions.

Format: 9 questions, each one a concrete case, each with exactly three options (A/B/C). Every option should be something a real person could actually say out loud about that case, in the first person, and none of the options should be the obviously sensible one. And no question may just ask "do you think virtual objects are real?" — the whole point is to sort people by what they say about cases, not by which label they like.

Two things I want baked in, both of which I've never seen anyone state cleanly, and which are kinda the point of the whole quiz:

- Ontology and ethics come apart. Everyone assumes that if virtual objects aren't real then virtual harms can't be either, and vice versa. I want at least one outcome that pairs "the sword is a fiction" with "the griefing was a real harm", and at least one that pairs "the sword is a real thing" with "the griefing wasn't a harm at all".
- The reload test. Imagine the state gets rolled back and nobody keeps any memory of the episode. Then ask what, if anything, was lost. Use this in at least three of the questions — it seperates the people who think harm lives in mental states from the ones who think it lives in violated norms or in the object itself.

Cases I want in there for sure: a duplication bug that spawns a second copy of a one-of-a-kind item; unwanted touching in a social VR room; a player's account being stolen; a platform deleting a memorial someone built for a dead friend; an NPC that begs you not to kill it; a player who loses an item in a duel they agreed to; and a world shutting down that people had paid real money into. Fill the rest yourself, but pick cases where two plausible views give different verdicts.

Must appear somewhere in the outcomes: the view that virtual objects are real non-material objects; the view that they're fictions; the view that they're real only as social/institutional facts (they exist because we enforce ownership); the eliminativist view that there's only the hardware and "the sword" is a handy label; the view that virtual harms are real whenever they produce real mental states; the view that a harm is real only under the description the participant accepts; and a deflationary view that says "real" is doing no work here and we should be asking what the words license instead. If any pair of those can't be seperated by any answer pattern, say so instead of fudging it. Also flag any answer combination that no real position occupies, and say whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.
