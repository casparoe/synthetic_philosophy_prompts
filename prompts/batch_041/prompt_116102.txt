I'm a stats grad student and I just got voluntold to help our university's admissions office with a communications problem. They released numbers showing we admitted a lower percentage of women than men overall, but a higher percentage of women than men in almost every department. Now the dean's office says this is just Simpson's paradox, the overall rate is meaningless, and they want to stop publishing it and release only the departmental breakdowns. I need a satirical FAQ for the student paper, written in the voice of the dean's office, deadpan, around 500-600 words, announcing the new policy. The humor should come from getting the paradox right -- unequal applicant pools, confounding by department, the fact that neither the aggregate nor the subgroup rate is automatically the "true" one, and the temptation to report whichever number flatters you. Include a fake quote from a "concerned statistician" who says the reversal is a real feature of the data, not a math error, and that choosing a rate is already a causal choice. Maybe add a sidebar on the new internal metric they've invented, the Holistic Yield-Adjusted Fairness Index (HYAFI), which weights departments by donor size. Don't name real people or cite real papers. The target is the use of Simpson's paradox as a rhetorical get-out-of-jail-free card, not the paradox itself. Make a statistician laugh rather than wince.
