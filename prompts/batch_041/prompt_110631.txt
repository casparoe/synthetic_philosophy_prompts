I run a lunchtime "science-y philosophy" discussion group at my job (I'm an engineer, no philosophy training, so whatever you design has to be runnable by an amateur). About 10 people, one 45-minute session. I want to do a session on the Reflection principle — I think it's van Fraassen's, the idea that if you're certain your future self will beleive P to degree r, you should already believe P to degree r right now. Design an in-class game where the group feels why that's so compelling, and then feels why it's also obviously false.

My angle: don't let them just bet on a coin flip. Make it two selves. Each person is their "current self"; a partner plays their "future self." The future self draws a card saying either that they've seen genuine evidence about P, or that they've swallowed a pill that makes them confident (or skeptical) about P regardless of what's actually true, or that they've simply lost some information the current self already has. The two of them then have to settle on a single price for a ticket that pays $1 if P, and the current self can only override the future self by paying a penalty. I want them to discover for themselves that deferring to your future self looks obviously right on the evidence card and obviously crazy on the pill card, and that the nasty part is the principle tells you to defer either way.

Give me: the claim(s) P to use (something resolvable within a week that nobody can game), the card design, the rules, the payoff and penalty scheme (no real money), a 45-minute timeline, and a debrief that ties what happened back to the principle. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about the one thing the game just can't reproduce so I have to say it out loud myself.

Format it as a JSON object with these fields: propositions (array of the claims to use), materials, rules, payoffs, timeline, debrief_mapping, failure_modes, cant_reproduce.
