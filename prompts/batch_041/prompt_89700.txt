Last night my friend Dan and I argued about money pumps for like two and a half hours and I'm still irritated, so I want you to write something for me.

Here's Dan's position, short version. If your preferences go in a circle — you'd trade A for B, trade B for C, and trade C back for A — then someone can walk you around that loop charging you a small fee at every stop, and at the end you're holding exactly what you started with, minus the fees. Dan says that isn't a cute parlor trick, it's the actual reason consistency is a requirement of rationality, and he gets from there to everything else: changing your mind over time is a defect, someone whose discounting flips as the date gets closer is a walking pump, and the example he loves about the guy who doesn't mind pain if it's next Tuesday but does mind it if it's next Wednesday is, in Dan's words, the profile of a person nobody should make deals with. I said the whole thing only works if I actually take each trade, and that I can just say no. He said saying no is worse — you end up refusing every trade, and you're not a person anymore, you're a rock. Then he said the thing that really got under my skin: he said if you refuse the trades, you're not irrational, you're just poor, because the pump is an opportunity and you're leaving it on the table. I think that's garbage but I couldn't say why at 1am.

So here's what I want. An advice column, in the form of a newspaper money-advice page: a column name, a tagline, then 600-700 words of letters and answers, four or five letters. The columnist is Dan's position with a byline — completely sincere, no winking, no getting cute about how obviously awful the advice is, and he never breaks and admits the whole thing is a reductio. Every piece of advice has to be something you can actually pull out of the pump argument. That's where the joke has to live: if you're going to be outrageous, be outrageous by being right, so that somebody who's read the literature laughs instead of wincing.

Letters you could use (swap freely): a guy asking whether it's okay to pump his roommate, who genuinely cycles between three arrangements of their furniture; a woman whose husband keeps accepting one more notch of something awful for a slightly bigger paycheck, each step small enough to be worth it, until he's in bad shape and can't point to the step where it went wrong; someone who only cares about his own future on certain days of the week and wants to know if he's allowed to sign a lease; someone who keeps buying commitment devices and breaking them, writing in for a better commitment device; and maybe a reader who has quietly built a small business out of exploiting his own inconsistency and wants to know if he's done anything wrong.

Don't cite anybody or give me a reading list, and dont put famous lines in anyone's mouth — if a reader recognizes one of these cases, it should be from the setup, not from a name drop. Plain English, no jargon walls. And dont let the columnist hedge into sanity at the end; if he has to hedge, hedge in character.
