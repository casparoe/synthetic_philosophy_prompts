Suppose William James was around today and someone sat him down with the Libet-style work on free will. The basic setup: people are told to flex a wrist whenever they feel like it, and a slow ramp of electrical activity over the motor areas starts a few hundred milliseconds before the movement — roughly a third of a second before the person says they were aware of having decided to move. Libet's own spin was that the last tenth or two of a second is still open to a conscious veto. Then throw in the messier later stuff: some researchers argue the slow ramp is just noise drifting across a threshold rather than a decision forming, and stimulating different patches of cortex can either make a hand move while the patient denies intending it, or give them a felt urge to move when nothing actually moves. What would James have made of all this? Would it threaten his conviction that consciousness does real work and that the will shows up in the effort of attention, or would he say it's answering a different question? Say which bits of the evidence he'd shrug at and which would actually bother him, and flag where your just extrapolating rather than reasoning from something he argued.
