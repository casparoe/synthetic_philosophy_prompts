ok so i'm in this seminar on minds, machines, and belief — its cross-listed between phil and cog sci — and we've spent like three weeks on the intentional stance and whether it makes any sense to say an AI system believes something. final paper is due and before i write it i want the actual spec straight, because every reading we do seems to argue from one example. someone says: look, a chatbot says it thinks your flight leaves at 6, and it's wrong, but obviously it doesnt *believe* anything, it's autocomplete. someone else says: you cant predict what a chess engine does except by treating it as wanting to win and thinking some move is best, so why not call that believing. and i dont feel like i know what any of these people think a theory of belief-attribution would even have to *do*.

so: write me the desiderata list for this area. what does an adequate account of when it's correct, or legitimate, or at least useful, to attribute beliefs (and desires, and degrees of confidence) to a system — artificial or otherwise — actually have to deliver? the purpose i care about is a theory good enough that i could use it to say something about real cases without getting laughed at by the ML people in the room, so calibrate to that.

be specific about whats behind each requirement, i want to defend the list, not just vibe it. that means: the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it cant collapse, the results about how these systems actually work that it cant contradict.

heres the case set we used in class, plus one our TA made up for the last problem set:

- a chess engine
- a spam filter that gets retrained every night
- a nav app that reroutes you
- a chatbot that tells you it believes your flight is at 6, and is wrong
- a thermostat (someone always brings up the thermostat)

distinctions i've got so far, tell me which are real requirements and which are just people being precious: believing vs. merely being in a state that carries information (tree rings carry information about age); having a belief vs. being disposed to emit sentences that sound like belief reports; all-or-nothing belief vs. degree of confidence; attributing a belief vs. attributing a goal; there being a fact of the matter vs. it just being a useful predictive stance. and the one thats bugging me — the identity/persistence thing: an LLM with no memory between chats, whose belief is it, and when? does the list need a requirement about the believer persisting through time, or is that smuggling in something?
