My office-mate is a third-year in my PhD program (I'm second-year, we're both in philosophy, she's the one who does the formal stuff) and for about a year now she's been keeping a notebook of one-shot prisoner's dilemmas with her verdicts in them, mostly to wind me up. The payoffs are always the same: we both cooperate, 3 each; we both defect, 1 each; I defect while you cooperate, 5 for me and 0 for you. One shot, simultaneous moves, no communication unless I say otherwise. Her calls so far:

- vs. an exact copy of herself: same code, same payoffs, both know it. Cooperate.
- vs. a machine that's built to defect no matter what, and everyone knows it. Defect.
- vs. a total stranger she knows nothing about. Defect.
- vs. a near-copy: same reasoning as her, compiled from a slightly older version of her code, differing on stuff she says is irrelevant, like which hot drink it prefers. Cooperate.
- vs. her exact copy again, except this one has been handed a payoff matrix where defecting is strictly dominant for it, and both of them know that. Defect. Her line is "mirrors cut both ways".

She calls the whole thing superrationality, and I can definately see at least two different rules that fit all five of those calls, which is the problem, because they come apart on the one case she hasn't ruled on:

You're up against a total stranger -- no shared history, no shared reasoning style, you know nothing about them. But you've both been given the same drug, and you know from the literature that this drug makes people pick cooperate in a one-shot PD about 90% of the time. You're about to move, and you notice you're feeling an unaccountable pull toward cooperating. You can still defect; the pull is just a pull. Question is whether the drug working on you tells you anything about whether it's working on the stranger, since you both got it from the same batch.

So: what's her call on the drug case? Give me the two or three rules that fit her five verdicts so far, what each one says about the drug case, which of them you'd bet she's actually running and why, and then the one further case you'd put in front of her to seperate the live rules -- something where they disagree and nothing else is going on.
