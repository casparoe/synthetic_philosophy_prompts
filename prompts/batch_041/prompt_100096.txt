Right, so this has turned into a thing. I'm a postdoc and for the last year I've been arguing — in a paper that's out for review and that I have to present next Thursday — that you can tell whether a research programme is progressive or degenerating while it's still happening, not only in hindsight. Which sounds like a small point but it's the whole paper, because the standard line is that this is exactly what you can't do, and if you can't, then the whole framework is useless for the only decision anyone ever actually faces, which is whether to keep funding the thing.

The gist: Lakatos's own picture only lets you call a programme degenerating after the fact. My fix is to score programmes on what I've been calling imported confirmations. A belt-tweak only counts as producing a novel fact if the confirmation comes from outside the programme — a technique, an instrument, a result the programme's own people didn't build. Count the belt-tweaks, count the imported confirmations, take the ratio over a rolling window. Near zero and falling means degenerating now. I run it on two cases, one everybody agrees was progressive and one that's contested, and the contested one comes out bad, and its a programme that pays several salaries in my building, possibly mine.

The reading group is the problem. About a dozen people, and I'm the only one in the room who actually runs experiments: three phil-sci people who know the Lakatos corpus and the Kuhn and Feyerabend fights in their bones, one unreconstructed Popperian, two working scientists from the field I'm accusing (one of them soft-funded by it, and he knows), a sociologist of science who thinks the appraisal-of-programmes business is normative fiction dressed up as description, and one first-year grad student who asks the question that actually ends you.

What I want is a Q&A prep sheet, not a pep talk. For each objection: who it's coming from, how they'll put it in their own vocabulary, how likely it is, how much damage it does, my two-minute reply, and where that reply is soft — the part I should concede instead of defend. Order them by damage. Tell me the one concession I should make before anyone even asks for it, and the one question I'm praying nobody asks. And please dont tell me to go read anything, I've read it, that's how I got here.
