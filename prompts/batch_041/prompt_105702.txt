My opening: you keep sliding between two different questions — what justifies a law and what motivates the people who pass it. Take my case. I want the council to stop building on a particular stretch of moorland, and my reason isn't that it harms anyone, it's that the place is holy, full stop — a claim you don't share and can't be argued into sharing. And my lot are only about forty years old, so half the council don't even reckon we count as a religion. Fine. I'm happy for the law to be justified to you on your own terms — recreation, ecology, future generations, whatever. I just won't pretend those are my reasons. Isn't that enough? If the law can be publicly justified even though my actual reason can't be, then your constraint isn't really about justification at all — it's about policing what citizens are allowed to say out loud when they ask for things.
