I am a novelist at work on a book whose central character is a machine that talks, and I have reached the point where my own intuitions are no longer a strict enough standard. I have been reading the current argument about whether large language models understand anything, and both sides seem to be disputing the criteria without ever stating them. I have also been treating understanding as one thing, when I suspect the interesting positions are those that divide it. What I want from you is the desiderata: what any adequate theory of linguistic understanding — of what it is for a system, human or otherwise, to understand a language — has to deliver.

Set out the requirements, each with the case or consideration behind it. I want the list to cover at least the following.

The phenomena it must get right, including three cases I have invented for the book and cannot settle: two systems that are behaviourally indistinguishable on every test, where only one has had its vocabulary tied to things in the world through a body; a system that annotates every metaphor in a novel correctly because a team of people wrote the annotations it consults; and the case that worries me most, a machine that does not fail to understand love so much as understand something else by it. A theory that can only return "understands" or "does not" seems to me to lose precisely what I am writing about.

The distinctions it must respect — between producing and recognising, between knowing a language and knowing about one, between understanding a word, a sentence, a stretch of discourse, and a whole practice — and any it may collapse with impunity. The results it must sit alongside, such as the dissociations between comprehension and production that appear after brain injury, and the fact that children manage with noisy and fragmentary evidence. What it must not be hostage to: in particular, whether it can stay neutral on consciousness, and whether it must admit that understanding comes in degrees.

Then tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude candidates, and flag any pair among them that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — I would rather learn this now than in chapter twenty. Keep any scorecard of existing theories brief; I care more about the criteria than the contenders.

Write your answer in American English.
