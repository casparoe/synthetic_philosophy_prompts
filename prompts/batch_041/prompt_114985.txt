My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about the astrology in King Lear, and I want to work the question through properly rather than just relitigate it. His view is that the play sides with Edmund: the speech in which Edmund mocks those who blame the sun, moon, and stars for their own vices, and calls that an admirable evasion, is the voice of the play's intelligence, while Gloucester's reading of the recent eclipses as portending mutiny, the cooling of love, and the cracking of the bond between father and son is the superstition the play means to expose. I think that is too quick, and I want a reading of the play through Kepler's reformed astrology rather than through either the popular astrology Gloucester practices or the naturalism Edmund professes.

Kepler is a useful third position because he was neither a credulous horoscoper nor a debunker. He retained the aspects, the angular relations among the planets, as the physically meaningful core of astrology, and discarded the signs and the twelve houses as having no basis in the geometry of the world. He held that the soul, and the sublunary world more generally, responds to celestial configurations as it responds to harmony, and that this influence shapes or inclines without necessitating particular outcomes, so it cannot carry the exculpatory weight Gloucester wants to place on it. He defended a purified astrology in treatises such as De fundamentis astrologiae certioribus and Tertius interveniens while mocking the vulgar appetite for prediction, and he cast horoscopes for patrons and for himself with what looks to me like deliberate hedging. He was also a Copernican, which is what makes the whole project precarious.

What I want from you: first, a reading of the play that treats the eclipse-talk and Edmund's counter-speech as a genuine philosophical disagreement rather than a clash of sense and nonsense; second, an account of where the play's dramatic structure, the storm, the heath, the persistent sense that the heavens are somehow implicated in the human catastrophe, needs something like Kepler's inclining-but-not-compelling influence, and where it instead needs the older, stronger astrology he dismantled; third, the honest limits, naming scenes or speeches where the Keplerian reading distorts the play, and where the play is simply indifferent to the physics; and fourth, what the tragedy adds to the philosophical debate that Kepler's treatises cannot. Please give the argument rather than a reading list, and paraphrase rather than quote. Say how confident you are in the reading as a whole, and single out the claim you are least sure of.
