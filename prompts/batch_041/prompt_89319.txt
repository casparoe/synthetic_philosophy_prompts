A colleague in my metaphysics reading group has been giving verdicts on cases of composition, and I want to predict how she will rule on cases she has not yet reached. Her verdicts so far: the parts of a cat compose something, as do the parts of a redwood, a single amoeba, and a lichen. Nothing is composed by the grains of a sand heap, the boards of a table that has been screwed together, the gears of a functioning wristwatch, the bronze of a statue, or the tissues of a corpse an hour after death. She has not yet ruled on a candle flame, a mitochondrion inside a living cell, a dormant seed, or a colony of ants. Say what she will say about each of those four; but the main work is to state the rules that fit her record so far, to give your best guess at the rule she is in fact running together with your reasons for preferring it to the rivals, and to identify which of the four new cases would best tell those rules apart. If her pattern is better explained by the thought that composition is brute than by any rule at all, say so, and say what that would imply for the predictions.
