I'm a novelist, and I'm working on a book about a woman who finds out she was conceived through a mix-up at a fertility clinic. The plot is really just an excuse to think about identity, and I've been thinking about this alot, and its driving me a bit nuts. I have a bunch of gut verdicts about whether she could have come from a different sperm and egg. I think philosophers call this origin essentialism, but I don't care about the label. I want to know what principle, if any, ties my verdicts together. Here's what I think. If a different sperm had fertilized the same egg that made her, that's a different person, not her. If the same sperm had fertilized a different egg from the same mother, also a different person. If the same sperm and egg had met a day later, that's still her. If the embryo had been frozen for ten years before implantation, still her. If she had been carried by a different woman, still her. If the egg's mitochondria were swapped out for another woman's, I think that's still her, because the mitochondria aren't really her. If a single gene was edited to fix a disease, still her, though I'm less sure if you edited a bunch of genes for height and eye color. Now the hard ones. If the embryo had split into identical twins, I don't think either twin would be her. It feels like she never made it, and two seperate people started. But that clashes with the sperm-and-egg verdicts, because the same sperm and egg are still there. And if two embryos fuse into one person, a chimera, I think that resulting person is not identical to either of the original embryos, but I'm honestly not sure. Is there a principle that gets all of this? Maybe something like: you are the particular zygote, not the sperm and egg pair, and if the zygote divides, you don't survive. Does that force me to drop one of my verdicts? What does it say about a case where the same zygote is split and one half is implanted and the other half is discarded, or where the same genome is assembled from scratch in a lab, or where the same sperm and egg produce a child who is a different sex? I don't want citations, just the principle, its verdicts on the cases I haven't listed, and an honest note on which of my verdicts it can't accommodate. Also tell me how confident you are in the principle you land on, and which bit you're least sure about.
