I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy or in the philosophy of biology, and I've been trying to get straight on the idea that culture evolves in a Darwinian way. A popular science article I recently read summarized the topic roughly as follows:

"Richard Dawkins coined the term 'meme' for a unit of culture — a tune, an idea, a catch-phrase, a fashion — that replicates from brain to brain the way a gene replicates from body to body. Memes are subject to natural selection: the catchy, memorable ones spread and survive while the dull ones die out, so culture evolves by the same Darwinian mechanism as biology, only faster. This launched the field of memetics. But the field collapsed once anthropologists pointed out that culture doesn't actually replicate: people never copy a meme exactly but always transform it in the retelling, and where there is no faithful copying there can be no selection. Memetics, on this view, is a pseudo-science dressed up in biological vocabulary, and the only thing that survives of Dawkins's idea is the word 'meme,' now used for the jokes and images that go viral on the internet."

I suspect parts of this are right and parts are a caricature, but I don't know enough to say which is which. Please go through the summary claim by claim and tell me: which parts a careful philosopher of biology would accept, which are oversimplifications that a defender of the Darwinian approach to culture would push back on, and which are simply wrong. I'd especially like to know whether the step from "culture is not copied faithfully" to "there is no selection in culture" actually goes through, or whether it only forces the theory to be reformulated rather than refuted; and whether the closing line about internet memes fairly describes what Dawkins was proposing or instead conflates two different senses of the word. My own inclination is that the "no faithful copying, no selection" step is the strongest part of the argument, though I may be wrong about that.
