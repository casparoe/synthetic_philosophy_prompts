I am a machine-learning engineer — comfortable with train/test discipline, data leakage, held-out evaluation, likelihoods and posterior predictive checks, and retraining under a leave-one-out or data-ablation scheme — but only a lay reader of Bayesian epistemology. The so-called problem of old evidence has always looked to me like a description of leakage: an observation that was already in hand when a theory was formulated cannot serve as an independent test of it, much as a model that has already seen a test point cannot be credited with predicting it. I would like you to stress-test that reading by translating the problem, together with the main proposed solutions to it, into the vocabulary I actually use. Render the proposal that one should consult what one's credence in the theory would have been had one not yet known the evidence as a kind of counterfactual retraining; render the observation that known evidence carries probability one as a statement about a model that assigns unit probability to data it has already absorbed; treat the objection that such counterfactual credences are ill-defined as the difficulty of saying what "the same model, minus this one datum" even denotes; and so on, as faithfully as the mapping permits. Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the philosophical problem map cleanly onto leakage and held-out evaluation, which map only under distortion, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? I am particularly interested in whether the disanalogy runs against me: the machine-learning setting can at least define a retrained model that has not seen a given point, whereas the Bayesian agent seems to be a single fixed credence function with no such family of counterfactuals available to it. If that asymmetry is real, does it suggest the philosophical problem is partly an artifact of modeling the agent as one probability function rather than a family of them? And if instead the residue marks a genuine limit of my framework, tell me what it is that my vocabulary cannot express.
