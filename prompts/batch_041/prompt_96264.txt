I've been rereading Nietzsche and something about him keeps bugging me: he's one of the only philosophers who treats his own digestion, his diet, where he lives, the weather, how far he walks, as if those were philosophical topics and not just biography. A friend in my reading group takes this to make him the first wellness guy, a proto-biohacker — track the body, optimize the inputs, treat health as an engineering problem you solve. I think that reading is backwards, but I want to test it against things he never could have seen, because the usual "Nietzsche on health" takes are all about illness and asceticism and none of them touch the actual weird stuff people do to their bodies now.

So: drop him into 2025 and ask him about each of these, one at a time.

1. Ozempic and the other GLP-1 drugs — appetite and food noise turned off by a weekly injection.
2. Dopamine fasting, digital detox, no-fap style regimens — self-imposed renunciation used as a productivity hack.
3. Veganism as an ethical movement, diet framed as a duty owed to non-human animals.
4. The gut microbiome stuff — mood, maybe even cognition, as downstream of your bacteria; you are what your flora eat.
5. Public health paternalism: sugar taxes, ultra-processed food regulation, produce prescriptions, the state managing what a population eats.
6. Continuous glucose monitors plus personalized nutrition startups plus supplement stacks — measure your own metabolism and eat only what the sensor approves.
7. Longevity maximalism: fasting-mimicking diets, cold plunges, VO2 max as a life project.
8. The idea that wellness culture is just the ascetic ideal in athleisure — clean eating as a secular purity ethic.

For each, give me a table with these columns and nothing else in the cells:

- issue: short label, matching my numbering
- verdict: his predicted position, 1-2 sentences, as close to his voice as you can get without pastiche
- physiological story: in his terms, what is this doing to the body and the instincts? He always has one, so give me the one he'd give
- basis: one of three — something he actually says somewhere (put it in your own words), follows from his stated principles, or straight extrapolation on your part
- confidence: high, medium, or low
- malformed: where he'd say the question itself is badly posed, or "none" if it's a clean question
- contested: would people who know Nietzsche fight about this row? If yes, what's the split; if no, say why it's settled

Before the table, 3-5 sentences on the general question my friend raised: is the proto-biohacker reading right, backwards, or something worse than either? Push back on me if you think I'm wrong — I'd rather be argued with than agreed with. After the table, 3-4 sentences on the two rows you'd bet on least and why.

Don't bother with page numbers or citations, I won't check them and you'll probably mangle them anyway; what I care about is the reasoning, and whether you're remembering him or building him. And be straight with me about which parts of this are settled among people who actually work on him and which parts are genuinely contested — dont dress up a reading that's disputed as if it were the consensus, and flag it if some row is really just you making him up in a plausible register.
