I am a high school student, and for a civics paper I am working on the various proposals to change who may vote: compulsory voting, a lower voting age, and voting rights for non-citizen residents. Before I evaluate any of those proposals, I want to know what a theory of the franchise is supposed to deliver. Please write that specification for me — the desiderata any adequate theory of who ought to have the vote must meet.

For each requirement, give me the case or consideration that puts it on the list. Here is what I have so far; add anything I have missed, and tell me if any item on my list is not really a requirement but only a prejudice of one side.

Cases the theory must get right: the sixteen-year-old who works and pays taxes; the resident who has lived here for twenty years, pays taxes, and has no path to citizenship; the citizen who left thirty years ago and does not intend to return; the adult with a severe cognitive disability; the citizen who has completed a sentence for a felony; the citizen who has never voted and does not wish to; the person who turns eighteen two days after election day; and, as a pressure test, children and people not yet born who will live with the consequences of long-horizon policy.

Distinctions it must not collapse: between holding a vote and being counted as a member of the people; between competence and having a stake; between a right and a duty, which matters for compulsion; and between being subject to a law and being affected by it, since those two seem to pull apart in the case of non-citizens.

Puzzles it must dissolve: the problem that any principle for drawing the boundary of the electorate must be applied by an electorate that has already been drawn, so who decides who decides; why eighteen rather than some other age, without simply ratifying the status quo; why being taxed without a vote seems a genuine grievance for resident non-citizens but not for minors; and whether a right to vote includes a right to abstain or whether that is a separate question.

Findings it must be consistent with: what is actually known about whether compulsory voting raises turnout, and whether it changes who wins or only who shows up; whether it increases spoiled or protest ballots; and whether lowering the voting age alters turnout habits later in life.

Finally, tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the preferences of a particular camp, whether any two of them cannot be jointly satisfied, and which single requirement rules out the most otherwise plausible theories. If it is quick, say which family of answers — competence, stake or being affected, membership, or subjection to the law — comes out best against the list. A table is fine. I do not need citations or a reading list.
