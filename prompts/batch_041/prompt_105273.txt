I am assembling a problem set for a graduate seminar on signaling and communication, and I want a case that forces the usual explanatory frameworks apart rather than illustrating them. Please construct a thought experiment involving a fictional but biologically plausible species—no peacocks, stotting gazelles, roaring red deer, or human luxury goods—in which a display is reliably correlated with some underlying quality of the signaller, is costly to produce or maintain, and yet is not a handicap in the sense that the cost itself is what guarantees honesty. The honesty should instead come from a structural feature of the signaller’s biology or life history that makes the display impossible to fake, so that the cost is real but epiphenomenal. Keep the scenario itself to a single paragraph; the analysis can be longer.

Then, in separate numbered steps: first, identify the signal, the quality advertised, the cost, and the mechanism that keeps the signal honest; second, state whether the handicap principle, the index-signal view, and a marginal-cost account of costly signaling each classify the display as a handicap, an index, or a costly signal, and explain why; third, say which single feature of the case is doing the work of separating the frameworks; fourth, describe what happens if that feature is removed—does the case collapse into a standard handicap, and if so, which one; and fifth, explain how a defender of the framework that comes out worst would try to redescribe the case to save it, and assess whether that redescription is legitimate or ad hoc. I am less interested in a verdict about whether the signal is honest in the real world than in a demonstration that the case meets these specifications, so make the construction do the work and be explicit about where it might be cheating.
