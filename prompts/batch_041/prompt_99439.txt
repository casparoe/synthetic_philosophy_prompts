I'm a graduate student in political theory, and I've spent the term circling a question I can't get a clean grip on: who counts as a member of the demos -- the people whose consent, votes, or claims are supposed to make a political decision legitimate. I keep noticing that every account I read is defended by appeal to a small handful of cases (long-settled residents without citizenship, a region voting to secede, people abroad who will be badly affected by a decision they cannot vote on, children, future generations, and so on), and each account seems engineered to get its favourite case right while quietly fudging the rest. Before I commit to any of them, I want the specification written out on its own, independent of the contenders: what must any adequate theory of the boundary of the demos actually deliver? I mean the phenomena it has to explain, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it cannot collapse -- between being subject to a decision and merely being affected by it, between standing to be counted and standing to contest, between membership in the demos and inclusion in some particular decision -- and the formal constraints it cannot violate, above all the one that a demos cannot simply vote itself its own boundary, since the vote already presupposes the very question at issue.

For each requirement, give me the case or consideration that forces it, and mark which requirements are genuine and which look like the prejudices of a particular camp dressed up as constraints. If some subset of them is jointly unsatisfiable, say so plainly -- a list that turns out to be inconsistent is the most useful thing I could get back. Two extras I'd like folded in. First, my roommate keeps proposing that the right test is a kind of reciprocity: an arrangement is legitimate only if you would accept it with your position swapped for any other party's. I want to know whether that is a bona fide desideratum a theory must meet or just one more substantive view smuggled in as a constraint. Second, if you can manage it without sliding into a full scorecard, a sentence or two on which single requirement does the most work in eliminating the usual candidates.
