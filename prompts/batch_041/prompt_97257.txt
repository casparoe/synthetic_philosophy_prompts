so i'm putting together a poetry night for my metaphysics reading group and i want a poem that actually enacts the special composition question instead of just name-dropping it. the question is when some things compose a further thing: do the atoms compose the chair, or is the chair just a useful way of talking? the usual answers are always, never, only when the parts are caught up in a life, only when they're in contact, only when they're fastened, and the question is broken. write me a sestina whose six end-words are always, never, contact, fastening, life, and broken. i want the repetition to make the reader feel how each answer keeps coming back and how the parts keep almost composing something. the envoy should turn on the fact that the poem itself is either one thing made of its lines or just a bunch of lines, and it should leave that open. make it accurate enough that someone who knows the debate would nod, and dont name any philosophers or cite anything. after the poem, add a short note (3-5 sentences) that separates what's actually settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this, and dont make any contested answer sound like the consensus. since a poem doesnt have a verdict, end with a final line that starts Verdict: and is just one sentence saying what the poem leaves unresolved.
