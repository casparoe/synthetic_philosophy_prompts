A friend of mine went back through four years of our group chat — me, her, and three other people — and wrote up a one-page 'user manual' for each of us: how we argue, what we're really asking for when we say we're fine, who needs to be told the plan in advance, that kind of thing. She printed them out and handed them around at dinner. Everyone laughed. She didn't dig anything up. Every line in mine comes from something I typed to those people or posted where anyone could see it, and if she'd asked me any of it point blank I'd of told her. Nothing in it is false. Most of it is flattering. It's also the most accurate description of me I've ever read, and that's the part I keep chewing on: the bit about how I get defensive when I'm actually the one who's wrong is dead on, and now I hear it in my head every time I do it, which somehow makes it worse. She's already updated it once, apparently.

Everyone else moved on and I'm still bothered, and when I try to say why, it comes out as 'it's wierd, ok?' I don't think she did anything wrong exactly — she did close reading on stuff I handed over freely, and she's using it to be a better friend, which, fine. There's probably a word for what's bugging me and I don't have it. So: is there a real complaint here, or am I just sore about being predictable? And either way, what do I say to her? I don't want to be the guy who can't stand being understood, but I also don't want to fake being fine with it. If the honest answer is that I'm being precious and there's nothing to fix, say that plainly instead of humoring me.
