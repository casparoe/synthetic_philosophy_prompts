My little brother's wedding is in three weeks and I'm doing the best man speech, and I want to try something that might be a terrible idea. He did a PhD on Dennett and it genuinely changed how he sees people — he's got this thing about the three stances. You can look at a person as just physics, a lump of stuff obeying laws; or as a thing that was designed to do a job, like you'd read a machine; or as an agent with beliefs and wants, which is the one you use when you're actually trying to predict what your mate will do next. He's spent his whole career insisting that last one isn't a convenient fiction we tell ourselves, that the patterns it picks out are as real as the ones the physicists pick out, real in the sense that they're the ones that let you actually compress what someone's like and see what they'll do before they do it.

That's the spine I want for the speech, but here's the difficulty. Half the room is his department — people who'll know exactly what I'm doing and will be quietly checking whether I get it right. The other half is our family and his school mates, who will glaze over the moment I say the word "stance". And it's a wedding, so it has to come out as affection, not as a lecture and not as some eulogy for his single life.

So what I want is a five-minute speech that actually walks through the three stances on him in order — opens with him as a bundle of atoms, moves to him as a thing built to do something (and here I want to lean on that idea he loves, about reasons that no one actually has, the sort of purpose you can read off a design even though nothing in there is doing any thinking — like a wing being for flying without the bird meaning anything by it) — and lands on him as the person we all know. And I want that last move to be the payoff: that the pattern of him and Sarah together is real in exactly the sense he means, not a story we tell about them but the thing that lets the rest of us see them coming.

Don't quote anything at me and don't cite anyone — I'm not putting a bibliography in a wedding speech, and I won't be able to remember it anyway. Just write the speech, in the voice of a slightly nervous brother who's read too many of his books and understood about two thirds of them. Make it funny somewhere, because he is.
