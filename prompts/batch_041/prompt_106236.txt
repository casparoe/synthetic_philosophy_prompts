I've spent years arguing about epistemic closure on rationalist forums and want to find out what my interlocutors actually believe rather than which label they happen to wear. Build me a decision tree for the closure debate that starts from the Moorean commitment that I know I have hands and am not a brain in a vat, and whose every branch node is a question answerable without already knowing which position it leads to.

Make each node a specific commitment about closure: whether knowledge is closed under known entailment at all; whether closure survives only when the entailed proposition is not lottery-like or otherwise a bare probabilistic prediction; whether the entailment has to be known or merely believed; and — the distinction I care most about — what each position's epistemic bill comes to, that is, which inference ordinary people must be declared unable to make in order to keep the knowledge they think they already have.

The leaves should be named positions — sensitivity/tracking in Nozick's sense, relevant alternatives, contextualism about knowledge attributions, safety-based accounts, Moorean or dogmatist replies, and scepticism — together with any combination nobody actually defends, with one line on whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Give tracking its own leaf, and say plainly which other leaves your tree cannot separate from it.
