Write a parody corporate style guide — a fictional PR firm's internal handbook — where the chapter on honesty is a deadpan manual for misleading people without ever lying. Numbered rules, worked before/after examples (a layoffs announcement, an apology after a data breach, a line about a product's safety), and a fake in-house metric the firm treats as sacred, like a "plausible-deniability margin" staff are told to keep above some threshold. The humor should come from the philosophy being right: get the gap between what a sentence literally says and what it implies right, so every rewrite stays technically true while clearly steering the reader toward a false belief, and let the reader slowly realize the whole "well, we never actually lied" defense is what's doing the moral work. Have one example sit right on the line — a half-truth or false presupposition the guide keeps insisting is "merely misleading," where a careful reader would say it's quietly crossed into lying and the guide never notices. Keep it deadpan, nobody winks at the reader. Add a short appendix from the firm's ethics officer objecting that misleading can be worse than lying precisely because it's deniable and leaves no one to hold to account — the guide should smother that objection in the most corporate way instead of actually answering it. Around 800 words, and dont use any real companies or people.
