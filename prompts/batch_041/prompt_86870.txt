My colleague in the next office does comparative ethics and she's been handing down rulings in our joint seminar all term, and I've been keeping a list because I genuinly can't tell what rule she's running. Her track record so far:

- Dueling aristocracies, the old honor-of-the-field business: clearly worse than a modern rights-based legal order. No hesitation, no caveats.
- But that same rights-based legal order, as it actually treats people in neighborhoods where it's basically abdicated: worse than the street code those neighborhoods run on. She will die on this hill. Her line is that the code treats you as a member who can be held to account, while the state treats you as weather.
- Campus call-out culture, the version with no process and permanent records: no better than the dueling aristocracy, possibly worse. She says it's a ledger pretending to be a floor — it tells you your standing is unconditional and then revokes it over a screenshot.
- A restorative justice circle that shames an offender publicly and then runs a formal reintegration ceremony: better than what the dignity order does instead, which is prison, because here the humiliation is consented to, bounded, and it ends.
- A small religious community that has members confess publicly, excludes them for a period, and then readmits them: she approves. The door stays open.

Now the new case, and I want your prediction before I put it to her. A women's collective in a small town, after something like a hearing, publishes the names and faces of men it has found to have beaten their partners, on a wall in the town square, permanently — no removal, no expirery, and the men's families catch the stigma too. Recidivism in the town collapses, the victims say they finally feel safe, even the local police say it works. Nobody can leave; the town is the town.

So: what does she say about the wall? Then tell me which rule she's actually running — her floor-and-ledger thing, a worst-off-outcomes rule, a contestability rule, or something else — what each of those predicts here, which one you think is really hers and why, and what case I should put to her next to find out. I'll take the prediction, but the rule is what I'm after. And if two rules fit everything she's said and only split on the wall, say so plainly instead of picking one to make me happy.
