I have to submit a short position statement on the metaphysics of time for a seminar, and before I do I would like a consistency audit of what I actually believe. My commitments are these. (1) I take special relativity at face value: there is no privileged simultaneity relation, and no frame-independent fact about which distant events are happening now. (2) I accept the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, and I regard it as a consequence of special relativity that needs no additional metaphysical premise; so I hold the block universe, on which all events are equally real and the past–future distinction is a matter of perspective. (3) I am a scientific realist: where a theory posits unobservable structure that earns its keep, I take that structure to be real, and I do not think facts about what exists are relative to a frame. (4) I do not treat the felt passage of time as evidence that anything passes, since physics gives us reason to think there is nothing there to be detected. (5) I also think the belief that time passes is more certain than any physical theory that might be used against it, so no such theory should be allowed to overturn it. (6) I think causation has a genuine, non-conventional direction: I can affect what comes later and not what came earlier, and this is a fact about the world rather than merely about me. (7) I am a compatibilist about free will, and I do not think the block universe makes deliberation or agency pointless.

Tell me which of these pull against one another, which pairs only appear to conflict, and, for the real tensions, which commitment has to give and what the cheapest repair would cost. Order the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally bad, and finish by naming the single revision that would resolve the most.
