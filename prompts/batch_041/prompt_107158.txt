I'm taking a seminar on aesthetics and politics this term and my final paper draft is due Friday. It's on Du Bois and the whole all-art-is-propaganda claim, and I'm worried it's either totally obvious or totally wrong, so I'd like real comments rather than encouragement. Please take the role of a philosopher of art who actually knows Du Bois's writing from the twenties and the Harlem Renaissance fights around it, not just the one line everybody quotes. Think it through carefully before answering, and start by laying out my argument in the strongest form you can manage before you start poking holes in it, so I can see whether I've even said what I mean.

The argument, roughly. Du Bois's claim gets attacked and defended as if he meant that art ought to be didactic, i.e. that the work should argue for a political conclusion. I want to say that's the least interesting reading, and that his real claim comes apart into three. (1) Propoganda by content: the work argues for something. (2) Propaganda by framing: the work doesn't argue, it selects, omits and idealizes, and the resulting picture does political work — but that's true of basically everything, so the thesis goes trivial. (3) Propaganda by existence: the work neither argues nor frames; the mere fact that it exists, was made by a Black artist, and is good is itself the argument, aimed at people who deny that Black people can make anything good at all. I claim (3) is what Du Bois was up to, and that Schuyler's The Negro-Art Hokum is a bad-faith reading of him, because Schuyler treats the claim as (1) when it's really (3).

Here's the part I can't fix. If (3) is right, then any work by a Black artist counts as propaganda just by existing and the thesis stops saying anything. My patch is that existence-propaganda only works if the work is excellent and actually seen — the demonstration only does its job if it can't be waved away as mediocre and if somebody bothers to look. Is that a real repair, or am I just adding epicycles to save a thesis I like?

So three things: (a) am I reading Du Bois fairly, or am I making him out to be a subtler philosopher than he was? (b) does the three-way split do any actual work or is it just relabeling? (c) if you think the patch is a dodge, say what would have to be true for you to change your mind, and where your own reasoning is most likely to be off. Also be honest about whether this is interesting enough for a seminar paper — I'd rather hear that now than in the grade.
