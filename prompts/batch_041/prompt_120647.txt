I am putting together the handout for the second meeting of a graduate seminar on collective harm, and I want it to be a sorting instrument rather than a survey of opinions. Please build me a decision tree, ten to fourteen branch nodes, numbered so that I can typeset the result as a flowchart, beginning from a reader who already accepts that the harms in question are serious and that no identifiable victim would have been better off had she acted differently, and carrying her to a position on whether her individual action — one flight, one burger, one vote, one porch light — is wrong. Every node must be a diagnostic question phrased as a forced choice between two concrete cases, or between two concrete claims about a single case, so that a reader can answer it without already knowing which position it leads to; no node may ask for a label, for a general principle stated in the abstract, or for what the reader thinks about the literature. Build the tree from the logical space first — work out which questions genuinely separate views, then place positions into the combinations of answers they allow — rather than starting from a list of positions and manufacturing questions to tell them apart. Where a combination of answers turns out to be unoccupied, say so, and diagnose it in a sentence: is nobody there because the combination is incoherent, because it is coherent but repellent, or merely because nobody has explored it? Wherever two leaves cannot be separated by the questions you have written, flag the pair rather than papering over the gap, and tell me either what further question would separate them or why they are one position wearing two coats.

The leaves must include at least the following: a view on which the action is not wrong because it makes no difference; a threshold view on which it is wrong only because enough others are acting and the total crosses a line; a view on which the wrong is the tiny increase in expected harm, computed straight; a view on which the wrong is complicity in, or endorsement of, a harmful practice, independently of causal contribution; a rule- or practice-oriented view on which the question is what the relevant practice would look like if everyone followed it; a view on which the duty is primarily collective, so the individual's obligation is to work on the collective rather than to abstain; a view on which over-compliance by some generates an obligation of compensation toward those who do less; and one view that treats the whole question as confused — roughly, that contributing is the wrong kind of concept for these cases. Label each leaf by its central claim in a few words, adding a conventional label in parentheses only where one genuinely exists, and give each leaf one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Do not give me authors, titles, dates, or reading suggestions; I want the logical space, not a who's-who, and I would rather see a leaf marked unnamed than a leaf marked with a name I cannot check.

I care most about the cases, because I want the tree to be broken by cases that no one has written about, so that it cannot be assembled out of memory. Use these: a street where each household's outdoor light, imperceptible on its own, degrades a nearby observatory's data, so that the harm is a continuous small increment with no threshold and no individual victim; a passenger who buys an offset with every flight and wants to know whether netting out is even the right form of the question; a driver who buys a heavier car for her own safety in a market where everyone does the same, so that her act protects her while making the aggregate worse; a person who declines a seat on a flight that is then sold to someone else, so that the harm proceeds at the same scale either way; and a household that cuts its emissions well below its share, effectively subsidizing its neighbors' excess. Invent two or three more in the same spirit, aimed at whatever distinctions your tree turns on but cannot yet reach. I also want one node that separates views about what is wrong with the act from views about what is wrong with the person, since my students keep sliding between the two without noticing. Please write in American English.
