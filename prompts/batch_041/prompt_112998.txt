I am the knowledge-representation lead on a small team building an internal multi-agent assistant for a hospital's scheduling and compliance office. Several software agents negotiate appointments, flag policy conflicts, and hand tasks to one another, and a separate belief module tracks, for each agent and each human operator, which propositions that party knows, merely believes, or has never entertained. Our belief module is built on a standard epistemic logic whose knowledge operator closes under logical consequence, and this is quietly ruining the system: because an agent explicitly knows a handful of policy statements, the module infers that it knows every logical consequence of them, including contradictions it would never notice and facts no one has ever told it. Our operators complain that the system treats them as though they know everything their own records entail. I need to replace the closure rule with a decision procedure my engineers can actually run.

Please write that procedure: an ordered set of tests that the belief module, or a human auditor reviewing its outputs, applies whenever it has derived "agent A knows φ" purely as a consequence of A's explicit knowledge, in order to decide whether to record φ as genuine knowledge, downgrade it to "A would know this if prompted" or "A could infer this," or refuse the attribution outright.

The procedure should be grounded in what is actually known about the problem of logical omniscience, and I want it to make use of three ideas that I can state plainly and that you should treat as raw materials rather than as a menu to choose among. First, the distinction between explicit belief — what the agent has actually represented — and implicit belief — what follows from it. Second, the relativization of knowledge to an agent's awareness, so that an agent can fail to know a consequence simply by not having the relevant concepts or topic in view. Third, a bound on reasoning, so that an agent can fail to draw a consequence because it is too many steps away, too costly to derive, or irrelevant to whatever the agent is doing. Combine these into a single ordered set of questions, say what counts as a sufficient answer to each, and be explicit about which steps are genuine tests and which are judgment calls dressed up as tests.

A few constraints. The procedure will be run many times a day on thousands of derived attributions, mostly by an automated checker with a human auditing only the flagged cases, so it must be cheap and it must be decidable on the information the module actually holds. Our engineers are competent programmers but not logicians. The hospital's compliance officer has final sign-off on any attribution that could expose the institution to liability, so the procedure must state clearly which outputs require her review rather than the checker's. And it must not simply refuse to attribute anything beyond the explicit: our operators do rely on the system to tell them what a colleague is committed to, and a procedure that answers "no" to everything is useless.

Then run the procedure on three cases. (a) An agent explicitly knows that no controlled substance may be released without a second signature, and knows that morphine is a controlled substance; the checker derives that the agent knows no morphine may be released without a second signature. Should the system say the agent knows this? (b) An agent knows a set of scheduling rules from which a contradiction follows only after a long chain of reasoning that no one has performed. Should the system record the agent as knowing the contradiction, as believing inconsistent things, or as knowing nothing of the kind? State what the procedure outputs and why. (c) A human operator knows that a particular patient's consent form was never filed, and knows that a particular procedure was performed; the checker derives that she knows the procedure was performed without consent. Here I expect the procedure to give an answer the operators will not like; tell me what it gives and whether I should override it.

Finish by saying what the procedure cannot do: the classes of case where no amount of refinement will let the checker decide, and where a person simply has to make the call. Do not give me a reading list or citations; refer to arguments and positions by description and I will fill in the references myself.
