I am setting the final exam for a graduate seminar on modal epistemology and want a single essay question on conceivability as a guide to possibility that cannot be answered by reciting the familiar claim that conceivability is defeasible evidence for possibility. The crux I want students to reach is the difference between two jobs the notion is asked to do: clearing an a priori obstacle — nothing we know rules P out and no contradiction is detectable in P — and positively supporting the possibility of P. Ask them to decide whether one and the same conceiving can perform both jobs, and to say what a defender of conceivability must concede if it can perform only the first. To give them a fixed target, have the question define an obstacle-free conceiving as one in which no known truth entails not-P and no contradiction is detectable in P, and ask whether obstacle-freeness is sufficient for possibility, too weak to count as evidence of it, or the wrong category altogether.

Please supply the essay question (no more than 120 words), a model answer of roughly 550 words that takes a definite position, and a marking scheme worth 20 marks. Build the scheme on named must-hit moves — at least one distinguishing the two jobs, one on why obstacle-clearing alone cannot yield positive possibility, one on the threat of circularity in the 'ideal conceiver' repair, and one on what an opponent of the approach ought to concede — giving the marks for each and a one-line note on the error that most often costs students those marks.
