I'm in a reading group working through Aquinas on war and sedition, and this reply is the one bit I can't get straight. Summa II-II q. 42 a. 2 asks whether sedition is always a mortal sin, and the objection he's answering is that "it is praiseworthy to deliver a multitude from a tyrannical rule", which can't be done without some dissension. His reply:

> A tyrannical government is not just, because it is directed, not to the common good, but to the private good of the ruler, as the Philosopher states (Polit. iii, 5; Ethic. viii, 10). Consequently there is no sedition in disturbing a government of this kind, unless indeed the tyrant's rule be disturbed so inordinately, that his subjects suffer greater harm from the consequent disturbance than from the tyrant's government. Indeed it is the tyrant rather that is guilty of sedition, since he encourages discord and sedition among his subjects, that he may lord over them more securely; for this is tyranny, being conducive to the private good of the ruler, and to the injury of the multitude.

Two things I can't square. First: if sedition is defined as what attacks the unity of law and the common good, then the first sentence is close to a tautology -- a rising that genuinely serves the common good just isn't sedition -- so why add the "unless" clause? That clause seems to grant that a rising against a tyrant can still be wrong because of how it turns out, which is a consequentialist move and looks like it belongs to a different argument than the definitional one. Second: I've been distinguishing two readings of "directed to the private good of the ruler" -- one about what the ruler aims at, one about how the benefits and burdens of the regime actually fall out. The first sentence seems to need the aim reading, the "unless" clause the outcome reading, and I dont see how he can hold both at once. Is this a real tension in the text, or am I reading him too analytically? Please stay close to the passage itself -- I mostly want it made clear, not the whole scholarly literature.
