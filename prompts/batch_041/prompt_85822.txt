ok so, background: I'm a 3rd year PhD student, working mostly on moral epistemology, and I've gotten myself stuck on the deference stuff in a way that's kind of embarassing. I keep having gut verdicts about cases where someone just takes another person's word on a moral matter, and I can't get them under any principle I can actually state. Can you try to find the pattern, tell me what it predicts about cases I haven't thought about, and be honest about which of my verdicts I have to give up?

Cases, all first person, all mine:

1. My sister is a vet and she tells me octopus farming is wrong because of what the animals go through, and that I should stop eating it. I beleive her and stop. Fine.

2. A friend who's read way more animal ethics than me tells me eating meat at all is wrong and I should go vegetarian. I do, on her word alone. I feel gross about this one, like I'm renting a conscience instead of having one.

3. A doctor tells me my father's treatment isn't worth continuing and I should agree to stop it. I do, on her say-so. Fine — even though there's a judgment buried in there about what kind of life is worth living, and I'm taking that on trust too.

4. A colleague tells me a famous paper's data was faked and that citing it would be dishonest. I believe her and cut it. Fine, obviously.

5. Some ethicist-type tells me I should give a third of my income away, and I do, purely because he says so. Feels bad, but I honestly can't tell whether that's different from 1 or whether I'm just stingy and dressing it up.

6. Someone tells me the way I treated my ex was cruel and that I owe an apology. I believe them and apologize. Fine — and this is the one I'd defend hardest, even though it's about me, which is supposed to be the worst case for taking someone's word.

7. I defer to the law, or to a professional code, about whether I have to report a colleague's misconduct. Fine.

8. Someone tells me a joke I tell is racist, and I stop telling it without being able to see it myself. Fine.

9. Someone tells me I'm a bad person, fundamentally, rotten to the core. I believe it. Not fine — and it feels like a different kind of not-fine than 5.

Things I've tried:

(a) Deference is ok for non-moral facts and for applying principles I already hold, not for the principles themselves. Handles 2 and 8, but 5 is just a principle and 9 isn't a principle at all, so it doesn't earn those.
(b) Ok when I couldn't have worked it out myself, not ok when I could have. Then 2 and 9 come out ok, which I don't want.
(c) Ok when it's about what to do, not about what to think of myself. But 5 is about what to do and still stinks, and 6 is about me and is fine.

So: is there one account that gets all nine, or do I have to drop something? And I'd especially like to know what it says about (i) asking a chatbot for a moral verdict, (ii) deferring to my community's majority view, (iii) deferring to someone whose overall moral views I think are worse than mine but who happens to know this one case, and (iv) a case where I act on the testimony but never actually come to believe it myself.
