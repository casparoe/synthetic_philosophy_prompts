I do reinforcement learning for robot manipulation, so I'm fluent in my own vocabulary — POMDPs, policies, observation histories, model-free vs. model-based, exploration and exploitation, credit assignment, generalization, reward hacking, finite-state controllers, meta-learning — but I've never took a philosophy class in my life.

There's this old naturalist story about a digger wasp. She paralyzes a cricket, drags it to her burrow, goes inside to check the burrow is still good, and if you nudge the cricket a few inches while she's in there she comes back out, drags it up to the entrance again, and goes back in to check. Apparently you can keep her in that loop basically forever. I think the forever part may be exaggerated by whoever first told the story, but the shape of it is the point: behavior that looks purposeful from the outside but is a fixed routine with no exit. Somebody coined a word for that, I want to say it's "sphexish." And then people started asking the obvious follow-up: are we sphexish too, just with a longer loop? Is the difference between her and me just the size of the program? Which then runs straight into free will.

Here's what I want from you: restate that whole cluster — rigidity, flexibility, the wasp, and the free-will question hanging off it — in the vocabulary I actually use. Be faithful to the original, don't flatter my framework by pretending it already contains everything.

Specifically:

1. Translation. What is sphexishness as an RL/control concept? Is it a finite-state controller with a tiny state set? A policy whose conditional branching degenerates — one observation that resets everything? A lookup table? A model-free policy with no meta-level on top of it? Tell me which of those is the right target, which are approximations, and where they're approximations rather than the thing.

2. Flexibility. I suspect my field's notion of it (generalizing to unseen states, robustness, adaptation, meta-learning, open-endedness) is not the same thing the philosophers are after, even though the words match. Show me where it lines up and where it quietly changes the subject.

3. A friend of mine offered a picture I can't tell is real or made up: describe the wasp as a policy that's a function of essentially one bit — "am I at the burrow entrance or not" — and say flexibility should be measured by how many such bits you need to describe the policy at a given timescale, so that everything looks flexible if you zoom in far enough and rigid if you zoom out. Is there anything in my vocabulary that cashes that out, or did he invent it? If it's real, tell me what it costs.

4. Free will. Take the disagreement about determinism and free will and restate it as a disagreement about what kind of object a policy is — deterministic function of history, function of history plus noise, something with a level that can represent and modify its own policy. Say how much of the original survives.

5. The residue report. For each of the above: what translated cleanly, what I had to fudge to get it across, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Then the interesting part — is the residue a real gap in the RL vocabulary, something my framework just can't represent, or is it a sign the original claim was vaguer than it sounded once you try to make it precise?

Don't do the cheap move where you map "free will = stochastic policy" or "flexibility = entropy" and call it a translation. If that's genuinely the honest answer, say so and then tell me what gets lost.

And tell me what would change your mind. Where's the weakest joint in your own translation — the step most likely to fall apart under pressure? What argument or consideration would flip you from "the residue is a real gap in the RL vocabulary" to "the residue is hot air," or the other way around? Be honest about your confidence rather than confident about everything.

American English, please.
