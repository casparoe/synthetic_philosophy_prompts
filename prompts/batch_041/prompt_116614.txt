I am assembling a specification for a theory of the value of knowledge, and I want the requirements stated before any candidate theory is assessed. Set out the desiderata that any adequate theory of why knowledge is more valuable than true belief must satisfy, giving for each the case or consideration that motivates it.

At a minimum, the list must cover: the traveler to Larissa, whom knowledge and mere true belief guide equally well; a practical-equivalence case of your own construction, in which knowledge and a true belief would produce the same action and the same outcome, so that no difference in value could be instrumental; a belief that is true only by luck, and so falls short of knowledge, yet serves its holder as well as knowledge would; a belief that is knowledge but was reached by an epistemically disreputable route, so that its value cannot lie in the route's being admirable or defensible; and a case in which what is valuable about knowing is its licensing of assertion or of reliance by others, as in testimony. It must also accommodate the first-person deliberative difference: the knower is entitled to treat the matter as settled and to stop checking, whereas the mere true believer is not.

The theory must respect, and must not collapse, the following distinctions: the value of a belief-state versus the value of the act of forming it; instrumental versus final value; the value of truth versus the value of justification; the value of knowledge versus the value of understanding; and the value of the components (a true belief, a reliable method) versus the value of the composite. It must also say whether the excess value of knowledge comes in degrees or as a threshold.

Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are commitments of a particular camp — reliabilist, virtue-epistemological, evidentialist, and so on. Identify the pair of requirements you judge most likely to be jointly unsatisfiable, and give the argument. Finally, state which single requirement eliminates the most of the going theories, and why.

The purpose is a specification strong enough to grade candidate theories in a graduate seminar on epistemic value; I will do the grading myself, so keep the weight on the requirements and the cases behind them.
