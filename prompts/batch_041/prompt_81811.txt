Iv been mulling over something I keep calling a "hermeneutical windfall" — the mirror image of hermeneutical injustice. The rough idea: a group's experience is so thoroughly baked into the shared stock of concepts, idioms, stock plotlines and default assumptions that its members never hit the blank-page problem when making themselves understood; the interpretive tools are pre-cut to their shape and everyone else is expected to meet them there. Compare that with Fricker's hermeneutical injustice — I want the seams between the two, not a summary of either. Is the windfall just the absence of hermeneutical injustice, or a seperate phenomenon with its own machinery? Can the two come apart in practice, e.g. a group that has the windfall and still eats a lot of testimonial injustice? Who actually bears the harm on each side, and is "harm" even the right frame for the windfall? What does the explanatory work in generating the asymmetry — sheer demographics, historical power, something else? And which of the two would you expect to be easier to fix, and why? Give me exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro or outro.
