My mate Rob is a historian and we've been having the same argument for about three years now, and I don't think either of us has moved an inch. It started because I build little agent-based simulations for a living (boring stuff, mostly supply chains) and I showed him a toy model where you nudge the starting conditions a hair and the whole thing ends up somewhere completely different — and I said, that's history, for want of a nail. He said no, that's a lava lamp. His line is that the nail doesn't matter, the horseshoe doesn't matter, what matters is that a war was coming anyway because of the alliances, the harvests, the class structure, pick your favourite; my line is that if you could rerun the tape you'd get something else, so the nail mattered. We've read the same books by now. Nothing has changed. And I've started to suspect we aren't actually disagreeing about the thing we think we're disagreeing about.

What I want from you is a diagnosis rather than a verdict on who's right — what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Some options I keep turning over, and I'm definately not just fishing for the one that makes me right. One: it's a real disagreement about determinism — except my models are deterministic and I still want to say the outcome was contingent, so that can't be the whole story. Two: it's about what counts as an explanation — Rob wants structures and general trends, I want counterfactuals (if not for the nail...), and maybe we're each marking the other's answer against the wrong exam paper. Three: it's verbal, we mean different things by 'contingent' — half the time I think I mean unforeseeable, half the time not-inevitable, and occasionally I think I mean 'no general law would have told you in advance'. Four: it's about which counterfactuals are even legitimate — he says you can't change one thing and hold everything else fixed, because the rest of the world wouldn't have sat still, and I say fine, that's literally what a model is for. There's also a thing that nags me: Rob says the nail can't be the real cause because the war had loads of causes and none of them was necessary on its own, but that seems true of every cause in the list, so does that make the nail more important or less? Tell me which of these is doing the real work and how you can tell — and if the whole thing is a muddle of two or three different questions wearing one coat, unmuddle it.

Finally: what would actually count as progress here? A distinction we're missing, a better toy model, a change of question, or just the honest admission that we're answering different questions and should stop? If the answer is that no amount of argument will settle it because it was never one question, say so plainly and tell me which question each of us is really asking.
