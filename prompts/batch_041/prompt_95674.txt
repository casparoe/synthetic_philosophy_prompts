ok so I've been trying to get straight on the indicative vs counterfactual conditional distinction and I keep bouncing between two pictures of what the difference even is, and I can't tell if they're rival theories or just two vocabularies for the same thing.

Picture one: both kinds are strict conditionals over a domain of worlds, and the difference is only which worlds get quantified over. The indicative restricts to worlds compatible with how things actually are / what's known; the counterfactual restricts to worlds that come out closest to the actual world under some similarity ordering, and the past-tense morphology ("were", "had") is just the device that shoves you off the actual world. Same machinery, different domain, and the whole indicative/counterfactual split is downstream of that.

Picture two: a conditional isn't a claim about a set of worlds at all. To hold "if A then B" is just to be disposed to raise your confidence in B upon coming to accept A -- a credence-transfer story -- and a counterfactual is the same move with the supposition flagged as one you aren't actually making or take to be false. No world-ordering, no truth conditions of its own.

Then someone in my reading group floated a third thing and I can't tell if it's a genuine option or pictures 1 or 2 in new clothes. On her view the split is about common ground management: an indicative offers the antecedent as a candidate addition to what we're all assuming, a counterfactual marks it as explicitly not-for-the-common-ground. No claims about closeness of worlds, no claims about credences -- the difference is entirely in what the speaker proposes we do with the antecedent.

Compare the three for me, and tell me whats actually at stake between them rather than just restating each. Concretely, run them against a case the textbook examples never touch: a fair coin that hasn't been tossed yet. "If it lands heads, I win" and "If it landed heads, I'd win" both seem sayable before the toss; neither antecedent is false or contrary to fact yet, and yet they don't feel interchangeable. Same shape with a pending job application: "If I get the job..." vs "If I got the job...". Does the world-similarity picture have anything to say when there's no actual-world fact for the antecedent to be remote from? Does the credence picture have anything to say when nothing is being supposed as false? And does the common-ground picture earn its keep here or is it a redescription of one of the others?

Finish by saying plainly which picture you think is carrying the most explanatory weight, and which one is most likely to be a verbal variant of another.
