Ok so I'm a backend engineer, no real philosophy background, and a friend roped me into a little reading group on hedonism and now I'm stuck on something I can't stop chewing on. The whole view seems to be that pleasure is the good thing, or what makes a life go well, but then when you actually press on what pleasure IS everybody just points at a different thing and moves on. A hot shower. The first sip of cold water when you're actually thirsty. Finally fixing a bug you've been staring at for three hours. A backrub. A song that gives you chills. Winning an argument online. The relief when a headache finally lifts. That gross little schadenfreude when a rival team loses. Scratching a mosquito bite. The buzz of anticipation before a trip. Half of those seem to have almost nothing in common, and I keep suspecting the word is doing like five jobs at once.

So before I read anyone's actual theory, I want the spec. Write me the list of things any decent theory of pleasure has to pull off. The cases it has to get right (relief feels like a nasty one, since it's basically the absence of a bad thing and yet it's plainly pleasurable, and people who enjoy pain is another). The distinctions it can't just wave away — liking something vs. merely craving it, feeling pleasure in the moment vs. judging afterward that you enjoyed it, bodily pleasures vs. the "higher" kind like solving a hard problem or finally getting an idea, pleasure as a distinctive sensation vs. pleasure as an attitude you take toward something. And any facts it can't contradict: I know from a podcast that people with depression can be unable to enjoy basically anything, and that some addicts keep chasing a thing they don't even like anymore, and both of those seem like constraints on what pleasure could be. For each requirement give me the case or consideration that forces it, and tell me which ones are genuinely non-negotiable vs. just what one camp happens to care about.

The part I most want: whether some of these requirements can't all be satisfied at once. If they clash, say which ones and what that implies — I half suspect the heterogeneity thing means "pleasure" is either a big disjunction or just a family-resemblance word, but I might be totally off. And if it's cheap, tack on a quick scorecard of the main families of view against the list — the "pleasure is a special feeling" crowd, the "pleasure is just whatever you want" crowd, the ones who say it's a kind of attitude — mostly so I can see which single requirement does the most work in killing them off. No readings or citations please, I just want the spec.
