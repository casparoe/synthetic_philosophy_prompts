I have been reading the material on amplified deliberation for years, and I have finally written down the package of views I actually hold about it. Before I build anything on top of them I would like an audit, because I suspect they do not all fit together and I cannot see where the fault lines are. Here is the package.

(1) The thing I ought to believe is what I would conclude under sufficiently extended, well-structured deliberation. This is not a heuristic for me; the ideal is the target. The HCH construction -- a human answering a question by delegating sub-questions to copies of themselves, each with a full budget of its own, and then combining the answers, so that the whole can outrun any individual inside it -- is the formalization of that target I trust most.

(2) That ideal is built out of copies of me, so it is agent-relative all the way down. It inherits my priors, my dispositions, and my sense of what counts as a good reason, and there is no external vantage point from which it could be judged and corrected.

(3) Where the ideal and my present belief come apart, the ideal is authoritative. My present belief is just an early, noisy sample of the ideal, and deference runs from the sample to the target, never the other way.

(4) Deliberation earns its power by factoring, and the intellectual work is in the factoring: any question worth deliberating about can be broken into sub-questions that a human can settle in a single sitting, each of whose answers can be checked more cheaply than it was produced, and the achievement is in carving the problem so that this becomes possible.

(5) The ideal's answer does not depend on how the question was factored. If it did, then the ideal would be an artifact of an arbitrary choice, and there would be as many ideals as there are factorings, which is to say none at all.

(6) The ideal is a limit, not an algorithm. I am not claiming there is a finite procedure that computes it, or even that it is computable in principle; it is what the deliberation converges to, if it converges.

My suspicion is that some of these cannot be held together as stated, but I honestly cannot tell which pairs are load-bearing and which merely look as though they clash. Please audit the set. For each pair that is genuinely in tension, tell me how severe the tension is and what the cheapest revision would be that restores consistency, along with what that revision costs me elsewhere. For each pair that only appears to conflict, say why it dissolves. Then rank the real tensions by severity, and tell me which single commitment, if I gave it up, would clean up the most.
