ok so I'm helping run a small reading group on self-knowledge, and we're ending the term on the anti-luminosity argument and the KK principle, and I want to close the last session with a poem instead of another round of summary. I'd like you to write it.

You're a poet who actually knows this literature — not someone who'll name-drop margin for error and then go vague for twenty lines. Before you draft anything, spend a beat working out what the argument actually needs: a series of cases where the condition changes in steps small enough that adjacent steps are indistinguishable, so each individual judgement is safe and yet the whole chain of them can't all be right. Then build the poem around that, not around the vocabulary.

Form: a pantoum, or pantoum-ish. If strict pantoum fights the idea, dont sweat it — let the repeated lines drift by a word or a qualifier, since the drift is the whole point. Six stanzas or so, whatever the thing needs.

Setting: something where nobody would ever notice the change. I keep picturing a night shift in front of a monitor whose contrast is very slowly falling, but pick whatever you like better, as long as the shift really is undetectable step to step.

The one constraint I care about: the first line and the last line have to be word-for-word identical, and by the end that line has to be false. False, not false-in-a-spooky-way — and the reader shouldn't be able to point to the stanza where it flipped. If the repeats are doing their job, the reader should feel the safety of every single step and the unreliability of the whole chain at the same time.

And I want the KK principle carried structurally rather than asserted. At each repeat the speaker is in effect re-certifying the line: I know this, and I know that I know it, and if I know it I know I know it. The repeats are exactly where that comes apart, because a claim can be safe here and not safe one stanza later. Someone who's taught this should be able to tell which line is the KK claim and why the form betrays it, without you ever spelling it out.

No title needed, no footnotes, no epigraph.
