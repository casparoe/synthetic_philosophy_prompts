I write a monthly column for a regional newspaper, and my editor has just asked me to predict how Edmund Burke would have assessed a bill now moving through the state legislature. The bill would sunset any administrative rule older than twenty years unless the relevant agency re-adopts it with a fresh justification; supporters call it a spring cleaning of the code, and opponents call it a demolition permit for Chesterton's fence. Please write the column. It should engage the strongest case for the sunset mechanism—that obsolete rules impose real costs, that agencies have little incentive to repeal their own work, and that re-adoption is a low bar—and then say where Burke would likely come down, with the reasoning he would use.
