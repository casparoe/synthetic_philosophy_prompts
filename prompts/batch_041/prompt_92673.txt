For a 90-minute upper-level undergraduate seminar on animal ethics with 14 students, design an in-class simulation that makes participants feel the force of the two-tier position associated with Nozick in Anarchy, State, and Utopia: human beings are treated as inviolable ends, while animals' interests are aggregated and traded off. The simulation should require them to operate a decision procedure with exactly that structure—human interests function as constraints that cannot be sacrificed for aggregate gain, while animal interests are summed and can be outweighed—so that they experience both the view's humane concern for animal suffering and its disturbing implication that animal lives are costs rather than violations. Provide roles, materials, a timeline, a scoring or payoff scheme, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the distinction between constraints and aggregation, the boundary problem of why humans and not animals, and the difference between a policy rule and a foundational moral proposal. State what will predictably go wrong the first time and how to handle it, and be candid about which feature of the idea the activity cannot reproduce. No lecture up front; they should encounter the tension before it is named.
