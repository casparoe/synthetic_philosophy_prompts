I'm redoing my intro ethics week on The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas, because the students just say "utilitarianism bad" and stop thinking. I want to run it through the Knobe effect instead. My hunch: the citizens of Omelas are all silently reciting the chairman's line -- I don't care about the child, I only care about the city's happiness -- except Le Guin never lets them say it out loud, and she leaves it open whether the child's misery is a side effect of the whole arrangement or the actual mechanism of it. So read the story as if it were one of the side-effect vignettes and tell me what the work on intentional action predicts we'd say about the citizens. Do we get the same asymmetry, i.e. that they harmed the child intentionally but didn't intentionally make anyone happy? How much does it matter wether the child is a tool or a byproduct? And the ones who walk away -- they know about the child and do nothing, so by the same logic are they intentionally harming it too, and does the story want us to see it that way? Tell me where this reading breaks down, and what the story does that the chairman vignette can't -- my guess is it makes you, the reader, one of the citizens, since the narrator keeps refusing to describe the city, which is a bit like an under-specified vignette where you fill in the norms yourself. No citations needed, just argue it.
