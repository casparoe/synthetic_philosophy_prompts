I am a third-year archaeology student enrolled in a seminar on method and theory, and I have been asked to chair the question period when our program's visiting speaker joins us next month. Her work defends an inference that several of my classmates find suspect. She argues that certain prehistoric communities drew a distinction between objects and places reserved from ordinary use and those available for it, and that this distinction can be read directly off the archaeological record without any assistance from texts. Her central evidence is a recurring pattern of deposition: pits, ditches, and foundation contexts containing arrangements of objects that are disproportionately complete rather than fragmentary, placed deliberately rather than discarded, and located at thresholds, boundaries, and points of entry or transition where a straightforwardly functional reading seems strained. She is forthright that she reasons by analogy from better-documented societies, and that this is where she attracts the most criticism. She is also careful to say she is inferring practice, and only cautiously belief.

I would like eight to ten questions that engage the substance of this position rather than inviting her to restate it, with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. Please make sure the sequence includes at least one question each on the following:

First, the criterion that is supposed to separate her deposits from other non-functional deposition. Abandonment, caching, storage loss, and simple waste can all leave complete objects in odd places, so I want to press her on what independent mark distinguishes her pattern from those, and what she would accept as evidence that a given pit was simply backfill.

Second, formation processes. I want a question that forces her to say how she rules out that the pattern was produced by a single rapid burial event or by gradual natural accumulation, and how she would tell a deliberately arranged assemblage from one that only looks arranged after the fact.

Third, the warrant for the ethnographic analogy. Does the analogy generate hypotheses that the material then tests, or is it doing evidential work on its own? What would count as a case where the analogy fails and the material still supports her reading?

Fourth, whether belief is really the target at all. If she is content to describe recurrent practice without attributing any mental content to the practitioners, I want to ask what the sacred-and-profane framing adds that a description of practice alone would not, and whether the belief claim is doing any work or is merely decorative.

Fifth, the move from repeated deposits to institutions. Can she get from a pattern of deposition to something like a specialist role, an inherited office, or a group with authority over the practice, and if so what in the material record licenses that step rather than merely permitting it?

Finally, a closing question on what would falsify her interpretation, phrased so that she cannot retreat to the position that the framework is simply a way of seeing.

I would also like one question that puts a specific pressure on the whole approach rather than on a single step: suppose a colleague working at a different site reports the same depositional signature but also has textual evidence showing that the deposits were made for entirely mundane reasons. Does that defeat her inference at the second site, does it cast doubt on the first, or does it leave both intact, and why?

For each question, give me the follow-up in the form of the reply she is most likely to make and the question that should come next. I would ask that you be explicit about which parts of this debate are settled among specialists and which are genuinely contested, and that you not present a contested position as though it were the consensus. Where you are unsure how the field divides, say so rather than smoothing it over.
