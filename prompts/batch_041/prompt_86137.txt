So I've been going down a rabbit hole on Community Notes and I want to know what the thing is actually committed to philosophically. As I understand it, a note only gets shown if it's rated helpful by raters who normally disagree with each other — cross-partisan agreement is the validity condition, not a majority vote or a moderator. Meanwhile the same app ranks your main feed by what keeps you scrolling, which is arguably teh best bubble-construction machine anyone has ever built.

I've internalised the bubble vs echo chamber distinction enough to think it matters here: a bubble is a gap in what reaches you, so you've simply never encountered the counterargument; an echo chamber is a gap in who you trust, so you do encounter it and it makes you *more* confident, because outsiders have been pre-labelled as liars or shills.

So what does the Notes design presuppose, given that distinction? Does it bake in the bubble diagnosis (exposure is the fix) rather than the chamber one (exposure backfires), and does that change whether it can work? Does "helpful to people who disagree" presuppose a consensus theory of truth, a reliability proxy for it, or just a triage rule that a correspondence theorist could run unchanged? Is the bridging requirement committed to both sides being roughly equally error-prone, or is that incidental? And do the engagement feed and the note system presuppose compatible pictures of what a reader is doing, or does one quietly deny what the other assumes? I definately want the implicit theory reconstructed, plus a note on where it's underdetermined — which rival views of truth and disagreement would license the identical mechanism.
