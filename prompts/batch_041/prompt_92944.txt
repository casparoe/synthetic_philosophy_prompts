I am a teaching fellow for a seminar on the causes of war, and part of my job is to anticipate the instructor's rulings before I lead a section. She has a settled procedure for every conflict the seminar covers: she names the parties, ranks them, and specifies what kind of responsibility each bears. Her rulings so far, as I have recorded them, are these. The Second World War: Germany bears primary responsibility, though she insists that this is the responsibility of a regime and its military leadership rather than of a people, with Italy and Japan carrying secondary shares; the 1939 pact and the interwar appeasement she calls failures of prudence that fall short of responsibility for the war. The Korean War: North Korea bears primary responsibility, with the Soviet Union as an enabler whose approval made the attack possible; she rejects the suggestion that the United States invited the attack by drawing its defensive perimeter, on the ground that an omission is not a cause. The Falklands: Argentina bears primary responsibility for resorting to force, but Britain carries a share for having withdrawn the signal that it would defend the islands, which she calls negligence rather than aggression. The Peloponnesian War: she declines to name a primary culprit at all, treating the war as the product of one power's growth and the fear it produced in the other. The 2003 invasion of Iraq: the United States and Britain bear primary responsibility, a verdict she maintains even while granting that the war would have been equally their responsibility had the intelligence been sound.

Next week the seminar reaches the July Crisis, and she will deliver her ruling on who, if anyone, is responsible for World War I. Predict that ruling and argue for the prediction. State the rule or rules that best explain her earlier rulings; state at least one rival rule that fits those rulings just as well; say what each candidate implies for 1914, which state or states it singles out, and how the shares would fall; say which rule you think she is actually running and why; and name the case or piece of evidence that would most cleanly separate the rivals. I also want her pinned on the kind of responsibility at stake -- causal, moral, or legal -- and on whether it attaches to a state, to a government, or to individuals. She does not accept the answer that it all depends on what one means by responsibility, so do not end on that note: commit to a verdict, and say what the verdict commits her to. I am not asking for the state of the historiography; I am asking what her own rule yields. Please write in American English.
