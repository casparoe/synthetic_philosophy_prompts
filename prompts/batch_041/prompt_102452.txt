The question I want the tree to answer: what makes it the case that an utterance counts as *doing* something — promising, naming, firing, apologising, opening a meeting — rather than merely describing or reporting? Root it in the one thing everyone in the debate seems to agree on (roughly: some utterances aren't true-or-false reports of what's already the case, they change it), and then branch on the assumptions people actually disagree about.

Some constraints, because otherwise I'll get the textbook four-way split and it'll be useless to me:

- Every node has to be answerable by a student who has no idea which leaf is downstream. So no node like "are you a Searlean?" — nodes should be about cases, or about what a view has to say about a case.
- I want at least these leaves: (i) the act is constituted by an antecedently existing convention or procedure; (ii) it's constituted by the speaker's intention to undertake or commit to something; (iii) it's constituted by uptake — the audience recognising it as that act; (iv) it's constituted by the speaker's standing or authority within the practice; (v) it's just a move that shifts what's now permitted/required/what counts as settled, no psychology required; (vi) it's individuated by its effects rather than by anything internal to the utterance. Add whatever else the space forces you to add, and if two of mine collapse into each other, say so instead of padding.

Cases the tree has to be able to handle — these are from my notes, please use all of them:
