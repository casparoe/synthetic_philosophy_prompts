I keep going back and forth with a mate about whether you can call a famine a political crime or whether it's just a natural disaster with a body count, and it's dawned on me we're arguing without any agreed test for when a story about a famine is actually any good. So before we go any further, write me the desiderata: what any adequate theory of famine causation has to deliver.

The cases it has to get right (Bengal in 1943, the Irish famine of the 1840s, the Chinese famine around the Great Leap Forward, the Soviet one in the early thirties, and at least one case where a food crisis was headed off or a famine didn't happen even though the food numbers said it should have), the distinctions it can't collapse (famine vs chronic undernourishment; excess mortality vs food availability decline; cause vs culpability; a policy that intends starvation vs one that simply doesn't care; market failure vs state failure; killing by omission vs killing by commission), and the findings it has to sit consistently with (that the rural landless often starve while grain leaves the region, that mortality clusters among the very young and very old, that the size of the shortfall is a poor predictor of the death toll).

Two things I especially want in the list. First, it has to force an attribution split: most famines are a natural trigger plus a political amplifier, and the theory needs to say something about the seperate weight of the two rather than defining one out of existence. Second, it has to say what would falsify the political reading -- a genuine food-availability collapse with no excess mortality, say, or a famine under a government with a free press and competitive elections, since the claim that famines don't happen in democracies is doing a lot of quiet work in this debate.

Then flag any pair of requirements you think can't be met at once, and tell me which single requirement knocks out the most candidates. Finish with a few lines on how the food-availability approach, the entitlement approach, and the "it's political violence" framing fare against the list -- brief, I don't need the full scorecard, I'll do that in the pub.

For calibration: I want a test sharp enough to decide whether "famine as political crime" is a causal claim or a moral one, not just a description of famines.

British English please.
