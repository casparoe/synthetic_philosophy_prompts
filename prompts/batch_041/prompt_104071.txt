I'm applying for a one-year research fellowship and need a two-page proposal. My area is decision theory, read mostly through the blogs and forums rather than through the journals, and the case I keep returning to is the Damascus one. I'd like the proposal built on it, but I want the question stated so that it isn't another lap around the causal-versus-evidential track.

Here is the version of the case I have in mind, put in my own words so that we're working with the same object: a man in a city learns that Death has already fixed on a place to meet him, that Death's expectation was formed before the man decides, that Death has never been wrong about such matters, and that the man's own choice will match that expectation whichever way he chooses. He must decide whether to stay or go.

My half-formed idea is that the standard treatments take for granted that this is a decision problem with well-defined options, each carrying a well-defined value, and I suspect that assumption is where the trouble lives. Three things bother me. First, if the prediction is perfect, then the value of each option is a conditional expectation computed from a joint distribution with no off-diagonal cases -- the man's action and Death's expectation are one fact described twice -- so the comparison may be undefined rather than merely difficult. Second, a perfect predictor looks like it requires a deterministic subject, but a deterministic subject has no unrealized alternative whose value could be weighed, while if the choice is genuinely open, perfect prediction of it looks impossible. Either way, the case as stated may not be a decision problem at all, and the real work is to identify the nearest coherent problem and to say whether the lessons people draw from the incoherent one survive the repair. Third, on the causal story the recommended action seems to flip abruptly as the predictor's reliability approaches certainty, which makes me doubt that certainty is a benign idealization rather than a different problem wearing the same name.

Turn this into the proposal: the question stated so that it has a determinate answer; why the going positions -- both the causal and the evidential camps, and those who think the case is about whether a decision is ratifiable rather than about outcomes -- leave it open; what I would actually do, month by month, across the year; what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and an honest estimate of scope, including whether the third worry is a separate paper.
