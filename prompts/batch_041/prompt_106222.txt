The episode I want analyzed is the British Columbia Citizens' Assembly on Electoral Reform and the two referendums that followed it. Here is what I understand to be the case, so that your answer does not depend on your recalling details: the assembly was created by the provincial government in 2003; it had 161 members, consisting of one man and one woman randomly selected from each of the province's 79 electoral districts plus two members representing First Nations communities and a chair; members were chosen by a civic lottery, with the selection process structured to balance gender, age, and geography; the assembly went through a learning phase with expert presentations, then a public consultation phase with hearings and written submissions, then a deliberation phase in which it voted to recommend replacing the existing first-past-the-post system with a form of single transferable vote it called BC-STV. Its recommendation was put to a referendum in 2005. A majority of 57.7 percent of voters province-wide supported the recommendation, and it carried a majority in 77 of the 79 electoral districts, but the government had legislated that the referendum would only be binding if it reached 60 percent province-wide, so no change followed.
