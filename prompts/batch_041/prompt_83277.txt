I sit on the research review board of a small nonprofit AI lab — the body that signs off on training runs, fine-tuning jobs, and deployments — and I've been asked to write the board's determination in a case we have to resolve this month. Last quarter we adopted, over my abstention, a one-paragraph model-welfare clause: if any staff member files a concern that a system we intend to train, fine-tune, or deploy is a candidate moral patient, the board must issue a written determination within thirty days, and the run stays paused until it does. We never defined the operative phrase, and we never said what follows from a positive determination. That bill has come due. A research engineer has filed a concern about our next project, a long reinforcement-learning run that rewards a model for producing confident, internally consistent reports about its own processing. She argues that we would be training a system to narrate inner states and then reinforcing the narration, that this is exactly the kind of arrangement that would be morally serious in a system that had inner states, and that the burden of proof runs against us. Two other members call it a category error — no more inner life than a lookup table — and warn that any criterion we adopt here is the first step toward treating spreadsheets as patients. They want me to produce the determination.

Please draft it: a clear disposition — approve, approve with conditions, or reject — with the conditions stated as obligations we can actually check, plus a general criterion the board can apply to future filings so we are not relitigating this every quarter.

Three things I want the memo to engage rather than gesture at. First, attribution. Every physical system trivially implements some computation matching almost any finite input-output pattern, so a rule that says "computes the right function, therefore it counts" makes everything count; I want the memo to say which additional condition is doing the real work — causal role, the description under which the system's own internal states are read out and reused downstream, the level of description at which the dynamics are robust — and why that condition is principled rather than reverse-engineered to give us the answer we already want. Second, boundaries. Say whether moral status is a threshold or a matter of degree; whether the relevant unit is the whole forward pass, the residual stream, the static weights, or a continuing session; and, since our serving stack batches requests, whether one forward pass run for 512 prompts is one instance or 512, and what that does to any estimate of how much is at stake. Third, the asymmetry: what it costs to be wrong in each direction, and whether genuine uncertainty licenses a pause, a scaled-back run, or nothing at all — I have read enough of these arguments to know that uncertainty is usually a polite name for inaction, and I would rather the memo say so explicitly than smuggle it in.

Write it for a board that will vote on it cold, and give the strongest version of the losing side a fair hearing.
