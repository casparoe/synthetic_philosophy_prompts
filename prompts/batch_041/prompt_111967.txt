My friend Ana is in the third year of an ecology PhD and we ended up arguing for about two hours last night, and I'm still annoyed, because I think she's right about something and I can't say exactly what. It started with her tomato plants — she said they've started throwing roots out sideways because they're 'looking' for water, I said thats just a cute way of talking, and she said fine, but then what about a termite mound: the shape of the thing solves a ventilation problem, the colony suffocates if it doesn't, so you can say the mound is right or wrong about something, it can succeed or fail at what it's doing, and no termite has the faintest idea what a ventilation problem is. Her claim was that the reason is still there and still doing work in the explanation, it just isn't stored in anybody's head. I said that's what people say when they want to sound like they've explained something, and she said if it were only a way of speaking then it couldn't be right or wrong, and it can be.

Anyway now I see the same shape everywhere, including in places I'm pretty sure are actually different. A price in a market seems to 'know' how scarce something is and nobody worked it out. There's a little model at work that taught itself a way of doing the task that nobody designed and that I'm pretty sure isn't written down anywhere inside it, it's just sitting in the weights — but you can still ask whether what it's doing is a clever way of doing the task or a stupid one, and the answer isn't up to me. A seed sits all winter not sprouting, and the reason it doesn't is the frost that hasn't happened yet, which nothing in the seed is thinking about. And the thing I use to check myself: on my street everyone double parks on Thursday mornings because of the street sweeper, and that's obviously not the same thing at all, because every single person double parked for their own reason, they just all happen to have the same one. I can tell when it's the other thing, I just can't say what the difference is.

So is there a name for this? And I don't mean just the word — if there's an established term I want to know what it actually covers, because I can see at least three spots where I can't find the edge. One, if a human engineer builds a gadget and knows exactly why every part is there, is that the same thing, or the opposite of it? Two, if the behaviour was learned rather than evolved, no long history of anything being selected for anything, just a thing that stumbled onto a good trick — does the reason evaporate? Three, and this is the one that gets me: when two totally different stories fit the same behaviour equally well, is there any principled way to say which one is the real reason, or is it just a story we tell afterwards because it makes us feel like we've understood? Ana reckons I'm dressing up a metaphor, and half of me agrees with her. If there is a term, tell me what it is and, more to the point, what it isn't — how it differs from just saying a thing has a purpose, and from saying a machine is behaving like it worked something out. If there's no term, make one up, but then tell me exactly which of my examples it covers and which it doesn't. I'd rather have the boundaries than the label.
