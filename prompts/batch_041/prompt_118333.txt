I teach a course on character and I keep bumping into a puzzle I don't have the vocabulary for, which is getting embarrassing. I'm in ethics, not metaphysics, so be gentle with me.

Here's the pattern. I say someone is generous, or brave, or honest, and then the behaviour doesn't show up — and there turn out to be several very different ways for it not to show up:

(a) The trait is gone exactly when the moment arrives. He's generous in every conversation about generosity, and the one time I actually needed money the generosity wasn't there — not overridden, just absent. He says he doesn't know what happened to him either.

(b) The trait is intact and the moment arrives, but something outside stops it. He reaches for his wallet and the account is frozen. Ask a second earlier and he'd have given it to you.

(c) The trait only shows up because the moment arrives. The crisis is what makes the team resilient; before it there was no resilience to speak of, and if the crisis had never come we'd never have called them resilient. And yet 'they'd have coped if it had come' still seems true.

(d) The trait is intact, the moment arrives, and nothing is standing in the way — nobody's frozen anything, nothing interferes — but nothing happens, because something the trait needs just isn't there. A tree that would fruit in decent soil, sitting in bad soil. No blocker, the background is just wrong.

I once sat in on a metaphysics reading group where someone used the word 'finkish' about a case like (a), I think, and never explained it, and when I asked a colleague he said 'oh yeah, finks and masks, that's a whole literature,' which was not helpfull, because he couldn't tell me which of my four cases is which, or whether (c) and (d) are anything at all rather than sloppy versions of (a) and (b).

So: what are the established names, which of my cases do they actually fit, and what's left over? I don't want the labels so much as the joints — where you cut and why. And I especially want to know which of these cases makes real trouble for the reading my students and I keep sliding into, that 'disposed to X' just means 'would X if the trigger came', because if one of my cases shows that reading is wrong I want to be able to say which one and how. If (c) or (d) has no name of its own, coin one, define it, and tell me what it rules out.
