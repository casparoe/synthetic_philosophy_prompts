I work as an analytics engineer, which means my job is to model data into a warehouse: a fact table at the finest grain I can get, dimension tables around it, and a semantic layer on top in which every metric is defined once and then rolled up to whatever grain a stakeholder asks for — region, month, plan tier, cohort. The whole discipline rests on a promise I have started to doubt: one source of truth, one definition per metric, and every level of aggregation consistent with every other. I keep hitting cases where that promise fails in a way that is not a bug. A support-ticket fact table, for instance: overall, tickets routed through the automated triage queue appear to resolve faster than tickets handled by a human, but within each product area the comparison reverses — automated is slower everywhere — because the automated queue takes a disproportionate share of the areas where tickets are quick to close no matter who handles them. My tech lead calls this a data-quality problem and wants it fixed in the metric definitions; a statistician I complained to called it Simpson's paradox and said the fix is to ask the right causal question, after which exactly one of the two rollups is correct. I have since gathered that people who think about causation argue about this: one camp says the paradox is arithmetic and dissolves once you specify which variable is being intervened on, another says the choice of grain is a normative choice about whose comparison matters, so that both numbers can be right answers to different legitimate questions. What I want is not a survey but a translation.

Restate both positions as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use — fact tables and grain, rollups and drill-downs, additive versus non-additive measures, materialized views, query plans and aggregation pushdown, metric definitions and lineage, the "one source of truth" ideal. I am fluent in that vocabulary and largely self-taught on the statistics, so do not soften the engineering side and do not assume I have the causal-inference side. Be explicit about where the mapping is exact and where you are fudging it: is a causal graph really anything like a lineage graph, is an intervention anything like a backfill, and does "conditioning on a stratum" have an honest counterpart in a system that only ever reads the rows it was handed? Then give me the part I care about most, what does not survive the translation, and here I have a specific worry to test. I half-remember that in some settings an association can fail to be stable across strata even when there is no confounding whatsoever, because of how odds ratios behave under conditioning; if that is right, it seems to cut against the tidy story that causal structure settles everything, and I want to know whether my vocabulary can even represent that kind of trouble or whether it is invisible from inside the warehouse. For each piece of residue, tell me whether it is a genuine gap in my framework — something the warehouse cannot express — or a sign that the philosophical claim was vaguer than it sounded and does most of its work by staying outside the schema. Close with what I should actually do on Monday: is there a metric definition, a metadata convention, or a discipline of asking for the grain before the number that would capture the surviving content of the debate, or does the honest answer require me to tell stakeholders something a semantic layer can never encode?
