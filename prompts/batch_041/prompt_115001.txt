Two different things go by the name of emergence in the literature on reduction, and I want them pulled apart before I go any further with them. Call the first limit-emergence: a higher-level description becomes exact, or arbitrarily accurate, only as some parameter is taken to a limit — system size and particle number to infinity, lattice spacing to zero, Planck's constant to zero — so that the higher-level regularities are not convenient approximations but, in the limit, as exceptionless as the lower-level laws they are supposed to rest on. Call the second coarse-graining emergence: no limit is taken at all; one simply declines to track fine-grained detail, and the resulting description turns out to be stable, predictive, and robust across vastly many micro-realizations, while remaining approximate and admitting exceptions.

Compare the two, in the following order. (1) Are they genuinely distinct, or is the first merely the idealized limiting case of the second, so that there is one phenomenon here and not two? (2) Which of them, if either, has a claim to ontological rather than merely epistemic emergence, and what settles that question besides terminological preference? (3) Can either underwrite downward causation? If so, state exactly what must be true of the lower-level dynamics; if not, say what the demand for it gets wrong. (4) How does each fare against the classical derivational model of reduction, in which higher-level laws are derived from lower-level ones with the aid of bridge laws — which of the two can that model absorb, and which resists it? (5) Where does a renormalization-group treatment of universality belong on this division? That is the case I find hardest to place, and I would rather you place it than dodge it. (6) Finally, which of the two does the real explanatory work in the sciences usually cited as emergent, and is that the same sense of emergence that philosophers of mind have in view?

Close with a table, one row per pattern, with exactly these columns: Pattern; Source of the higher-level description's authority; Behaviour in the limit, or the absence of one; Reducible by derivation (yes, no, or qualified, with the reason in brief); Supports downward causation (yes, no, or qualified, with the condition); A scientific setting where it plainly appears; Verdict (real emergence, apparent emergence, or neither). Keep the prose compact; the table should be usable on its own.
