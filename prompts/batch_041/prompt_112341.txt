I've been covering the free will wars for years and I want to do something other than another he-said-she-said. Get a committed compatibilist and a committed libertarian — the kind who thinks compatibilist "free will" is a bait-and-switch that keeps the word and drops the thing — to write the joint statement they'd both actually sign after a real adversarial collaboration. Not a dialogue, the document: the claims both can put their names to, including about what "worth wanting" is even asking and which variety of free will does the work we care about; then the precise points where they still split, each written so that side would accept the wording of its own view; the cruxes; and for each crux a case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. And here's the part I actually care about. Run both of them against a case the stock examples don't cover: a future AI agent that's reasons-responsive, has the right kind of history, but no felt experience at all. Would either call its freedom the kind worth wanting, and does that split them or cut across both camps? If part of their fight turns out to be about words, tell me which part and why.
