Our housing co-operative — eight households, unincorporated, and with no outside body that we have agreed to appeal to — is rewriting its governing document, and we have reached a difficulty we cannot talk our way out of. The document is meant to be wholly self-amending: any article, including the article that says how articles are amended, may be changed by a two-thirds vote of the members present at a meeting of which a week's notice has been given. It also contains an eternity clause: three provisions — one household, one vote; the building may never be sold to a private landlord; no household may be evicted except for non-payment — that no amendment may alter. And it creates a returning officer who decides all questions about whether an amendment was validly adopted, with no appeal from that decision. A developer has now offered us a great deal of money, and six of the eight households are tempted.

What are the most important implications of a rule system's being able to rewrite its own amendment rules — for what an eternity clause can really accomplish, for what the returning officer is actually deciding, for the standing of acts done under an amendment that is later ruled invalid, and for what we eight households can honestly promise one another? I am not asking for a survey of what jurists have said about self-amendment; I want to know what follows for this document.

Please organize your answer as follows.

Section 1 — The structure, restated (about 120 words). Say back what is load-bearing here, and correct me if I have misdescribed my own document.

Section 2 — The implications, ranked (about 800 words). The four or five most important implications, each under its own heading, in order of importance, each ending with a sentence that begins "For our document, this means..."

Section 3 — What does not follow (about 200 words). Two or three conclusions that people commonly draw about self-amending rules which do not actually follow in our case.

Section 4 — Bottom line (about 80 words). The single change we should make before the next meeting.
