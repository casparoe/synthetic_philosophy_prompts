I'm running a small reading group on consequentialism and I keep getting tangled up in the "cluelessness" thing — the worry that for basically any action we can't possibly know the total long-run consequences, so consequentialism can't tell us what to do. I want a one-page decision-tree handout that starts from someone who already accepts something minimal, like "the rightness of an action depends only on its consequences," and walks them through branch questions to a named position on what cluelessness means for that view.

Each branch node should be a question the reader can answer without already knowing where it leads, so nothing like "are you a fanatic?" I want nodes covering at least: whether the thing being judged is a single act, a rule or disposition, or the whole pattern of someone's choices over a life; whether the view treats consequentialism as a criterion of rightness or as a decision procedure; whether cluelessness is "we don't know the actual outcome but can still assign credences" or "we genuinely have no probabilities to assign"; whether the standard is expected value or something else; whether the unknown far-future effects are thought to roughly cancel out or to be systematically tilted one way; and whether the reader treats the implication "a one-in-a-gogol chance of astronomically huge good outweighs any certain small harm" as a reductio of the whole approach or as a bullet they'll happily bite.

Leaves should carry the name of the position where there is a name (the label is enough, no need to attribute it to anyone or cite anything) and "unnamed" where nobody really defends it, each with one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Also tell me which positions the tree can't actually seperate — I suspect the "expected value is fine, we just need credences" view and the "we should follow robust heuristics" view might come apart on a question my tree doesn't ask, so flag that if so — and which combinations of answers are unoccupied, with a guess at whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. The point is to sort people by what they actually say about the cases, not by the labels they like.

Keep it to about a page, and don't lean on any literature (I won't have the papers with me and neither will they). Last line should be a one-sentence summary starting "Bottom line:".
