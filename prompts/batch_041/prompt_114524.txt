"Computational functionalism is the idea that what makes something conscious isn't what it's made of but what it does — the right kind of information processing. So any system running the right program is conscious, and if it's conscious it can be harmed, so it deserves moral status. The problem is a mathematical result showing that every physical system implements every computation, because you can always map its physical states onto the program's states in a way that fits. Which means a rock, or a bucket of water, implements the same program your brain runs. So functionalism says a rock is conscious, which is obviously absurd. That's why the whole 'it's the computation that matters' thing falls apart, and why you can't settle an AI's moral status by asking what it computes — you have to look at the physical substrate it's made of. It's the same lesson as the Chinese room: a simulation of pain isn't pain, and shuffling symbols around doesn't give you understanding."
