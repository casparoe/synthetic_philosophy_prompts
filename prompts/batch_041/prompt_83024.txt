Mathematicians routinely work with the same objects under constructions that cannot all be correct at once, and treat the resulting differences as beneath notice. The natural numbers are identified with the finite von Neumann ordinals in one book and with the finite Zermelo numerals in another; the ordered pair (a, b) is coded as {{a}, {a, b}} here and by some other device there; the real numbers are Dedekind cuts in an analysis course and Cauchy sequences in the course next door. Nobody apologizes and nobody takes the two treatments to contradict each other. Yet the practice has a rigid side that is just as much a part of it. The question whether 3 is an element of 17, which the two treatments of the natural numbers answer differently, is dismissed as an artifact of notation rather than a question about numbers; 3 < 17 is treated as a fact about numbers that no construction could overturn; a statement such as the twin prime conjecture is treated as true or false whether or not anyone will ever prove it; and a theorem proved about Dedekind cuts is read without further argument as a theorem about the real numbers, and therefore about the Cauchy sequences as well.

What does this practice commit us to? Reconstruct the conception of mathematical objects it presupposes, given that it treats the several constructions as interchangeable representations of something that is not any of them. Say which rival views it quietly rules out — in particular the unpretentious reductionist who holds that the natural numbers simply are the von Neumann ordinals and that 3 is therefore an element of 17, and the formalist who holds that there is no fact of the matter about either 3 ∈ 17 or the twin primes. Then separate what the practice strictly requires from what merely sits comfortably with it: could a structuralist, a conventionalist, or a fictionalist about mathematical objects license everything the practice does, including the determinacy of unproved arithmetical statements? And if the objects are not the constructions, how does the practice's own epistemology work? The only route to knowledge it recognizes is proof followed by transfer along an isomorphism, and I want to know what conception of mathematical knowledge that presupposes, and whether it answers the familiar difficulty about knowledge of abstract objects or only relocates it. Finally, tell me which parts of the practice would have to be abandoned if we took the reductionist reading seriously and fixed a canonical construction, and which parts would survive a constructive or anti-realist reading of the truth-value claims. I take it this is adjacent to what is called Benacerraf's dilemma, but the standard statement of that problem is framed as a difficulty about numbers and knowledge; I am after what this everyday working practice presupposes, and whether it can consistently have everything it takes for granted.
