I write a column on science and ideas, and I have been working on a short piece about Skolem's paradox. I would be grateful for your comments on the draft below, particularly on whether the central distinction I draw is sound, whether I have the mathematics right, and whether the piece is genuinely interesting to a philosophically literate reader or merely rehearses the standard line.

Draft:

The paradox is usually stated like this. The downward Löwenheim-Skolem theorem says that any first-order theory with an infinite model has a countable model. Set theory, if consistent, has an infinite model, so it has a countable model. But inside that model there are sets -- the set of real numbers, for instance -- that the model itself regards as uncountable, because no bijection between them and the natural numbers exists in the model. From the outside, however, the model is countable, so those sets are countable too. The model says uncountable; the metatheory says countable. That looks like a contradiction.

The standard response is to say that uncountability is relative to a model, and that there is no absolute fact of the matter about whether a set is countable. I want to argue that this response, while correct as far as it goes, obscures what is really troubling about the paradox. The paradox is not primarily about the relativity of set-theoretic notions. It is about the gap between a formal theory and its intended interpretation. The axioms of set theory do not pin down a unique model, and the Löwenheim-Skolem theorem shows that they cannot pin down even the cardinality of the model. What the paradox reveals is that our formal resources underdetermine the ontology we take them to describe. The model's internal notion of uncountability is not false; it is indexed to a model. The mistake is to think that the intended interpretation is somehow guaranteed by the axioms. It is not.

I then want to say that this is a problem for a certain kind of platonism, but not for structuralism or formalism. If you think that set theory is about a unique universe of sets, then Skolem's paradox is a genuine threat, because it shows that your axioms cannot distinguish that universe from a countable one. If you think that set theory is about structures, or about the consequences of axioms, then the paradox is a curiosity, not a crisis.

My questions: Is this a fair way to frame the paradox? Have I misdescribed the role of the metatheory? And is the final distinction -- platonism threatened, structuralism not -- too neat? I am particularly interested in whether the paradox has a bite for structuralism that I am missing, and whether the phrase the model's internal notion of uncountability is doing more work than it can bear.
