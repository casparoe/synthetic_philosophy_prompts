I am assembling a taxonomy of positions on the relation between knowing how to do something and knowing that something is the case. I have five candidates, each given by a single claim:

- Retrieval: all apparent practical know-how is ordinary propositional knowledge together with a reliable capacity to retrieve and apply the relevant propositions at the moment of action.
- Indexical: the know-how is itself propositional, consisting of indexical knowledge of how to proceed here, now, with these means.
- Dispositional: the know-how is a non-propositional disposition to form and act on the right propositions under the right conditions.
- Model-based: the know-how is the possession and manipulation of a non-propositional model or simulation of the task, which accounts for performance better than anything the subject could state.
- Irreducible: some know-how is non-propositional and cannot be reconstructed from any propositions the subject could in principle grasp.

Rank these five by how much you would trust each as the correct account of the relation, with a sentence or two of justification for each placement. Then say which one you would drop from the taxonomy, which pair is closest to each other and what single case would best separate them, and which of the five does the most explanatory work in accounting for a skill that is performed fluently but degrades when the performer tries to say explicitly what she is doing.
