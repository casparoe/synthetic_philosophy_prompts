Write me a deadpan product review — the kind you'd get on a consumer tech site — for a home 'sentience meter': a little gadget you point at an AI, a dog, or your flatmate and it gives you a score from 0 to 1 for how morally considerable the thing is. About 600 words, first person, and let the reviewer slowly work out what he's actually holding without ever naming it.

The funny has to come from the real problems and not made-up ones: that the thing can only ever test for the functional markers (reportability, integration, recurrent loops) and never for whether anyones home; the old headache that under the right mapping just about any system implements just about any computation, so the meter is really reading the reviewer's own criteria back at him; the lopsidedness where a false positive costs you a bit of caution and a false negative costs you everything; and the small detail that the reviewer is himself a system the meter could be turned on.

Dont say what's being satirised — keep it straight. It should be funny to someone who's done the philosophy of mind and still readable to someone who hasn't. British English please.

Finish with a short afterword (a hundred words or so) flagging where the piece leans on a contested reading or exaggerates a real position, and what would actually change your mind about the target — which argument or finding would make you think the satire was aimed at the wrong thing.
