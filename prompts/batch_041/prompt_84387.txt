Write an essay on a controversy in the theory of imprecise credence: whether a rational agent's doxastic state can be a set of probability functions rather than a single sharp distribution. Weigh the strongest considerations on each side and reach a verdict, with reasons; do not close on a non-committal note. Do not supply citations, a bibliography, or appeals to what published authors have said — the argument must be carried by the case and the stipulations below.

The case. An experimenter presents two opaque urns. Each urn contains 100 balls numbered 1 to 100, and in each urn exactly 60 of the balls are red and 40 are black. The agent knows this about each urn separately. Both arrangements were produced by a single machine whose settings she is not permitted to inspect, and her evidence is exhausted by the two colour counts: nothing tells her whether the same numbers tend to be red in both urns, whether they tend to be red in one and black in the other, or whether the two arrangements have nothing to do with each other. The experimenter will choose a number uniformly at random and report the colour of that numbered ball, first from Urn B, and then, if asked, from Urn A.

Stipulate that the agent's credal state about the pair of colours at the chosen number is the set of all joint distributions over that pair whose two marginals are both 0.6, and that she updates by conditioning every member of that set on what she learns.

Before any report, her credence that the chosen ball in Urn A is red is exactly 0.6, and it is 0.6 under every member of her set; a bet at even odds on that outcome is better than declining under every member. The experimenter then reports that the chosen ball in Urn B is red. Her credence that the chosen ball in Urn A is red now ranges from 1/3 to 1: if the two arrangements share all 60 of their red numbers, the report makes red in Urn A certain, while if they share only the 20 numbers that they must share, the report lowers her credence to 1/3. The even-odds bet on Urn A is no longer better than declining under every member, though it remains better under some — and this shift was produced by evidence she had no basis for connecting to Urn A at all.

The essay must take up, at a minimum:

- Whether the change just described is a genuine discovery that her evidence about Urn A was incomplete, or whether it shows that a set of probabilities cannot be the state of a rational agent, given that she began with a determinate credence about Urn A and an observation about Urn B alone unsettled it.
- Whether the case is better explained by saying that her state before the report was mis-specified — for instance, that the little she knows about the machine's settings should have been converted into some definite distribution over possible relations between the two arrangements, in which case nothing is unsettled.
- What she is now required or permitted to do about the even-odds bet: whether it is acceptable that no option is required, whether a rule that maximizes the minimum expected utility is warranted, and whether any such hedge reinstates a sharp credence at a higher level — a definite distribution over the members of her set — so that imprecision is abandoned rather than defended.
- Whether an agent in this position may rationally prefer not to hear the report at all, and what that shows. (Note that under a rule maximizing minimum expected utility, the report reverses her choice about the bet.)

Conclude with a verdict on the general controversy. If rational credence may be imprecise, say what the agent is required to do about the bet and why the case is not a counterexample. If it may not, say which feature of her state, or which stipulated claim, has to be given up, and what should take its place. State the strongest objection to your verdict and answer it.
