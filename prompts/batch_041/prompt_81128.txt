I'm closing out my Zhuangzi seminar next week with a poem rather than a lecture, so I'd like you to write it for me. The moves I want it built out of: the great smith casting bronze who'd call it a bad omen if the metal jumped up and demanded to be made into a sword; him drumming on a tub and singing after his wife dies; the talking skull. So: 16-20 lines, four or five stanzas, free verse, spoken by the bronze — metal being poured into a mould, metal that has been cast before and will be cast again. Two constraints, and they're the whole point. First, no word for death anywhere in it — not death, dead, dying, gone, lost, passed, buried. The arguement has to be carried entirely by casting and cooling and recasting, and by the seasons. Second, every stanza has to end on the exact same line, word for word — the same metal in a new mould — and its sense has to shift each time without one word of it changing. And it must not console: he doesnt tell you it's fine, he just moves the camera, so if my students walk out feeling soothed I've wasted the last ten minutes of term. Someone who knows the text should recognize the moves; someone who doesnt should just think it's a good poem.
