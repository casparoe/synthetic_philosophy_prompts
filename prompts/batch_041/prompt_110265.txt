my friend and i have been arguing about the utility monster for like two years and i want to force us to actually write down where we stand. she's a totalist -- she thinks the monster is a coherent possibility, that its welfare is just more of the same stuff the rest of us have, and that if it existed we'd be obligated to hand it everything. im the other one: i think the monster argument is basically a reductio, and what it really shows is that "summing" welfare across different subjects isnt a real operation at all, that the total is a bookkeeping fiction. write the joint statement we could both sign after a genuinely good-faith collaboration.

what i want in it: (1) a numbered list of claims we both accept -- about what the monster argument does and doesnt establish, about what a welfare total would even have to mean for it to be the thing we're maximizing, and about which parts of our fight are really about value versus about metaphysics. (2) the disagreements, each stated in wording the side it belongs to would actually sign off on for itself -- no strawmen and no weasel phrasing that makes either of us look dumb. (3) the cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions where settling one would move at least one of us, and for each crux a case or argument we'd both agree bears on it, plus what each of us predicts about it, written down in advance. format it as the numbered agreed-claims section and then a disagreements table. dont bother citing anyone, just write it as the two of us.

two things i want built in. first, make the doc distinguish monsters of degree from monsters of kind: a monster of degree is just like us but scaled up, more of the same welfare, a bigger multiplier; a monster of kind has a welfare that isnt commensurable with ours at all, its good isnt more of our good but a different thing. i suspect that distinction is doing a lot of hidden work in the debate, so i want the doc to say which of our arguments survive if only degree-monsters are on the table and which ones need kind-monsters.

second, run both our positions against three cases the doc should spell out in its own words so theres no ambiguity. (a) an opaque monster, one whose welfare we could never independently verify, because every sign it could give us would be identical whether it was in bliss or in agony. (b) a hive monster, where the "monster" is really a big collective whose members each have modest welfare but the whole thing is supposed to count as a single subject. (c) the reverse case, a being of enormous suffering rather than enormous bliss -- does our whole exchange just flip, and if it does, is that a feature or a bug of each side? for each case give each side's verdict and the principle it falls out of.

and if you think part of what we've been fighting about is just verbal -- that we mean different things by welfare, or by adding up -- say exactly which part and why, and whether fixing the words would actually dissolve the fight or just move it somewhere else.
