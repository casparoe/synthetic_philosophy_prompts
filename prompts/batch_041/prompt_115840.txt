I'm a science-and-ideas journalist, and I've somehow talked my way into being the outside speaker at a theology faculty seminar next week. What I'll be defending: the standard knock-down objection to penal substitution — "you can't punish an innocent person, that's just scapegoating dressed up" — actually misses, because the doctrine only ever needs the *penalty* to move, not the guilt. Split those the way you'd split a debt from the debtor and the transfer looks no stranger than a guarantor paying off someone else's loan; the creditor isn't wronged and nobody has to pretend the guarantor did the crime. I got the germ of this from a short David Lewis piece years ago and I've been running with it ever since, so it's half his and half mine by now (I don't have the paper in front of me and I won't be quoting it).
