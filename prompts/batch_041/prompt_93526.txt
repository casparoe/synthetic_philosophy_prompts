My friends and I got into a long, slightly drunken argument last week about whether you can ever be sure of what you're feeling, and it reminded me of something I half-remember from a philosophy podcast — the claim that basically no condition of the mind is luminous, meaning that even when you're in it you might not be in a position to know that you're in it, because there's always some nearby situation where you're in a very slightly different state and you'd swear on your life it was the same one. The host also brought up the KK principle, that knowing something is supposed to entail knowing that you know it, and said it definately breaks down in exactly these borderline cases. I'd like to see that whole idea as a story instead of an argument, because I think the argument is easy to nod along to and the story is where it gets uncomfortable.

So here's what I want: a short story set in a small tea-processing plant in the hills above some generic South Asian town, where a woman named Farida has spent twenty years as the one person who decides whether a batch of tea has picked up a faint smoky taint from a dryer that runs a little hot. Her entire professional identity rests on being able to taste the difference, and the taint is genuinely there, but so slight that the gap between a clean batch and a tainted one is smaller than the gap between two batches that are both perfectly clean. I want the story to live in that margin — the moments where she signs off on a batch and then, walking home past the drying sheds, honestly cannot say whether she knew it was clean or merely felt confident, and where the KK principle quietly falls apart in her hands. Don't turn it into a lecture with characters as mouthpieces; make it a life, with the plant, the coworkers, the smell of it all. It can end however it wants, but I'd like the final beat to sit on the impossibility of being certain rather than on any kind of resolution.

Please write it as continuous prose — no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, just the story the way you'd tell it to a friend over email.
