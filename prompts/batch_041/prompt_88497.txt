ok so I have to give the commencement address at the little college where I teach psych, and every draft turns into the same speech about trusting your gut and knowing who you really are, and I've been reading enough of the introspection stuff lately that I feel like a fraud standing up there saying it. My rule for myself is: nothing in the speech that someone who actually works on this would call false, and no leaning on the idea that your inner voice already knows the answer. But it still has to be a real commencement speech for a mixed crowd — the graduates, their grandparents, a couple of trustees, nobody in that field has read any of this and I don't want to lecture them. What I want the thing to turn on is that the reasons you give for your own behaviour are mostly a story you assemble after the fact, while your attention, where it's actually pointed right now, is something you can still trust — and the hard part is making that land as a gift to an eighteen-year-old rather than as nihilism. Keep the research out of it, no names or studies, carry it on one example from ordinary life. About eight minutes. Then, after the speech, a short note just for me: what in that picture is genuinely settled among people who study it and what's still being fought over, so I don't stand up there and hand them one camp's line as if it were the consensus.
