I'm presenting at our metaphysics-and-biology reading group next week, defending origin essentialism — the thesis that you couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg. But I'm not running the standard Kripkean "it just seems impossible" line; my actual argument is that zygotes/embryos have no trans-world identity conditions, so there's no possible world in which "your" zygote is a numerically different one, and I want to use the messy empirical cases — monozygotic twinning, chimerism, and now three-parent / mitochondrial-replacement embryos — as support for that rather than letting them be counterexamples.

The room: two philosophers of biology who work on biological individuality and are allergic to modal intuition-mongering, one committed four-dimensionalist who thinks persons are space-time worms and origins are properties of stages, one Humean who thinks all de re necessity is just a projection of our naming practices, and a handful of generalist metaphysicians who'll mostly poke at the trans-world identity machinery.

Give me the Q&A prep sheet: the seven or eight objections I'll actually get from this crowd, ordered by how likely and how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way that person would put it, with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft.
