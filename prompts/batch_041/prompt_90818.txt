So I've been going back and forth on the adaptationism wars — Gould and Lewontin's spandrels thing and everything downstream of it — and I've landed somewhere I'm not totally comfortable with, so I want you to try to knock it down.

The view, stated as strongly as I can make it: for basically any given trait, the default assumption should be that it is NOT an adaptation. Selection is mostly a weak tidy-up force, and the real reasons organisms are built the way they are are developmental and historical constraint plus a lot of frozen accident. The burden of proof sits with whoever wants to call something an adaptation, and in most interesting cases that burden isnt met — the thing is a byproduct, a lock-in, or drift. I already know about the move of splitting adaptationism into a method ("it's fine to go looking for adaptation") vs an empirical claim vs a big-picture explanatory thesis, and I'm telling you I mean the empirical/explanatory version, not the harmless methodological one.

What I want:

What would actually count against this? Not "adaptationists disagree" — I mean the specific observations, experiments, formal models or case studies that, if they'd come out differently, would sink the view, and which of those have arguably already happened. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about the gap between "this is evidence selection did something here" and "this is evidence selection is the main story."

Which of my predictions have actually been tested? A view like mine should stick its neck out. What does it predict that a moderate adaptationism doesn't, and how has that gone?

The unfalsifiability worry, which is my real fear: I suspect "it's a spandrel" can be bent to fit anything, same as "it's an adaptation" can. If that's true, is it a problem for my view in particular, or for the whole dispute? And if the honest verdict is that I've quietly qualified the thing into something untouchable, just say so rather than being polite about it.

What would someone who genuinely holds my view concede counts against them, as opposed to what a critic would throw at them? I want my own side's exposure, not the cheap shots.

And here's the part where I suspect my view actually falls apart: constraint and adaptation seem to bleed into each other. Constraints get built by selection; selection only ever acts through constraints; a frozen accident can later get co-opted for a function and become a real adaptation. If that bleeding is real, does "adaptation or spandrel" even have a determinate answer, or does the whole distinction need replacing with something else? If so, with what — and is that good or bad news for me?

One more, and it's the thing I'd most want you to be straight about: what would move *you* personally the most, and why that rather than the dozen other candidate objections. For what it's worth, what would move me most is a case where the spandrel reading is just flatly wrong and everyone can see it — but I have a nagging feeling I've set the bar so that nothing can clear it, and I'd rather you tell me that than help me keep believing it.
