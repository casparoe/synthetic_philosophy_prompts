I never went to collage and I've been chewing on this one for a couple years, so bear with the way I put it. Everyone agrees you should keep your promises, but I want to know why a promise actually sticks. What is the thing that makes it bind? I keep running into four answers and they don't fit together:

1. It's about the other person counting on you. They rearranged their life, so breaking it hurts them. (So no harm, no foul?)
2. It's about a kind of claim or authority you hand over. When I promise, I give them a say over me, and breaking it wrongs them even if they never find out and it costs them nothing.
3. It's about the practice. Promising is a social tool, like money, and the duty comes from the rules of the tool, not from the one act.
4. It changes my reasons directly. The promise itself is a new reason, not a report about someone else's expectations.

I made up a few cases to test these and now I can't let go of them.

The ghost promise: I promised my grandfather I'd finish the book he was writing. He's dead now. Nobody knows about the promise, nobody's counting on it, and no one would be hurt if I dropped it. My gut says it still binds, and that bugs me, because answer #1 says it shouldn't.

The release: my friend promised to help me move. I later told them honestly to forget it, I've got it covered. They said they still feel like they owe me. Are they right? Does my letting them off actually dissolve the thing, or does a promise have a life of its own once it's out of my mouth?

The stranger: you promise your spouse you'll do something, and twenty years later you're genuinely a different person, different values, and you'd never make that promise now. Does the old you get to bind the new you? And here's the one I really can't sort out — imagine an app where you put $500 in escrow and if you break the promise the money goes to a cause you hate. Does that make the promise more binding, or does it turn a moral reason into a bribe and stop being a promise at all? A friend says it's just insurance, and insurance is what you buy when you don't trust the promise in the first place.

So: write me an essay on this. Weigh the four answers against each other and against my cases, and don't wimp out at the end — land on a verdict about what actually makes a promise bind (it's fine if the answer is "a mix, and here's how the mix works," but say which piece is doing the real work and why the others can't carry it alone). Then after the essay, give me a short structured summary as a JSON object with these fields:

- "verdict": one sentence, your bottom line on what makes promises bind.
- "cases": an array, one entry per case above (ghost promise, release, the changed person, the escrow app), each an object with "case", "binds" (one of "yes", "no", "it depends"), and "why" (one or two sentences).
- "strongest_objection": the best reason someone could give against your verdict.
- "what_would_change_my_mind": what evidence or case would actually make you abandon the verdict.
