A friend and I have spent three evenings arguing about whether Gödel's incompleteness theorems have any legitimate life outside mathematics, and next week I am interviewing someone whose answer is yes, so I want the questions to be worth the tape. His position is deliberately modest: he rejects the old anti-mechanist reading and claims only that any rule-governed practice rich enough to encode statements about its own rules and its own derivations — his running example is a legal system that can adjudicate the validity of its own enactments — cannot be both consistent and complete, so there will always be cases it neither authorizes nor forbids, a phenomenon he insists is distinct from vagueness or open texture. Draft eight to ten questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer he is most likely to give. I want at least one question on whether his practice really satisfies the hypotheses of the theorem — an effectively enumerable axiom set, an effective proof predicate, and enough coding power to run the diagonal construction — and one on the intensionality worry: whether the undecidable case is a feature of the practice or an artifact of the particular formalization we choose, since different ways of defining provability for the same system need not agree. I also want one on his casual assumption that consistency is the relevant virtue for a legal system at all, given that actual systems resolve conflicts by priority rules rather than exploding, and one on whether the self-trust he says is impossible is genuinely blocked or merely deferred to a stronger practice that can prove what the weaker one cannot — and whether that makes his point a case of incompleteness at all, or rather of the reflection phenomenon, that a system cannot prove that whatever it proves is correct unless it already proves the thing outright. Close with the question I expect him to dread: what would count as a counterexample to his claim, rather than as evidence that the practice in question was never the right kind of system to begin with?
