I am a PhD student in philosophy writing a chapter on standing to blame, and before I read further into the literature I want the desiderata any adequate theory of standing must meet, not a survey of positions. Write the requirements: the theory must distinguish standing from warrant and from blameworthiness; it must explain why hypocrisy sometimes undermines blame and sometimes does not, including the case where the hypocrite's criticism is correct; it must handle the reformed wrongdoer, the concurrent wrongdoer who is also a victim, the beneficiary who blames others for a system he upholds, the meddling third party, institutional and role-based blame, the blamer who is not in a position to make a difference, and the case where the blamee waives the standing objection; it must say how standing is lost, regained, and transferred; it must distinguish standing to blame from standing to resent, punish, and forgive; it must say whether there is standing to praise and why the asymmetry with blame exists; and it must avoid both global skepticism about blame and the trivialization of hypocrisy while accounting for the social function of blame. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, mark it as non-negotiable or as a commitment of a particular camp, and flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. Do not hedge: commit to which requirements are genuine and which are prejudices of a camp. Then take a definite position on which single requirement excludes the most popular theories, and briefly say how the main families of view fare against the list. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
