Ok so me and a friend were up late arguing about whether living in an infinite universe would make ethics pointless, and we built this argument and now I cant sleep. I think it proves way too much but I can't tell which line to cut.

(1) A world's goodness is the total welfare of everyone in it: the more welfare the better, and you get a world's total by adding up the welfare of all its people.
(2) If you divide a world's population into two groups and nothing is left over, the world's total welfare is the total of the first group plus the total of the second.
(3) In a world with infinitely many people you can always split the population into two groups such that each group on its own is a perfect replica of the whole — take the people in even-numbered positions, relabel them, and the lineup looks exactly like the original.
(4) So the world's total welfare W equals W + W, that is, W = 2W.
(5) Subtract W from both sides and you get W = 0.
(6) So every world with infinitely many people has total welfare zero, which means they're all exactly as good as each other.
(7) So if the universe is infinite, whatever we do to it — save lives, make people happy, torture them — its total welfare stays zero and its goodness never budges.
(8) Therefore in an infinite universe nothing we do makes the world better or worse.

I dont buy (8) for a second; torturing someone obviously makes things worse. But which line is actually the bad one? (1) seems obviously true to me and (3) seems like it's just a fact about counting. My friend says the fix is to stop adding up totals and instead score a world by the limit of the average welfare of the first n people as n grows — I don't know if that actually rescues us, or whether it gives sane answers when welfare keeps climbing forever, or when people's welfare levels are scattered all over the place.

So: which step do I give up, why did it look fine, and what do the main ways out cost me — ditching totalism, ditching additivity over infinite splits, or comparing worlds by something other than a single sum?
