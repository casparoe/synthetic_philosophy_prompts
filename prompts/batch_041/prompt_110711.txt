I'm a novelist — third book, and the one I'm currently two-thirds through has been a problem child for about two years now. It's set at a small regional theater company during a season in which the company is coming apart, and the conceit I've been chasing is that the company itself is a character. Not a symbol of one, not a mood, not a metaphor. An actual somebody whose experience the reader is inside for stretches of the book. My agent keeps asking what it's about and I keep saying "a theater company," which is technically true and also a dodge. Anyway, the drafts keep failing in the same specific way, and I'd like to know whether the thing I'm running into is a known thing with a name, because I'd like to stop experiencing it as my own personal inadequacy.

Here's the concrete version. I can write three hundred individually vivid people in a room — the opening night party, the tech rehearsal, the meeting where the board votes to cancel the season — and what I get on the page is a list, or a mood, or crowd noise with some sharp elbows in it. It never becomes one mind. And I dont mean it's hard to write; I mean that when I push on it, the thing I've made either stops being a group or stops being a mind, and there's no third option waiting. Same deal with a "we" narrator: I write something like we decided to cancel the Thursday show, and every reader I've handed it to takes it either as shorthand for a decision somebody made, or as a sci-fi hive mind, but nobody takes it as the group's own point of view — the group is always borrowing somebody's. Meanwhile the reverse is basically free. I split one character into two voices, or two selves with seperate memories who don't know about each other, and readers follow it instantly, no explanation, no strain. A friend who's in a philosophy PhD program read a draft and said the thing I'm circling is the combination problem that panpsychists worry about. I looked it up and I don't think that's it, or at least it isn't the part that's biting me. As far as I can tell they're asking whether you can build one big experience out of lots of little experiences, which is a question about parts and wholes; what's biting me feels more like: anything I assemble out of subjects has nobody home, and anything I disassemble out of subjects still has somebody home. Why would one direction be free and the other one impossible? Those seem like they should be the same problem read backwards, and they're obviously not.

So what I want is not just a term. If there's an established name for this, tell me what exactly it covers and where it stops — is a jury a case? A corporation? A family that finishes each other's sentences? A marching band? Is the asymmetry itself a named thing, or is it just a consequence of something else, and if it doesn't have a name, coin one and give me its edges. And if the honest answer is that this really is just the combination problem under a different description, then tell me what makes it the same thing despite my feeling that it isn't, and tell me which of the neighboring ideas — emergence, supervenience, group minds, collective whatever — is the nearest miss and how I'd tell in a particular case which one I was looking at. I care much more about the carving than the label.
