I am putting together a position statement for a graduate seminar that sits between epistemology and the philosophy of mind, and I would like it audited for internal consistency before I present it. The commitments I want to defend are the following.

(1) Knowledge requires safety. If I know that p, then p is true, and it is no accident that I believe it: among the nearby possible worlds there is none in which I hold the same belief while p is false. This applies to higher-order knowledge as much as to first-order knowledge.

(2) A certain class of present-tense mental conditions is luminous, in this sense: whenever I am in one of them — a dull ache, a faint ringing in the ears, a tickle at the back of the throat — I am in a position to know that I am in it. Not merely to believe it correctly, but to know it.

(3) For the same conditions the KK principle also holds: if I know that I am in such a condition, then I am in a position to know that I know it, and so on as far up the hierarchy as one cares to go.

(4) These conditions are not all-or-nothing. They come in degrees, and the passage between neighbouring cases is gradual: between a clear case of the condition and a clear case of its absence there runs a long series of cases, each differing from its neighbour only imperceptibly.

(5) Introspection is nevertheless fallible. I can misclassify which condition I am in — take irritation for amusement, or a shiver for a shudder — and no amount of attention guarantees that I get the classification right.

I have a persistent sense that this package cannot be held as it stands, but I cannot find the fault, and I am not sure which member I would be least willing to give up. What I want is an audit, not a survey. Tell me which commitments are genuinely in tension with which others and how severe each tension is, ranked rather than merely enumerated; which combinations only appear tense and can in fact be held together; and, for each real tension, what the cheapest repair would be and what that repair would cost. I am especially interested in whether the trouble lies where I assume it does, and in which single adjustment would resolve the most while conceding the least. Be explicit about which commitment does the damage and why.
