I am presenting at an AI safety workshop a paper arguing that inverse reinforcement learning cannot recover human preferences from behavior, because behavior is always generated under feasibility constraints; what IRL recovers is not the preference ordering but the projection of that ordering onto the feasible set. I call this the feasible-set projection problem and claim it is conceptual, not a mere identifiability issue to be fixed with more data or better priors. The audience will be mostly reinforcement learning researchers who treat IRL as Bayesian inference over reward functions, plus a few econometricians working on revealed preference and cognitive scientists who model planning. Prepare my Q&A prep sheet: the objections I will actually get, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, each phrased the way that person would phrase it, with what I should concede up front, what I should hold, a two-minute reply, and a note on where the reply is weakest.
