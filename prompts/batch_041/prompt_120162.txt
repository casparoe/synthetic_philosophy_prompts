Alright, I want to use you as a dry run before I inflict this on actual referees, because I've got a bad feeling about it. I've been writing on biological functions for years and there's a pile of things I assert in different papers, and it's finaly dawning on me that they might not all be able to be true together. What I want from you is an audit, not a lit review: which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and for the real clashes, what's the cheapest fix and what it would cost me.

Here's the pile.

1. I'm a monist about function. There's one thing the word picks out in biology, and it's the etiological one: a trait's function is the effect it was selected for — the effect that explains why the trait is there in the first place.

2. Function talk is normative. When I say the heart's function is to pump, I'm not merely reporting what hearts do; I'm saying what a heart is supposed to do, and that's what makes 'this heart is malfunctioning' come out true rather than just meaning 'this heart is unusual.'

3. Functions are objective. Whether some structure has a function, and which one it has, isn't relative to whatever a particular biologist happens to be curious about.

4. A trait can be for something the very first time it appears. There's a beetle on an island I keep thinking about: one lineage turns up with a slightly different tarsus that grips the local bark better than the alternatives, and it's the first time this variant has ever existed. I want to say that tarsus is already for gripping. I don't want to have to wait a few generations of selection before it's allowed to earn the word.

5. Meanwhile in my physiology teaching I'm constantly saying things like 'the function of the loop of Henle is to concentrate urine,' where all I really mean is that it makes a certain causal contribution to a capacity I've decided to care about. I take those statements to be straightforwardly true, not convinient shorthand.

So: which of these can't all hold at once? Of the ones that clash, which clashes are real and which are me confusing two different questions with each other? Rank the real ones by how bad they are, and for each tell me what would have to give and what I'd lose by giving it. I'm most confident about (1) and (2), for whatever that's worth, and least clear on how (4) is supposed to work — but I honestly can't tell which seam is the load-bearing one here.
