The upshot, the way he put it, is that you can't fix a reasoner by adding an axiom saying "whatever I prove is true" — that axiom doesn't make it more reliable, it makes it able to derive literally anything, including stuff it knows is false. Trusting yourself in general is incoherent; the only version that actually works is trusting yourself about stuff you already believe. And he says the same wall shows up when you try to trust the *next* version of yourself or some successor agent — which is why people trying to build systems that rewrite their own code and then certify the rewrite keep getting stuck.
