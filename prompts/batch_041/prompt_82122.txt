For my advanced high school philosophy seminar, I am writing a paper on pain asymbolia -- the condition in which a person reports feeling pain but shows little or no aversion to it -- and what it implies about why pain is bad. Before I evaluate any particular theory, I want a specification of what a theory of pain's badness must accomplish. Please set out the desiderata, giving the case or consideration behind each: the need to handle pain that is felt but not minded, pain that is deliberately sought or endured, and ordinary pain; the need to distinguish sensory intensity from unpleasantness, badness for the subject from moral badness, and intrinsic badness from badness that depends on the subject's attitude; and the need to respect whatever constraints on pain's motivational and affective structure are non-negotiable. Mark which requirements are genuine and which are the preferences of one camp, identify any pair that seems jointly unsatisfiable, and say which requirement does the most to rule out otherwise attractive accounts.
