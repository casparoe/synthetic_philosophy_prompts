I am grading a midterm in a graduate seminar on statistical mechanics and information. The exam posed the following problem. A box contains a single classical particle in a one-dimensional chamber of length L, in thermal contact with a bath at temperature T. A demon runs a cyclic protocol, which we called the Kestrel cycle: (i) it measures which half of the chamber contains the particle and records the result in a one-bit memory register; (ii) it inserts a partition at the midpoint; (iii) it lets the particle perform isothermal expansion against a piston on the side it occupies, extracting work; (iv) it removes the partition and resets the piston; (v) it erases the memory register. In class I introduced a two-ledger convention. Ledger G records the thermodynamic entropy of the gas. Ledger M records the Shannon entropy of the demon's memory register, evaluated by an external observer who does not condition on the demon's measurement outcome. The exam asked: Does the Kestrel cycle violate the second law? If not, state where the compensating entropy increase appears, and explain why the compensation is not merely an accounting trick. The student's answer follows.

Student answer:
The cycle does not violate the second law. The demon extracts work k_B T ln 2 per cycle: confining the one-particle gas to half the chamber lowers its entropy by k_B ln 2, and the isothermal expansion from L/2 back to L lets the gas absorb k_B T ln 2 of heat from the bath and perform k_B T ln 2 of work. This work is not free. By Landauer's principle, erasing one bit at temperature T dissipates at least k_B T ln 2, so the net work cannot exceed zero. The compensation appears in Ledger M. After the measurement the memory register has Shannon entropy k_B ln 2, since from the external observer's perspective it is equally likely to read 0 or 1. Erasure reduces that entropy and sends at least k_B T ln 2 of heat into the bath. The measurement step itself also costs k_B T ln 2, because acquiring the information requires the demon to reduce the memory's Shannon entropy; that cost, together with the erasure cost, is what makes the cycle safe. This shows that information is physical: Shannon entropy and thermodynamic entropy are the same quantity, so the demon's information is a real thermodynamic resource. One might object that the argument is circular because it uses Landauer's principle to save the second law, but Landauer's principle is a consequence of the second law, so the argument is not circular.
