I'm a member of a small, fairly close-knit community choir, and for the past year or so we've been trying to be more deliberate about how we handle conflict among ourselves. Last spring we adopted a two-page "accountability and repair" document, drafted by a subgroup I sat on, which lists a handful of things that count as serious harms — harassment, misappropriating the group's funds, and a few others — and lays out a process: a private conversation, an opportunity to make amends, and only if that fails, a vote to suspend someone's membership. I mention all this because it matters that we have a written procedure, and because I helped write it, so I have some stake in how it's being used.

Now something has come up that the document simply doesn't cover. A longtime member — call her Dana — made a remark at rehearsal that several people found belittling: she was teasing another singer about how badly he'd mangled a solo, and it landed far worse than she intended. No one invoked the document or called for any formal process. Instead, within about a week, Dana found herself quietly left off the sign-up sheet for the next performance, uninvited from a section dinner, and on the receiving end of a long group message from several members explaining that they needed "space" from her. She has apologized twice and asked what she could do to make it right, and the replies have been vague. When I asked one of the organizers whether this was fair, she said, "We're not punishing her — these are just consequences of how she made people feel." Dana came to me in tears asking whether she deserves this and how long it's supposed to last, and honestly I don't know what to tell her.

Something feels wrong to me, but I can't articulate it. It isn't that I think what she said was fine, and it certainly isn't that I think anyone has to socialize with someone they don't want to. I'd like you to help me think it through and then tell me what I should actually do — whether to raise this with the group, and if so, what to say. Please don't hand me a list of techniques for hard conversations; I want the underlying question taken seriously. What is this thing that's happening to her, exactly, and under what conditions would it be justified rather than not? Write it as continuous prose, no headings, bullets, bold text, or tables — just a letter I can actually read and think about.
