A friend and I argued late into the night about secession, and I came away with a thesis I would like you to take seriously even if you ultimately reject it: that any principled case for a region's right to self-determination is self-undermining, because the very principle that licenses Catalonia or Scotland to leave also licenses the sub-regions, dissenting enclaves, and Indigenous nations within them to leave in turn, so that the doctrine can be halted only by importing an external premise -- the sanctity of existing borders, or the privileged status of some favored unit -- that the doctrine was supposed to displace. Grant me the thesis for the sake of argument, or at least grant that it has teeth. Describe its most important implications: what it does to the moral standing of the remainder, the people inside a seceding territory who voted to stay; whether self-determination can survive only in a remedial form, as a response to a particular injustice rather than a standing entitlement; and whether any secessionist movement can be coherent in its own terms. Be concrete about where the regress genuinely bites and where it is only a cheap trick.
