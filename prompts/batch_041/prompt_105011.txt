I'm taking a seminar on the metaphysics of physics, and I'm writing a short paper on the hole argument. Rather than reconstruct the standard presentation, I'd like to build a toy case from scratch, and I want you to construct it to the following specifications.

Make the case a small, fully specified discrete world — a finite graph, lattice, or similar arrangement of locations, together with an explicit rule that assigns states to locations and updates them — rather than a continuous spacetime or anything lifted from general relativity. The world should admit two rival interpretations that agree on every observable fact an inhabitant of the world could record, yet disagree about whether the world's future is determined by its past. The case is meant to isolate whether the indeterminism in the original argument is a fact about the world or an artifact of a metaphysical assumption about what it takes for two possibilities to count as distinct, so each interpretation must be one a competent philosopher could actually hold, and neither verdict may be stipulated — each has to fall out of applying the interpretation to the toy rule.

A few constraints. The world must be finite and short enough that I can write it out in a few lines. The two interpretations must be stated precisely. Exactly one feature of the setup should be responsible for the divergence between them; identify that feature and show what happens when it is removed — whether the disagreement collapses or survives, and why. Do not appeal to a preferred coordinate system or to a God's-eye labeling of locations; if the case requires labels at all, make their role explicit and show that they are doing work rather than smuggling in the conclusion.

After the scenario, walk me through the following: what each interpretation says about determinism and why; which feature of the case is doing the work; what happens to the case if that feature is removed; and how a defender of the interpretation that comes out worse would attack the case to stop it from counting. A case that simply asserts the verdicts instead of deriving them will not do, since the whole point is to see where the indeterminism actually comes from.
