Write a short story about two people who share a single external store of memory — one notebook, app, or device that each of them relies on so completely that neither can think through certain problems without it. Make that store the site of a betrayal: one of them edits or erases part of it, and the other experiences the change not as lost data but as a gap in her own recollection. The story should dramatize, without ever naming or explaining it, the question of whether the shared store counts as part of one mind, both minds, or a single mind running on two bodies, and it should leave that question genuinely open, pressed on the reader by what happens rather than settled by a character. Don't let anyone lecture about extended cognition; let the reader feel the disorientation of a self whose edges are someone else's to touch. The strongest version will quietly make the reader notice that the usual way of drawing the line — inside the skull versus outside it — has stopped doing any work.
