I am preparing a position statement for a workshop on truthfulness, and before I defend it I would like an audit of the position itself. What follows is the whole of it, as compactly as I can put it.

(1) A lie is a false signification uttered with the will to deceive — the mind that holds one thing and puts another on the tongue. It follows that one who says what is false while believing it true does not lie, however careless he is; and that a joke or a fiction, which carries in its tone and its setting a plain sign that no deception is meant, is not a lie either.

(2) One may also lie while saying what is true, if one takes it to be false and utters it in order to deceive.

(3) No lie is ever permissible. There is no exception for the lie that would save an innocent life, none for the lie that would turn a murderer away from his victim, none for the lie told to unmask a heretic or to defend the faith. To allow a single exception is to concede that a good end may license an evil means.

(4) What makes a lie wrong is intrinsic to the act and to the agent: it is a rupture in the liar's own soul, a severing of the will from the truth it was made to love. The harm a lie does to others is not what makes it wrong, nor is the good it does what makes it right; that is why the helpful lie is not on that account a permissible one.

(5) Even so, lies are not equal in gravity. The helpful lie — told to benefit another and to hurt no one — is the least grave; the lie told for gain is graver; the lie told to injure is gravest. The grading follows the will behind the lie and the harm in view.

(6) Concealment is not lying. To keep silent, to decline to answer, to withhold from someone what he has no right to know, is not to signify falsely, and may be not only permitted but required.

(7) I take the scriptural deceivers at face value. The Hebrew midwives lied to Pharaoh about the infants; Rahab lied to the king's men about the spies; and scripture sets both before us as blessed. I read these as lies and not as mere concealment, and I do not think that God's blessing rests upon a sin as such.

Please treat this as an audit and not as a defence or a general essay on lying. Work through the commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be; of the genuine ones, order them by severity. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what giving it up would cost the rest of the position; for each apparent one, say briefly why it dissolves. Finally, tell me which single revision would resolve the most, and what the position would look like afterwards.
