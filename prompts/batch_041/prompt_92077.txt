I'm a science and ideas writer — long features, mostly public health and climate — and I've only just realized I've been carrying around a set of views about causation that probably can't all be true at once. Here's my best attempt at putting them down. (i) I'm a realist about causation: there's a fact of the matter about what caused what, and it doesn't shift around with whatever we happen to care about. (ii) The but-for test is the backbone of the whole thing — c caused e only if e wouldn't have happened without c — and I'd defend it as more or less what we're doing whenever we argue seriously about causes. (iii) Absences aren't causes. There's no thing there, no event, nothing doing any causing, so strictly speaking a lack of rain can't kill a crop and a city's failure to open its cooling centers can't kill anyone. (iv) Which of the many but-for conditions we single out and call the cause is interest-relative — we pick the deviation, the thing that wasn't supposed to happen. That's why the story blames the city and not the weather. (v) A real explanation gives you a mechanism, some process running from cause to effect; but-for dependence with no mechanism underneath it isn't an explanation at all, and I think explaining is alot of what my job is. (vi) And I think you can be genuinely to blame for something you didn't do, even if the not-doing isn't a cause of anything.

What set this off: in July I filed a piece about a heat wave in a mid-sized city where a couple dozen people died, mostly old and mostly alone, and the city government had decided not to open the cooling centers it had already paid for. My headline said the failure to open the centers killed them and I stand by it, but (iii) says a failure can't kill anyone, and I honestly can't tell whether I'm just doing the loose-talk thing where what I really mean is the mayor's decision to keep them shut, or whether I've been quietly helping myself to the idea that omissions are causes all along. A editor I argued with about it said the whole question is a parlor game and I should just pick whichever theory lets me blame the right people, which struck me as cheating.

So: which of these really are in tension and how bad — rank them by severity rather than telling me they're all equally in tension, and say for each what would have to give — which ones only look like conflicts once you look closely, and why. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most with the least collateral damage, and what I'd lose by making it. I'm not attached to any one of these in particular, I just want to know where the load-bearing wall is.
