Two friends and me have been argueing about the sunk cost thing for about a year now and I want someone from outside to tell me whats actually going on. The shape of it is simple. One of them says the money, the years, the effort are gone, only the future counts, and honoring a sunk cost is always a mistake. The other says no, sometimes you should keep going: you finish the degree, you keep the failing restaurant open another season, you don't pull out of a war because of the soldiers already lost, you don't walk away from the person you've been with for a decade because it got hard. And we never move an inch. Whatever case gets raised, one of us says thats not the sunk cost doing the work, thats commitment, or thats just the fallacy wearing a nice hat.

I've collected four arguments from people that I think are doing the real work while nobody notices:

- a friend says the sunk cost is a clue. If you put three years in, that's evidence you saw something real that you've since forgot, so going on is a way of trusting your own past judgement.
- someone else says there's a theory where being rational means sticking to the plan you made when you were calmer, and that re-deciding at every fork, every time, is the actual failure.
- my girlfriend says quitting is a kind of disloyalty to the version of you that started it, which I think is really a question about whether you're the same person over time.
- and I read that in real life, as opposed to in the lab, bailing out early is often the worse bet, so maybe the rule of thumb is fine and the experiments are just rigged to make it look dumb.

So: why has this gone nowhere? Are we having a real disagreement about what to do, or are we answering different questions under one name? Is there a hidden factual question, like whether the habit actually pays off in the long run, that would settle it if we knew? Is it a fight about what rational even means, maximising something versus being able to justify your life to yourself? And is sunk cost naming one thing at all, or two, a tendency people have and a mistake people make, with all of us sliding between them? My own guess is that we agree about the facts and disagree about what rational means, and neither of us will admit it.

Tell me what kind of disagreement this is and what would count as progress given that diagnosis. Exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no wrap up.
