So I'm in a small reading group on anthropics and one of the regulars keeps insisting the whole self-sampling vs self-indication business is nothing but an urn. Her version goes like this: the observers are the balls, the urn holds all the observers who ever exist, you are one of the balls, and since nobody got to pick which ball they'd be you have to reason as if you were drawn at random from the urn. So if theory A says the urn holds ten times as many observers who have your evidence as theory B says it does, A is ten times more likely to have produced the ball that is you. That's self-sampling, done, no mystery. And self-indication, on her telling, is just the same story with the balls weighted by how many observers each one stands for, which is cheating, because in a real urn every ball is equally likely. She then uses the urn to settle the presumptuous philosopher: the guy who announces that since theory B gives a gazillion civilizations and theory A gives one, and his evidence is equally likely on both, he's now practically certain of B -- he's just forgotten he's a single ball in an urn, and no ball ever gets to count its duplicates as extra chances of coming out.

I'd like you to audit the urn. Give me the correspondence table first -- what plays the ball, the urn, the draw, the hand doing the drawing, the fact that you're the one holding the ball when it comes out -- then where the picture breaks, and be specific about which breaks are fatal for her two conclusions (self-sampling is the obvious reading, self-indication is a cheat, the presumptuous philosopher is refuted) and which are just decoration. Things that keep tripping me up: nobody drew me, and there was no chance the draw came up somebody else; the number of balls is never observed, it's whatever the theory under test says it is; every candidate ball carries exactly my evidence, so there's nothing corresponding to learning which ball came out; and the reference class problem means we don't even know which urn we're arguing about. Anyways, here's the part I most want answered. If I take her urn literally and install an actual chance mechanism in it -- a real lottery that selects an observer, with each observer's chance of being selected proportional to how many observers there are -- do I get her conclusion back, or do I get the self-indication one? Because it looks to me like the literal version of her own analogy is exactly what she's calling cheating, and that her conclusions only survive as long as the sampling is never a real chance event, which would make "drawn at random" a metaphor carrying the entire argument. If that's right, say so and then tell me what the better picture is, or whether there isn't one and the honest thing to admit is that anthropic reasoning has no good analogy at all because there's no selection going on. A quick word on whether the same urn picture is what's doing the work in the doomsday argument, and whether sleeping beauty is the same picture or a different one, would help too.
