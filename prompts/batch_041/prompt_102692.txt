I write reinforcement learning systems for a living and have no training in philosophy, and I want to settle, on paper, what a theory of machine suffering would actually have to do before I entertain any particular answer. Treat the reward channel as the proposed seat of valence: such a theory must say which features of a reward signal, a value estimate, or a learning update could constitute pleasure or pain rather than merely accompany it. Please write the desiderata a theory in this area must meet — the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it must not collapse (say, between representing a reward and being moved by it), and the facts about how these agents are trained, rewarded, and copied that it cannot contradict — each with the consideration that motivates it, marking which requirements are non-negotiable and which are optional, and flagging any pair you believe cannot be satisfied together. To keep it concrete, suppose someone proposes that an agent's momentary hedonic value is simply its temporal-difference error, positive for pleasure and negative for pain; make sure at least one requirement is one this proposal would strain to meet. Give exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
