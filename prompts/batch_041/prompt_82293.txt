Construct a self-contained thought experiment: a compact fictional episode in an imaginary mathematised science, featuring two rival research programmes and no real scientists, no real theories, and no appeals to the actual history of science. Appraise it using Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes, and engineer it so that the verdicts cut against the usual textbook pairing. Constraints:

- At the moment the episode closes, the first programme is degenerating on Lakatos's own criteria (its newest protective-belt hypothesis accommodates a known anomaly without adding corroborable excess content), yet a rational, methodologically informed scientist must still be right to persist with it.
- The second programme is progressive in the strict sense (its newest belt hypothesis yields excess content that is later corroborated), yet the case must leave it genuinely arguable that this corroboration earns less than it appears to.
- A single variable must be load-bearing: altering only that variable (for instance, whether a certain fact was already used in constructing the programme rather than merely being known in the field) flips both verdicts.
- The episode must span at least two time-slices, so that one fixed body of facts is appraised differently depending on when the appraisal is made.
- No stubbornness, irrationality, or sociological factor may do any work; the scientists must be doing precisely what the methodology licenses.

Give the episode in one paragraph. Then demonstrate that it meets each specification: for each programme, name the Lakatosian element doing the work (hard core, negative or positive heuristic, protective belt, accommodation versus prediction, excess content) and state the verdict at each time-slice. Then identify the single load-bearing feature, say what happens to both verdicts when it is removed, and explain how a Popperian and a Feyerabendian would each attempt to defuse the case. I intend to use this to show what the standard progressive-versus-degenerating example cannot show, so close by stating what the case is a case of.
