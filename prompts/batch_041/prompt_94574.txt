The idea: discussions of transcendental arguments always treat them as a two-step machine — (a) we undeniably do or think X, (b) X is only possible if Y, so therefore Y. If that's right, then either transcendental arguments need a kind of modal premise nobody has known how to supply, or they're a distinctively incomplete form of argument and we should stop asking them to do the work they're usually asked to do.
