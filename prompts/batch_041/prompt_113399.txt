Write a deadpan internal audit report from the "model welfare" office of an AI lab, reviewing one RL training run and deciding wether the agent in it suffered. Keep the corporate-audit form real: scope, method, findings, a dissent, recommendations, about 600-700 words, no winking and no jokes that aren't already in the material.

The office operationalizes suffering as the integral of negative reward over the run, discounted by the agent's own gamma — which drags them into the awkward fact that gamma is a hyperparameter the lab also tunes for performance, so the cheapest way to lower the measured suffering is to train more myopic agents, and the report has to say out loud what it thinks that implies about the metric. Let it quietly take positions on: whether reward is valence at all or just a control signal, whether a policy whose only preference is the reward function can be said to dislike anything, wireheading and reward hacking as self-medication vs self-harm, and whether checkpointing/rollback makes the audited agent one agent or a sequence of them. The recommendations at the end should follow from the findings rather than landing as a punchline.

Should be funny to someone who knows RL and the AI-welfare debate and still readable to someone who knows neither. Invent the lab, the run, and the office's internal jargon; dont name any real lab, product, or person.
