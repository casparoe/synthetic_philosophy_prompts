So my little film-and-philosophy meetup just finished a month on David Lewis — we did the modal realism stuff, possible worlds as concrete universes just as real as ours, counterpart theory, the whole bit about how actual is just an indexical term and every world's inhabitants rightly call their own world actual — and next month I want to pair it with a movie, and I keep coming back to Everything Everywhere All at Once. The lazy pairing is that it's a multiverse movie and Lewis is the multiverse guy, but I'm pretty sure thats cheap and the interesting stuff is where the film and Lewis actually fight. So what I want is a real reading of the film through modal realism, not a "the movie illustrates possible worlds, cool" thing. Where does it genuinely dramatize Lewis — the other universes are real and concrete, not just possibilities, the IRS auditor is a real person somewhere, the rocks on the cliff are a genuine world — and where does it flatly contradict him? Verse-jumping is causal traffic between worlds, which Lewis's own definition of a world forbids (worlds can't causally interact, that's what makes them separate worlds at all), so the entire engine of the plot, borrowing your other selves' kung fu, would just collapse into one big world on his account. And the emotional core, that Evelyn is reaching her daughter across universes and that Joy is still Joy in every one of them, seems to need trans-world identity, which is exactly the thing counterpart theory denies. So is this a modal realist film or secretly an anti-modal-realist one?
