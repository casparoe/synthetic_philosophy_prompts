Ok so I took early retirement from the lab a couple years back and I've been on a philosophy binge since, mostly the seventeenth century, partly because Leibniz is a hero of mine from the maths and physics side and I felt I owed him the metaphysics too. Anyway I've run into a modern fight I'd never heard of and its definately been bugging me.

One side is David Lewis and his counterpart theory: when I say I might have become a doctor, I'm not talking about me in another possible world, I'm talking about a different but similar guy, my counterpart — and the matching-up is loose, not one-to-one, and I can have lots of counterparts. The other side says no, it's the very same individual who exists in the other world, just with different properties (Kripke and co., I think).

What I want from you: predict what Leibniz would have made of this. Which way would he have gone — and don't fob me off with "he'd say the question doesn't arise" unless you're going to spell out exactly what he'd put in its place. I want you to actually work through his own machinery, at least these bits:

- the complete-concept idea — that every truth about an individual is already folded into its concept — and what that does to the notion of the same person showing up in a world with a different history;
- the no-two-indiscernibles principle, and whether it really pushes him toward the counterpart picture or whether it's doing something else entirely;
- whether he'd have any grip at all on the idea that two worlds might differ only in which individuals fill the roles and not in any qualities (people call that haecceitism) — and if he would, which way he'd jump;
- and the God-picks-a-whole-world point, i.e. how much of his answer is theology and how much is logic.

Since he was a natural philosopher as much as anything else, if his views about substance or his monads or his physics would tilt him one way, say so and say why. And finish by telling me where you'd expect him to surprise us — a spot where he wouldn't slot neatly into either modern camp.
