I've been reading about 'program equilibrium': instead of just playing a game, each side writes a program, the programs are handed over so each can read the other's source, and then they play. In a one-shot prisoner's dilemma that can get you mutual cooperation, since each program can be written to cooperate just in case the other one does. What are the most important implications of this for me and four mates who each run a small trading bot, all fighting over the same short-lived arbitrage, and who could each stick our bot's source up on GitHub — even though we've no way of proving that the bot actually running is the code we published? I want the ones that actually bite: what publishing the source really buys us and what it doesn't, whether the trick survives going from two of us to five, and where it stops working when the opportunities are gone in milliseconds and half of us dont read the code we're trusting anyway.
