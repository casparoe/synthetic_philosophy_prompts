I am putting together a ninety-minute session for a graduate interdisciplinary reading group—philosophers, computer scientists, and economists—on decision theory under uncertainty. Most participants can state the difference between causal and evidential decision theory and have seen Newcomb's problem, but almost none have thought carefully about counterfactual mugging or XOR blackmail, and I want the session to leave them able to see why those cases are supposed to put pressure on ordinary decision theory rather than merely being exotic bets. I am not asking you to explain the cases to me; I need a teaching plan. What has to be introduced first for the question even to be well posed—the distinction between a decision's causal consequences and its evidential bearing, the difference between acting in a branch and acting on a policy, the role of the predictor's accuracy, something else? What single example should carry the session, and why that one rather than the other? What must the example have, and what must it conspicuously lack, so that the audience feels the pull of both the pay and the refuse answers before any formalism arrives? Which misconceptions will they form—that the problem is just a weird bet about a coin, that the answer turns on how probable the predictor is, that XOR blackmail is ordinary blackmail in disguise, that updatelessness requires backward causation, that evidential decision theory is simply irrational—and at exactly which point in the sequence should I head each one off?

I also want to end with a variant I have been developing: the predictor is not an omniscient demon but a publicly audited model trained on a transcript of the agent's earlier deliberations, and the agent knows the model is running before the coin is tossed. I want to ask whether that variant sharpens the problem or merely adds machinery. Show me where that capstone belongs and what it is supposed to test. Finally, give me one question I can ask at the end to tell whether they have understood the structure of the problem rather than merely memorized a verdict, and tell me what I should openly refuse to cover at this level and why.
