ok so I've got a decision puzzle thats been bugging me for weeks and what I want out of it is the principle behind my gut calls, not a pep talk.

Here's the setup. I'm driving home and there are two exits off the highway. I either get off at an exit or I stay on. Numbers: getting off at the first exit is worth 0 to me, getting off at the second is worth 4, and staying on past both is worth 1. The catch is that the exits look identical, and once I'm at one my memory of the drive is wiped — I can't tell whether I'm at the first or the second. I know all of this in advance.

My verdicts:

1. Planning at home: stay on at both, take the 1. If I adopt the rule "get off," the rule fires at the first exit I come to, so I collect the 0. So, stay on.

2. Standing at an exit, though, the other thought is loud: it's either the first or the second, and I can't rule out that it's the second, and if it is the second then staying on throws away a 3. Getting off feels like the right move right here, in a way that 1 says is wrong. 1 and 2 are the pair I can't square.

3. Same highway, numbers flipped: first exit is +5, second is -10, staying on past both is 0. Here I say get off, and not because this exit is good — it's because the rule only ever gets applied at the first exit, which happens to be the good one. Same shape of reasoning as 1, opposite answer, and I'm confident about both.

4. If a passenger who can read the signs rides along, I get off at the second one and collect the 4. Same if the car itself tells me which exit I'm at. So the blanking is costing me about 3.

5. A friend says I should just flip a fresh coin at each exit and only get off on heads, and that this beats 1 on average. I ran it and hes right, which bugs me, because if a coin beats staying on then verdict 1 is wrong and I don't know what's supposed to replace it.

6. I drive this route every week and blank out every time. Something in me says that over a hundred trips, sometimes getting off has to be right. But at any given exit I still can't tell which trip is which, so I don't see how that helps.

So: is there one principle that gets 1 through 4, and what does it say about 5? If it forces me to give up 1 or 2, which one and why that one? And what does it predict for the case I genuinely can't call — three identical exits where only the third is good and staying on past all three is 1?
