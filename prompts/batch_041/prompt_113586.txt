I have become persuaded by the rule-following argument that Kripke draws out of Wittgenstein: no fact about my past computations, my dispositions, my mental images, or anything else about me settles whether I have meant addition rather than a deviant function that coincides with addition on every case I have ever considered and diverges thereafter. I want to take this seriously and repair the situation rather than merely restate it. My proposal is to attach to each arithmetical expression I use a fully explicit rule — a written specification that fixes, in advance and without remainder, what is to be done with that expression in every case that could arise — so that my future uses answer to the specification rather than to my own past usage. A colleague proposes something weaker but more practical: a self-verifying notation in which each numeral carries a short checkable code, so that any departure from addition would be detectable in the notation itself rather than having to be inferred from behavior. I have two questions. First, would either proposal suffice to restore the fact that my symbols mean what I take them to mean; and if not, is the missing ingredient of a kind that some stronger supplement could supply — for instance a causal or informational connection between the rule I have in mind and the arithmetical facts themselves, of the sort often thought to underwrite reference in general? Second, does Kripke's argument depend for its force on the rules in question being unstated or tacit? Once everything is written down and checkable, has the argument simply lost its target?
