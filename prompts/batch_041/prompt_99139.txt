I've been reporting a piece on what happens to ethics if the universe turns out to be spatially infinite — the cosmologists keep inching that way, and a philosopher I had lunch with insisted it quietly breaks every standard flavor of consequentialism, because on an infinite canvas any finite pile of good or bad turns into a rounding error, or worse, all infinities start looking the same size. We ended up sketching a half-baked fix on a napkin that she called value density: instead of comparing total amounts of welfare, you compare how densely value is packed into a region and how that density behaves as you zoom further and further out. I honestly dont know if that's a real move or just a metaphor that dissolves the moment you push on it.

So now I'm trying to figure out what's actually worth chasing here — not the stuff everyone already recycles, the repugnant conclusion and Pascal's mugging get written up every other week. If you were doling out a few years of a philosopher's attention in this area, which open questions or lines of work should come first, and why? I care about the ranking and the reasoning behind it: whats upstream of what, what a non-mathematician can actually make progress on, and what would have to happen for a question to move up or down the list. Give it to me as a numbered list, exactly five items, each just a sentence or two, with no intro and no wrap-up — I'll be pulling straight from it.
