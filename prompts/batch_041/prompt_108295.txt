I'm in this little reading group on philosophy of mind and cognitive science, and last week we read some Hofstadter — the careenium stuff, simms, simmballs, the whole weird billiard-table analogy. I missed the meeting, so my friend Jen sent me her summary of what she thinks Hofstadter is up to. I'm supposed to lead the next discussion, and I want to know if her summary is actually fair before I repeat it. Here's what she wrote:

'Hofstadter asks us to imagine a giant table covered in countless tiny magnetic marbles, which he calls simms. They bounce around and stick together into clusters called simmballs. The simmballs are the brain's symbols. The question is whether the simmballs — the meanings, the symbols — actually cause the simms to move, or whether the simms just move according to physics and the simmballs are along for the ride. Hofstadter's answer is that the simmballs really do push the simms around: when a simmball representing the word I collides with another simmball, it literally exerts a force on the individual simms, redirecting them. So Hofstadter believes in downward causation: higher-level symbolic patterns are real causes that can override or supplement the lower-level physical laws. That's how the self can be a genuine agent rather than an epiphenomenal illusion. The careenium is supposed to show that the mind is not just the brain, because the simmball level has its own causal powers.'

I have a feeling this is mostly right but overshoots in a couple places. I'm pretty sympathetic to the idea that higher-level patterns are real and do explanatory work, but I don't want to walk into group defending a dualist reading of Hofstadter if that's not what he's saying. Could you go through her summary claim by claim — tell me what someone who knows Hofstadter well would accept, what they'd wince at, and what is just wrong? I'm especially interested in the causal-direction part: does Hofstadter think symbols push the substrate around, or is that a confused way of putting it? And does the conclusion that the mind is not just the brain follow from anything he says? Please be explicit about how confident you are in each part, and single out the point you're least sure about.
