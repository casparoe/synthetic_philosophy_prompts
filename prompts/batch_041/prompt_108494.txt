Not sure if this is a philosophy question or a math question, but here goes.

I've been making up coin games and I keep producing the same kind of monster, and I'd like to know if it has a name. Fair coin, flipped until it comes up heads. If heads first lands on flip n, the payoff is 2^n/n dollars — you win it if n is odd, you lose it if n is even. So the amounts run 2, -2, 2.67, -4, 6.4, -10.67, ... and the game ends on flip n with probablity 1/2^n, so flip n contributes 1/n to the average, with alternating signs. Now watch. The wins by themselves add up to 1 + 1/3 + 1/5 + ... which is infinite. The losses by themselves add up to 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/6 + ... which is also infinite. But add them up in the natural order and you get 1 - 1/2 + 1/3 - 1/4 + ... which comes to about 0.69. And if I add them up in some other order I can get any number I want, or plus or minus infinity. Every outcome has a definite payoff and a definite chance of happening; nothing here is vague. And yet the game has no expected value. Not an infinite one — none.

I know the St Petersburg paradox, where the payoffs are 2^n and the average is infinity, and this seems like a different animal. So: does this have a name? Is "undefined expected value" the whole story, or is there a sharper term? Is my game a special case of something bigger, and what are the neighbouring things I shouldn't confuse it with? What exactly is the difference between an expectation that's infinite and one that doesn't exist? And what's supposed to follow — does "no expectation" mean there's no fact of the matter about whether to play, or does something else take over? I don't want a bare label, I want the edges: where the phenomenon starts and where it stops.

Two more wrinkles, because I can't tell whether they're the same phenomenon. (a) Flip a coin: heads, you get the St Petersburg game (flip till heads, win 2^n); tails, you pay out the St Petersburg game. That's symettric — it's exactly as likely to win X as to lose X, for every X — so everyone I've asked says it's obviously worth nothing, and yet its expectation is infinity minus infinity, so the theory can't even say that much. (b) Take the monster game above and add a dollar to every payoff. It's plainly the better game, since it pays a dollar more in every single outcome, but it doesn't have an expected value either, so the expectation rule can't tell me it's better. Is there one name that covers all three, or am I lumping together things that are actually different?

And tell me how confident you are in what you say, and flag the bit you're least sure of.
