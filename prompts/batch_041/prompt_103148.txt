A lab I know is building a deliberation engine: it takes a hard question, breaks it into subquestions, farms each subquestion out to a smaller model, then a summarizer that never sees the original question stitches the subanswers into a final answer. They say this is kind've an engineering version of an old alignment idea about factored cognition, sometimes called HCH, and that it scales deliberation past what any one model can hold in its head. What philosophical commitments does that practice presuppose? It looks like it assumes deliberation decomposes into independent pieces, that trust in the parts transfers to trust in the whole, and that a summary can preserve whatever mattered without knowing the question it's answering. Are those assumptions actually required, or could the pipeline run under a more holistic picture where subquestions only make sense in light of the whole? Which views of rationality or judgment does it quietly rule out, and what would break first if deliberation turned out to be non-compositional?
