George Berkeley's economic writing is a set of numbered queries on Irish money, credit, and industry, animated by three commitments: that money is not itself wealth but a ticket or counter whose value lies in its power to command the labour and goods of others; that a nation's real wealth is the industry of its people rather than its stock of gold and silver; and that public authority may issue paper credit to set idle hands to work, so a metallic base is not the necessary limit of a sound currency. Predicting Berkeley's view, argue where he would come down on two monetary arrangements he never saw: a state-issued central bank digital currency, and a privately issued, supply-capped cryptocurrency such as Bitcoin. The crux is whether his conventionalism about money leads him to bless Bitcoin's fixed scarcity as honest money or to condemn it as a ticket that mistakes itself for treasure, and whether he would prefer the public instrument to the private one. In your answer, say which of the three commitments does the real work, give the strongest objection he could raise against his own conclusion, and note what he would have to concede if he landed on the other side.
