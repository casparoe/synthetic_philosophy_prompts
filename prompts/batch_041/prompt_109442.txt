I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, but I'm writing a character who's a field biologist and she's always going on about what things are "for," and I've realized I have alot of firm gut feelings about which of her claims are right and which are just her being romantic, and I want to know if there's one principle behind my gut or if I'm just being inconsistent.

Here's where I land. Heart: its function is to pump blood — obviously, no argument. Feathers on a bird: function is flight, even though I know feathers started out keeping the animal warm long before anything flew. Fine, flight. An organ that shrank to a nub over millions of years and now does nothing at all: I want to say it doesn't have a function anymore, it just has a history. A mutation that popped up, spread by pure luck, and happens to help the organism in some small way — I feel like it's doing something, sure, but calling that its "function" feels premature, like it hasn't earned the word yet. Then there's the rock on my desk that I put on a stack of papers so they don't blow out the window. I'd say its function is to hold down papers. Immediately, no earning required. And I can feel those two answers rubbing against each other but I can't say why.

More. A bird that stopped flying, wings still there, now used for balance when it runs: I want to say the function changed — balance now, not flight. But the vestigial nub, whose original job is also gone, I want to say has no function at all, rather than "changed" to nothing. A lump of bone that shows up in exactly one animal, never seen before or since: no function. A trait that's genuinely useful but only kicks in once every few years, like a tolerance for some rare poison: I want to say yes, function, even though it's idle most of the time. And a moth that flies into a flame and dies — the thing that makes it do that, I would not call its function, even though it's the most reliable thing the poor thing does.

And the one I keep going back and forth on: a feature that was selected for one thing long ago and now does a seperate useful thing, and the old thing doesn't happen anymore at all. Is the function the old one, the new one, both, or neither? Also, is there a real difference between what a trait is for and what it merely does? My biologist would say yes, my gut says sometimes.

So: is there one principle that gets all these, or close? State it as precisely as you can, tell me which of my verdicts it can't keep and why, and say what it predicts for a case I haven't listed — say, a trait that's useful now only because humans changed the environment, so nothing in the animal's own history ever selected for it. No reading lists or citations please, I won't understand them and I don't want to fake my character's dialogue out of a bibliography.
