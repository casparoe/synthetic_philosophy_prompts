I am a third-year student taking a metaphysics course on the problem of universals, and I also work in programming languages: I am reasonably at home with type systems, parametric polymorphism, structural versus nominal typing, subtyping, and the type/token distinction. My study group tried to restate the standard positions in the universals debate -- Platonic realism, immanent realism, class nominalism, resemblance nominalism, and trope theory -- in the vocabulary of type theory, and we got stuck. Our best attempt was to equate Platonic realism with types that exist independently of any program that instantiates them, class nominalism with a type being nothing over and above its inhabitants, and resemblance nominalism with structural typing, leaving the other two rows blank. Please carry out the translation systematically, one position at a time, and keep it honest: do not let the target vocabulary silently settle the question in advance. For each position, tell me what carries over cleanly, what survives only with distortion, and what has no counterpart at all -- and then give me the residue report. Which parts of the debate (the explanatory demand that universals make particulars what they are, the requirement that a universal be wholly present in each of its instances, grounding, resemblance as a non-type-theoretic relation) are simply unsayable in type theory, and is that residue a defect of the type-theoretic framework or evidence that the original dispute was less clear than it looked? Relatedly, is the stratified hierarchy of types in type theory any kind of counterpart to the regress worries that push people away from realism, or is that only a coincidence of notation? I would rather have the honest residue than a tidy table.
