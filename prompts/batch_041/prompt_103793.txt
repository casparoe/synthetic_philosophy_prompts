I am a novelist at work on a book set in a fictional institute for rational decision-making. One of my characters is an epistemologist who has talked herself into the following position, and I am inclined to think she is right, but I want to be sure before I commit it to the page. She begins from accuracy-first epistemology, the view that the sole fundamental epistemic good is accuracy, and from epistemic utility theory, which evaluates credences with strictly proper scoring rules. She says that a strictly proper scoring rule is one whose expected value is maximized by the credence function that matches the truth. Therefore, she concludes, the ideally rational credence function is the true one. Since she does not know which credence function is true, she proposes to approximate it by adopting the credence function that would be true if her current best theory were correct: credence 1 in that theory, 0 in its rivals. Hedging, she says, would only make her less accurate if her theory is right, and if it is wrong she is inaccurate anyway. Is this a sound application of accuracy-first epistemology? If not, where exactly does the reasoning go wrong, and what would the view actually imply about how she should set her credences? Please be explicit about how confident you are in your correction, and about what would lead you to think her argument is defensible after all.
