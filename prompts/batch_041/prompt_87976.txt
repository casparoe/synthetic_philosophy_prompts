I sit on the citizens' commission my governor put together to recommend changes to how our state taxes wealth at death, and we vote Thursday, so I need the written recommendation ready to read into the record. Here's what's in front of us. Today we have a plain estate tax: 16% on everything above a $2 million exemption, collected from the estate. The bill on the table replaces it with an inheritance tax collected from each recipient instead -- spouses exempt, half a million exempt per recipient, graduated rates up to 35%, and then the part everyone's yelling about, a special 5% rate on anything a recipient gets out of a trust that was created more than two generations back. The sponsor says that trust provision is the only thing in the bill that actually stops a few families from hardening into permanent dynasties, and that taxing what you recieve is fairer than taxing what someone else leaves behind. Two members say the opposite: the money was already taxed when it was earned, it's trivially dodged by handing assets over before you die, and it punishes exactly what we claim to admire, parents building something for their kids. Our mandate letter says the whole point of the exercise is protecting equality of opportunity, which is why I care about the trust provision at all. Please draft the recommendation I'll read out: a concrete yes, no, or amend on the bill, the reasoning, and an honest version of the strongest objection -- the members who disagree should be able to read it and not feel strawmanned.
