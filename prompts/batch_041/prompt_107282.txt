Here's the student's answer:

"Color phi is when two spots get flashed on a screen in quick succession, one above the other and in different colours — say a blue spot on top and then a red spot lower down. Instead of seeing two seperate flashes, the subject sees a single spot that appears to move from top to bottom and, oddly, changes from blue to red partway along the path. The strange part is that the subject seems to see the colour change happen before the second spot has even been flashed, which shouldn't be possible if the first flash is experienced on its own first.

Dennett says explanations of this come in two flavours. On the Orwellian hypothesis, the subject really does first experience a blue spot, and then the memory of that experience is edited after the second flash arrives, so that what you end up remembering is a spot that was already turning red — like the Ministry of Truth rewriting old newspaper records. On the Stalinesque hypothesis there's no separate experience to rewrite: the brain reconciles the two flashes before the content ever reaches consciousness and presents the finished, already-consistent story, like a show trial where the verdict is decided in advance and the trial is just for show.

The important thing is that the two hypotheses agree completely about what happens in the brain: they put the same causal revision at the same point, and differ only over whether we call that point before or after the onset of experience. Dennett's move is that since both fit everything we observe, and since subjects would be unable to tell a misbegotten experience from an immediately misremembered one, we can't choose between them on evidence. And because the colour change is clearly being constructed in the brain before we ever experience it, Dennett concludes that the Stalinesque hypothesis is the one that's actually correct. He also says the reason we can't finally decide between the two is that our brain-imaging methods aren't fast enough yet to catch the revision in the act — with better temporal resolution we'd be able to settle it. The deeper point he draws is that both hypotheses quietly assume a 'Cartesian theatre,' a single place where the show gets presented to the mind, and once you give that up the question of exactly when the edit happens stops having an answer."
