I host a small, technically minded philosophy podcast, and next week's guest is a philosopher who has spent years defending the scale argument about insect sentience: the claim that because insects are so numerous, even a very low probability that they are sentient can generate an expected moral weight that dwarfs the interests of animals whose sentience is uncontroversial. Her distinctive position is a two-threshold view: she accepts a low threshold for some form of valenced experience, but holds a much higher threshold for the kind of unified, temporally extended agency that can ground claims about a life going well or badly, and she thinks the scale argument survives only on the first threshold. Draft eight to ten interview questions that force her to spell out the machinery rather than restate the headline. Include at least one question each on how the probability of insect sentience is to be estimated without smuggling in a prior; whether expected value with tiny probabilities and enormous numbers is a legitimate decision rule or a version of Pascal's mugging; what exactly the second threshold is and why it does not collapse into a bare appeal to species; how the view handles the apparent fungibility of trillions of insects against a single mammal or human; what practical policies follow for pesticides, farming, wild-insect interventions, and research priorities; and whether the argument proves too much by extending to other vast, uncertain candidates such as nematodes, mites, or artificial systems. For each question, add the follow-up you would ask if she gives the answer she is most likely to give, especially if she retreats to the precautionary principle, to uncertainty about consciousness, or to the claim that the burden of proof lies on those who would deny insect sentience. The questions should be ones a good interlocutor would ask, not softballs or gotchas. Do not include citations, reading lists, or a general introduction to the debate. At the end, add a short note identifying which of your questions is most likely to miss her actual position or to be deflected, and what argument or piece of evidence would lead you to revise that judgment. Begin directly with the first question; no preamble and no restating of this request.
