I have just come out of a long argument with a friend about how a rational agent should revise her degrees of belief when the new information arrives in conditional form—if A then B—rather than as an outright proposition. Before we continue, I want the specification: what must any adequate theory of updating on conditional information be able to do? Give me the desiderata, each with the case or consideration behind it. It should handle the Judy Benjamin problem and say what makes that case difficult; reduce to ordinary conditionalization when the input is a proposition; be invariant under equivalent reformulations of the conditional and of the partition; say what happens when the antecedent is initially assigned probability zero; allow sequential updating without order effects; and respect the distinction between learning a conditional and learning that a conditional has a certain probability. Mark which of these are genuine requirements and which are stipulations of a particular camp, and tell me whether any subset cannot be jointly satisfied. Then, briefly, how the main families of response—minimizing relative entropy under a constraint on the conditional probability, imaging, and the suppositional or Ramsey-style approach—fare against the list.
