For an upper-level seminar on the divine attributes, I want a one-page decision tree that takes a reader from a bare starting commitment — that "God is omnipotent" is at least intelligible — to a particular resolution of the paradox of the stone. Build it from the logical space first rather than from a list of authors: every internal node should be a question a reader can answer without knowing which resolution it leads to, every leaf a named position (or "unnamed," if nobody holds it) with one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Aim for eight to ten leaves.

I want the main axis to be a distinction the standard discussion runs together: between "God cannot do X" meaning he lacks the power and "God cannot do X" meaning there is no such act to perform — and, alongside it, between having surrendered a power and being unable to exercise a power one still has. Include as a branch node a case in which God has promised never to do X: is he now unable to do X, or only unfaithful if he does? That should prise apart views the stone case leaves tangled. Include further nodes for whether God can do evil and whether God can cease to exist, which should separate positions on whether God's goodness and existence are prior to or downstream of his will.

The leaves must include: a resolution that preserves omnipotence by treating the paradoxical act-descriptions as incoherent; one that preserves it by restricting the quantifier to the logically possible; one that grants God power over the logically impossible; one on which God has voluntarily limited himself; one that replaces coercive power with persuasive power; one that revises logic instead, treating the contradiction paraconsistently; and one that reads the paradox as a successful reductio. Flag any pair of leaves that no answer can separate, and mark which combinations of answers no one occupies, with a one-line diagnosis of whether the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
