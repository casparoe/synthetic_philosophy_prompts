Construct a thought experiment to the following specification. Work from your own resources: no citations, no bibliography, and no attribution of the positions below to anyone by name. I care about the structure of the case, not about who held what.

Set out the background briefly before you give the case. A teleosemantic theory of content explains a state's aboutness by its function, and its function by selection. There are two places such a theory can put the explanatory weight, and they are not equivalent:

- Producer-side: the content of the state is fixed by what the mechanism that produces the state was selected to be tokened in response to.
- Consumer-side: the content of the state is fixed by what the mechanism that consumes the state was selected to do with it once tokened.

These come apart whenever the selection pressure bearing on production differs from the selection pressure bearing on consumption. That is the fault line I want you to exploit.

Now build the case. It must satisfy all of the following.

1. Ordinary biology only. No swamp duplicates, no brains in vats, no histories stipulated away, no invented neurophysiology, no designers or demons. A small organism, or a small set of them, is enough.
2. The state must be a representation on both accounts. The case must not work merely by having one side deny that the state represents anything at all.
3. The two accounts must assign different contents to the same state type in the same organism. State what each assigns, and why.
4. A third account, on which content is the information the state actually carries when tokened, with no appeal to selection history, must assign a third content, distinct from both. Say what it assigns.
5. Everything else is held fixed: same mechanism, same wiring, same inputs, same outputs, same developmental story. The divergence must trace to a single isolable fact, and you must name that fact explicitly.

The twist: the state's function must be fixed by learning or imitation rather than by genetic selection, and the single isolable fact must be a fact about what each side learned, or could have learned, rather than about which ancestors survived. After you have given the case, say whether either account treats culturally transmitted functions differently from genetically selected ones, and whether that difference is principled or a patch.

Then run the checks.

(a) Delete the fact you named. Show exactly which verdicts converge and which do not, and say whether the case collapses into agreement or degenerates into a smaller, different puzzle.
(b) Say which account the case damages, then give the strongest reply available to a defender of that account. Not a dismissal: an actual reply, plus what it costs the defender to make it.
(c) Is this a counterexample to that account, or only an illustration of the indeterminacy of content? Take a side and defend it.
(d) Generalize. State the condition under which producer-side and consumer-side selection pressures must come apart, in a form general enough that I can generate further cases of the same shape without reusing your organism.

Additional constraints on the answer. The case itself in at most one paragraph, then the analysis. No stock examples from this literature (frog and fly, bee dance, bat sonar, vervet alarm calls, magnetotactic bacteria). Do not hedge about whether the teleosemantic project is viable; take the two accounts at face value and let them deliver verdicts. The case is for a seminar on whether selection-based theories can yield determinate content, so what counts as failure is a case that stipulates its verdicts rather than generating them from the theories.
