A friend in my cognative science program sent me her notes on Dennett's four kinds of creatures (Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian) and I want to know how much of it I can trust before I repeat any of it in a seminar paper. Here's what she wrote:

'Darwinian creatures come out of the box fully programmed. Natural selection did all the thinking, so they just run the program — no learning at all. Skinnerian creatures are the behaviorist ones: Dennett names them after Skinner because at that level there is nothing going on inside the animal, just rewards and punishments shaping its behavior from the outside. Popperian creatures have an inner model, so they can try things out in their heads and let their hypotheses die in their stead instead of dying themselves. Gregorian creatures are us — language, culture, tools of thought, all the stuff we get from other people instead of from our genes. And it's a ladder of evolution: fish are Darwinian, rats are Skinnerian, chimps are Popperian, humans are Gregorian, and once you climb onto the next rung you're not really on the one below anymore. The payoff is that consciousness only shows up at the top, because you need language and cultural tools before there's anybody home, so Gregorian creatures are the only ones that are actually conscious. Dennett calls the whole thing a tower of generate and test.'

I've more or less swallowed the ladder picture, and the consciousness bit sounded to me like the whole point of the thing, but the Skinner sentence bugs me — Dennett isn't a behaviorist, is he? Go through her notes in order and tell me what's right, what's subtly off, and what's flat wrong, and what Dennett would actually say instead. Then tell me straight whether her conclusion about consciousness survives once the rest is fixed, or whether that's the part that has to go. Don't give me the menu of interpretations — commit to a reading, and if something really is ambiguous in Dennett, tell me which reading you'd bet on and why. No citations needed, I can't check them anyway. Keep the whole thing under 550 words.
