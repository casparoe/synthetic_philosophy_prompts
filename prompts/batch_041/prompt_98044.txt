OK so I just lost two hours arguing with my mate Dan, who's a public defender, about the Knobe thing — the vignettes where a CEO says he doesn't care about the environment, he only cares about profits, and then it turns out the environment either gets harmed or helped. Word for word the same story except one word flipped, and everyone says he harmed it on purpose but not that he helped it on purpose. Dan's actual position is that this asymmetry is correct and should just be written into the criminal code, because if jurors reliably attribute intent that way then that basically IS what intent means. So I said fine, write the law that way and let's see how it reads.

Write me the official guidance document that a jurisdiction would put out after replacing the mens rea test with this asymmetry — something like a Department of Criminal Intent "plain-language guide for jurors." Deadpan, no winking, no scare quotes, keep it sounding reasonable; the horror should come from how sensible it all sounds.

It should cover, in numbered Q&As: what counts as doing something intentionally; that not caring whether it happens, and even wishing it wouldn't, is apparently enough; the help/harm asymmetry stated straight-faced as a feature of the law; what happens when the side effect is the *normal* thing to expect (Dan reckons the real trigger is just whether the outcome was out of line, not what the defendant actually wanted — let the document quietly concede this and not notice it's conceded anything); failed attempts where the skill was clearly there but it didn't come off; and a short bit distinguishing intent from motive, since alot of people think those are the same thing and they aren't. Maybe 500-600 words. It should be funny to someone who's read the experimental philosophy on this and still readable to someone who hasn't, and a specialist should feel the parody is fair rather than a strawman.
