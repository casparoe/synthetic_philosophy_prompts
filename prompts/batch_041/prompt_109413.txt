ok so my reading group has been doing functionalism and multiple realizability all term, and last week someone dropped an argument that split the room and we never resolved it. I want an outside verdict, because every step of it is something he says we already signed up for, and thats exactly what makes me nervous.

(1) What makes a state a mental state is its functional role -- what typically causes it, what it typically causes, how it interacts with the other states -- and not the material it happens to be made of.

(2) The same functional role can be had by systems made of totally different stuff. That's just multiple realizability.

(3) For a system to have a functional role just is for there to be a mapping from the system's states and transitions onto the states and transitions that define the role. And you can always cook up such a mapping: pick a coarse enough grain, or a strange enough labelling of the parts, and any system's transitions will line up with some role or other. A rock, a dishwasher in the middle of its cycle, the traffic pattern in a city -- each of them, under some description, matches some functional profile.

(4) So each of those things, under that description, is in whatever mental state that profile defines. The rock has a pain. The dishwasher wants its door open.

(5) The rock does not have a pain. My dishwasher doesn't want anything.

(6) So something in here has to go.

I'd really like to keep (1) and (2) -- there kind of the whole reason any of us call ourselves functionalists. But I can't make myself say the rock is in pain. And I honestly can't tell if the argument is airtight and just awful, or if there's a slide buried in it somewhere.

So: which step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost? Don't just point at one and shrug -- explain why that step looked so innocent when he said it. If the move is to demand a better account of what "implementing" a role actually amounts to, tell me what the better account is and what it rules out that maybe we didn't want ruled out. If instead we have to keep (1)-(4) and pay the price on (5), tell me exactly what that commits us to about rocks, dishwashers, and thermostats. And if one of these premises is quietly doing two different jobs, say which one and what the two jobs are.
