I have no training in philosophy or economics, but I have been thinking on my own about how groups make up their minds, and I have constructed a small example that I cannot resolve to my own satisfaction. Five members of a hiring committee must decide whether to offer a candidate a job. They have agreed in advance that an offer is warranted precisely when three conditions hold: the candidate is strong enough technically, the candidate fits the team, and there is room in the budget. Each member's own opinions are entirely coherent. Four believe the candidate is strong enough technically, four believe the fit is good, and four believe the budget allows it; yet only two believe that all three conditions hold at once, so three of the five are against making the offer. Now, if we tally the committee's verdicts on the three conditions one by one and then apply the rule the members agreed on, the committee offers the job; if we instead tally the committee's verdicts on the offer itself, the committee withholds it. I have taken to calling this the two-headed committee, and I suspect the name is doing more work than the phenomenon deserves.

Please argue for a position I have come to hold but cannot defend well: that the committee should decide by tallying the three conditions and then applying the agreed rule, and that the appearance of a contradiction under the other count is an illusion, since a committee is not a single mind and so cannot in the relevant sense contradict itself. I would like the argument to take seriously a complication I keep returning to, namely that the three conditions are not all of the same kind: the budget is a fact about the world that the committee could in principle check, whereas the technical and fit judgments are matters of assessment. I have been calling the error of demanding that a group be consistent in the way an individual person is consistent "borrowed consistency," and I would like you to make the strongest case that this demand is a mistake, that abandoning it removes the air of paradox entirely, and that my preferred method follows once we stop importing a standard of consistency that belongs to individual thinkers onto a body whose members were never of one mind to begin with.
