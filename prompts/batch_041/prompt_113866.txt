Here is my opening move. The familiar monster is a single being whose capacity for utility is so great that feeding it the world maximizes the total. I want to alter the case in a way that I think makes trouble for any purely aggregative view. Suppose the monster's enormous utility is not enjoyed by one enduring subject. Instead, it is distributed across an astronomical number of momentary subjects: each exists for an instant, experiences a small but genuinely positive pleasure, and then ceases, with no memory, no continuity, and no subject who is present across the whole. The aggregate is still overwhelming—far greater than the total welfare of all ordinary people. But now ask whose welfare is being maximized. If you say it is the aggregate itself, you seem to be treating a sum as a subject of value, which is exactly the kind of metaphysical entity a naturalistic utilitarian should resist. If you say only enduring subjects count, you need a principled account of why duration or continuity matters, and that account may not be available to the classical utilitarian. So: does your utilitarianism feed this swarm-monster, and if so, what exactly is the locus of the value you are maximizing? If it does not, what premise of utilitarianism does the case force you to give up?
