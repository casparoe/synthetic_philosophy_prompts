Two friends have been needling me for months to write down where I actually stand on hard choices, because I keep contradicting myself mid-argument and they want to see the whole thing in one place so they can take a run at it. So here's my pile, roughly. (1) Some pairs of options are genuinely incomparable — not one better, not one worse, not exactly equal; there's just no fact of the matter about how they rank. (2) But I think the commonest hard-choice case isn't that flat kind of incomparability, it's a fourth thing, being "on a par": the options really are comparable in some respect, but neither is better and they aren't equal either — and the tell is that a small improvement in one of them (my go-to example is a slightly nicer office tacked onto the job) does make it better than the other. (3) I'm also a decision theorist at heart: a rational agent's preferences are complete and transitive and can be represented by a utility function, and the rationally permissible choices are exactly the ones that maximize expected utility. (4) When a choice is genuinely hard, though, I think more than one option is permissible — you haven't done anything wrong by picking either one, and you're not answerable for the value you leave on the table, even though I do think it's fitting to feel regret about what you didn't get. (5) Regret, guilt, feeling like you owe someone an explanation — all of that is fitting when you've acted wrongly. (6) And I'm not a relativist about progress: I think it's simply true that we're better than a society built on slavery, and that that isn't just us preferring our way of life.

I've never actually wrote the whole list down before and I suspect a couple of these are quietly inconsistent, but I genuinely can't tell which. I know (1) and (2) sound like I'm hedging, but I mean them as two seperate diagnoses for two different kinds of case, not as a dodge.

What I want is an audit, not a summary or a lecture: which of these actually pull against each other and how badly, which ones only look like they conflict, and for the real ones, which commitment has to give and what it would cost me to give it up. Please rank the real tensions by severity — I don't want everything returned as "in tension to some degree" — and if there's one revision that would clean up the most at the least cost, say which and why. And tell me how confident you are in each of your verdicts, and single out the one point in your whole diagnosis you're least sure of, since I'd rather know where you're winging it than get a uniformly confident answer.
