Consider the following six proposed grounds on which a person may lose the standing to blame another. Rank them from strongest to weakest as grounds for forfeiting standing, where "strongest" means forfeiting standing even when the underlying criticism is true and the blamer's motives are otherwise decent. Lead with your complete ranking in the first sentence, then justify each placement in one or two sentences, and close by naming any ground you take to be merely a special case of another.

1. Hypocrisy: the blamer is currently guilty of the very fault they are condemning.
2. Complicity: the blamer's own conduct, or their willing membership in a group whose conduct does so, contributes to the fault being condemned.
3. Non-acceptance: the blamer would not accept the same criticism if it were directed at themselves, or does not genuinely endorse the norm they are invoking.
4. Ex-ante cost: the blamer would not have been willing to bear the cost of conforming to the norm they now invoke, had that norm been applied to them in their own circumstances.
5. Meddling: the fault is none of the blamer's business, so the blame oversteps a boundary of privacy or jurisdiction.
6. Instrumentalization: the blamer raises the charge chiefly to gain some advantage, rather than out of concern for the norm or for anyone wronged.

For each ground, say whether it defeats standing outright or merely weakens the force of the blame, and identify the one you think does the most independent work rather than collapsing into the others.
