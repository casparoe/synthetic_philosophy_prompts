My buddy and I just spent like three hours arguing over whether your beliefs can actually change what you see, and somewhere in there I got an idea for a story I can't stop thinking about. In it there's a clinic that offers a procedure — they call it "clearing" — which cuts your prior expectations out of your visual experience, so you supposedly see the world raw, with nothing filled in. The people who sign up are mostly experts: radiologists, birdwatchers, air traffic controllers. They all come back saying they lost something. The radiologist can still describe every pixel of the scan, but the tumor doesn't jump out at her anymore, and she keeps trying to explain that her expectation of a tumor was never a bias, it was thousands of scans worth of skill — she says her beliefs had to reach into her eyes for her to see anything worth seeing at all, and my friend says thats exactly the loop that should worry us, because the belief is what makes the seeing, and the seeing is what confirms the belief. I want the story to make the reader feel that tension without deciding who's right — no cheap twist where the clinic turns out to be secretly evil or secretly perfect, and don't let the ending settle whether the debiased perception is the "real" seeing or not. Maybe 900-ish words?
