Design a thought experiment that isolates knowledge as the sole variable in the dogmatism effect: the idea that if you know P, then any evidence against P is misleading, so you may rationally discount it, whereas a merely justified true believer may not. Constraints: one paragraph, an ordinary non-philosophical setting, no skeptical hypotheses, and no stipulating that one agent knows while the other does not—the epistemic difference must instead fall out of how each agent came by her belief, while the two agents share exactly the same total evidence, including the same counter-evidence. The counter-evidence must be substantial rather than a triviality, and the second agent's belief must be true, well supported, and yet fail to be knowledge for a non-skeptical reason. After the scenario, for each agent state the belief, the counter-evidence, and the verdict on whether she may rationally keep believing and dismiss that counter-evidence—first on the knowledge-first view, then on a view holding that evidence must always be weighed by its strength regardless of whether the belief amounts to knowledge. Name the single feature of the case doing the work, say what becomes of the verdicts if that feature is removed, and give the strongest line of attack open to a defender of whichever view fares worse. Keep the scenario to one paragraph and the analysis to no more than 400 words.
