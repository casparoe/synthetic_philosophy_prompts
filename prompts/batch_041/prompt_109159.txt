I work in admissions — well, institutional research adjacent — and there's a pattern I've been staring at for about three years and I cant find a word for it.

Here's the thing. We have a number we're supposed to hit for first-generation students. Nobody fakes anything; the numbers are honest. But every cycle the count comes in short, somebody looks at how we're counting, and discovers that the boundary was in the wrong place. First it was parents with a bachelors degree. Then it was any college at all. Then someone points out that a parent with a degree from a university in another country doesn't really count, culturally, so those students get moved into the first-gen bucket. Next year the target is met. Same story with a program that started out as "women in engineering" and is now "women and gender minorities", which changed the numerator without a single additional hire or admit. And I've watched "underrepresented" quietly do the work that "minority" used to do at three different institutions. I gather the same fight happens in the countries with hard board quotas — the number stays fixed and the argument is over what gets counted toward it.

What bugs me isnt that anyone's cheating. Each redefinition is defensible on its own terms and I'd probably sign off on most of them. It's that the redefinitions never go the other way, and they always seem to arrive right when the count is short, and nobody involved experiences themselves as moving a goalpost. They experience themselves as fixing a measurement error.

I know this is in the neighborhood of Goodhart's law, and of that line about measures changing the things they measure. But Goodhart is about people gaming a metric, and the reactivity stuff is about the measure changing the behaviour of what's measured. Here nothing is gamed and nobody's behaviour changes. What moves is the membership rule of the group the target is about, and it moves in the direction that makes the target achievable, and it's invisible because the new boundary is always more defensible than the old one.

Is there a name for this? If there isn't, fine, coin one — but the name is the least of it. What I actually want is the carving. Is this one phenomenon or three? Where's the line between this and ordinary concept drift, or a legitimate refinement of a rough category that we should be doing anyway? What would a case where the category got narrower look like, and is that the same thing or the mirror image? And how would you ever tell this apart from a category just being genuinely improved over time, which I assume is sometimes what's actually happening. Don't send me a reading list, I won't read it.
