I'm in a third-year seminar on philosophy of mind and cognition, and last week we did Dennett's Kinds of Minds, where he sorts creatures into four kinds. Darwinian ones learn by natural selection — the trial and error happens across generations and the failures are dead animals. Skinnerian ones learn by operant conditioning, so the trial and error happens within one lifetime, and the failures are just things that don't get repeated. Popperian ones move the trial and error inside the creature: it can generate candidate behaviours or hypotheses and eliminate the bad ones before they cost it anything, so the bad hypothesis dies instead of the creature. And Gregorian ones (named after Richard Gregory, I'm pretty sure) are the ones whose minds get kitted out with culturally transmitted tools — words, numbers, maps, notation — so they can do things far beyond what the bare brain could manage. Our prof kept insisting the four are a tower, not a ladder: each stage is built on top of the one below and keeps it running rather than replacing it, so nothing that's Popperian stops being Skinnerian. Anyway, after the seminar two people in my group chat went at it for like an hour about whether a large language model is a Popperian creature, and honestly it got good and neither would budge.

Maya said no, it's Skinnerian at best, and probably not even that, because everything interesting about it was produced by selection from the outside — pretraining, then human raters, then whoever fine-tuned it. Nothing in it ever discarded an idea *because it was bad*; the bad ideas got punished out of it by us. And she said you can't get a Popperian creature by Skinnerian means, that's the whole point of the distinction — the stage has to be reached from the inside, otherwise every thermostat is a scientist. Dev said she's confusing the biography of the system with what it's doing right now. The Popperian test is functional: does the trial and error happen inside the creature, so the bad variants are eliminated before they cost anything? When the model writes a candidate answer, notices it doesn't follow or contradicts itself, throws it out and tries again, the hypothesis died instead of the creature, and that's the Popperian trick. If you also demand that it reached that stage by itself, you're asking about origins, not about what kind of mind it is — and the thermostat objection proves too much, because a chess engine that searches ahead and rejects losing lines is doing exactly what the category was invented to describe, and calling that "just behaviour" is the move behaviourists used against everybody else. Maya came back: the engine scores lines against a function someone handed it, and the model's "trying again" is just more of the same computation with the same weights. For it to be Popperian in the interesting sense, the variants have to be things it's treating as candidates *for being true*, checked against something. Otherwise "discarding a hypothesis" is a story we're telling from outside about a matrix multiplication, and then anything with a loop counts. Dev said fine, but then say what the extra ingredient is without smuggling in a little inner theatre where a homunculus inspects the candidates — if the check has to be a check the system itself performs, the model performs checks, that's what a chain of thought is; if it has to be performed by something that also *feels* like it's checking, that's a different question and you've switched from kinds of minds to consciousness. Then there was a second arguement I can't sort out at all. Dev said if anything the model is the most Gregorian creature ever built, since it's made of nothing but culturally transmitted stuff — all the words and distinctions and formats we've ever written down, compressed into one thing, which is exactly what the category is about. Maya said that's backwards: Gregorian creatures *use* the tools, and the interesting claim is that a fairly weak individual mind becomes capable of things it couldn't do alone by taking the tools on board, so there has to be a distinction between the creature and the toolkit. A thing that just is the corpus, with no remainder, isn't a creature using culture — it's culture that happens to be running — and if you count it you lose the ability to say what the tools do for a mind, which was the whole reason for the category.
