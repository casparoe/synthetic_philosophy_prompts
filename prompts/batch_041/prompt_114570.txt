Here's roughly where I've landed on side effects and low-impact AI. (a) Impact has to be measured against a baseline — what would have happened if the agent hadn't got involved — because on its own, a change in the world means nothing: everything changes the world. (b) Because the agent's own later choices are part of that counterfactual, the baseline can't be a frozen snapshot of things as they stand; it has to be the trajectory the agent's own default, do-nothing-much policy would have produced. (c) The penalty term has to be value-free, in the sence of not presupposing what the human actually cares about — otherwise it's a utility function smuggled in through the back door and it'll be wrong precisely when the human's values diverge from whoever wrote the measure. (d) The agent should be corrigable: it lets itself be shut down, it doesn't fiddle with its off-switch, and I want that to be lexically prior to the impact penalty, never traded off against it. (e) The way to get (c) is to count how much the agent shrinks the space of options open to the human, measured against the baseline trajectory from (b).
