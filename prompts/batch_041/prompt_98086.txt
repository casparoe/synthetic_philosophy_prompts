Here is my opening move. Two agents, A and B, are in separate rooms and cannot affect one another in any way. Each is running the same decision algorithm, and each knows this. Each must choose one of two policies. If both choose COOPERATE, each gets 3. If A chooses DEFECT and B chooses COOPERATE, A gets 5 and B gets 0; if A chooses COOPERATE and B chooses DEFECT, A gets 0 and B gets 5; if both choose DEFECT, each gets 1. There is no causal link between the rooms, no communication, and no future interaction. I am A. I say I should defect: whatever B does, defecting gives me more — 5 rather than 3 if B cooperates, and 1 rather than 0 if B defects. You say I should cooperate, because my choice of policy is evidence about B's choice, since we are running the same algorithm. But B's choice is already fixed by the time I choose, and my choosing COOPERATE does not cause B to choose COOPERATE. Why should I give up 2 points to a mere correlation? And if I do cooperate, what stops me from reasoning at the moment of action that B's algorithm has already run, so defecting is the better move? This looks like the toxin puzzle dressed up in new clothes. Answer me as the acausal trader.
