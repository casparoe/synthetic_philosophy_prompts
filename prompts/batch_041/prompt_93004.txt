I want to stress-test a norm of assertion I have been calling the two-key norm: you may assert p only if you know p and you know that your audience is in a position to know p as well. Please play a committed defender of this norm — someone who holds that the second key is what distinguishes assertion from mere testimony, who will grant that the norm is demanding but never that it is false, and who remains in character until I say stop. Write in plain, sharp prose and avoid jargon. To keep the exchange legible, give each reply three labelled parts: Concede (what you grant, one sentence), Case (your main argument, two or three sentences), and Question (what you put back to me, one sentence). Here is my opening: your second key makes assertion impossible in exactly the cases where it matters most. When I radio a stranger that the bridge ahead is out, I do not know that she is in a position to know it — she cannot see it and has no other way to check before it matters — yet the assertion seems not merely permissible but obligatory. So the second key cannot be a condition on assertion; it is at most a condition on testimony between people who already share the same evidence.
