Heres a thing I keep running into and I dont know if it has a name. It's about information — facts, test results, news, that sort of thing. What I've noticed is that whether a fact is worth anything to me seems to depend completely on whether it would change what I do. If I'm going to make the same choice either way, then finding out is worth zero for the decision — it might change what I believe, it might be fascinating or awful, but it doesn't move the choice, so as far as the choice goes it's worth nothing. Fine, I can live with that. But here's the half I can't square. There are lots of facts I'm clearly better off not having. The test result I'd rather not open. The message I already know is bad news, sitting there unread. The thing about my friend I'd rather not be told. Because once I know, I either have to do something about it — say something, leave, report it — or I have to carry it around, and either way I'd have been better off ignorant. And I've got this nagging memory that there's a result saying the opposite, that more information can never leave a rational person worse off, because you can always just ignore it and act as if you'd never heard it. So either I'm quietly sliding between two senses of better off, or that result assumes something I'm quietly violating. Can you untangle this? And I don't just mean, tell me the name of the theorem. I mean: is the thing I'm circling — that a fact's worth to me depends on what I'd do with it, and that it can go negative because of what knowing would commit me to — a recognized phenomenon with a name of its own, or is it just a smear of a few different things? If it's a smear, which things, and where are the lines between them. If it's one thing, give me the edges: what it is, what it isn't, and what to call it.
