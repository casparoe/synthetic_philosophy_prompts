Write a satirical FAQ page of about 750 words, in the deadpan voice of the customer-service department of an insurance firm that has recently begun underwriting cosmological risks: vacuum decay, a collision with a hostile bubble, the possibility that the claimant is a Boltzmann brain that has hallucinated a life — including this policy. Numbered questions and answers, in the flat register of a real insurer's help centre, straight-faced throughout.

The comedy has to come from the technical content, so that a cosmologist or a philosopher of physics laughs rather than winces, while a reader who has never heard of eternal inflation can still see what is being mocked. Three threads in particular should be doing the work. First, the measure problem: there is no canonical way to assign probabilities across an infinite multiverse, so every premium and every payout is relative to a measure the customer is invited to select at signing, and different defensible choices make the same event certain or impossible. Second, anthropic selection: which observers count, whether the person filing the claim is the person who bought the policy, and what the firm does when a risk is "explained" by the fact that anyone in a position to notice it is, by construction, in a branch where it has not yet happened. Third, the firm's frankly non-empirical underwriting philosophy, drawn from the landscape of string vacua: a model is accepted not because it predicts anything new, but because no rival has been constructed, because it accounts for more than it was built to explain, and because its being the sort of theory we could arrive at is itself treated as evidence in its favour.

The target is the practice of extracting quantitative, insurable-looking claims from a framework whose probabilities are undefined, and the habit of treating unfalsifiability as a mark of depth. Nobody in the piece should announce the objections; the exclusions clause, the definition of "the insured", the claims procedure and the premium table should carry them. Invented details are welcome, but do not satirize any named living person.

Begin directly with the FAQ itself, with no preface, no commentary on what you are doing, and no restatement of this brief.
