I run a small coffee roastery with six other people. We're a worker co-op — everybody owns a share, one person one vote, and whatever's left at the end of the year after costs and a reserve gets split by hours worked. I'm taking a continuing-ed course on Marx, mostly for fun, the reading is brutal, and the parts about exploitation and alienation keep landing weirdly hard on my actual life. I'd like you to lay out the most important implications of those two ideas for our situation specifically, not for capitalism in general.

Here's what's actually going on. Two years ago we got busy enough that we hired two part-timers, but we did it through a staffing agency — the agency bills us $28 an hour and pays them $19, and they're not members, they don't vote, they don't get a dividend. One of our founders put in $30k of her own money at the start and we agreed she gets a 4% preferred return on it before anything else gets split, which nobody thinks about untill the checks go out in January. And we just moved to a subscription model with a tablet-based roasting app that sets the profile and the timing for you, which has made the work less fun in a way I can't quite describe; a friend who reads alot of theory said the process got reorganized around the equipment rather than us just owning the equipment, which sounds about right and also sounds bad. The thing I can't square is this: I felt way more like myself back when I was just an employee at somebody else's shop, and now that I literally own the place I feel more estranged from the coffee, from the customers, and from my co-owners than I ever did then. I also have a nagging feeling that our two temps are being exploited even though none of us are bosses in the classic sense and we'd never call ourselves that, and I can't tell whether that feeling is right or just guilt about the agency's cut.

So what are the most important implications of Marx's account of exploitation and of alienation for us — for whether the temps should be made members and what that does to the numbers, for the founder's preferred return, for whether the roasting app is a real problem or just nostalgia, and for what my weird inversion of feeling more alienated now than before actually tells me (including if it tells me nothing and it's just burnout)? If the two ideas pull in different directions here, say which one I should weight more and why. American English please, and skip the reading list — I'm not going to do it.
