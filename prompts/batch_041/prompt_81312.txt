Our dept's reading group is doing Keynes on probability next month and I've been voluntered to make the handout. I want it to be a decision tree that sorts people by what they actually think is going on when they say "probably", not by which school they'd name. Start from the basic commitments -- is a probability claim a fact about the world, a fact about your head, or a relation between a particular body of evidence and a hypothesis? -- and end at leaves for: Keynes's own logical view (probability as a partial entailment from evidence to conclusion), the subjectivist/Bayesian view where any coherent set of degrees of belief is fair game, the frequency or propensity view, the Knight-style risk-vs-uncertainty split, and a deflationary option that says there's no fact of the matter about what to believe, only about what to do.

Every branch node should be a concrete case someone can answer without already knowing which leaf it leads to. Please build it around cases like: a bet on a unique event that will happen at most once; a coin you know is biased but not which way; two people with exactly the same evidence who put different numbers on the same hypothesis; a claim backed by lots of weak, individually inconclusive evidence versus one backed by a single strong piece; and a case where extra evidence doesn't shift the balance of the argument at all but does make the judgement feel more solid. Each leaf should name the position and add one line on its most awkward commitment. Also tell me which answer combinations are empty and whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just never explored, and flag any two leaves the tree can't really seperate -- I suspect Knightian uncertainty and low-weight-of-evidence Keynesian cases will be hard to tell apart, and so will imprecise-probability Bayesians and Keynesians. A dozen or so nodes is plenty.
