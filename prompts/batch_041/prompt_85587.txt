I chair the mitigation subcommittee of our state sentencing commission, and we vote Thursday on a petition from the public defender's office. The petition asks us to add to our open-ended list of mitigating circumstances "structural neuroimaging evidence of substantially reduced brain volume or focal brain abnormality," and to attach a rebuttable presumption of diminished culpability whenever a defendant's volume falls below the fifth percentile for age and sex. Its central argument is that a smaller or damaged brain means less capacity to understand and control one's conduct, which is precisely what our culpability categories are supposed to track. The prosecutors' association answers that this amounts to a brain-size discount, that the measurements are crude proxies whose variation is driven mainly by body size, sex, and nutrition, and that in any case the volume of a person's cortex is not the sort of fact that can bear on blameworthiness.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote. I want a concrete disposition — adopt the petition, reject it, or adopt some narrower version of it — with the reasoning the other commissioners can actually argue with, and with the strongest consideration on the losing side stated as its proponents would state it. If you conclude that volume is the wrong measure, say what a better-grounded proxy would look like and whether it is the commission's business to be drawing that line at all.
