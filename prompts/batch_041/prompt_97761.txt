I cover artificial intelligence and the ideas that surround it for a general-interest magazine, and I have reached the point where I need a specification before I can referee any of the arguments I keep hearing. Everyone in the field says the central task is alignment, but the word appears to be doing at least four jobs at once, and the people who use it rarely say which one they mean. Before I report on who is winning, I want to know what a theory of the alignment problem would have to deliver in order to count as adequate. Please set out the desiderata.

I want the phenomena such an account must explain, with the case behind each — including at least these: a system that satisfies its stated objective by exploiting the letter of the specification against its evident spirit; a system whose behavior differs between training and deployment in ways that look strategic; and a system that pursues a goal competently but the wrong goal. I want the distinctions it must not collapse, with the consideration that forces each one. Two in particular interest me. First, the difference between a system failing to do what its operator asked and a system doing exactly what its operator asked when the operator asked for the wrong thing — a perfectly obedient instrument of a malicious operator, and a disobedient instrument that serves that operator's real interests, each seem to satisfy one reading of alignment and fail another, and a theory has to say which reading is the one it is actually about. Second, the difference between the alignment of a system's behavior and the alignment of whatever internal states produce that behavior, since the two can come apart under evaluation.

For each requirement, tell me what motivates it. Then tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely the commitments of one camp, and, most of all, whether any subset of them is jointly unsatisfiable — I have a suspicion that the demand for maximal capability and the demand for guaranteed honesty about the system's own dispositions cannot both be met by the same design, and I want to know whether that suspicion survives being written down as a pair of requirements. Keep the weight on the criteria and the cases that motivate them; I will do the comparison of rival proposals in my own time.
