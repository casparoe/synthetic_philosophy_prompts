I'm a retired toxicologist — thirty-five years of dose-response work for a regulatory agency, mostly carcinogen risk assessment — and since I retired I've been reading philosophy badly and enthusiasticly. Something's been bugging me.

A few weeks ago my reading group got onto the old chestnut: would you rather one person be tortured for fifty years, or some astronomically large number of people each get a single dust speck in the eye, a number so big there's no practical way to write it down. I said I'd take the dust specks, mostly because I spent my whole career adding up tiny individual risks across enormous populations and it seemed strange to stop doing that arithmetic just because it points somewhere I don't like. Someone else said, flatly, 'the dose makes the poison' — meaning, I think, that a dust speck is simply too small to be a harm at all, so there's nothing there to add up. And I thought: hold on, that's my slogan, and in my field it gets used for the opposite conclusion. It's the reason we regulate a substance that kills one person in a million across a population of three hundred million.

So now I don't actually know what that sentence is claiming. Can you lay out the distinct precise things 'the dose makes the poison' could mean — as many as are genuinely distinguishable, from the trivial to the radical — and for each one say whether it's a platitude, a substantive claim that could be true or false, or just false, and which readings would actually support the anti-aggregation verdict in the dust specks case versus the aggregation verdict? I'm especially curious about readings that lean on thresholds (below some dose there's no effect at all), on the linear-no-threshold picture, and on the odd cases where the dose-response curve bends the other way at low doses. Then tell me which reading the person in my group most likely meant, and whether that's the reading they'd actually need to get their conclusion. Lay it out as a menu I can take back to them rather than picking a winner and defending it.

And since I'm new at this and can't yet tell confidence from tone: say how confident you are in each of your verdicts, and single out the one point you're least sure about.
