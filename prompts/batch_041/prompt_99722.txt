Here's my line, roughly. Emergence talk is only ever doing one of two jobs: bookkeeping (we can't in practice derive the upper-level story from the lower-level one) or genuine novelty. And I claim the only defensible version of "genuine novelty" is a compression criterion: a system has an emergent property when the shortest model that predicts its behavior under a given description-length budget does *not* factor into local models of the parts with sparse coupling between them. Degree of emergence = how many bits you save by moving up a level. So emergence is a fact about our best models rather than about new causal powers — but it's an objective fact about them, it doesn't depend on who's looking. I use the molecular-dynamics-to-Navier-Stokes limit as the worked case and I gesture at flocking to show it travels. I'm against strong emergence (downward causation, new powers) and against the deflationist who says emergence is just ignorance.

The room: two condensed-matter people, one postdoc one prof, who think the founding insight of their field is that more is different and who will hear any epistemic reading of emergence as a slur on their discipline; one Humean about laws who'll say my whole apparatus collapses into best-system talk and can't distinguish emergence from mere complexity; one person who builds information-theoretic measures of emergence and will say my criterion is either a notational variant of theirs or not well-defined; one philosopher of mind who's a strong emergentist about consciousness and will say I've defined the interesting thing out of existence; and one historian of science who's allergic to these analytic distinctions and will want to know what work the word was doing before we formalized it.
