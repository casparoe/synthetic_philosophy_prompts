I'm in a philosophy of psychiatry reading group — mostly grad students, one actual psychiatrist, and a psychologist who keeps threatening to quit — and we've spent the last month on Ian Hacking's looping effects, i.e. the idea that classifying people changes the people being classified, which changes the classification, which changes the people, round and round. For the last session we're each supposed to bring something, and I said I'd do a parody: a fake entry from a fictional upcoming edition of a diagnostic manual, for a disorder whose entire defining feature is that it exists because of the manual. It has to be funny to someone who knows this literature — the criteria should be circular in exactly the way real criteria are circular, and every absurdity should be something the manual already does, just turned up a notch — and still land for someone who's never heard of Hacking and just thinks it's a strange manual entry. The target is the practice of writing criteria, not the people who get diagnosed, so nothing that reads as punching down; the imaginary committee writing this entry should be perfectly sincere and maybe a little pleased with itself.

Call it something manual-plausible — I had "Criterion-Reactive Disorder" or "Nosological Feedback Syndrome" in mind, but make up your own if you have a better one. A few things I'd love the entry to embody without ever announcing them: that the patient's presentation is supposed to have been shaped by having read the earlier description; that prevalence tracks whether the thing is currently being diagnosed; that full remission is expected if the category is later deleted; and that the criteria keep needing revision because people have started satisfying them. Deadpan the whole way through, no winking, and the whole thing should leave you feeling the category can't possibly be a natural kind.

Give me exactly five numbered criteria, each one or two sentences — specifiers and exclusion clauses are fine, they're half the joke — with no preamble and no closing note. Just the criteria, so the thing stands or falls on them.
