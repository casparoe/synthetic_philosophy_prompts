Longtime reader of the rationalist blogs here. I keep seeing Kavka's toxin puzzle cited as a deep result about the structure of intention, and I want to push back on the framing. As I understand it, the rational play is to form the intention to drink the toxin, collect the prize, and then decline to drink once the money is secured — the puzzle being that it seems rational to intend but irrational to act. But an intention is just my best estimate of what I will in fact do; intending to drink while knowing I won't is nothing more than a knowingly false prediction, and no rational agent can conjure one on demand. So isn't the real content here simply the mundane point about precommitment — that I should want a commitment device, or to self-modify into someone who actually drinks — rather than a genuine paradox about the will? I'd like to know what I'm missing about why it is treated as troubling.
