In a discussion about animal welfare, someone invoked Descartes and said that on his view animals are machines, and that this shows the whole notion of animal minds to be a pre-scientific confusion — animals are just complicated automata, so nothing is owed to them morally. I suspect several different claims are being run together under that one phrase, and I want them separated before I try to answer him.

Could you lay out the distinct precise theses that the slogan 'animals are machines' might be standing for, as a menu rather than a single preferred interpretation? I am especially interested in the difference between: a claim about what animals can think; a claim about what they can feel; a claim about how their behaviour is best explained; and a claim about what they are made of. Please also distinguish the claim that the body is a machine — which I take Descartes to have held of human bodies too — from the claim that the animal is nothing but a machine, and say whether that distinction does any real work or is only verbal.

For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible position, or something false or incoherent, and note which ones are genuinely attributable to Descartes rather than to later caricature. Some readers insist he denied animals any feeling at all, others that he denied them only thought; I would like to know which is right, and why the difference matters for the moral conclusion my interlocutor wants. Keep in view the two tests Descartes is usually said to have offered for the difference between us and animals — one turning on language, the other on the generality of reason — and say what each does and does not establish. Finally, tell me which reading my interlocutor actually needs in order to get from the slogan to his conclusion that animals have no moral standing, and whether the move works on that reading.

If I have mis-stated the doctrine, or if the slogan is too indeterminate to yield verdicts of the kind I am asking for, please say so plainly rather than smoothing it over. Write in British English.
