The setup as I understand it: two thought experiments. In both cases your own life is untouched, and yet people report that their projects, their ambitions, the things they care about, start to feel pointless — not because they'll be dead, but because there won't be any people after. Scheffler calls the continued existence of humanity a kind of collective afterlife, and he thinks alot of what we value actually depends on it. So extinction is supposed to be a loss that isn't just the sum of the individual deaths.
