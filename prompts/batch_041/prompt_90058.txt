I'm in a reading group on scientific progress and next week its my turn to put my cards on the table, so I'm trying to write down what I actually think about truthlikeness before everyone starts poking at it. I'm a realist, and I think the whole point of the notion is to make sense of progress, but every time I state the package out loud it feels like I'm quietly helping myself to something. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Mature scientific theories are at least approximately true, and later theories are typically closer to the truth than the ones they replace — that's just what I mean by progress. (2) Whether B is closer to the truth than A is an objective, mind-independent matter: it doesn't depend on our standards, our community, or what we can currently test — though I'll happily admit that we're usually in no position to know which of two live theories is closer. (3) The old Popper-ish content definition is a dead end, the more-true-consequences-and-fewer-false-ones thing. The upshot of that whole line of work is that it can never rank two false theories: each of them has some false consequence the other lacks, so neither comes out ahead, and since none of our theories are true, the definition can never deliver the comparisons I actually care about (Newton vs Aristotle vs Einstein). So I'm a likeness person: a theory's truthlikeness is a matter of how far its models are from the actual world. (4) Related: truthlikeness has to be sensitive to how much a theory says. You shouldn't be able to raise a theory's score by disjoining something true onto it (Newton's laws, or it's raining in Oslo right now) or by quietly hedging a claim you haven't tested. Saying more, and being right about it, is the whole game. (5) I accept that any likeness measure presupposes a metric, or at least a partition, on the space of possibilities, and that nothing in the world privileges one metric over another — different but equally natural choices (minimum vs average distance, how heavily you weight one dimension against another) can flip which of two theories comes out closer. (6) And I'm fairly convinced by the pessimistic meta-induction: phlogiston, caloric, the ether — the central posits of past successful theories get thrown away wholesale, and what survives is mostly structure and equations, so if I'm honest the continuity I actually believe in is structural rather than ontological.

So: which of these are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and how bad is each one? Rank them by severity instead of dumping them all out as equally bad, tell me for each what would have to give and what that costs me, and then tell me which single revision cleans up the most. I can't look anything up right now, so please don't send me a reading list or citations — I want the audit, not the literature. And be specific about which pairs conflict rather than telling me it all depends on how I define my terms.
