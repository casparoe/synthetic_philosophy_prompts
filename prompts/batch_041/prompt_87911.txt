For the last session of my graduate seminar on collective action, I would rather have a joke than another summary, since a joke is the sharpest test of whether someone has actually internalized the structure of a race to the bottom. Write one. It should turn on the real content of the idea — a decentralized dynamic in which each party's locally rational move leaves everyone worse off, no party wants the outcome, and there is no villain and no single lever anyone could pull — and it should be built on an unfamiliar case rather than the stock arms race, fishery, or advertising war; ordinary life or academic practice would serve well. The sting should fall on the difference between a coordination failure that trust or an enforceable agreement could dissolve and a selection-driven trap that survives perfect information and universal goodwill, because whoever restrains is eliminated and replaced. Give me the voice of someone inside the trap who understands it completely and still cannot stop. Keep it short enough to deliver from a podium in under a minute, and follow it with no more than a paragraph on what it gets right about the structure; no lecture.
