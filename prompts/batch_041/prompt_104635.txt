I am a graduate teaching assistant in a large lecture course, and I have to decide what to do about a suspected case of plagiarism. Before I ran the department's text-matching tool, I would have put the probability that this student copied from an online source at about 0.15. The tool flagged the paper. From the tool's documentation and my own experience, I know that it flags about 90% of papers that were in fact copied and about 10% of papers that were not. But the flag is not conclusive, and I would say that I am only about 80% confident that this particular flag is a true positive. A classmate in my epistemology seminar tells me that I should simply treat the flag as certain evidence and update my prior on it as if it settled the matter. Another tells me that I should first update on the tool's reliability and then on the flag, and that the order of these updates can change the result. I need to report a posterior probability to the course coordinator and decide whether to recommend a formal hearing. Please give me a definite recommendation, with your reasoning, rather than laying out the options and leaving the choice to me. In particular, is there a single correct posterior here, or does it depend on how I model the uncertain evidence? If it depends, tell me which model I should adopt and why.
