I've been going down a rabbit hole on panpsychism lately. It started because a friend in my consciousness reading group is a committed panpsychist and every time we meet I try to articulate why the view leaves me cold, and the answer is always the same: the combination problem. The panpsychist says micro-experiences somehow add up into my experience of, say, the smell of coffee; the critic says you can't get one subject out of a crowd of subjects, or that even if you could, the qualities wouldn't fuse into a single unified field; the panpsychist comes back with a story about "phenomenal bonding" or emergent subjects, and the critic says that's just hand-waving past the hard part. It feels like this has been going on for over a century — I'm pretty sure James was already making a version of the mind-dust objection — and nobody moves an inch.

What I want from you isn't a survey or a verdict on who's right. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is the combination problem a genuine unsolved theoretical problem, or is it functioning as a reductio the panpsychist can never wriggle out of because the trouble is baked into the starting assumptions? How much of it is verbal — a fight about what "combination" or "subject" is even allowed to mean — and how much is two sides working with different standards for what would count as a solution (a mechanism? a conceptual necessity? just a coherent story?)? I also want to test a hypothesis of mine: maybe the real impasse is that panpsychism needs composition to be intelligible in a way our concepts of subjects and experiences just don't permit, so the debate can't advance until someone redefines what "a subject" is — at which point it quietly stops being panpsychism as anyone actually defends it. And I've noticed some people try to dodge the whole thing by flipping it around, starting from one big cosmic subject and explaining how it breaks up into little ones, but that just looks like renaming the problem to me. Tell me if my hypothesis holds up, and given your diagnosis, say concretely what would count as progress here — a new distinction, a formal constraint, a shift to a view that dissolves the problem rather than solves it — or argue that nothing should move it and explain why.
