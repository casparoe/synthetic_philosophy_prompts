Ok so I'm on student council and we've got about $4k left over this year and I actually have to argue for something at the next meeting. Two options are easy: new gear for the robotics team, or funding the breakfast program that some kids depend on because they dont eat at home. But the fight is about a third idea, which is just to cancel the end-of-year awards assembly where the top students get called up and handed trophies. The reasoning is that the assembly makes the kids who never win anything feel like they're beneath everyone else, so cancelling it makes things more even. But cancelling it helps those kids in no way at all — nothing gets spent, they just don't have to sit through it. I said that's a crazy reason to do anything since nobody ends up better off afterward, and someone said "well at least its fairer now." Is that actually a reason?

And it gets weirder, because someone proposed a version where we cancel the assembly and use the hour for a study session the struggling kids can drop into. So now the worst-off kids gain a little and the top kids lose something they really care about. Does that flip the answer? Also my friend said something I keep chewing on — she said that caring extra about the worst off only really bites when people are genuinely bad off, and at our school basically nobody is, so we're just comparing kids who are all fine, and the whole "give priority to the worst off" thing might not even say anything about a case like ours. Is she right? What do I say at the meeting?
