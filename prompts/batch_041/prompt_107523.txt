I am preparing a graduate seminar on decision theory and would like your help in anticipating how a colleague of mine will rule on a new case. My colleague has the following settled verdicts on the standard problems:

1. In Newcomb's problem, with opaque boxes and a highly reliable predictor, she takes one box.
2. In the voting case, she holds that voting is rational even though one vote has negligible causal impact, on the ground that voting is evidence of how like-minded people vote.
3. In a case where an action is causally efficacious with respect to an outcome, she chooses the action that maximizes causally expected value.

Next week she will consider a new case, which I will call the "common cause" case. A genetic factor, fixed at birth and unaffected by anything she does, causes both a tendency to choose one box and a tendency for the box to be full. No predictor is involved; the correlation between her choice and the contents of the box is due entirely to the common genetic cause. The payoffs are the same as in the standard Newcomb problem.

Predict what she will say about the new case. Identify the decision rule she is implicitly running, say what each candidate rule -- causal decision theory, evidential decision theory, and any others you think fit her verdicts -- predicts about the new case, give your best guess with the reasons for preferring it, and name the further case that would tell the rules apart.
