Treat the following as a closed-book exercise: reason from first principles, and do not provide citations, bibliographies, or attributions of any of the propositions below to a named author or work. Several of the propositions paraphrase claims that can be found in the literature; others were constructed for this exercise; you are not being asked to tell which is which, and any guess at a source would be worthless here.

The background question is whether there can be laws of history in the sense that Karl Popper's critique in The Poverty of Historicism denies. Your task is to evaluate seven candidate generalizations by that standard and to rank them.

Apply these five tests, which I state so that we are working with the same criteria rather than with your recollection of anyone's formulation of them.

Scope. A genuine law must be a universal conditional, with no hidden restriction to a particular period, region, or type of society. A generalization that holds only of, say, early modern European states is a singular historical statement dressed as a law, not a law.

Counterfactual support. It must say what would have happened under conditions that did not in fact obtain.

Falsifiability. It must forbid something: some describable observation must count against it.

The Oedipus test. If the generalization were widely believed, would the belief tend to bring about the outcome it predicts, or to prevent it? A generalization whose truth is systematically altered by its own acceptance is a poor candidate for a law, because the announcement of the prediction is itself an event inside the system being predicted.

The growth-of-knowledge test. Popper's central argument is that the future growth of human knowledge is both unpredictable in advance and a principal cause of historical change, so that no unconditional prophecy about the overall course of history is available. Ask of each candidate whether its content is hostage to discoveries, inventions, or ideas that do not yet exist.

The candidates:

L1. Whenever the number of aspirants to elite positions in a polity substantially exceeds the number of such positions available, political instability increases within one generation.

L2. As a society's per-capita energy capture rises, its institutional complexity rises faster than its per-capita output; when the gap passes a threshold, the society collapses.

L3. No state that loses a war of attrition fought on its own territory retains its prewar constitutional order.

L4. Once a technology of mass communication allows a population to coordinate without intermediaries, hierarchical control of that population becomes progressively more costly.

L5. A regime that fails to reproduce its founding coalition within two generations becomes vulnerable to a legitimacy crisis.

L6. The probability of civil war in a country in a given decade rises with the severity of that country's economic contraction in the preceding decade.

L7. The history of human societies is the record of a progressive expansion of the sphere of individual freedom.

Deliverables. Lead with a single sentence stating your headline verdict on whether any of the seven deserves the name of a law of history. Then give a JSON object with exactly this schema, with the candidates array ordered from rank 1 to rank 7:

{
  "headline_verdict": string,
  "genuine_law_fraction": number,
  "candidates": [
    {
      "candidate_id": string,
      "rank": number,
      "law_score": number,
      "kind": string,
      "scope_restriction": string,
      "disconfirmation": string,
      "auxiliary_assumption": string,
      "oedipus": string,
      "conditional_or_prophecy": string,
      "growth_of_knowledge": string,
      "rationale": string
    }
  ],
  "best_candidate": string,
  "best_candidate_argument": string,
  "structural_obstacle": string
}

Field specifications. candidate_id is one of L1 through L7. rank runs from 1 for the candidate closest to a genuine law to 7 for the furthest. law_score is a number between 0 and 1. kind is one of universal_law_candidate, ceteris_paribus_trend, probabilistic_regularity, mechanism_sketch, definitional_or_tautologous, directional_philosophy_of_history. scope_restriction names the hidden spatio-temporal or other restriction that keeps the candidate from being a universal conditional, or is the string none. disconfirmation gives a concrete observation that would count against the candidate, or explains why nothing could. auxiliary_assumption gives the extra premise needed to make the candidate testable, or the string none_needed. oedipus is one of self_fulfilling, self_defeating, none, both, followed by a clause of explanation. conditional_or_prophecy is one of conditional_prediction, unconditional_prophecy, neither. growth_of_knowledge is one of survives, fails, partly, followed by a clause of explanation. rationale is one sentence. genuine_law_fraction is a number between 0 and 1 giving the fraction of the seven you would call laws. best_candidate is the candidate_id most resistant to Popper's objections, and best_candidate_argument defends that choice in two or three sentences. structural_obstacle is one sentence naming the single most important reason, as this exercise revealed it, that laws of history are hard to come by.

Keep every string field to one sentence except best_candidate_argument, do not add fields, and use the string none rather than omitting any field.
