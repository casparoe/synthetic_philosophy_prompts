Hey, so I'm a junior in this course called Risk, Science and Public Policy and we just finished the unit on the precautionary principle. For the seminar I have to do a "creative response" instead of a normal paper, and I'd much rather hand in something good than something safe (lol). Can you write it for me?

What I want: a FAQ page, about 600-700 words, from the website of a fictional national regulator. Make up the country, make up the agency name, something like the Bureau of Precautionary Affairs or the Office of Anticipatory Safety. The whole thing is in the voice of the agency itself: sincere, slightly bureaucratic, faintly proud of itself, deadpan the entire way through. No winking at the reader, no narrator stepping in to say it's a joke, no smug asides. The agency must never sound stupid or cartoonish -- it should sound like a real regulator that has thought hard about this and made a deliberate choice it can defend. All the humour has to come from following the precautionary principle's own logic out to where it goes, not from me inventing absurdities. If someone who actually works on risk regulation reads it they should wince-laugh and think it's fair, and someone who's never heard of the principle should still follow what's happening.

Things I want it to trip over along the way (these are all real features of the view, so lean on them, don't make stuff up):

- the burden of proof flipping: it demands proof of safety from anyone proposing anything new, and somewhere in the FAQ it quietly treats the absence of proof of safety as itself proof of danger -- ideally without ever noticing that it has done this.
- the regress: does the principle pass its own test? Have a question where the agency nearly approves its own guidance, and then doesn't, and give a completely straight-faced reason.
- asymmetry: it counts a missed hazard as infinitely costly and a blocked benefit as free, so it can never update. Let one answer slip, almost in passing, that the standing moratorium has a body count of its own -- and have the agency treat that as an acceptable cost without ever admitting that's what it's doing.
- the vagueness: an answer where it concedes that if it ever wrote down what counts as safe, someone could show it was wrong, so it doesn't write it down.
- capture: it should keep using 'novel' in a way that means 'whatever the incumbents didn't do first,' and never notice.

Also include one question from a member of the public asking, basically, "so what am I actually supposed to do," and have the answer be perfectly circular while sounding helpful. And end with a short standard disclaimer, the kind agencies put at the bottom, that undercuts the whole page without meaning to.

Please don't name any real countries, agencies, people or documents -- keep it all invented so I don't get a date or a title wrong in front of my seminar. And definately don't quote anyone real, I don't want to have to go check a source. No philosophy-paper-with-jokes-bolted-on vibe either; it should read like a real webpage that has gotten completely carried away with being careful.
