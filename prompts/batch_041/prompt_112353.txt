Something's been bugging me for a while and I can't find the right word for it. I get indignant on other people's behalf a lot -- I read about someone being treated badly, someone I've never met, and I'm angry about it. Fine, everyone does that. What's odd is what happens to that anger. The usual ways of talking me down don't work: I don't care that the person who did it was stressed, or didn't realise, or was having a terrible year -- none of that is what's actually bothering me, and when someone tries it I just think they've missed the point. But the second the actual victim says "honestly, it's fine, I've let it go", my anger goes limp. And I can't tell you what changed about the person who did it, because nothing did. It's like the victim is holding a switch that turns my indignation off, and I think that's genuinely strange -- I wasn't borrowing their anger in the first place, so why does their letting go dissolve mine? Flip side: if the victim is dead, or a small kid, or never finds out, nobody's holding the switch, and the anger just sits there with no way to be discharged. It feels permanent in a way that other anger doesn't.

I've heard "standing to blame" thrown around for this, but I don't think it's that -- if the problem were just that I've got no business being angry about someone else's wrong, then the victim saying "I forgive him" wouldn't change anything, I'd have had no business either way. The forgiveness clearly does something. So what is it doing to me, if it isn't changing any fact about the offender? Is there an established name for this, or the nearest few names and why each is off? What I actually want is the carving, not the label: what the victim's release is doing to my attitude, what this isn't (it's not them speaking for me, and it's not me being told to mind my business), and where the edges are -- e.g. does it still work if the forgiveness is coerced, or misinformed, or if the victim has forgiven but I think they shouldn't have? If nothing fits, coin something and define it, but I'll be unsatisfied with just a word.
