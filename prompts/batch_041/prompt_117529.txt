In my reading group on epistemic utility theory, one member closes every session by declaring that accuracy is the only epistemic good — most recently to dismiss my question of whether a credence can be rationally defective for being incoherent even when it is highly accurate, and then to wave away my suggestion that understanding might be a distinct epistemic achievement. Treat that one-liner as something to be disambiguated rather than defended, and lay the readings out as a menu running from the near-trivial to the radical. What does accuracy name — a score assigned by some strictly proper scoring rule (one that rewards a credence for being closer to the truth), the closeness of a credence to the truth itself, or a property that attaches to full beliefs as well? What does only quantify over — the sole intrinsic epistemic good, the sole epistemic good of any kind, or merely the sole thing we can measure and compare? And what is the claim a claim about — credences, doxastic states generally, or whole agents and their inquiries? For each reading, give a verdict (platitude, substantive and defensible, or false) and say explicitly which readings would license dismissing the coherence worry and which the understanding worry; then identify where the slogan equivocates — which banal reading is doing the rhetorical work of the strong monism.
