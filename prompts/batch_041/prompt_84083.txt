I write about science and ideas for a regional paper, and I'm the lay/community member on the ethics committee at the big teaching hospital in town — usually I just nod along, but next Thursday we actually have to decide something and I've been handed the drafting.

The case: our residents do 24-hour call, and a couple of the surgical programs have quietly started letting people take modafinil or something similar off-label on the worst rotations, and one attending has been nudging the ones who look wrecked at 4am to take it. The chair wants a policy — ban it, allow it, or something in between — and I have to write the opinion we vote from. The pro side says sleep deprivation is a real impairment, that it's a condition and not a lifestyle, and that we're the ones who caused it. The con side says a healthy 28-year-old whos tired because of a schedule we wrote isnt a patient, that a pill just papers over the fact that the schedule is the actual problem, and that once it's allowed the person who declines it is the one who looks bad.

Give me the memo: a concrete recommendation with a time frame, and reasons the program directors can actually push back on. The thing I keep tripping on is the baseline — 'treatment' is supposed to bring you back up to normal, but normal for who? Normal for someone their age, normal for them when they've slept, or normal for someone working the shift we told them to work? Those three give different answers here, and I can't tell whether that means the whole therapy/enhancement line is a mess or whether it's just a drafting problem I can finesse. No reading lists, I don't have time for them. And please be straight with me about the strongest thing the side I end up siding against has going for it — I don't want to get shredded in the room.
