Alright, I need a satirical piece for a handout, deadpan the whole way through, no winking.

Setup: a fictional mid-size company, say Halcyon Bioworks, has an ethics committee, and the committee has read its way into the evolutionary debunking argument against moral realism and voted to make the conclusion official company policy. Write the internal memo that goes out to staff — a headline plus about 650 words, numbered sections, memo voice, absolutely straight-faced. A reader who's never heard of the debate should still be able to follow what's being satirized.

The comedy has to come from the argument itself, not from the committee being dumb. So the memo should keep the metaphysical claim (there are no stance-independent moral facts) separate from the epistemological one (our moral beliefs are unjustified), and should notice that a genealogy of a belief only debunks it if we have some reason to think the origin story is truth-tracking — otherwise you're just committing the genetic fallacy. It should say something about why the same reasoning doesn't obviously take out our beliefs in arithmetic or in ordinary perceptual claims about tables and chairs, and it should be honest that this is a modus tollens from an unexplained correlation rather than a proof. And then, obviously, the memo should be completely unable to stop blaming people for ignoring it — performance reviews, the bonus grid, the disciplinary section — without that ever registering as an inconsistency to the person who wrote it.

Don't aim it at any real person and don't name any real living scholars; the target is the argument and the fantasy of running an institution on it. American English. Dont worry about citations.
