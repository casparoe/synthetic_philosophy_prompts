Heres the pile. (1) General relativity is our best theory of spacetime and I don't think we get to bolt extra rules onto it just because some solution strikes us as strange — if it's a solution, and the matter in it is stuff we'd otherwise allow, then it's physically possible in the sense that matters. (2) Gödel's rotating universe is a real solution of that kind, and it contains closed timelike curves, worldlines that loop back into their own past, so GR by itself doesn't forbid a round trip into the past. (3) I'm a B-theorist: no moving now, no privileged present, no passage; past and future are as real as here and there, and the sense we have of time flowing is something about us rather than about the world. (4) The way I read Gödel, the real point of his solution is that a single objective now shared by the whole universe doesn't fit with relativity at all, and I think he's right about that. (5) I still think you can't change the past, and that going back and shooting your own grandfather isn't a coherent scenario — not because the universe would somehow stop you, but because there's nothing back there to alter. (6) The past/future asymmetry — we remember one direction, causes come before effects, entropy climbs — is a real feature of the world that needs a real explanation, and I'm not satisfied by "it just comes from the low-entropy starting condition".

What I keep tripping over: (2) and (5) look like a flat contradiction to me and I can never quite say why they aren't one. (4) might be circular given (3) — if I've already got the block, what is Gödel's result supposed to be doing for me? And (6) might be me smuggling in an arrow of time that the theory doesn't have, which is exactly the sort of thing (1) says I'm not allowed to do.
