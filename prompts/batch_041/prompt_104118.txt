A philosopher I'm reading has these verdicts on whether a moral belief is undermined by an evolutionary explanation of why we hold it. The belief that incest is wrong: the evolutionary explanation is complete, and the belief cannot be independently justified by reflection; she says it is undermined. The belief that gratuitous cruelty is wrong: the explanation is partial, and the belief can be independently justified by reflection; not undermined. The belief that women are inferior to men: explanation complete, cannot be independently justified; undermined. The belief that all humans are equal: explanation partial, can be independently justified; not undermined. The belief that ingroup loyalty is a virtue: explanation complete, cannot be independently justified; undermined. The belief that one ought to reciprocate favours: explanation partial, can be independently justified; not undermined. She has not told me her general criterion. Next week we are discussing the belief that we have a duty to aid distant strangers: the evolutionary explanation is partial (a by-product of our evolved tendency to reciprocate), and the belief cannot be independently justified by reflection. Predict her verdict, say which criterion she is implicitly running—whether the explanation is complete, whether the belief can be independently justified, or some combination—give your best guess with reasons, and tell me which further case would settle the matter. Please answer in British English.
