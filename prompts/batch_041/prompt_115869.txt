My old Latin teacher turns 70 next month and her kids have asked me to give the toast at the party. Half the room read the De Senectute with her back in school and half are family who've never opened it and think classics people are a bit ridiculous. She's got a bee in her bonnet about that dialogue: she says everyone reads it as four seperate rebuttals — you're not useless, not weak, not pleasureless, not on death's doorstep — when really Cato's four answers are one answer, because all four complaints are the same complaint, that old age shoves you off the stage, and Cato's whole move is that the stage was never the point. She'd want that in the toast, and honestly it's the most her thing there is. The problem is she's the least sentimental person alive, so "and now she can finally rest" or "her best years are ahead of her" will get me a look I won't recover from. Write the toast — a few minutes, lands for the family without turning into a lecture on Cicero, and doesn't say anything she'd privately think was false.
