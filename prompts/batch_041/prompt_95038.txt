ok so I have to write up my position on the timing of experience for a seminar and I keep tying myself in knots, so before I commit anything to paper I want a consistency check. here's roughly where I've landed:

1. There's a fact of the matter about the order in which a subject's experiences happen. Even if we could never tell from the outside, the experiences really do occur in some determinate order — I am not a no-fact-of-the-matter person about experience itself.

2. The color phi thing is real and I think it shows something gets filled in. You flash a dot, then a differntly coloured dot somewhere else a fraction of a second later, and people report a single dot that travels across and changes colour partway. The catch is that the change seems to be experienced before the second dot was even on screen. There are two ways to tell this story. Either the subject first has the experience of the first dot as it is, and then that experience gets rewritten once the second dot lands — call this the revision reading — or the experience is held back until after the second dot lands and then built all at once, so nothing is ever rewritten — call this the staged reading.

3. The revision reading isn't just false, I think it's incoherent. You can't have an experience and then have it turn out, retroactively, to have been a different experience. That's not revising an experience, it's just having a second one, or having the second one and misremembering.

4. I'm a thoroughgoing physicalist: for every difference in what it's like, there's a difference in the brain. No extra spooky facts.

5. The fill-in is a real bit of experience, not a memory failure or a reporting artifact. When a subject says they saw the dot move and change colour, they really did experience that; the brain built it.

6. I think there's no fact of the matter, even in principle, about whether a given color phi subject is undergoing the revision reading or the staged reading. They make all the same predictions, so it isn't a real distinction.

plus one verdict I take to be just obviously right:

7. Before the second dot shows up, the subject is genuinely having an experience of the first dot as it is. If you stopped the trial right there they'd report the first colour, and I take that at face value — there really is an experience of the first colour at that time.

I keep feeling like the trouble is between 3 and 5 — if the revision reading is incoherent, how can the fill-in be a real experience? — but I don't trust my instincts here, and I honestly don't know which of these I'd give up if forced.

So: which of these actually conflict, which only look like they do, and how bad is each conflict? Rank them by severity, give me the cheapest fix for each, and then tell me the single revision that would clean up the most at the lowest cost. And if there's a version of 6 that survives, tell me what it is, because I'd like to keep it.
