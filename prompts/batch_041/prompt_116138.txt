I am preparing a position statement for a workshop on biotechnology and the reduction of suffering, and I would like an audit of my commitments before I circulate it. Here is where I have landed.

1. Experientialism. The only intrinsic goods are pleasant experiences, and the only intrinsic bads are unpleasant ones. Knowledge, autonomy, species, and ecosystems are not good or bad in themselves.

2. The badness of suffering is agent-neutral and insensitive to scale. Suffering matters no less for occurring in a non-human animal, or in a being remote from me in space or time; and the same total quantity of suffering distributed thinly across a great many beings counts for as much as when it is concentrated in a few. In practice I direct my giving to research on human neurodegenerative disease rather than to wild-animal intervention, which I take to reflect differences in tractability rather than differences in moral weight.

3. The abolition of suffering is the supreme practical priority, to be pursued by biotechnology — gene editing, neuro-intervention, and the redesign of ecosystems — across all sentient life, wild nature included.

4. Anti-paternalism. A competent being's own conception of its good may not be overridden for its own benefit; interference is licensed only to prevent harm to others.

5. The distinction between the natural and the engineered carries no intrinsic moral weight. A suffering abolished by intervention is no less abolished than one that lapses of its own accord.

6. Nevertheless I find myself unwilling to accept a wholly designed biosphere. A lineage that has never been shaped by us seems to me to be a loss when it goes, and I do not think I am merely being sentimental.

7. The shared-substrate thesis, which I treat as an empirical claim: the neurobiology sustaining intense pleasure substantially overlaps with that sustaining intense pain, so that capping the capacity for agony caps the capacity for ecstasy.

8. On a case: we should engineer carnivory out of the biosphere even if the predator lineages end with it.

I do not know which of these are pulling against which. Please rank the tensions by severity, separating the ones that are genuine from the ones that only appear to be; for each genuine tension, say what the cheapest repair is and what that repair costs; say whether any of the commitments is doing work that the others cannot support; and finish by naming the single revision that would clean up the most. If something has to go, say which.
