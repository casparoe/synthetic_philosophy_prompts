I am a fourth-year PhD candidate in decision theory, and I am drafting the take-home component of our field examination on social learning and information aggregation. I need one long-form examination question, together with a model answer and a marking scheme. The question should be answerable in roughly ninety minutes and should carry twenty-five marks. The students have already seen the canonical sequential-learning model, so the question must not be answerable by reciting that model. It should force them to work through a variant that separates the notions of a herd, a cascade, and a correct cascade, and that requires them to quantify the informational externality. Please use the following scenario, which I have constructed, and do not introduce any outside references, reading lists, or citations; the examination is closed-book and the students cannot search.

Scenario: Three risk-neutral agents A, B, and C act sequentially. The state is θ ∈ {0,1} and is equally likely. Each agent chooses adopt (1) or reject (0). The payoff is 1 if the action matches θ and 0 otherwise, minus any information-acquisition cost. Before acting, an agent may purchase a private signal s ∈ {0,1} at cost c > 0. The signal is correct with probability p ∈ (1/2, 1). The purchase decision and the signal are private, but the action is public. A moves first, B moves second, and C moves third. An agent who does not purchase a signal acts on the prior and the observed history. Agents cannot commit to a strategy before knowing their signal.

I want the question to have three parts. Part (i) should ask students to derive the perfect Bayesian equilibrium for specified values of p and c; use p = 2/3 and c = 1/20 unless the arithmetic forces a minor adjustment, and if you adjust, state why. Part (ii) should ask them to identify whether the equilibrium involves a herd, a cascade, or both, and to explain the difference in this game. It should also ask them to show that a later agent can rationally ignore the fact that an earlier agent's action was itself based on a purchased signal. Part (iii) should ask for a welfare comparison between the equilibrium and a social planner who can force B to purchase a signal. It should require students to state whether the equilibrium exhibits a negative informational externality, whether the herd is fragile to a public signal, and how the answer changes if a public signal of precision q > 1/2 is released after A acts.

The model answer should be about 800 to 1000 words, should derive the relevant posteriors explicitly, and should not simply name results. The marking scheme should total twenty-five marks, break the marks down by part and by specific move, and include for each part a one-line note on the most common error students make, such as conflating a herd with a cascade, treating a cascade as necessarily incorrect, or ignoring the information-acquisition cost in the welfare comparison. Please write the examination question first, then the model answer, then the marking scheme.
