Consider a position I'll call crane universalism: the claim that every apparent skyhook in the biological and cognitive sciences—every explanation that seems to invoke an un-decomposable cause—can in principle be replaced by a crane, i.e., a process of blind variation and selective retention, without either eliminating the phenomenon (which would be greedy reductionism) or smuggling the skyhook back in under a new name. What is your credence that crane universalism is true for the specific case of human normative self-criticism—the capacity to evaluate and revise one's own evaluative standards? Please decompose the question: the probability that the phenomenon is real and not an illusion; the probability that a crane-style account can explain its emergence; the probability that such an account can preserve its normative authority rather than debunking it; and any other term you think belongs. Give a number and one line of justification for each, then the combination. Finally, tell me which term you are least confident in and what argument or consideration would shift your overall credence most.
