Ok so I'm putting together a set for a philosophy grad student comedy night (yes, that exists, unfortunatly) and I need one joke about sphexishness. Quick refresher in case it's not on the tip of your tounge: the sphex wasp digs a burrow, paralyzes a cricket, drags it back by the antennae, drops it at the mouth of the burrow, then goes inside to inspect the nest before bringing the prey in. If you nudge the cricket a few inches away while she's in there, she'll haul it back, drop it at the mouth again, and go re-inspect the burrow. Reset, repeat, over and over, same script. Dennett and Hofstadter both made this the poster child for mindless, machine-like behavior that looks thoughtful but is really just a routine running — hence "sphexishness" for the same thing in humans. There's a wrinkle too: a philosopher named Keijzer pushed back on the story, noting the wasp does eventually adapt if you keep messing with it, and that re-inspecting a nest that some giant keeps disturbing is arguably just sensible. And the anecdote itself has been copied from textbook to textbook for decades with not much re-checking, which is its own kind of ironic.

I want you to write me a joke that would actually land with someone who knows this stuff. Take on the role of a philosopher of mind who moonlights as a stand-up, think carefully about what's genuinely funny here instead of reaching for the obvious robot/zombie gag, and start by brainstorming 3-4 angles (the reset loop, the antennae thing where she won't just grab a leg, the free will angle, the meta-joke about the anecdote itself being sphexish) before you commit to one. The punchline should turn on the actual content — the rigidity, the failure to register that the situation has changed, the way a mind can be running a script it can't see — and not just on "lol bugs are dumb." It also has to make some amount of sense: a philosopher of mind hearing it shouldn't think I have no idea what sphexishness is. Give me the setup and punchline, then one line on why it works.
