I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, and I keep running into a distinction at work that I can't tell whether philosophy has already named. The short version: some systems have internal states that we describe as being about something — a thermostat's reading is about the temperature, a chess engine's evaluation is about who is winning, a trained model's activations are about whatever they are about — and I can't work out what settles the "about what" question. It seems to be settled from outside the system, and in more than one way. Sometimes the answer is what the designer intended the state to track. Sometimes it is what the state actually tracks in the world, which may be something else entirely — a detector that was supposed to key on one property but in fact keys on a correlate that happened to be present in the data it was fitted to. Sometimes it is simply what we, the users, find it convenient to read the state as, as when a subsystem is repurposed and its old meaning is retained by convention. These three can point in different directions at once, and when they do, nobody concludes that the state means nothing; the content gets reassigned, and usually to whichever reading makes the system's behavior come out as sensible rather than arbitrary.

What I want to know is whether there is an established name for the contrast I think I'm seeing: between content that a system has only because an interpreter assigns it, and content that a system has in virtue of its own causal and functional organization. If there is, I'd like to know how closely the standard version matches what I've described, which of the neighboring ideas it is closest to — treating a system as if it had beliefs and desires, content fixed by what a thing was selected or designed to do, the difference between a state that represents and a state that merely correlates — and how it differs from each. I'm not after a label. I want the phenomenon carved: whether a system can have the assigned kind of aboutness and none of the grounded kind; whether the grounded kind can exist with no interpreter anywhere; whether the difference is one of kind or of degree; what happens when two interpreters assign conflicting content to the same device and there is no fact about which of them is right; and whether there is a name for the specific pattern I keep hitting, in which the content we assign and the content the system's own workings would support come apart, and the assignment quietly follows the interpreter's convenience rather than the machine's.
