Ok, I need a sanaty check on something before I write it up, because I keep going back and forth and I've stopped trusting my own ear on it. I'm trying to state my position on the precautionary principle for a workshop paper, and here's what I actually think when I'm not trying to make it sound tidy:

1. Strong precaution. Where the evidence points to a credible prospect of serious or irreversible harm, the fact that we can't nail it down scientifically yet is not a reason to hold off on measures that would be cheap and effective.
2. Precaution is not a decision rule. It's a burden-shifting device: it tells you who has to make the running — the proponent of the risky activity — not what the verdict should be.
3. Precaution has to be symmetric. Regulating is an action with its own consequences, and those get counted like any other consequences. If the ban kills people too, the ban answers for that.
4. But there's still a legitimate asymmetry between incumbents and newcomers. Stuff already in use gets grandfathered; novel practices have to earn their place. Without something like that you get paralysis, because for any new thing there's a story about how it might go wrong.
5. Formally I think the right home for all this is maximin over models: take the set of models that survive the evidence, and pick the act whose worst outcome across that set is least bad.
6. Substantively, though, I'm a boring expected-utility person. Knightian uncertainty is a myth — "no probabilities" just means second-order uncertainty — and rational choice is expected utility maximization under some distribution, full stop.

And a concrete case to keep me honest: given a new chemical where I have a plausible mechanism plus one small field study, I'd regulate. Given an old chemical and exactly the same evidence, I wouldn't. That feels obviously right to me, and I'd like to know whether it should.

So: which of these actually fight each other, which pairs only look like they fight, and what's the cheapest way to get a coherent package out of it? Rank the real tensions by how bad they are, say what specifically has to give in each case, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I don't want a survey of the literature, I want to know where my own set is load-bearing and where it's just decoration.
