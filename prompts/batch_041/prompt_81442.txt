This isn't really a legal question, or maybe it is, but the legal answer isn't the one I'm after. Last winter four of us were living in a rented house with an old gas boiler in the cellar, and over about ten days four things happened. I heard the boiler make a wierd noise one morning and assumed one of the others would deal with it. Priya found a letter from the gas company saying the annual safety check was overdue and left it on the kitchen table. Tom, who is a qualified heating engineer and was staying with us for a fortnight between jobs, smelt gas in the hall one evening and figured the landlord would get round to it. And Sam -- the only one of us on the tenancy agreement, and the one who'd had two emails from the letting agent about the check -- didn't do anything either. On the 19th there was a small explosion. Nobody was seriously hurt, but the cellar door is in the garden and we've all been in hotels since. Everyone blames Sam. Sam says that's arbitrary: if any one of us had picked up the phone it wouldn't have happened, and anyway the engineer who finally came said the fault was the kind a routine check often misses, so who's to say the check would even have caught it. Which is fine, except then I don't know what it is Sam is supposed to have done wrong.

What's really chewing at me is that I can't find the line. If my not ringing the plumber counts as a cause of the explosion, then so does my not knocking on the cellar door the night before, and my not becoming a heating engineer, and my not buying the house ten years ago and fixing the boiler myself. All of those are true in the same way: if I'd done any of them, no explosion. But saying I caused it by not buying the house is obviously stupid, and I can't say why it's different from not ringing the plumber. My girlfriend says it's about what you were supposed to do, but that feels backwards -- we decide who we're allowed to be angry at, and then we go back and declare that person the cause. And if that's the whole story, then Tom knowing exactly what that smell meant counts for nothing, since nobody expected a guest to do anything, while Sam, who understood less than Tom, is the one we've all settled on.

So: is Sam right that it's arbitrary? If not, what actually separates his doing-nothing from mine, and from my not buying the house? I'd like the real shape of the argument rather than "it's a matter of perspective" -- I'm happy to be told Sam is the one at fault, I just want to know what's doing the work when we say so.
