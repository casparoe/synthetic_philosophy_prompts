Ok so I've been going back and forth on the parodox of tragedy and I think I've basically solved it, but I want a sanity check before I get too smug about it. The paradox is supposed to be: why do we enjoy sad movies if sadness is unpleasant? But this seems to me like it's just the paradox of fiction wearing a costume. The only genuinely weird thing is that we get emotional about people who don't exist — and honestly even that isn't that weird, because we can get worked up about hypotheticals and daydreams all the time, and nobody treats that as a paradox. And since the characters aren't real, the sadness isn't real sadness anyway, it's more like make-believe sadness that we're playing along with. So once you notice that, there's nothing left to explain, right? The "tragedy" part is just a special case of make-believe emotions. And if there's anything left over it's just that people enjoy the catharsis, or the safe thrill of feeling something intense with no real stakes, and that seems like a pretty complete answer to me. So why do philosophers keep going on about the paradox of tragedy like it's some deep unsolved problem? Is there something I'm missing, or is this just a case of a puzzle that disolves the moment you stop assuming the sadness is real?
