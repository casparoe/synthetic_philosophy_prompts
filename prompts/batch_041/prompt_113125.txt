My cousin is a grad student in comparitive ethics and last Thanksgiving he gave me his spiel on Confucian role ethics. I wrote it down so I'd have it straight:

"Confucian role ethics says the self is nothing over and above its roles. You're a son, a father, a student, a friend, and that's all there is -- there's no little inner person hiding behind the roles. Ethics, then, is about performing your roles well, not about following rules or maximizing outcomes. But here's the problem: since it tells you to love your parents more than strangers and to defer to your elders, it's basically a philosophical defense of favoritism, and it flatly denies that all human beings are morally equal. That's why it can't be squared with human rights or with any impartialist ethics. Mencius doubles down when he jumps on Mozi for saying we should care about everyone equally -- for him that's a crime against your own father. So what you end up with is a deeply conservative, family-first ethics where you can never step outside the role you were born into and criticize the system. Which is the opposite of what I want from an ethics."
