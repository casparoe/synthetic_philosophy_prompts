My office mate's been handing down verdicts on time travel cases in our reading group for weeks and I want to get ahead of what she'll say about the one we're doing Thursday.

The question she answers is always the same: is this a possible history, yes or no. Her rulings so far:

- Traveler goes back and kills his grandfather before his father is conceived. No. Not unlikely -- she says there's no history there at all, the description is just contradictory.

- Same trip, but every attempt fails and the trip itself is what introduces his grandparents to each other. Yes. Not a paradox, just a loop, and the feeling that the universe is protecting itself is an artifact of us describing the whole thing from the outside.

- Bootstrap: he has the plans for the machine because his older self gave them to him, and the older self has them because he handed them to his younger self. Nothing causes the plans. Yes. Causal loops aren't contradictions, she says, and the demand that every fact have an origin outside the loop is just a prejudice.

- The billiard ball: ball goes into the wormhole mouth, comes out in the past, hits its earlier self and knocks it off course so it never enters. Yes -- there's a fixed point if you let the ball go through twice, and nothing has to conspire.

Thursdays case: a lab sends a signal back through the machine to stop a reactor meltdown. There is a consistent solution -- signal arrives, crew acts on it, meltdown averted, signal sent. But it only exists if the frequency is dialed to about one part in 10^30. Move it a hair and you get a flat contradiction of the kill-your-grandfather kind: signal stops meltdown, so no signal is sent, so meltdown, so signal is sent.

So whats your call, yes or no? I want the rule she's been running over those four, the other rules that fit them just as well, what each predicts here, which you'd bet on and why, and the case I should put in front of her that would actually seperate them. And tell me honestly if the first four don't pin down anything -- I've been assuming they do.

(She's an eternalist, past is as real as the future to her, in case that's doing work. And she means possible history literally: yes means there's a history satisfying all of it, not that this could happen to you.)
