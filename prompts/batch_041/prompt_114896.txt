I have no academic background — I run a small shop — but I have spent much of the past year thinking about the difference between doing something that ends a person's life and allowing their life to end, because my aunt has been a hospice nurse for more than twenty years and I keep putting cases to her. She is patient with me but she is not a theorist; when I press her for her rule she says only that she will stop doing things to people but will not do things to them. I would like you to work out what she will say about a new case. I want your own considered judgement, not a survey of what other people think.

Her verdicts so far, as she has given them to me:

1. A patient in unrelievable distress begs her for an injection to end her life. She refuses, and says she would refuse even if it were lawful, and even though she agrees the suffering is unbearable.

2. A patient on a ventilator, awake and able to speak, asks for it to be withdrawn. She withdraws it, and regards that as plainly right.

3. A patient who is expected to need a ventilator and who says she does not want one: my aunt regards it as plainly right not to start it.

4. A patient who can no longer swallow and who has said she does not want to be fed through a tube: right not to place one, and right to remove one that is already in place.

5. A patient dying of cancer who is frightened and breathless and who tells her she does not want to struggle for breath. My aunt increases the morphine until the patient is comfortable, knowing it will probably shorten her life by a few hours. She says this is right, and denies flatly that she has ever hastened a death.

6. A dying woman whose implanted defibrillator keeps firing and causing her agony, and who asks for it to be switched off. My aunt thinks that is right, though she says it feels different from taking away a ventilator, and that she would want the heart specialist to be the one to switch it off.

7. A patient's family, worn out, ask her to give a large dose of sedative to a patient who is suffering but who has not asked to die and has not asked to be made unaware. She refuses.

8. A woman in the last days of dying of cancer develops a chest infection. The family ask for antibiotics; my aunt withholds them, saying that antibiotics would not cure her of what is killing her and would only draw the dying out. The woman had said, while she could still speak, that she wanted nothing done that would only prolong things.

The new case. A woman of about eighty with advanced dementia, in a nursing home. Two years ago, when she could still recognise her family and hold a conversation, she signed a document saying that if she ever reached the point of no longer recognising her family she did not want to be kept alive, and naming chest infections in particular as something she did not want treated. She now recognises no one. She is not, however, in any obvious distress: she eats with help, smiles at the staff, seems to enjoy music, and shows no sign of pain. Six months ago, on what the staff call a good day, she said she was happy and wanted to stay. She is not dying of anything else. She has developed a chest infection. With antibiotics she will probably recover and go on much as she is for an indefinite period; without them she will probably die of it within a week or so. Her family are united in wanting it left untreated and say they are following her document. My aunt is the nurse on duty, and if she does not ring the doctor and ask for antibiotics, no one will, and the woman will die.

Tell me what my aunt will say, and why. Set out the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that would fit every one of the verdicts above just as well, and what each of those rules predicts about the new case. Say which of her earlier verdicts is doing most of the work in your prediction, and name the further case that would best tell the rival rules apart. Where her past answers genuinely leave the new case open, say so rather than manufacturing a consistency she does not have.

I want a committed answer: pick the verdict you think she will give, say how confident you are in it and why, and give the reasons that decided you, rather than laying out the options and leaving me to choose. Please write in British English.
