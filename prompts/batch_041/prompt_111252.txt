I run a small political theory reading group and next month's guest is a philosopher who's been pushing a line on the boundary problem — you know, the "who belongs to the demos in the first place" mess. Her claim is that it's a psuedo-problem: the circularity only bites if you insist that "the people" has to exist as a single collective agent before it can authorise anything, and she wants to swap that for an account where the demos is a standing, always-revisable question, constituted by decisions it retroactively ratifies and kept legitimate only by staying open to challenge. I need 8-10 questions that actually get inside this, not softballs. I want at least one on the circularity itself (doesn't "open to challenge" just presuppose a body that decides whose challenges count?), one on whether her view quietly collapses into the all-affected principle and inherits its "everything affects everything" indeterminacy, and one on the ugly cases — a demos that excludes a minority and simply refuses to entertain the challenge; secession; resident non-citizens; future generations. Give each question a follow-up aimed at the answer she's most likely to give, and be upfront about where you're guessing at her position rather than reasoning from it. Also: what would actually change your mind — what answer from her would convince you she's solved the boundary problem rather than just renamed it?
