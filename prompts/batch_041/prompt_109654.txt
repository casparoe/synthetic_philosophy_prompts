For my philosophy club I wrote up the following argument, and now I am not sure whether it is a reductio of the extended mind thesis or simply a bad argument I should abandon. (1) A state or process belongs to a person's mind when it plays the functional role an internal state would play, provided it is reliably available when needed and its deliverances are accepted without deliberation. (2) Otto's notebook does exactly that for his beliefs about the museum, so it is part of his mind. (3) But my phone, the search engine on it, my planner, my best friend, and the public library all play that same role whenever I can reach them: they are reliable enough for the purpose and I trust them without stopping to deliberate. (4) Nothing in (1) requires the resource to be carried on my person or owned by me. (5) So they are all parts of my mind. (6) But if the boundary of my mind is fixed by whatever I happen to be reliably coupled to, then it is fixed by nothing in particular, and the mind swells until it takes in the whole world. (7) That cannot be right. Which step should I give up, and what does giving it up cost? I would rather keep (2) than (7), but I want to know what I am paying either way, and why the step I give up looked so plausible in the first place.
