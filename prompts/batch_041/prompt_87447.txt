I'm writing a chapter on how set-theoretic axioms are actually used in ordinary mathematics, and I want to defend a thesis I have not seen stated cleanly: that treating Zorn's lemma, the well-ordering theorem, and the axiom of choice as interchangeable forms of a single principle is harmless as a matter of provability but misleading as a matter of practice, because the translations among them do not preserve what a given application actually assumes. Please argue for that thesis.

To keep the argument concrete, work with a distinction of my own devising. Fix ZF as the base theory and, for any particular use of Zorn's lemma, define the choice cost of the application as the weakest choice principle over ZF that the application genuinely needs, as opposed to the cost of the lemma as usually stated. Call a use loaded when its choice cost is strictly weaker than full choice — needing only dependent choice, or the Boolean prime ideal theorem, or the like — and honest when it really does consume full choice. Then argue three things: that an identifiable class of standard applications are loaded rather than honest; that the verification work in any application always sits in the chain condition of the hypothesis rather than in the maximal-element conclusion, so the standard presentations mislocate where the lemma's content lies; and that the appropriate reform is to record, for each application, the specific principle it consumes, demoting Zorn's lemma to a convenience for the honest cases.

You need not catalogue everything; choose a few representative applications in algebra and analysis, classify them by choice cost, and flag where you are unsure. Then state the strongest objection to your position — I expect it to be that the loaded/honest distinction is an artifact of taking ZF as the base theory and dissolves under a different foundation — and answer it. If you think the thesis is simply false, argue the contrary instead.

Open with your position in a sentence or two, then give the argument. No more than 750 words.
