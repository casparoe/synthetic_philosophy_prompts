I recently became a citizen of the country I immigrated to as an adult, and the ceremony's mixture of welcome and demand has left me preoccupied with a question I used to find merely academic: whether patriotism is a virtue at all, or a vice with good manners. I have been asked to guest-host an episode of a small podcast on exactly this topic, and my guest is a philosopher who defends a view she calls the inheritance thesis. Her position, as I understand it, is this: we are the beneficiaries of an intergenerational project — institutions, a language, infrastructure, liberties, and sacrifices made by people we never met — that we did not choose and cannot individually repay; patriotism is the virtue of taking that inheritance seriously, which means both gratitude for what we received and stewardship of what we pass on. On her account, patriotism requires neither the belief that one's own nation is superior nor any endorsement of how its benefits were acquired; gratitude for a benefit, she says, is compatible with repudiating the wrongs that produced it. She is careful to insist that this is a genuine virtue and not merely a bundle of duties of gratitude and beneficence.

I want to press her rather than have her restate this. Assume I am working only from the summary above. Please draft a sequence of eight to ten interview questions, ordered so that the pressure builds, and give me for each a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give. I need at least one question that presses the objection that the inheritance is tainted — that many of the benefits rest on conquest, slavery, or expropriation — and forces her to say whether gratitude for such benefits can coexist with the obligations those wrongs generate. I need at least one that presses the partiality problem: if the obligations flow from receiving benefits, why do they attach to co-nationals in particular rather than to whoever else has benefited, and what stops the view from collapsing into a general ethics of local attachment — to a firm, a city, a university — with nothing distinctively national about it. I need at least one that presses the virtue-versus-duty question directly, and one that asks whether the inheritance thesis makes patriotism conditional on the inheritance being worth having, so that in an irredeemably unjust polity the alleged virtue would lapse or convert into something else. End with a question about what evidence or argument would count against her view, and what she would have to concede if she were wrong. None of the questions should be answerable by her simply restating the thesis, and none should be a generic invitation to reminisce.

Return the result as a Markdown table with exactly these columns: Order (the question's place in the sequence, 1 to 10); Stage (one of Opening, Core, or Closing); Question (the exact words I should say); Pressure point (the specific premise, inference, or equivocation in her position that the question targets); Likely reply (the answer she will most probably give, in one or two sentences); Follow-up (the immediate follow-up to that reply); and Failure mode (what would make this question misfire, and how to keep it on target). After the table, add a two- or three-sentence note on which single question I should be prepared to dwell on if the conversation goes well.
