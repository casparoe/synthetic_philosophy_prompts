I'm a second-year PhD student in philosophy who came over from physics, so I can read a bit of math but I'm not going to prove anything new, and I'm trying to decide what to actually spend the next three years on. I keep circling the cluster of questions around chaos, determinism and predictability, and I have alot of candidates and no way to choose between them, so I want someone to tell me what deserves the time and then defend the choice. The annoying thing is that my friend Priya, who's a physicist, keeps deflating everything I bring up: she says chaos is just sensitive dependence on initial conditions plus finite precision, that determinism and predictability were never the same claim in the first place, and that once you see that, most of the philosophical literature here is either a confusion or a footnote to the shadowing lemma (numerical trajectories that wander off the true one are still tracked by some true trajectory, so the statistics survive). I half believe her. But I also think something is left over: the idea that some deterministic systems are computationally irreducible, so there's no shortcut to the future and you have to run them step by step; whether the "effective randomness" you get out of a chaotic system is a fact about the world or about our access to it; the asymmetry between forecasting forward and reconstructing backward; whether any of this bears on free will or is just a red herring nobody can let go of; and the butterfly thing, which everyone cites and nobody I know can state as a precise claim about what it's supposed to support. Laplace's demon also bothers me — I suspect it smuggles in more than determinism, like the state being finitely specifiable and the dynamics being computable from it, but I can't tell if that's a real point or a nitpick.

So: of the open conceptual questions in this area, which two or three should someone with about three years, no plans for original mathematics, and a co-author who's already skeptical actually work on, and in what order? The end product is a dissertation that's a real contribution plus maybe a couple of papers pulled out of it, so I care about questions that are upstream of others rather than just interesting on their own, and about whether a question can be moved with conceptual work instead of new theorems. If the honest answer is that the best question is one I haven't even named, say so.

Give it to me as four sections. First "Bottom line", about 150 words, naming the two or three questions I should take up, in order, and saying plainly which of my list is a waste of three years. Then "Why this order", roughly 600 words, going question by question and giving me the criteria you're using — how many other questions hang on this one, how tractable it is for someone doing philosophy rather than physics, whether it survives Priya's deflationary take, and how much would actually change downstream if it got settled. Then "What I should stop circling", about 250 words. Then "What would change the ranking", about 250 words, on the argument or result over the next year or so that would reshuffle things. No reading list please, I can find the papers myself — I want the argument for the ordering.
