I'm running the last session of a reading group on mechanistic interpretability and I want to close it with a poem rather than a summary — a ghazal, 9 or 11 couplets, about superposition. The thing I want the form to do rather than describe: a network has way more features than dimensions, so one direction in activation space ends up doing duty for a pile of unrelated things, and a neuron that fires for one thing fires for a dozen others. So every couplet should end on the same rhyme and the same repeated word or phrase (the radif), but each couplet should be off in its own world — if the radif is working, you should feel one shared element getting quietly reused for things that have nothing to do with each other. What I dont want is for the poem to settle the question the group has been arguing about all term: is that recurring element one real feature the model represents, or just a direction that several genuinely distinct features happen to share, or something we've imposed by choosing one dictionary (sparse autoencoders, probes) over another? Keep it open — and maybe let the closing couplet, where the poet usually names himself, name the interpreter instead and leave the reader there. Let one couplet turn on the difference between stuff we can decode out of the activations and stuff the model actually uses, since I think thats the distinction that decides the realism question. And please dont let it become a lecture in verse — the technical words should be earning there place. Someone whos trained a sparse autoencoder should recognize their own problem in it; someone who hasnt should still get a poem.
