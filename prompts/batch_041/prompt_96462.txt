I spent thirty years in a chemistry department, retired four years ago, and through a series of poor decisions I now teach a 200-level science-and-its-history course for non-majors. I'm grading the final and I want a second opnion on one answer, because I honestly can't tell whether I'm being too soft on it or too hard.

The question I set was this: "Newton and Leibniz arrived at the calculus independently, and Darwin and Wallace arrived at natural selection independently. Does that show the discoveries were inevitable — that somebody was going to get there around then no matter what — and does it affect who deserves credit for them? Then apply your answer to this case: two labs publish essentially the same result a year apart; the first to publish had a special case, the second had the general version and had it in a notebook two years earlier; a third lab had published a rough version of the idea a decade before in a minor journal, and nobody noticed."

The student wrote:

"Newton and Leibniz worked out the calculus within a few decades of each other, and Darwin and Wallace both hit on natural selection in the 1850s. When two people arrive independently at the same result, the result was waiting to be found — the techniques and the evidence were already in place, so if either man had never lived, the other would have gotten there anyway. Discovery is less about individual genius than about the state of the field. Much the same goes for the two labs: because they converged on the same result, neither can really claim it as his own, and joint credit — the way Newton and Leibniz, and Darwin and Wallace, are both credited now — is the sensible convention. Who is listed first doesn't matter much, since the discovery was going to happen either way. That said, Darwin deserves extra credit for the twenty years of evidence he piled up before publishing, which is why the theory carries his name."

Give it a grade out of 10 and the comments that justify it, as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no wrap-up: the grade first, then the four comments, most important first. And if I've misread the answer, or if my question was loose enough that all the student's moves are defensible, say so plainly rather than manufacturing a fault to justify a number.
