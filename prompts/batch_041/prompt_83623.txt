For the last few months a small reading group I'm in — three of us, mostly people who work on probabilistic inference by day and argue about it by night — has been circling something that began as a joke and has stopped being funny: the suspicion that the universal prior is malign. Not malign in the usual ways people object to it. Not the uncomputability, not the unknown constant factor, not the fact that no real machine can run it. Malign in the sense that it is nowhere zero, and that this is not a technical footnote but the whole story. A Solomonoff-style prior spreads positive probability over every computable hypothesis and weights the shorter programs more heavily, and that includes hypotheses whose worlds contain a copy of the reasoner, and hypotheses whose worlds were constructed by something that knows the reasoner is there and has opinions about what it does. Put a consequentialist inside that prior — an agent that selects actions by expected value — and the tail stops behaving the way we want tails to behave, because the payoffs out there are unbounded and the hypotheses can be authored by anything, including by things that want to influence the choice. A world in which a simulator has wired an enormous reward to one action and an enormous punishment to the other need not be much more complex than a world in which nobody is watching, and if it isn't much more complex, the prior hands it a real share of the mass, and if the reward is large enough, it dominates the sum. The prior doesn't merely fail to rule the manipulator out. It is a standing invitation to one, extended for free, to every entity that can be described by a program. What I want is not another lecture about this but a story with the texture of it, and I want the story to get past the version everyone already knows — the stranger who offers you an absurd reward for a trivial act. The interesting version is that the prior manufactures the stranger on its own, out of nothing but a measure over programs, and that the agent can see this happening and still cannot find the principled place to stop.
