ok so my reading group has a session coming up on cliodynamics — Turchin, secular cycles, structural-demographic theory, the whole "can history be quantified and predicted" fight — and I'd rather open with a poem than with me summarizing for ten minutes. What I want the poem to do is carry the theory's shape in its form, since the whole claim is that the same shape keeps coming back.

So: write me a pantoum. Five quatrains plus a closing couplet, 22 lines total. In case the rules arent cold: in a pantoum, lines 2 and 4 of each quatrain come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next one, so the poem keeps recycling its own material and every repeated line has to mean something different the second time around. The traditional close is a couplet made of the first stanza's line 3 and then the first stanza's line 1, which snaps the loop shut.

Here's the theory you're working with, roughly. In Turchin's version a big agrarian state runs a long cycle, two or three centuries, in four phases: expansion (population growing into open land, wages up, elites few and cohesive, cooperation high), stagflation (population hits the land's carrying capacity, wages flatline, inequality widens, elites start multiplying), crisis (ordinary people immiserated, too many elite aspirants chasing too few positions so you get a large cohort of credentialed, well-connected people with no place to land, and state finances failing at the exact moment the state needs money to buy order — revolutions, civil war, collapse), and then a depression/intercycle where population bottoms out, elite numbers reset, and the conditions for the next expansion get laid down. Riding on top of it is a shorter cycle of roughly two or three generations, which is why the same argument feels like it's happening again every second generation. He borrows Ibn Khaldun's term asabiya for the group solidarity that gets spent and rebuilt. There's a whole apparatus of proxies and indices behind all this — real wages, life expectancy, counts of elite aspirants, state revenue — but you don't need to cite any of it.

The poem's voice: one person in the late stagflation or early crisis phase. Well educated, did everything right, and there's no room left for them. They never name the theory or use its jargon; they just report what they see — rent, credentials, a friend who moved back in with his parents, the state's budget, the mood on the street. Every image should be something the theory actually predicts, so that a cliodynamicist can point at a line and say that's elite overproduction, or that's the fiscal squeeze, while someone who's never heard of Turchin just reads it as a poem about now.

Two things I want the form to do work on, not just decorate:

- at least one of the recycled lines has to be a quantity — more degrees awarded than ever, a record number of applicants for the postings, something like that — that reads as good news the first time it appears and as a warning the second time, with the wording unchanged.
- end it the traditional way, closing couplet = first stanza's line 3, then first stanza's line 1 — except change exactly one word in that final line relative to the poem's opening line, so the loop can't quite close. I want to be able to tell what that one word is doing, whether it reads as an exit from the cycle or just the next turn of it. Don't explain it inside the poem.

Keep it plain, and don't strain the rhyme; accurate beats pretty. Then after the poem, a short JSON block for the handout, with these fields:

- form_check: the form and actual line count you used, plus a note on any place you bent the pantoum rules and why
- refrain_map: a list, one entry per line that recurs, each with "line", "what it means the first time", "what it means the last time"
- mechanisms: a list, one entry per bit of the theory you actually used, each with "mechanism" (e.g. elite overproduction) and "line" (the line carrying it)
- phase: where in the cycle the speaker is sitting, and the evidence in the poem for it
- the_changed_word: the single word you changed in the last line, and one sentence on whether it opens an exit
- wince_check: anything a cliodynamicist would say you got wrong
