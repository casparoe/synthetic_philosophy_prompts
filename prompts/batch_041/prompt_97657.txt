Ok so I'm recently retired — 34 years in combustion research, mostly flames and how they hold together — and I've fallen down the metaphysics rabbit hole since. Two people in my little reading group have been going at each other for months: one holds that an object is wholly present at every moment it exists (she calls herself an endurantist, or a three-dimensionalist), the other says objects are spread out in time and have temporal parts the way they're spread out in space (a four-dimensionalist, perdurantist, whatever). Honestly half the time I think they agree about everything and are just talking past eachother, and the other half I think they really do disagree about something.

I want you to write the joint statement the two of them could actually both sign, after a real good-faith collaboration — not a debate transcript, an agreed document. Here's what I want in it:

- A numbered list of claims they both accept. Including the boring ones — that things change, that identity across time is what the whole fight is about, that they're not disagreeing about any measurement I could ever make, that sort of thing. Don't pad it, but don't leave out the obvious stuff either.
- Then the disagreements, each one written so the person who holds that side would say "yes, that's my view" — neutral wording, no strawmen.
- The cruxes: the sub-questions where, if you settled them, at least one of them would have to give ground.
- For each crux, a case or argument they'd both agree actually bears on it, and what each side predicts about it going in.

Couple of things I specifically want them to chew on, because they're what got me thinking:

1. My flame. A gas-jet flame that's been burning for six hours has none of the same molecules it started with, but it's still, obviously, "the same flame" — same shape, same place, feeding itself. Is that persistence, or just a pattern? Do the two views say anything different here, or do they agree on all the facts and differ only on how to describe it?
2. If I stick a wire through a candle flame and it splits into two — did the original flame become two, or did it die and two new ones start? Same question for a dividing amoeba if you like. Does identity survive splitting? Each side should say what it predicts.
3. The "two things in one place" business — the statue and the clay it's made of. How does each view count what's there?
4. And does the physics help at all? Since simultaneity is frame-relative, there's no universal "now" — does that actually favor the four-dimensional picture, or does it just look like it does? I have a suspicion this one is doing less work than people think, but let them say.

Finish by telling me honestly whether any part of their disagreement is just verbal — a fight about words, or about how we count — and if so, which part. I'd rather you say so than manufacture a seperate disagreement that isn't there.

Table for the disagreements please, I like tables.
