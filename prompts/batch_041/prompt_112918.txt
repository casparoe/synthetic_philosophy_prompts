ok so i want to write a parody self-help column for a newsletter — the deadpan kind of thing a smug productivity/wellness guru posts, "the 5 rules of intention-based manifesting" energy — except the whole system he's selling is secretly just Kavka's toxin puzzle, and he has no idea he's walked into it. the puzzle, in case you need it: an eccentric billionaire puts a vial of toxin in front of you; drink it and you're painfully ill for a day, no lasting damage. he'll pay you a million bucks tomorrow morning if at midnight tonight you genuinely intend to drink it tomorrow afternoon — he pays on the intention, the money's already in your account hours before drinking time, and you're completely free to change your mind and skip the toxin. so by the time the afternoon rolls around you're already rich and have zero reason to drink the stuff. give me the guru's five rules as a numbered list — exactly five items, one or two sentences each, no intro, no outro, no sign-off, just the rules. the jokes have to come out of the actual machinery of the puzzle rather than generic guru-speak: each rule should sell a real feature of the paradox as though it were a feature of his system (that the payoff attaches to the intention and not the act, that you can't just fake an intention, that you can't intend something you're certain you won't do, that once the cash has landed the drinking is pointless, that his own success story is the reductio). someone who's taught the puzzle in a seminar should laugh rather than wince, and the guy should never once notice hes living inside a paradox — if anything he should be a little proud of it.
