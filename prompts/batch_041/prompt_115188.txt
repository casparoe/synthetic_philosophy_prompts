I am a junior at a high school with a small cognitive science club, and I would like help with a question that has been nagging at me since the start of the term. Our club's regular warm-up is a set of what our faculty advisor calls transformation puzzles. Each puzzle consists of two short strings of lowercase letters, written without spaces, which show how the first string changes into the second; the puzzle then supplies a third string and asks what that third string changes into. Nothing is ever explained, and no reason for the change is ever given. The first puzzle we were given was "abc becomes abd; what does ijk become?"

One of the other members, Priya, answers these in a few seconds without writing anything down, and I have been keeping a record of her answers because I want to understand what she is actually doing. Here is everything she has said so far:

1. abc becomes abd; what does ijk become? — ijl
2. abc becomes abd; what does mno become? — mnp
3. abc becomes abd; what does ihg become? — ihh
4. abc becomes abd; what does icj become? — ick
5. mno becomes mnp; what does qrs become? — qrt
6. mno becomes mnp; what does aab become? — aac

Next week's puzzle is "abc becomes abd; what does mnopq become?"

Typing the record out, I have noticed something that may matter: every string Priya has ever been shown, both the example pair and the string she has to complete, has been exactly three letters long. That is probably why I cannot settle on an answer for the new puzzle, and why I suspect she is following a rule that her record does not yet pin down. What I would like to know is, first, what she will write for mnopq; second, what rule she is running, together with the other rules that fit her six answers equally well; third, what each of those rules predicts for the new puzzle, and which prediction you would bet on, with your reasons; and fourth, which single puzzle I should give her next, and what her answers under the surviving rules would show that the puzzle had done its job. If a rule fits her answers and predicts something I have not considered, I would like to hear about that as well.
