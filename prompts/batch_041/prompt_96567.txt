I am a novelist at work on a speculative novel, and I need a document that two of my characters -- and, more importantly, two philosophical positions -- could both sign. The setting is a careenium: a brain made of an enormous number of billiard balls in a sealed, deterministic Newtonian box, with no additional forces and no quantum indeterminacy. The only things that exist in the box are the balls, their positions, and their collisions. Yet the careenium exhibits stable macroscopic patterns -- simmballs -- that persist, merge, divide, and interact in ways that track features of the environment and that we would ordinarily call symbols and inferences. One of my characters, the Pattern Causalist, holds that the simmballs are real causes: when the careenium tokens a symbol, that symbol pushes the substrate around, and any complete causal account that stops at the balls is incomplete. The other, the Substrate Causalist, holds that only the balls push; simmballs are indispensable summaries, perhaps even real patterns, but they do no causal work, and every effect is fixed by the exact path of the balls. They have argued past each other for years. Everything you need is described here; do not assume any particular source or literature.

Draft the joint statement they should have produced. It should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign -- about what the careenium is, what the simmballs are, what follows and what does not follow from the fact that every ball's motion is fixed by the laws, what it means to say that one thing pushes another, and what would count as a difference-maker; (2) a table of their remaining disagreements, each side's position phrased in wording that side would accept; (3) the cruxes -- the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side -- and for each crux a concrete case or intervention both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. They must address at least these cases: (a) two careenia with different micro-realizations but the same simmball trajectory; (b) a careenium whose simmball is preserved while the exact paths of its balls are altered; (c) an intervention that changes a simmball without changing any individual ball's law, versus an intervention on individual balls that leaves the simmball intact; and (d) a careenium in which a simmball is systematically correlated with an environmental feature but is causally idle at the level of the balls. If you conclude that part of their dispute is merely verbal -- for instance, about what cause or push is allowed to mean -- say which part and why. Do not give me citations, reading lists, or a literature review; I want the joint statement itself, written so that both could sign it, including the parts that state their disagreement.
