I have been trying to get clear on the metaphysics of dispositions, and I have ended up defending a version of the counterfactual analysis that I would like you to stress-test. On the bare version, to say that a thing is disposed to behave in a certain way when certain conditions obtain is simply to say that it would so behave if those conditions were to obtain. I know the bare version is too crude, so the position I actually want to hold is the repaired one: a thing is disposed to M in circumstances C just in case, were C to obtain while the thing retained whatever it is about it that grounds the disposition and no interfering condition were present, it would M. I am drawn to this because it promises to explain dispositions without positing anything occult beyond ordinary properties and counterfactuals, and because it seems to make dispositional claims answerable to how things would actually go.

I am aware of the usual battery of counterexamples, and I do not want you merely to recite them. There are cases in which a thing loses the disposition at the very moment the trigger arrives, so that the counterfactual comes out false although the disposition was plainly there; cases in which the disposition survives but something blocks its manifestation, so that the counterfactual comes out false for the wrong reason; and cases in which the characteristic behaviour occurs without the disposition, so that the counterfactual comes out true for the wrong reason. What I want instead is a map of my position's exposure. For each kind of case, tell me precisely what my repaired version gets wrong and what it would cost me to patch it, and then assess whether the patches actually work. In particular, I want to know whether adding a clause about the disposition's basis remaining intact, or a clause ruling out interfering factors, is a genuine repair or whether it smuggles in the very notion it was supposed to analyse, leaving the view true by stipulation rather than by discovery.

Three things especially trouble me and I would like them taken seriously rather than waved away: dispositions that are irreducibly probabilistic, where the thing really is disposed to behave in a certain way yet the corresponding counterfactual seems simply false because the manifestation is not guaranteed; dispositions that have many different manifestations depending on circumstance, so that it is unclear what the consequent of the conditional should even be; and whether my account predicts anything that a rival, which treats dispositions as irreducible, does not also predict, so that it risks nothing at all. Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which of them a defender of the analysis would themselves concede as counting against the view rather than merely dismissing, and be honest about whether what I have stated has been insulated from refutation and whether that insulation is a defect in a view of this kind. I think I would be most moved by a case in which the analysis's own verdict about a thing's dispositions conflicts with what anyone not in the grip of a theory would say about it.
