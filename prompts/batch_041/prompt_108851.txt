Last night a friend and I spent three hours going in circles about the teletransporter, and I came away convinced that we were not really disagreeing about the case at all but about what a theory in this area is even supposed to deliver — my friend kept saying that the replica simply is not him and that this settles the matter, and I kept feeling that he had helped himself to a standard he never stated. So before I read another word of the literature, I want the specification written out: the desiderata that any adequate account of what matters in survival has to meet, each with the case or consideration that generates it, and with a clear-eyed division between the requirements that are genuine and those that are merely the prejudices of one camp.

I want the list to include at least the following, though you should add anything I have missed and cut anything that turns out to be doing no work. First, the datum that I have a kind of concern for my own future that I do not have for anyone else's, and that a theory must either locate that concern or explain it away. Second, the teletransporter in both of its forms — the one where the original is destroyed in the scanning and the one where the original survives alongside the replica — since a theory that treats these as the same case has already conceded something. Third, fission, where one person divides into two who each remember being me and each go on to live, together with the demand that a theory not privilege one branch by fiat. Fourth, the gradual cases, where nothing is copied and nothing is destroyed but the psychological connections thin out across decades, so that the infant and the old man are linked only by a long chain of overlapping stages. Fifth, the requirement that whatever the theory says about survival be able to carry the weight we place on it elsewhere: promises, debts, punishment, grief, and the standing of someone asked to honour a commitment made by a person who has changed beyond recognition. Sixth, the requirement that the theory either close or explicitly explain the gap between what is true of personal identity and what matters, since the reductionist move is precisely to let these come apart. And then the assumptions I suspect are smuggled in unargued and do most of the real work: that survival is all-or-nothing rather than a matter of degree; that identity is transitive and one-one, so that two distinct fission products cannot both be identical with the original; and that there is a fact of the matter about who is who independent of our conventions. Mark each of these as non-negotiable or as a preference of a particular camp, and if some subset of them is jointly unsatisfiable — I have a strong suspicion that all-or-nothing survival, no privileged branch in fission, and identity being what matters cannot all be held at once — set out the argument, because that is the result I actually want from this.

Two cases of my own devising should be run through the list, since my friend kept appealing to intuition and I want something that cannot be settled by intuition alone. The first is a firm selling what it calls continuity insurance, which pays out to whichever branch of a policyholder is alive at the end of the term, the sum being identical no matter which branch collects, so that a theory positing a fact about which branch is you owes us an account of what the payout is tracking. The second is a person undergoing a slow, medically unremarkable replacement of everything psychological — no copying, no destruction, just the kind of change we all undergo — until nothing of the original's memory or character remains, and who is nonetheless treated by everyone, himself included, as the same person throughout; I want to know whether a theory can call this survival without also calling the teletransporter survival, and if so, exactly what the difference is supposed to consist in. Then tell me which single requirement eliminates the most otherwise plausible candidates, and keep any scorecard of the existing contenders to a sentence apiece — the criteria are the deliverable here, not the grading.
