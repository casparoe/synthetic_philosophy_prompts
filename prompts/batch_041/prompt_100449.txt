I am practicing the opposing line, so here is my opening move. I want to defend the view that the Boltzmann brain problem is rationally inert as a reason to reject a cosmological theory, because it is self-undefeating in a way that sets it apart from ordinary empirical objections. Consider two ways a theory can undermine the reasoning used to establish it: vertically, by making the very inferential faculties that produced the argument unreliable or deluded, and horizontally, by making it unlikely that this particular reasoner, rather than some other observer, happened to arrive at it. I claim that only the horizontal kind is at stake here — the physics, the mathematics, and the ordinary canons of inference are the same whether one is a fluctuation or an evolved being, and the soundness of an argument is a fact about the world, not about which observer runs it — and that horizontal self-undefeat is benign, since we already accept that any correct theory will make "me in particular" unremarkable.
