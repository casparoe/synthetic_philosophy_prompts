ok so I've never studied philosophy properly, just read a lot of blogs and argued with people online, and I've ended up with a pile of opinions about knowledge that I'm pretty sure dont all fit together. can you tell me if they do?

here's roughly where I've landed:

1. I'm not a skeptic. I know there's a table in front of me right now, I know I've got two hands, and I know I'm not a brain in a jar being fed fake experiences.

2. For something to count as knowledge it can't be a lucky guess. Your belief has to be actually hooked up to the fact, not just happening to match it by accident.

3. Circular reasoning is worthless. If the only thing backing up a claim is the claim itself, or some short loop that comes right back around to it, thats not knowledge, thats just going in a circle.

4. I think its totally fine to use a sense to check that a sense is working. Like if I'm not sure my eyes are reliable, I can look at the clock and then look again, and if both readings agree I've got good reason to trust my eyes and to trust the time on the clock. Same with memory: I can call up a bunch of times my memory got things right, and that seems like a decent reason to believe my memory works. I've done the same thing with a news site — I figured it was trustworthy because I'd read its articles and they kept turning out right, though I suppose I only knew they turned out right because I'd read it there in the first place.

5. But I also think that if a clock has stopped and it just happens to be showing the right time, reading it gives you no knowledge of the time. You're right by accident. And glancing at it twice doesn't help either, because a stopped clock shows the same thing both times by definition.

So do these hang together? I've got a feeling 4 is in some kind of fight with 5, and maybe with 3 as well, but I can't work out which one has to give, or whether 4 is really doing anything wrong. I'd also like to know if any of them only *look* like they clash and actually don't, because I dont want to give up a view thats fine. And be straight with me about which clash is the serious one and what it'd cost me to fix it — I'd rather hear the one real problem than a list of everything that could possibly be doubted.
