I'm a semanticist by training who has drifted into philosophy of law, and I keep finding that the two literatures talk past each other. I'd like you to do something precise for me. Take the central dispute in American statutory interpretation between textualism and purposivism — the disagreement over whether a statute's meaning is fixed by the linguistic content of the enacted text or by the aims the enacting legislature was trying to advance — and restate it, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary of post-Gricean semantics and pragmatics. Use that vocabulary the way a specialist would: sentence meaning versus speaker meaning, what is said versus what is implicated, conventional versus conversational implicature, minimal semantic content versus contextually enriched content, presupposition and accommodation, and the Stalnakerian apparatus of common ground and context set.

For each element of the legal dispute, tell me which semantic or pragmatic notion it maps onto cleanly, which it maps onto only with distortion, and which it cannot be expressed in at all. I am especially interested in four residues. First, the corporate or collective character of legislative intent, which has no single speaker whose intentions could be recovered the way a Gricean hearer recovers a speaker's. Second, the normative, authority-conferring aspect of legal meaning — that a court is not merely describing what a text means but is bound by it. Third, the role of precedent and of texts that are amended over time, so that later readers and later enactments partially determine content. Fourth, the absurdity canon, in which interpreters appear to override what is said in light of downstream consequences.

Then give me the residue report in full: state plainly which parts of the textualist–purposivist disagreement survive translation into the semantics/pragmatics vocabulary, which are subtly altered by the translation, and which vanish entirely. Close with your own assessment of whether the vanishing parts show that the semantic/pragmatic framework is missing something the legal theorists are tracking, or instead that the legal disagreement is less substantive than its practitioners suppose.

Please write in American English, and write for a reader who is a specialist in both fields — no need to define the basic terms.
