I'm told this is the unity-of-the-proposition problem, or Bradley's regress with new paint on it, but every write-up I can find either tells me Bradley was a monist so he was fine with the conclusion, or that Russell thought the regress was harmless and we should all relax — and neither one tells me which line to give up. So here's my own version, written out. It ends somewhere I definately can't live with, and it seems to eat itself on the way out.

(1) Take any complex thing at all. Either its just its parts, lumped (a heap, a list, a pile), or there's something over and above the parts that makes it one thing instead of many.

(2) The proposition that Ana loves Bo is complex. Its parts are Ana, Bo, and loving.

(3) It isn't just its parts lumped. Ana, Bo, loving — that's a list of three items and nothing has been said. No claim has been made yet.

(4) So by (1) there's something over and above Ana, Bo and loving that turns those three into a single thing that says something. Call it the unifier.

(5) The unifier is doing work in the proposition, so it's in the proposition. Now there are four things in there.

(6) Four things need unifying exactly as much as three did. Apply (1) again: a second unifier. Then a fifth item, a third unifier, forever.

(7) So the unifying never finishes, and there's no finished proposition — nothing that says anything.

(8) But an unfinished claim isn't a claim, and a heap of objects isn't a claim either. So there are no propositions, no thoughts, no truths. Including the argument I just gave, which therefore isn't true, if it isn't even a proposition.

I want to keep (3). Drop (3) and a thought is just a pile of stuff, and I no longer know what thinking is supposed to be. I want to keep (8) too; a half-made claim is nothing. What I keep calling the drawer test is the thing I can't get past: Ana, Bo and loving sitting in a drawer is not the proposition, and I can't say what the difference is without smuggling in something that's itself in there.

Two people have offered me exits and I can't tell whether either one works. Sam says the relation is not another item parked next to Ana and Bo, it's the relating, the connecting — so (5) is already wrong, because the unifier isn't in there as a part, it's what the parts are doing. His analogy is spinning: the spinning of a wheel isn't another part of the wheel. Maybe, but then what separates Ana-loves-Bo from the three items in the drawer, if the connecting is nothing over and above them? And a wheel has to be doing the spinning — is the doing of it a further item, and don't we start again? Priya says I've run two different regresses together, one of parts and one of why-questions, and that explanations can stop at brute facts, so (7) is a cheat. Maybe. But then what is a brute fact, and is saying the unity is brute just a way of saying there's no answer?

So which line do I cut, and what does cutting it cost? And if the answer is that the regress is harmless, harmless in what sense — I need to know whether the proposition is one thing or an infinite queue of things, because (3) and (8) both seem to want the first answer.
