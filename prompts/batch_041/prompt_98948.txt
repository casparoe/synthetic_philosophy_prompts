I've been on a bit of a Ramsey kick lately — not the money guy, the Cambridge one who died at 26, the one who translated Wittgenstein as an undergrad. What got me is that little epilogue he wrote near the end of his life about the meaning of life, where he basically says he doesn't feel small in front of the vastness of the heavens, that physical size just doesn't impress him, that the things that do impress him are things like being able to think and to love, and that his picture of the world is drawn in perspective rather than to scale. Most people read that as a nice bit of humanist consolation and move on. I want to push it alot harder than that.

So here's the position I'd like you to argue for. Take Ramsey's move and treat it as a substantive thesis, not just a mood: namely, that the meaningfulness of a life is not a function of its causal footprint — not how much it affects, not how long it's remembered, not how big a chunk of the universe it manages to register in. A life could be maximally meaningful even if literally nothing downstream depends on it and every trace of it gets erased. I'll call it meaning without footprint. Defend that. And I want you to do it in a way that survives the obvious objection, which is the one my friend keeps hammering at me: that "meaning" just is mattering-to-something, so a footprintless life is meaningless by definition, and Ramsey is just smuggling in a warm private feeling and slapping the word "meaning" on it. So part of the job is to say what, if anything, the word is tracking if it isn't tracking downstream effects.

A couple of things I'd like you to engage with along the way, since I think they're where the real action is. First, a test case: two lives, lived identically in every internal respect — same intensity, same loves, same projects, same felt sense of mattering — except that one is the last conscious thing in a universe that then blinks out, so its footprint is exactly zero, while the other gets remembered and reverberates for a while. If you're right that meaning is footprint-independent, the two should come out equal; say whether you accept that and why. Second, I keep getting stuck on whether this makes meaningfulness response-dependent in the way colors or tastes are — something that exists only from inside a perspective, like Ramsey's foregrounded picture — or whether you can still be a realist about it and just insist that footprint is the wrong dimension to measure it on. Those feel like very different positions to me and I want you to pick one and own it. No need for citations or reading lists, I just want the actual argument, pushed as far as it'll go.
