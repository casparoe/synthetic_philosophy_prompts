I grew up pretty religious, left it a couple years ago, and my mom keeps sending me Einstein quotes to prove that you can be a scientist and still religious — and honestly she's not totally wrong, which is annoying, so I finally went and read the actual essay (the one from around 1930 that usually gets reprinted under the title Religion and Science) instead of just the quote cards. And now I'm stuck on it in a way I wasn't before.

Heres the chunk that's messing me up, as best I can render it without the book in front of me:

Science is in the business of describing what is, and it has no way of getting to what should be — value judgments lie outside its domain, so we need religion for the ought. But the only religion he'll actually sign off on is what he calls the cosmic religious feeling: awe before the rational order of the world, no dogma, no personal god, no commandments, and he says that feeling is the deepest motive behind real scientific work. And then, in roughly the same period, he says he doesn't believe in freedom of the will at all — he borrows a line from Schopenhauer about how a man can do what he wants but can't want what he wants — and he says this is what makes him tolerant instead of judgmental.

So here's my confusion and I'd like help sorting it out. If (a) ethics needs religion because science can't derive an ought, (b) the only religion he'll endorse is awe at lawfulness, which seems to have zero moral content, and (c) there's no free will, so nobody could have done otherwise and blame starts looking like an illusion — then where is his ethics actually supposed to come from, and what is the ought even doing in the picture? Is the fact/value split just a division of labor he states but doesn't really need? Does the cosmic feeling secretly carry moral content I'm missing? Or does he think you can have a real ethic — tolerance, humility, not being cruel — without free will and without commands, just as a kind of attitude that falls out of seeing yourself as part of a determined order?

Can you walk me through what he's actually committed to here, and whether those three pieces can be held together, or whether one of them has to give? I'd rather understand the position on its own terms before I decide if I buy it, and I'd definately like to know what work the word religious is doing in the cosmic-feeling part, since it feels like he's using it in a way that neither my mom nor my old pastor would recognize.
