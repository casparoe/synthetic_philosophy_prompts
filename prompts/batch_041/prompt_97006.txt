For a seminar on applied ethics I need a teaching text, so please write a satire of about 600 words. The form should be a mock Code of Practice — a deadpan, unblinking institutional document with headed clauses — issued by a fictional organisation of your choosing (a farm assurance scheme, a zoo, a veterinary school) whose ethics committee has adopted Kant's account of our duties regarding animals without amendment or apology. The humour must arise entirely from the doctrine rather than from the animals' suffering, which should never be dwelt upon: nothing cruel is to be staged for laughs. In particular, the code should declare in its preamble that animals are not ends in themselves and that nothing is owed to them; should ground each of its protections in duties to humanity or to the agent's own character — cruelty prohibited because it blunts the compassion we need for one another, wanton destruction prohibited as an injury to the agent's own moral disposition, and recognition for an animal that has served the organisation made payable, quite literally, to its keeper; and should contain at least one clause whose plain application makes an animal's protection contingent on the psychology of the human concerned, so that the protection lapses where no sympathetic disposition is at stake, or where the work is done by machinery. Keep the document internally consistent — no clause may smuggle in a claim on the animal's own behalf — and never let its author wink. A reader who knows the doctrine should find the piece fair; a reader who does not should still be able to tell what is being mocked. Write in British English.
