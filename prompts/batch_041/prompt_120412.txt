Student's answer:

Both kinds of conditional ask us to suppose something and work out what follows, and both are properly assessed by the Ramsey test: you accept if A then B to the extent that you would accept B after adding A to your beliefs. Neither is a material conditional. Take (1) If the wiring didn't start the fire, something else did, and (2) If the wiring hadn't started the fire, something else would have. In the actual case the wiring did start it. Both sentences can be true, and neither is made true just by the falsity of its antecedent, which is what you'd get if if meant the material conditional. The difference between the two kinds is a difference in presupposition. Counterfactuals presuppose that their antecedent is false, which is why the subjunctive is the natural form for them, and indicatives presuppose that their antecedent is true, which is why an indicative conditional is only assertable when you already take the antecedent to be the case. So (1) and (2) have different presuppositions but the same truth conditions, and the semantics of if only has to be given once, with the mood marking doing the rest.

For the truth conditions I'll follow Lewis. On his analysis, if A, would B is true just in case B holds in every A-world, and if A, might B is true just in case B holds in some A-world. Since the antecedent holds throughout the A-worlds, the analysis predicts that strengthening the antecedent is valid for counterfactuals: if B follows from A, it also follows from A together with anything else. That seems right, because if the wiring had started the fire then the alarm would have gone off, then it's also true that if the wiring had started the fire and the room had been empty then the alarm would have gone off.

Adams's thesis is that the probability of a conditional equals the conditional probability of its consequent given its antecedent, so the semantics should assign each conditional a truth value equal to that conditional probability. This is the cleanest option, and although the triviality results are usually read as showing that it can't be combined with a truth-conditional semantics, I take them to show that for indicatives we should fall back on the material conditional analysis after all, with the extra strength of the conditional coming from pragmatics rather than from its truth conditions.
