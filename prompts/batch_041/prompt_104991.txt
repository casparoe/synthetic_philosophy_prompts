So I've been reteaching the evolution unit at my school and I keep getting asked by kids, and by one very persistent parent, about that Popper line where he supposedly said Darwinism isn't really testable, and I've been reworking my own views to answer them. Here's where I've landed and I'd like a sanity check before I say any of it out loud:

(1) Falsifiability is the right demarcation criterion — it's what separates a science from a non-science — but it's not a criterion of meaning, so unfalsifiable claims can still be meaningful and even valuable.
(2) As a bare schema, natural selection really is close to unfalsifiable — the fit survive, and the fit are just the ones who survive — so Popper was basically right to file Darwinism as a metaphysical research programme.
(3) And that's exactly how I answer the parent: intelligent design isn't science, and unfalsifiability is why.
(4) That said, evolution by natural selection is clearly the best-supported theory in biology and I'd call it a science if anything is.
(5) I also really like Popper's evolutionary epistemology — knowledge grows by conjecture and refutation, which is basically a selection process.
(6) I think Popper was right the first time and his later walk-back on the Darwin thing was just him folding under pressure from biologists.

Which of these actually pull against each other? I have a feeling a couple of them only look like they clash because I'm being sloppy with unfalsifiable and metaphysical, but I dont trust my own read here. Rank the real tensions by how bad they are, tell me the cheapest fix for each and what it costs, and then say which single one I should just drop.
