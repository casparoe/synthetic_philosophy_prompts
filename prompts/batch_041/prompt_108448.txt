Two people in my seminar got into it about a statement a politician made after a scandal. The line was: "I'm sorry if anyone was hurt by what happened, and we'll do everything we can to make sure it never happens again." Here's how it went, roughly:

(1) He said it's neither an apology nor a promise. The "if" makes it conditional and "everything we can" can't be checked against anything, so the guy didn't perform either act — he produced what he called apology-shaped noise.
(2) She said performatives dont run on magic words; the act is fixed by how a competent audience takes it, and everyone took it as an apology. Insincerity doesn't undo an act — an insincere promise is still a promise, thats why we get to call it insincere.
(3) He said that proves far too much. If uptake decides, the audience is unconstrained, and a guy could apologize by saying "drop dead" in a room that misheard him. Then nothing is left of what the speaker did.
(4) She said uptake is constrained by convention, and that the alternative — intention decides — is worse, since then you can never be held to a promise you secretly didn't mean. She also said there's a difference between an act that comes off badly and one that never comes off at all, and by every convention that matters this one came off. The hedges are just evidence about sincerity, not a different act.
(5) He said the "if" and the hedges are exactly the conventions that mark that he isn't undertaking anything, so an audience that heard an apology simply misread the convention. She said that's begging the question about which conventions govern.

I stayed out of it. Can you adjudicate this move by move — who's right at each step, where each of them is missing something, and what the dispute actually turns on?
