Draft the joint statement that two parties could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration: a committed cliodynamicist, who holds that the structural-demographic research program associated with Peter Turchin — secular cycles, elite overproduction, the fiscal-demographic squeeze — is a genuine predictive science of history; and a committed historian-skeptic, who holds that the apparent cycles are artifacts of aggregation, detrending, and post hoc curve-fitting, and that history does not admit lawlike forecasting. Neither party may supply references, reading lists, or remembered statistics; every empirical commitment must be expressed as a threshold, a direction of effect, or an ordinal comparison.

Produce the document in four parts.

First, a numbered list of agreed claims: what the theory actually asserts and does not assert; what a cycle is supposed to be; which classes of evidence both sides concede exist and are usable; what would count as bad practice on either side; and what explanatory work neither side is entitled to.

Second, the residual disagreements, each stated in wording its own side would accept, with the genuinely empirical disagreements separated from those that are definitional or verbal — say explicitly which are which, and if the quarrel is partly over what counts as a prediction, a cycle, or a mechanism, make that exact rather than leaving it implicit.

Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side.

Fourth, for each crux, a test both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Both sides must either accept the following benchmarks or state in the document why they do not: a holdout test, with parameters fixed on one set of cases and then applied without refitting to cases neither side has tuned on; a clock benchmark, in which any cyclical model must beat a trivial model that forecasts instability from time elapsed since the last instability, using no causal variables at all; a surrogate test, in which a detrended series must show more cyclical structure than a trend-preserving random walk or AR(1) surrogate; and a prospective forecast, stated as an ordinal or threshold prediction about a specified class of events in a specified region over a specified horizon, scored by a rule both sides name in advance. Require the statement to distinguish retrodiction with free parameters, conditional prediction given measured drivers, and unconditional prospective prediction, and to say which of the three each side is actually claiming.

Close with a ledger in which each camp names the result that would count as a genuine failure rather than a refinement or a scope restriction, an undertaking to flag any post hoc rescue as such, and a short paragraph on what both sides agree would not settle the dispute, and why. Format the agreed claims as a numbered list and the disagreements as a table giving, for each: the disputed claim, each side's own wording of its position, the crux it turns on, the test, and each side's recorded prediction.
