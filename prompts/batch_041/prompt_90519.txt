I'm a backend engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and a few of us at the office have a lunchtime reading group that started out on distributed systems papers and has drifted, over the past couple of months, into epistemology. Last week we read something about higher-order evidence — evidence not about whether some claim is true, but about whether you've gone about forming your belief in it properly — and about epistemic akrasia, the state of believing something while also believing that your belief in it is irrational or unjustified. I may be mangling the terms. What struck me is that this isn't an exotic condition at all; it's more or less the standing condition of anyone debugging a production incident at two in the morning. I'd like a joke about it, and I'd like the joke to turn on the actual structure of the problem rather than on the general fact that philosophers disagree about things.

Here's a seed you can keep, cut, or replace: two engineers are pairing at two in the morning; one says he's 95% sure the fault is in the cache invalidation; the other points out that engineers of their experience level are reliably overconfident, and that this same colleague was 95% sure about the DNS an hour ago and was wrong, so the honest figure is nearer 70%; the first accepts this and revises downward, at which point the second observes that the correction was itself produced by the same overconfident process, so perhaps the revised figure needs revising too, and so on. Where I want the joke to land is somewhere in that regress, or in the odd position of someone who keeps acting on a belief while simultaneously judging that he isn't entitled to hold it — I gather this is where the serious dispute lies, over whether such a combination can ever be rational — and I'd like it to be funny to someone who knows that dispute. A short two-hander exchange is fine; a one-liner is fine if it's good enough. It should be tellable out loud in under a minute, and I'd prefer the laugh to be slightly uncomfortable rather than purely silly.

After the joke, give me a sentence or two on which piece of the debate it's exploiting, so I can check whether I've followed it. Don't bother with citations, reading lists, or names of philosophers — I'm not going to track any of it down, and I'd rather the joke stand on its own.
