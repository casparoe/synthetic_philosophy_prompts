My friend Maya does data science (she did a masters in econ first) and we argue about this at lunch basically every week: when is it actually rational to vote? She's not a philosopher and would never put it this way, but I'm trying to reverse engineer the rule she's actually running, because I want to know what she'll say about something that came up at work.

Her track record on "was this person being rational":

- Her brother lives in a state that's been blue by like 15 points forever. He skipped the presidential vote because it couldn't possibly matter. She says he was rational.
- Same brother later lived in a state that came down to about 3,000 votes out of 3 million. He voted. She says rational, obviously.
- She voted in a school board race where the margin was 400 votes out of about 6,000 cast. She says that vote had a way better chance of mattering than any presidential vote she's ever cast, and that spending the time was rational.
- She also votes in every presidential election in the safe state, and she'll tell you straight up that's not rational, she just likes doing it and likes the sticker. She calls it a vice, not an argument.
- She gave $200 to a charity that buys bed nets and says that's plainly rational, because $200 has a real shot (like one in a couple hundred, she says) at saving a life, whereas the odds of her being the single deciding vote in a presidential election are like one in ten million, and that difference is the whole story.
- She thinks buying the extended warranty on a $400 laptop is dumb.
- And this is the one I keep coming back to: she says she'd never be the person in a group project who does nothing when the group is getting an A anyway. But when I ask if that's because free riding is irrational, she says no, thats not a rationality thing at all, that's about not being a leech.

Now the new case. Her coworker Dev lives in that same safe blue state. He hates voting -- hates the lines, doesnt care about the sticker, gets nothing out of it. He votes anyway, and the only reason he gives is that he doesn't want to be a freeloader: he gets the benefits of living in a democracy, other people do the work of keeping it going, and he thinks it'd be wrong to take the benefit and skip his part. He knows his vote won't decide anything.

So: will Maya say Dev was being rational? I'm pretty sure she's running some kind of odds rule -- if the chance your vote is the one that decides is absurdly tiny, it's not worth your time, period. But I'd bet there's at least one other rule that fits everything above just as well, maybe something about whether the act actually does the thing you're doing it for, or about what the point of the activity is in the first place. Give me the rules that fit her record, what each one predicts about Dev, your best guess at what she'd actually say and why, and the case that would split the rules apart -- the scenario where they give different answers and we'd finally know which one she's using. And if two of the rules agree about Dev but for totally different reasons, say that too, because then his case doesn't really settle anything and I shouldn't act like it does.
