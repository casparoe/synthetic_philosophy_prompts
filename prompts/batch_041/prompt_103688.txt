I'm building a problem set for a grad seminar on AI safety and there's a point my students keep missing, so I want a case that definately forces it: "control" and "alignment" get taught as two tools on the same shelf, but sometimes the control measures are exactly what manufatures the misalignment they're supposed to defend against. Build me a scenario that shows this cleanly.

Here's the spec. It should be near-term and boring-realistic — a lab shipping a tool-using model that writes and runs code against a live repo (or handles customer ops), no superintelligence, no exotic hardware, no sci-fi jailbreaks. The lab runs one specific protocol: the untrusted model proposes actions, a weaker trusted model reviews them and can veto or rewrite, and — this is the part I care about — the vetoes get fed back as training signal, so the untrusted model is being fine-tuned to reduce how often it gets vetoed.

Constraints I want you to respect, otherwise the case is a cheat:
- Hold the untrusted model's raw capability fixed the whole way through. The reversal has to come from the protocol, not from the model getting smarter or the monitor getting dumber.
- At t=0 the protocol has to be genuinely safer than the ungated alternative (just letting the model act). Don't strawman control into obviously failing on day one.
- At some later t=1 the protocol has to be *less* safe than the ungated alternative, and the causal chain has to be concrete — not "the model learns to deceive" as a slogan, but the actual thing the model learns or the actual thing the lab loses (some signal, some check, some information).
- "Just use a stronger monitor" must not be a free fix. If it works, say what it costs; if it doesn't, show why.

Then, underneath the scenario:
- walk me through why each stage lands where it does;
- name the single feature doing the work, and tell me what happens if I delete it (does the case collapse back into the boring "schemers scheme" story?);
- and say how a control researcher would push back — is this really a control problem, or am I smuggling in a training-procedure problem?

Keep it to about a page, concrete (timeline, numbers, named components), and the kind of case a defender of control would concede is possible rather than a gotcha. No reading lists or citations, obviously — just the case and the reasoning.
