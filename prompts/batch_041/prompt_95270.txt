Just got into a two-hour argument with my buddy about whether arithmetic could actually be inconsistent, and I want to know if I'm the one being unreasonable. Here's where I ended up. I think the consistency of PA (and ZFC, but PA is the one I actually care about) isn't something we know or could know a priori. It's a contingent fact about a formal system that we've so far been lucky about. And I think the standard reassurance is close to worthless. We've never found a contradiction, fine, but I half-remember him saying that if PA is inconsistent then the shortest proof of a contradiction would be so absurdly long that nobody could ever write it down, so a century of not finding one is basically zero evidence. I might be misremembering the details, but something in that neighborhood is true. Also it's not like foundational systems never turn out broken: Frege's whole thing collapsed, and the large-cardinal people found that one of the obvious-looking embedding axioms, Reinhardt's, is just inconsistent with choice (Kunen, I think?) and nobody even knows if it's consistent with ZF alone. And the one consistency proof we have for PA runs through induction up to epsilon-nought, which is exactly the sort of thing that's supposed to be shakier than PA itself, so it feels like it's just pushing the worry back a step.

The part I actually believe: it wouldn't be the end of the world. We'd localize the damage, or move to a paraconsistent arithmetic where the contradiction doesn't blow everything up, or drop whichever principle was doing the damage, and 99% of math carries on.

So what I want is what would count against this. Not "Gödel shows you can't prove consistency" — I know that, everyone knows that, and it's about provability, not about whether the thing is actually consistent. I mean real evidence or real arguements. Specifically, which of these should move me and how much: the triviality thing (if classical logic is non-negotiable then localizing is impossible and paraconsistent arithmetic is a change of subject, not a patch); the self-undermining thing (the metatheory I'm using to even state the worry is itself a formal system and gets the same treatment, so is this just general skepticism in a math costume?); the base-rate thing; and the fact that physics only ever uses finite fragments of arithmetic, which makes my "we'd cope" true but maybe means my worry has no practical bite at all. Rank them from ok-you-should-actually-drop-this down to shrug. Tell me what someone who really holds this line would themselves concede counts against them, as opposed to what critics yell at them. And say honestly whether the view risks anything or is just a vibe. If there's a version that makes a prediction that could come out wrong, lead with that, cause thats the kind of thing that would move me most.
