Assume the role of a philosopher of mind who works on panpsychism and the metaphysics of consciousness, and who is willing to tell people that their favorite solution does not do the work they think it does. Read the whole request before you answer, and begin by brainstorming — list the demands that various parties actually place on an account of how micro-level experience could add up to a macro-level subject — before you commit to a final list.

I want a specification, not a survey: the desiderata that any adequate solution to the combination problem must satisfy. Make the criteria themselves the deliverable and keep the weight there rather than on scoring theories.

For each requirement, give (a) the requirement, stated as sharply as you can; (b) the case, distinction, or consideration that motivates it; and (c) your verdict on whether it is non-negotiable or merely the prejudice of one camp. The list should at least cover the cases of phenomenal unity and disunity that any account must get right; the distinctions it must not collapse (for instance, between combining subjects and combining qualities, between a unified subject and a merely unified stream, between what a theory posits and what it explains); and any empirical constraints it must respect.

Then take seriously the following case, which I constructed and have not seen treated, and let it generate at least one requirement of its own. Suppose two streams of experience are integrated for a while and then separated again — call it reversible fusion. The integration is genuine while it lasts: the system meets whatever functional and informational criteria one might lay down for a single subject. During the fused interval, is there one subject or two? When the streams separate, what becomes of the subject that existed during fusion — does it cease to exist, divide in two, or persist as a fragment alongside a new one? And if a theory's answer turns on facts about the parts rather than on the integration itself, say what those facts are. Whatever an account of combination says here, it should not be allowed to buy its answer by quietly denying the phenomenology of the fused interval.

Flag any pair or subset of your desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — if the list is itself a result, say so and say why. Tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude candidate theories. Then, briefly, say how the main strategies fare against the list: subject-summing panpsychism, phenomenal bonding, cosmopsychism together with its problem of getting many finite subjects out of one cosmic subject, and illusionist or eliminativist strategies that deny the existence of a genuine macro-subject.

Be committed. Do not lay out the options and leave the choice to me: state which requirements you think are genuine and which are not, take a position on whether the desiderata are jointly satisfiable, and give your reasons. If you think my reversible-fusion case is confused or begs a question, say so plainly rather than accommodating it.
