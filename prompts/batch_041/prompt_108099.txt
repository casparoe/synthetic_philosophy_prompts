Construct a thought experiment to specification. The target is the argument that special relativity, taken together with a realist reading of the present, leaves no room for an objective now or for genuine becoming — the argument that is supposed to force a block universe. I want the case in order to explain to a skeptical physicist friend why so many philosophers treat that conclusion as forced, so the case has to earn its verdicts rather than announce them.

Build a single scenario in flat spacetime with three inertial observers, A, B and C, who coincide at one event and then separate, with no acceleration, no faster-than-light signalling and no exotic physics. Engineer the case so that one distant event E lies on A's simultaneity slice through the shared event, before B's slice through that event, and after C's slice through it — so that E is present, past and future respectively for the three observers, who nevertheless agree completely about the physics. The construction must be numerical: assign explicit relative velocities and explicit coordinates for the relevant events in one chosen frame, so that every verdict can be checked rather than taken on trust.

The case must satisfy the following conditions. Every simultaneity verdict must follow from the Lorentz transformation for the velocities you assign, and you must exhibit the transformation for at least the decisive pair of events. No observer may be able to detect the disagreement or signal about it; the puzzle concerns what is real, not what anyone can find out. The case must exhibit in coordinates that simultaneity within a single frame is an equivalence relation while the relation 'simultaneous with, in some frame' is not transitive — give a chain of events in which each link is simultaneous in some frame but the endpoints are never simultaneous in any frame. The construction must not privilege one frame: each of the three observers must be able to run the same argument with themselves as the reference observer. Finally, describe the spacetime diagram in words — which events lie on which slices — so that the geometry can be inspected.

Then, using that case, do the following.

1. Give the verdict table: for E and for each link of the chain, what A says, what B says, what C says.
2. Separate the assumptions doing the work into those that are physics (relativity of simultaneity, the Lorentz transformation) and those that are metaphysics — for instance, that the present is real, that 'is real' is not relative to a frame, or that events simultaneous with one another must share all their properties.
3. State what presentism, the growing-block view and the block universe are each forced to say about the case, and which of the three is embarrassed by it and why.
4. Identify the single assumption that, if dropped, dissolves the puzzle, and say what dropping it costs.
5. Explain how someone who wants to keep objective becoming could reply — in particular by positing a privileged simultaneity relation or a preferred frame — and what that reply costs, given that nothing in the case can distinguish their preferred frame from any other.

Finish with one paragraph on which feature of the case is doing the real work, and what becomes of the argument if that feature is removed. A case whose coordinates do not actually deliver the three verdicts has failed, and so has one that stipulates the verdicts instead of deriving them. Don't worry about what the argument is called or who is credited with it; I want the case and the demonstration.
