My reading group just wrapped the Dialectic in the first Critique and for the last session of term I want to do something a bit silly but hopfully not stupid: what would Kant have made of the fine-tuning argument. Fine-tuning being the claim that the constants and initial conditions of the universe sit in the narrow window that permits life, and that this calls for explanation by an intelligent cause. He died in 1804 so he never saw it, which is the whole point of the exercise.

I don't want "he'd call it transcendent and move on." I want the actual collision. So, first: is fine-tuning just the physico-theological proof in new clothes? My memory of his complaints about that one is that it gets you at most an architect and not a creator, that it can't reach the moral attributes, and that it quietly leans on the cosmological proof (which leans on the ontological one). Tell me if I've mangled that, and then tell me which of the three survive the change of evidence, because the evidence is now the values of the constants rather than the contrivance of organisms. Does moving the design out of the parts and into the laws matter to him?

Then the standard modern replies. The multiverse: many universes, so there's no coincidence left to explain. And observer selection: we could only ever find ourselves in a life-permitting universe, so finding one isn't surprising — some people add that the original inference is an inverse gambler's fallacy, treating a throw that had to land somewhere as evidence of many throws. Would he treat the multiverse as a legitimate hypothesis, as a regulative idea being used constitutively, or as a fresh antinomy with proofs on both sides? And does the observer-selection point dissolve the argument for him, or just relocate the wonder?

Last bit, and it's the part I actually care about: does any of this do work for him inside his own system, given that God is already a postulate of practical reason and theoretical support is arguably beside the point? And is there anywhere the fine-tuning argument forces him to say something his texts don't already cover — that's what I want out of this, a place where he has to extend himself rather than just apply himself. No reading list, just the argument.
