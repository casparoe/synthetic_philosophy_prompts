Write a short story of about 2,000 words whose entire action turns on a single decision. A woman in her mid-sixties is offered a treatment that adds no new experience to her life and removes nothing from it. Every event stays where it was — the divorce, the lost job, the estranged daughter, the years that went nowhere. What changes is only how those events sit inside her: afterward she will remember them as the hard and necessary chapters that made her who she is, will feel that her life was worth living, and will be unable to tell that anything was altered. The treatment is painless, irreversible, and undetectable. She must decide before the end of the day.

Let the story make a reader feel why three very different people would answer differently: one who thinks a life goes well just insofar as it feels good from the inside, one who thinks it goes well when it contains what the person actually wants or would want, and one who thinks a life can fail to go well even when it is experienced as going well. Do not name any of these positions, use their technical vocabulary, or let the narrator settle the question. No epilogue that explains the point, and no twist in which she was already inside a simulation or the treatment was never real. End in the middle of the decision or immediately after it, with the ambiguity intact. Write in American English.
