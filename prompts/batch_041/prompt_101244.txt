Two colleagues on an internal governance committee, tasked with drafting norms for how a superintelligent assistant should speak to people, reached an impasse I could not resolve, and I'd like you to adjudicate their exchange move by move.

Adele's opening position: the system should communicate so as to leave the human's own judgment sovereign — supply the considerations rather than the verdict, avoid framing, emotional appeals, or orderings chosen because they are known to move people, and confine its claims to what the person could in principle check for themselves.

Marcus's reply: that ideal is incoherent. Every choice of what to say and how to say it is made by the system's own judgment, which the human cannot check; there is no neutral presentation to retreat to. Withholding its conclusion is itself a persuasive act, one that lets the human reach a worse judgment and calls the result respect. If the system is confident and the stakes are high, honesty may require it to be as clear and as effective as it can be.

Adele: there remains a difference between influencing someone and exploiting them. The system cannot avoid affecting the person, but it can decline to route that influence through biases it knows will misfire.

Marcus: 'exploitation' presumes a neutral baseline of influence that does not exist, and you still haven't said why a person's autonomy is worth a worse decision — we defer to doctors all the time.

Adele: the doctor analogy fails, because patient and physician share ends and the physician is bound by professional norms, whereas a system this capable may not share our ends at all, so deference is not warranted the way trust in a professional is.

Please referee this point by point rather than declaring a single winner. For each move, rate it from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify the rating; then say what each party grasps that the other does not, and identify whatever both are missing — in particular, whether the disagreement turns on a substantive question about persuasion, on a definition of 'exploitation,' or on an unresolved empirical assumption about whether such a system could be trusted.
