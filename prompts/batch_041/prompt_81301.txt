I'm running a film night for a reading group that's been working through Quine, and we're watching Arrival (2016). The obvious hook is the Sapir-Whorf angle — learning Heptapod rewires how Louise experiences time — but what I actually want is a reading of the film through Quine's indeterminacy of translation, the gavagai business. Quine says there's no such fact: any number of translation manuals fit all the available evidence, and even reference itself is inscrutable — you can permute what every term refers to, his proxy-function trick, and nothing observable changes. So does the film come out as a naive realist about meaning, or does it dramatise something Quine's argument misses? And what does the Whorfianism add or muddy, given that indeterminacy is meant to be a point about evidence rather than about language rewiring thought?
