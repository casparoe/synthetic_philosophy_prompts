I've been a psychiatric nurse on inpatient wards for about nine years and I've ended up reading alot of the sociology-of-psychiatry stuff on my own time, because of something that keeps happening and that I don't have a word for.

Here's the pattern. A category gets some publicity, a documentary or a big magazine piece, and then on the ward you start seeing two opposite reactions in the people the category is about. Some of them come in already fluent in it. They've read the symptom list, they describe themselves in its vocabulary, and their presentation lines up with the textbook almost too neatly. Others have read the same thing and they push back hard: not me, thats not a real thing, thats just who I am and you lot are pathologising it. And the part I can't get past is that the second group seems to get counted as evidence for the category just as easily as the first. Not wanting the label gets charted as lack of insight, denial, or, in some of the personality frameworks, as itself a feature of the thing. So whichever way a person goes it comes back as confirmation. The ones who took the label on show the category was always there; the ones who fight it show how deep it goes, because they can't see it. I've seen the same shape in the online communities where people swap symptom lists: you get presentations that match the list in order, top to bottom, which isn't how anything presents in the wild, and you also get the people who think the whole category is a scam, and that gets filed under the category too.

I know there's a well-known idea about people changing when they get classified, and I know about self-fulfilling prophecy, and this is obviously in that neighbourhood, but neither one is quite the thing I'm pointing at. What strikes me isn't the changing, it's that the changing counts as evidence no matter which way it goes, and nobody seems bothered that the two directions can't both be evidence.

Is there a name for that specific move, a category that recruits the reactions of the people it sorts as confirmation of itself, so nothing they do can embarrass it? If there's an established term, tell me which one and how exactly it differs from the looping thing, from self-fulfilling prophecy, and from a theory that's just unfalsifiable in the ordinary sense. If there isn't one, coin one and define it properly, including the edges: what would actually have to happen for the category to lose credibility on this score, and how would I tell my pattern apart from a case where the category is simply accurate and the people pushing back are simply resisting being told the truth about themselves.
