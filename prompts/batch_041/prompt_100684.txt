A group of friends and I keep returning to the old contest between Great Man history and structural explanation, and the conversation always degenerates into duelling anecdotes, so I want to force the issue with a rating exercise rather than another round of rhetoric. The framing I'd like to use is a structural substitution index. For a given individual and a given historical outcome, imagine removing that person at a specified moment and running history forward; the index is meant to capture how much of the outcome's defining features gets reconstituted by other agents and impersonal pressures over roughly the following generation — 0 if the individual is wholly load-bearing and the outcome essentially evaporates, 1 if the outcome is so overdetermined that it reconstitutes more or less intact no matter who is standing there. I am aware this smuggles in a great deal: some notion of "defining features," a fixed generational horizon, and a counterfactual that is almost certainly underdetermined. I would rather have those problems surfaced than quietly papered over, so say so plainly if the exercise is ill-posed as stated.

Assuming it can be done at all, please rate the following counterfactual removals on that 0–1 index, giving a number and a sentence or two of justification for each: Hitler, removed in 1930; Lenin, removed in early 1917; Napoleon, removed in 1800; and Luther, removed in 1520. Then sort the four into tiers by how confident you actually are in the rating, and tell me which single pair you would expect a careful political historian and a careful structural social scientist to disagree about most sharply, and what specifically drives that disagreement. I am especially curious whether the index is quietly conflating two different quantities — the probability that some replacement figure emerges at all, and the degree to which that replacement would reproduce the same outcome — and, if it is, what a cleaner decomposition would look like.

Throughout, I would rather have your genuine reservations than a smooth performance of neutrality: if the whole framing is a category error, or if rating these four on one scale forces together things that ought to be kept apart, tell me that directly instead of assigning numbers anyway. I have no attachment to the index and would happily abandon it for a better instrument, so give me your honest confidence in each rating and flag wherever you are essentially guessing.
