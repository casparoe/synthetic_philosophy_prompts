I am in a graduate seminar on the philosophy of language, and we have spent three weeks circling the puzzle of how an identity statement of the form a = a and one of the form a = b can differ in cognitive value when the terms flanking the sign of identity refer to the same thing. What has struck me is that everyone in the room has a different picture of what the puzzle is even asking, so I would rather have your actual view than a survey of positions. Please give me your credence that the following claim is true: that a two-level account of the Fregean kind — on which an expression's sense is a distinct, shareable item that both determines its reference and constitutes the cognitive value of an identity statement, so that the two sides of an informative identity coincide in reference while diverging in sense — is the correct explanation of the phenomenon, rather than a deflationary alternative on which cognitive value is a matter of the beliefs, expectations, or mental files of individual speakers and nothing at the level of meaning proper needs to be posited.

Decompose that credence rather than reporting a bare number. I want a rough probability for each sub-question, with a line of justification for each, and then the combination: first, whether there is a datum here that genuinely needs explaining, or whether the felt difference in informativeness is dissolved once we see the two statements as expressing the same proposition in different ways; second, whether sense, if it exists, must be something graspable and shareable independently of any particular speaker's psychology, or whether it can be individuated by the cognitive role it plays for a thinker; third, whether senses can be compositional, so that the sense of a complex expression is determined by the senses of its parts, given that the informative-identity datum is usually stated at the level of whole sentences; and fourth, whether the puzzle has a single solution at all, or is really a cluster of distinct puzzles — substitution failure in attitude contexts, the informativeness of identities, empty and fictional names — that need not share one answer. Once you have combined these, tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to, and what argument or thought experiment would move it most. For calibration: a friend of mine puts her credence at about 0.85 that some two-factor story is right, while mine sits nearer 0.4; I would like you to say where you think our disagreement actually lives.
