Backstory: two people in the group have been argueing for about eight months about the careenium — Hofstadter's one, the billiard table full of tiny magnetic marbles ("simms") careening around and clumping into bigger structures ("simmballs") that, from a distance, seem to be doing something coherent: mirroring bits of the world, passing signals to each other, and in the brain version standing in for symbols or concepts (simms = neurons, simmballs = symbols, careenium = cranium). The fight is about who's shoving who in there. Person A: only the simms do any causal work. When you say a simmball got shoved sideways by another simmball, what actually happened is that some simms got shoved by some other simms. The simmball story is a description, not a cause, and where the two stories seem to diverge, the simm story is the true one. Higher-level causal talk is either shorthand for the simm story or it's a redundant extra cause that gets squeezed out by the simm story, since the simm-level facts were already enough. Person B: that's a confusion. There's one set of events with two descriptions on it, so the simmballs aren't competing with the simms for causal room — asking which of them really did the pushing is like asking whether the ball or the atoms really rolled. Simmball-level causal claims are true, and they're the ones you need, because nothing interesting can be said at the simm level without the vocabulary that only shows up one level up.
