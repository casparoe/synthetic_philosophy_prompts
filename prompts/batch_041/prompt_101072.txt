I'm a novelist and I've fallen down a rabbit hole with structural realism — I spend my days building worlds out of relations between people, and I've started to wonder if thats what the physicists are up to as well, which is how I got here. What I want explained is the split inside the view. On one side there's the epistemic version: structure is all we can know, but there's some intrinsic nature underneath that our theories never reach — the continuity-of-equations argument, how the older wave-optics equations reappear as a limiting case inside Maxwell, so structure is the thing that survives theory change. On the other side there's the ontic version: structure is all there is, full stop, and asking what the relata are is a mistake.

The specific thing I don't get is the Newman objection. As I've seen it put, you write the theory's content as a Ramsey sentence, and it turns out any domain of the right cardinality can be rigged to satisfy it — so the structural content washes out to cardinality claims and nothing more. I don't see why that's fatal rather than just a fact about how we choose to write the theory down. So: does the objection actually kill the epistemic version, and if so, does the ontic version dodge it or just inherit it in a different form? Take a position, don't hand me the menu of options.

And then, since I think in stories: if relations are primitive and there are no relata, that sounds to me exactly like a novel with no characters in it, only the relations between them. Is that a fatal disanalogy, or is it the whole point of the view? Commit to an answer — I'd rather have a defended verdict than a hedge.
