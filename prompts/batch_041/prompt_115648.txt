Ok, I've talked myself into a view about psychiatric classification and I want to know what would kill it. The view is this: human kinds are interactive — hand someone a diagnostic label and you hand them a script, and the script feeds back into the very symptoms the label was supposed to be picking out, so the categories end up manufacturing some of their own instances. The standard move from there is just to say that validity testing gets harder. I want to go further: I think none of the usual confirmation for a psychiatric category — shared course, shared drug response, running in families — counts as independent evidence, because the loop sits upstream of all of it. And I'll add a prediction: the more culturally loud a diagnosis gets, the more its symptom profile should drift, and the less its validity should hold up across revisions. Tell me what would count against this, ranked from the ones that should actually make me drop it to the ones that are merely awkward for me. Be specific about which of these a defender of the view would themselves concede and which they'd wave off, and be honest about whether I've built something unfalsifiable — if every failure to find a loop gets met with "it just hasn't kicked in yet", thats a problem, right? I'd be most moved by a category that got invented, went big culturally, and then stubbornly refused to loop. Plain prose please, no headings, bullets, bold, or tables — write it like an email.
