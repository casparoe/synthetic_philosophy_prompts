I am looking for the name, if there is one, of a pattern I keep noticing, and I care more about its precise boundaries than about the label. The pattern is this: two people agree on every observable fact about a case and still disagree about what the case is — whether an unrequested recommendation is advice or manipulation, whether a drone strike is an act of war or a police action, whether a model's output is a prediction or a decision. Each party treats the new case as a version of some familiar case, and the real disagreement is over which familiar case should govern. When I try to talk someone out of their classification, I can only offer a different familiar case; I can never point to a fact that settles it, and each of us can go on producing fresh comparisons indefinitely, so the argument never reaches neutral ground. This seems stronger than framing, and unlike mere vagueness or equivocation, neither side is making a verbal mistake. I have a suspicion that the general phenomenon is not a special talent for metaphor but the basic operation of thought itself. Is there an established thesis to that effect? If the closest named views are family resemblance, prototype theory, or the claim that metaphor structures thought, explain how the thing I am describing differs from each and which fits best; if nothing fits, name and define it, and say what it commits one to and what it rules out. A bare name would leave me where I started.
