I'm taking a metaphysics seminar and we just finished a unit on dispositions, and I keep flip-flopping on the cases, so I want to figure out what I actually think and whether there's a principle behind it. I get why the plain conditional analysis is supposed to fail -- if a thing would M when S, then it's disposed to M when S -- that's not what I'm asking about. What I want is to know what my own verdicts add up to, because right now they feel like a pile.

Going on gut, here's what I say. A wine glass wrapped in bubble wrap and dropped on carpet: still fragile, obviously, the wrapping doesn't make it tough. A glass that looks the same but has a little charge rigged in the base that shatters it the instant anything strikes it, and that does nothing otherwise: not fragile, it's just booby-trapped. The wire from class, hooked to a gadget that keeps it dead and switches the current on only while a conductor is touching it: not live, I want to say, even though it lights the bulb while you hold it. The mirror-image wire, actually live, with a gadget that cuts it the moment anything touches it: I want to say live, even though it never lights anything. A sugar cube in a sealed dry box that will never see water: soluble. The same cube fully encased in epoxy so no water can ever reach it: soluble, I think, though I notice the cube-shaped object I'd pick up isn't. Someone who's genuinely short-tempered but is on a mood stabilizer: still short-tempered, the temper is in there, it's just not coming out. But my friend who took a hit to the head and became placid: not short-tempered anymore, even though before the injury he was, and I can't make that turn on the pills being 'in' him too, since they are. A plant carrying the gene for drought tolerance that's spent its whole life in a watered greenhouse: drought tolerant, no question. And the one that started this: a chatbot that would happily produce slurs if you jailbroke it, but whose guardrails stop it every time. My gut says it's not disposed to be offensive -- but only if the guardrail is baked into the weights rather than a filter bolted onto the output, and I can't tell which from the outside, and it bothers me that a verdict about what the thing is disposed to do should hinge on something I can't see.

I keep wanting to say the difference is whether the interfering condition is internal or external, but the gadget in the wire cases is external and I still say the finked wire isn't live, so that's dead. Is there a principle that gets all of these, or all but one? Something that says what has to be true of the intervening condition for the disposition to survive it -- and that tells me, for the cases where I can't see inside, which way the answer goes? If no single principle does it, tell me which verdicts you'd make me give up and why those rather than the others. And then apply whatever you end up with to one I haven't listed: a person who is generous, but who has only ever been in situations where generosity costs them nothing, so it's never been tested -- is that generosity real, and is it the same kind of thing as the bubble-wrapped glass or the epoxied sugar cube?
