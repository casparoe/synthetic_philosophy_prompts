ok so I'm in a small seminar on social kinds — basically metaphysics of social science — and the prof spends most of every session giving verdicts on whether whatever category we're discussing is a real kind. Next week it's generations (Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z, the whole zoo) and I have to lead the discussion, so I want to work out what she's going to say before she says it.

Her track record so far, as best I can reconstruct it:

- race: real, but social, not biological. She was firm that the boundaries are ours and don't line up with anything in the genome, and that this doesn't make it less real.
- the "Hispanic" box on the US census: she said it started out as an administrative convenience, a box on a form, and is now a real kind of person — institutions took it up, and so did the people it was applied to.
- left-handedness: real. Someone objected that handedness comes in degrees and we just cut it somewhere, and she said that's true of basically everything and it isn't what she cares about.
- the top 1% by income: not a real kind. Her line was that you're just describing everyone above a line, and everything else you can say about them — health, politics, connections — is downstream of being above the line. The group has no life of its own.
- alcoholic / addict: real, and she went out of her way to say the label does something, that people reorganize their lives and their self-understanding around it, so the classification is part of what holds the category together.
- middle class: not real. Just a number someone picked.
- teenagers: real. Someone pointed out this is also an arbitrary cut on a continuous variable and she said yes and moved on.

Two remarks I keep coming back to. Once, while we were arguing about exactly where the race/ethnicity lines fall, she said she doesn't care where you draw the line, she cares what holds the group together. And in the Hispanic discussion she said a category can start out as nothing and become real by being used. Those feel like they're pointing at different tests, but I can't tell whether they actually conflict or whether they're two halves of one thing.

So: are generations real kinds? Are Boomers and Gen Z kinds in the same sense race or left-handedness are, or is this just astrology for HR? Does it matter that the birth-year boundaries get drawn in different places by different outfits, and that they keep shifting? And here's a thing from my stats class that I can't stop thinking about: age, period and cohort are locked together — if you know how old someone is and what year it is, you already know when they were born — so you can't estimate a separate generation effect without adding an extra assumption, like that two of the three are equal. Does that bear on her view, or is that a different question wearing the same word?
