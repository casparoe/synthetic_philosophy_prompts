I've been sorting through the regularities around me, case by case, trying to decide which count as conventions, and I want to know what principle my verdicts add up to.

Everyone in my country driving on the right: a convention. The village path everyone takes across the field because it is simply the driest route, and they would take it whether or not anyone else did: not a convention. QWERTY: a convention, even though a better layout exists, because switching alone would only make things worse for the switcher. A rule of keeping promises: a convention, I think, though it sits more heavily on me than the road case does. A regularity that holds only because the police fine violators, and that would dissolve overnight if enforcement stopped: not a convention. Two strangers on a narrow sidewalk each stepping to the same side to get past the other: not a convention, since it is a one-off rather than a standing practice. Using money: a convention. A community's habit of not eating with the left hand, which each member would keep up even if no one else did, out of sheer discomfort: not a convention.

Two of these pull against each other, and I can't tell which to give up. Driving on the right is backed by law, yet I count it a convention, while the police-sustained regularity I don't — so whatever is doing the work cannot be enforcement. And promise-keeping I count a convention even though its grip on me doesn't seem to depend on others' conformity the way the road case does.

Is there a single principle that gets all of these, or nearly all? What does it say about a regularity that began as coordination among a few people and spread by precedent until it became common knowledge — does how it spread matter to whether it is a convention? And which of my verdicts does the principle force me to abandon?
