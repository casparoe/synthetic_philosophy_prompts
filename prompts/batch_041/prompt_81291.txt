I'm putting together a seminar for people who already know the Protestant Ethic backwards, so please skip the 101. Treat yourself as a historian of social theory — someone who's actually read Weber's methodological essays (the Roscher and Knies piece, the objectivity essay) and the China volume, not just the wikipedia version of the thesis.

What I want is a prediction. Weber died in 1920, so he never saw any of the modern culture-versus-institutions fight: Banfield and the amoral familism stuff, Landes on culture and prosperity, the Acemoglu-Robinson line that it's institutions and not culture, Henrich's book where the western church's marriage rules do the causal work. Tell me where he'd land in that fight, argued in his own vocabulary and not ours. Use his tools: ideal types, the difference between a value-judgment and value-relevance, legitimacy, the difference between explaining by meaning and explaining by mechanism.

Start with a scratchpad where you brainstorm which of the modern positions would even be intelligible to him and which would look like a category mistake. Then the main prediction.

Here's the twist I actually want you to chew on. A development economist I've been reading claims the whole culture-causes-growth literature collapses into one measurable thing, which she calls the patience channel: groups that discount the future less heavily save more, invest more, grow faster, and Protestantism was just an early technology for lowering a discount rate. On her view Weber is a romantic, pre-formal anticipation of a single parameter in a growth model. Does Weber accept the translation, or does something get lost in it that he'd refuse to lose? And if the spirit of capitalism turns out to be a discount rate, does the thesis still say anything, or does it dissolve?

Two more things. Would he take sides in the normative argument that rides along with all of this — that some cultures are just better at growth? His value-freedom doctrine says he shouldn't, but his critics say his own account is soaked in evaluation, so tell me which way you think he'd actually go and whether the doctrine gives him a clean exit. And finally, where would he be out of his depth? What in the modern debate would he simply lack the concepts to see?

No bibliography, no page numbers, no invented letters — I don't need citations and I'd definately rather you not guess at any. Think it through seperate before you write; I'd rather have one careful answer than a list of bullets.
