I am midway through an MPhil on counterfactual reasoning in history, and this term my supervisor has been running a reading group on the subject. She has four verdicts that she repeats almost verbatim whenever a counterfactual comes up, and after a year of supervision I have begun to suspect that all four are generated by a single criterion she has never actually stated. First, there would have been a great-power war in Europe even without the assassination at Sarajevo; the Archduke's killer, as she puts it, was the occasion and not the cause. Second, there would have been a Reformation without Luther — some rupture with Rome was coming, and only its theological complexion would have differed. Third, penicillin would have been discovered within a decade without Fleming. Fourth, and to my surprise the one on which she is most immovable: there would have been no October insurrection without Lenin, which she treats as the case that rescues the individual from being written out of history altogether. We argue about this most weeks, and I keep losing: I concede Lenin, and then find I cannot say what makes Hitler different.

Next week the group turns to the 1930s, and the question she has set us is the one I actually care about: would there have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler? Please do not simply adjudicate the great-man-versus-structure debate; I know that literature well enough. What I want is a prediction about her. Tell me what verdict she will give; the rule she appears to be running across her four earlier verdicts; at least one other rule that fits those four just as well but predicts something different about the Nazi case; which of the candidate rules you would bet on, and why; and the fifth counterfactual — not the Nazi one — that would best discriminate between them. If her four verdicts genuinely underdetermine the new case, say so plainly, and tell me what would have to be true of her for each answer to be the correct prediction.

I have a suspicion that everything turns on how finely the outcome is described: whether Nazi Germany means that particular party-state, with its Führer cult and its Holocaust, or merely some authoritarian, antisemitic and revanchist German regime that the structural pressures of the 1930s were going to produce in any case. Tell me whether that is where the real disagreement lies, or whether I am using the point about descriptions to dodge a question that her verdicts already answer.
