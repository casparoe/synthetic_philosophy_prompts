Hey, I'm in this archives-and-power seminar and for the final project I have to write a short story that dramatizes one of the seminar's ideas, and I want to do the one about how a record doesn't just describe power, it's how power makes things count as real. The bit that got me: a town wants to mark something awful that happened there, but the only surviving paperwork about it was written by the people who did it, so every time they try to tell the story they end up sort of quoting their own victimizers' filing system back at themselves. And the flip side -- something that definately happened but nobody ever wrote down is, officially, just a rumor until it gets on the record, and the people it happened to have to fight to get it entered before anyone will treat it as real.

I don't want the obvious version where a heroic researcher finds the one dusty box that proves everything. I want the absence itself to be the subject -- characters stuck because the exact thing they need is what was never allowed to be written. And I want the main character to be the person making a record, not finding one: someone who has to decide whether writing it down protects the people it's about or exposes them, since once it's in the archive it's real but it's also findable. Can you write me about 1,500 words of that? Real story with people in it, not a lecture in a trenchcoat.
