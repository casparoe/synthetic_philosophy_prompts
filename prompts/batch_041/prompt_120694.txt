Bea: There is no responsibility gap. When an autonomous system does something catastrophic, a human designed it, a human trained it, a human deployed it, a human turned it on. Responsibility always traces back to a person. The "gap" is people not wanting to look hard enough.

Owen: Tracing only works if somebody could have foreseen the outcome. The whole point of a learning system is that the behavior that does the harm wasn't in anybody's head beforehand. You can't blame someone for what they had no way of knowing. So the gap is real.

Bea: Negligence law has handled this forever — you're responsible for the risks you create, foreseeable or not, that's literally what "should have known" is for. You built a thing you couldn't predict and let it run loose. That's on you.

Owen: Then you get blame-by-hindsight, where every bad outcome retroactively proves someone should have known. That makes the designer the insurer of every downstream harm, which is unfair and would strangle the whole field. Also, if the designer is 30% responsible and the operator 40% and the end user 20% and the vendor 10%, that's not a gap, it's just fractions — and what people actually want is someone answerable, someone who has to explain and can be held to account, not someone who *deserves* to suffer.

Bea: Then it's a gap in accountability *practices*, not in responsibility, and that's an institutional design problem, not a metaphysical one. And we already do this elsewhere — I have a vague memory that military law holds commanders responsible for war crimes their troops commit that they didn't order and didn't know about, if they failed to set up proper training and oversight. If we can do that to a colonel, we can do it to a CEO.

Owen: Command responsibility works because a commander has a hierarchy that can actually be made to obey, and because the stakes justify something close to strict liability. A deployed model isn't a subordinate — nobody can order it to stop misclassifying. And you're ignoring the crumple-zone thing (I know there's a name for this and it's escaping me): the human babysitter in the loop is partly there so that there's a warm body to blame when the machine screws up. That's not a fix for the gap, it's a way of hiding it.

Bea: That just shows the gap only exists if you insist responsibility has to be *deserved*. You keep sliding between "nobody deserves blame" and "nobody is responsible," and the second one is just false.
