Hi — I'm in a reading group on consciousness and I keep hitting a wall with the "user illusion" thing, and I'm hopeing you can straighten me out. Please set yourself up as a philosopher of mind who actually knows both the philosophy and the interface-design/cognitive-science background here, think it through carefully rather than giving me the standard tour, and maybe start by sorting out the different jobs the word "illusion" is doing in this literature — I suspect my confusion is part verbal and part not.

Here's my problem. As I understand it, the claim is that consciousness is a user illusion. I gather the phrase originally comes from the people doing the first desktop interfaces at Xerox PARC — windows, folders, the little trash can, all of it a simplified picture for the person at the keyboard — and that it then got picked up by Dennett and by a Danish science writer with a book actually called The User Illusion, and the idea is that the brain builds a simplified self-model, a kind of dashboard, so that the organism doesn't have to know what's really going on under the hood. Fine. But an illusion is something that appears to someone, right? A user illusion is an illusion *for a user*. So if consciousness is the user illusion, who is the user? It can't be consciousness itself, because that's the thing that's supposed to be illusory. If it's the brain, then the brain is the one being fooled — but the brain isn't a person, and anyway the brain is what's doing the fooling, so that looks circular. And if there's genuinely no user at all, the whole phrase seems like a contradiction: you've told me the interface is fake and then told me nobody's at the keyboard. My friend pushes this harder. She says it's the same move as the Buddhist no-self stuff, and that illusionists basically think we're zombies — nobody home, just a machine that falsely believes it's having experiences. And she says that if the illusion is supposed to be about our experience having these special "phenomenal" properties, then the illusion still has to *seem like something* to someone, which she thinks makes the position either trivially false or straight-up incoherent. That can't be right, but I can't say exactly why.

So: who is the user, and if the answer is "nobody," doesn't that make the view self-undermining — doesn't an illusion need a subject to be fooled for there to be an illusion at all? Don't just reassure me that the view is respectable; walk me through what the actual commitments are, what "illusion" is and isn't being used to mean here, and where exactly my picture (and my friend's) goes wrong.
