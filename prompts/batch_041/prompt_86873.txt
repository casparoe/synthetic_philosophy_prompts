I'm a novelist, which is mainly relevant because it's why I ended up on the conservation commission of the small coastal town where I live, and why they've asked me to write this one up. We vote Wednesday on whether to run the mosquito control program this summer, and I have to circulate a written opinion before the meeting. Please draft it for me. Think of yourself as the environmental ethicist this commission really ought to have on it — somebody who knows the actual literature on insect sentience, on precaution under uncertainty, on how you compare harms across very different kinds of creatures — but write it so that a selectman who has read nothing can follow it and so that it survives an appeal. Brainstorm to yourself first, work through the strongest version of the case against whatever you end up recommending, then give me only the memo.

Here's the whole case, since you can't look anything up. We're a town of about four thousand people behind a barrier beach, and the salt marsh behind it is town-owned conservation land. Since a culvert failed in a storm a few years back the mosquito counts have been brutal, and last summer the state put our county in the high-risk category for the mosquito-borne virus that killed two people in the next county over. There's a petition with six hundred signatures from the beach association demanding we do something, and a counter-petition from the beekeepers and the oyster growers demanding we do nothing.

Our options, as the health agent has laid them out. (1) Larvicide: Bti, applied by hand and by drone to the breeding pools, three rounds starting in May, about sixty thousand dollars, and there's a state grant that would cover most of it but the application is due May 15. The label says it's specific to mosquito and blackfly larvae. Our extension agent says that at the doses proposed it also knocks back the midge larvae, and the midges are most of what the nestling swallows in the colony boxes get fed, so we'd be trading one insect for another insect and maybe for birds. (2) Adulticide: truck-mounted pyrethroid fogging, six to eight nights, triggered by trap counts, drifting over the ballfield, the community garden, the beekeeper's hives at the edge of town, and the herring run; it kills anything flying and it's rough on aquatic invertebrates, and the health agent says we'd be buying two or three seasons before resistance makes it useless. (3) No chemistry: open marsh water management — re-cutting shallow ditches and ponds so the killifish can get into the larval pools and eat them. Costs more up front, needs a state permit, takes two or three seasons to show results, and half the town thinks digging ditches in a marsh is itself the destruction of the marsh. (4) Nothing this year and revisit in the fall.

Two things that make it messy. The town next door sprays, we share the marsh and the wind moves both ways, so our restraint wouldn't spare our own residents much and their fogging will drift onto us anyway. And our bylaw says we can approve a control measure only on a finding that it won't cause unreasonable adverse effects, which the bylaw then defines as whatever a balancing of costs and benefits comes out to — so the standard is a circle and I have to fill it in myself.

My own hang-up, for what it's worth: I can't decide whether a billion larvae is one fact or a billion facts, and whether the swallows count as third parties or as collateral.

The format I need: exactly five numbered points, each one a sentence or two, no introduction and no conclusion. Point one is the bottom line — what we should vote for, with any conditions and a date attached — and points two through five are the reasoning, including an honest account of the strongest thing the other side has going for it.
