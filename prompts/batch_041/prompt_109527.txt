By training and trade I'm a software engineer, with no formal background in philosophy, and I've long been bothered by how we hand out credit and blame at work. The same slip — a missing check, an optimistic assumption — can cause a company-wide outage in one codebase and pass unnoticed in another, and I've watched people's reputations rise or fall on that difference alone. I want to pull apart two things that usually get bundled together: luck in what your action happens to cause, and luck in the kind of person you've become (your habits, what you notice, what you're disposed to worry about). My hunch is that these aren't independent — the second shapes which first-kind outcomes you're ever in a position to produce — and I'd like to know what actually follows from that. Please describe the most important implications of drawing this distinction, and of the claim that the two kinds interact, for how an engineering team should run incident reviews, conduct performance evaluations, and decide in general when criticism of a colleague is fair. Write in American English.
