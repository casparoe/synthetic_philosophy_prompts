I'm helping a small alignment-theory group set its research agenda for the next two years, and our chosen target is HCH-style factored cognition: the ideal in which a deliberator answers a question by delegating subquestions to other deliberators, who delegate in turn, until the recursion bottoms out in some base case of unaided human judgment. We want to treat this as an idealization of deliberation and ask what the idealization assumes away. Which open conceptual questions about this ideal are most worth working on, and why? Give me a defended agenda rather than a survey: rank the questions, and justify the ranking in terms of which are upstream of which, which a couple of philosophers could make progress on without new empirical results, and which only look important because their interest is inherited from a question that is really doing the work. Two things I especially want addressed: whether the base case carries all the normative weight, so that HCH is a relabeling rather than an improvement on the individual deliberator; and whether HCH is best read as a limit that actual factored systems approximate in degree or as a normative target that fixes what the right answer is. Close by naming the question you would drop, and saying what argument or evidence over the next two years would move something up the list.
