I have been reading about money-pump arguments and would like to construct one to order, on the diachronic rather than the synchronic side. Please build me a case to the following specifications.

The scenario itself should be one paragraph, realistic, and made of ordinary consumer goods and ordinary money: no addictions or drug-like preference reversals, no infinite or indefinitely long sequences, no exotic physics, no demon and no perfect predictor. The agent must be synchronously rational — at each moment, their preferences over the options then before them should be complete, transitive, and consistent with expected-utility maximization — so that nothing in the case turns on a synchronic defect. The agent must also have full foresight: they know the whole sequence of trades in advance and are free to refuse any of them, so the pump cannot work by surprise or naivety. Each trade, when offered, must be one the agent strictly prefers by their own lights at that time. The upshot is that the agent ends where they began, minus a sum of money.

After the scenario, walk through each step and identify the preference that makes it rational, then state precisely the principle of diachronic rationality that the agent violates; I want the principle stated, because I need to know whether the case requires one such principle or several. Then do the checks. Say which single feature of the case is doing the work and what happens when it is removed: in particular, whether the pump still runs if the agent's preferences are required to be stable across time, and whether it still runs if the agent is permitted to bind themselves in advance. Finally, put the strongest case for the agent: how would someone who denies that rationality imposes any cross-time consistency requirement, holding that only each time-slice's own preferences matter, describe what has happened — and is that a refutation of the case or merely a redescription of it?

I want the case for a narrow purpose: to see whether the money-pump argument for diachronic consistency can be run against an agent who is synchronously impeccable, or whether every such argument must quietly smuggle in a synchronic defect. Please assess the case against that standard.
