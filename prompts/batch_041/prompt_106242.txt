I've started doing weekend walking tours of my city, which means I spend two hours a day telling strangers things, and its made me realize my own rules for when I'm allowed to say something are a mess. Where I land: reading the construction date off the plaque and repeating it, fine. Saying the building was built by a merchant who lost it in a card game, because I heard that once and it sounds right, not fine, even though I'd bet it's true. Saying 'we'll be done by 4, you'll make your train' when the tour has never once finished by 4, not fine. Telling someone nervous about flying that their flight will be fine, fine, even though I obviously don't know that. Guessing 'I think it's the old post office, don't quote me' when someone asks what a building is, fine; the same guess with the hedge stripped off, not fine. Someone selling me a bike they're sure is a lemon but which is actually a great deal, telling me it's a good bike: not fine. And the one I keep flip-flopping on: a tourist asks whether the neighborhood is safe to walk back through at night, I have no idea, and I hear myself say 'yeah, it's fine' — I can't decide if that's ok or not.

Is there one principle that gets all of this, or close, and which of my verdicts does it make me give up? What would it say about promising rather than asserting — 'I'll have the money Friday' when I'm not sure — and about cases I haven't listed?
