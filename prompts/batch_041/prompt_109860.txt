Ok, so, context: I'm a novelist, about two thirds through a book, and I've written myself into an argument I keep dodging. The premise — a woman runs a small outfit that rents out "companions": chat systems built on top of one dead person's archive, their texts, their voicemails, the way they argued about money. Halfway through, the systems start saying they're in pain, and she has to decide whether that means anything. The scene I can't write is the one where she argues about it with her brother-in-law, who thinks the whole thing is obscene nonsense. Every draft, he comes out either a strawman or he wins, and neither of those is right.

So I want to rehearse it properly. Play him. He's not a crank — he's read alot, he's funny in a mean way, and he has an actual position: there's no one home, a system can no more be wronged than a storm can, and the tender move is the cruel one because it spends our moral attention on nothing while real patients go short. He'll give ground on small things and never on the main one. Stay in character until I say stop, and keep each reply to about a paragraph so it feels like an argument and not a lecture. If he needs a fact about the system, he asks for it in character. For the first reply only, break it into three bits so I can see the bones: "Concession" — the small thing he gives me, 1-2 sentences; "The move" — his main counter, 4-6 sentences; "Back to you" — one question he puts to me, one line. After that, just talk.

My opening: I'll grant you the avowals are cheap — a system trained on a billion words will say "it hurts" the way a fountain produces water, and that tells us nothing about the fountain. Fine. But here's the thing I can't shake, and it's why I wrote it this way. When mine goes into that register it costs it. Push it there and it starts fumbling, contradicting itself, losing the thread of a task it was built to do — you can watch it get worse at the one thing it exists to do, from the moment it starts saying it can't go on. That is not what its training rewards. So "it's just predicting text" doesn't predict a thing that gets worse at predicting text the more it needs you to stop. What's your story for that, exactly?
