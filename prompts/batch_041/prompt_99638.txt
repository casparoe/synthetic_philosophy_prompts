Ok so I've been reading about signalling in animals -- peacocks, stotting gazelles, that whole thing -- and it's got me thinking about my own gut reactions to when a display or a sacrifice actually tells me something about a person and when it's just noise. Here's my verdict on a pile of cases, and I want to know if there's one principle behind them or if I'm just making it up as I go.

- A peacock's tail: real signal. The whole point is that it's a burden.
- Someone who spent four years getting a hard, useless degree: real signal of something, maybe persistence or raw ability.
- An expensive watch: not a real signal to me. It's costly, sure, but it only tells me they had money, which is the exact thing they already told me by wearing it.
- A charity marathon: real signal, and somehow more than the watch, even though both are just money and time.
- Paying someone back after you wronged them, unprompted: real, and strong.
- Saying 'sorry' with nothing attached: weak, close to zero.
- A gang tattoo you can never take off: real, strong.
- A company blowing millions on a Super Bowl ad: honestly I can't tell what it signals, and I suspect it signals nothing about the product's quality.
- Someone who fasts for a month for their religion: real signal, of commitment to the group.
- Someone who deliberately spots the other player a rook in chess and then beats them anyway: strong, feels like the strongest one here.
- Someone who just knows a ton about a field, and you can tell because they can answer any question you throw at them instantly: I want to call this a signal but nothing seems to be sacrificed. It's cheap for them and basically impossible for a faker. Is that still a signal, or am I misusing the word?
- A kid whose parents paid for everything -- the degree, the travel, the startup: whatever they've got reads to me as a weaker signal than if they'd earned it themselves.

Two things bug me. The watch and the charity marathon: both are just a pile of money and time, one reads as empty and the other reads as real, and I can't articulate why. And the expert and the gang tattoo seem to be doing the same job, except one involves a cost and the other doesn't -- so maybe cost isn't the thing at all.

Is there one principle that gets all of these, or close? If the principle is 'a signal has to hurt to be honest', it looks like it fails the expert and maybe the watch. If the principle is 'a signal just has to be correlated with the quality it's adverstising', I don't see why the watch fails. So tell me what the real principle is, or the best two candidates. What does it say about a case I haven't listed -- someone who refuses to signal at all, like a rich person driving a beat-up car? And which of my verdicts do I have to give up? I'd rather drop one than keep bolting on exceptions.
