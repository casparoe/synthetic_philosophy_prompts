ok so i've been trying to write a poem about acausal trade for like three weeks and it keeps coming out as a philosophy essay with line breaks, so i'm outsourcing it to you. quick version of the idea in case it's not already in your head: two agents who can never talk to each other — different light cones, maybe different universes, maybe one of them only *possibly* exists — each works out what the other one would do by modelling its reasoning, and then each makes a concession it thinks the other would reciprocate for. no signal, no enforcement, nothing that could ever be checked. the deal is real only in the sense that your own choice is evidence about theirs. a handshake in the dark with someone you've deduced is reaching back.

so: a villanelle. nineteen lines, five tercets and the closing quatrain, refrains alternating and then forced together in the last two lines. make the two refrains the two sides' bids, and make each one recognizably one half of the bargain — something cheap for the speaker and dear to the absent partner, and the mirror of that in the other refrain — so that someone who knows the concept can tell which side is speaking in which refrain. the refrains circling each other and never touching until the form shoves them side by side at the end is the whole thing; let that do the work instead of explaining it. one stanza should own up to the weirdest part, that one of the parties might not exist at all, and that this doesn't change the offer. and please don't let it turn into a basilisk thing or a lecture on decision theory — i want it to land as a poem about reaching for someone who is reasoning their way toward you at the same time. if you have to choose, accuracy over prettiness.
