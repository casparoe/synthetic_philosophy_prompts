I'm a third-year student in a course on historical method and international security, and for the final seminar I have to present a short philosophical self-portrait of my views on how the past bears on present policy, so that my classmates can press on it. Before I stand up and defend it, I'd like you to audit it for internal consistency. Please act as a philosopher of history and of the social sciences — someone at home with the covering-law debate, with case-based and analogical reasoning, and with the difference between idiographic and nomothetic explanation — and think carefully before answering; it would help if you first brainstorm about what each commitment presupposes about the others, and only then write the audit.

Here, roughly, is where I've landed. (1) History yields no nontrivial general laws, so reasoning about the present has to proceed case-to-case; historical analogies are therefore indispensable. (2) Historical knowledge is knowledge of particulars — the historian's competence is over the individual case, not over patterns. (3) The Munich analogy, as it was used to frame Vietnam, was a paradigm case of analogy going wrong. (4) The so-called Thucydides trap names a real structural regularity: a rising power and a ruling power tend toward war. (5) Historical outcomes are radically contingent — the same structural situation could resolve peacefully or violently, and it is hindsight that makes the actual outcome look inevitable. (6) A historical analogy does not predict; it directs attention. Its job is to make you notice features of the present case you would otherwise miss, not to license a forecast.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture. Tell me which of these pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are and why, and rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad. For each genuine tension, say which commitment would have to give or be qualified, what the cheapest repair would be, and what that repair costs me elsewhere; then tell me which single revision would clean up the most at the least expense. Be specific about which commitment is doing the work in each tension, and please don't assume I already know where the problem is — if one of the apparent tensions dissolves, I'd rather understand why than be told it does.
