I'm in a philosophy of mind reading group and we've been going round in circles for weeks on the Dennett–Searle thing about original versus derived intentionality, and I'm definately starting to think we're not even disagreeing about the same thing. The setup everyone knows: Dennett's vending machine that's rigged to reject slugs and only take genuine US quarters — his "two-bitser" — and his point that if you can't tell from the outside whether a system has real aboutness or merely behaves as if it does, then the line between "original" and "derived" intentionality isn't doing any explanatory work, it's just a stance we adopt. Searle comes back and says no, the machine's "representations" are derived and observer-relative through and through — the aboutness belongs to us and our purposes, not to the mechanism — and only something with the right causal powers (brains, on his view) gets original or intrinsic intentionality. Then Dennett says Searle has just assumed the conclusion, Searle says Dennett has confused the ascription of intentionality with the metaphysics of it, and around we go. Nobody has moved in decades and I don't think a new paper is going to move them.

Here's the extra wrinkle that's making me think the whole thing is mis-framed. I've got a cheap little wheeled robot at home that avoids obstacles and plays a sad beep when it gets wedged under the couch, and my group immediately started arguing about whether it "wants" to get out — the Dennett people saying sure, at the intentional stance, who cares; the Searle people saying thats exactly the sloppy move that erases the difference between the robot and me. But it struck me that if I wired that robot's beeping into a real reinforcement learner that kept an internal state tracking the couch and driving the escape behavior, and it got better at it over time, both sides would just dig in harder, not converge. Which makes me suspect the stall isn't about the robot or even about the quarters.

So what I actually want from you is a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right. Is this a verbal dispute about the word "intentionality"? Two camps answering two different questions — Dennett a predictive/interpretive one ("when does treating it as a believer pay off?") and Searle a constitutive one ("what makes a state a genuine representation?") — under one label? A suppressed empirical dispute about whether biology is doing any real work? Or two sets of starting intuitions, functionalism versus biological naturalism, that neither side can argue for from neutral ground? And if it is one of those, tell me what would actually count as progress here — some distinction, or a case both sides would have to accept, or a reason to think the question is just ill-posed — or else argue that no progress should be expected and why. Don't just summarize the two positions; I know them. I want to know what kind of fight this is.
