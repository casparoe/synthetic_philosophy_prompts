Two camps have been arguing past each other about generations, and I want the document they should have produced together. The first camp, call them cohort realists, holds that labels like Boomer, Millennial, and Gen Z pick out real social kinds: clusters of people whose shared formative experiences and mutual influence give them shared dispositions that are not merely reducible to age or period effects. The second camp, cohort nominalists, holds that these labels are administrative and marketing artifacts laid over a continuous birth-year distribution, that their apparent content is an artifact of age and period effects plus the labels themselves, and that generation earns no place among the kinds we should posit.

Draft the joint statement these two camps could sign after a serious, good-faith adversarial collaboration. It should have a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table, and it should be written so that neither side feels it has conceded its own view in the agreed section or been strawmanned in the table.

In the agreed-claims section, include at least the following, phrased so both camps would sign them: what the data can and cannot show; the point that age, period, and cohort are linearly confounded, since cohort is period minus age, so an additive model treating all three is unidentified without extra constraints; the fact that within-cohort variance typically dwarfs between-cohort mean differences; the fact that the conventional boundary years have themselves been revised over time by the very organizations that use them, which bears on whether boundaries are discovered or stipulated; and a shared rejection of the idea that generations are biological kinds. Avoid claims that are vacuous or that quietly assume one side's framework.

For the disagreements table, state each residual disagreement in neutral wording, then give each side's own preferred formulation in wording that side would accept, then the crux. I expect at least these cruxes and want them handled explicitly: (a) whether the generational cluster meets something like a homeostatic-property-cluster standard, that is, whether there is a shared mechanism holding the properties together rather than a mere disjunction of birth years; (b) whether a cohort effect is identifiable at all, where a promising design is cross-national comparison exploiting period shocks that arrive in different countries in different years, which can break the collinearity; (c) whether the label loops back and produces the phenomenon, in the sense of a classification that alters the classified; and (d) whether the boundary problem is a genuine difficulty for realism or an artifact of vague kind terms, i.e., whether two people born a day apart across a cutoff year are thereby of different kinds.

For each crux, give a concrete case, test, or argument that both sides agree would bear on it, and record each side's prediction about that case in advance, so that the collaboration would actually update someone. Close with a short section identifying which parts of the dispute are merely verbal, turning on what a defender of real kinds is required to demand, and stating what each side would count as evidence against its own position. Keep it concise and free of citations or reading lists.
