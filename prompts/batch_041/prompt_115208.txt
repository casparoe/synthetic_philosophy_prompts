A friend and I spent most of a long evening arguing about whether lottocracy is a real alternative to elections, and I would like to turn that argument into an activity for my upper-level seminar on democratic theory — sixteen students, one ninety-minute session. Design something that makes them run into two things I could not make land in conversation. The first is that a lottery can randomise who sits in the room while leaving the question they are asked, and the briefing they are handed, entirely with whoever commissioned the assembly — so the lot launders the question-setter's power rather than distributing it. The second is that deliberation destroys the independence on which a random sample's epistemic advantage depends: the group can become more confident and more agreed without becoming any more accurate. Neither point should be announced beforehand; the rules, the draws and the stakes should arrange for them to discover both.

Have the response open with the activity's core mechanic in a single sentence, then give the design. Include the timings and staging; what is drawn by lot and what is not, and by whom; the materials (the motion, the briefing pack, the procedure for those not selected, and how the second run changes the rules); a stake that is real enough to matter without money; and a twenty-minute debrief that maps each moment of what actually happened onto the two points above, plus the expertise carve-out — whether any posts should be filled by election rather than lot — as something the facilitator has to raise rather than something the activity can show. Say what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — I expect the unselected to disengage or turn resentful, and I expect one student to spot the loaded framing early and make everyone else perform suspicion — and what to do about each. Be frank about what the activity cannot reproduce: the scale, duration and bindingness of a real assembly, and anything about whether those on the losing side would accept the outcome. No bibliography or reading list; I want something I can run next term with a whiteboard, an envelope and a stack of index cards.
