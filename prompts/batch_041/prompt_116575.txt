I'm starting a small podcast where I talk to philosophers about stuff that overlaps with my day job as a software engineer, and next weeks guest is someone who's spent years arguing that the "genetic code" is a genuine code and not just a metaphor biologists got lazy with. I want questions that make them actually work for it instead of just restating the two sides. Some things I want to poke at. First, the codon table looks arbitrary -- nothing about a given triplet forces it to map to a given amino acid, which is weirdly like how a word doesn't resemble what it means -- does that arbitrariness buy you a real code, or is it just a contingent fact about chemistry that could've gone the other way? Second thing that bugs me: every code I've written has a coder and a separate machine that reads it, but here the thing doing the reading (the ribosomes and the tRNAs) is itself built from the same instructions, so it feels circular -- does a code need a reader that stands outside it, and if the reader is inside, does "code" still mean anything? Third, in my world information is stuff you can send and measure regardless of what it means, whereas the biology people seem to want information that means something (this triplet is *for* that amino acid) -- press on whether that "for" is real or just us talking. Also get them on whether the word is doing any actual work: if we banned "code" tomorrow, what prediction or experiment would we lose? And throw in one on whether the whole fight is secretly just a verbal dispute about how to use a word. I know they'll deflect a couple of these, so give me a follow-up keyed to the answer they're most likely to give for each. Keep it tight, like eight questions plus follow-ups.
