I'm in an undergrad seminar on interpretability and we've been reading a bunch of work on probing classifiers, "features," and directions in activation space — you know, the kind where someone finds that a particular direction in the residual stream lines up with the model tracking whether a statement is true, or a single unit that fires on sentiment. My seminar leader keeps talking about "what the network represents" as if that's a meaningful question with a real answer, and I think I've spotted the flaw. Those "features" aren't discovered, they're made up. The researcher picks a probe, picks a label, picks a direction, and of course you can always find *some* direction in a few-thousand-dimensional space that correlates with *some* human concept — that's just linear algebra plus confirmation bias. So the network doesn't represent anything; the representation lives in the eye of the interpretability researcher. Which means the whole "do neural networks have representations?" question is confused, right? The answer is just no, and anyone who says yes is projecting.

But I feel like thats too quick, because my seminar leader (who is annoyingly sharp) said something like "nobody thinks the label is sitting in the weights, that was never the claim," and I didn't get a chance to push back. So: is the answer to the debate really just no, that it's all projection? Or is there a version of "yes" that survives the observation that any labeling is ours?
