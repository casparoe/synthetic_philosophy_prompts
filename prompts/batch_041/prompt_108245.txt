Assume, for the sake of argument, that human extinction would be bad at least partly because of the deaths it involves. The disputed question is whether it would be bad beyond those deaths. Rank the following six candidate explanations of that further badness, labelled A through F, from most to least defensible. They are stated in deliberately non-standard form so that they cannot simply be matched to textbook positions.

A. Future-person deprivation. Extinction is bad because future people who would have lived valuable lives are deprived of those lives. Note the familiar objection that a person who never exists cannot be deprived in the ordinary way.

B. Project-continuation dependence. Many present people’s projects, commitments, and loves are structured around a continuing human future, so extinction retroactively undermines the value those projects had for them. This is the Schefflerian afterlife conjecture, stripped of the claim that what matters must be belief rather than hope.

C. Retrospective completion. Past lives, struggles, and achievements are made sense of as parts of a still-unfolding human story; extinction forecloses that completion and thereby changes the meaning of what already happened.

D. Narrative closure value. A human history that ends with extinction has a different aesthetic or narrative value from one that continues or ends well, and this difference is not reducible to anyone’s welfare.

E. Responsive agency. Extinction eliminates the possibility of future agents responding to past wrongs and to one another’s moral claims; it is a loss of the ongoing practice of accountability.

F. Archive and plurality value. Extinction destroys the human cultural and biological archive and the plurality of forms of life, which has value independent of any person’s experience of it.

In ranking A–F, address each of the following. Which of them are genuinely additional to the deaths, and which collapse into the deaths or into a general impersonal value of life? Which can be endorsed by someone who denies that merely possible people can be harmed? Which require a controversial metaphysics of value, such as impersonal value, aesthetic value, or the value of a story, and which do not? Are any two of them not distinct after all? Which would be accepted by a thoughtful proponent of the view that only the welfare of actual people matters, and which would not?

Give a tier list with reasoning for each placement. Do not give a bibliography or citations. End with a single line in the form \answer{...} containing the ranking as labels separated by >, from most to least defensible, for example \answer{A > B > C > D > E > F}.
