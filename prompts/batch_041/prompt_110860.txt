I'm a backend dev with no philosophy training, and I've been drafting an essay for my blog on the idea that consciousness is a "user illusion". Could you read my notes below and tell me where im wrong or where the argument has a hole? I care most about (a) whether I'm accidentally strawmanning or just reinventing some standard view without knowing it, and (b) whether the last section actually works or whether I've only renamed the problem.

Notes:
- Open with the GUI analogy: the desktop, the icons, the trash can. The pixels are real, but nothing on screen resembles the transistors. The interface is a useful fiction that hides the machine.
- Claim: saying consciousness is an illusion doesn't mean nothing is going on. It means the properties we introspect — the redness of red, the ache of the ache — aren't properties the brain actually has. The brain has whatever it has; the UI renders it as simple, private, ineffable.
- The objection I keep running into: an illusion is normally an illusion *for someone*. If the "user" is itself part of the illusion, then illusion relative to what? Feels like I'm sawing off the branch I'm sitting on.
- My attempted reply: drop the word "illusion" and say the mind is a model that leaves the modeler out. Like a debugger that prints the program's state, but whose own readouts never show up in the state dump. The "user" is a readout, not a reader.
- Then I try to make this more than word games by predicting something: if the UI is built for control rather than accuracy, we should expect it to represent its own workings as atomic and ineffable even though they're composite and computational. So the ineffability of experience is a design choice, not a deep fact.
- Why the hard problem feels hard: the UI can't render its own implementation, so it reports a gap. Like a VM that can't show you the host's memory layout.
- Where I'm stuck: if I explain why it *seems* like there's a hard problem, have I explained consciousness or explained it away? And is "an illusion that there's something it's like" even coherent without a subject to be fooled?

Be blunt, especially on that last bit.
