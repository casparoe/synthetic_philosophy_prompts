In my seminar on the uses and abuses of the concept of "collapse" in history and archaeology, I have to write an essay that takes a side on a conceptual controversy and defends it. The controversy is this: does "collapse" name a genuine kind of historical event — a real, identifiable outcome that societies sometimes undergo — or is it a retrospective label we impose on societies that merely changed, with the label tracking the observer's disappointment rather than any fact about the society? Please argue for a verdict rather than surveying the field.

To keep the essay from being a recap, I want it built around two puzzles the seminar kept raising, and I want you to resolve them in the course of defending your position.

First, the threshold problem. Any supposed collapse seems to consist of gradual changes — a few percent of population lost per generation, one institution quietly superseded by another — none of which is itself a collapse. If no single generation experienced a discontinuity, in what sense did a collapse occur, and is the answer anything more than a convention about where to draw a line?

Second, the insider/outsider problem. In some cases the people living through the supposed collapse seem to have carried on, reorganized, and even flourished by their own lights, while the "collapse" is visible only from the standpoint of the abandoned center, the lapsed institution, or the later narrator. Consider a society that loses its capital but keeps its language, religion, and farming, versus one that keeps its capital but loses its economy and its ability to feed itself: which, if either, has collapsed? If the discontinuity is invisible from the inside, is "collapse" an explanation of what happened or just a description of what the observer valued?

Reach a conclusion: either collapse is a real and useful category that can be given a non-arbitrary definition, or it should be retired in favor of something like reorganization or transformation. If you defend it, give the criterion and show that it survives both puzzles; if you retire it, say what we lose by doing so and why the loss is worth it.
