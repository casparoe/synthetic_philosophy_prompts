I've been rereading Gödel, Escher, Bach and I think I'm missing something about the MU puzzle, so bear with me. Hofstadter makes a big deal of the fact that you can't get from MI to MU inside the system, and that the only way to actually see why is to joot — jump out of the system — and reason about the whole thing from outside. Fine, I get that the invariant argument does the job. But doesn't the move sort of undercut itself? If jumping out of the system is always available, then any time I hit a wall like MI to MU I can just joot, and if the outside view says no, can't I joot again to a level where the answer comes out yes? Why isn't the impossibility of MU just a fact about the particular rules he happened to pick, which a bit of jootsing lets me sidestep by adopting better rules instead? And if that's right, doesn't Gödel's incompleteness theorem stop being scary — because whenever a system can't prove something that's true, we just jump out and prove it from the meta-level, then carry on as before? So is jootsing basically a get-out-of-jail-free card for formal systems, or am I conflating two different things here? I'd like to understand where the confusion is, not just be told that the MU puzzle is unsolvable.
