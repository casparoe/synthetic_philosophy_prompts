My partner wrote: "Logical positivism is the view that a statement only means something if you can test it with experience, or if it's true just because of the words in it, like in math and logic. Ayer applies this to religion in Language, Truth and Logic and says statements about God and about an afterlife fail the test. So he doesn't say 'there is an afterlife' is false. He says it's not even false, because it isn't the kind of thing that can be true or false in the first place — it's meaningless, and religious language is more like a way of expressing how you feel about the world than a bunch of claims about it. That's the part people get wrong when they call him an atheist; he's not denying the afterlife, he's saying the sentence doesn't get far enough to be denied. About survival specifically, his argument is that nobody can observe themselves being dead, and nobody else can check whether whatever shows up afterward is really still you, so there's no possible observation that would count for or against it. He does admit that in the weak sense of verification, some experiences could be relevant — like a medium telling you something only your dead uncle could have known — but that's never conclusive, so the claim stays unverifiable and therefore meaningless. Then in 1988 he had that near-death experience, where he saw a red light and some figures he took to be ministers of the church, and afterward he wrote an article saying it had slightly weakened his confidence that death would be the end of him.
