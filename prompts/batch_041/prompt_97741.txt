Construct a case that drives apart two construals of utility that agree on every choice the agent makes yet disagree about what utility is: (a) utility as a representation of choice, on which an agent whose choices over sure options and simple lotteries satisfy the standard consistency and expected-utility axioms has a cardinal utility function, unique up to positive affine transformation, that measures how much the agent prefers one option to another; and (b) utility as a measure of how well off the agent is, on which the agent's choices fix at most a ranking, and the size of any gap between options has no content beyond that ranking. Specifications: a realistic setting and modest stakes (a person weighing jobs, treatments, or relocations; no infinite or ruinous payoffs); one paragraph; the agent's choices over sure options alone must determine only an ordinal ranking, so that any cardinal difference between two options is supplied entirely by the agent's lottery choices; and the agent's considered, non-choice judgments about how much better off each option leaves them must treat two of the options as nearly equal while the fitted cardinal scale implies a large gap, with no choice axiom violated by this combination. After the scenario, state what each construal takes the agent's utility function to be and why, name the feature of the case that produces the divergence, say what becomes of the disagreement if the lottery choices are removed, and give the strongest reply available to a defender of the choice-based construal, who will want to deny that the considered judgments are evidence about utility at all. If the two construals would recommend different decisions for a third party acting on the agent's behalf, include one such decision.
