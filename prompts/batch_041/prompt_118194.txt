I'm circulating a position statement ahead of a reading group on epistemic game theory, and I'd like an audit of it before I send it out. Here is where I have landed.

(1) Aumann's agreement theorem is correct: two agents who share a prior over a common state space, are Bayesians, and have common knowledge of each other's posterior probabilities for the same event must have equal posteriors.

(2) I treat the theorem as a genuine constraint on rational disagreement rather than a curiosity about idealized agents.

(3) All the same, persistent disagreement among people who are each being reasonable is a normal and legitimate feature of inquiry, not a sign that someone is being irrational.

(4) The disagreements I actually care about are mostly disagreements about how to frame or model a question — which state space, which partition, which events count as relevant — rather than disagreements about what follows once the frame is fixed.

(5) When a disagreement is public — both parties know they disagree, and each knows the other knows, and so on — I count that as common knowledge between them.

(6) I lean toward thinking that rationality does not require the parties to share a prior; a shared prior is a modeling convenience, not a demand of coherence.

Please tell me which of these genuinely pull against each other, which only appear to, and how severe each real conflict is. I don't want to be told that it all hangs together, and I don't want every pair flagged as equally fraught: rank the genuine tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Several of these formulations may be vague enough that the tension depends on how the terms are sharpened, so for each candidate conflict say whether it survives once the terms are made precise, and tell me plainly if a formulation is too loose to assess — or if I have posed this in a way that cannot be answered well — rather than manufacturing a tension to be helpful.

End with a final line beginning 'Verdict:' that names the one commitment I should revise and the direction of the revision.
