ok so my friend and i have a long-running argument about what counts as pseudoscience and i want to nail down my own view, because i keep contradicting myself and it's getting embarassing. here are my gut calls. i'd like you to find the principle (or the small set of principles) that gets them, then tell me what it says about cases i haven't listed, and be honest about which of my calls it can't save.

my verdicts:

- astrology: pseudoscience, no question.
- fomenko's new chronology, the thing where basically all of ancient history gets redated: pseudoscience.
- ancient-aliens-built-the-pyramids stuff: pseudoscience.
- wegener's continental drift back when every geologist rejected it: NOT pseudoscience, just an idea ahead of its time.
- cold fusion: NOT pseudoscience, even though the original claim was wrong and the whole area is a mess.
- homeopathy: pseudoscience.
- string theory and the multiverse: NOT pseudoscience, even though i honestly can't see how you'd ever test them.
- a woman selling crystal healing that 'realigns your energy field' to cure cancer: pseudoscience.
- a lone crank claiming a new fundamental force, but who posts all his raw data, begs people to try to falsify him, and publicly drops the idea when they do: NOT pseudoscience, even though i think he's wrong.
- a research program that's made testable predictions for decades and blames every failed one on the 'negative intentions' of the people running the tests: pseudoscience.
- phlogiston, and the steady-state universe: dead and wrong, but NOT pseudoscience either — those were honest people doing science badly.
- the old 'scientific' racism: pseudoscience, it claimed to be science and then defended the conclusion no matter what the evidence did.
- the simulation hypothesis: genuinely torn. is it pseudoscience, or just philosophy wearing a lab coat?

two that really trip me up:

1. a group that shields a claim from all criticism, cherry-picks, shouts down skeptics, refuses to run the obvious experiment... and turns out to be right. i still want to call the belief pseudoscientific. but how can a true belief be pseudoscience? that seems impossible if pseudoscience is a fact about the content of the claim.

2. a claim that's false, unfalsifiable, and pursued by completely honest people who'd abandon it tomorrow if you gave them a reason (pick your favorite interpretation of quantum mechanics): i don't want to call that pseudoscience at all.

my tentative guess at a principle: it's not about the theory's content at all, it's about (a) whether the people holding it would actually change their minds if the evidence flipped, and (b) whether they're claiming to be doing science in the first place (so theology is out, but astrology is in). but that makes 'pseudoscience' a fact about people rather than theories, and it lets the true-but-insulated case in as pseudoscience, which feels both right and weird.
