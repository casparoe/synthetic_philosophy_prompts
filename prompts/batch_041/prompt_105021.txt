I'm a science journalist and I'm working on a long piece about how we think about moral status across the whole zoo of things — insects, fish, embryos, plants, maybe AIs, maybe thermostats. The position I've sort of talked myself into, and which I'd like you to try to knock down, is what I've been calling the dimmer-switch view. The claim is: there's one thing that matters, how much it's like something to be the entity — the richness and intensity of its experience — and moral status is just a smooth, monotone function of that one quantity. No sharp line anywhere. An insect is a low setting, a fish higher, a dog higher still, and so on up; anything with the faintest flicker counts for a little, anything with none counts for nothing. And crucially the ordering is total: for any two creatures you can in principle say which one's setting is higher.

What would count against this? I want the strongest case, not a survey of the arguement. In particular: does sentience actually come in one dimension, or is it a bundle (pain here, pleasure there, perceptual richness somewhere else) such that two creatures could be genuinely incomparible and the total ordering collapses? Is being a subject at all perhaps all-or-nothing, with degrees only in the content and not in the status? Does moral status really track sentience alone, or do agency, being alive, or belonging to a community do independent work? And is there any way to actually measure the dimmer, or have I just built something that can't be wrong?

Rank the defeaters for me, fatal down to merely awkward, and tell me which ones a defender of the view would themselves concede and which they'd wave away. If you think it's been quietly qualified into unfalsifiability, say so, and say whether that's fatal or just the price of doing business. The thing that would move me most personally is a case where our moral intuitions clearly outrun the dimmer — some creature or system we're confident matters more, or less, than its supposed setting predicts. A concrete example there would be worth more to me than any amount of theory.
