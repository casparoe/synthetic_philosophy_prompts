Heres a thing I keep going back and forth on and I'd like you to take it apart. When people say an RL agent's reward signal is its version of pleasure and pain, I can never tell which of two quite different claims they mean, and I want them compared head on. Reading one: reward is more like a thermometer — the number is supposed to track something that is actually good or bad for the system, and thats what makes it a candidate for valence at all. Reading two: reward is more like the setpoint on a thermostat — a target some designer chose, and "good for the agent" only ever means "good relative to that choice". Then here's what makes me suspicious of reading two, and I want you to tell me whether it holds up. You can scale every reward in a task by 7 and the agent learns exactly the same policy, so magnitude seems to carry no behavioral weight — yet if reward were valence, magnitude is the one thing you'd most expect to track intensity. And the features the reward is defined over are just whatever the designer decided to represent, so swap the feature basis and the same situation comes out with a different number. Also, if one agent gets +1 for stepping on a tile and an otherwise identical agent gets -1 for stepping on it, the first runs toward the tile and the second runs away, and I don't think anyone wants to say the second one's inner life is the negative of the first's. Is that a real argument or just a restatement of the fact that behavior doesn't settle valence? Where you land is up to you, but keep settled stuff and genuinely contested stuff apart, and dont hand me one side of it as if it were the consensus.
