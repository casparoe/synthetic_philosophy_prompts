Im TAing an upper-level phil of mind seminar and I have to write one essay question for the final on the Malcolm/Dennett debate about whether dreams are experiences. I don't want a question students can answer by just reciting "Malcolm says dream reports aren't reports of experiences, Dennett's cassette theory says dreams might not be experiences either." I want them to have to say what's actually at stake — is this an empirical dispute, a conceptual one, or are the two even disagreeing about the same thing? Here's the twist I want to build in: imagine future neuroscience shows that dream reports are always produced by a narrative-construction process that runs in the first few minutes after waking, not during sleep. Does that vindicate Dennett? Does it refute Malcolm? Or does it show the whole question was confused? Write the exam question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme out of 25 that seperates an A from a B by naming the specific moves each must make. Also flag where students typically go wrong.
