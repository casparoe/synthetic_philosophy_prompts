Whorf argued for the strong version, mainly on the basis of Hopi, which he said has no real way of talking about time, which is why he thought the Hopi couldn't make sense of Western physics. His other stock example was that Inuit languages have far more words for snow than English does, so their speakers see the winter differently. Later research mostly killed the idea: Berlin and Kay showed that color terms are universal, so perception isn't shaped by language after all, and Pinker pointed out that thought runs fine without language. About all that's left is that people are a little faster at matching colors when the color names are short, which nobody thinks is deep."
