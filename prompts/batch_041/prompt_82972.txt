I'm taking a seminar in philosophy of mind, and we have just finished the unit on Searle's Chinese Room argument. I have ended up rejecting his conclusion, though not for the reasons we rehearsed in class, and not quite in the form in which the systems reply is usually stated. My position is this. The person in the room does not understand the Chinese symbols he manipulates, and no proper part of the room understands them either; understanding is a property of the whole system. But that is not because the system is larger than its parts, and not because its states are "integrated" in some unspecified sense. It is because the system's internal transitions are sensitive to the content of its states across a wide range of counterfactual inputs, so that its competence is generated by recombinable constituents rather than matched case by case. On this criterion the room as described is at best a borderline case — its rulebook's coverage is stipulated rather than generated — whereas a system that satisfied the criterion would understand whatever it was made of: silicon, neurons, or a nation of people.

What I would like is an honest map of how exposed this position is, rather than a summary of the debate. In particular: which of the standard replies to the Chinese Room argument it commits me to, and whether it commits me to anything I ought to find embarrassing; what would count against it, ranked from fatal to merely awkward; whether it can survive the observation that any finite competence can be implemented by a table with the right counterfactual profile, and if it cannot, whether the repair is principled or ad hoc; whether the position merely stipulates a definition of understanding that presupposes what the argument was meant to establish, and if so whether that is a defect or a legitimate refusal to accept the argument's framing; and what it predicts about present-day large language models, with a candid judgement as to whether that prediction is a cost or a vindication. Please also tell me which of these defeaters my own defenders would concede rather than dispute, and be willing to conclude that the position is well insulated — and to say whether that insulation is a problem.
