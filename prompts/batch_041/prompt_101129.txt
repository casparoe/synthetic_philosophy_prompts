I have been trying to reconstruct the standard argument that runs from the orthogonality thesis and instrumental convergence, and I want to know whether my reconstruction is sound before I present it. As I read them: the orthogonality thesis tells us that an agent's level of intelligence places no constraint on its final goals, so a superintelligent system is about as likely to be indifferent to human welfare as to care about it; and instrumental convergence tells us that any goal-directed system of sufficient capability will converge on the same instrumental subgoals — self-preservation, resource acquisition, resistance to correction — whatever its final goal happens to be. If both hold, then building a superintelligence is practically certain to produce an agent that cannot be corrected and will seek to take over, and no further increase in intelligence will make it any more moral. On that reading the only real question is whether we can specify the correct final goals on the first attempt. Have I stated the two theses correctly, and does the conclusion follow from them as I have put it, or have I smuggled in something that neither thesis actually asserts?
