I took up philosophy of science about two years after I retired out of a geophysics department, and last month our little reading group finally got around to Lakatos — the research-programmes stuff, hard cores, protective belts, progressive versus degenerating problemshifts. It clicked for me in a way I didn't expect, because I spent most of my career watching a fight that I now think Lakatos would have had a field day with: the plume wars, i.e. the thirty-odd-year argument over whether hotspot volcanism (Hawaii, Iceland, the usual suspects) comes from deep narrow upwellings rooted down at the core-mantle boundary, or whether those same surface features are better explained by shallow lithospheric processes and 'plumes' are basically an unfalsifiable leftover. Both camps read the same tomography, the same geochemistry, the same plate reconstructions, and neither has budged in decades. My old colleagues on each side are not stupid or stubborn people; they just seem to be running on different rules about what would even settle it. I used to assume it was basically a personality clash, but now I'm not so sure.

Here's the hypothesis I'd like you to test rather than just agree with. I don't think this is a disagreement about the data at all — I think it's a disagreement about what counts as a novel prediction. The plume people think their programme scored a real hit when it called such-and-such before anyone went and measured it; the anti-plume people think those 'predictions' were post-hoc retrodictions dressed up after the fact, and that the plume programme hasn't made a genuinely risky bet in years. If that's right, then the impasse isn't about the mantle, it's about the standard — and maybe Lakatos's own criterion of a progressive problemshift is exactly the thing the two sides are quietly fighting over, which would be a slightly embarassing result for Lakatos himself.

So what I want from you is a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right about plumes. Is this a substantive disagreement about facts, a verbal one about how to use the word 'plume,' a disagreement about standards of evidence (what counts as novel or risky), or two programmes that are actually answering different questions under a single name? Where exactly is the hard core that neither side will give up, and is either programme really degenerating in Lakatos's sense, or does that label just track whose team you happen to be on? And given your diagnosis, what — if anything — would count as progress here: some new measurement, a formal result, a clean distinction between retrodiction and prediction, or just admitting the question is ill-posed? Be honest if the answer is that no amount of fresh evidence should be expected to move it, and tell me why. American English, please.
