I am preparing a position paper for a graduate seminar on bargaining theory, and before I circulate it I want an audit of whether the views I have settled on can actually be held together. Here is where I have landed.

(1) A two-person bargaining problem is given by a feasible set of utility pairs and a disagreement point. A solution rule should depend on those data alone, and should be invariant under independent positive affine transformations of the two players' utility scales.

(2) A solution rule should be weakly Pareto efficient, and symmetric: a problem that is symmetric in the two players should receive a symmetric outcome.

(3) A solution rule should satisfy independence of irrelevant alternatives in the Nash sense: if the outcome chosen for a problem lies inside a subset of the feasible set, then the rule applied to that subset, with the same disagreement point, should choose that same outcome.

(4) A solution rule should satisfy individual monotonicity: if the feasible set is enlarged so that one player's maximum attainable payoff rises while the other player's maximum does not fall, the first player's payoff under the rule should not decrease.

(5) The procedure I would actually use is this: identify the ideal point, namely each player's best feasible payoff; draw the segment from the disagreement point to that ideal point; and select the point where the segment meets the Pareto frontier. I understand this is the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution.

(6) A solution rule should be fair. I take fairness to require that players in symmetric positions receive equal gains over the disagreement point, and I take the disagreement point to be genuinely doing work: two problems that differ only in their disagreement points should generally receive different outcomes.

(7) I think utility is ordinal. The real content of a utility assignment is the ranking of outcomes, not the sizes of the intervals between them, so I am uncomfortable with axioms that treat utility differences as though they carried independent meaning.

Please audit this package. I have a nagging sense that something here cannot be right, but I cannot locate it. Tell me which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be in tension and why the appearance is misleading, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, say which commitment has to give, what the cheapest revision would be, and what that revision costs. I do not want a general survey of bargaining theory; I want a focused audit of whether this particular set of commitments hangs together. Please write your answer in American English.
