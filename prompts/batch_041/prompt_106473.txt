I was just appointed as the lay member of my hospital's critical-incident review panel, and my first assignment is to draft the consistency memo for next week's hearing. The panel's earlier findings, each framed as whether a clinician's failure to act caused the patient's death, run as follows: an on-call physician who never answered a page about a deteriorating patient he was covering was found to have caused the death; an off-duty physician who was elsewhere in the hospital and did nothing while the patient's assigned team was already managing the crash was found not to have caused it; a surgeon who had performed an uncomplicated operation and then declined to return for a complication only he was equipped to handle was found to have caused it; and a physician who noticed an alarming lab result while covering a colleague's patients but who was not the patient's clinician said nothing while the patient's own team was actively managing the case, and was found not to have caused it. The new case is a physician who was off duty, had no responsibility for the patient, and had done nothing to bring about the patient's condition, but who was the only person present capable of preventing the death and knew it; she paused to check the patient's pulse, then withdrew without intervening, and the patient died, although her intervention would probably, though not certainly, have saved him. Predict how the panel will rule, name the rule it appears to be running, set out the alternative rules that fit all four earlier findings just as well and what each predicts about this case, give me your best guess with your reasons, and identify the single further case that would tell those rules apart.
