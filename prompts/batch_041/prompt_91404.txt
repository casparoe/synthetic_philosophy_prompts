I am an undergraduate currently taking a course on consciousness and cognition, and I have been trying to get clear on the colour phi phenomenon and what it shows about the timing of experience. As I understand the phenomenon, when two differently coloured dots are flashed in quick succession, observers report a single dot that moves between the locations and changes colour somewhere in the middle, even though the second colour is not presented until the second flash. The familiar distinction is between an Orwellian reading, on which the experience is at first just the first colour and is then retrospectively revised in memory so that one recalls having seen the colour change, and a Stalinesque reading, on which the visual system waits for the second stimulus and builds a unified conscious experience with the colour change inserted at the right moment. I have come to accept the Orwellian reading, and more broadly the view that the felt timing of experience is assigned retrospectively rather than fixed when the stimulus arrives. What I would like from you is a serious account of what would count against that position. Which experimental results, psychophysical effects, or conceptual arguments should move me? I have in mind things like the flash-lag effect, the cutaneous rabbit illusion, backward masking, Libet-style timing paradigms, and no-report paradigms, but I do not want a mere inventory; I want to know which of these would be decisive against the Orwellian view, which would be merely awkward, and which the view can absorb. I also want to know what a committed Orwellian would themselves concede counts against them, as opposed to what critics say. Does the view predict anything that its Stalinesque rival does not, or is it in danger of being unfalsifiable because any apparent timing of experience can be redescribed as a memory revision? If it is unfalsifiable, is that a fatal problem or an acceptable feature of a view about the metaphysics of experience? Please discuss the defeaters in order of how serious they are, and be honest about whether the Orwellian reading has any real exposure at all. I would like the answer in British English, written as continuous prose with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of a long email or letter.
