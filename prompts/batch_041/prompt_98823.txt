Ok so here's the thing that's been bugging me. Take how we certify safety gear — smoke detectors, circuit breakers, GFCI outlets, that kind of thing. The whole regime is built around the idea that the device has a disposition: it trips when it sees overcurrent, it screams when it sees smoke. Manufacturer ships a batch to a lab, the lab applies the trigger, watches what happens, and if the thing responds it gets certified. Pass = the disposition is now attributed to the design, and by extension to every unit on the shelf.

Now watch how we actually talk about it, because I think we're running two incompatible stories at once.

Story one, the marketing/legal one: the disposition is just a standing feature of the unit, full stop, sitting there in the plastic regardless of surroundings. "This device will protect your family." It's there on Tuesday when nothing's happening, it's there whether or not anyone ever tests it, and it's there in the field even if the house burns down because the smoke never reached the sensor.

Story two, the lab one: whether the thing HAS the disposition is decided by how it behaves in a test rig, and the rig is not innocent. When a unit fails, the standard move is to blame the setup — the fixture was pre-loading the contact, the test current itself heated the element and changed what we were measuring, the bench supply had a weird transient. So we re-test with a different apparatus, it passes, and now it's "certified." The stimulus is doing double duty: it's supposed to reveal the disposition, but applying it is also perturbing the very thing we're trying to measure.

And then story three, the liability one, which flips back: when there's a real-world failure and no one got hurt, everyone says the disposition was always there, something external just got in the way — a jammed mechanism, a spider, a fuse upstream. We treat in-field non-manifestation as a blockage, basically never as evidence the disposition was absent. But in the lab, non-manifestation is treated as evidence of absence unless we can pin it on the rig. Those can't both be unqualified.
