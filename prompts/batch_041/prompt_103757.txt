Topic is mechanistic anomaly detection — the setup where you've validated some mechanism in a system (a model, a brain, a program, whatever) and now you want to catch the cases where it's producing the same-looking behavior via some different mechanism, so your validation no longer transfers. The fight I keep having is about what "same mechanism" even means, and I want teh adversarial-collaboration writeup.

Two camps. On one side, call them the realists: whether two systems run the same mechanism is a determinate fact about their internal causal/computational organization at the relevant level, and there's a privileged level — roughly the level at which the validated computation is actually implemented — so "different mechanism" is a fact of the matter and anomaly detection should be anchored to it. Same behavior from a re-implemented circuit is a different mechanism even when the outputs match. On the other side, call them the interest-relativists: "same mechanism" only ever makes sense relative to a contrast class and a level picked by whoever is doing the explaining, there's no privileged level, and a mechanism is really individuated by the capacity you invoked it to explain, so "same mechanism" just means "close enough for the question we're asking" with no deeper fact underneath.
