A colleague of mine, arguing that the Nordic welfare states are a dead end for anyone hoping to copy them, keeps returning to a single comparison. A Nordic country, she says, is like a large extended family: a small, tightly knit circle whose members already trust one another, already share a rough sense of what is owed, and already notice when someone free-rides. High taxes and generous benefits, on her telling, are simply what families do when they pool resources; they work because the family is the family, and they would come apart if you tried to run a large, diverse, low-trust society on the same plan. She offers the comparison not as decoration but as an argument: the family resemblance is meant to explain the Nordic outcome and to license the conclusion that the model is not exportable.

I would like a careful audit rather than a verdict on the politics. First, set out the mapping explicitly — a table of correspondences is welcome — saying what plays the role of the family members, the shared history, the mutual knowledge and monitoring, the norms of reciprocity, the enforcement mechanism, the exit options, and the boundary that separates insiders from outsiders, and noting which of these the analogy supplies for free and which it quietly assumes. Second, identify where the mapping breaks, separating the disanalogies that matter for her conclusion from the ones that are merely colourful. Third, say what the analogy imports that the target may not contain — in particular, whether it treats trust as a precondition that institutions merely spend or as something institutions can also generate, and whether it lets homogeneity carry the explanatory weight. Fourth, and most important, tell me whether the exportability conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the argument the whole way.

One further request. A student of mine proposes a rival comparison — the Nordic arrangement as a members' club with high dues and excludable benefits — and claims it does better on exactly the question of export. Assess that alternative against my colleague's, say which features of the target each one captures and each one distorts, and tell me which, if either, can actually support a claim about whether the model travels.
