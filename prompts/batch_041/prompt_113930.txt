ok this is a weird ask but bear with me. I'm a software engineer, never took a philosophy class in my life, I get most of my ideas from podcasts I half-hear while walking the dog. But I've been stuck on one thing for like two months and I'm starting to think the only way to get it out of my head is to have it turned into a poem.

so, the thing. Some words, mostly names — a person's name, or the name of a substance — seem to point straight at the thing itself. Not the guy who did X, not the stuff that looks like this. They just point. And because they point, they'd still point at that exact thing in a world where every description we hang on it is wrong: where the guy didn't do X, where the stuff looks nothing like it. The pointing doesn't ride on the description at all. A description is flimsy by comparison — the guy who did X would've landed on somebody else if the world had gone a little differently. I think the podcast called the name side of this a rigid designator and I'm probaly mangling it, but you get the idea.

here's the part that actually keeps me up. Sometimes we've got two names for what turns out to be one thing, and we only find that out by going and looking. Thinking harder gets you nowhere, it's news. But then everyone says: once it's news, it isn't just true, it couldn't have been otherwise. Which seems insane to me, because if I had to go look, how was it already locked? It was locked the whole time and I just found the lock. That combination shouldn't be possible and apparently it is.

So: can you write me a poem about that. 14-20 lines, no strict form required, but I want the form doing real work, not just the words. Here's the mechanism I had in mind, take it or improve on it. Pick a name and bring it back in every stanza, same words, same position if you can manage it. Then around that name let the descriptions churn — swap them, drift them, let one of them be flat out wrong about the thing. Bonus points if the wrong one is the description we originally used to point at the thing in the first place, and the name just doesn't care. The name sits there untouched while the descriptions are the ones that are up for grabs. Then one turn, somewhere late, where it lands on the reader that we found this out by looking, so from where we were standing it could've gone the other way, and yet it was locked the whole time, so it couldn't. Don't resolve it. Don't explain it. Don't split it into the part we know and the part that's real. I want to feel the pinch, not read a diagram of the pinch.

And a few constraints so it doesn't come out generic: no philosopher names anywhere in the poem, and skip the morning star / evening star thing and the water-is-H2O thing, everyone reaches for those and I've honestly read them alot. Don't end on some tidy line about how it was really about language all along. I want it accurate enough that somebody who's actually studied this reads it and goes ok, they get it — but a normal person who's never heard any of it should still walk away knowing why the name and the description aren't the same kind of animal.
