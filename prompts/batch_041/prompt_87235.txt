I'm sitting in on a seminar on the philosophy of marriage this term — I'm a Kant person myself, so I mostly go to make trouble — and the most interesting thing in the room is a colleague in the department who's been arguing about what marriage is for since before I got here. Her track record, as best I can reconstruct it from seminars and one very long argument at a party: she's all in for same-sex marriage; she thinks a couple who say they'll never have kids can obviously marry; she wasn't bothered that an eighty-something widow and widower were marrying mostly for company and to pool their pensions, and said something like, the looking-after is the point, the rest is scenery; she thinks three mutually committed adults who share a house and are raising kids together shouldn't get a licence, and when I pushed her she said the trouble is the three, not the consent; she thinks two adult siblings shouldn't be allowed to marry even with no kids, but she was clear her reason is what being raised in a family does to consent, not sex or romance; and she was unmoved by two friends who married so one of them could get a visa, though she added that her problem there was immigration law treating marriage as a test, not with the two of them.

Next week's case is a pair of men in their fifties — not a couple, no sex, no romance, but fifteen years of being each other's emergency contact, caregiver, mortgage co-signer and next of kin, and now they want the licence, mostly so the legal package attaches to what they already are. What does she say? I care much less about the yes or no than about the rule she's actually running: I can think of at least two that fit everything above and pull in opposite directions on these two, so tell me which fits her record best, what each candidate rule predicts here, and what further case would force her to choose between them. If the record genuinely underdetermines it, say that too, and tell me what she'd be buying into on either branch.

And go easy on the confident pronouncements: separate what's actually settled in this area (if anything is) from what's genuinely contested among people who work on it, and dont hand me the care-and-dependency line as though it were the consensus — I referee enough of this stuff to know better.
