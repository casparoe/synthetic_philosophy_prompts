Somethign I keep running into and dont have a word for. Someone makes a long-range prediction -- Malthus on population outrunning food, or those computer models from the early seventies that had us running out of resources -- the date comes and goes, nothing happens, and the response is never "we were wrong". It's "we were early", plus a story about why the mechanism is still there: the crash hasn't come because people saw it coming and changed what they were doing, or because the very pressure the theory posits slows down the thing that was supposed to blow up. And here's the part that really gets me. That story then gets counted as evidence *for* the theory, since the theory said the mechanism would operate and the mechanism operating is exactly what stopped the outcome. So you can't lose: the disaster arrives, the theory wins; the disaster doesn't arrive, the theory wins, because the near miss is the mechanism doing its job. I know about ad hoc rescue, and Popper on immunizing moves, and the Lakatos line about research programmes that stop producing new predictions, but none of those feels like the thing I'm describing -- this isn't patching one anomaly, it's a standing redescription of the same non-event, and the redescription is what does the confirming. Does this shape have a name? If it doesn't I'll take a coined one, but I want the edges marked: what separates it from an ordinary self-fulfilling prophecy, whether the sliding-deadline version is the same phenomenon or just a cousin, and whether a theory with this structure could ever be wrong about anything.
