I am assembling a small research group — myself, one postdoctoral fellow and one doctoral student, for three years, working on conceptual questions only, with no laboratory and no prospect of running experiments — and I have to decide what we should actually spend those years on. Our remit is narrow: whether a static artefact could be a conscious subject. The founding case is the old thought experiment in which a brain is transcribed, cell by cell and mark by mark, into an enormous book; suppose, to sharpen it, that the original brain has been destroyed and that nobody ever reads the book. The book does nothing: it does not compute, it does not change, and it is not disposed to do anything. Does it have a point of view?

I want an agenda, not a survey, and I want it defended. Tell me which of the open questions in this area are most worth our three years, and rank them. For each, I want to know why it is upstream of the others — which of the other questions would change their answers depending on how it is settled; how far it can be advanced by argument and careful reading rather than by experiment; whether it is neglected, contested or already well worked over; and what would move it up or down my list. A hard question that matters is worth more to us than an easy one that does not, but a question we cannot bring to a defensible conclusion in three years is worth less than one we can. Do not assume that functionalism is either true or false; where the ranking depends on which view of realisation one holds, say so.

I have been sorting the candidates in my own notes along a distinction I am not sure survives contact with the literature: some questions are about the vehicle (what kind of thing can bear a mental state at all) and some are about the occasion (what has to happen for a state to occur). Much of the writing I know seems to slide between the two. Here are the candidates I have so far. Tell me whether they are the right ones, which of them are really instances of one and the same question, and what I have left out — if you think I have missed something, put it in the agenda rather than commenting on it separately.

- whether a state can be realised in a medium that does no causal work, and whether the book's inertness is disqualifying;
- whether marks that are discrete and macroscopic can be isomorphic, in the way that matters, to a brain's continuous microphysical state, or whether any such correspondence is too coarse-grained;
- whether a conscious state could be instantaneous — one state, with no succession and no stream — or whether the very idea is incoherent;
- whether the book's status depends on an interpreter, that is, on there being someone disposed to read the marks as standing for something;
- whether the book would be one subject, very many, or none, given that it encodes the states of an entire life;
- whether the puzzle is about consciousness at all, or about something else — intentionality, personhood, agency — and whether running these together is what makes it feel insoluble.

Finally, say plainly if any of these is a pseudo-question, or if the whole framing is one.

Return your answer as a single JSON object and nothing else, using exactly these fields:

- "agenda": array of objects, ordered by priority (first = most worth our three years), each with:
  - "rank": integer, starting at 1;
  - "question": string, the question in one sentence;
  - "upstream_of": array of integers, the ranks of the questions in the agenda whose answers depend on this one;
  - "tractability": object with "rating" ("high", "medium" or "low") and "note" (one sentence on why, for a desk-based group);
  - "neglectedness": object with "rating" ("neglected", "contested" or "well-worked") and "note" (one sentence);
  - "why_it_matters": string, two or three sentences;
  - "what_would_move_it": string, one or two sentences on the argument or finding that would change its place in the ranking.
- "consolidations": array of objects, each with "candidates" (array of short strings identifying the candidates as I listed them) and "verdict" (one or two sentences on whether they collapse into a single question).
- "deprioritised": array of objects, each with "question" and "reason" (one or two sentences).
- "pseudo_questions": array of strings, possibly empty.
- "central_dependency": string. One sentence naming the dependency on which the rest of the agenda most depends.
- "confidence": object with "rating" ("high", "medium" or "low") and "note" (one or two sentences on what you are least sure of).
