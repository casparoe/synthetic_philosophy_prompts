Write me an essay on this. Archaeologists keep turning up hoards -- picture a bog full of bronze axes, none of them worn, several snapped in half on purpose -- and the standard move is to call it a "ritual deposit" and then infer that the people had beliefs about gods or ancestors. A colleague of mine has a rule she calls the "no idle gods" principle: you're only allowed to explain a deposit as ritual if you can say what the rite actually did -- who it bound to whom, what it marked, what changed in the world -- not merely that the stuff had no obvious practical use. She thinks most "ritual" labels are confessions of ignorance dressed up as findings. I half agree with her and half think she's overcorrecting, since deliberate breakage and non-use are themselves evidence that something non-utilitarian is going on, which is already a kind of inference to belief. So take a position: can we ever legitimately infer people's beliefs from the objects they left behind, or is "ritual" just a placeholder for "we don't know"? Weigh the main arguments on both sides and land somewhere -- don't cop out with "it depends."
