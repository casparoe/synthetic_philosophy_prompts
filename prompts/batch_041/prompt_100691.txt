ok so I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, but I've been reading around this methodological individualism / holism thing for the book I'm stuck on, and I want to lay out where I've landed and have someone tell me if the whole pile actually holds together. Here's my position, roughly:

(1) When you explain anything social — a divorce, a riot, a neighborhood going fancy — you are ultimately explaining what individual people did and thought. There's no extra ingredient in the universe. "The market did X" is always shorthand for a lot of people doing X.

(2) But some of those outcomes you can't get to from the individuals alone. Same hundred people, same beliefs, same wants, drop them into a different housing market and you get a completely different story. So the structure is doing real work, and that work isn't stored in anybody's head.

(3) As a writer I believe the moment you explain a character by her circumstances you've stopped writing a novel and started writing sociology. Characters have to be able to have done otherwise or there's no book there.

(4) My best-recieved novel has a woman in it who I know, in my bones, could not have done otherwise given where she was born and what she could afford. And I don't think that's a flaw in the book — I think it's the whole point of it.

(5) I don't believe groups have minds. "The West wants" and "the working class feels" are convenient lies; only people want and feel things.

(6) I think class is as real as a wall — it pushes people around — even though I don't believe in any group mind.

So: which of these are actually pulling against each other, which ones only look like they conflict, and how bad is each one? I'd like them ranked by severity rather than all listed as equally bad, and for each real clash, tell me what would have to give and what I'd lose by giving it. Don't just tell me everything's fine — I have a feeling at least one of these is a genuine problem and I can't see where it is, and I'd rather find out now than 200 pages in.
