I'm not a lawyer or a philosopher, I'm just someone who reads too much news and has gotten into alot of arguements about politics, and I keep catching myself holding opinions that can't all be true at once. Everyone from the EU treaties to the German constitution to the Swiss one to American states-rights people says some version of decisions should be made as close to the people as possible — I think the word I've seen for it is subsidiarity — and then they all turn out to mean completely different things by it. When my city wanted to keep cars out of the center I thought that was obviously the city's business; the people who'd have paid for it were commuters who live outside and can't vote here. When a state government did something I thought was cruel, I wanted the national government to come in and overrule it. When a country inside the EU told Brussels to shove off, I cheered, and when Brussels made a country do something it didn't want to do, I also cheered. So I'm pretty sure I'm holding four or five incompatible views at once and I want to know which one is actually mine.

Here's my best shot at naming the gut feelings I keep flipping between. One: closer is better because it's my community and we should run our own business, even when we run it badly. Two: closer is better because the people down the road know things the capital can't know no matter how clever the capital is. Three: closer is better because people want different things and one rule for everybody fits nobody. Four: the bigger level isn't there to boss the smaller one around, it's there to help it do what it can't do alone, which means the bigger level actually has a job and not just a fence around it. Five: honestly, closer to the people might be a slogan that never settles anything, and what really decides things is who holds the purse strings and who you can sue. And there's one more thing I can't shake: whether the community that gets to decide is a place or the people who are affected. A city's car ban hits commuters, a country's smokestacks hit everyone downwind. Maybe the real question isn't which level at all but who's affected.

What I want is a sorting quiz I can read down and go, that one, that's me. Five positions, and for each one, the single question or case that separates it from the view it's most easily confused with, plus its most awkward commitment. Use my cases where you can: the car ban that hits commuters, the river where the upstream state's farms do the polluting and the downstream ones have no say, the town that votes to ban something its own minority does, the rich region that resents paying for the poor one, the national government redrawing a city's boundaries or scrapping an elected local council, and whether a place gets to walk out of the bigger thing altogether. If the who's-affected thing is really just one of the five in a hat, say so in whichever item it belongs to instead of inventing a sixth.

Format: numbered list, exactly five items, a sentence or two each, no intro, no conclusion.

And keep the honesty up. Separate the parts that people who study this actually agree on from the parts they genuinely fight about, and tell me if one of my five is a view nobody who knows the topic really holds, or if two of them can't be told apart by any case at all. I'd rather have a messy list I can trust than a tidy one that's wrong.
