I have been turning over a puzzle I cannot settle by argument, and I would rather see it dramatized than answered. Picture a small, unglamorous brokerage whose whole trade is arranging swaps between people who care about completely different things — someone who would give up a great deal to end a practice most people ignore, paired with someone who would give up a great deal for a cause the first person finds merely quaint. Because the two never value the same things, there is no common currency: a swap works only if each side judges, by its own lights, that it came out ahead, and since a clean two-way exchange is rarely possible, the broker often has to assemble a long chain of people in which each person's favor is paid for by the next. What I keep tripping over is this: the swaps that would matter most always seem to require someone to do a thing they had regarded not as costly but as simply not done — off the table rather than merely expensive — and to do it as one link in a bargain, which somehow alters what the deed is. Write me a short story, told from the point of view of the broker or of one person in the chain, in which this difficulty drives the plot rather than being discussed in it, and in which no one is a villain and no one is simply correct.
