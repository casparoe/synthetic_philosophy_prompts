Okay so I've fallen down the gender-equality-paradox rabbit hole again — you know the thing, richer/more egalitarian countries somehow show *bigger* sex differences in what people pick to study and work at — and every online discussion of it turns into nature-vs-nurture name-calling within two comments, which drives me nuts. I want to turn it into a proper decision tree / taxonomy I can post on my blog and reuse for a reading group, so people get sorted by what they actually believe instead of which team they're on.

Build me the tree. Constraints: no citations, no reading list, no named-studies, assume whoever reads it can't look anything up. Every fork has to be a question someone can answer without already knowing which label it leads to, so no node like 'do you think it's nature or culture?'. I want the in-the-weeds stuff in there, specifically these distinctions:

- whether the pattern is a real cross-country fact or a measurement artifact (survey response styles in rich countries, or the whole thing being a story about *variance* rather than *means*)
- whether the egalitarianism measure is itself built partly out of the very outcomes it's supposed to predict — i.e. is the independent variable entangled with the dependent one
- whether the mechanism is interests/preferences, or constraints and opportunity structure, or something specific like how a welfare state changes the price of care work
- the sneaky point that 'preferences' and 'constraints' can be observationally equivalent unless you find a case where they pull apart (a country that got richer without its gender norms shifting, a field where pay is flat, etc.)
- whether any of this has a normative upshot for justice at all, or is just a descriptive curiosity

I want these as leaves: the greater-male-variability view, the construct-validity-artifact view, the care-work-decommodification view, the view that preferences are themselves socially shaped so the nature/culture split is a false dichotomy, and a deflationary 'this tells us nothing about justice' leaf. Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually separate.

Format it in these sections, in this order:
1. 'The trunk' — what you have to accept just to be inside the tree, ~100 words.
2. 'The forks' — each branch node as a question with its 2-3 answers, the bulk of the piece.
3. 'The leaves' — each position, one sentence on its most awkward commitment.
4. 'Empty rooms' — combinations nobody occupies, and whether that's because they're incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.
5. 'Ties' — pairs of leaves the tree can't tell apart, and what extra question would.

Keep it casual, I'm not submitting this to a journal.
