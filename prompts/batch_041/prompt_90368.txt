my metaphysics reading group is finishing the term on the identity of indiscernibles and i want to close the last session with a poem instead of a summary. the thing we kept circling is Max Black's old thought experiment: a universe containing nothing but two iron spheres, same diameter, same colour, same temperature, same stuff, some distance apart, with no third object anywhere to serve as a landmark. the Leibnizian principle says that if nothing tells the two apart then there aren't two of them, there's one; but everyone in the room felt the pull of wanting to say, look, obviously there are two. thats the tension i want the poem to sit inside, not solve.

heres the constraint, and it has to be load-bearing. write the poem as two panels, A and B, where B is built only out of the lines of A -- the same lines, rearranged (reversed is fine, or any permutation you can actually justify), same metre, same rhyme sounds, so that not one word appears in B that isn't already in A. because of that, nothing in the language of either panel tells you which panel you're reading; the only thing left to separate them is where they sit on the page -- their order, their position -- which is exactly the contested question in Black's case, whether a sphere's location is a property of it or just a relation or nothing much at all. i want a reader who covers the page with a hand and reads one panel at a time to have no way of telling A from B. then give me a short coda, four or five lines at most, where the speaker tries to say what the second panel has that the first one doesnt, reaches for the obvious answers -- it comes after, it's the one im reading now, it's the one that isnt that one -- and hears how each of those is either position again or just a roundabout way of saying it's itself. let the coda trail off rather than land.

accuracy matters here more than prettiness. someone who has actually taught this debate should be able to read the poem and see that the counting intuition, the symmetry of the whole setup (swap the two spheres and nothing in the universe changes), and the lure of impure properties, the move where you distinguish them by saying that one of them is identical to that sphere, are all doing work in it, without any of that being announced in a footnote-y way. dont come down for Leibniz and dont come down for Black; if the form is doing its job the reader should feel both pulls at once and be unable to say which panel is which. you dont need to mention me or the reading group anywhere in the poem.
