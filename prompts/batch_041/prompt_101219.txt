Write a satirical what-I-eat-in-a-day wellness post, in first person, as if Nietzsche wrote it in the late 1880s — a missing chapter for his 1888 autobiography, maybe. Around 500 words plus a headline. He reviews his own daily diet and rates each item by whether it belongs to a life that's ascending or one that's declining. The jokes have to come from getting his actual late views on diet and health right: the suspicion of alcohol, the suspicion of coffee and tea, the water-drinking, the light meals, the contempt for heavy German cooking, the idea that cuisine and climate are part of a philosopher's physiolgy, the sense that moralizing food is itself a symptom of ressentiment. The target is the modern habit of turning eating into a spiritual or moral project — and the way Nietzsche's own health regime gets turned into a lifestyle brand. Keep it deadpan and in character: no modern slang, no winking at the reader, no self-quotes, no footnotes. It should be funny to someone who's read the late Nietzsche and readable to someone who hasn't. End with a recommendation that sounds like ordinary nutrition advice but is secretly a metaphysics.
