I'm trying to get through Schopenhauers stuff on music and I keep bouncing off this passage. Here it is: “Music is as immediate an objectification and copy of the whole will as the world itself is, indeed as the Ideas are, the multiplied phenomenon of which constitutes the world of individual things. Therefore music is by no means like the other arts, namely a copy of the Ideas, but a copy of the will itself, the objectivity of which are the Ideas. For this reason the effect of music is so very much more powerful and penetrating than is that of the other arts, for these others speak only of the shadow, but music of the essence…The inexpressible depth of all music, by virtue of which it floats past us as a paradise quite familiar and yet eternally remote, and is so easy to understand and yet so inexplicable, is due to the fact that it reproduces all the emotions of our innermost being, but entirely without reality and remote from its pain.”

What I dont get is what "copy of the will itself" is supposed to mean here. If the will is blind striving, and the Ideas are supposed to be the objectifications of will, how can music copy the will directly? Doesn't music have structure, notes, rhythm, harmony—so why isn't that copying Ideas or concepts or at least forms of feeling? And what does it mean that it reproduces emotions "entirely without reality and remote from its pain"? Is he saying music gives us the shape of emotion without any actual object or stake? If so, why is that a copy of the will rather than just a formal play with affect? Can you break down the distinctions he's drawing—will vs Idea vs phenomenon, music vs the other arts—and say what work the "copy" claim is actually doing, and where it's just evocative rather than literal?
