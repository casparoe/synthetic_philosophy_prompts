Two months ago I left a philosophy department for a product team that ships a conversational assistant to the public, and the part of the system I cannot stop thinking about is not the model but the scaffolding around it. The assistant is built on what my colleagues call a two-voice design. One component drafts each reply under no ethical instruction at all; it is optimized only for fluency and helpfulness. A second, wholly separate component — the team calls it the conscience — then reads the draft and passes it, rewrites it, or blocks it, applying a written rulebook that a committee maintains and versions like software. Every block is logged and periodically audited. The company tells the public that the model is a neutral instrument and that the ethics reside in the conscience; the same company advertises that the assistant can book, purchase, and cancel on the user's behalf, and describes it as accountable when it does so.

I would like an analysis of what this practice presupposes. First, what view of moral reasoning does a separate conscience-component encode — that moral judgment is the application of codified rules to finished material, or something else? Which rival accounts, such as moral reasoning as a skill exercised within deliberation, as perception of salient features, or as a virtue of the whole agent, does the architecture quietly rule out? Second, what view of agency and responsibility does it assume? If the ethical check sits outside the process that produces the act, in what sense is the acting system the bearer of the act at all, and is that any different from a person who outsources her conscience to a device? Third, are the two halves consistent — the neutral-instrument claim and the accountable-agent claim — or does the practice need both while being able to have only one? Fourth, what is strictly presupposed versus merely well-served: could a virtue-ethical or a judgment-based design also fit this practice, with the rulebook doing no real work? Finally, if the system were rebuilt so that ethical consideration were woven into generation rather than applied afterward, which of the practice's commitments would have to be abandoned, and which would survive? I care less about which design is right than about what this one has already decided.
