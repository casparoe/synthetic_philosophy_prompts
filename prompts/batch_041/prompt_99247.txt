I am a novelist, and I want a scene I could actually stage between two characters, built to a specification. Construct a thought experiment in which two people with genuinely opposed value systems strike a compromise that is a strict improvement by each one's own lights — neither revises a value, and neither concedes that the other's values have any merit. The surplus must come from an asymmetry in what each concession costs its giver relative to what it buys the other, not from any convergence of values and not from either party's superior insight; and the arrangement must not be a midpoint or any weighted blend of their two starting positions, so that a plain split-the-difference halfway house would leave both worse off than the deal you invent. Constraints: a realistic domestic or workplace setting, no longer than a paragraph, and no deception or hidden information — each sees exactly what the other wants. After the scenario, isolate the feature that generates the surplus by showing that equalizing the two parties' relative costs makes the surplus vanish and turns the same situation into a pure zero-sum trade; then state what a critic who regards any compromise between incommensurable value systems as a sellout would say to deny that either party has truly gained. Begin with the scenario itself, with no preface, then give the analysis.
