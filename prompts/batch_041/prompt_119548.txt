(1) The three types are real, not just labels: honor culture treats worth as fragile and something you defend yourself by retaliating; dignity culture treats it as intrinsic, so slights are beneath response and disputes get handed off to impersonal institutions; victimhood culture treats it as precarious, so worth has to be claimed by publicizing grievances and getting third parties to recognize them. (2) Each one is a fairly intelligible adaptation to its conditions - honor where the state can't protect you, dignity where it can and treats everyone the same, victimhood where protection is uneven and status is contested. (4) At the same time I think cultures can be ranked by how much needless suffering they generate, and victimhood culture does badly on that metric: it turns small slights into moral emergencies and hands the accused over to authorities with no process. (5) But the dignity-culture move of just letting slights roll off only works if your standing is actually secure, and I'm not going to tell people whose standing is under real attack to grow a thicker skin.
