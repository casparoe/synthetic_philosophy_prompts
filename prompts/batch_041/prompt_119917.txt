I have been working through the Sleeping Beauty problem and keep running into a wall that I suspect is not the wall people think it is. The setup is familiar enough: a fair coin decides whether Beauty is awakened once (Heads, Monday) or twice (Tails, Monday and Tuesday); each awakening is followed by amnesia, so at any interview she cannot tell which day it is or whether she has been awakened before. The standard answers are 1/2 and 1/3, and there is a third position -- sometimes called the double-halfer view -- on which both her credence in Heads and her credence in Heads conditional on its being Monday are 1/2, which requires giving up ordinary conditionalization for context-sensitive or self-locating propositions in favor of a rule that redistributes credence across centered possibilities rather than conditionalizing. I understand the mechanics of each. What I do not understand is why the argument has not settled after decades, and I want a diagnosis rather than another defense of one answer.

My hypothesis is that the three camps are not disagreeing about the same quantity. The thirder is tracking something like the long-run proportion of awakenings on which Heads is true; the halfer is tracking something like the objective chance of the coin outcome given the total evidence, and treating self-locating information as evidentially inert; the double-halfer is tracking something like the rational betting disposition at an awakening and then denying that the proposition that it is now Monday is the sort of proposition that can be conditioned on in the usual way. If that is right, then the dispute is partly verbal -- a clash over what credence is supposed to measure -- and partly substantive, because the parties also disagree about which updating rule is correct for de se evidence, and that is a real disagreement with real consequences for decision theory.

Tell me whether that diagnosis is right, and if not, what the real structure of the disagreement is. In particular: is the disagreement best understood as (a) a verbal dispute about the referent of credence, (b) a substantive dispute about the correct dynamics of self-locating belief, (c) a dispute about which cases count as evidence of what, (d) a dispute about whether the question is well posed at all, or some combination? Support the diagnosis by engaging what each side actually says -- the indifference step, the no-new-evidence step, the betting argument, the redistribution move -- rather than by surveying the literature. Then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a distinction, a formal result, a decision-theoretic constraint that one side cannot meet, a redescribed experiment, or nothing at all. I am especially interested in whether the double-halfer position is a stable resting place or an unstable halfway house, and whether the debate would dissolve or sharpen if everyone agreed in advance on what a credence is for.
