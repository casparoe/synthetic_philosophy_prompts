I am putting together a one-year postdoctoral fellowship application and need a research proposal of about two thousand words. Here is my half-formed idea, which I would like you to turn into something a selection panel could actually assess.

The idea is this. Ordinary talk of 'heritability' conflates at least three quantities that can come apart. First, the share of phenotypic variance in a given population that is attributable to genetic variance, holding the prevailing environments fixed. Second, a counterfactual or marginal quantity: how much a trait would move under some specified environmental intervention, which can be large even when the first quantity is close to one. Third, a transmission parameter, capturing how much of a parent's trait reaches the offspring through all channels, genetic and environmental together. My suspicion is that several standard disputes — whether a high heritability estimate tells against environmental intervention, whether within-group heritability licenses any inference about between-group differences, and what, if anything, heritability adds over a plain correlation between relatives — are stalled because the participants slide among these quantities without registering the shifts. I also suspect a further and less discussed problem: the descriptive quantity may fail to be well defined once genotypes and environments are correlated, so that the variance decomposition is not unique. I want to call the resulting issue the reference-class problem for heritability, by analogy with the reference-class problem for base rates.

Assume the work is conceptual and formal rather than empirical: I will build minimal models, not gather data, so the proposal should not rest on access to datasets.
