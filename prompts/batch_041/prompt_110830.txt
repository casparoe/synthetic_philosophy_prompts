I am applying for a one-semester research leave and need a short proposal — two pages at most — for the project I would do with it, and I would like help converting a half-formed idea into something with a definite question and a realistic plan. The idea sits in the neighbourhood of imprecise probabilities, which I teach in a graduate seminar on credal states and have never been entirely easy about. The received picture is that an agent's evidence may leave a whole set of probability functions admissible — a representor — so that her credence in a proposition is an interval rather than a single number. My dissatisfaction is that the field speaks of "imprecision" as though it were one thing, when at least three quite different situations are being run together: imprecision from conflict, where the evidence positively supports two incompatible sharp hypotheses and neither prevails (two models fitting the data equally well); imprecision from sparsity, where the evidence merely leaves a parameter underdetermined, as with a sample too small to fix a rate; and imprecision from indeterminacy, where the proposition or the partition is itself vague or ill-defined (how many grains make a heap), so that there is no fact of the matter as to which sharp hypothesis the evidence bears on. I suspect the standard decision rules — take the act with the best worst case (maximin); take the acts optimal for at least one admissible probability function (E-admissibility); take the acts that no alternative beats under every admissible function (maximality) — were framed without that distinction in view, and that they are not equally well motivated across the three cases: E-admissibility strikes me as defensible where the imprecision is conflict, dubious where it is sparsity, and possibly not even well defined where it is indeterminacy. That is the thought. It has survived a year of my own attempts to kill it and one workshop's worth of unhelpful reactions, and I now need it disciplined into a proposal.

Please therefore write the proposal itself. I want the central question stated so that it has a determinate answer rather than an inviting vagueness; an account of why the existing positions leave it open, given in terms of arguments and positions rather than authors, since I shall supply the references myself; a method — what I would actually do, in what order, across a semester, and how I would tell whether the distinction is doing genuine explanatory work rather than merely decorating a familiar point about ambiguity; an explicit statement of what a result would look like and of what would count as the project's failing, including which of the three cases is likeliest to turn out to be a redescription of the others; and a scope that is honest about what fits in a single paper of roughly eight thousand words. Two constraints. First, do not let the proposal drift into a defence of imprecision against precise Bayesianism: I regard that quarrel as settled enough for present purposes and want the project to be internal to the imprecise framework. Second, do not pad it with a literature review or a reading list; every paragraph should be doing argumentative or organizational work, and I will add the citations.
