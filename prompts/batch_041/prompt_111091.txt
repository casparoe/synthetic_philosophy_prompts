I'm trying to write a short thing on Grice and implicature and I keep going back and forth on wether the central move is any good. Rough idea, in note form, then tell me what you think.

Most people read Grice's maxims as a theory of what speakers mean. I want to read them as a theory of what speakers get held to. On this reading an implicature isn't a private intention the hearer decodes, it's a kind of debt that the hearer's inference creates, and the speaker pays it whether or not they meant to incur it. I've been calling this the "on the hook" reading, though that phrase is doing a lot of work for how ugly it is.

Why I want it: Grice says implicatures are cancellable, and that's supposed to be the tell that separates them from entailments. But cancellation only works if you get there first. If I say something with an entrenched implicature, you act on it, and then I say "I didn't mean that" -- the implicature has already been banked. Late disavowal doesn't cancel the debt, it just makes me the kind of person who won't stand behind their implicatures. So cancellability looks like a property of the timing rather than of the implicature itself. That seems like it should be a bigger deal than it is.

Second thing, a case I haven't seen named so I'm going to name it badly. Borrowed implicatures: a speaker uses a form whose implicature is entrenched in their community without intending it, and the community holds them to it anyway. Someone who grew up using "some" in a way that carries the not-all reading, who isn't thinking about the not-all reading at all. Either Grice's story says they didn't implicate it, in which case most implicature talk is about the wrong thing, or it says they did, in which case meaning has drifted a long way from intending.

What I want from you: is the on-the-hook reading a real alternative reading of Grice or am I just redescribing him in worse words? Is the late-cancellation point actually a problem, or does it just fall out of something already in the framework? And if I can only keep one of the two bits, which one? Be honest about whether this is interesting or whether it's the kind of thing that seems clever at 2am.
