Write a deadpan style guide for contributors to a fictional journal, Harmonices Astrologicae, founded in the 1620s by followers of Kepler who are trying to practice his reformed astrology. The piece should be a parody of a modern academic style guide or contributor FAQ, around 700 words, with numbered sections. The humor has to come from getting Kepler's actual views on astrology right, not from generic astrology jokes. So: aspects and geometrical configurations are in; zodiac signs, houses, and most traditional rulerships are out or heavily demoted; the causal story is that the soul (or some soul-like faculty in nature) responds to harmonic proportions, much like the ear responds to music; the practitioner is supposed to be doing serious natural philosophy even while mocking the vulgar fortune-telling of the marketplace. The style guide should give concrete, absurdly specific rules: how to cast a chart without houses, what to tell a client who wants to know if they'll marry, whether weather prediction is allowed, how to phrase a prediction so it sounds like music theory rather than fate, what to do with a patron who demands a straightforward yes or no, and how to handle the fact that the founder himself kept casting nativities and advising emperors. The irony should be legible without winking: the journal's standards are so purified that the astrology becomes almost useless as prediction, yet everyone involved insists it is the only truly scientific astrology. Dont quote any real text; paraphrase if you need to reference anything. Keep it readable to someone who knows Kepler only for the planetary laws, but make it satisfying to someone who knows the astrology treatises and the harmony stuff.
