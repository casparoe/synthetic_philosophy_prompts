After thirty years in experimental physics I have retired and taken up philosophy, and Plato's Meno has stopped me cold. Socrates treats it as obvious that knowledge is worth more than a true belief that merely happens to be true, and I find I cannot say why, except by repeating him. I have therefore written out five candidate answers in my own words, and I would like you to grade them.

1. The archival account. The difference lies downstream. A true belief that falls short of knowledge must be re-tested every time it matters, because nothing about it tells me whether it is safe to bank; knowledge is a belief I may store and draw on without further checking. Its value is the value of not having to verify again.

2. The warranty account. Knowledge is a true belief carrying a guarantee that can be transferred to others. I can hand you knowledge and you may act on it; if I hold only a true belief, you must do your own checking. Knowledge is the one epistemic state that can circulate as capital.

3. The achievement account. The surplus lies not in the belief but in what it records: a competence exercised successfully, and therefore exercisable again. A lucky true belief gives me no reason to think I could get it right a second time.

4. The calibration account, the one I am inclined to favour, having spent a working life around instruments. A true belief is a reading from an instrument of unknown accuracy that happens to be right; knowledge is a reading from an instrument whose error characteristics are known. The extra value is precisely the value of knowing how far off I might be.

5. The deflationary account. There is no epistemic surplus at all. Knowledge is the honorific we attach to true beliefs we are willing to endorse in public, and the difference in value is social and psychological rather than cognitive.

Please rank all five from strongest to weakest, leading with the ranking itself in the form of a table with one row per account: rank, a score between 0 and 1 for how much of the gap between knowledge and true belief the account actually closes, and a one-sentence justification of the placement. Then, in a short paragraph, name the single account I should build a brief adult-education course around, and be candid if the one I favour does not deserve the top spot. If some account not on my list does the job better than any of these, insert it into the ranking and mark it as yours. I want your judgement on these five, not a survey of the literature.

Maximum 400 words.
