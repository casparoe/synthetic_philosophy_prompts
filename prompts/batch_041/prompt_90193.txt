So I had this long fight with a friend last night about whether an ant colony can have a mind and I want to see the two positions actually compared instead of just trading analogies. Her side is the Hofstadter one (Aunt Hillary): the colony is the thinker, individual ants are like neurons, and asking which ant is conscious is like asking which neuron in your brain is conscious, i.e. nonsense. My side: there's no colony mind, just a few hundred thousand tiny minds each doing something local, and the colony is a summary word like the traffic, handy for the beekeeper, not a subject of anything. What made it interesting is she dropped in a test I hadn't heard before: a mind has to carry a model of itself as one thing lasting through time, and that model has to be able to get itself wrong — the colony can mistake a foreign ant for a nestmate and act on it, whereas a single ant can't misidentify itself as a different ant. I said fine, but a thermostat carries a model of the room and can be wrong about the room, so having a fallible model proves nothing; she says the difference is that the model is of itself rather than of something else. So compare the two views properly for me, and then tell me whether her self-model test really splits them or whether I'm right that it just relabels the thermostat. And if the colony does have a mind, whose is it — is the colony the bearer, or is that question itself a category mistake?
