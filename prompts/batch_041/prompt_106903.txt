For the last year I've been part of a small reading group that grew out of a philosophy of science seminar and now meets weekly with two people from a machine-learning lab; our nominal subject is what happens when a system has to act on behalf of a user whose vocabulary it does not share. The case that keeps us stuck is not exotic. A model is trained on one description of the world and then asked to serve someone who carves the world up differently, and there is a gap between the two vocabularies: the human's term picks something out, the system's internal features pick things out, and nothing the system can observe from where it sits tells it which of its features, if any, answers to the human's term. What makes it worse is that the settling facts often seem to lie outside anything the system could ever get evidence about — they may be facts about the user's dispositions, about regions of the world the system has no channel to, or about how the user would have spoken in situations that never arise. So we end up with a family of puzzles: whether the mapping is even a well-defined thing to ask for; what a system should do when it knows its own ontology is underdetermined; whether failing to know which referent is meant differs from the referent being indeterminate; and how any of this bears on whether we can hand a system a goal at all. This matters to me more than academically, because I'm about to commit my postdoc to it. I can bring roughly three or four collaborators and about two years, and I would rather not spend them on the wrong half of the problem.

Given that, I want a prioritization rather than a survey. Which open questions in this cluster are most worth working on first — the ones that other questions depend on — and which are downstream, or likely to dissolve once the upstream ones are settled? Please defend the ordering explicitly: for each candidate, why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it looks to a small theory group with philosophical training rather than engineering resources, how much of the surrounding work silently assumes an answer to it, and what kind of result over the next two years would move it up or down the list. I am especially interested in your judgment about whether the deepest question here is about the structure of the world, about the limits of what a system can know about itself, or about what we are even asking for when we ask for a shared vocabulary — and in the case for treating one of those as the crux rather than as three separate literatures that happen to share a vocabulary. Where you think a line of work is a dead end or a terminological confusion rather than a real question, say so and explain what makes it one; I would rather be told that half of this dissolves than receive an even-handed catalogue. No reading list, please — I want your reasoning about where the weight sits, not pointers to who has said what.
