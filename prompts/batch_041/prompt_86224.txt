I'm about 200 pages into a novel whose main character is a type designer, and I've given her this conviction — almost a religious one — that every 'a' is the same 'a'. All the thousands of fonts out there are, in her view, just different stabs at one thing she's trying to get right. I got the idea from a line of thinking about what actually makes every 'a' an 'a', this idea that a letter's identity and its style are two seperate things you're always juggling. Seemed perfect for her.

But the more I actually sit and stare at 'a's, the less I believe it. A blackletter a, a geometric sans a, a handwritten a — different construction, different stroke count, sometimes the top of it closes over and sometimes its just a bowl with a stem hanging off the side. They share a name and honestly not much else. So here's whats worrying me: isn't the essence of the letter a just whatever a given culture happened to get used to? And if it's convention, then it's arbitrary, right — which means my character is a fanatic chasing a shape that doesn't exist, and the book is secretly a tragedy about someone who mistook habit for truth.

Am I wrong about that? I can write her either way, and I'd rather write the honest version.
