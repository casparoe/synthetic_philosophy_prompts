ok this has been bugging me for a while and I dont really have the words for it, so bear with me.

where I live, dog fighting is a felony. people go to prison for it. cockfighting too, and I think that one got banned around the same time as the crackdowns on gambling, which might be a clue. if you beat your dog in the street and somebody films it you're in serious trouble. there was a whole news thing about a guy who left his dog in a hot car.

but the chicken operation twenty minutes out of town keeps tens of thousands of birds in sheds so packed they can barely turn around, and thats just... legal. normal. nobody's getting charged. the veal calves are in crates you could not legally keep a dog in for one afternoon. and I'm fairly sure the cruelty laws actually have a carve-out in them somewhere for whatever counts as normal farming - like the animal's suffering only counts as cruelty if the practice isn't just ordinary business.

so the same physical thing, confining an animal so it cant move, is a crime when it's a dog and a line item when it's a pig.

what I cant work out is what this says we actually believe. one story is that we do think animals matter in their own right, and the farm stuff is just a blind spot, or us not wanting to pay more for eggs. the other story is darker: that the laws were never really about the animals at all but about us - we ban dogfighting because we dont want people who enjoy watching things suffer walking around among us, and nobody's character is on the line with a chicken. someone once told me that second story is a real position some famous philosopher actually held about animals, that being cruel to them is wrong only because of what it does to the person doing it. I have no idea if he was messing with me.

so what I want to know is: does having a cruelty law at all already mean we've signed up for animals counting in themselves, or can you hold the whole package - felony dogfighting, factory farms - and be completely consistent? is there any way to tell those two stories apart from the outside, or do they spit out the same laws either way? and if we do mean it, whats the honest version - what would the law have to look like then?
