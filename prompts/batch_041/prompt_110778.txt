I am preparing a graduate seminar on the practical sensitivity of knowledge, and before my students evaluate any of the accounts on offer I want a clean statement of what an account of this phenomenon would have to deliver. Write that specification: the desiderata any theory of the relation between practical stakes and knowledge must satisfy, each paired with the case or consideration that motivates it. No survey, no bibliography, no citations — I know the literature; what I need is the criteria.

Treat the following as the phenomena the theory must accommodate, and convert each into a requirement while saying what it forces:

- The basic contrast: the same person, with the same evidence and the same truth on her side, is credited with knowing when little turns on it and denied knowledge when a great deal turns on it, and competent speakers do not take themselves to be contradicting one another when they disagree about such a case.
- The third-person case, in which the attributor's stakes come apart from the subject's, and the subject's from what is objectively at stake.
- The retrospective case: we learn afterward that the stakes were enormous all along. Does the earlier denial or ascription get revised, and if knowledge is a state someone had at a time, what does it mean for the stakes of that time to be settled only later?
- Stakes the subject cannot in principle assess — an agent whose action will matter enormously but who has no way of knowing that — and the related worry that a stakes-sensitive theory makes knowledge depend on facts nobody could have known.
- The collective case: many agents, each facing stakes too small to matter to her individually, whose combined decisions produce a catastrophic outcome. Whose stakes count, and at what level of aggregation?
- The self-serving cases: an agent who tries to buy knowledge by lowering her own stakes — resolving in advance to follow a checklist, delegating the judgment to someone else, deciding not to care. Is that a legitimate route to knowledge or a loophole, and can the theory say which without making the threshold arbitrary?
- The linguistic evidence: retraction, apparent faultless disagreement, and the behavior of stakes-sensitivity inside conditionals, under negation, and in reports of what someone else knew.

Then tell me which of these are genuine desiderata and which are the commitments of a single camp dressed up as requirements; whether some subset is jointly unsatisfiable — this is the part I care most about, and I want the trade-off named rather than smoothed over; and which single requirement does the most to eliminate candidates. Finish with a few sentences scoring the four families against the list — context-sensitive "knows," stakes-sensitive knowledge, the classical stakes-insensitive account, and assessment-relative attributions — but keep the weight on the criteria, since that is where the thinking is.
