A colleague in my reading group has an argument which she claims shows that brain size is morally relevant, and I cannot decide whether to accept the conclusion or to identify the step that has gone wrong.

(1) A creature's moral status depends on the range and richness of the mental life it is capable of: its capacity for suffering, for planning, for self-awareness and so on.
(2) Those capacities are implemented in the brain, and for any given kind of creature a brain with more of the relevant neural machinery sustains more of that mental life.
(3) A brain with more of the relevant machinery is, other things equal, a larger brain.
(4) Therefore, other things equal, the creature with the larger brain has the higher moral status.
(5) Human brains differ considerably in size, and average brain size differs systematically between the sexes and between human populations.
(6) Therefore moral status varies within our own species as well, and some human beings matter more than others.

I am unwilling to accept (6), and still more unwilling to abandon (1). Please tell me which step is doing the illegitimate work, why that step looked innocent when I first read it, and what it would cost me to reject it. Answer in British English.
