Next month I have to give the after-dinner speech at the annual banquet of a big mathematics conference -- couple hundred people, mostly working mathematicians, plus a scattering of historians of math and one or two philosophers who'll be watching me like hawks. I want to build it around Hobbes's geometry: the man took up Euclid in middle age, fell for the deductive method hard, decided it was the model for absolutely everything including ethics and politics, and then spent the last twenty-odd years of his life certain he'd squared the circle, with John Wallis demolishing him in print again and again and Hobbes never conceding an inch. The mathematicians will know the Wallis half of that story; most of them wont have read a word of Hobbes; the historians will catch it if I fudge anything. What I want is something that's actually funny and warm toward the mathematicians in the room, and I want the point to land on me too, since I'm a philosopher with opinions about mathematics, i.e. Hobbes with a worse beard. What I really dont want is for it to come off as either 'philosophers should stay in their lane' (they would enjoy that far too much) or 'experts are just gatekeepers' (cheap, and false anyway, since Wallis was right). Please write the speech. Hard limit 550 words, and dont put any words in Hobbes's mouth -- no fake quotes.
