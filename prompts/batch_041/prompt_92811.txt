I'm a trustee of the small family foundation my mother ran until her death last spring, and I'm now the one who signs off on our grant decisions. A week from Monday the board votes on the chair's proposal — my older brother's — to decide one specific question by futarchy rather than by discussion: do we keep funding the job-training program we have run for nine years, or move that same money into unconditional cash grants to the same families? His mechanism is two subsidized conditional markets, run in-house on a spreadsheet with a market maker supplying liquidity, since we cannot count on real volume: one contract pays out if we adopt training and our chosen metric (share of participants in full-time work twelve months later) rises above its current baseline, the other pays out if we adopt cash grants and that same metric rises. The board would commit in advance to adopting whichever conditional price is higher, and my brother wants the commitment made before anyone sees the prices. Three of the seven trustees call this either a gimmick or a way for him to ratify an answer he already holds; I am genuinely undecided, and I have to circulate my opinion before the meeting.

Draft that written opinion. It should take up, at minimum: that the metric is itself a value choice we would be smuggling in by fiat, and whether the market can legitimately be used to settle it; whether a market with perhaps two dozen possible participants, all of whom know each other, can carry any information at all, and how much subsidy would be needed before the prices mean anything; what to do about the fact that the people best positioned to trade — our program staff and our grantees — are also the people whose salaries and stipends turn on the outcome; and what the board should do if the two prices come out within noise of each other, which is the outcome I privately expect. Give me exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, leading with a concrete recommendation and ending with the strongest consideration against it. No introduction and no conclusion.
