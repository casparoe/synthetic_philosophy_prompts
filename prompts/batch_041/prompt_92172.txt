I've been going down a rabbit hole on supertasks and I want to nail down the principle behind my gut reactions, because I suspect they dont actually hang together. A supertask, for my purposes here, is a task with infinitely many steps crammed into a finite stretch of time, so there's a moment (call it t=1) that all the steps pile up toward but never reach. Here are my verdicts on a bunch of cases. I've tried to describe each one fully so we're both picturing the same thing.

1. The lamp. A lamp starts out on. You flip the switch at t=1/2 s, again at 3/4 s, again at 7/8 s, and so on, halving the gap each time. At t=1 s, is it on or off? My gut: there is no fact of the matter. Not "we can't know" -- genuinely nothing it is. The flips never stop, and neither on nor off is the answer.

2. The rolling ball. A ball rolls from x=0 toward x=1, and the trip is chopped up so it covers half the remaining distance in the first 1/2 s, half the rest in the next 1/4 s, and so on. At t=1 s it's at x=1. Done, settled, no mystery -- even though this also involves infinitely many little steps.

3. The urn, version A. Start empty. At t=1/2 s put in balls 1-10 and take out ball 1. At t=3/4 s put in balls 11-20 and take out ball 2. And so on: each step adds ten and removes the next-numbered ball. At t=1 s the urn is empty. Ball 7 got pulled at step 7 and never came back, and the same is true of every ball. Definitely empty.

4. The urn, version B. Same deal, except each step you remove the highest-numbered ball you just dropped in (step 1 removes ball 10, step 2 removes ball 20, ...). At t=1 s there are infinitely many balls left, and I can tell you exactly which: everything except the multiples of ten. Also definite.

5. Here's the one that I think breaks my own pattern. Same lamp as case 1. I ask a different question: how long was it on, in total? The on-stretches are [0,1/2), then [3/4,7/8), then [15/16,31/32), and so on. That's 1/2 + 1/8 + 1/32 + ... = 2/3 of a second. And that feels like a hard fact about the lamp. But I just said there's no fact of the matter about whether it's on at the end. So the lamp has no final state yet a perfectly determinate total on-time. I can't tell whether I should give up one of those two verdicts.

6. The reapers. For every single instant between now and t=1 there's a reaper scheduled, and each one is instructed to kill you at his moment if you're still alive. There's no earliest such instant, so there's no first reaper. At t=1 s, are you dead? My gut: the setup itself has gone wrong. There's no coherent story where you're alive (some reaper would've got you) and none where you're dead (no reaper actually did it, since each acts only if every earlier one failed). So I don't think there's an outcome here; I think the scenario is impossible, or the reasoning that generates it is broken somewhere.

My tentative principle: an outcome is determinate just in case the sequence of finite-stage states converges. Lamp: on/off/on/off never settles, so indeterminate -- matches case 1. Ball: positions converge -- determinate, matches case 2. Urns: each individual ball's membership converges, since every ball is eventually settled one way or the other -- determinate, matches 3 and 4.

But case 5 seems to contradict the principle, since the lamp's states don't converge even though the total on-time does. So either I complicate the principle or I drop my verdict in case 5, and I don't know which. If I complicate it, the obvious fix is to apply convergence per-question: a question about a supertask is settled iff the finite-stage answers to that question converge, rather than the states themselves. Then the running on-totals converge to 2/3 even though the state never does. Does that fix quietly break something else?

Write me an essay that:

(a) states, as precisely as you can, the principle or small set of principles that actually captures cases 1-6;
(b) says which of my verdicts has to go if we keep the simple version, and which has to go if we complicate it;
(c) reaches a verdict on which version is right and defends it -- I don't want a shrug at the end;
(d) says what the surviving principle predicts for a case I haven't listed: a lamp switched at the same times as case 1, 1/2, 3/4, 7/8, ..., but which cycles through four colors in order, red, green, blue, white, red, ... . Is the color at t=1 determinate?
(e) takes a position on case 6 -- whether the reapers are a supertask with an indeterminate outcome, or not a coherent supertask at all, and which the principle says it is.

Make it an actual essay with a thesis, not a list of bullet points. And please don't hand-wave case 5; it's the whole reason I'm asking.
