I'm making a little zine of decision-theory poems for a reading group and I need one about counterfactual mugging and its meaner cousin, XOR blackmail. Heres the setup so you dont have to go look anything up. Omega flips a coin. Tails: it asks you for $100, and you either hand it over or you dont. Heads: it hands you $10,000, but only if it already predicted you would have paid in the tails branch. You are in exactly one of those branches and you never get to see the other one. The XOR version is when the whole thing is set up so that the payoff in your branch is read off your willingness to pay in the branch you're not in — the good outcome and the bad outcome are mutually exclusive, so you can never both pay and be rewarded, and from inside the heads branch it looks like you're being asked to pay for a branch that didn't happen.

I want a villanelle. The two refrains should be the two branches talking: one line from the branch where you pay, one from the branch where you're paid, and each line should sound like the thing that branch would say. The form has to do the work — the refrains keep coming back and insisting, and then in the closing quatrain they have to sit next to each other, which is exactly the moment where the decision theorist says the two branches are really one choice. Someone who knows the problem should be able to tell which refrain belongs to which branch, and should feel both pulls at once: the one that says dont pay because you're not in that branch, and the one that says the predictor already read your answer off the branch you're not in. Dont resolve it. The whole point is that the loop keeps going. 19 lines, proper villanelle, rhyme and meter can be loose but the refrains have to return word for word.
