I'm in Year 12 and I've somehow ended up running the economics society at my sixth form — we meet on Wednesdays, there's about eighteen of us, and next week it's my turn to take the whole fifty minutes. I want to do it on price controls, rent control and the minimum wage, because I've been reading about the housing situation in my city (my cousin spent four months looking for a flat and kept getting outbid by people paying over the asking rent) and every time I try to argue about it with my mates we just end up shouting past each other — one lot says "it's just supply and demand, it's definately going to backfire", the other lot says "but people need somewhere to live, that's the whole point". I don't want to give a talk, I want to set up a game or role-play where they actually feel the thing rather than hear me say it. The part I find genuinely unsettling, and the part I'd like them to trip over, is that when you cap a price the shortage doesn't disappear, it just changes who gets to decide and who quietly gets nothing — and the people who lose are the ones who aren't in the room to complain, so you can argue about it for hours without ever seeing who actually pays. I'd also quite like it if the rationing has to happen anyway somehow — a queue, or the landlord just picking whoever he fancies, or people paying something under the table — because that's the bit that feels unfair to me and I can't work out whether that's a reason against the policy or just a reason against landlords. And if you can get the minimum-wage version in there too, where the shop responds by cutting everyone's hours or working them harder rather than firing anyone outright, that would be brilliant, since that's the bit I never see anyone mention.

So please design me one session I can actually run: the rules, what materials I need to print or hand out, how the roles get assigned, a payoff or scoring scheme that works without real money (we're completely broke), a rough timeline for the fifty minutes, and then a debrief that goes back over what just happened and points at exactly where in the game each piece of the idea showed up — plus the honest bit about which part of the idea the game can't reproduce and that I'll have to just say out loud myself. Don't give me a lecture to deliver at the start; ideally they should build the thing themselves and only get the label at the end.

Please commit to one design rather than giving me three options and leaving me to pick — make the definite calls (who's in the room, who's conveniently not, what counts as winning, where the hidden margin is) and tell me why you made them.
