OK so I'm a third-year PhD student working on value theory / decision theory, and I've been voluntold to write a dialogue for a reading group thing next month. Topic is whether 'better than' is transitive, i.e. the spectrum arguments. Could you just write it for me?

Two characters, one defends transitivity, one thinks it fails. Both should be sharp. I dont want the transitivity person to be a stiff who just repeats that transitivity is a logical truth, and I dont want the other one to be a mystic about incommensurability. Assume both know the literature — just don't let either of them win by citing it.

Here's the case they're fighting over, so we're all on the same page. W1: one person suffers 50 years of intense torture. W2: two people each suffer 25 years at the same intensity. W3: four people, twelve and a half years each. And so on, halving the individual duration and doubling the headcount, until at the far end you get Wn: some astronomically large number of people, say 10^15, each with a brief, mild but genuine discomfort — a stubbed toe, that kind of thing. The intransitivity person runs this: every step looks like an improvement, since nobody suffers as much as the worst-off person in the previous world, but the far end seems worse than the start. So either one of those adjacent improvements is secretly a worsening — a sharp cutoff nobody can point to — or better-than isn't transitive. The transitivity person will have to give up one of those claims; my guess is they'll locate a cutoff somewhere, but let them choose and actually defend it.

That's just the opening though, and I don't want the whole thing to be that. What I want is the crux, so please get them to:

(1) The money pump / trading argument. Have the transitivity person spell out an actual sequence of trades or choices concretely instead of naming the argument — we're at W2, you say W3 is better, so you'd swap and even pay something to do it, then W3 for W4, and where does that leave you. Then the intransitivity person tries to wriggle out: betterness isn't preference so the pump only hits preferences; or nobody could hold all those pairwise judgements at once, so it's a merely possible trap and not an actual one. I want them to genuinely fight about whether the wriggle works.

(2) The on-a-par move. I want the intransitivity person to come up with it themselves rather than cite it — the thought that the crucial adjacent pairs aren't cases where either is better, they're on a par, so neither is an improvement. Then the transitivity person presses: if the adjacent steps aren't improvements, hasn't the spectrum argument just collapsed? And the intransitivity person should have a reply. After that, let the transitivity person float their own rescue — the cutoff is real but vague, so it's indeterminate where it falls — and the other one should push on whether vagueness helps at all or just relabels the brute fact.

(3) At least one small concrete exchange where one of them proposes a case (something little and specific, not the big spectrum) and the other says what their view says about it, and the first pushes back. I want it to read like they're actually listening to each other.

And please dont have them converge on 'well it depends what you mean by better' and shake hands. I'd rather they each land a real hit and leave one specific question unresolved. Also I should be able to tell who's talking with the name tags off — one drier and more technical, one more example-driven is fine, but both doing real philosophy.
