I'm a retired chemist — forty years on reaction kinetics, and for the last three I've been trying to teach myself philosophy, badly. I've been reading Hofstadter's GEB and got snagged on his distinction between the frame message, the outer message and the inner message: roughly, the groove on a record is only music because there's a player that knows it's a record (outer) and a set of conventions for reading the groove (frame), and the tune itself (inner) is arguably the least of the three. I've written some notes towards an essay and I'd like your comments on them.

The notes:

- The common mistake, I think, is to ask where meaning is located and then point at one thing — the groove, the ink, the neurons. But it's never in any one of the three; it lives in the whole arrangement, and above all it hangs off the frame, which isn't part of the message at all.
- My own extension, from the lab: a spectrum is only data given the frame of the instrument's calibration and the conventions for reading it. Swap the frame and the same squiggle means something else, or nothing. So there's no such thing as frame-free data — which I think has teeth against the just-look-at-the-evidence crowd.
- The bit I actually want to push: a frame can itself be read as an inner message one level up (a grammar is data to a linguist), so you get a regress, and I want to say it bottoms out not in some final, self-interpreting frame but in shared practice — in what a community of decoders actually does. Meaning, on this view, is a fact about an interface, not about an object.
- I'd also like to claim this dissolves the in-the-head-or-in-the-world question as a false dichotomy, but I can't yet seperate those two claims and suspect the second one is hand-waving.

What I'd like from you: first, have I got Hofstadter roughly right or have I traduced him; second, does the spectrum example actually do any work, or is it just the DNA example in a lab coat; and third, where's the weakest link — the regress-to-practice move or the interface claim. And an honest verdict on whether any of this is interesting, or whether I'm just restating something everyone already knows and doesnt say out loud. British English please, and don't spare my feelings.
