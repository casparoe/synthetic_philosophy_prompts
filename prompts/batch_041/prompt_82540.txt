Next month I'm giving a work-in-progress talk to my department's metaphysics reading group and I need a Q&A prep sheet. The room: two four-dimensionalists (one worm theorist, one stage theorist), someone who works on grounding and fundamentality, an Aristotelian hylomorphist who thinks form is a real principle of unity and not just a way of counting, a deflationist who thinks the whole constitution debate is verbal, and a philosopher of language who works on mass and count nouns — plus our ancient guy, who will ask how I teach the Ship of Theseus in week one.

My line is that the statue and the lump are the same portion of matter under two sortals, and that 'how many things are on the pedestal?' just has no sortal-free answer: it's true that there are two things relative to a mixed count, false relative to a single-sortal count. Nothing gets added to the clay — the second object is a count licensed by the sortal, not an entity the sortal brings into being. And I claim that's all the problem of material constitution ever was: the felt pressure to give a sortal-free count. The bit I'm nervous about is that the two sortals here dont nest — a statue isn't a kind of lump, so I can't just say the counting conventions stack.

Give me the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this crowd, ordered by how likely and how damaging, phrased the way these particular people would phrase them, each with a short reply and a note on where the reply is soft. Tell me what to concede in the first five minutes, and finish with the one question I'm hoping nobody asks. No reading lists, I know the literature.
