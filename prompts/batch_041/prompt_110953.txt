I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I am helping a small historical society design a digital archive. The society faces a recurring conflict: its oral history holds that the town was founded by a group that arrived by one route and for one reason, while the surviving shipping records, land deeds, and census entries point to a different route and a more mundane reason. Some members want the archive to present a single, corrected account; others want the oral history to stand as the authoritative account. I would like you to argue for a specific position: that the archive should maintain two distinct layers, an evidentiary layer that preserves the documentary record without alteration and a commemorative layer that presents the community's memory as its own authoritative account, and that when the two conflict, the commemorative layer should give way to the community's memory rather than to the record. Please make the strongest argument you can for that position. In doing so, address the obvious objection that this arrangement institutionalizes falsehood, and explain why the two-layer structure is not merely a pragmatic compromise but a principled division of labor between two different questions, namely what happened and who we take ourselves to be. I am not asking for citations, reading lists, or references to existing literature; I want the argument itself, developed in your own terms.
