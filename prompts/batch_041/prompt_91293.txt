I'm writing a novel and I need one scene to carry a whole argument, so I want you to draft it for me. Two teachers at a French lycée, cornered in the staffroom, going at each other over whether the school should keep making room for religious and cultural difference or insist everyone leaves it at the gate. One is a committed laïcard: the republic's neutrality, he says, is the only thing that actually protects kids from getting sorted into the community they were born into, and "recognition" is just a prettier word for locking people inside that box — the protections meant to shelter a group quietly become its cage. The other taught for years in Toronto and thinks that's a fairy tale: the supposedly neutral secular schoolroom is already somebody's culture, and making minorities file in and drop what they carry isnt neutral, it's just the majority's habits wearing a costume. Write the dialogue. Make them both sharp. The laïcard has to have a real answer to the charge that neutrality is never neutral (something about how hard the exit from community is, and what it costs the kid who can't leave), and the multiculturalist has to have a real answer to the assimilation-is-a-cage worry. No strawmen, no talking past each other — each one should pick up what the other actually said before hitting back. Keep it tight.
