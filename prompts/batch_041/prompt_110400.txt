I'm buying a house that comes with about two acres of scrubby woodland out back, and I'm trying to figure out what to do with it. My plan was to clear most of it for a lawn and a veg patch, and maybe sow a wildflower strip along the back fence. My brother-in-law has gotten really into animal welfare lately and says I've got it exactly backwards. According to him the woods are the cruelest option: they're packed with mice, voles, songbirds, insects, and almost every one of those animals ends up starving, freezing, or getting eaten alive, so a wood is basically a suffering factory. A plain mown lawn supports almost nothing, so almost nothing suffers. The wildflower strip would be the worst of all, a bug nursery. And he won't let me off the hook with "they wouldn't have existed otherwise," because he says bringing a life into the world that's likely to be mostly bad is itself the harm. My daughter has already named one of the foxes, so I'm not exactly unbiased here. I think he's nuts — I can't believe mowing down a wood counts as a kindness — but every time I try to argue back I end up sounding like I just personally like trees. What should I actually do with the land? And where does his argument go wrong, if it does?
