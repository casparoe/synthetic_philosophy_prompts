I'm preparing a seminar on personal identity and modal metaphysics, and I want to read the film Gattaca through the lens of origin essentialism — the thesis, standardly associated with Kripke's work on names and necessity, that an individual's origin is essential to it, so that you could not have come from a different sperm and egg, or more carefully, that no individual originating from a different pair of gametes would have been you. I want a reading that takes both the film and the metaphysics seriously, and that is faithful in both directions: it should not reduce the film to an illustration of the thesis, and it should not bend the thesis to fit the film.

Some framing that may help, and that I would like you to engage with rather than set aside. The society of the film appears committed to something I'll call genetic essentialism — the view that a person's identity and destiny are fixed by the sequence of their genome, hence its division of people into the genetically engineered and the rest, and hence the premise that Jerome's samples can stand in for Vincent's. But genetic essentialism and origin essentialism are different theses: one concerns what fixes a person's traits, the other concerns what fixes which individual a person is across possible worlds. The plot seems to require that identity can be borrowed, with Vincent living under Jerome's genetic profile, and the film treats that borrowed identity as a lie or a fraud rather than as a metaphysical impossibility. I would like you to ask whether the film, in doing so, dramatises a counterexample to origin essentialism or actually presupposes it; whether the swimming contests between Vincent and Anton present will overcoming genetic fortune or quietly rely on the very genetic determinism the film means to reject; and whether the ending — Jerome's death and Vincent's departure for Titan — resolves the tension or merely changes the subject. I am also interested in the film's treatment of the body as a kind of document or archive, and in what that does to the very idea of a person's origin.

In the final section I want your honest view on whether the film contributes anything to the philosophical debate that the arguments alone do not.
