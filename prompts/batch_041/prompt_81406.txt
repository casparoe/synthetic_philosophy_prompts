I am putting together the opening session of a graduate seminar on translation under formal constraint, and before we compare particular renderings I want the specification. Write the desiderata for an account of constrained translation: what any adequate account must deliver — the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it may not collapse, the facts about languages it must remain consistent with — with the consideration that motivates each requirement. The account is wanted for the practical business of defending a rendering to a reader who cannot check it against the source, so adequacy should be understood with that use in view. Keep the weight on the criteria and on the cases behind them.

Treat the following as cases the account must handle, and say what each one forces it to commit to:

- a text whose constraint is a numerical count, where the unit counted is not the same in the two languages (morae in one, syllables or stresses in the other);
- a text that avoids a common letter, where the letter avoided in the source is not the most common letter of the target, so that preserving the constraint at all requires avoiding a different letter;
- a rhyme scheme resting on key words that are rhyme-poor in the target;
- an acrostic in a writing system the target does not use;
- a poem whose constraint is visual, so that it lives in the shape on the page rather than in the language;
- a song whose constraint includes the melody, so that the rendering must be singable to the same notes;
- a text whose constraint exists only because of a grammatical accident of the source (gendered agreement, honorifics, verb-final order) and has no counterpart in the target;
- a source in which the poet appears to observe a constraint without ever signalling that it was deliberate;
- a source whose constraint was designed to be untranslatable, as a provocation to translators;
- a source whose constraint is a joke about the source language itself, so that preserving it seems to require the translator to explain it — which raises the question whether notes and apparatus count as part of the translation or as evidence that it failed.

Then identify which pairs of your requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, and why, and which single requirement does the most to rule out the familiar positions: that the form is part of the sense and must be reproduced; that the sense is what matters and the form may be sacrificed; that a rendering should reproduce the effect the original had on its own readers.

Two further things I want from the list. First, a principled way of sorting constraints into those the account must treat as part of the text's identity and those it may treat as optional, or an argument that no such sorting is available in general; if the latter, state it as a result about the list rather than as a hedge. Second, a verdict on a conjecture of mine: that fidelity is zero-sum, so that any gain in formal fidelity must be paid for in semantic fidelity, and any account that promises both is covertly trading one for the other. If the conjecture turns out to be an artifact of a bad requirement, say which requirement produced it.
