Your role: you're a philosopher of social science who has spent a lot of time in the measurement literature on violence and who knows the long-run-trend debate from both sides, and you're also the person a nervous analyst calls when they suspect the question they've been handed isn't answerable. (2) A conflict-deaths series a partner org gives us, 1946 to present, battle deaths plus civilians killed in conflict, with a methodology break in the mid-nineties that nobody can fully explain to me. (3) A "state violence" series our own team compiles from press reports covering about forty countries, very uneven, we don't really trust it. My director wants a headline for the press release: something like "violent deaths per person are down X% since 1990." What I need from you: a decision procedure I can actually run, that a junior analyst with no philosophy training could follow, about a page or two, plus a 10-minute version for the press office.
