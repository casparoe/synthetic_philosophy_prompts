I've got into forecasting lately — I keep a spreadsheet of my probabilities on everything now, elections, whether my sister's going to move back home, rain, all of it — and last week I got into it with my flatmate Dan, who's one of those people who insists he doesn't "do" probabilities, he has hunches. I pinned him down anyway and he said 30% chance of rain tomorrow, 60% chance of no rain. So I got him to sell me a ticket paying £1 if it rains for 30p, and another paying £1 if it doesn't for 60p. One of the two has to pay out, I paid 90p for the pair, so he's down 10p whichever way the weather goes — and that's using his own numbers, not mine. He shrugged and said he wouldn't actually take those bets, so who cares.

That bugged me and I've now talked myself into a position I want you to attack. The position: what makes a set of credences rational, fundamentally, is that they can't be Dutch booked. That's the whole content of the demand that credences obey the probability axioms and update by conditionalizing — not a useful idealization, not one argument among several, the criterion.

So what would count against it? Really count, not the stuff that gets recited. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones a defender of the position would themselves concede rather than brush off. Things I specifically want handled: does the argument secretly assume what it's proving, given that it needs me to already be an expected-utility maximiser who takes every favourable bet? Is Dan's "I just wouldn't bet" a real escape hatch, or does it just show he has no credences to be irrational about? The money thing bugs me too — a sure loss of pounds isn't a sure loss of utility if you're skint, and if you patch that by betting in utilities instead, is it still an argument about belief or has it become one about preference? Same question for the versions that run across time, where the trick seems to need me to forget the earlier bet. And a friend of mine says her credences aren't precise numbers at all, they're ranges — does my position commit me to calling her irrational? I'm not sure I want to say that.

I don't need a reading list or who said what first, I want the map of what this thing is exposed to, and whether it's been quietly qualified into something that can't be wrong anymore. If it has, say so plainly, and tell me whether that's actually a problem for a claim like this or just the price of doing business.
