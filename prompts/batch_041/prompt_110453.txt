ok so i'm running a reading group on kripkenstein's rule-following puzzle — the bit where you ask what makes it true that i meant plus rather than some weird function that agrees with plus on every sum i've ever actually done and then goes haywire after, say, 57 — and the last session was a mess because everyone kept talking past each other. so instead of another arguement i want a decision tree i can hand out next week.

Set the root at the point everyone in the room already grants: nothing about my past behaviour, my inner experiences, or my training settles what i meant, so you can't find the fact that pins it down. From there, each branch node should be a question someone can answer without already knowing which label they're heading for. The splits that matter for us are: whether there really is no fact of the matter about what i meant, or only no fact of the kind the sceptic went looking for; whether correctness is fixed by what the community would agree to or by what's independently right and merely tracked by agreement; whether the deliverable is a theory of what meaning consists in or a redescription of what we're up to when we say someone means something; and whether the paradox goes through for basic logical moves and not just arithmetic — if it does, tell me which leaves survive that.

Use these cases as nodes, since they're the ones that definately split us up: a person who coins a rule for her own private notation and dies before ever applying it again; a community that unanimously agrees how to go on and is, by some standard none of them can reach, wrong; a machine we'd all say is computing a function but which has no community behind it; and someone who is right now on their very first occasion of going past the cases they've already handled.

At least these leaves need to show up somewhere: meaning facts that are real but not reducible to how we're disposed to go on; the view that there are no meaning facts and that saying someone means plus just reports what we're licensed to say given that we all go on alike; the view that the question was confused from the start; and the error-theoretic option where every ascription of meaning is literally false. Otherwise, place positions wherever they fall in the space rather than working from a list of names.

Two things i care about. First, mark the combinations of answers that nobody occupies, and say briefly whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Second, if two of your leaves make all the same predictions about every case, say so and tell me what could ever seperate them — i'd rather the tree admit the gap than paper over it. Each leaf gets a name and one sentence on the commitment that's hardest to live with. Keep it to eight leaves or fewer.
