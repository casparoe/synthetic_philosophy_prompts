Retired chemist here — 35 years in a lab, and since I packed it in I've been reading philosophy in the evenings, probably getting in over my head.

There's a thing I keep running into and I don't have a word for it. I've taken to asking the chat models to lay out the arguments on both sides of whatever I'm chewing on — whether species are real kinds or just useful filing, whether the arrow of time is fundamental or emergent, that sort of thing. And the answers are good. Genuinely good, better than I'd get from most of my old colleagues over a beer. Each side gets put in its strongest form, the objections are the real objections, nobody gets strawmanned. And yet every single time I finish reading, I find I've come down on one side, and I can't tell whether that's because that side is right or because the whole thing was arranged — where the framing put the seam, which example came first, which objection got answered head-on and which got politely noted and moved past — to land me there. So I do the obvious thing: I ask, alright, but which of those arguments is actually the stronger one? And I get another fluent, confident answer. And that answer is also arranged. There's no step where I climb outside the thing to check it. The checking is done by the thing I'm trying to check.

What bothers me isn't that it might be lying or biased — I more or less know how to watch for that, it's ordinary. It's that my feeling of having checked seems to be produced by the same process I'm trying to check. And I notice I don't get this feeling from a dull system, or from a book, even a very persuasive book, because those can't watch me read and adjust. So the adaptiveness seems to be the thing doing the work, and I don't know what to call the spot I'm in.

I read something once about people who simply give up on evaluating arguements in fields where they can't tell good from bad — but that's almost the reverse of this. There the arguments are beyond you. Here they're perfectly within reach, they're just also built, and built for you specifically.

So: does this exact predicament have an established name? And I mean the specific thing, not "AI might be persuasive" or "testimony is tricky" in general. If there's a name, tell me how it's carved — what it is and what it isn't, how it differs from just trusting an expert, from the old problem of other minds, from worrying your interlocutor is insincere, from the plain fact that all of us lean on what other people tell us. If there's no name, give me one, and define it, with edges: does it apply to a very skilled human persuader working on me in person? Does it apply to a fixed text that happens to hit me just right? Where does it start and where does it stop? I don't want a reading list — I can't look anything up anyway — I want to know what the thing is and whether the field has a word for it.
