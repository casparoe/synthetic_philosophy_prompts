My friend Priya is writing a thesis on pleasure and shes got very firm opinions about which experiences count as pleasures. Her calls so far: savouring a ripe peach on a hot day — pleasure. The moment a bad headache finally lifts — pleasure. The little aha when a crossword clue clicks, which she insists involves no bodily sensation at all — pleasure. Finally landing the job shed wanted for years, which left her completely flat — not a pleasure; she says wanting a thing and getting it just isnt the same as pleasure. Crying-laughing at a genuinely stupid joke — pleasure. Scratching a mosquito bite till it bleeds — pleasure, though she calls it "a bad pleasure." A dream she once had in which she was happy — not a pleasure, because, she says, she wasnt there for it.

She also gets weirdly annoyed when I ask what the peach and the crossword have in common. She says theyre pleasures "in totally different ways" and then insists theres obviously one thing, pleasure, that they both are.

Now I've got a new case for her. For about a year Ive been getting this thing — maybe once a week, lasts five minutes, a warm fuzzy glow sort of behind my eyes. I like it while its happening; its genuinely nice. But I could not care less whether it ever happens again. I dont look forward to it, I wouldnt take a pill to make it more frequent, and if it stopped forever I wouldnt miss it for a second.

Do you think shell say the glow is a pleasure? I care less about the yes or no than about the rule shes actually running — I think theres more than one rule that fits everything shes said so far, and I want to know what each of them predicts about my glow, which one youd bet on and why, and what case I should put to her next to actually tell them apart. And separately: does anything in her list already give away whether she thinks all pleasures have to share some single feature, or is she quietly contradicting herself there?
