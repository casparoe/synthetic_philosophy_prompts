A close friend of mine, Nadia, is finishing a review chapter on cognitive penetration and has spent the past few months working through the experimental literature, classifying each finding as either a genuine case of cognitive penetration or not. She keeps a running list, and I have it in front of me. She counts the memory color effect, in which a grey banana shape looks yellower to subjects who know that it is a banana, as penetration. She counts the ambiguous-figure result, in which being told that a line drawing depicts a face rather than a meaningless glyph shifts the organization you see, as penetration. She counts a study in which subjects who are told that a surface is polished granite rather than dull slate report it as glossier. She does not count the Müller-Lyer illusion, on the ground that the arrowheads do their work at the sensory end. She does not count the motion aftereffect. And she does not count a study in which a faint tone played in the background makes a stationary dot appear to drift. I have been trying to reverse-engineer her standard, partly because I suspect she is applying one without ever having stated it, and partly because her record looks to me to be compatible with more than one.

Here is the case she has to classify next, and I would like your prediction before she does it. In the new study, a brief arrow flashes to one side of a display; two physically identical grey patches then appear, one on the cued side and one on the uncued side; subjects reliably report the cued patch as lighter. The effect survives a matching procedure designed to control for response bias, the arrow carries no information at all about the patches, and subjects are told in advance that it is irrelevant to the task, so whatever is happening seems to be a matter of exogenous attention rather than of any belief or expectation about how the patches look.

I am less interested in a bare verdict than in the machinery behind it. Tell me what she will say, and then lay out the two or three most plausible rules that reproduce every one of her earlier calls equally well, what each of those rules predicts about the arrow study, which rule you think she is actually running and why, and which further experiment she should classify next if we want to force the remaining candidates apart. If you think one of the candidate rules fits her record only on a strained reading of one of the cases she has already ruled on, say which case and why. And if the honest answer is that her past calls genuinely underdetermine the new one, tell me what she would be committing herself to, in each direction, by the call she makes.
