So I've just got back from my mate Tom's birthday drinks where he and I spent a solid two hours going at each other about uploading, and now I want a joke out of it, mainly because I'm still annoyed and mainly because he thinks he won. Here's the shape of the arguement so you can see what I'm after. Tom thinks a destructive scan-and-copy kills you — the thing that wakes up in the machine is a copy, he's perfectly happy to say so, he'll say it loudly. But he also thinks that if a surgeon swapped out your neurons one at a time for functionally identical silicon chips, with you awake and chatting through the whole procedure, then you'd survive it and walk out the same person with different hardware. I said fine, so you've got a scheduling view of death: the only differences between the two procedures are how fast the replacement goes and whether the original is destroyed before or after the new one is running. He says no, it's about continuity of the process, and he's got this line he keeps repeating, he calls it the loyalty card — if you survive 1% replacement then by induction you survive 100%, and if you don't survive 1% then you don't survive anything, so either nobody ever survives anything or everybody survives everything. Which I think is a dodge, because the case that actually made him go quiet was chunking: swap 1% at a time and he's relaxed, swap a whole hemisphere in one go and he starts fiddling with his glass, and there's no principled place to put the line. There was also a bloke at the end of the table who kept saying it's all just atoms anyway, which didn't help.

What I want is a joke about this, and it needs to land for someone who actually knows the uploading and gradual-replacement debate — no generic nerd gag about teleporters being weird, no Ship of Theseus with neurons, that one's been done to death. The punchline has to turn on something real in the positions: the induction, or the chunking problem, or the fact that the two procedures leave behind quite different people as far as the paperwork is concerned, or whatever hinge you think is the sharpest one. Don't name any philosophers and don't give me a reading list, I don't care who said what, I just want the joke to work standing on its own. Set it anywhere you like — a clinic, a call centre, an insurance claim, a wedding speech — and write it in British English. After the joke, give me a short JSON object with these fields: "joke" (the whole thing as text, setup and punchline together), "setup", "punchline", "the_claim_it_turns_on" (the actual philosophical point that has to be true for the punchline to work), "whose_view_it_flatters", and "who_wont_laugh_and_why" (the position that would find it unfunny or incoherent, and what they'd say back). Keep the whole JSON under 250 words.
