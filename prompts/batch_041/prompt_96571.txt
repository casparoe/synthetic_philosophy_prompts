So I stopped eating meat about four months ago — mostly guilt about factory farms — and I felt settled about it until two people started poking holes and now I'm stuck at the supermarket every week. My flatmate says the animals I'm sparing by skipping chicken are chickens, and I'm eating lentils instead, and lentils come off fields that get mowed, sprayed and harvested, which must kill a huge number of small animals per calorie, so on his arithmetic I might be responsible for more deaths now than before. My cousin then says the real thing everyone forgets is that the vast majority of conscious animals on earth are wild — insects, fish, birds, rodents — and if those lives are mostly short and nasty, then nearly all the suffering in the world is out in nature, not on farms, so whatever I buy is a rounding error, and in some cases farmland might even hold less suffering than the same land left to go wild. He's got this rule he calls pain-years per acre, where for each option you count how much land it takes and roughly how much suffering lives on that land. So: grass-fed beef from the farm up the road (they say it's poor hillside, can't be cropped), supermarket chicken, or lentils. Am I overthinking this? Whose argument is better, and is there a principled way to decide, or does it just come down to numbers nobody actually has?
