Ok so I've been convinced for a while now by that four-rung ladder of minds you get from Dennett -- Darwinian creatures that are just wired up and can't really learn, Skinnerian ones that learn by trial and error and reinforcement, Popperian ones that can run a simulation inside themselves and let the hypotheses die instead of them, and Gregorian ones whose minds are partly made out of external tools and words they've soaked up from the culture around them. I think this is a real, informative carving of the space and not just a tidy story: the rungs are genuinely distinct, they stack (a Gregorian creature is also Popperian, Skinnerian, Darwinian), and each rung buys you a new kind of thing a mind can do that the rung below couldn't.

I want you to try to break it. What would count against this framework being a real distinction rather than a neat post-hoc ladder? I'm after the actual defeaters -- the empirical cases, the arguements, the results from comparative cognition or learning theory or wherever -- sorted by how much they'd actually cost me, from fatal down to merely awkward. Be specific about which ones a fan of the ladder should concede really do count against it, and which ones they'd reasonably wave away.

A few things I keep worrying about, take these seriously rather than skipping past them. Creatures or systems that seem to sit at two rungs at once, or to skip one -- is there a plausible case of something that offloads onto external tools, so it looks Gregorian, but never does the offline simulation that's supposed to be the Popperian step? Or something that's Popperian in one narrow domain and purely Skinnerian everywhere else? Also whether 'Gregorian' is doing any independent work or just means 'Popperian, plus it uses a notepad.' And whether the ladder smuggles in a progressive higher/lower reading that plenty of people would rightly resist -- is there a normative or teleological slant hiding in the ordering, and does the framework survive once you strip that out? Personally I think a clean empirical case of a rung-skipper would worry me most, but tell me if I'm weighting that wrong.

And be honest about the insulation worry: could a fan keep the ladder come what may, by redefining which rung a creature is on whenever an awkward case shows up? If so, is that fatal for a framework like this, or just the normal cost of a coarse-grained taxonomy?

Tell me where the real disagreements actually are among people who work on this stuff, and don't dress up a contested call as if everyone agrees. I don't need citations or reading lists, just the reasoning. End with a last line that starts 'Verdict:' and names the single defeater you'd say does the most damage (or 'none' if you think it comes through clean).
