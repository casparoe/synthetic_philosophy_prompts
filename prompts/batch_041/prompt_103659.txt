Quick refresher so we're on the same page, since I'm sure you don't need it spelled out but I want to pin down the version I mean: two people are splitting some surplus, there's a feasible set of agreements each giving the two of them some utility, there's a disagreement point they fall back on if they can't agree, and the set is convex because you can randomize between deals. Nash picks the point that maximizes the product of the two players' gains over the disagreement point. Kalai-Smorodinsky instead looks at the ideal point, where each player gets the best payoff they could get from any feasible deal, draws a straight line from the disagreement point to that ideal point, and takes the point where the line hits the frontier, so it equalizes how much of their own maximum possible gain each player is giving up. Both of them are Pareto efficient, symmetric between the players, and unaffected by rescaling someones utility. Where they split is that Nash also satisfies independence of irrelevant alternatives, meaning that if the feasible set gets bigger but the solution still lies in the old set, it doesn't move, and KS throws that out and takes on some monotonicity condition instead, roughly that if the feasible set changes so that at every level of what one player could demand the other could get at least as much, then that other player shouldn't end up worse off.
