Something's been bugging me for a while and I'd like to think it through with you.

I'm in a small reading group — a couple of historians, an epidemiologist, and one philosopher who barely talks — and we keep having the same fight about how much of the big shape of history is down to non-human stuff: pathogens, climate, volcanic cooling, that whole family of causes. There's an "environment did it" camp and a "no, it's contingency and politics" camp, and they've been circling each other for decades. They read the same books. They agree about the underlying facts — the epidemics happened, the harvests failed, the volcanoes really did put a dent in global temperatures. And nobody moves an inch. I've thought about this alot.

Here's my hypothesis, and I'd like you to tell me if it's wrong. I dont think they're disagreeing about the facts, or even about how much weight each factor carries. I think they're answering different questions, and the difference is the contrast class. The determinist asks something like "why did this part of the world end up in this relation to that one, rather than the reverse?" — and given what you're holding fixed, the answer is going to be environmental. The contingency person asks "why did it happen this year and not next, here and not somewhere else?" — and the answer is a decision, a battle, a dead king, a rumor. If causes are always relative to a contrast — X didn't cause Y, full stop; X rather than Z caused Y rather than W — then the two camps aren't contradicting each other at all and the whole fight is an illusion.

I don't buy it, though, at least not all the way, because the thing has real heat, and heat usually means something's actually at stake. Also one of the historians keeps objecting to calling germs or volcanoes "actors" or "agents" in these stories, which the others treat as a harmless figure of speech, and I suspect that squabble is the same disagreement wearing a different hat.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, really? Is my contrast-class story the whole diagnosis, part of it, or off? If there's something left over, what is it — a fight about what counts as an explanation of a particular historical event (can a slow background condition explain a battle, or only set its stage)? A fight about whether historical causes are events or standing conditions? Or is it a moral and political fight in disguise, where people suspect that "the environment did it" is a way of letting the conquerors off the hook, and the methodology is downstream of that? And if your honest answer is "you're all just arguing about magnitudes and it's an empirical question", say so and defend it.

Skip the survey of who said what — I know the names. I want the diagnosis, and then I want you to tell me what would change your mind: which argument or consideration would flip your verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to be weakest. And if your diagnosis is right, is there anything that would actually move this thing — a distinction, a bit of evidence, a different question — or is it one of those fights that just goes on forever?
