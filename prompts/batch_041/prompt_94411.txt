Background: we've been going through Wittgenstein's On Certainty notes, and the prof ended the session by saying that maybe the claim that Moore has two hands isn't something Moore *knows* — it's something that just stands fast for him, a hinge, not a claim anyone could doubt. Devi and Marcus took it from there, and I'm reporting it as best I can.

(1) Devi: if it can't be doubted at all, that's the strongest status anything could possibly have. Calling it not-knowledge takes the most certain things we've got and files them somewhere *below* knowledge. That's backwards.

(2) Marcus: it isn't weaker, it's a different job. Knowledge claims are the ones you can give reasons for and check. Hinges are what you check with. A hinge isn't a badly supported belief, it isn't a beleif doing that job at all.

(3) Devi: then how do you tell a hinge from something someone just refuses to question? If you pick hinges out by seeing what a community won't doubt, then whatever a group treats as bedrock counts, superstition included. Hinge turns into a sociological label with no teeth, and you lose the ability to say the conspiracy theorist is doing anything wrong.

(4) Marcus: not every unshakable belief is a hinge. A hinge is one that's *needed* — doubt it and inquiry itself stops, not just this or that claim. Refusing to question something you could doubt and still do everything else is just dogmatism.

(5) Devi: I don't buy the needed test. Take a ward. One nurse is certain patients report their pain honestly; another is certain they exaggerate to get meds. Neither doubt shuts down nursing — they just do the job differently, and both look as bedrock-from-the-inside as Moore's hands. Your test doesn't seperate them. And when it does seperate something, it does it by the standards of the practice we're already in, which is exactly the circularity I just pointed at.

(6) Marcus: someone who actually loses one of these — can't trust their own memory, isn't sure the people around them are real — usually can't do anything at all. That's not a mistake, it's a collapse. But can't-function doesn't tell us they're wrong, only that they can't run our practices. A hinge feels like bedrock from the inside either way, so the feeling can't be the test.
