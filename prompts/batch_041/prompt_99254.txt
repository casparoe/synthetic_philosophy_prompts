- 6'4": definately tall.
- 6'3": definately tall.
- 5'2": definately not tall.
- 5'3": definately not tall.
- 5'10": she says "there's no fact of the matter here, it just isn't true or false whether he's tall."
- 5'11": same deal.
- For any two men an inch apart, if the shorter one is definately tall she insists the taller one is too, and she refuses to ever name the inch where tallness "starts" -- she says the question is malformed.

Next week she's grading a problem set and I want to bet a beer on what she'll write for two new items.

Item A: the same 5'10" guy, but the question is "compared to a room full of jockeys, is he tall?" and then "compared to a room full of NBA centers, is he tall?"

Item B: "Either the 5'10" man is tall or he is not tall. True, false, or neither?"

Predict her verdict on each. Then tell me which theory she's implicitly running -- epistemicism, supervaluationism, contextualism, or a degree/fuzzy view -- which of the other theories fit her notebook just as well, what each of them predicts for A and B, which one I should actually bet on, and the single further case that would most cleanly separate the two she's torn between. I care more about the reasoning than the verdicts.
