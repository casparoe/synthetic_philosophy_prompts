Write a mock press release, about 650 words, in the flat institutional register of a learned society's announcement, issued by a fictional academy that has undertaken to end the mathematical disputes of Thomas Hobbes by administrative means. Get the substance right and let the humour arise from it. The background you must build on: Hobbes held that reasoning is a kind of computation performed upon names, that definitions are stipulations we lay down rather than discoveries we make, and that geometry is demonstrable precisely because we draw its figures ourselves, so that its first truths are of our own making; he admired geometry as the one science that had escaped controversy and proposed to model civil philosophy on it; and from the mid-1650s into the 1670s he insisted he had squared the circle, defending his attempts against the refutations of the Oxford mathematician John Wallis. The academy in the piece should proceed on the assumption that mathematical dissent is a failure of definitional discipline rather than a disagreement about anything, and should accordingly promulgate a schedule of governing definitions, a commission empowered to fix them, and a procedure for reopening cases when the mathematicians remain unconvinced, reissuing its ruling each time. The satire should land its real target without ever stating it: that stipulating definitions cannot make a false construction true, because the necessity of the conclusion is not ours to decree. Keep the voice deadpan and the piece legible to a reader who knows nothing of the controversy; do not quote Hobbes, and close with a paragraph of meeting logistics.
