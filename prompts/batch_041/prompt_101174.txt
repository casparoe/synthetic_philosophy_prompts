Ok, I've been going down a rabbit hole on this idea that consciousness might just be along for the ride — like a steam whistle on a train, making noise but not pulling anything. What I keep getting stuck on is this: if my experience of redness doesnt do any work, then how do I end up believing I have it, talking about it, remembering it? My words about red have to come from somewhere.

Write a dialogue between two people arguing about this. One thinks experience is a causally inert byproduct (the whistle), the other thinks that view eats itself — if experience can't cause anything, then it can't cause you to believe you have it or to say so, so the very claim that experience is inert has nothing backing it. Make both of them sharp; neither should be a punching bag. Give the byproduct person a real move instead of having them fold: say that the same brain state that produces the experience also, on its own, produces the belief and the words, so the two run in parallel and the belief is reliably about the experience even though the experience never caused it. Then have the other one push hard on whether a belief can really be *about* something under that arrangement or whether it's just a lucky coincidence, and on whether the byproduct person could ever know what they're even claiming. Let them actually go back and forth and answer each other's points, and get into whether being about something requires being caused by it, and what knowing your own experience would even mean if the experience is doing nothing.
