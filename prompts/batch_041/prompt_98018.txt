Write me a satirical op-ed, about 600 words, for a small-town paper. The columnist is a slightly pompous retired schoolteacher who has read Rousseau's letter to d'Alembert about the theatre and taken it entirely to heart, and she is dead set against her town's plan to build a new "entertainment district" anchored by a multiplex and a streaming-funded concert hall.

The funny has to come from her getting Rousseau's actual case right, not from inventing stuff, so keep every argument she makes one Rousseau really makes in that letter, just dragged into the present. The real moves, roughly: that a theatre is a luxury that flatters and corrupts a simple, hardworking community; that actors are professional counterfeiters of feeling, paid to fake what they dont feel, which makes them a suspect influence; that the audience goes to see and be seen, feeding vanity instead of virtue; that a small town cant sustain such a thing without ruin and without being distracted from its work; and that the genuine alternative isnt more spectacle but open-air public festivals where everybody is a participant rather than a spectator. She can be ridiculous, but she must not be wrong about what Rousseau actually said, and she should never wink at the reader or admit she's being extreme -- play it straight, like a real letter to the editor from someone who means every single word.

Finish with her own modest counter-proposal for the town square. Dont name any living people.
