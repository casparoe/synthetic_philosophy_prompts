ok so i just burned an entire evening arguing with a friend about whether philosophy does anything for science and i need an actual referee, not more vibes. here's the thing. his whole case rests on one analogy that he is extremely pleased with: philosophy of science is to scientists what ornithology is to birds. he thinks some physicist said it (feynman? weinberg? i honestly can't remember which and he wouldn't tell me where he got it). what he takes from it is: philosophers just stand at a distance and describe what scientists are already doing, they can't tell them anything they don't know, and therefore the whole field is a luxury at best. the conclusion he wants to land is that philosophy of science has no bearing on how science actually gets done, so nobody should be pretending it does.

i want you to treat that as an argument and audit it, not just tell me both sides have merit.

first give me the correspondence table, explicitly: what plays the role of the birds, the ornithologists, the binoculars, the field guide, the birders, the funding agencies, whatever else you think is load-bearing. then the places the mapping breaks. then tell me which of those breaks actually matter for what he's using it to prove, and which are just cute and can be set aside.

some stuff that came up, since this is where the analogy got squeezed:

- he says every case study philosophers trot out is either fake or stolen. examples we fought about: the p-value mess and journals changing their policies, which he says was statisticians; the fight over the race correction in the kidney function estimates and whether the variable even refers to anything real; the brain death criteria, which as far as i can tell was doctors and philosophers jointly inventing a definition because the law needed one; whether hybrids count as protected under endangered species law; causal diagrams in epidemiology, which he dismisses because the guy who made them is "basically a scientist with a philosophy hobby". sort those into ones that are really philosophy doing work, ones that are science doing work with a philosophical coat of paint, and ones that don't settle anything either way.
