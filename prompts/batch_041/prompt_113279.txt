Write a pantoum in which Achilles and the Tortoise reenact the regress from Lewis Carroll's dialogue "What the Tortoise Said to Achilles." Give the Tortoise the first and third line of every quatrain and Achilles the second and fourth, so that the form's rule -- lines two and four returning as lines one and three of the next stanza -- does the structural work: each conditional Achilles offers is never applied, only handed back to the Tortoise as one more premise. Keep the Tortoise faithful to the original: it grants every premise as true, including each new conditional, and never denies that the conclusion follows; it merely declines to take the step. Let each stanza be Achilles adding the conditional that would license the previous one, and use the Euclidean triangle if a concrete case helps. The closing quatrain must return to the poem's opening lines, so that the poem ends where it began, with no resolution and no closing moral about ineffable practice. Someone who knows the regress should recognize that what the Tortoise withholds is the use of a rule rather than assent to a truth, and that no further premise ever closes the gap; someone who has never heard of it should still find the exchange intelligible and the poem worth reading aloud.
