Write a mock archival finding aid for a fictional institutional collection — about 600 words, in the flat, professional register of a real repository's published description: collection number and title, extent, biographical note, scope-and-content note, series arrangement, access and reproduction conditions, processing note, and preferred citation. The papers belong to an invented mid-century civic organization, and the satire must live entirely in how the document handles what is absent: a series 'closed pending donor review,' a folder catalogued only as 'miscellaneous — see processing note,' labor-relations files appraised as 'not warranting permanent retention,' an oral-history project whose interviews were never transcribed because the narrators declined to sign the release, and a women's auxiliary that survives in the record only through its treasurer's receipts. The piece should read to any outsider as deadpan bureaucratic comedy, while someone trained in archival theory recognizes that the jokes are the field's own doctrines — appraisal as neutral judgment, description as transparent, the repository as passive receptacle — pushed a step further than the field likes to admit. Never name a real archive, archivist, or theorist, and include no citations.
