I keep running into a move in arguments that I don't have a good name for, and it's been bugging me alot. Say a friend of mine posts something about a clothing brand using sweatshop labor. Someone replies, "You're wearing a phone made with cobalt mined by kids, so you can't say anything." Or a guy I know who has never catcalled anyone in his life criticizes a sexist joke, and someone says, "You're a man, you benefit from patriarchy, sit down." In both cases the original criticism might be completely true, and nobody actually argues that it's false — they just say the person isn't in a position to make it. I used to lump this under hypocrisy, but it doesn't fit: the first friend isn't secretly running a sweatshop, and the second guy isn't secretly sexist. It's more like being entangled in, or benefiting from, the very thing you're criticizing. I've also seen it where the person has benefited in the past but not now, or where they could stop benefiting but it would be really costly, and people disagree about whether that matters. Is there a name for this specific thing? I know there's a general family of ideas about when someone's own record or position affects whether they get to criticize — and I know the tu quoque label, and "whataboutism" — but those seem either too broad or too narrow. What I want is the carving: what exactly is the phenomenon, what distinguishes it from hypocrisy on one side and from a plain ad hominem on the other, and where the edges are. Does it matter whether the critic is a member of a group that benefits, or just a random person who happens to benefit? Does it matter if they're currently benefiting vs. benefited once? Is it ever a legitimate objection, or is it always a dodge?

Also, and this is the part I care about: don't just give me a label and a Wikipedia summary. If there's an established concept, tell me which parts of my examples it captures and which it doesn't, and what the nearest rival concepts are. If there's no single established concept, coin one, define it, and give me a borderline case or two. And please say how confident you are in what you're claiming — especially about whether this is definately distinct from hypocrisy — and single out the point you're least sure of, because I'd rather know where the map is blurry than get a confident answer that's wrong.
