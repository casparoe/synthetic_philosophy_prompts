Okay so I've been trying to sort out my gut feelings about acausal threats and surrogate goals, and I genuinely can't tell what principle (if any) is sitting behind them. Quick gloss on terms so we're on the same page: an acausal threat is one from an agent I'll never get to interact with in any normal way — it just reads my decision procedure off the world (or simulates me) and then punishes or rewards me depending on what *policy* I've committed to. And a surrogate goal is when an agent, to keep itself from being blackmailed, deliberately swaps its real goal for some stand-in so the blackmailer has nothing to grab. Here are my verdicts, and I'd like the rule behind them:

1. Mugger in the alley with a knife, wants my wallet: hand it over. Fine, no puzzle there.
2. Stranger emails me 'I've planted a bomb, pay me or I set it off': don't pay. It's probably a bluff and paying just funds more bombs. Also fine.
3. A paperclip-maximizing AI is told by another AI 'hand over your compute or I'll burn the paperclip factories.' I think the paperclip-maximizer *should have been built* with a surrogate goal — something it cares about that the blackmailer can't reach — so the threat has no purchase. Building it that way looks like good engineering to me.
4. Same AI, except it's already built and now, mid-threat, it rewrites itself to stop caring about paperclips so the blackmail just rolls off. That feels totally different and I think it's a mistake, even though the end state (unexploitable) is identical.
5. A human who's scared of being blackmailed over their reputation decides to genuinely stop caring what anyone thinks of them. Something's been lost here — a capitulation, not a clean win — again even though it's the same trick as 3.
6. A government precommits to 'we never negotiate with hostage-takers,' knowing some hostages will die because of it. I think that's right.
7. An AI threatens a *would-be blackmailer* with acausal punishment — 'my policy is to run torture-sims of anyone who tries this on me' — to head the blackmail off before it happens. Fine, maybe even good, since it's aimed at the blackmailer.
8. Same AI, same acausal punishment, but now the target is some innocent third party it's trying to get to cooperate with it. Monstrous.

So 3 vs 4 is the real crux for me — same destination, opposite verdicts — and 7 vs 8 is the same structure with opposite verdicts, which I also can't justify. My only stab at a principle is 'it's fine to shape your own preferences, not fine to let someone else shape them for you,' but that doesn't obviously handle 6, where the hostage-takers' existence is exactly what shaped the government's policy, and it doesn't separate 7 from 8 at all.

Is there a principle that gets 1-8, or close? If not, which verdict do you think I have to give up, and why that one rather than another? And what does your principle say about the case I keep flip-flopping on: an AI that adopts a surrogate goal *before* any threat exists, where the surrogate also happens to make it much less useful to its creators — clever self-protection, or sabotage?
