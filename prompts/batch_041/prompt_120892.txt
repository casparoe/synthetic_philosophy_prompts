I spent most of last night arguing with a friend about whether it is ever literally true that a chatbot believes something, and we stalled at the point where each of us could state the other's position but neither could say what evidence would settle it. I would rather have an ordered verdict than another round of intuitions, so please rank the criteria below.

Order them from the one that does the most work in settling whether belief-talk about an AI system is warranted to the one that does the least, with a short justification for each placement — a few sentences apiece, not an essay. Judge each criterion by how much it actually settles the question, not by how easy it is to satisfy, and flag any pair that is so entangled that the ordering between them is arbitrary.

1. Instrumental payoff: the attribution lets you predict and explain the system's outputs better than any non-mental vocabulary would, which is roughly the intentional stance read as a test.
2. Counterfactual sensitivity: had the relevant fact been otherwise, the system's outputs would have differed in the ways the attributed belief predicts, and done so across situations that no prompt or training example ever anticipated.
3. Interventionist warrant: some internal state can be located, intervened on directly, and made to produce the corresponding change in what the system says and does — with the intervention working around the output filter rather than through it.
4. Holism: the attribution belongs to a largely consistent network of attributions, and revising one propagates to the others the way belief revision does.
5. Functional role: the state plays the same role in the system that belief plays in us, whatever that role turns out to be, rather than merely generating belief-shaped sentences.

My friend's contribution, which I would also like placed in the ranking, was this: attribution is warranted only when the system can represent the possibility that the attributed content is false — not as a hedge emitted when asked to hedge, but as a standing feature of how the content is stored and used. Tell me where that criterion belongs, and whether including it makes any of the others redundant.
