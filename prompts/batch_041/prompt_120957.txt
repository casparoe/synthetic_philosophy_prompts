I am a novelist, and the book I am writing is narrated almost entirely from inside one character's mind. That has left me preoccupied with a question my own craft cannot settle: what would justify anyone in believing a report of an experience? In reading around the subject I have come across heterophenomenology, Dennett's proposal that a science of consciousness treat what a subject says about their experience as a text to be interpreted, granting the subject authority only over how things seem to them and then checking that text against everything else we can observe. Before I decide what I can borrow from it, I want the specification rather than the defense.

Write the desiderata: what any method for the science of consciousness must deliver in order to count as adequate. Give me the phenomena it has to account for — the difference between what is attended and what is merely present, dreams, blindsight, the split-brain cases, phantom limbs, anesthesia, the plain fact that most of experience goes unreported — the distinctions it cannot collapse (between a report and the experience reported, between being unable to report and having nothing to report, between what a subject is authoritative about and what they are merely sincere about), the constraints it cannot violate (consistency with the timing and localization findings of neuroscience, no hidden inner observer doing the explanatory work, applicability to subjects who cannot speak), and the cases on which it must simply render a verdict, however uncomfortable. With each requirement, state the case or consideration that motivates it. Then tell me which requirements are genuine and which are merely the prejudices of one camp, and which single requirement eliminates the most candidates. I suspect one pair of them cannot be jointly satisfied — the demand that a subject's reports be our only evidence and the demand that we explain why those reports can be systematically false — but tell me whether that is the real fault line or a false one. Finally, score heterophenomenology briefly against your list and say plainly what it leaves unexplained. Please write in American English.
