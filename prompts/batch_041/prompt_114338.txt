The position: The dollar auction is usually treated as a parable of individual irrationality, a case where sunk costs and the desire not to waste what has already been spent drive people to keep bidding past the point of reason. I think that reading is largely wrong. On my view, the escalation in a dollar auction is mostly structural rather than psychological: once a bidder is in the auction, continuing to bid can be the rational response to the rules, and the real irrationality, if that is the right word, is collective and mechanism-induced, not a failure of individual decision-making. The auction is better understood as a war of attrition with an all-pay component than as a straightforward sunk-cost fallacy. The psychological story may describe how people feel, but it does not do the explanatory work.
