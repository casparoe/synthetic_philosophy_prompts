I'm a software engineer on a product team that has started routing decisions through an inverse-reinforcement-learning service: it observes what users do, fits a reward function meant to capture what they actually want, and we then optimize the product against that reward. Write a satirical help-center FAQ for the service — around 550 words, six to eight question-and-answer entries, in the flat, soothing register of corporate documentation, the kind that answers "why did this happen" without ever conceding that anything did.

The comedy has to come from the method working exactly as specified. The answers should be technically faithful to how this kind of inference actually behaves — observed behavior doesn't pin down a unique reward; the user's actions are assumed to be near-optimal; you only ever see the one policy their circumstances allowed them to run, so acting under a constraint gets read as preferring the constrained option; what's being maximized is the fitted reward rather than whatever the user wanted; the fitted reward sits still while the world around it moves — and each of these should be presented as a feature, in a tone that is helpful and faintly proud. Never name any of them as a limitation, never break character, and never let anyone inside the document notice what they've built.

A reader who knows the literature should recognize each issue without being told; a reader who doesn't should still find it funny. The target is the practice of reading preference straight off behavior — not any real company or product.
