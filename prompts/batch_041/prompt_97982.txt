I am designing a ninety-minute session for a mixed reading group — several philosophers, two cognitive scientists, and one engineer who builds reinforcement-learning agents — and I need a plan for how to teach a single concept, not an explanation of the concept itself. The concept is sphexishness: the idea that a creature or system whose behavior looks purposeful and adaptive may in fact be executing a fixed routine that runs to completion regardless of what the situation actually calls for, so that its apparent intelligence dissolves on inspection into a script that cannot revise itself. The group has just finished a unit on free will, and several members arrive already persuaded that sphexishness is either determinism wearing a new coat or an unfalsifiable put-down. I want the session to leave them able to use the idea, and to see precisely where it bites and where it does not.

Give me the teaching plan. Specifically:

- The order in which the components must be introduced, and the reason for that order: what has to be established before a piece of behavior can even be read as rigid rather than merely unlucky, what has to be in place before the regress question can be posed at all (once you have some device for detecting sphexishness in others, what exempts the device itself), and what has to come before the link to free will can be argued rather than merely asserted.

- The one carrying example to build the whole session on, with what it must have and what it must lack in order to do that work. I would rather not anchor the session in the digger-wasp anecdote itself, which I have seen recounted in several incompatible versions; tell me what the wasp is still good for, if anything, and what should replace it as the load-bearing case. The example should also open the question I care most about: rigidity and flexibility are properties relative to a level of description, so the real issue is whether there is any level at which a system is not sphexish, or whether the description can always be pushed down until the flexibility vanishes.

- The misconceptions the group will bring or form, in the order they will surface, and exactly where in the sequence I should head each one off — including the engineer's likely demand that sphexishness be made measurable, and the suspicion that the whole notion is unfalsifiable.

- One question or short task to close with that would show me whether they have grasped the distinction between flexibility and mere complexity or adaptivity, rather than merely learned a new word to apply to things they dislike.

- What I should openly decline to settle at this level, and how to say so without leaving the impression that the concept is empty.

Keep this as a plan for an explanation: no reading list, no citations.
