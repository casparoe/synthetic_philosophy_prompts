I am trying to find a name, if there is one, for a certain structure in philosophical disputes. The clearest instance I know is the disagreement between Mencius and Xunzi about human nature.

On most of what a neutral observer would call the facts, the two seem to agree. Virtue is not automatic; it must be taught and practised, and most people never acquire much of it; bad surroundings can strip away whatever good tendencies a person has, much as a hillside can be cut bare and then grazed until it looks as though it had never borne trees. Where they part is over what the word 'nature' is to mean. Mencius uses it for the incipient moral tendencies — compassion, shame, deference, a sense of right and wrong — which he takes to be ours from birth and to grow if they are nourished. Xunzi uses it for whatever is inborn and unlearned, and since everything good must be acquired by deliberate effort, he concludes that nothing good can be nature, and that the doctrine of natural goodness is an invitation to dispense with teachers, rituals and sages.

What interests me is the shape of the standoff rather than the winner. Each side can state its position so that it comes out true of the world the other describes, which makes the dispute look terminological; but neither side treats it as terminological, and each holds that the other's preferred use of the word is not merely unusual but harmful. Pointing out that the two are using 'nature' differently dissolves nothing, because which of the two uses ought to govern is exactly what is at issue, and the question does not look as though it could be settled by further empirical inquiry. The same shape seems to me to recur in smaller disputes — over whether a virus is alive, say, where the parties can share all the biology and still disagree.

Is there an established concept for a disagreement of this kind? If so, I would like to know which of the nearby candidates fits best, and why the others fall short — in particular, how it differs from a merely verbal dispute, understood as one whose parties would be content to dissolve it by disambiguation, and from a straightforward disagreement about the facts. I would also like to know whether there is a separate name for the manoeuvre by which one party tries to settle such a dispute by redefining the contested term rather than by producing evidence. If nothing established covers it, please coin a term and define it, marking the boundaries: what must be true of a disagreement for it to be of this kind, and what rules a disagreement out. A label by itself would not help me; what I need is the carving.
