Ok so a group I'm in has been arguing for like a year about national character, and I want a diagnosis of why it's stalled rather than another round of the argument itself. Both sides basically agree on the facts: there are real measurable average differences between populations on psychological measures, and the within-population spread is huge. But the people who think national character is a legitimate object of study and the people who think it's a confused pseudo-concept just keep restating themselves. One of them keeps saying a national-character claim is just a base rate and base rates are fine; the other keeps saying a base rate about people is already a stereotype and that's the problem. My hunch is these are two different questions wearing one name -- a descriptive one (do stable population-level regularities exist?) and a normative one (when is it ok to reason from someone's group membership to claims about them) -- and I'm definately starting to think each side keeps dragging its own question into the other's turf, so they never actually meet. Is that the right diagnosis? Or is there a genuine conceptual question in here that I'm collapsing, like whether 'character' can even attach to a population rather than a person, or whether any group generalization is doomed by the reference-class problem (who exactly counts as 'the Italians')? And whatever the diagnosis, tell me what would count as progress here: a sharper distinction, an agreement to split the question in two, or a real argument that the whole notion should just be dropped.
