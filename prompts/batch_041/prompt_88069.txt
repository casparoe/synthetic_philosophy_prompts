I teach a graduate seminar in the history of ethics, and the final unit is on Bertrand Russell's writings on marriage, sex, and the family. My students arrive with the impression that Russell is simply a prophet of the sexual revolution, and they leave with that impression more or less intact. They miss the eugenic and perfectionist strands in his work -- his willingness to let experts and the state shape the good life rather than merely protect liberty -- his confidence that scientific expertise should govern intimate life, and the extent to which his libertarianism was bounded by his own tastes and class position. A colleague has pressed me that Russell's real principle was not liberty but aristocratic experimentalism: that he wanted the intelligent and the bold to be free to try new forms of life, and cared much less about the freedom of the ordinary. I am not persuaded, but I want the answer to test it.

Suppose Russell had lived to the present -- say, to 2025 -- and had continued to write on these subjects. I want a prediction of his verdicts on a set of developments that postdate him. To keep the answers disciplined, I have asked them to sort each verdict into one of three categories: what follows from his explicit principles; what follows from his temperament, class position, and historical formation; and what his principles leave genuinely underdetermined or self-contradictory. I have also given them a distinction I have been working on, between liberty of conduct -- the freedom to do as one likes sexually without state interference -- and liberty of status -- the freedom to have one's chosen relationship form recognized, supported, and protected by the state. Russell was an energetic defender of the first and said almost nothing adequate about the second; I want the students to see what happens when they try to extend him.

The developments are: the legalization of same-sex marriage across most of the West; the split between full decriminalization of sex work, as in New Zealand, and the Nordic model that criminalizes buyers but not sellers; the growth of commercial gestational surrogacy and cross-border reproductive travel; the campaign for legal recognition of polyamorous relationships, including multi-parent adoption and civil-partnership-style status; the #MeToo reckoning and the due-process objections to it; the use of preimplantation genetic testing and polygenic embryo screening to select embryos; the spread of AI romantic companions; the so-called incel movement and its arguments about a right to sex or the redistribution of sexual access; and the visibility movements for asexual and aromantic people. For each, say what Russell would have said, on what grounds, and with what confidence -- and where the three categories come apart, tell me which one is doing the work. I am not looking for a sanitized Russell who lands on the progressive side of every question; if his principles would have led him somewhere uncomfortable, say so. Nor am I looking for a list of his actual writings -- I know them. What I want is the extrapolation, done carefully, with the seams showing.
