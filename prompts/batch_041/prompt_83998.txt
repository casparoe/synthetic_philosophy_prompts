I'm a graduate student in a metaphysics seminar, and there is a move in discussion that I keep running into and cannot evaluate. We spent a session on the view that ordinary composite objects — tables, trees, cats — do not strictly exist, and that the world contains only fundamental particles arranged in various ways and never any further thing over and above them. When the debate grew heated, another student waved it off: the dispute, he said, is just a verbal dispute, because the two sides mean different things by 'exists' and each could translate the other's language without loss. On that basis he concluded that there is nothing left here to argue about. I think this is too quick, but I cannot say precisely where it goes wrong, in part because I cannot say what calling something a verbal dispute is supposed to mean.

Please lay out, as a menu I can take back to the seminar, the different precise claims that calling a disagreement a verbal dispute could be making, ordered from the nearly trivial to the most radical. For each reading, tell me what it commits you to, whether it is plausible for the case at hand, whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, or something false, and — what I most need — whether it actually supports the conclusion my classmate drew, that there is nothing left to argue about. Distinguish in particular: a reading on which the two sides attach different senses to a key term but may still have a genuine disagreement once that is sorted out; a reading on which they agree about all the facts and differ only over which sentences to utter; a reading on which there is no fact of the matter about the disputed question; and any reading on which the dispute is not a genuine disagreement at all, but only a clash of frameworks or of practical choices. If some of these readings would vindicate the thought that the disagreement is real even though it turns on words, say so, and tell me which single reading the dismissal actually needs in order to work and whether my classmate is entitled to it.
