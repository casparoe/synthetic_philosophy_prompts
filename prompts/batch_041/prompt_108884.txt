I am a high school student who has just finished a year of AP Statistics, which is to say I am reasonably fluent in populations and samples, sampling frames, bias, the sampling distribution of a statistic, unbiased estimators, and confidence intervals, and I have seen the German tank problem version of estimating an unknown maximum from observed serial numbers. Beyond that I have no formal machinery at all, and I would rather you did not pretend I do.

I have been reading about the doomsday argument, the one that runs roughly like this: I am one human among all the humans who will ever have lived; on the usual estimates something like a hundred billion have lived so far, though the precise figure is not doing any work; if my position in that sequence were a random draw, then with 95 percent probability I would fall in the last 95 percent of the sequence, which would put the total number of humans ever at under about twenty times the number who have lived so far. Please restate that argument as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually have — population, sampling frame, draw, rank, serial number, estimator, sampling distribution, coverage — treating my birth rank as an observed index and the total number of humans ever as the unknown quantity being estimated. Do not quietly improve the argument while translating it; if the translation only goes through on a charitable repair, say where the repair was.

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the argument carry across cleanly? Which parts can be carried only if I let my words change meaning — for instance, if a confidence interval has to be read as a degree of belief about a fixed but unknown quantity, or if the population has to be treated as already fixed and enumerated before the draw? And which parts have no expression in my vocabulary at all? In particular, tell me whether the step from "I am a random human" to "I am a random draw" is a translation or a replacement, since in my course randomness always lives in the selecting procedure and never in which unit I happen to be.

I do not want a reading list. I want a verdict rather than a menu: say whether the residue is a defect of the statistical vocabulary — something my course simply left out that a better-trained statistician would supply — or evidence that the original argument was doing something it never made clear, so that the translation fails because the original was under-specified. Commit to one of those and defend it. If, once translated, the argument turns out to be obviously valid or obviously silly, tell me so plainly and explain why.
