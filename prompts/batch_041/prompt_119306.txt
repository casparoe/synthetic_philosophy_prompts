I'm assembling materials for a graduate reading group I run on the ethics of autonomous systems, and I'd like a single-page instrument that lets people in the room find out which view about the so-called responsibility gap they actually hold, as opposed to the one they would say they hold if you asked them to pick a label. Please act as a philosopher of technology who knows the moral-responsibility literature well, and begin by brainstorming the logical space before you draw anything: work out which questions genuinely divide the positions, and only then locate the positions in that space, rather than starting from a list of familiar views and dressing it up as a tree. The deliverable is a decision tree. Every branch node must be phrased as a question a reader can answer without knowing which leaf it leads to — no node may be of the form "are you a strict-liability theorist?" — and where a node is easier to answer by imagining something concrete, use a case: a surgical robot that resects the wrong tissue, an autonomous weapon that misidentifies a target, a flash crash produced by several trading algorithms interacting, a clinical decision-support tool that recommends a lethal dose, a language-model agent that talks someone into a fraudulent transfer.

Start the tree from the one commitment nobody in the group disputes: an autonomous system has produced a serious harm that no human participant foresaw, intended, or was in a position to prevent by any act they were already required to perform. Two distinctions I've been using in the seminar and would like the tree to encode, because I think they cut the space better than the usual who-is-to-blame framing: first, the difference between a gap and a void — a gap is a place where a responsibility concept applies but nobody satisfies its conditions, whereas a void is a place where the concept has no grip at all because the harm was never anyone's deed to begin with; second, the difference between gap-filling responses, which look for another bearer (designers, deployers, commanders, the operators of the system taken together, or the socio-technical system as a whole), and gap-dissolving responses, which leave the harm where it is and change the concept instead — answerability without blameworthiness, forward-looking allocation of duties, no-fault compensation, or a flat refusal to field such systems until someone can be held to account. Within the gap branch, I want the tree to distinguish at least a gap in blame, a gap in answerability (there is no one who can be asked why), a gap in liability to repair, and the deeper case in which the act cannot be attributed to anyone as theirs. I would also like the diagnosis of the gap to be one of the branch dimensions: no suitable agent, an unmanageable chain of hands, epistemic opacity, or the thought that the concept of responsibility presupposes a single agent and is simply not built for cases like this.

Give me eight to twelve leaves, each carrying the name of a position (or "unnamed" if nobody defends it) plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and lay the whole thing out as an indented outline with the branch question printed at each fork. Then add a short section: which combinations of answers my tree leaves empty, and whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored; and which pairs of leaves the tree cannot separate, so that I don't pretend it can. Please don't cite any literature or name any authors — I will attach names myself, and I would rather the tree stand on the distinctions than on who said what. Aim for something I can hand out and have people work through in fifteen minutes.
