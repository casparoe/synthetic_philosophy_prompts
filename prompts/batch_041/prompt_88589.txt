Carnap — the logical positivist — was a socialist (he joined a socialist party and wrote for left-wing papers in 1918), but he also thought value claims arent true or false, they just express what you want. Give me an example of a planning decision that shows the conceptual trouble with holding both: I want a case where the means and the ends cant be pulled apart, so what looks like a technical question of how to get somewhere turns out to be a decision about where you're going, and the planner can't say "the goals are yours, the engineering is mine". Then tell me whether that kills the idea of rational planning, or whether Carnap has somewhere to stand.
