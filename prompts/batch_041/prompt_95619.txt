Tronto's four phases — caring about, taking care of, care-giving, care-receiving — and the four qualities that go with them (attentiveness, responsibility, competence, responsiveness), plus her insistence that care is a political rather than a merely domestic concept, and the fifth phase she adds later, caring with, which I think is about whether a public actually holds care as a shared value; correct me if I've got that wrong. So I want a verdict, argued properly: is the novel a reductio of care ethics, showing that a perfectly caring relationship can flourish inside an atrocity and leave it untouched, or is it a correction to it, in which case Tronto's care-receiving is where the theory has teeth and the donors are never once asked?
