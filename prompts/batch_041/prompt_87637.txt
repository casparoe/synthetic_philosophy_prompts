I am a high school student, and I serve on the committee that is rewriting our student council's bylaws. The draft before us makes three promises at once. First, every question that comes before the council is settled by majority vote. Second, each member may decide, on her own authority, one yes-or-no question that she has designated as personal to her. Third, the council will never adopt a position that every member agrees is worse than some other position that was available to it. A teacher has told me that Sen's liberal paradox shows that these three promises cannot all be kept, and I want to understand what that means for the rules we actually adopt.

Please give me the most important implications of the paradox for our situation. I am especially interested in whether the difficulty lies in granting individual members any rights at all, in our having written those rights as decisions over a single pair of options rather than over a whole area of policy that a member claims as personal, or in the requirement that the council's collective preferences form a consistent ranking. I would also like to know whether anything changes if a member is permitted to trade her personal question to another member in exchange for that member's support on a separate vote, and which of the three promises a person who takes the paradox seriously would advise us to give up, together with what we would lose by giving up each one.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and without restating my question.
