For several years I've been reporting on the economics of long-horizon problems — climate damages, nuclear waste, infrastructure meant to outlast everyone who approves it — and I keep encountering a move I have no word for. Someone assigns a single number to how much a future benefit or harm is worth today: three percent a year, five percent a year. Press them on why the future should count for less at all, and they don't defend caring less about people who come later. They offer something else entirely — that we can't be sure the future will arrive, that a catastrophe might intervene, that our descendants may not value what we are trying to hand them. Those sound like reasons to be uncertain about the future, not reasons to care about it less. Then, a moment later, the same number is back in use as though it were simply the claim that a future person's good matters less because it is future. I have watched the identical slippage in pension and sovereign-wealth debates, where a rate defended on the ground that the money could have been invested at a return gets carried over into comparisons of harm to people who cannot invest anything, because they do not yet exist. Each justification is individually respectable; the difficulty is that the number stays fixed while the reason for it changes to fit whatever objection is on the table, and no one ever totals up how much of the discounting is risk, how much is foregone return, and how much is the bare belief that later matters less.

Is there an established name for this? I'm aware of pure time preference and of discounting for uncertainty, but I'm after the specific pattern in which one discount rate is defended by borrowing whichever rationale neutralizes the present objection, so that the rate looks innocent while performing work no one would endorse if it were stated plainly. If there is no name, please coin one and define it precisely: what counts as an instance, what is merely ordinary disagreement about the correct rate, and where the boundary lies between this and honest uncertainty discounting, opportunity-cost discounting, and the plain compounding of a constant rate. I don't want the label so much as the carving.
