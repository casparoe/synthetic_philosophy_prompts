I've been trying to put a name to something and keep failing, so I'll just describe it and see if it's already a thing.

The shape is this. I believe something — that a certain policy is a bad idea, or that a mate of mine has been unfair to me. And I also believe, fairly firmly, that I only believe it because of how I was brought up, or because it suits me, and not because the evidence really supports it. I'll say that out loud and mean it. But it does nothing. I don't lower my confidence, I don't start hedging, I don't feel any pull to change my mind. The two beliefs just sit next to each other, and the second one never gets its hands on the first.

I know the obvious label is believing against your own better judgement — akrasia, weakness of will in the doxastic register. But that's not quite it, because when I'm genuinely akratic it's uncomfortable; there's a tug-of-war. This is the opposite of uncomfortable. It's more like the self-knowledge gets acknowledged and then quietly filed somewhere it can't do any damage. And I don't think it's just me being stubborn, because I see it in arguements all the time: someone concedes that their view is basically an inheritance from their community, nods along quite sincerely, and then carries on arguing for it exactly as before, as though nothing had been conceded. It doesn't look like bad faith. The concession is real. It just has no consequences.

Two things I want to know. First, does this have a name — a bit of self-knowledge that is sincerely accepted and yet never feeds back into the belief it's about? I can see it's in the neighbourhood of higher-order evidence and of epistemic akrasia, but I'd want to know exactly where it sits: what it is, what it isn't, and what you'd call the cases at the edges. Second, is there a principled reason it doesn't feed back, or is that just motivated reasoning in a clever disguise? The argument I keep half-making is that if I'm the sort of creature whose beliefs are set by upbringing, then my belief that this belief is set by upbringing is itself set by upbringing, so leaning on it to revise anything would be circular. But if that's the reason, it seems to eat everything — nothing I noticed about my own psychology could ever revise a belief, which can't be right. So either there's a principled place to stop, or the whole thing is a trick I'm playing on myself. Which is it?
