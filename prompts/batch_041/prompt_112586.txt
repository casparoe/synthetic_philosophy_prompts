I'm a philosophy master's student in a graduate seminar on probability and rationality, and next week I have to present and defend my term paper to the group. My thesis has two parts. First, the standard Dutch book argument establishes only that incoherent credences are dominated — that there is a set of bets guaranteeing the agent a loss — and the step from "dominated" to "irrational" requires a premise about what credences are for that the argument never supplies. Second, and this is the part I expect to be attacked: I argue that the theorem only forces coherence on an agent who is transparent about her own credences, because the book is built from the odds she would accept, and "would accept" presupposes she can read her own degrees of belief off reliably. Once you separate the credence ledger from the betting ledger, incoherence forces consistency between the two only for an agent whose credences are luminous to her. So I want to say the Dutch book argument is really a theorem about introspectively transparent agents, and its reach is narrower than its fans suppose.

The audience is a formal epistemology reading group: two Bayesian statisticians who treat the betting interpretation as obvious and think the coherence question is long settled; a couple of philosophers of probability who are impatient with pragmatic justifications and think the real work is done by accuracy-based dominance arguments; and one person who works on the metaphysics of credence and will press on whether degrees of belief are even the sort of thing that can be bet on. Draft my Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I will actually get from this room, not the ones I'd get from an intro class, ordered by how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way that member of the audience would put it, with a two-minute reply and an honest note on where the reply is soft. Tell me what to concede up front, what to hold, and which objection I should be hoping nobody asks. No citations or reading lists — everyone in the room knows the literature, and I can't look anything up during the session.
