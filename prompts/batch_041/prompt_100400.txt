I have been reading Popper on Darwinism, and the verdict I want to interrogate — that Darwinism is a metaphysical research programme rather than a testable scientific theory — seems to lean on an analogy he returns to throughout his later work: that the growth of knowledge and natural selection are one and the same kind of process, a blind variation that a selective environment then filters, with what survives retained and built upon. Conjectures play the role of variations, criticism and testing play the role of selection, and successful theories are inherited and modified by their successors much as fit lineages are. From this he draws the conclusion that the Darwinian core is a near-vacuous schema — the survivors survive — which orients research without being testable in itself. I would like the analogy audited. Set out the correspondence explicitly: what corresponds to the variation, to the selection pressure, to the environment, to the mechanism of inheritance, to the timescale. Then say where the mapping breaks, naming the features of Darwinian selection that have no counterpart in theory change and the features of theory change that have no counterpart in biology. Distinguish the disanalogies that matter for his conclusion from the ones that are merely decorative, and tell me whether the conclusion is genuinely carried by the analogy or whether the analogy was doing the persuading on its own. If the parallel survives only in weakened form, sketch the analogy that would do the job better.
