I run a small reading group on criminal law theory and I want to hand out a decision tree for the punishment session, because my students keep collapsing the whole thing into "retributivist vs consequentialist" and I want to force them apart. Start the tree from "the state may deliberately make a wrongdoer suffer", and make every branch node a question a student could answer without already knowing which label it leads to. The distinctions I care about are: whether the justification looks backward to desert or forward to effects; whether desert is sufficient on its own or only caps how much hard treatment is permitted; whether the offender forfeits a right not to be punished or whether the state is owed punishment because he took unfair advantage of others' compliance; whether the justifying good is the suffering itself or just the censure that suffering conventionally expresses; and whether the aim is to change the offender, frighten others, or remove his capacity to reoffend. The leaves should include pure retributivism, limiting/negative retributivism, act-utilitarian deterrence with no side constraints, rule-utilitarian, expressivist/communicative, fair-play, self-defence/forfeiture, quarantine/incapacitation and rehabilitation, each with a one-line note on its most awkward commitment. Also tell me which combinations of answers nobody actually occupies and whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive or merely unexplored, and flag any two leaves your tree can't really seperate. Keep it to about a page, and write it in British English.
