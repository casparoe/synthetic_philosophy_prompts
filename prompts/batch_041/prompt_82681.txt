My friend spent most of last night insisting that knowledge is transparent to the knower — that if you know something you are in a position to know that you know it, and, more strongly, that whatever mental state you are in, you can always tell from the inside that you are in it. I don't believe him, but every case I produced he waved away as a borderline case, or as my not having looked carefully enough. So I should like a case built to order, and preferably not one of the usual ones.

Here are the specifications. The subject must genuinely know the first-order thing: on a safety-style account, the belief is true, formed by an ordinary method, and does not go wrong in nearby worlds. The second-order claim must fail: the subject is not in a position to know that they know. Beyond that, no sorites-style series, no continuum of slight differences, no borderline cases, no clock-reading, no temperature or other inexact magnitudes — I want the state to be the sort of thing that looks like a paradigm candidate for transparency, such as a felt sensation, an occurrent desire, or a passing mood. The setting must be ordinary and non-sceptical: no demons, no vats, no fake barns, and no general doubt about whether the subject's introspection is reliable. And it must be legible to someone who knows the shape of the debate — luminosity, safety, the margin-for-error idea — without having the literature at their fingertips.

Keep the scenario itself to a single paragraph; the commentary may be longer. Then do the work: state the belief and the method, describe the nearby worlds, say why the first-order belief is safe and the second-order one is not, and tell me which single feature of the case is carrying the weight. Say what happens when that feature is removed, and put the best case for the other side — how would someone who thinks such states are transparent attack my example, and could they plausibly insist that the state in question is exact and therefore immune? If the specification turns out to be unsatisfiable as stated, say so, give me the closest satisfiable case, and tell me which constraint has to give. The verdicts must fall out of the theories rather than being built in.

Finally, tell me how confident you are in the verdicts, and single out the point you are least sure of.
