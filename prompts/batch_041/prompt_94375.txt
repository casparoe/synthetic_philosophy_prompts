I'm one of a dozen equal owners of a small, worker-run furniture workshop — there's no boss, everyone does every job, and we split the proceeds evenly. We're now considering a computer-controlled cutting machine that would take over the slowest and most exacting part of the work. Nobody would lose their job; we'd simply have more time and could bid on bigger orders. Two of my partners are firmly against it. They say that handing our skilled handwork over to a machine degrades us whoever owns it, that we'd end up as mere attendants to the machine, and that the know-how which makes each of us valuable would drain away into a box none of us really controls. The others say a machine is only a tool, and that tools have only ever harmed workers when a boss used them to grind us down — which, they point out, isn't our situation. I think both camps are onto something, but I can't say what. How should I think this through? Please reply in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
