Here's one I keep going back and forth on and can't settle. My friend thinks any claim that a single event has an "objective chance" over and above (a) the long-run frequencies the setup produces and (b) what a perfectly rational agent should believe is just a projection -- that "propensity" adds no truth conditions beyond the frequencies plus a vague gesture that the setup is chancy. I think she's wrong but I can't say why in a way that survives contact with her.

Made-up case to focus it: a black box with a button. Press it, a light goes red or green. Over millions of presses red comes up 1/3 of the time, stable, and you can't gerrymander a reference class that gives a different limit. No symmetry in the box, no hidden randomizer you can point to, and for all anyone knows the sequence is produced by some gnarly deterministic process. Does the box have a 1/3 chance of red on the next press, or just a 1/3 frequency plus a rational credence of 1/3?

So: what's your credence that there's a fact of the matter here beyond frequency + credence -- i.e. that the propensity/objective-chance interpretation is genuinely distinct from and not reducible to frequentism or subjectivism? I'm at about 0.6 myself and it's mush. Decompose it for me: how much rides on whether a Humean best-system reduction of chance works, how much on whether credence can be pinned down without begging the question (the reference-class thing), how much on the single-case problem -- whether propensities even make sense for events that happen once -- and whatever else you'd add. A number and one line each, then how they combine. Then tell me which term you're least sure of, what would actually move it, and where your decomposition is most likely to be wrong.
