ok so for my decision theory seminar we have this assignment where we have to write a poem that actually does some philosophical work, and I want to do the money pump — you know, the thing where if your preferences run in a circle (you like A over B, B over C, and C over A) then someone can just walk you around that circle taking a little cut each time until you're broke and back where you started. I want a pantoum, 16 lines, 4 quatrains. The form has to carry the argument: pantoums repeat lines and shuffle them into the next stanza, and the last stanza folds back into the first, so the poem literally ends on the line it started with but the person is poorer. That's the whole pump and I want someone who's never heard of the concept to feel it in the repetition scheme instead of just being told about it.

Two things I really want in there, because I think they're where it gets interesting and I dont want just the cartoon version:

1) She is not naive. She knows about the cycle, she sees it coming, and she actually pays something up front to stop herself from getting pumped later — a fee, a commitment device, a subscription, whatever — and that payment is already the first squeeze. So foresight doesn't save her; being smart enough to pay for protection is exactly what puts her in the red. I think that's the real bite of the diachronic version but tell me if I'm overstating it.

2) The pump only "works" if money is something she values in the same ranking as the goods she's cycling through, and if she keeps saying yes to each trade one at a time. So I want the poem to make the trades feel innocent — each step a small yes, a reasonable yes — and only the shape of the whole thing is damning. Maybe the two refrain lines should be the two things she keeps swapping back and forth (what she sells in the morning and buys back at night), so the reader hears her reverse herself in the same words over and over.

Please dont write her like a fool. She should sound like me. And dont resolve it at the end — I dont think the poem should tell us whether her preferences are irrational or whether she's just unlucky about the market. Leave the "is this a defect in her or in the setup" question hanging.

Also keep the language plain, no jargon like intransitive or diachronic inside the poem itself, that stuff can stay in the class discussion and not in the verse.
