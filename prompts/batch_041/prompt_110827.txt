I have recently taken over a business that is losing money, and I have gone to Machiavelli in the expectation that he is the realist's guide to precisely this sort of situation. What I cannot work out is how to put his account of fortune and virtù to use. He concedes to fortune something like half of what we do, and I take virtù to name the integrity and firmness of principle that keep a leader from being corrupted by his office and let him act on his own judgement; between the two of them, fortune and virtù are meant to account for the outcome. Is there a disciplined way to determine, before I commit myself, which of the two a given decision falls under, so that I know when I am obliged to act boldly on my own resources and when the sensible course is to accept that the result lies with fortune and merely try to limit the damage? The decisions I have in mind are unglamorous ones: whom to keep and whom to let go, which product lines to cut, whether to stake the remaining capital on a single strategy.
