I have been trying to get clear about a pattern that shows up whenever a theory's central claim is about a long-run tendency rather than a dated event, and I cannot find a name for it that does any work. I have been reading about Malthus and about later forecasts made in the same spirit, and I keep meeting the same structure.

Here is the shape of it. A theory asserts a mechanism and, as a consequence, a date or a window. The theory says that population grows faster than the means of subsistence, so within some number of decades the poorest will be pressed back to the edge of starvation; or that a country's debt will become unserviceable by a stated year; or that a fish stock will collapse by a stated year. The date arrives and nothing happens as advertised: yields rise, growth and inflation outrun the interest, the fleet simply gets more efficient and the stock lasts longer. The defender does not narrow the theory's scope or repair a parameter in any way that can be tested on its own. He re-times the claim. The mechanism is intact, he says; the improvement in yields is itself a response to the pressure; the reprieve is borrowed time; and in the fisheries case the delay is offered as further evidence of the pressure, since only growing capacity could have postponed the collapse. The failed date is never counted against the theory, and the theory keeps its grip on the claim about how things work.

Sometimes this strikes me as plainly legitimate: the mechanism really does seem to be operating, and the timing was always the most fragile part of the claim. Sometimes it strikes me as a shell game. I cannot say what distinguishes the two, and I suspect the difference is not just how often the date has been moved.

The labels people have offered me do not land. Ad hoc rescue and auxiliary hypothesis both suggest a repair that could be tested independently; here the repair is a new date, and the new date is as fragile as the old one. Unfalsifiable is wrong, because the mechanism claim is still exposed — if the pressure mechanism were shown not to operate, the theory would be in trouble. Degenerating research programme is a verdict on a whole sequence of moves, and I want the name of the single move, the one where a prediction is disconfirmed and the response is to change when, not what.

So: does this pattern have a name? If it does, give it, and tell me where my description misdescribes it. If it does not, coin one, define it, and mark its edges. In particular, distinguish it from four neighbours: a theory that never claimed a date; a theory whose date is missed and then abandoned; a case where the delay is itself derived from the theory's counteracting mechanisms and so counts as a success; and a case where the mechanism is confirmed by independent evidence and only the model's timing was an artifact. Then tell me what, if anything, separates a defensible deferral from a rhetorical one — whether the difference lies in whether the re-timed theory forbids anything new, or in whether the counteracting factor was predicted rather than discovered after the fact.

Keep the answer under 700 words. I do not need citations or a reading list, and a bare name without the carving would leave me where I started.
