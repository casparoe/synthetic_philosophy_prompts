In a seminar on the methodology of economic modelling, a professor repeatedly used one line to close down objections: 'the realism of a model's assumptions is irrelevant; all that matters is whether its predictions are accurate.' The occasion I keep returning to concerned a model in which households live forever and know the entire future path of prices with certainty. A student objected that this assumption is precisely what generates the model's headline conclusion about the effects of a tax, so that conclusion tells us nothing about an economy of short-lived and imperfectly informed households. The professor repeated the line and moved on.

I would like to know what that line can actually be claiming, since I suspect several quite different theses are being run together under it. Please lay out the distinct readings it might bear — from the nearly trivial, through the substantive and defensible, to the false or incoherent — and say of each whether it is plausible and whether it would genuinely license the professor's dismissal of that particular objection. I would also like to know which reading is usually intended by those who reach for the line in this setting, and whether the student's objection is the sort of thing the line is equipped to answer at all, or whether it is directed at something else entirely.
