My two roommates went at this for like an hour at dinner and I stayed out of it, but I actually want to know who was right. Dana's view: sentience isn't a yes/no thing, it comes in amounts, so moral status should come in amounts too — an insect gets a sliver, a mouse more, a dog more, a person more. There's no bright line, and pretending there is is just us being lazy.

Marcus definately pushed back. He said "amounts of sentience" is really just amounts of brains, and being smart isn't what makes you matter. He also said the sliding scale gives you crazy answers anyway: if bugs count even a little, then a billion bugs outweigh a dog, which nobody actually believes, so the scale view either needs something extra tacked on or it's just false.

Dana said he's mixing up two separate questions — whether status comes in degrees and whether you're allowed to add it up. And she said mattering isn't about smartness, it's about how bad things can get for a being. She put it as a multiplier: something whose inner life tops out at "mildly unpleasant" can only be wronged mildly, no matter how many of them there are. That's a degree claim, and it doesn't need a cutoff anywhere.

Marcus then offered a test a lab partner of his uses: a thing counts only if it can be of two minds — if it has at least two states that can pull against each other, so that being conflicted is even possible. A thermostat can't be conflicted, a dog can, so there's your line, and it's on/off, not a dial.

Dana said that's a test for a rich mind, not for mattering. A newborn can't be conflicted either, and he's not about to score a newborn a zero. She said he keeps sliding from "sophisticated" to "matters." She also said he's wrong that her view has no floor: there's a real difference between a state that tracks a condition and a state that's felt as good or bad. The thermostat has the first and not the second, and the line between any experience at all and none is binary. The degrees are all above it. So: is anyone home, yes or no; and how much can it hurt, on a scale. Two questions, not one.

Marcus came back that she's made the hard case vanish by fiat — nobody has ever met a creature that's only a little bit there. And if the floor is binary, then somewhere back in the history of life there was a first creature with a flicker of experience and a parent with none, and she's fine with that pair differing in kind, which is exactly the sharp line she started out mocking. He also said her multiplier can't be measured even in principle, so it's a degree with no units, which makes it useless for deciding any actual case.

They asked me to weigh in and I had nothing. Can you go through it point by point and say who's right about each move, what each of them is missing, and who had the better of it overall? American English please.
