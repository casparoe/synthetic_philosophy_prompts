My roommate and her brother spent most of dinner on this and I couldnt referee it, partly because half the time I couldn't tell if they were disagreeing about the words or about the world.

(1) He said moral status is a sliding scale running from insects on up: a shrimp is worth a little, a dog a lot, a person more, and the scale tracks how sentient the thing is. (2) She said the scale is fine but it isn't sentience that's graded — sentience is on or off. Either there's something it's like to be the thing or there isn't. What comes in degrees is everything built on top of it: how rich the experience is, whether the creature remembers it, whether it has plans. Calling a shrimp 30% sentient is like calling a puddle 30% wet. (3) He said the machinery is obviously graded — more neurons, more integration, more of whatever does the work — and if you turn an anesthetic up slowly, experience doesn't snap off, it fades. So where's the switch? (4) She said a heap of sand fades too, and there's still a fact of the matter about when it stops being a heap, we just can't know it; vagueness about a word isn't a half-heap out there in the world. And "the machinery is graded" only says the correlates are graded — a bulb can be dim or bright, but it isn't more or less switched on. (5) He said fine, but she's still stuck on the thermostat. Either there's a faint glimmer of experience in a thermostat or there isn't, and people really do argue that it isn't obviously zero. If it's zero, then the line between "matters not at all" and "matters a little" is a hard line hiding somewhere in the dark, which is worse for her than a scale. If it isn't zero, she's just conceded the scale. (6) She said that's a false choice, because a sliver of experience isn't a sliver of moral status: status turns on what the experience is of, and on whether things can go badly for the creature, not on how much experience there is. He said that's just moving the scale from "amount of sentience" to "richness of sentience" and renaming it.

Please adjudicate this move by move — rate all six from clearly mistaken to clearly right, say what each of them is getting right and where each is confused — and then give me an actual verdict on the underlying question: does sentience come in degrees, and does that even matter for how moral status is graded? Don't just split the difference. Also tell me where your own reasoning here is most likely to be wrong, and what would change your mind.
