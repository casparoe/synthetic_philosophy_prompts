I keep getting into the same fight in my metaethics reading group and I want to know what the fight is actually about. The relativists say moral judgments are true or false only relative to a standard, and that when two people with different standards disagree about, say, whether some practice is wrong, neither is making a mistake—there's faultless disagreement. The objectivists say that's just changing the subject: if one says wrong and the other says not wrong, they're contradicting each other, so one of them has to be wrong, and the fact that both feel justified doesn't show anything. Both sides agree on the anthropology—there's a lot of deep moral disagreement, people have different codes—and both agree that moral language has an action-guiding function. Nothing moves.

My hunch is that this is not one disagreement but at least three stacked on top of each other. First, a verbal dispute about how to use the word true in ethics. Second, a substantive dispute about what makes moral judgments true when they are true. Third, a dispute about which phenomena are the data—does faultless disagreement actually exist, or is it an artifact of context-shifting? And maybe a fourth: whether relativism is even a thesis about truth at all, or really a thesis about the authority of moral reasons, i.e. that no standard can bind someone who doesn't accept it. I've also started wondering if the word relativism is covering three different jobs: content-relativism (the same sentence expresses different propositions in different contexts), truth-relativism (same proposition, truth relative to a standard), and standard-relativism (no neutral standard for choosing standards). Each side might be attacking a different one of those and calling it the same thing.

Diagnose it for me. Which of these is the real axis of the stall, and which are red herrings? If it's partly verbal, say exactly where the verbal part is and what would be left if we fixed the terminology. If it's substantive, say what the two sides are actually committed to that makes them keep talking past each other. Is the impasse a sign the question is ill-posed, or just hard? And what would count as progress: a distinction, a new piece of data, a formal result, or an argument that no progress should be expected?

Also, say what would change your mind about your diagnosis—what argument or consideration would make you revise it—and where your reasoning is most likely to be wrong.
