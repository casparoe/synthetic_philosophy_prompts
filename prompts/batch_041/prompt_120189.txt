Write a mock application form, in the deadpan register of a government licensing office, for a fictional Bureau of Scientific Status that decides what counts as a science by asking whether a theory forbids anything. Include numbered fields with an applicant's answers and the reviewing officer's marginal notes; aim for 550-650 words, with no more than two applicants. The humour has to come from the philosophy rather than from jokes about paperwork, and the form should keep colliding with the real difficulties: a theory can be eminently falsifiable and worthless; a refuted theory can be kept alive indefinitely by adjusting some auxiliary assumption, so that no observation ever kills anything; the theories everyone agrees are science — the historical and biological ones — can rarely name the single observation that would refute them; and some of the things everyone agrees are not science can name one quite easily. Give the bureau a clause for theories that are already refuted (an applicant wants the refutation counted in its favour), a clause requiring the applicant to specify which member of a conjunction of hypotheses a failed prediction actually refuted, an annex for requesting an ad hoc rescue, a rubric scoring how severe the theory's tests have been, and a closing note in which the officer observes that in the bureau's history the rubric has never rejected a physics application and never approved a psychoanalytic one for reasons that had anything to do with the rubric. Keep it legible to a reader who has never heard of the demarcation problem, and never let the form state the objections outright; someone who has heard of it should find the satire fair and wince only where the argument really is weak.
