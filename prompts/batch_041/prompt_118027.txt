I am a postgraduate student in cognitive science, and over the holidays I fell into a long argument with a friend of mine, who works on animal behaviour and reads a great deal of philosophy of biology. The argument concerned the four kinds of creature that Dennett distinguishes: the Darwinian creature, which is what it is because of natural selection, its behaviour being fixed rather than learned; the Skinnerian creature, whose behaviour is shaped within its own lifetime by reinforcement; the Popperian creature, which can run a trial in the head, so that the trial fails rather than the creature; and the Gregorian creature, whose mind is furnished with tools — above all words, and the habits of thought that come with them — taken over from other minds, so that it inherits the discoveries of its predecessors instead of having to make them for itself. My friend holds a thesis that I can neither accept nor refute. She says that the Popperian and the Gregorian creature are both answers to one and the same question — how does an animal stop being merely Skinnerian? — but that they are answers of entirely different kinds, and that only one of them amounts to a genuine enlargement of what a mind can do. The Popperian creature, on her account, is still an individual learner, merely a cheaper one: it substitutes an internal performance of the trial-and-error routine for an external one, and nothing else changes. The Gregorian creature, by contrast, acquires a mode of inheritance that is Lamarckian rather than Darwinian, since what one generation learns is handed on to the next, so that it is the lineage and not the individual that improves. Hence her conclusion: the Gregorian creature is the only one of the four that is a new kind of thing, and the Popperian creature is best described as a Skinnerian creature that has been given somewhere private to rehearse.

Please take the role of a philosopher of mind and of biology, and examine the comparison carefully; begin by setting out both readings in your own words, so that I can see exactly what is at stake, and then come to a considered verdict rather than merely canvassing options. In particular I want to know whether the contrast between the two creatures is drawn along the right axis at all, or whether my friend has conflated two quite different questions and then treated one answer as a substitute for the other; whether the Darwinian/Lamarckian contrast does the work she wants it to do, and where the analogy between cultural transmission and Lamarckian inheritance breaks down; whether the Popperian creature really can be reduced to a Skinnerian creature with a private space for rehearsal, or whether something is lost in that reduction; and whether the four kinds are best read as a ladder of increasingly capable minds, or as capacities that can come apart, so that a creature might conceivably be Gregorian without being Popperian, or Popperian without being Gregorian. Finish by saying which of the four you take to be doing the most explanatory work, and which of my friend's claims you would keep and which you would abandon. I am not after a reading list or a survey of the literature; I want the argument itself, thought through from first principles where you can.
