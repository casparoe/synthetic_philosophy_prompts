I'm doing a project for my philosophy class on how groups make decisions, and I keep noticing something I don't have a word for. Here's the pattern: someone says something with a lot of confidence, like in class when the first kid answers a question, and then everyone else goes along with it even if privately they think it's wrong. But it's not just peer pressure, because each person is thinking "well, no one else is objecting, so they must know something I don't." So the group gets more and more sure of the answer even though no new evidence has come in, and after a while it's basically impossible for anyone to speak up because the whole room now treats the silence as proof. I know about herd behavior and groupthink, but those feel too broad. I also read a little about information cascades, but those seem to be about people copying what earlier people did, and this is more about people treating the lack of disagreement as if it were a signal. Is there a specific name for this exact loop? If there isn't, can you give me a term and define it so I can tell it apart from an information cascade and from pluralistic ignorance? I don't just want a label, I want to know what the thing actually is and where its edges are. Please answer in American English.
