The reading group has been going at the sea-battle problem since about March and I definately can't tell anymore whether we're disagreeing or just using the same words for different things. Here's roughly where the four of them have landed. Ravi: the sentence about tomorrow's battle is already true or false today, we just can't know which — the future is open for us but not for truth. Mel: that's incoherent, if it's true now then it was always going to happen, so until the battle either happens or it doesn't the sentence is neither true nor false, and she's fine going three-valued. Jonah: three-valued semantics breaks excluded middle, which he won't give up, so he wants the supervaluation fix — the sentence itself is neither true nor false, but 'either there will be a battle or there won't' still comes out true even though neither disjunct is. Ines, who just joined: you're all answering the wrong question, because 'will' isn't a tense, it's a modal carrying a settledness presupposition, so the real question is when that presupposition fails, which isn't the same as asking whether the future is open.

Two things keep us circling. First, the retraction thing: if you predict a battle and there isn't one, you were wrong, you don't get to say 'oh well, I made a bad bet' — Mel treats that as data about truth, Ravi treats it as data about assertion norms, and neither can say which without assuming the answer. Second, Jonah's move of granting that there's still one privileged branch which is the actual future, which Mel says is just cheating and Jonah says is the only way to keep both excluded middle and the retraction data.

So: is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one about what 'true' is supposed to mean (truth versus determinacy), or three different questions sharing one heading — the logician's about which semantics preserves which theorems, the metaphysician's about branching, the linguist's about 'will'? My hunch, and I'd like you to either back it up or shoot it down, is that the excluded-middle fight is really a fight about what a semantic theory is for, and that the branching-time stuff is mostly decoration on top of it. If that's right, what would count as progress — a formal result, a distinction, or just admitting we're answering different questions? And if it's wrong, tell me what the real disagreement is.
