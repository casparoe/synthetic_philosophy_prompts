ok so im putting together a little thing on the weird ESP detour in Turings 1950 imitation game paper, the part where he says the statistical evidence for telepathy is overwhelming, which he clearly finds annoying, and then points out that if telepathy is real the interrogator might just read the machines mind, or the machine might read the interrogators, and so youd need a room that blocks telepathy for the test to prove anything about thinking. I want a villanelle built out of that. Two refrains, and they should be doing opposite work: one is the scientists wish that the whole thing be nonsense, the other is the grudging admission that the evidence wont let you just say that. The form should carry the argument, not just decorate it. The refrains keep coming back no matter what the stanzas try to do, which is exactly the shape of the worry: you can seal the room, soundproof it, run the game again, and the question of whether youre testing thinking or testing telepathy just walks back in. Dont let it resolve. The last two lines should put the two refrains side by side so it reads as a stalemate, not a verdict. And make the room, the interrogator, the machine, and the mind-reading all actually present as images, not just name-dropped, so someone whos read the paper recognizes the setup and someone who hasnt still gets a poem out of it. Accuracy matters more than prettiness here but it should still be a good poem.
