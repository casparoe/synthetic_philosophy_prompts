Im doing an MA in information design and I keep tripping over Otto Neurath — apparently ISOTYPE and the Vienna method of pictorial statistics are the grandparents of everything I do, and I've read enough secondhand summaries to know the slogans (pictures instead of words, the go-between who turns expert talk into pictures, statistics made legible to workers) but not enough to know what the actual position was. So here's what I want. Take Neurath's picture-language project as a serious epistemological proposal, not just a design style, and restate it as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually work in: visual variables and encodings (position, area, colour hue and value), perceptual channels and how accurately we read off each, scales and units, small multiples, the grammar-of-graphics separation of data from aesthetic mapping, redundancy and annotation, the lie factor, reading a chart versus reading a sentence, and the whole question of whether an encoding can be honest independently of what it's being used to show. Translate his claims about what a picture language can do into claims about what an encoding can carry, and keep the translation honest — don't quietly hand him the assumptions of modern dataviz and then call it a translation.

Then the part I care about most: the residue report. Which of his commitments survive the trip into my vocabulary intact, which have to be distorted to fit, and which simply have no expression there at all? I have a suspicion that a few things won't make it: the idea that a picture language is somehow free of the metaphysical muddles that infect ordinary language and therefore belongs to a unified, physicalist science; the encyclopaedia picture of knowledge as an assembly of interlocking, revisable modules with no foundation under them; the claim that choosing a pictorial symbol is itself a political and pedagogical act rather than a neutral communication decision; and whatever he thought the difference was between informing people and persuading them. For each of those, tell me whether the failure to translate is a defect of my framework — something dataviz can't even state, let alone answer — or evidence that the original claim was doing less work than it sounds like. I'd honestly rather find out that my whole field has quietly dropped a question than get a tidy one-to-one mapping, so don't sand the edges off. No citations needed, I'll chase those down myself.
