You are a philosopher of science and formal epistemologist, and I want you to help me fix the specification for a theory before I commit to defending one. The area is accuracy-first epistemology: the program that treats the accuracy of an agent's doxastic state — how close its credences come to the truth — as the fundamental epistemic good, and tries to derive norms of rationality from expected accuracy. I am not asking for the theory, and I am not asking for a survey. I am asking for the desiderata list: what any adequate theory in this area must deliver.

Start by brainstorming, for your own use, the phenomena, cases, formal pressures, and awkward examples you think the area has to accommodate; then organize the list. Do not show me the brainstorm, but let its coverage show in the result. Work carefully and be explicit where you are uncertain.

For each desideratum, give (a) the requirement stated crisply; (b) the case, puzzle, or consideration that motivates it, stated concretely rather than gestured at; and (c) whether you regard it as non-negotiable or as a preference of one camp within the program, with a sentence on who would reject it and why.

The list must cover at least these pressure points:

- The core case: credences over ordinary bivalent propositions, scored against a truth value, with the demand that the scoring be truth-directed and that it not reward credences for reasons unconnected to getting at the truth.
- The demand that the scoring rule be proper, or strictly proper, so that an agent cannot expect a better score by holding credences other than the ones it takes to be warranted, and an account of what that demand is supposed to buy.
- Whether an accuracy score can be bounded, continuous, and strictly proper all at once, and what a theory gives up if it surrenders one of the three.
- Whether the score for a whole credence function may be assembled from local scores for individual propositions, and what additivity or its denial costs.
- The gap between accuracy and evidence: accuracy scores are blind to what the agent has seen, so say what must be added to explain why an agent should hold the credences its evidence supports rather than whichever credences happen to score better.
- Credal states that are not a single probability function — imprecise, interval, or set-valued states — and what scoring a state of that kind would even mean.
- Doxastic attitudes other than credence: full belief, suspension of judgement, and question-directed attitudes. If the theory says the value of settling a question reduces to the accuracy of the resulting credences, say so and either defend or attack the reduction.
- Propositions whose truth value is unsettled or not bivalent — borderline cases, indeterminacy, future contingents — and whether accuracy is even defined for them.
- The group case: the epistemic value of a community's division of cognitive labour, where no single agent's accuracy score seems to capture what is good about the arrangement.
- The why-care demand: what the theory owes by way of an answer to someone who asks why accuracy, of all things, is the good to maximize.

Then the part I most want. Flag every subset of these desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, name which member of each pair or triple you would surrender and at what cost, and tell me which single requirement does the most work in ruling out candidate theories. Close with the one desideratum whose failure would make you abandon the accuracy-first program rather than patch it.

No citations, no reading list, and no appeal to who said what — I want the criteria and your reasoning, and I will not be checking your claims against a bibliography. Aim for the level of detail a demanding referee would want, not the breadth of a survey article.
