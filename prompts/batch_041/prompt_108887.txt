I have been reading popular science about evolution, and I keep getting stuck on a word. When a museum label says that the heart is *for* pumping blood, or that a wing is *for* flying, is that only a shorthand for "it pumps blood" and "birds fly with it," or is it a claim about what those parts were selected to do? I suspect I have been sliding between two or three different positions without noticing, and I would like to have them laid out properly. Please build me a decision tree — a taxonomy — of the positions on what it is for a biological or built structure to have a function. Each branch node should be a question I could answer about a concrete case without already knowing which position it commits me to, and each leaf should be a named position (the standard name if there is one, a descriptive label if not) with one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

To keep the tree about the cases rather than the labels, please make it sort by the following, some of which I have invented and some of which I have adapted from arguments I only half-remember: (a) a deep-sea snail whose faintly glowing patch arose by random drift, was never selected for anything, but now happens to attract mates in the dark; (b) a cave fish's eyes, which were selected for seeing in its ancestors but are now blind vestiges; (c) a creature that simply pops into existence fully formed, with a heart that pumps blood, and has no ancestors at all; (d) a bony ridge that arose only as a byproduct of how the skull develops and is now used as a display to attract mates; (e) a screwdriver, which its owner also uses to pry open paint cans; (f) a subroutine inside a self-replicating computer program that nobody designed for anything in particular but that the program needs in order to keep copying itself; and (g) a trait that was selected for one job and now reliably does a different one, where the two cannot be told apart by any observation. I am especially interested in three things the questions should pull apart: whether selection history is required for a function, whether the current contribution is ever enough on its own, and whether something can have a function it no longer performs. Where the tree yields a combination of answers that nobody defends, please say so and tell me whether that is because the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored; and where two positions cannot really be separated by any question I could answer, flag that rather than papering over it.

For what it is worth, here is where I come out, so that you can place me at a leaf: I am quite sure that the heart of the creature that popped into existence is for pumping blood, even with no history behind it, and I am equally sure that the cave fish's eyes have stopped being for seeing — yet I have no idea what to say about the glowing snail, and I suspect my two confident answers come from different theories that cannot both be right. As you go, be explicit about which of your questions I would have to answer differently to move me to a neighbouring leaf, and tell me where the whole construction is most likely to fail or to have smuggled in a distinction that does not exist. End with a single line of the form \answer{...} giving the name of the leaf my stated answers put me on, followed by nothing else.
