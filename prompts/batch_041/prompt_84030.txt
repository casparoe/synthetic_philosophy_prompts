I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I'm trying to settle a design question about an internal LLM system. We can build it as a tool AI—one request in, one bounded answer or artifact out, with no persistent goals, memory, or self-directed action—or as an agent AI that receives an objective, plans, calls tools, keeps state, and decides when it is done. I understand the rough distinction, but I want to know what actually follows from it. Please describe the most important implications of committing to the tool-AI side, especially once I add planning, memory, and self-critique. I care about three issues: how the choice changes the right way to specify objectives, which failure modes become likely or unlikely, and whether there is a principled test for when a system that still looks like a tool has quietly become an agent. I have been calling the property I want agentlessness under composition: the system may use tools, maintain a scratchpad, and even plan, but its objectives are always inherited from a human request and are never revised by the system itself. What are the most important implications of that target for architecture, evaluation, and permissioning?
