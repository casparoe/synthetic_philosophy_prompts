After thirty-five years in experimental physics I retired, and I have spent the last two of them reading philosophy with more enthusiasm than method. I keep returning to Otto Neurath — not the logical-empiricist figure he is usually filed under, but the later Neurath who wanted a rigorous language of pictures to serve the unity-of-science enterprise he spent his last decades promoting. The picture-statistics he built with his collaborators were, as I understand them, meant to be read as statements rather than ornament: a chart was to assert something and to be answerable for its truth the way a sentence is. What puzzles me is how much of the programme was left unfinished at the foundations. He seems to have held both that such pictures belong inside the same encyclopedia as verbal claims and that the encyclopedia is a heterogeneous assembly of many languages whose unity is a task rather than a given; I cannot see how both can hold of the same picture unless pictures are doing something quite different from what sentences do. And I notice that a repeated figure standing for a fixed quantity is doing arithmetic under a convention that no picture can itself depict, so the grammar of the system appears to lie outside the pictures — which makes the claim that pictures are statements look either circular or incomplete. Negation, conditionals, modality, and the difference between a claim about the world and a claim about a drawing seem to me the points where a picture language would either earn its keep or fail outright, and I have found nothing in what I have read that settles them.

I am trying to decide where to spend the next two or three years of reading and writing, and I would rather not spend them rediscovering dead ends. Suppose you were advising someone in my position: a competent amateur with a physicist's training, no formal work in logic or art history, and the patience to move slowly. Which open questions, or lines of inquiry, in this area most deserve attention, and in what order? I want a defended agenda, not a survey. For each item, explain why it is upstream of the others, whether it is tractable for someone with my background rather than requiring a specialist's command of interwar Vienna, and what evidence or argument over the next few years would move it up or down the list — including, if relevant, that a question I take to be open has in fact been settled and merely forgotten. I care more about the reasoning that fixes the order than about the order itself, and I would rather you drop something with a clear rationale than pad the list.
