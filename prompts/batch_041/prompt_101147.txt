I am writing to seek your advice on a decision that has become oddly paralysing, and I suspect the difficulty is philosophical rather than practical. I am a parent in a medium-sized town, and I must submit a strict ranked list of six secondary schools for my daughter by Friday; no ties are permitted. The schools differ along only two dimensions: journey time from our home and average class size. The figures are approximately as follows. School A: 15 minutes, 32 pupils per class. School B: 20 minutes, 30 pupils. School C: 25 minutes, 28 pupils. School D: 30 minutes, 26 pupils. School E: 35 minutes, 24 pupils. School F: 40 minutes, 22 pupils. When I compare any two adjacent schools, I find the trade-off straightforward: the next school is a little further away, but its classes are a little smaller, and I judge the next school to be better overall. So I judge B better than A, C better than B, D better than C, E better than D, and F better than E. But when I compare F with A directly, I judge A to be better: a 25-minute longer journey each way is not worth a reduction of ten pupils per class. I have checked repeatedly, and I am not changing my mind about any of these pairwise judgments. I am not applying different criteria in different comparisons; it is the same two criteria throughout. Nor do I find any pair impossible to compare; each pair seems perfectly orderable on its own. The trouble is that my rankings run in a circle, and the form demands a single consistent order.

I need to decide what to do. Should I treat one of my pairwise judgments as mistaken, and if so, which one and on what basis? Is there a rational procedure for producing a ranking when my judgments have this structure? Or does the situation reveal that the notion of one option being better than another is less well-behaved than I assumed? I would be grateful for a concrete recommendation about how to fill in the form, together with an explanation of what is going wrong in my reasoning. I am happy with a technical answer; I simply need one that I can act on by Friday.
