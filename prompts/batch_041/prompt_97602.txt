Okay so I'm in debate club and we've been doing "why are some countries rich and others poor" and there's a move people keep pulling that bugs me and I want to know if it has a name. Here's the move. Someone says country A is rich because of its culture — hardworking, saves, trusts people, whatever. You ask how they know the culture is like that or that it's the cause, and the answer is basically "well look how rich they are." Then flip it: country B is poor because of its institutions, and how do we know the institutions are bad? Because the country is poor. So the same fact is doing dubble duty, it's the cause and also the only proof of the cause, and there's no way it could ever come out false. I get that "correlation isn't causation" and I've heard of just-so stories, but those feel like a different thing — what bugs me is that the factor they name (culture, institutions) seems to be just the leftover, the part they couldn't explain with geography or whatever, and then they treat that leftover like it's a real force pushing on things. Is there a name for this specific move of naming the unexplained remainder and then pointing at it as the explanation? If there isn't, can you coin one and tell me exactly where the line is between it and plain circular reasoning, and between it and a question that's genuinely open but we just can't test yet? I don't just want a label, I want to know what the thing actually is and what it isn't.
