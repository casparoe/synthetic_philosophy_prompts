I've fallen down a rabbit hole on voting systems over the past year — I'm not an academic, I do the books for a small landscaping company, but I'm in the local chapter of a group that's trying to get ranked choice voting passed in our city and I've spent alot of late nights reading about this stuff on my own (wikipedia, a few youtube explainers, a long reddit argument). Next month I've got twenty minutes on the agenda at our chapter meeting and I want to use it to say something I know is going to annoy people: that our whole pitch is a lie. We go around telling voters that ranked choice is the fair system, that it kills the spoiler problem, that you can vote your conscience and never waste your vote — and I've come around to thinking Arrow's impossibility theorem means there is no fair system at all, that every alternative we sneer at (approval, score, whatever) has its own hole in it, and that we should just be honest and sell RCV on one narrow thing only: third parties stop being spoilers, and it's easy to explain at a doorstep. If somebody asks whether that's fair, we should say fair isn't on the menu. Someone in the group chat also mentioned there's another theorem, Gibbard-something, that says any voting system can be gamed — I don't fully get it but I think it's on my side. And for a laugh I ran a ranked choice poll of our own members last week about where to hold the summer picnic, and the option that won would of lost a straight head-to-head against every single other option, which I think proves my point but which people are going to wave away as a fluke of one silly poll.

The room is mostly volunteers. There's a retired civics teacher, a guy from the city planning office who's actually decent with numbers and will probably poke holes in my poll, three or four people from the county Democratic party who only care whether this helps or hurts them next cycle, and two true believers who've been at this for a decade and honestly think ranked choice is the cure for polarization. Nobody there has a philosophy or economics background and half of them tune out the second numbers come up. Can you prep me for the Q&A the way it will actually go — the objections I'm really going to get from these specific people, not the ones I'd get from a poli sci seminar, ordered by how much damage each one does to me, each phrased in the words that person would actually use, with a two minute answer and an honest note on where my answer is soft? Tell me what I should just concede up front instead of fighting, which objection I should genuinely be scared of (and whether any of them means I should drop the whole idea), and the one question I'm really hoping nobody in that room thinks to ask.
