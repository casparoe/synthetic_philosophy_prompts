I'm a backend engineer with no formal philosophy background, so sorry in advance if this is a thing that already has a whole literature I somehow never ran into. There's a pattern I keep seeing and I can't find a word for it, and it's bugging me enough that I want someone to actually carve it up for me rather than just hand me a label.

Two places I keep running into it. First, at work, when a team picks a technology. The way it actually goes is that everyone picks it because everyone else is picking it — nobody ran the benchmarks, nobody did the real evaluation, they just took the crowd as a stand-in for the evaluation. And the one person who did have doubts said nothing, so from the inside the room looked unanimous, and that fake unanimity is what the next person went on. Six months later nobody remembers why we chose it, and the official reason has quietly become "well, it's what everyone uses," which is the same reason we started with, now doing all the work by itself. Second, more or less the same shape, our deploy process: it's this way because it's always been this way, and every new hire assumes someone upstream had a good reason, and nobody ever checks, so the assumption gets passed down and gets stronger each time it's passed down.

The reason I don't think it's just "people are sheep" is the silence part. The people in the middle aren't copying a decision they can actually see the reasons for — they're reading the absence of pushback as if it were evidence. No one objecting looks like no one objecting for a reason, and that gets treated like information, and then the thing feeds on itself. There's also something about the direction it runs in: it's really hard to get started, but once it's going it's almost impossible to reverse, because reversing means being the first one to speak up against something that now looks unanimous — and the longer it goes, the more unanimous it looks.

I've had people tell me this is just information cascades, or herd behavior, or groupthink, and none of those quite fits what I'm pointing at. Cascades, the way it's been described to me, are supposed to be about people doing a genuine update on real signals, and they're supposed to be fragile — a little new information topples them. Here nobody's updating on much of anything, and new information doesn't topple it at all. Groupthink seems to be about a tight group in one room talking itself into something, whereas this happens across hundreds of people who never meet and often don't even like each other. And herd behavior is too loose to be useful; it covers everything and explains nothing.

So what I want is this: is there an established name for this exact thing, and if there's a family of near-misses, what are the closest ones and where's the line between each of them and this? What is this phenomenon, precisely — what's inside it and what's outside it — and what would have to change for it to count as a normal, healthy consensus that people genuinely did evaluate? If it doesn't have a name, definately coin one, and give me a definition plus a couple of edge cases it should and shouldn't cover. I honestly don't care about the label; I care about the carving.

Write it as plain prose, please — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. Just paragraphs, like you're explaining it to me over email. I read alot of docs at work and I'd like this one to feel like a person wrote it.
