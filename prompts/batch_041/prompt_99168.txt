I work on the data side of an alignment team — we collect a ton of pairwise comparisons from crowdworkers, fit one reward model on top of the pile, and when annotators disagree we basically treat it as label noise to be averaged away. Lately it's been bugging me what we're quietly assuming. If disagreement is just "noise," we're assuming there's one true preference ordering that the noise obscures — but a lot of the disagreements we see look like genuine value conflicts, not mistakes or sloppiness. So what does this pipeline presuppose about values? That individual preferences are commensurable and comparable across people, that there's a single thing — "the values of the users" — that can be maximized, that aggregation is legitimate in the first place? Which of those does the design strictly need versus just happen to fit, and where is it underdetermined — could a liberal-neutrality view, or a pluralist one that refuses to aggregate at all, license something like the same pipeline for other reasons? And if we actually took neutrality seriously, what would have to change in how we collect and combine the data? Be honest about what's settled versus genuinely contested among people who know this area — dont hand me one camp's position dressed up as the consensus.
