I'm a TA for a second-year philosophy of science course and I'm putting together a one-page handout: a decision tree on incommensurability that students can work through to figure out which position on paradigm change they actually hold. Every term I get back says something like Kuhn thinks paradigms are incommensurable and then just stops, so I want the tree to force the distinctions apart. Start the student at a commitment nobody in the class disputes -- that there have been episodes you could call scientific revolutions, e.g. phlogiston to oxygen, caloric to kinetic theory, Newtonian mechanics to relativity -- and then make every node a two-way question they can answer about one of those concrete cases without already knowing which label it leads to. No node should just be do you think paradigms are commensurable. Branch on things like: whether the meaning of a term is fixed by the whole theory it lives in, or whether the term can keep its referent across the change; whether there's any observation report both sides would accept on the same terms; whether the standards for a good theory -- accuracy, simplicity, fruitfulness, whatever -- are themselves paradigm-internal or shared; whether a switch between paradigms is more like a gestalt flip or more like a translation; and whether the claim that scientists in different paradigms work in different worlds is meant literally or is just a vivid way of talking about changed meanings.

I want the leaves to include, at minimum: radical/global incommensurability of the Feyerabend-ish sort, where there's no common measure at all and theory choice comes out non-rational; Kuhn's own more moderate line, where comparison is possible but never neutral; the later-Kuhn distinction between translating a theory and learning to use it like a bilingual speaker; a semantic-holism-without-incomparability view where meanings shift but reference survives; the Davidson-style deflationary line that the whole idea of a self-contained conceptual scheme is incoherent, so there's nothing for incommensurability to be; a Lakatos-style research-program view where rival programs are ranked by whether they're progressing or degenerating; and a quietist/instrumentalist option where incommensurability is a non-issue because theories are tools rather than pictures. If you can fit it, add a leaf for someone who thinks scientific incommensurabilty is the same phenomenon as value incommensurability in ethics (Berlin/Williams style) and say what has to be true for that analogy to hold.

Build the tree from the logical space -- the questions, and which combinations of answers are even coherent -- and then place the named positions inside it, rather than starting from a list of names. Where a combination of answers is empty, tell me whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Flag any two leaves the tree can't actually separate. Aim for 8-11 nodes and 7-9 leaves, and keep each node phrased as something a student could answer about one of those historical cases without having read a word of Kuhn.
