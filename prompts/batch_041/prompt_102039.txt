She's a formal epistemologist by training but has gotten really into the epistemic-democracy stuff — the idea that democratic decisions track the truth because of something like the Condorcet jury theorem: if every voter is independently better than a coin flip, the majority's probabilty of being right goes to one as you add more voters. I kept pressing her that the whole thing is basically a toy. She pushed back that the interesting work is exactly in relaxing those assumptions: heterogeneous competences, correlated signals with a shared component, endogenous competence where deliberation shifts the competence parameter up or down, jury-theorem analogs for non-binary or continuous choices, and the whole gap between aggregating judgments versus aggregating preferences. We ended up disagreeing about which of those actually matters, and I don't think either of us was arguing in good faith by the end.
