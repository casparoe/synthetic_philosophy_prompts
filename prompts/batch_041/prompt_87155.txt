Write a dialogue between two epistemologists who disagree about how to update credences when the new information is a conditional probability that is itself uncertain. The dialogue should be set in a variant of the Judy Benjamin problem.

The scenario: You are a paratrooper who has been dropped into a territory divided into Red and Blue, each of which is divided into Headquarters and Second Company. You initially have equal credence in all four cells. A duty officer, whose reliability you have reason to trust but not to treat as infallible, tells you: "I am 70% confident that if you are in Red, you are in Headquarters." You have no other information. The question is how you should update your credences in the four cells.

The two epistemologists:

- The first holds that the officer's report is evidence about the officer's credence, not directly about the world. To update rationally, you must conditionalize on the proposition that the officer has that credence, which requires you to model how the officer's credence depends on the state of the world. The first epistemologist argues that this is the only principled approach, because it treats the report as an ordinary piece of evidence and uses the standard rule of conditionalization.

- The second holds that the report is best understood as a constraint on your posterior credence in the conditional. You should update by minimizing relative entropy from your prior subject to the constraint that your posterior credence in "if Red then Headquarters" equals 0.7. The second epistemologist argues that this is the natural generalization of conditionalization, because it respects the information actually contained in the report without requiring an arbitrary model of the officer's reliability.

The dialogue should contain real back-and-forth: each character should respond to the other's actual points, not talk past each other. Both should be equally competent; neither should be a strawman. They should discuss concrete numerical implications where possible, and they should address what happens if the officer's reliability is unknown, or if the report is about a conditional whose antecedent is not something you can directly conditionalize on. They should also consider whether the two approaches can be reconciled, and if not, what the deepest disagreement is.

After the dialogue, provide a JSON object with the following fields:

- "scenario": a one-sentence summary of the scenario.
- "view_a_summary": a two-sentence summary of the first epistemologist's position.
- "view_b_summary": a two-sentence summary of the second epistemologist's position.
- "key_disagreement": a one-sentence statement of the deepest point of disagreement.
- "proposed_update_a": the first epistemologist's recommended posterior credences in the four cells, if determinable, or a statement of what additional information is needed.
- "proposed_update_b": the second epistemologist's recommended posterior credences in the four cells, if determinable, or a statement of what additional information is needed.
- "crucial_assumption_a": the assumption on which the first epistemologist's recommendation depends.
- "crucial_assumption_b": the assumption on which the second epistemologist's recommendation depends.
- "unresolved_issue": the most important issue the dialogue leaves unresolved.
