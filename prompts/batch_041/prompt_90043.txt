I keep encountering two phrases in arguments about how far evolutionary explanation can reach, and both get deployed as though they settled the matter. The context is a discussion group I belong to on the evolution of mind, so they are usually applied to language, morality, and consciousness. The first is 'cranes, not skyhooks'. As the image has been explained to me: a crane lifts by pushing off something that is already there and builds upward in stages, whereas a skyhook is a hook hanging in the sky — something that lifts without resting on anything already in place, a free lunch. The second is 'greedy reductionism', which is applied to claims to the effect that morality is nothing but kin selection, or that love is nothing but a chemical reaction; I have also heard it glossed not as the error of reducing too much but as the error of trying to get the explanatory work done without any cranes at all. In one exchange I sat through, the crane line was used to insist that some trait required no explanatory ingredient beyond ordinary selection; in another, a proposal was called greedy reductionism and the discussion stopped there.

What I want is a map rather than a defence. Lay out the distinct precise claims each phrase could be making, ordered from the trivial to the radical — for the crane line, from the modest preference for explanations that do not posit unexplained explainers up to any blanket prohibition on fundamental posits or primitives; for 'greedy reductionism', from mere impatience with detail, through claims about what a lower-level account can predict, to outright elimination of the higher-level phenomenon. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, an empirical claim that could be tested, or something false or confused, and say which reading is actually in play when the phrase is used to end a discussion. I would also like to know whether the two phrases are mirror images of one another, what work the word 'greedy' does beyond expressing disapproval of the level of description someone chose, and whether anyone can consistently wield both.

Please push back if the request is misframed. If one of these phrases does not really admit of several readings — if it is one thesis buried under a great deal of loose usage — say so instead of padding the menu to match the other, and tell me plainly which parts of what I have asked for cannot be delivered. Where your verdicts are contestable, mark them as contestable rather than asserting them flatly.
