I'm not a philosopher, I just read a lot and I've gotten wierdly into this thing I keep seeing called "the paradox of deontology," and now I've somehow volunteered to run a session on it at my book club — eight people, plenty smart, no philosophy training at all, and one guy who will definately bring up Kant and get him slightly wrong. Fifty minutes, a whiteboard, they're coming in cold, no reading assigned.

Don't explain the paradox to me, I think I've got it well enough. Tell me how to teach it. What I want:

- The order the ideas have to go in, and why that order and not another — what has to already be in their heads before the paradox can land as a paradox, rather than as "philosophers disagree, how shocking."
- The one example to build the whole thing on. My instinct is to stay away from the trolley / organ-harvest stuff, both because half of them will have heard it and because it smuggles in the wrong thing — but tell me if I'm wrong about that. And tell me what the example needs to have and what it needs to lack to actually carry the paradox instead of just illustrating it. (The version that seems sharpest to me is a constraint against the very act it's supposed to prevent — like, I may not lie even to stop five lies, so my honesty rule ends up producing more lies than it stops. Is that the right shape? Why or why not?)
- The misconceptions they'll walk in with or form along the way, and exactly where in the sequence I should kill each one. My guesses: that "deontology" just means rule-following, or religion; that the paradox is a knock-down refutation rather than a puzzle; that the deontologist is being selfish, keeping their own hands clean while other people get hurt; and that the whole thing is just about numbers — i.e. they'll hear "the numbers don't matter" when what they need to hear is "the numbers don't add up the way you're assuming."
- One question to ask at the end that would actually tell me whether the paradox landed, as opposed to whether they can parrot the setup back at me.
- What I should openly say I'm not going to cover at this level. I'd rather flag a gap out loud than fake my way through it.

Also a quick thing: is the "paradox" one thing, or am I lumping together a couple of different complaints that people all give the same name? If it's really two or three, tell me which one to teach and what to do with the others in a session this short.
