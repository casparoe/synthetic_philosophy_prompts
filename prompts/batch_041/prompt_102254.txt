Hey, I'm a phd student putting together discussion cases for a population-ethics unit and I'm sick of the stock repugnant-conclusion example that every textbook (and every model) has memorized, so I want you to build me a case to order instead.

Set up three views like this:
- total utilitarianism: value of a population = the sum of everyone's welfare.
- average utilitarianism: value = the mean welfare.
- critical-level utilitarianism: value = the sum over people of (their welfare minus a fixed critical level c), so a life below c drags the total down even if that life is worth living.

Plus a fourth I'll spell out so we're aligned — call it lexical totalism. Fix a threshold H. Rank populations first by how many people they contain with welfare above H; more is strictly better, and no finite number of lives below H can outweigh a single life above H. Only when two populations tie on that count do you fall back on total welfare to break the tie (say whether you're totaling everyone or only the below-H lives).

Now build me the case, with concrete integer welfare levels and small finite populations. I need three things:
1. A base scenario where total utilitarianism ranks a big population of barely-worth-living lives above a small population of very happy lives, while average and critical-level both rank the small happy population above the big one. The barely-worth-living lives have to be clearly positive — better than nonexistence — so it's a real repugnant conclusion and not you sneaking in negative welfare.
2. A nearby tweak of that scenario where average and critical-level come apart, so it's clear they arent secretly the same view.
3. A nearby tweak where lexical totalism comes apart from critical-level.

Constraints: finite populations, integers, no infinities, no probabilities, and leave out the non-identity problem and all the metaphysics of comparing existent vs nonexistent people — I just want the axiology doing the work. A few populations per case is plenty.

Then actually do the analysis: for each case give every view's verdict and why, name the one feature that's doing the work in each disagreement, say what happens to the disagreement if you remove that feature, and tell me how a total utilitarian (or whoever comes out worst in a given case) would push back that the case is rigged. The whole point is for me to show students that the verdicts fall out of the axioms rather than being stipulated, so a case that only 'works' because you wrote the conclusion into it is a fail.

No citations, I don't care who said what, I just want the cases and the reasoning.
