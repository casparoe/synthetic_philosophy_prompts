I'm on the board of a small food co-op, about 200 member-owners, and we're in the middle of something ugly. Our treasurer, whose been with us for years, siphoned off around $40k over two years — lots of small amounts nobody noticed until the audit. Now we have to decide what happens to him, and the board has split in a way that maps almost too neatly onto a philosophy seminar I sat in on once. One faction says the point is that he did something wrong and has to get what he deserves, full stop — they'd expel him and hand it to the police even if that made it less likely anyone ever steals from us again. The other faction says the only sane question is which response produces the best consequences — restitution, lower recidivism, other members not feeling like chumps — and they'd happily go easy on him if that worked better. A third person, our secretary, keeps pushing a compromise she came up with herself and calls a "desert floor, deterrence ceiling": whatever we do has to be at least as harsh as he genuinely deserves, we're allowed to go harsher than that if harsher is needed to keep the next treasurer honest, and we're never allowed to go harsher than that. Nobody on the board can quite say whether that's a real position or just a fudge.

What I want from you is the most important implications of the retributivist-vs-consequentialist debate for how we should actually handle this case, and for what kind of disciplinary policy we should write down for the co-op going forward. Please don't just recap the two theories — I've read enough of that. Tell me what follows for us. And I'd like you to include the question of whether a co-op like ours is even the kind of thing that's entitled to do retribution in the first place, as opposed to just protecting itself and its members. Give it to me as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one a sentence or two, no intro and no closing paragraph.
