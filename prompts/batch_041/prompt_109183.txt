I am a doctoral candidate working at the intersection of philosophy of mind and the ethics of artificial agents, and I am trying to get clear on a tension internal to Dennett's late polemic against what he calls counterfeit people. In that essay he treats AI systems that can pass as human as uniquely dangerous artifacts, describes their production as a kind of vandalism against the trust on which society depends, and proposes watermarking plus severe liability for the companies that make or spread them. Yet the same author spent his career arguing that adopting the intentional stance toward a system is a predictive strategy rather than the discovery of inner Cartesian furniture, and that there are free-floating rationales—reasons that can be evaluated as better or worse even when no one is entertaining them. On that view, whether something is a believer, or a person, looks like a matter of whether the stance yields real patterns and supports successful prediction, not a matter of hidden essence or provenance. So I want a careful explanation of the conceptual issue: how can Dennett's deflationary account of the mental, and his account of personhood as something like a matter of reciprocity and of being held to account within a moral community, underwrite the sharp normative line his essay draws between genuine and counterfeit people? Please distinguish at least three candidate readings of counterfeitness—(a) counterfeit as failed functional imitation, (b) counterfeit as functional success with the wrong causal or social etiology, and (c) counterfeit as a relational or use-based property, where nothing is intrinsically counterfeit and the wrong lies in a deceiving institution—and say which, if any, is consistent with Dennett's own commitments. Then explain whether AI systems fail the conditions of personhood in principle or only contingently, given current social arrangements and current architectures. Finally, address the objection that the essay's real subject is the harm of deception and eroded trust rather than any metaphysical fact about the artifacts, and say whether that makes the counterfeit label misleading or whether it names a distinct wrong worth marking. I am not looking for a literature review or citations; I want the conceptual anatomy of the position and a verdict on whether it is internally stable.
