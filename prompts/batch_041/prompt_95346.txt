I've been sat on the safety review group at a mid-sized lab for about a year now — the sort of committee that has to actually sign off before we ship, not the one that writes the principles. We meet Thursday to decide whether to approve the next deployment of a control-style setup: our strongest model runs behind a weaker, trusted monitor that reads its reasoning trace and flags anything suspicious for a human, and we only ever act on what gets flagged. Red-teaming reckons the monitor catches maybe two thirds of the sabotage attempts our own red team dreams up, and the control people are upfront that the guarantee is conditional on that attack distribution being the right one — which is awkward, since the same four people write both the attacks and the monitor spec.

The bit I genuinely can't get straight: half the group thinks building the whole stack around "the model might be scheming against us" is a self-fulfilling prophecy — it makes the misalignment story load-bearing, quietly crowds out the alignment work you'd need if it were true, and hands you a number that reads like a guarantee but isn't one. The other half says alignment is the unfalsifiable side of the shop and control at least gives you something measurable this quarter.

Please draft the opinion I'll circulate Wednesday night. I want a concrete recommendation — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with a time frame attached, and I want the strongest version of the losing side in there properly rather than strawmanned, because I'll be sat across from those people. If there's a way to say what would actually change our minds over the next couple of quarters, put that in. Assume nobody reading it has followed the control literature closely, and don't send me off to papers.
