"The alignment problem splits into two. Value alignment is the big scary one: making sure an AI actually cares about the right things, i.e. human values, so it does good stuff instead of turning everything into paperclips. Intent alignment is the smaller, more technical one: making sure the AI does what you told it to do. The whole point of the distinction is that intent alignment is the easy part. You can solve it completely and still get catastrophically burned, because a superintelligence that perfectly carries out its designers' intent will still wreck the world if that intent was bad or incomplete. So the serious people say the real problem is value alignment, and intent alignment is basically a distraction. And really intent alignment is just obedience — it's the 'make the genie do what you say' problem, and honestly since we can already get models to follow instructions pretty well with RLHF, the narrow version of the problem is more or less solved. The interesting part is that the two come apart: you can have perfect intent alignment with zero value alignment, and that's exactly the scary scenario."
