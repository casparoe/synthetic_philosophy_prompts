I'm defending a version of the old Malcolm/Dennett line that dreams aren't experiences, but with a wrinkle I havent seen anyone put quite this way and I'm scared it'll get shredded. Two moves. First, I split the dream *content* from the *episode of experiencing* it: the content is perfectly real, it's caused by what the brain is doing at night, it's probe-able, it tracks REM and pre-sleep stimuli and all that — but the episode of experiencing that content is an artifact of the waking report. The report doesn't report a perception, it constructs one, and we then mistake the construction for a memory of an experience. My analogy is that it's like asking whether the past is experienced: the content can be about a real event without there being any episode of experiencing it at the time.
