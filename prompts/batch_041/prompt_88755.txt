Ok so I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training past one undergrad ethics class, and a friend from a history discord has talked me into a corner. I'd like to know where the escape hatch is.

The argument is his, but I've cleaned it up into numbered steps so I can argue with him about it:

1. For a civilization to fall, it has to stop existing. Not change shape. Stop.
2. When the western empire's central government came apart in the fifth century, most of the rest kept running: alot of the same people in a lot of the same towns (fewer of them, sure), Latin slowly turning into French and Spanish and Italian, Roman law still being used, the church still organized roughly the way the empire had organized itself, roads and aqueducts still in use, just worse maintained.
3. If the people, the language, the law and the institutions all carry on and develop, then the civilization didn't stop existing. It changed shape.
4. So the west didn't fall, it got refactored. What we call the fall is really historians preferring one shape (one empire, one emperor) over the shapes that replaced it -- like preferring a monolith over a swarm of microservices.
5. That preference is a fact about the historian, not a fact about the world.
6. So "the western Roman Empire fell" isn't a fact about the world either.

If 6 is true then I think the whole idea of collapse falls apart (ha). Nothing falls, ever. The USSR didn't collapse, it just reorganized into fifteen countries that kept speaking Russian. The Maya didn't collapse. Whatever happened around 1200 BC in the eastern Mediterranean didn't happen. A startup that gets acquihired and shuts down next quarter didn't die, it "transitioned." And his line is that all the usual explanations -- lead in the water, drought, plague, the Huns showing up at the border -- are answers to a question that doesn't exist.

I have two problems. One is that I don't believe 6, and the other is that I can't find the bad step. Every single one of 1 through 5 looks fine when I read it by itself. And I don't want to be the guy whose whole objection is "come on, everyone knows Rome fell" -- especially since the refactoring point does seem to capture something real that the old barbarians-at-the-gate story misses.

So: which step do I give up, and why did it look so reasonable before I noticed? Then tell me what giving it up costs me. If I drop 1 or 3, am I stuck saying the people who lived through it were just wrong about their own world ending, or that saying the Maya "collapsed" is me being a snob about centralized states? If I drop 5, what exactly am I claiming about the world that isn't just a preference? And if I have to pick, I'd rather keep 2 and the refactoring bit then keep 6, so price that out for me.
