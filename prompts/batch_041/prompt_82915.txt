I write about science and ideas for a living, so I'm used to not having a word for things — but this one's been nagging me for about two years and i'd like to know if it has a name, or, if not, what you'd call it.

The pattern. I do a lot of pieces that touch on the individualism vs collectivism argument, East Asia against the West, and there's a specific move that keeps happening. I'll describe someone — say a woman in Osaka who turns down a post in Berlin partly because of how it would sit with her parents — and she'll tell me, unprompted, that it isn't that they'd forbid it, it's that she'd feel she'd done something wrong to them, and that she'd have felt that way even if they'd never found out. Now watch the two readings.

Reading one: this is a relationship doing what relationships do. The obligation isn't a weight sitting on top of her; it's part of who she is, and the fact that she owns it, that she'd say it's hers, is the whole point. Call it the thick reading.

Reading two: this is exactly the pathology. She was raised inside it so early that she can't tell a value she holds from a pressure she's absorbed, and the fact that she says she's fine with it is not evidence of anything except how deep it goes. Call it the thin reading.

Here's what actually interests me, and it isn't the East/West thing itself. Both readings cite the same evidence — her own account of herself — and for one it's the best evidence there could be, while for the other it's automatically inadmissible, and there's nothing else in the world you could point at that would seperate them. She can't settle it by saying she means it, because saying she means it is the very thing in dispute. And notice the mirror case: a kid in Sheffield who stays put to look after her dad and calls it her own choice gets taken at her word by exactly the people who won't take the Osaka woman at hers, which makes me suspect the thing I'm after is about the observer and not about the culture.

I know the neighbourhood. There's the work on preferences that quietly reshape themselves around what's actually available to you, and the work on whether autonomy is something a person has on her own or something that only exists between people. Neither is quite it, or at least neither is quite the bit that bugs me. What I want is a name for the observer's bind: the situation in which a person's own endorsement of a relational obligation is at once the only evidence in the case and structurally incapable of deciding it — because the coercion reading can absorb any endorsement as a symptom, while the authenticity reading treats that same endorsement as proof. If there's no term, give me one, and make the definition earn its keep.

So: is it different when the pressure is loud and visible rather than internal? What if the person has, once, actually gone against the group and then come back — is that evidence for the thick reading, or just more evidence that she can't leave? What about obligations to the dead, where the other party can't be consulted at all? And what is the nearest thing that already has a name, and why doesn't it fit — because if the answer is 'that's just adaptive preferences' or 'that's just false consciousness' or 'that's just socialisation, everyone has it', I want to see why the version I'm describing is or isn't a special case of each. Skip the reading list, I can't look anything up where I file from. British English, please.
