Here's what we actually did in class, so you know the level. The main argument in the 白馬論 goes roughly: "horse" is what we use to name shape, "white" is what we use to name color, and what names color isn't what names shape, so a white horse isn't a horse. Then there's the other strand: if someone wants a horse, a yellow or black one will do, but if they want a white horse it won't, so "horse" and "white horse" don't pick out the same thing. And then the 堅白 stuff with the white stone, where hardness and whiteness look separable. The prof keeps saying the real crux is whether 白馬非馬 is a claim about words or about things, and whether the "seeking a horse" argument is the thing that commits the equivocation everyone accuses it of, or the thing that rescues the whole thesis.
