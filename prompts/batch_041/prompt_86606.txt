I've been in a small reading group on decision theory and we keep coming back to the absent-minded driver problem — you know, the one where you're driving home, there are two exits, at the first exit you get nothing if you take it, at the second you get a decent payoff, and if you keep going past both you get a small payoff, but the whole gimmick is that you can't tell the two junctions apart and can't remember whether you've already passed one. We've spent three sessions arguing about whether the planning-optimal thing (randomising at each junction with some probability, which gives you the best expected payoff from the armchair) and the action-optimal thing (what you'd actually do if you found yourself at a junction, knowing only that you're at some junction) can be reconciled, and whether the fact that they come apart means something deep about rationality or just something about how we model imperfect recall. Half the group thinks it's a cute puzzle with no bearing on anything, the other half thinks it's a counterexample to standard decision theory. I'm definately somewhere in the middle and I want to write a term paper that actually goes somewhere.

So: if you had a term — say ten weeks, maybe 15-20 hours a week — to work on the absent-minded driver and its neighbourhood, which open questions or lines of work would you prioritise, and in what order? I don't want a reading list or a survey of who said what. I want a defended agenda: for each item, why it's upstream of the others (or not), how tractable it is for someone with a philosophy background and decent but not expert maths, how neglected it is, and what would move it up or down your list — like, what result or argument over the next few months would make you say, right, that one's settled, move on. I'm especially interested in whether the interesting action is in the foundations of decision theory itself (dynamic consistency, the distinction between planning and action optimality, subjective versus objective expected utility) or in the connections to other imperfect-recall puzzles and to things like updatelessness and evidential decision theory, or in whether the problem says anything about agency over time and the unity of the agent — or whether the really important question is something I haven't listed. Please be honest about which of those are dead ends. British English please.
