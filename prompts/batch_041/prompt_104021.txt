So I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy or econ degree, and I've been reading about rent control and the minimum wage on and off for a couple years now -- mostly because my city keeps arguing about both and every thread about it feels like two groups talking past each other. My friends and I do a little zine and I want to put a poem about it in the next one, so I'd like you to write it.

Here's the thing I want the poem to be built on. A rent ceiling and a wage floor are, as far as the graph goes, the same move from opposite sides. One is a legal lid under the market price, the other a legal floor over it. In both cases the price that would clear the market is not allowed to be the price, and in both cases you get the same shape of gap, just showing up on different sides of the ledger: at the controlled rent, more people want apartments than there are apartments, and the gap comes out as queues and favors and landlords picking tenants for reasons that aren't money, places going soft because nobody's incentive to maintain them survived the lid; at the mandated wage, more people want jobs than there are jobs, and the gap comes out as fewer hours, harder hiring bars, work sliding off the books. And in both cases whoever did it had a decent reason: the person on the weak end of the trade shouldn't have to take whatever the market hands them.

So: 20 lines, two mirrored halves, 10 and 10. First half rent control, second half minimum wage. Hard rules: line 10 has to be line 11, and line 20 has to be line 1, so the whole thing loops -- because that's honestly how these arguments feel, they never close. The mirroring has to be real, meaning the second half should read as the first half flipped over the equilibrium, same shape, opposite sign, not just "and now here's the other policy." Somewhere in each half give me one concrete image you can actually see, not the deadweight-loss triangle (I keep thinking about a hallway nobody's painted since the lid went on, and a job posting that's technically full time and 32 hours).

And don't let it turn into a free market sermon. The ethics need to be in there -- the whole point of the lid and the floor is that the market price might just be a lousy price, and the poem should sit inside the fact that we don't really know how to settle that, and that the minimum wage studies seem to fight with each other anyway. Leave the tension open, don't hand me a verdict.

No economist names, no papers, no citations, I'm not writing a term paper and half of it I couldn't source anyway. Just make it right enough that someone who knows the econ doesn't wince, and good enough that someone who doesn't still gets why the two halves are the same poem.
