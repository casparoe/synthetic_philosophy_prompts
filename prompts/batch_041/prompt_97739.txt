I teach political philosophy and a colleague in my department keeps pushing this scheme she calls the 'algorithmic veil': when we set the rules for AI training data and who gets paid for it, we should imagine we don't know whether we'll end up as a data producer, a model owner, or just someone whose life gets inferred from the data. She insists Rawls would have been all for it. I'm not so sure. What would Rawls actually have made of this? Would the difference principle even apply to something like data dividends, or does the whole thing break because the parties behind the veil aren't choosing the basic structure of a closed society? And what would he say about the fact that the 'data producers' include people who never consented and maybe aren't even born yet? I want to know whether this is a natural extension of his view or a misuse, and exactly where the analogy to the original position starts to strain. Also, would he treat this as a seperate question from justice as fairness or just another application of it?
