We did Popper on evolution in my phil of science reading group last week and two people went at each other for like forty minutes and I honestly couldnt referee it. Can you?

Heres roughly how it went.

Ravi: Popper showed that Darwinism isn't a scientific theory, it's a metaphysical research program. Take survival of the fittest. Whoever survives is by definition the fittest, so the claim goes in a circle, and no possible observation could refute it. A theory that can't fail isn't a theory.

Marta: Thats the cartoon version. For one thing Popper himself backed off — a few years after that essay he said he'd changed his mind and that natural selection could be tested after all. And the circle charge only lands on the lazy formulation. State it properly — heritable variation plus differential reproduction in a specified environment — and it makes predictions. This variant should spread here and not there. We should see intermediates. We shouldn't see acquired characters being inherited. Those can fail.

Ravi: Retractions under social pressure aren't arguments. And look at what you just listed. The intermediates and the nested hierarchy come from common descent plus geology, not from selection. You're giving selection credit for the whole Darwinian package. As for the rest, give me one observation that would make you say selection itself is false, not just that this or that adaptation story is wrong. Because whatever you name, there's an auxiliary move sitting there. If a trait looks costly, the answer is that it must pay off somewhere we haven't measured, or that drift is masking it. The framework guarantees that a selection story will eventually be found. That's not a theory, that's a research program, which is exactly what Popper said the first time.

Marta: The demand for one decisive observation that kills the bare core with no auxiliaries attached is impossible for any theory — Newtonian mechanics, atomism, you name it. You always test a package. That's basically what Lakatos built his whole hard core / protective belt thing around, and it's a fair complaint against Popper. And there are real falsifiers here. If heritable variation turned out to be irrelevant to reproductive success, or if complex functional structure kept turning up with no incremental path and no other explanation, selectionism would be in trouble. It forbids things.

Ravi: No other explanation is doing all the work in that sentence, and that clause can absorb anything. So can drift is masking it. We're back to a program that can always be saved.
