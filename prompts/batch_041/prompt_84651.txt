I am a third-year PhD student in philosophy working mainly on formal epistemology, and I need a two-page research proposal for a departmental workshop on logic and epistemology. It will be circulated in advance and used to assign me a commentator, so it has to be self-contained and to state its question precisely enough that a reader can see what would settle it. My half-formed idea is this. Discussions of logical omniscience generally treat it as one defect of possible-worlds semantics: agents end up knowing every logical truth and every consequence of what they know. I suspect this lumps together at least two failures with different sources and different remedies. The first is closure under entailment: an agent who knows p and knows that p entails q is credited with knowing q. The second is insensitivity to presentation: because the semantics identifies necessarily equivalent propositions, an agent who knows that 2 + 2 = 4 is credited with knowing every trivial tautology and every algebraic rewriting of it. Conjunction elimination and disjunction introduction look like different animals here, and I want a small battery of cases that separates them — a conjunction and its conjunct, a disjunction with a wholly irrelevant disjunct, a short tautology versus a long one, a conditional and its contrapositive. My working hypothesis is that the closure failures should not be repaired by weakening the knowledge relation but by relocating them: an agent who knows p and grasps that p entails q need not thereby know q, yet is rationally committed to q and is criticizable if she denies it, and it is the commitment rather than the knowledge that closes under entailment. The obvious objection is that this merely relabels the problem, and that any account fine-grained enough to block disjunction introduction will also leave agents ignorant that a conjunction implies its conjuncts, which is absurd. So the proposal must say exactly which non-trivial closure is preserved and why that much and no more. Please write it: the question stated so that it has a determinate answer, why the existing options — possible-worlds semantics with an awareness or impossible-worlds patch, relevance-based and topic-based accounts, proof-theoretic and syntactic accounts — leave it open, the method and the order of work across a semester, what a successful outcome would look like as against a result showing the hypothesis is empty, and an honest estimate of whether the case battery plus a toy semantics is feasible in the time. Assume I will supply the citations myself.
