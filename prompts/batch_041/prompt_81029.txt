I am a novelist, and the book I am writing has a scene I cannot make stand up. Its protagonist is a woman living at the end of the last ice age who makes the first deliberate image: a hand pressed into ochre on a cave wall, four fingers and a thumb. The whole architecture of the novel rests on that mark being a work of art — the first one — and a friend who reads philosophy has been trying to persuade me that the scene is incoherent. His argument runs as follows.

(1) Nothing is a work of art unless a community of makers and appreciators treats it as one; art-status is conferred by a practice, not discovered in the object.
(2) A practice of treating things as art exists only where there are already works of art to be treated in that way.
(3) So every work of art presupposes such a practice, and every such practice presupposes earlier works of art.
(4) Hence every work of art presupposes an earlier work of art, and that one an earlier one still, and so on without end.
(5) No chain of that kind can exist: each link waits upon the one before it, and there is no first link to end the waiting.
(6) Therefore there are no works of art, and my cave wall is blank.

He tells me this is the institutional theory of art properly understood, and that I have been attacking a caricature of it; I cannot see the difference. I am confident the conclusion is false — I have spent my life among the things — but I am also unwilling to surrender (1), since a folded blanket on a gallery floor and the same blanket on a bed are not two different objects. Tell me which step to abandon, why it looked innocent until it was pressed, and what abandoning it costs. If the answer is that I must give up (1), say what account of art survives without it and how that account handles the objects I care about; if the argument is a straw man, show me where the real version slips the regress. I am not after a reading list. I want the diagnosis, and I want to know which of my convictions I am paying with. Please write in British English.
