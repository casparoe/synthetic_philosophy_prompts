I run a small weekly reading group and two of my regulars have been at each other's throats for about a year now. One is a committed particularist — reasons are holistic, no moral principle ever really does any work in getting you to a verdict, the whole skill is seeing the case aright. The other is a stubborn generalist — there have to be principles, or at least hedged or default ones, otherwise moral thought is unteachable and there's nothing left to argue about. At this point they're just repeating themselves and I'm tired of referreeing. So draft the document they should have produced a year ago: an adversarial-collaboration joint statement they could both actually put their names to. Start with a numbered section of claims they both accept, then a disagreements table in which each side's position is stated in wording that side would accept as its own. I want the real cruxes named. My hunch is one of them is whether holism of reasons actually rules out principles or only rules out unhedged ones, and another is whether they're even disagreeing about the same thing — is particularism a claim about the metaphysics of reasons, about how a verdict gets justified, or about how you come to see what to do? I suspect a fair chunk of this fight is verbal and I want that part flagged as such. For each crux, give a case or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. And make them face this case, because I keep throwing it at them and they keep talking past it: the feature "this benefits my own child" is normally a reason to do the thing, but when the thing in question is allocating a scarce public resource I administer, that same feature looks like a reason against doing it — a reason to recuse instead. Does a generalist principle survive that just by getting hedged, or does the particularist win by default? Is the feature still a reason in the second case, a weaker reason, or no reason at all — make each of them commit to an answer. Keep the whole thing a document both of them could sign, not a transcript of the fight.
