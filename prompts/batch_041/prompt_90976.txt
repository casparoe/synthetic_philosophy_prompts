I work as a software engineer, have no background in philosophy, and have ended up in an argument with a colleague that neither of us can settle. I would like you to write a dialogue between two people who disagree about the bucket of water that bulges up its sides when you spin it. One of them thinks the bulge shows that spinning is a real property an object can have all by itself, not merely a matter of how it happens to sit relative to other things; the other thinks there is nothing to spinning except motion relative to other matter, so a bucket alone in an empty universe could not be spinning at all, whatever it looked like. Make both of them equally sharp and equally good at their jobs: each should answer the other's actual objections rather than talk past them, and neither should be a fool or a caricature. The case I want them to fight over is the one that started the argument. Suppose the universe contains exactly two things, the bucket and a single small rock far away. First we spin the bucket and hold the rock still, and the water climbs the walls. Then we do the reverse, holding the bucket still and swinging the rock around it, and my colleague says the water has to climb the walls again, because now the water is the thing moving relative to the only other matter in existence. I want the dialogue to land somewhere on whether he is right, and to bring out what follows either way: if the rock does the trick, whether a speck of dust would do it too, and how much matter it takes to make a difference; if it does not, what the first experiment was really showing. I would also like them to consider whether the bulge itself matters to either position or is just a fact about water, so let one of them propose filling the bucket with a frictionless fluid that refuses to be dragged into spinning at all. Please do not attribute either position to any historical figure and do not cite anything; I only want the two views argued out on their merits. Write it as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, just paragraphs the way one would write a long letter.
