I'm working through this old Lewis Carroll dialogue where Achilles and the Tortoise argue about modus ponens — the one where the tortoise keeps getting Achilles to write the conditional down as one more premise. I get the first few steps, but the tail end of it just looses me. Here's the bit I keep rereading:

"Do I?" said the Tortoise innocently. "Let's make that quite clear. I accept A and B and C and D. Suppose I still refused to accept Z?"

"Then Logic would take you by the throat, and force you to do it!" Achilles triumphantly replied. "Logic would tell you 'You can't help yourself. Now that you've accepted A and B and C and D, you must accept Z!' So you've no choice, you see."

"Whatever Logic is good enough to tell me is worth writing down," said the Tortoise. "So enter it in your book, please. We will call it

     (E)  If A and B and C and D are true, Z must be true.

Until I've granted that, of course I needn't grant Z."

Two things I can't get straight. First, what exactly is the tortoise doing when it says whatever Logic tells it is worth writing down? It feels like it's pulling something illegitimate — treating a rule of inference as if it were one more fact to be entered in the notebook — but I can't say precisly why that's a mistake rather than just a stubborn refusal to play along. Second, a friend told me the real moral is that no pile of extra premises will ever close the gap, because accepting a conditional is never the same as being bound to use it, so there has to be something non-propositional in inferring at all (you either just see that Z follows, or you're trained to go on that way). Is that the standard diagnosis, or is it too quick? And if it is right, how does Achilles' crack about "obtuseness would certainly be phenomenal" fit — is Carroll just letting him call the tortoise perverse, or is something being conceded there? Also: is there any reply to the tortoise that survives without either begging the question or simply refusing to write it down? I'd rather you walk me through the passage than just gesture at the regress.
