My two housemates have been going at each other all week about something a friend of ours did — she signed up for one of those paid memory studies, a couple of hours of button-pressing for cash. What nobody told her (no one lied, it just never came up) is that the data gets sold on to a marketing firm afterwards. She'd been up about thirty hours finishing her disseration and she was completely skint, and the money was the only reason she was there at all. She now says she'd never have agreed if she'd known, and that they never really got her consent.

Sam says consent is always consent to a description — you say yes to some particular account of what's going to happen, and if what actually happens is something else then you didn't consent to it, full stop. Silence does the same work a lie would do, so it makes no difference that nobody said anything false.

Alex says that can't be the standard, or no one ever consents to anything, since nobody knows every downstream use of what they hand over. Alex says the real question is what she had a right to know, and that's fixed by the norms of the practice rather than by consent itself. Alex also points out she'd probably have signed anyway, since she was there for the money — so on any but-for test the omission didn't do any work at all.

Sam says that test is horrible, because it makes consent hinge on how desperate you are: the more you need the money, the less anything you weren't told can count. And anyway, forget the information — she was broke, this was the only offer she had that week, and a yes squeezed out of someone with no decent alternative isn't consent, it's just the least bad option. Alex says an offer with bad alternatives is still an offer, and if that counts as coercion then so does every wage, and the word stops meaning anything. Sam says that's exactly the work the word should be doing — it's pointing at the background that made the choice unfree.

Then there's the sleep thing. Alex says being knackered isn't incompetence: capacity is task-specific, and she understood 'we will sell your data' perfectly well. Sam says you can understand a sentence and still not be in any state to consent to it.

Neither of them will budge and I'm sick of being asked to referee. Can you go through it move by move and say who's right at each step, and what both of them are missing? Point by point, please, not just an overall verdict.
