Suppose we set aside the two familiar defenses of an advance directive — that it expresses the autonomy of the earlier self, and that it protects the interests of the later self — and instead treat it as a promise that the earlier self made to the person she would become. Describe the most important implications of that promissory reading, both for a case in which a patient with dementia no longer recognizes her family but is now cheerful and says she wants to live, and for the general question of how much deference a directive should receive once the later self no longer endorses it. Be explicit about which implications are distinctive of the promissory reading and which are shared with the two views we set aside.
