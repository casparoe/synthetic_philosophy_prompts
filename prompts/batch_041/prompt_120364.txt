ok so i'm building a grad seminar unit on thought experiments in science -- how the hell do they give us knowledge -- and i want the checklist *before* i get to the theories. every reading i've got tells me how one thought experiment works and then generalizes, and i keep wanting to say: ok but thats not a requirement, thats your answer. so write me the desiderata. not the accounts, the spec. what does any account of thought-experimental knowledge have to deliver to be worth taking seriously?

things i think i want on the list, but push back if i'm wrong to want them:

the central weirdness. you're in a chair. nothing is measured, nobody new is consulted, no data enters the room. and you walk out with a belief about how the world actually is -- or at least with the strong sense that you've discovered something. any account has to either (a) explain how that's possible or (b) say the sense of discovery is a psychological illusion and the real epistemic work is being done by something else: the premises, the formalism, a hidden appeal to prior experience, whatever. and it has to tell me which of the two it's doing. a story that quietly does (b) while talking like (a) is the thing i most want flagged.

the case zoo. it has to get the standard cases right, and i dont just mean wave at them: the two weights tied together, Maxwell's demon, the twin who comes home young, Newton's bucket, the cat in the box. and it has to treat them as *one kind of thing*, or tell me why they aren't. an account that handles the ones where you imagine a little machine (demon, cat) but falls over on the ones where you imagine a law of motion is a theory of something else wearing this theory's name.

heres the injection i actually care about. split the cases two ways and tell me if the split is load-bearing: thought experiments where the scenario is *physically impossible* (demon, cat, the young twin) vs thought experiments where the scenario is perfectly possible but nobody's built it yet (the elevator, the tied weights, chasing a light beam). does an adequate account have to treat these differently? because it *feels* like the impossible ones can only be doing argument-work, while the possible ones are standing in for an experiment that just hasn't happened yet -- but i'm not sure thats right, and i want the requirement stated in a way that doesn't just assume the answer.

then this: what makes one go *wrong*? thought experiments misfire, get retracted, get reinterpreted a century later (the demon, for instance, only gets settled once you've got a theory of information in hand). an account that can only explain the successes isn't a theory of the method, its a theory of the hits. so the spec needs a clause: it must say what a *failed* thought experiment is, and whether the failure is the scenario's fault, the intuition's fault, or the background theory's fault.

the distinctions it cant collapse, with a line on why each matters: thought experiment vs. real experiment; thought experiment vs. plain argument in vivid dressing (theres a whole fight about whether the first is just the second -- dont resolve it, but your spec has to be neutral enough that both sides can sign on, or say why one side is a non-starter); the verdict on the imagined case vs. the intuition that delivers the verdict; and discovery vs. justification.

last thing, and this is the part i most want. tell me if any of these cant all be had at once. my suspicion: "yields genuinely new knowledge about the world" + "brings in no new empirical input" + "all knowledge of the world is empirical" is already a contradiction, so one of my three has to go -- which one, and does the choice just *determine* the rest of your theory for you? if theres another pair like that, name it. then tell me which single requirement on the list does the most throat-cutting, i.e. which one most of the going accounts die on.

dont score the accounts, i'll do that in class. i want the list, the case or consideration behind each item, and a marker on which are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's taste. a seperate column saying what each requirement would look like if you denied it would be great, but i can live without it. table's fine.
