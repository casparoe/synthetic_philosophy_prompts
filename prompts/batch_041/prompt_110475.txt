Ok so I help run a small reading group on philosophy of physics and two of the regulars have been argueing for like three months now about Gödel's rotating universe, and at this point the rest of us would settle for them just writing down what they actually agree on so we can move on to something else.

The setup: one of them thinks Gödel basically won. Time, in the robust sense, has to involve a single objective now that passes; relativity gives you a four-dimensional continuum with no such thing in it; and the rotating universe is the showpiece because it shows you can't patch this up by appealing to some cosmic time picked out by the universe at large. So time as we naively think of it just isn't a feature of the world, it's a fourth dimension we happen to experience a certain way. The other one thinks that whole argument only runs if you already grant that real becoming requires one global now shared by everybody, which is a premise relativity never gave us and which you don't have to accept to believe things genuinely happen. They keep trading the same four or five moves and I want the document instead of the argument.

So write the joint statement those two could both actually sign. Numbered section of agreed claims first: what they agree relativity does and doesn't say about simultaneity, what the rotating universe does and doesn't show (does it do any real work, or is the closed timelike curve stuff just decoration on top of an argument that already goes through in special relativity?), what objective passage is even supposed to mean, and what either of them would accept as counting against their own side. Then a disagreements table: each row a genuine disagreement, each side's position worded so that side would sign off on the wording, the crux underneath it, and then the case or argument both agree bears on that crux with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Two things I want forced in there because we keep skating past them. First, a test case: a world with a preferred foliation of the whole thing, an objective global now, but completely undetectable from the inside, no dynamics pick it out, nothing about it makes a difference to anything that happens. Both of them have to say what they predict about whether passage is real in that world, and I want it to be a real commitment, not a hedge. Second, suppose it turned out that some version of quantum gravity needs a preferred foliation after all. Is that a win for the passage side, or is a foliation just a bookkeeping device with nothing to do with becoming? Also make them deal with the fact that our own universe expands and does seem to have a cosmic time, which one of them keeps waving away.

And tell me where the fight is verbal. My guess is at least one of their disagreements is really about which sense of time is in play and would dissolve if they'd say which one they mean, but don't take my guess on faith, check it.

Don't give me a survey or a both-sides shrug. Where you have to come down, come down, and say why. And no reading lists or see-the-literature-on-X stuff, neither of us can look anything up, just do the work.
