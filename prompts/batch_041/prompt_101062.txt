I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, so please keep the exposition self-contained. I have been following the discussion of "tool AI" versus "agent AI," and I have come to suspect that the distinction is more slippery than the discussion admits. I would like you to give me one example of a conceptual issue in this area, presented as a concrete system rather than as a hypothetical superintelligence.

Here is the wrinkle I want the example to bring out. Whether a system counts as a tool or as an agent appears, on inspection, not to be a property of the artifact but of the loop in which the artifact is embedded. A model that merely proposes completions is a tool when a human reads each suggestion and decides; the same model becomes an agent, in the ordinary sense of the word, once its suggestions are pipelined into actions that no human reviews. If that is right, then "tool" and "agent" name not two kinds of software but two ways of wiring software into a division of labor, and the questions that matter — about autonomy, oversight, and responsibility — attach to the wiring rather than to the code.

Please give me one worked example of a realistic system, something an engineer could plausibly build and ship, that makes this point vivid. It should meet three conditions. First, the system must be one that can be described equally well both ways — as a tool whose goal is supplied entirely from outside, and as an agent pursuing a goal of its own — with nothing in the code settling which description is correct. Second, the two descriptions must lead to different practical verdicts, for instance about whether it is safe to remove the human from the loop or about who is answerable when the system causes harm. Third, the case should turn on a feature I would not have thought of on my own, drawn from outside the familiar examples: not a chatbot, not a trading algorithm, not a self-driving car.

I am not asking for a survey or for citations. I want a single well-chosen case, a precise account of the conceptual issue it exposes, an explicit statement of which description you think is correct, and an identification of the feature that carries the weight — so that I can tell whether your verdict would carry over to a nearby case.
