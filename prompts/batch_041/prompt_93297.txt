My friend and I spent most of last night going at each other about Nozick's tracking theory, and I came out of it convinced I'd stumbled onto something, so I want to get it into a proposal before the idea evaporates. My department runs these little one-semester pilot grants, a few thousand dollars, and the application is basically a two-page proposal with a budget line. Deadline's in three weeks.

Here's the arguement we were having. Everyone knows tracking has a closure problem. Nozick wants to say you know you have hands but you don't know you're not a handless brain in a vat, so he has to accept that knowledge isn't closed under known entailment, and a lot of people treat that as the price of the view and either pay it or walk away. The other standard move is to swap sensitivity out for something safety-flavored, which is supposed to buy closure back. My friend thinks the whole approach is dead on arrival, I think he's missing something, and I can't yet say exactly what.

My half-formed idea: all these closure cases get run at the level of the proposition — is *this* belief counterfactually dependent on the truth. But the tracking conditions were never meant to be proposition-level; Nozick relativizes them to the method you used to arrive at the belief. Once you keep the method fixed across the inference, I suspect a big chunk of the so-called closure failures just vanish, and the ones that survive are precisely the ones where the inference quietly forces you to switch methods (perception to skeptical hypothesis-elimination, say). If that's right, closure failure is real but much narrower than advertised, and it's tracking method-changes rather than entailment as such. So: define a notion of a method-preserving inference, ask whether tracking is transmitted across method-preserving inferences, and ask whether that criterion classifies the familiar cases the way we'd want — saving the ones that should be saved without saving the ones that shouldn't.

Turn this into the proposal. I need the question stated precisely enough that it has a determinate answer, an honest account of why the existing responses leave it open, the actual method month by month (four months, and be realistic about what I can do — I've had one grad logic course and no real semantics background), what a result would look like, and what would count as the project failing. I'm especially worried about two failure modes and want them named in the proposal: the criterion turning out to have no bite because every closure failure can be trivially re-described as a method change, and the criterion being so permissive it's just full closure in disguise. Also say something about whether "method" can even be individuated non-circularly, since if it can't the whole thing collapses.

Don't do a literature review or give me citations — I'll add those myself. But be careful to seperate what's actually settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this. I don't want to walk into a workshop assuming everyone agrees closure fails, or that everyone agrees what a "method" is, if neither of those is true.
