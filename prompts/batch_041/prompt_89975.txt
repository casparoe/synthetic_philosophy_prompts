Im putting together a short proposal (two pages, for a small internal grant that buys me one semester, no fieldwork money) and I have a half-formed idea I want you to turn into something real.

Heres the idea. When we dig up a pattern -- every house in a settlement oriented the same odd way, a deposit sealed under a floor and never opened again, tools standardized more precisely than the job requires -- we usually explain it one of three ways: people had no choice (material or labour constraints), people were made to do it (somebody enforced a rule, i.e. an institution), or people thought the world worked that way (a belief). In practice these get used almost interchangeably, and a lot of the fights in the field -- "this is ritual" / "no, its just storage" -- feel like they're really about which of the three is being claimed, not about the evidence.

My suspicion is that these arent a ladder from safe to speculative. They're underdetermined in different directions, and the tell is the exception. Constraints predict what can't happen. Institutions predict what happens at the edges, where enforcement reaches and where it doesnt. Beliefs predict what people keep doing when it costs them and no one is watching -- and especially in places nobody could check afterwards. So maybe each grade needs its own evidentiary signature: enforcement should leave traces of maintenance (redundancy, replacement, monitoring, precision beyond function), while belief should leave traces of unrecoverable cost (effort that could never be audited: sealed deposits, work on surfaces that then get covered, marks no one would ever see). And then the interesting bit: an absence of enforcement traces is positive evidence for the belief explanation, not just a gap in the record.

Turn this into the proposal. I need the question stated so that it has a determinate answer -- right now "can we infer belief from objects" is too vague to even fail at -- plus why the existing ways of carving this up leave it open (positions and arguments, not a reading list; I'll add the citations myself), what I'd actually do month by month for a semester, and why this signature approach rather than the obvious alternative of just doing more careful contextual reading site by site. Then say what would count as the project failing; I'd rather have a falsifiable dud than a vague success.

Two things to keep it honest. Keep the examples at the level of textbook-recognizable patterns rather than famous named sites, since I want this to stand on its own. And dont let me treat "institution" as one thing -- if enforcement, coordination and mere convention need seperate signatures, say so, and tell me straight whether that makes the project too big for one semester. If you think the whole three-way split is bogus, argue for a two-way one instead of humoring me.
