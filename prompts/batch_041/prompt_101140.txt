A friend of mine who runs a philosophy-of-mind blog sent me a draft post on the color phi phenomenon and I can't tell how much of it to trust. He's smart and reads alot, but he's not a vision scientist, and I only did a bit of cog sci in undergrad, so I'm a bad judge. Here's his summary, more or less as written:

"So here's the color phi effect. You flash a dot in one spot, then a fraction of a second later flash another dot somewhere else, and if the second dot is a different colour, people report seeing a single dot glide across the gap and change colour partway along — as in, before the second dot has even been flashed. Which is crazy, because how does the colour of the later dot get into the earlier part of the experience? The only way is if the brain runs behind reality and then fixes up the footage, filling in the bits that were never there. My take: this proves two things. (1) Your conscious experience lags the world by roughly a tenth of a second, so what you think you're seeing right now has already happened. (2) The brain patches up the memory after the fact, Orwell-style — I call this the Orwellian option, after 1984, where the past gets rewritten so it lines up with the present. Dennett denies that and goes for the other option, the Stalinesque one — the show trial gets staged once the evidence is all in but before the public is let into the room, so the edit happens before consciousness rather than after. And his reason for liking Stalinesque is that the two stories leave the same memory trace, so the question of when the experience actually happens is just an artefact of a badly designed experiment. Which leaves us with: if even Dennett can't tell which story is true, then neither is, and really the whole thing just shows the self is an illusion."

Can you go through this claim by claim and tell me which bits someone who actually works on the timing of conscious experience would happily accept, which they'd wince at, and which are just wrong? And separately: does his closing conclusion follow from whatever survives, or is he getting more out of the experiment than it can give? Not looking for a reading list — just where he's right, where he's fudging, and where he's off the map.
