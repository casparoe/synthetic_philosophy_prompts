I spent thirty years in condensed-matter experiment before retiring, and I have spent the first years of my retirement reading my way into metaphysics. A reading group I joined has been working through the dispute between endurantists and perdurantists about persistence, and I have reached the uncomfortable point where I can state both views and still not see what the fight is about. So what I want from you is the document the two camps should have produced years ago: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration, signed by one committed endurantist and one committed perdurantist, that records what they have actually established between them and what they still dispute.

It should open with a numbered list of claims both signatories can put their names to without embarrassment: about what each view is and is not committed to, about which of the familiar arguments the two of them jointly regard as inconclusive rather than decisive, and about what a genuine discovery in this area would look like as opposed to a redescription. I would guess that at least three of the real cruxes are the problem of temporary intrinsics, the case of the statue and the lump of clay, and the argument from vagueness for four-dimensionalism, though you should tell me if the load-bearing cruxes are somewhere else entirely; I came into this from physics, so I also want to know whether anything in relativity is doing work here or whether it is decoration. Please do not pad the agreed section with claims that are true but empty, the sort of thing any philosopher of any stripe could sign; if a claim is vacuous, cut it. Then state the residual disagreements precisely enough that each side would accept the wording of its own position and not feel ambushed by the other's, and give me the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one signatory, each with a case or argument both sides agree bears on it and each side's prediction for that case written down in advance.

I have one test case of my own that I want them to face, because I suspect it is where the two descriptions do different work: a material thing whose every constituent part is exchanged between any two instants of its career, so that no two instants share a part, while its trajectory, its causal powers and its interactions with neighbouring things are as smooth and unremarkable as those of a stone on a beach. Both signatories must say what their view delivers about it, and their predictions must be fixed before either is allowed to argue. Finally, there is a suspicion I want tested rather than indulged: that a good part of this dispute is verbal, a quarrel between two languages for describing one world, and that the parties have mistaken a choice of notation for a choice of fact. Give me a definite verdict on that, saying where the verbal part lies and where the disagreement is substantive, rather than laying the options out for me to choose between. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table that carries the cruxes and the two predictions, and keep the whole document to 1,200 words or fewer.
