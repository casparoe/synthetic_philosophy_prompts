Write a deadpan internal incident report, roughly 600–700 words, from the fictional Facilities and Symbolic Operations division of a research institute that maintains a careenium: a large box of tiny magnetic marbles, called simms, whose clustering into larger aggregates, simmballs, is said to implement a symbol-manipulating mind. The incident is that the careenium has stopped thinking, though every simmball is exactly where it should be. The report should be written by an invented operations manager who is fluent in neither philosophy nor physics but has absorbed enough of both to be dangerous, and it should take the form of a numbered incident review with a root-cause section, a corrective-action section, and a short FAQ for staff. The satire should come from the actual conceptual terrain, not from invented nonsense: the simmball-level story is causally complete but explanatorily useless; the symbolic-level story cites a higher-level pattern as the cause of the lower-level motions that constitute it; a technician is reprimanded for downward causation after kicking the box; the warranty explicitly excludes multiple realizability; and someone proposes replacing the careenium with a simulation of the careenium on the grounds that the symbols were doing all the work anyway. Do not state the philosophical thesis directly or resolve the question of whether symbols push the substrate around; let the report’s attempts to assign blame make the reader feel the force of both eliminativist and higher-level-causation positions. A reader who knows the debate should find it fair rather than wince-inducing; a reader who does not should still follow the comedy.
