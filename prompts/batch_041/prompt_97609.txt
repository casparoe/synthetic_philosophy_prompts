I run the classifications team at a national statistics office — we decide which categories go into the standard demographic and social surveys, the ones every other agency then has to use in funding formulas and legal reporting. Every revision cycle we get lobbied to add things: a nonbinary option on the sex question, an 'essential worker' flag, 'food desert', a new ethnicity tick-box. We're statisticians, so when someone asks whether a proposed category is real or just made up, we freeze, and we end up doing whatever the loudest stakeholder wants. I know 'socially constructed' is supposed to be the interesting middle ground between real and made up, but I need something a person with no philosophy training can actually run in an afternoon, because we've got two weeks and the chief statistician signs off on everything.

So: turn the philosophy of social construction into a decision procedure for us. The questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, where it hands off to negotiation with the people being classified, and where it hands off to the chief statistician because it's a values question and not a fact question. Keep whatever distinctions actually survive contact with an office — that some categories are held up by collective acceptance rather than by measurement, that some are just socially caused, that classifying people can change the people being classified, that some categories are being redesigned for a purpose rather than discovered — and drop the ones that don't. Be straight with me about where the procedure will misfire.

Run it on three proposals: adding a nonbinary option to the sex question; an 'essential worker' flag for pandemic-era reporting; and one where I expect the procedure to tell me something I won't like — pick whichever of the three that is and say so.

Give me the procedure as a table with these columns: Step | Question | What counts as a good enough answer | If no or unclear, then | Fact call or values call. Then, after the tables, give me the same content again as a JSON object with fields steps[] (step, question, sufficient_answer, if_not, judgment_call) and cases[] (proposal, verdict, deciding_test, negotiate_with, downstream_failure, confidence, unresolved).
