I am a high school student preparing a presentation for my school's philosophy club on whether reality has a fundamental level. I want to avoid the standard summaries and focus on a specific question: What is your credence that the grounding relation is well-founded—that is, that every chain of grounding terminates in facts that are not themselves grounded in anything further? By grounding, I mean the non-causal relation in virtue of which one fact obtains because of another. Please give me a number between 0 and 1, and decompose it into the sub-questions you think it depends on. For example: the probability that grounding is a single, unified relation rather than a family of disparate relations; the probability that grounding is irreflexive and asymmetric, so that self-grounding and circular grounding are impossible; the probability that an infinite descending chain of grounding is metaphysically possible; and the probability that if such a chain were possible, it would still be reasonable to posit a fundamental level for explanatory reasons. Assign a rough probability to each, explain how they combine, and then tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to. Finally, what argument or consideration would most move your credence, and in which direction?
