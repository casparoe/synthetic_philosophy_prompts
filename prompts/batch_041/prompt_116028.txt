I have been trying to get Skolem's paradox straight, and the usual resolutions strike me as too quick. As I understand the downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem, the countable elementary submodel is built by closing a countable set under Skolem functions, so that whenever an existential statement holds in the submodel, a witness for it already lies inside. Take such a submodel M, and let A be a set that M regards as uncountable. In the ambient universe there is a bijection from A onto ω, so the statement that such a bijection exists ought to have a witness in M; yet M satisfies the claim that A is uncountable. One of these has to give: either the Skolem hull is not genuinely elementary, or Cantor's theorem holds only relative to a model and there are no absolutely uncountable sets. Which is it — and if the second, does set theory have any determinate subject matter at all?
