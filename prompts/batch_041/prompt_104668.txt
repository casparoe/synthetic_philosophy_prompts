So I've been reporting on alot of "everyday ethics" stuff lately — tipping apps and the new default-percentage screens, that recurring viral fight about whether you have to queue in the correct order at a bus stop, cities quietly decriminalizing jaywalking — and after a year of it I've drifted into a position I can't quite shake. I now think these little conventions aren't merely etiquette or local color; they're genuine (if minor and defeasible) moral obligations, and because they're real obligations, a visitor to a place is bound by its versions of them too. The tourist who "doesn't know" to tip, or who strolls across the empty road because it's legal back home, isn't just gauche — they're doing something they have reason to feel a bit bad about. My friends think this is obviously too strong, and honestly I'd rather stress-test it than defend it.

So: what would count against it? I don't want a survey of the literature on convention — I want a map of this position's exposure. What observations, arguments, intuitions, or formal results would actually count against the claim that these conventions are moral obligations and that visitors must follow the local ones? Sort them by how decisive they'd be. I'm especially curious about three things. First, which of these defeaters would the view's own defenders concede, as opposed to the stuff only critics throw at it? Second, is the "conventions are obligations" claim even the kind of thing that can be falsified, or have I quietly hedged it into "if the convention is morally acceptable and the stakes are non-trivial and nobody's being exploited and..." — and if I have, is that a bug or just what a view like this has to look like? Third, what does the view predict that a rival doesn't — say, the rival that these are mere coordination equilibria, or the rival that only locals are bound? And I'd like to know where the visitor obligation actually bites: is there a threshold below which it lapses (a penny tip, a queue that doesn't matter, an empty road at 3am), and if the thresholds just turn out to be whatever's locally normal, has the view stopped saying anything at all? Push hard on it — I'd rather find out it's hollow than keep half-believing it.
