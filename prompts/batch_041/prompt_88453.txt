As part of a metaphysics-and-the-social-sciences course I'm taking, I've been asked to construct a thought experiment that separates two things that are constantly run together: a kind's being socially constructed in the constitutive sense — its members have the property only by virtue of an ongoing collective practice, so the property would cease to exist if the practice stopped — and a kind's being socially constructed in the merely causal sense — social practices causally shape which things come to have a property that could in principle exist without them.

Please design a single scenario, set in an ordinary contemporary institution (a workplace, a school, a hospital, a market — nothing science-fictional), containing two kinds of thing that have exactly the same membership at the moment of the story but pull apart on which sense of construction applies to each. Constraints: first, neither kind may be race, gender, or money — I need something less worn. Second, the extension of both kinds must be identical under either reading, so that a naive observer simply listing who is in and who is out would have no way to tell them apart; the difference must lie entirely in what grounds the membership, not in who is a member. Third, the case must not stipulate its own verdict — the verdict should fall out of how the practice and the property are related, such that a reader could reconstruct it from your description of the dependence rather than being told it.

After the scenario, do the following. For each kind, state which sense of construction it satisfies and give the reasoning. Identify the single feature of the case that is doing the work of separating the two kinds. Then say what happens to the distinction if that feature is removed — that is, whether the two kinds collapse into one, and if so, which one. Finally, as a check on the case: explain how a thoroughgoing naturalist who holds that all such kinds are merely causal constructions would attack the scenario, and what the constructionist would have to say to hold the line without simply reasserting the distinction. Keep the scenario itself to a single paragraph; the analysis may run longer.
