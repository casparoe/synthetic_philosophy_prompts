I am writing a critical notice of a political philosopher's book on inheritance and equal opportunity, and my central objection turns on a case the book never reaches, so I need to predict her verdict on it before I commit to the objection.

The book announces that a just inheritance regime must secure equality of opportunity, but the formula does no work on its own; what the book actually does is endorse or reject a series of proposals. Her verdicts are these.

1. A steep tax on estates above roughly ten times median household wealth, with the proceeds earmarked for early childhood education and care: just.
2. A hard lifetime ceiling on inheritances, with everything above it taxed at 100 percent: unjust. She says it is confiscatory, that it treats the family as a mere conduit for the state, and that it would raise very little.
3. An exemption for family farms and closely held businesses below a value ceiling: unjust. She says it shelters precisely the fortunes that reproduce advantage across generations.
4. A tax on large lifetime gifts, at the same rates as bequests: just.
5. A scheme under which wealth that has already been inherited once is taxed more heavily when it is inherited again than wealth being inherited for the first time: just.
6. She holds that whether the tax is levied on the estate or on the recipient is a matter of administration rather than principle.

The proposal she never discusses is this: a low-rate tax on every inheritance above a small floor, so that ordinary families pay alongside rich ones, with the proceeds dedicated to a capital grant paid to every citizen on turning eighteen, so that each cohort receives a lump sum regardless of what their parents left them.

Predict her verdict on it. Then say which rule she is in fact running, and which other rules fit her published verdicts equally well; what each surviving rule predicts about this proposal; which of her earlier verdicts is its closest analogue and why; and which further proposal would best tell the surviving rules apart.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, and don't restate the question.
