We've been treating Zorn as an axiom, and the convention in this course is that rings have a multiplicative identity; maximal ideal means a proper ideal not properly contained in any other proper ideal. Here's the answer:

Zorn's Lemma: If P is a nonempty partially ordered set and every chain in P has an upper bound in P, then P has a greatest element.

Let R be a commutative ring with identity. Let P be the set of all proper ideals of R, ordered by inclusion. P is nonempty because {0} is a proper ideal. Let C be a chain in P. Let U be the union of all I in C. U is an ideal because it is a union of ideals. U is proper because every I in C is proper. So U is an upper bound for C. By Zorn, P has a greatest element M. Since M is a proper ideal and is greatest, no proper ideal properly contains it. Thus M is maximal. Therefore every commutative ring with identity has a maximal ideal.
