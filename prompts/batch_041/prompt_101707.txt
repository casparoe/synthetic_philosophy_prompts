I do safe RL for a living -- constrained MDPs, reward plus a separate cost channel, Lagrangian relaxations, feasibility vs. optimality, that whole bag of tricks. A friend keeps going on about "the paradox of deontology" and I want to know whether my vocabulary can actually hold it or whether I'm just going to nod along. My rough grip on it: deontologists say there are things you must not do even when doing them would stop more of the same thing from happening -- don't kill the one to save the five, don't lie even to prevent a bigger lie. And the "paradox" is supposed to be that this constraint can't justify itself. Either the reason you can't kill the one is that killing is bad, in which case why aren't you minimizing the bad? Or the constraint has no reason behind it, which makes it look like an arbitrary taboo. Plus the thing seems to defeat the very point of having it.

So: restate the paradox as faithfully as you can in my terms. Reward, cost, budgeted constraint vs. per-step hard constraint, feasible set, scalarization vs. lexicographic ordering, multipliers, Pareto frontier, constraints that are exogenous to the problem vs. constraints the agent imposes on itself. Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. What maps cleanly? What did you have to fudge? And what in the philosophers version has no counterpart at all here? Specifically I want to know: does "agent-relative" mean anything once there's only one optimizer in the room? Does the doing/allowing distinction survive once "do nothing" is just another action in the action space? And is there anything left of the idea that the constraint binds me in a way it doesn't bind someone optimizing over everyone? My hunch is the whole thing is just "you can't both be the thing that picks the constraint and be constrained by it" -- so tell me straight whether that hunch is right, or whether there's something real in their version that my setup literally cannot express, and if so whether that's my framework being impoverished or their claim being mush.
