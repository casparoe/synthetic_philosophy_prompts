Write a joke about Nietzsche's three ways of doing history in the Uses and Disadvantages of History for Life essay -- monumental, antiquarian, and critical. It should be funny to someone who's actually read it, so the humor has to come out of what each mode really does: monumental history needing its heroes and quietly distorting them in the service of present action, antiquarian history's piety toward the small and local and its tendency to mummify the life it loves, critical history putting the past on trial and condemning it while still living off the very thing it condemns. Give it a concrete scenario to hang on: someone clearing out their dead grandmas house and deciding what to keep, sell, or burn. Maybe tell the same little story three times, or one joke whose punchline changes meaning depending on which kind of historian is telling it -- don't just staple the three labels onto a generic joke about old stuff. If you can work in a nod to the other triad from the same essay (the unhistorical, historical, and suprahistorical) go for it, but dont conflate the two lists. After the joke, add a short note on where it takes liberties with Nietzsche or leans on a contested reading of the three modes versus where it's just following the text, and be upfront about what scholars actually disagree on here instead of passing a contested reading off as the consensus.
