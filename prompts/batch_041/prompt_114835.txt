I'm not an academic, I just hang out in a couple of AI-safety discords and I keep getting into the same fight with people about what it even means for a powerful AI to be "aligned" — everyone's talking past each other and I want to build a little flowchart for the server so people can figure out where they actually land instead of just yelling. Take the role of someone who teaches both political philosophy and AI ethics, and please think carefully before you write anything: first brainstorm out loud the questions you think actually split these views apart, then build the thing.

The question I want the tree to sort on is: whose values should a very capable AI system end up serving, and how do you get from a bunch of individual people with different values to the one thing the system actually does? Branch nodes should be questions a normal person can answer without knowing the name of any position, and each branch labeled with the thing that splits it. Some of the forks I keep running into, which you should use at least most of: (a) is there a fact of the matter about what's right that the system could in principle be pointed at, or is "right" just whoever's preferences we happen to load; (b) does it serve what people actually want right now, or what they'd want after thinking about it properly, being better informed, less of a mess; (c) who's the "we" — the person typing the prompt, the company that deployed it, the country that regulates it, everyone alive today, people not born yet, animals; (d) when there's more than one person, does the system need a single ranking of outcomes (add up welfare, average it, weight the worst off most, protect the worst off no matter what, that kind of thing) or does it refuse to rank and just run some fair process instead; (e) is being neutral between different visions of a good life even possible, or is neutrality just one more substantive vision (I read somewhere that the liberal state has to be neutral and I could never tell whether that's a real position or a trick); (f) should the system lock in the values it starts with, or keep people's ability to change their minds intact even if that means its own goals drift.

For the leaves: give me like 10-12 named positions, each with one sentence on the most awkward thing it has to swallow — e.g. the view where the AI just does what its owner says has the obvious problem that whoever holds the leash wins, but I want the same honesty about the ones I'd probably agree with. If a position doesn't have a name, say so and call it unnamed rather than inventing a fake tradition for it. Where the tree genuinely can't tell two positions apart, say so instead of pretending — I'd rather have a footnote than a distinction that doesn't do any work. And for combinations of answers that nobody seems to actually hold, say whether that's because the view is incoherent, because it's coherent but horrible, or just because nobody's thought about it yet. Please don't just hand me a list of famous philosophers with their views bolted on; I want the logic of the space first, with the names attached where they fit. The audience is people in my server, some of whom have never read any philosophy, so keep the wording plain.
