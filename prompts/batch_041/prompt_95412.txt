I am preparing a one-page handout for a graduate seminar in the philosophy of biology. The theme is the vocabulary of information and coding in molecular biology, and the recurring suspicion that these terms began as metaphors and have quietly been promoted to literal descriptions. The students know their basic molecular biology but have read none of the philosophical literature, so the handout has to stand on its own.

Write a decision tree that begins at a point no one in the debate disputes — that genes are causally implicated in the production of proteins — and proceeds by a sequence of questions to the distinct positions one can hold about genetic information and the coding metaphor. Each internal node must be phrased as a question a reader can answer without already knowing which position it leads to, and each branch must be labeled with the answer that selects it. Every leaf should carry a short name for the position, or the label "unnamed" if you believe no one actually holds it, followed by one sentence stating its most awkward commitment.

Build the tree around at least these four splits, and add others you judge necessary:

1. What must be true for something to count as information? Is a reliable causal or statistical correlation between two states sufficient, or must the state also be about something, or have the function of representing something, or be consumed as information by a system that uses it?
2. Is the mapping between codons and amino acids arbitrary — a convention in the way the mapping from dots and dashes to letters is a convention — or is it so constrained by chemistry and by selection that it is better described as a regularity than as a code? Some lineages read a codon differently from the way the standard code reads it; say whether that cuts for or against the arbitrariness claim.
3. Does the coding vocabulary do explanatory work that a description in terms of molecules, sequences, and reaction rates cannot do, or is it dispensable in principle?
4. Where does the information reside: in the DNA sequence, treated as a set of instructions, or distributed across the whole developmental system, with DNA as one resource among many?

Flag explicitly any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, and any combination of answers that leaves a position empty, with a one-line diagnosis of whether that view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Keep the whole thing to a single page.
