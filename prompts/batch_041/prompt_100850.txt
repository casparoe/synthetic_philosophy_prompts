Instead of the old top-down story of states and economies, microhistorians zoom in on one tiny place or one person's life, usually somebody who got in trouble with the authorities and so left a paper trail in a trial record. The method is basically the thick description that anthropologists pioneered, just turned loose on archival documents. And the point of picking such a small case is that it's representative: study one village carefully enough and what you find there is what was happening everywhere, so you can generalize about a whole society from a single life. That's how the microhistorians showed the grand structural histories -- the ones about long-term economic cycles and the slow drift of civilizations -- had basically gotten it wrong.'
