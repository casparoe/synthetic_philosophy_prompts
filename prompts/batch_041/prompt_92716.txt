Write a dialogue between a relationist and a substantivalist about Newton's bucket, but skip the version everyone knows — the one where the water sits at rest relative to the bucket and is still concave. Put them in this instead: the whole universe is exactly two identical sealed buckets of water, both turning the same way at the same rate, the water in each co-rotating with its own bucket, and nothing else anywhere. The relationist says the description is close to empty: the only real fact in that world is the relative turning, which here is zero, so nothing is rotating relative to anything and both surfaces should come out flat. The substantivalist says no, both buckets are still rotating relative to space, so both waters climb the sides, and thats a real prediction. Let them actually go at it — does "same direction, same rate" even mean anything on the relationist's terms, what changes if the two buckets turn in opposite directions instead, and how does each side explain why the concavity sticks around once the water has spun up to match its bucket? No strawmen, both should be sharp, and they should answer each other's actual points instead of talking past each other. Keep it tight.
