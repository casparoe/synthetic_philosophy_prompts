ok so I'm a second-year PhD student in cog sci, and my whole dissertation plan rests on something I've started to doubt, so I want you to genuinely try to take it apart rather than reassure me. The view is roughly this: when mech interp finds a feature -- a direction in activation space, an SAE latent, a circuit, whatever -- and that feature is causally load-bearing in the right way (ablate it and the behaviour goes, steer along it and the behaviour comes back), then it's fair to say the network literally represents that thing. Not a useful fiction, not a redescription of input-output behaviour, but an actual representational state with determinate content, in the same sense that a belief has content. Call it realism about mech interp. I know this is the working assumption of basically everyone in my subfield, which is exactly why I want the case against it laid out properly.

What I want is the map of what would count against it. Not the lazy version (it's just a model, models don't represent things) -- I mean which experimental results, which formal arguments, which conceptual distinctions should actually move someone who holds this view. A few I'm already worried about and want you to weigh in on: the no-privileged-basis point (the computation is invariant under a change of basis in the residual stream, so why is my SAE's basis the one carrying the real features?), feature instability across random seeds and hyperparameters, the probing-classifier control-task stuff where probes happily learn to predict random labels, and the general underdetermination of representational content by behaviour. Then add the ones I haven't thought of, and rank the whole lot from fatal through merely awkward through actually fine once you say a bit more.

And the bit I really care about: which of these would a committed realist concede as a genuine defeater versus wave away, and does waving them away quietly turn the view into something unfalsifiable? Because if there's no possible network we could probe where the realist says fine, no representations here, then I worry the view has become a definition rather than a claim -- but maybe that's acceptable for a view of this kind and I'm overreacting. Tell me honestly which it is.

Also please keep it concrete. If you tell me basis-relativity would count against it, tell me what result I'd have to actually see in the lab, what the numbers would look like, and why the realist couldn't just redescribe it. For what its worth I've been running a little toy where a 2-layer net is trained on a task with a known ground-truth factor, and two different SAE runs hand me two different latents that both steer the behaviour fine.
