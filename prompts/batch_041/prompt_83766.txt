My old cognitive science professor is obsessed with the Darwinian/Skinnerian/Popperian/Gregorian ladder, and I've been collecting his verdicts on cases in office hours beacuse I want to know what rule he's actually running before I bring him my hard cases. So far: a sunflower that tracks the sun across the sky is Darwinian, not Skinnerian. A rat that learns to press a lever for food is Skinnerian. A chimp that fails to reach a banana, sits for a bit, then stacks boxes is Popperian. A child who is told that if she wants to get to the other side she should use the bridge, and then uses that rule in a new park, is Gregorian. A sea slug that habituates to a repeated poke is Darwinian, not Skinnerian. A dog that learns to sit for treats is Skinnerian. A crow that drops stones into a tube to raise the water level is Popperian, even though it never got to practice the trick first. A human who uses a written checklist to overcome her own memory limits is Gregorian. He also said a thermostat is not even Darwinian, because it doesn't have a goal of its own. And he said an ant colony that finds the shortest path to food is Darwinian, not Skinnerian, because the colony isn't learning by reinforcement, the pheromone trail is just a fixed rule plus evaporation. I think there might be more than one rule that fits all of these, and I want to know what he'll say about two new cases.

First, a robot that is trained by reinforcement learning but also learns a predictive model of its environment and uses that model to simulate several actions before it moves; it has no language, no body that matters here, and no biological evolutionary history. Is it Skinnerian, Popperian, or something else? Second, a large language model that was trained on text and can now produce a chain of reasoning to itself before answering; it has no body, no reinforcement from a real environment, and no evolutionary lineage. Is it Gregorian, or just a very fancy Skinnerian or Popperian artifact? I care alot less about the labels than in the rule he seems to be using, the other rules that fit his earlier verdicts just as well, and which of those two new cases, or what further case, would best tell the rules apart. If the earlier verdicts genuinely underdetermine the new cases, say what principle he'd be adopting with each possible answer.
