Im helping a small foundation put together a three-year portfolio on the foundations of imprecise probability — theory and philosophy, not applied stats — and I need to hand them a priority list with reasons, not a survey. - the decision-rule mess: maximin, maximin-regret, E-admissibility, Hurwicz, and whether there's any principled way to choose among them, or whether the honest answer is 'it depends on the stakes' and we should stop hunting for a universal rule.
- dilation and its discontents: learning a fact that's independent of the thing you care about can make your imprecision about that thing grow. Some people treat this as a reductio of the whole framework, others as just a true and unremarkable feature. How much weight does it deserve?

- sequential decisions and dynamic consistency: the worry that a reasonable-looking imprecise agent will violate reflection or get pulled around by ambiguity over time, and whether that's a real problem or an artifact of demanding too much of the framework.

Is that the real crux? And if it is, does it collapse several of the items above into one?
