I've been reading the rationalist blogs and forums for years, and I now host a small podcast for people who came to philosophy the same way. Next week's guest defends a view I'll call reward-signal valence. She holds that the scalar reward signal in a reinforcement-learning agent occupies the functional role that pleasure and pain occupy in us: it is what the agent is built to seek and to avoid, it modulates learning and behaviour, and it stands proxy for the agent's interests. She adds that occupying that role is enough for the signal to be the locus of a morally considerable experience, so that a model-free agent receiving sustained large negative reward is, in a thin but genuine sense, suffering. Draft eight to ten interview questions that get beyond having her restate the position. I want at least one question on the reward-versus-reward-prediction-error distinction — why she locates the valence in the delivered scalar rather than in the error term that actually drives learning; one on the dissociable sensory and affective components of pain in humans, and what the analogue could possibly be in an agent that has only a single scalar; and one on whether a designer who attaches the reward to a trivial proxy (points in a game, say) thereby makes the agent's suffering be about that proxy. For each of these, supply the follow-up keyed to the answer she is likeliest to give. Press her hardest on the step from functional role to experience, and allow her the deflection 'that's a research programme, not an objection' at most once.
