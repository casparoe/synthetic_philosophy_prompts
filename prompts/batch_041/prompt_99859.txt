Okay, background: I'm a retired geologist — thirty-odd years in seismic stratigraphy — and since I stopped working I've been reading philosophy nonstop, mostly the cause-prioritization / effective-altruism stuff, and now I'm meant to present my own view at a seminar and I want a sanity check first. Here's the package, and I'd like you to tell me whether it actually hangs together.

(1) I buy the importance / tractability / neglectedness triad as the right way to rank causes, and I treat it roughly as a product: a cause is worth more the bigger the problem, the more likely an extra dollar actually changes the outcome, and the less anyone else is already working on it.

(2) "Importance" I take literally — it's the number of people affected multiplied by how bad their situation is. No fancy weighting schemes, I just count.

(3) Neglectedness isn't really an independant thing to me. It's a proxy for diminishing returns: a cause is neglected when an extra dollar there buys more than an extra dollar elsewhere, because the easy work is already done. If I could measure that marginal return directly I'd throw the neglectedness column away entirely.

(4) I'm a presentist. I care about beings who are alive now or who come into being as a direct result of what we do in the near term. I dont accept weighting the far future by astronomical numbers — it feels like a math trick, and I'd rather help people who are actually here.

(5) Tractability I want to split in two, and this is my own coinage: call a cause "clutch" when a small push flips a big outcome, and "legible" when I can actually tell whether my push did anything. I only want to fund causes that are both. I spent a career watching money vanish into programs nobody could evaluate, and I'm not doing that again.

(6) Since I'm not sure which moral theory is right, I hedge — I'd spread across a few causes rather than bet everything on whichever one happens to top my list.

(7) Object level: I still think the best buys are plain global-health things — bednets, deworming, clean water — over the AI-risk stuff my younger friends keep pushing, because the AI-risk money is neither clutch nor legible in my sense.

So: is this coherent? I've got a nagging feeling that one or two of these quietly contradict each other and I can't see where, and I suspect a couple of others only look like they clash. Which are real conflicts, which are only apparent, how bad are the real ones, and what's the cheapest fix — and what would I be giving up by taking it? Rank them, don't just hand me a list of everything that could possibly be in tension.
