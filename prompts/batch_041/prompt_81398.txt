I'm writing the take-home essay for my upper-level seminar (mixed law and philosophy students, we've just done a unit on self-binding) and I need one question on precommitment that can't be answered by reciting Ulysses-and-the-mast stuff. I want it built around two cases I'm giving the students cold, no reading required: a woman with an early diagnosis of a progressive cognitive illness signs an advance directive refusing a particular treatment; two years later, still legally competent, she asks for that treatment, and the brother she named as enforcer of the directive refuses. Same week, a city council has a rule entrenched into the city charter by referendum, so that no future council can undo it without a two-thirds vote plus a waiting period, and a new council elected on an explicit mandate to undo it finds it can't. The question should make them work out whether those are the same structure or not: who authored each rule, who is bound, whether the bound party is the same party as the author, and what happens to the regress (who binds the enforcer of the enforcer). It should force them to say what would have to be true for each binding to count as a precommitment rather than one party simply overriding another, and whether any of the justification survives if the same person assumption fails.

So: give me the exam question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme worth 20 marks total. I want the marking scheme as exactly five numbered points and nothing else, each one a sentence or two, naming the specific move an A answer has to make and the marks it carries, and for at least two of them the specific wrong turn that should cost marks (students who think the directive is straightforwardly binding because autonomy stretches backwards, students who think the constitutional case is easy becuase majorities can always amend). Also make the question doable in 60 minutes. Dont give me an intro paragraph or a wrap-up at the end, just the question, the model answer, then the five points.
