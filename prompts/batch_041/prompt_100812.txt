I write a long-form ideas column, and I am working on a feature about how nations manufacture their own antiquity. For the piece I have devised two crude yardsticks and I would like your help applying them. The first is backdating yield: how far into the past a mechanism can push a nation's apparent origins relative to the effort it takes. The second is the seamlessness premium: how well a mechanism conceals its own recentness from the people who live inside the resulting tradition. Please sort the following mechanisms into tiers, from those that deserve the most explanatory weight in any account of national antiquity to those that deserve the least, giving one sentence for each to justify its placement: (1) state-sponsored ritual invention; (2) romantic folk revival and the standardising of dress, ballad and language; (3) print-capitalism and the rise of standardised vernaculars; (4) bureaucratic homogenisation through schooling, census and maps; (5) the retroactive reinterpretation of genuinely old customs as national symbols; (6) antiquarian forgery and literary fabrication; (7) diaspora and exile myth-making. I am less interested in the tiers themselves than in the reasoning, so make your criteria explicit and say where the two yardsticks pull against each other.

Please structure your answer as follows: 'Bottom line' (about 80 words), leading with the top tier and the overall shape of the ranking; 'The tiers' (about 350 words), the ranked tiers with a sentence on each mechanism; 'The two yardsticks' (about 150 words), explaining how you applied them and where they conflict; and 'What would move an item' (about 120 words), setting out the evidence or argument that would shift a placement up or down. Write in British English.
