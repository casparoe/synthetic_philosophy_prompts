ok so in psych class we did this lab with two dots. first one flashes on the left and its red, then like a tenth of a second later another dot flashes on the right and its green. if the timing is right, everyone reports seeing a single dot move from left to right and turn green before it reaches the right side, even tho the green dot wasn't there yet when they say it turned. our teacher says this is the color phi phenominon and that it proves the brain must be editing the experience itself, beacuse you can't remember a color change that hasn't happened yet. but my friend says no, the brain just edits the memory afterward, so the actual experience was red then green and the in-between was filled in later. our teacher calls the first view Stalinesque and the second Orwellian i think.

the part i'm stuck on is that the lab handout says to press a button when we see the color change, and then the teacher treats the button press time as the timestamp of when we became conscious of the green. what does that practice actually presuppose about the timing of experience? does using reaction times to date a conscious event only make sense if the Stalinesque view is true, or can an Orwellian view use the exact same button press data and just reinterpret it? and if we really took the Orwellian view seriously, what would have to change about how the experiment is designed and what we're allowed to conclude from it? also is that practice consistent with the same teacher telling us to trust our retrospective reports about when the dot changed color? that seems like it can't be both.
