I'm in a philosophy of science reading group and we've spent the last two sessions on Hempel's raven paradox, and I think I've got a case that the usual responses don't cleanly handle. Here it is: I'm walking down the street and in a shop window there's a framed color photograph of a white raven — I actually took the photo myself last summer, so I know it's genuine, some albino bird I caught in a park. Now, the photograph itself is a thing, and it is definately not a raven, and it's mostly white, so it isn't black either. So by the original logic it's a non-black non-raven, i.e. an instance of everything non-black is a non-raven, which is supposed to mean it hands a tiny bit of confirmation to all ravens are black. But the photo is obviously evidence *for* white ravens existing, which is evidence against that same hypothesis. So the same object seems to confirm it and disconfirm it at the same time, depending on whether you look at the object or at what it shows. Am I onto something here, or am I just confused about what counts as a thing?

Suppose I'm onto something. I wrote down five responses we threw around and I want you to rank them from best to worst — tiers are fine, but give me a sentence or two on each saying what it buys and what it costs. (1) The object and the content pull in opposite directions: as a physical thing the photo confirms a sliver, as a picture it disconfirms a lot, net disconfirmation, and the real lesson is that evidence is a proposition about the world, not a physical object you can point at. (2) The instance rule only applies to natural objects, so photographs, paintings, models and taxidermy don't count as instances at all. (3) Whether the photo is evidence depends on how you were sampling: if you were out looking at birds, a shop window tells you nothing about ravens; if you were out looking at non-black things, it tells you a sliver; so the case shows the question was under-specified rather than that anything is broken. (4) Instances have to belong to the same kind as the subject, and photographs and ravens aren't the same kind of thing, so the photo was never an instance — same reason a green leaf never was. (5) my own half-baked idea, which I'm alot less sure about: that what gets confirmed is each equivalent formulation of the hypothesis separately, and since the photo is an instance of some formulations and not others, the case shows that "instance" was never formulation-independent to begin with.
