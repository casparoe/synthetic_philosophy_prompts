For a while now I have been trying to work out, on my own, what it takes for several things to add up to one thing — whether a heap of sand or my own body is a single object over and above its parts, or whether that is only a manner of speaking. The people who have thought hardest about this seem to divide into two camps. One holds that any things whatsoever already compose a further thing, so that even wildly scattered objects exist and the question of which pluralities compose has the trivial answer that all of them do. The other holds that nothing is ever composed of parts, so that strictly speaking there are no composite objects and all our talk of tables and trees is a convenient fiction. I would like these two camps to produce the joint statement they could both sign after a serious attempt to collaborate, rather than another round of each explaining why the other is plainly mistaken.

I want the claims both sides accept — about the formal machinery they share, and about what ordinary experience and natural science actually commit us to; then the precise point where they part, each formulation worded so that the side it describes would accept it; then the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side; and for each crux a case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. I also suspect that part of this dispute is verbal rather than substantive — that each side can paraphrase away the other's claims without loss — and if so I want the statement to identify exactly which part, and why. One further constraint: both sides should say whether their disagreement survives if we allow that the answer might be a messy, disjunctive, or context-sensitive condition rather than a single exceptionless principle, since that possibility is the one that most troubles me.

And please tell me plainly if I have set the task up badly — if the two camps I have named are not the two that matter here, or if demanding a joint statement is the wrong thing to ask of this dispute — rather than producing the document anyway.
