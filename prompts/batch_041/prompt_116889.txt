I'm assembling readings for a graduate seminar on the units-of-selection debate and want something livelier than another summary. Write the minutes of the annual general meeting of a genome: deadpan corporate-minutes register, about 550 words, formatted as numbered agenda items with resolutions and vote counts. The shareholders are genes, the organism is a vehicle the board controls, and each agenda item should be a real accounting dispute from the debate rather than a pun. Include a motion to authorize an altruistic act that costs the vehicle but pays out in copies carried by siblings (the cost-benefit condition and the coefficient of relatedness must be handled correctly); an audit exception charging the bookkeeper with crediting genes that were merely passengers in a winning vehicle, and with crediting individual genes for effects that exist only when averaged across a whole gene pool; a minority shareholder who proposes to enrich itself by cheating its own transmission at the firm's expense; a proxy challenge from a caucus insisting that the only meaningful ledger is the group's; and a boundary dispute over whether the firm even ends at the organism, given colonial invertebrates and eusocial colonies. The personification is the joke, so it must never license a biological falsehood, and the gene's-eye view should win the arguments it actually wins; the target is the habit of treating genes as the agents and beneficiaries of evolution, not the view's explanatory power. Table the final motion. No citations and no named authors.
