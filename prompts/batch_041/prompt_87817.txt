I am a novelist, midway through a book about the wellness industry, and I have promised a short satirical column to a lifestyle magazine while I research my next chapter. Please write me a deadpan self-help piece, about 650 words, in the voice of an unfailingly serene modern Stoicism coach who has converted the old distinction between what is and is not up to us into a workplace productivity method. I want the piece to hold its character from first line to last — no winking, no authorial nudges — and I want the comedy to come from the doctrine itself rather than from invented absurdities: the discipline of assent, the claim that only virtue is genuinely good while health, wealth, and reputation are merely preferred indifferents, the unattainable sage offered as the working model, the promise that grief and anger are simply errors of judgment to be corrected. The best version will be genuinely funny to a reader who has sat with Epictetus and merely pleasant to one who has not. Give it a title and the shape of a real column. Don't hedge across several possible tones; choose one target and one voice and let the consequences follow. Finish with a note of no more than three sentences telling me where the column takes liberties with the Stoic material, or leans on a reading of it that specialists dispute, since I would rather not embarrass myself in print.
