Dr. Elara Voss is a Bayesian epistemologist known for her work on updating on uncertain evidence. Over the years, she has given the following verdicts on a series of updating problems.

1. A subject learns that a coin is biased towards heads with probability 0.7, and nothing else. Dr. Voss says the subject should update by Jeffrey conditionalization: set the probability of heads to 0.7, and leave the conditional probabilities of other propositions given heads and given tails unchanged.

2. A subject learns that if it rains, the picnic is cancelled, and learns nothing about whether it rains or whether the picnic is cancelled. Dr. Voss says the subject should conditionalize on the material conditional.

3. A subject learns that the conditional probability of cancellation given rain is 0.8, and learns nothing else. Dr. Voss says the subject should minimize the Kullback-Leibler divergence from her prior subject to the constraint that the conditional probability of cancellation given rain is 0.8.

4. A subject learns that the conditional probability of A given B is 0.6, where A and B are logically independent and the prior is uniform over the four combinations. Dr. Voss says the subject should minimize the Kullback-Leibler divergence from her prior subject to that constraint.

Now a new case. Judy Benjamin is parachuted into a field divided into Red and Blue territories, each of which contains an HQ and a non-HQ area. She has equal credences in the four possibilities: Red HQ, Red non-HQ, Blue HQ, Blue non-HQ. An informant tells her: I do not know whether you are in Red, but if you are in Red, then you are in Red HQ. So she learns the conditional: if Red, then Red HQ. She learns nothing else.

What would Dr. Voss say Judy should do? In your answer, identify the rule or rules that best fit Dr. Voss's earlier verdicts, say what each predicts for Judy's case, give your best guess at what Dr. Voss would actually say and why, and name a further case that would distinguish between the rules.
