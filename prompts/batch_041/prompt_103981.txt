I am designing a graduate seminar on scientific ontology. Consider a theory whose state space contains distinctions that its observation map cannot resolve, so some information about the theory's states is permanently inaccessible. Rank the following five rules for identifying the theory's ontology from most to least defensible, and give a one-sentence rationale for each: (1) posit only the equivalence classes of states that the observation map can distinguish; (2) posit the full state space but treat inaccessible coordinates as gauge; (3) posit only the invariants under the theory's symmetry group; (4) posit whatever entities are needed to render the dynamics deterministic; (5) posit the ontology that minimizes the number of independent posits while preserving all observable regularities. Then state which rule you would trust most for a case where the inaccessible distinctions are structurally entangled with observable ones, and which for a case where they are cleanly separable, explaining briefly why the ranking might shift between the two cases.
