Write a short dialogue between a legal moralist and a defender of the harm principle. The moralist argues that a society's shared morality is an interconnected fabric — a public good that is depleted unless the law sustains it — so the criminal law may punish conduct simply because it is immoral, judged by the ordinary reasonable person's sentiment rather than the expert's, and he adds that the harm principle is itself a moral doctrine, not a neutral test. The liberal replies that the erosion thesis is unproven, that granting the necessity of some shared morality does not license punishing every immorality, and that the public-good argument would equally justify enforcing a religion. Have them press each other on two hard questions: whether the liberal can explain the law's punishment of a wrong that harms no one (say, desecrating a corpse) without abandoning his principle, and whether the moralist's appeal to the reasonable person is consistent with his claim to be protecting society rather than enforcing a majority's tastes. Both should be equally competent, and each should answer the other's actual argument rather than a caricature.
