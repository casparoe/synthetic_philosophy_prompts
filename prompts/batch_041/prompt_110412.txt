I serve on the restitution working group at my museum, and before I draft our report I would like a consistency audit of my own position. Here is roughly where I have landed.

(1) A community has a moral right to the material heritage that constitutes it. Where an object left a community by force, or through a transaction the community had no real power to refuse, it should go back, and the present-day community is the bearer of the claim.

(2) There is no universal-museum exception to (1). The fact that an encyclopedic collection lets millions of strangers encounter an object does not outweigh the source community's claim, and I would not trade that claim away for a promise of long-term loans.

(3) I am not an essentialist about cultures. What a community is, is continually remade; there is no fact of the matter about which present group is the true continuation of a past one, and the groups that colonial administrators recognised are no more artificial than the ones that preceded them.

(4) Where an object left through a transaction that was lawful under the law in force at the time, however unequal the parties, the moral claim to return it is weaker than in a case of outright seizure.

(5) Returned objects should go to communities rather than to national governments, and they should end up on public display somewhere; I take the meanings a good display generates to be as real as any the object carried before.

(6) I reject inherited guilt, and I do not think moral claims are inherited the way debts are. All the same, I think we should hand back the objects at issue, and I do not think we need anyone's apology to justify doing it.

One verdict I hold independently of the rest: our holdings from a colonial punitive expedition, acquired under a transaction that was lawful at the time, should be returned without conditions.

Please audit this package rather than survey the restitution debate. Which of these commitments actually pull against each other, and how badly? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and what that would cost. Some of them, I suspect, only look like tensions; say which those are and why. Then tell me which single revision would clear up the most, and whether the rest of the package would still be recognisable as mine.

I would also like the answer to state how confident it is at each major step, and to single out the point it is least sure of, with a note on what would change its mind.
