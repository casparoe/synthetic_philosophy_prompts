Popular summaries often present Amartya Sen's capability approach as a third candidate answer to the equality-of-what question, alongside welfare and Rawlsian primary goods. I would like to put that framing to work. Suppose I have household survey data recording, for each person, several distinct capabilities: being adequately nourished, being able to appear in public without shame, being able to participate in the life of the community, being able to take part in political decisions, and so on. My plan is to aggregate these into a single capability index for each person — choosing weights for the components and summing them — and then to treat that index as the currency of distributive justice: locate the people whose index is low and raise it until the index is equal across the population. Since the three positions seem to differ only in the quantity they name, the real work seems to be choosing the right variable and then applying the familiar egalitarian formula. Two questions follow. First, is there a principled, data-driven way to derive the weights, or should I simply use equal weights? Second, once the index is built, is the correct target strict equality of the index, or should I instead maximize the minimum index? I would appreciate guidance on how to set this up rigorously.
