I'm a physician-scientist in metabolic medicine. I run feeding studies and spend my working life inside a specific vocabulary: energy balance and substrate partitioning, glycaemic and insulin responses, micronutrient adequacy, dose-response curves, the pharmacology of stimulants and sedatives, sleep architecture, circadian and seasonal variation, thermoregulation and acclimatisation, HPA-axis and inflammatory load, and the perpetual headache of inter-individual variation. I also read Nietzsche for pleasure, and I have been having a running argument with a colleague who insists that his late autobiographical writing is really just a physiology of values in an unscientific idiom. I would like you to test that claim properly rather than adjudicate it in the abstract. Take the dietetic and health-related material from the last years of his writing — the stretch where he treats nutrition, place, climate, and recreation as the real questions, claims that alcohol and other narcotics are poison to him, prefers light and simple food to heavy German cooking, moves cities as though climate were a drug, presents his own recovery as a physiological experiment on himself, describes himself as both a decadent and the opposite of one, and reads other philosophers' doctrines as involuntary confessions of their bodies — and restate it, as faithfully as you can, in my vocabulary. For each claim, I want the version I would actually have to write into a protocol: what the intervention would be, what the endpoint would be, what confounders he is ignoring, what the dose is, and what design could test it at all, if any could.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I care about. Which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion (say what gets bent and in which direction), and which have no counterpart whatsoever. I expect the third bucket to cluster around his insistence that these matters are irreducibly individual — that a regimen is good only for the particular physiology it serves — that illness and recovery are occasions for self-formation rather than states to be normalised, and that the point of the whole exercise is something larger than health as I would measure it. On that bucket I want your judgment on the interesting question: is the leftover a defect of my framework, because clinical nutrition reasons about populations, averages, and endpoints measurable in strangers, so it cannot even represent a regimen as a personal act of self-legislation? Or is it evidence that his claims were never claims in my sense, in which case the residue is not a translation gap but a category difference and my colleague is wrong? Please also run the traffic the other way: what does my vocabulary assert or presuppose that his cannot state at all — that a regimen can be bad for someone who feels well on it, that a physiological claim can be tested against strangers, that health is a state rather than a task? I know the shape of the standard answer, that he is a vitalist and I am a mechanist, so please do not give me that. I want the specific ledger of what survives contact between the two, and I want the honest half of it to be what fails.
