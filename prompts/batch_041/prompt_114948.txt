Neither of them has read a word of philosophy, so please judge the claims as they actually made them rather than as a philosopher might have meant them.

The scene: we'd spent the evening gluing a 1000-piece jigsaw and mounting it in a frame. Sam looked at it and said, "there — now it's one thing." Alex said, "it was already one thing, you just couldn't hang it on a wall." And off they went. Roughly:

(1) Sam: "Nothing became one thing. Before there were 1000 pieces and a frame; now there are 1000 pieces glued to a frame. Glue doesn't conjure a new object into existence. You can't bring a thing into being by sticking other things together unless something about the world actually changed, and 'now it's a picture' is a fact about us, not about the world."

(2) Alex: "Fine, here's the something. Some collections of things are working together as a single mechanism — the cogs in a clock, the cells in a heart, the pieces in a glued jigsaw that now holds its shape. When parts are organised so that they function as one unit, they compose a whole. When they're just lying next to each other, they don't."

(3) Sam: "That just tracks what we care about. A heap of bricks functions as a unit when it's holding up a wall. Two odd shoes function as a unit once you decide they're a pair. My pile of paperwork functions as a unit when I'm hunting for one document. Your criterion makes composition depend on our interests, and our interests don't make objects exist."

(4) Alex: "So it's vague round the edges. Everything's vague round the edges. There's no exact number of grains at which a heap becomes a pile, and nobody concludes that heaps don't exist."

(5) Sam: "Existence is the one thing that can't be vague. If it's vague whether the jigsaw-and-frame is a single object, there must be an exact moment during the gluing when it flipped from not-a-thing to a-thing, and there obviously isnt one. So it never became a thing — or else it always was one, glue or no glue. Either way your 'functioning as a unit' idea is doing no work."

(6) Alex: "Then you're stuck saying there's no such thing as me, or you, or the cat. Thats not a conclusion, that's a reductio."

(7) Sam: "I didn't say that. There's no table, only a load of particles holding that shape. No you, only a load of particles doing what you do. Same trick for the jigsaw."
