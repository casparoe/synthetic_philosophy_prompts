I am a novelist, and I am trying to finish a book whose two central characters have been arguing for thirty years: she has come to believe that taxation is theft in the plainest sense of the phrase, and he believes it is nothing of the kind, that it is simply what one pays for living under laws one had a hand in making. I have read their quarrel in a hundred drafts and it never resolves, because each keeps answering a question the other did not ask. What I want from you is the document they could have written together after a long, good-faith adversarial collaboration, one that both of them would put their names to.

Begin with what both can accept: about what property is, about what a state does when it collects, about what coercion is, and about whether either of them can stand outside some legal order and check who owns what. Then set out the disagreement, each side's position phrased as its own adherent would accept the wording, with no caricature of either. Then the cruxes, the smaller questions such that settling one would actually move one of them, and for each a case or argument both agree bears on it, with what each predicts it would show. I suspect one crux is the baseline: whether my taxes are measured against what I would have had without them, or against what I would have had in a world with no state at all, where the thing I am said to own might not have been enforceable in the first place. I suspect another is whether the wrong, if there is one, lies in the taking or in the pretension of authority to take. And I suspect a third is whether the word theft is here doing descriptive work or merely naming whatever taking the speaker dislikes. If part of their quarrel turns out to be only about words, say so, and say which part.

I do not need names or citations; I want the arguments themselves. Write it as one continuous piece of prose, with no headings, no numbered sections, no bullet points, no bold text and no tables, in the manner of a letter from two people who respect each other to a third. The cruxes and the predictions must be carried by the sentences rather than set off from them.
