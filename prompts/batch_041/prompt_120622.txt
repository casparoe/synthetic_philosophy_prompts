AI safety people often try to stop agents from having "side effects" by penalizing the gap between what the agent actually does and what would've happened if it had just sat there doing nothing — snapshot the world before and after, or check what the agent could still reach afterwards, and dock it for changing any of that. I get the appeal, but what does the whole move presuppose? It looks like it needs the do-nothing trajectory to be the value-neutral baseline, i.e. that "no impact" is a well-defined thing to aim at before anyone has said what's good. Which is funny, since the same crowd will happily tell you that impact can't be measured without a full value specification. So reconstruct the view of value and agency the practice actually commits to, separate what it strictly needs from what it merely happens to fit with, and tell me whether several incompatible value theories could underwrite the same penalty. And if the presupposition is wrong — if doing nothing is itself a choice with effects, or preserving the status quo is already a substantive value — what actually changes in how you'd build the thing?
