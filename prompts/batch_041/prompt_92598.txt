I run a small grantmaking outfit — four of us, roughly $2m a year, maybe sixty grants. More and more of the pitches we get are Pascal's-mugging-shaped: some tiny chance, one in a million or worse, of a payoff so enormous that on straight expected value it swamps everything else we fund, and the asker wants the whole budget or a big slice of it. I don't want to wave these off as crazy, and I also don't want to wire $400k to someone whose numbers come off a napkin. Can you turn whatever the philosophy here has actually worked out into a procedure my team can run on a pitch — questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where it stops with a no, and where it hands the call back to us because it's a values question rather than a factual one? I need it usable by someone who hasn't read any of this stuff, runnable in about twenty minutes per pitch, and honest about which steps are just taste dressed up as arithmetic — if the whole thing collapses into "multiply probability by value and compare," that's what I already do and it's no help. Run it on two pitches: one where the tiny-probability/huge-payoff story is at least internally coherent, and one that's obviously crankish but still spits out a giant number. If the procedure says fund the crank, or that we can't coherently refuse either, say that instead of softening it for me, and tell me what the procedure still can't do. And if you think the task as I've posed it is confused, just say so.
