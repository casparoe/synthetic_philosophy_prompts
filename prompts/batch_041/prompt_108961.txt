I'm putting together a set of cases about panpsychism's combination problem and I want one built to order rather than the usual mind-dust / split-brain stuff everyone recycles. Here's what I need.

Set up two cases, mirrored, that pull apart two things the combination problem normally lumps together. Call a combination quality-preserving if every phenomenal quality had by the parts shows up somewhere in the resulting experience, and subject-preserving if the result is a single subject having those experiences at once rather than a bunch of subjects each having their own.

Case A: quality-preserving but not subject-preserving. Nothing is lost from the parts' experience, and yet there's no one it's like to be the whole.

Case B: subject-preserving but not quality-preserving. There is exactly one subject at the macro level, and some quality the parts had is provably not in its experience.

Make the two cases as close to each other as you can in every other respect, so the variable is genuinely isolated.

Rules. Use only stuff a biologist or chemist would sign off on: no new physics, no quantum anything, no appeal to consciousness being non-physical. Don't reuse the standard props: no split-brain patients, no ant or bee colonies, no neurons swapped out one at a time, no zombies, no "the whole universe is one mind" setups. Each case gets at most a paragraph, and keep the scale ordinary - a person, an animal, a tissue sample, a colony of cells.

The point of the pair is to see whether the two halves of the combination problem can actually come apart, so a case that only works by stipulating its conclusion - just asserting that there's no subject, or just asserting that the quality is gone - doesn't count.

After the two cases: say what each is supposed to show, and be explicit about which feature is doing the work in each, including whether the case would flip if I changed just that feature. Then say how someone who thinks the combination problem has one big solution - a single move that handles both the quality side and the subject side at once - would attack these cases, and whether the attack lands.

Two more things. Keep seperate what's actually settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this - whether panpsychism is true isn't settled, and neither is whether the subject side is really distinct from the quality side, so don't write as if theres a consensus. And keep the whole thing under 700 words.
