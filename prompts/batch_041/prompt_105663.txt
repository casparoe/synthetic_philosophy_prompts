I have spent a few years reading in the cliodynamics and quantitative-macrohistory space — the program that treats large-scale political history as something with measurable regularities, in the tradition of secular cycles, elite overproduction, and the fiscal exhaustion of states — and I am now trying to establish whether the set of views I have assembled is internally stable. I would like an audit, not a survey of the field.

Here is what I hold.

(1) Large-scale history exhibits robust quantitative regularities — the accumulation of elites faster than the positions that can absorb them, the immiseration of the commoner population, the fiscal strain that precedes state breakdown — and these regularities are strong enough to license genuine forecasts decades into the future, not merely retrospective narratives.

(2) There are no historical laws in the strict sense. What actually happens is contingent: an accident, a bad harvest, a single decision by a single person can divert a trajectory that looked overdetermined, and any appearance of necessity is largely an artifact of hindsight.

(3) The only serious test of a macrohistorical model is out-of-sample predictive accuracy. A model that fits the past but cannot be run forward on data it was not fitted to, and beat a naive baseline, has told us nothing.

(4) I am a thoroughgoing methodological individualist. I do not believe in social forces, class dynamics, or the state as agents with causal powers of their own; whatever happens is the upshot of individual people responding to the situations they find themselves in, and any macro-level variable is at most a convenient summary of those responses.

(5) Historical systems are reflexive. A forecast of instability that becomes widely believed changes the behavior it describes, so the thing being predicted is partly constituted by the prediction itself.

I suspect that at least one of these has to go, but I cannot tell which tensions are real and which are only apparent. Please sort them. For every pair that is genuinely in tension, say how severe it is, what would have to be abandoned to resolve it, and what that abandonment costs elsewhere; for every pair that only looks incompatible, explain what dissolves the appearance. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and what it would leave unexplained.
