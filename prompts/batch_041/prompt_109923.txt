I want to state a version of hinge epistemology as sharply as I can, and then be told what would count against it.

The view is this. Certain propositions function as hinges for a practice: they are presupposed by any act of justifying, doubting, testing, or evaluating within that practice, and for that reason cannot themselves be justified or doubted from within it. I take the mark of a hinge to be functional rather than propositional — what makes something a hinge is the role it plays, not its content, its felt obviousness, or its origin. And I take the class of hinges to be open-ended and practice-relative, so that different practices, and the same practice at different times, may rest on incompatible hinge commitments, with no practice-neutral standpoint from which to adjudicate between them.

My worry is that this formulation is either too weak or too strong, and I want the defeaters laid out so that I can see which. Consider the case in which a practice abandons a hinge because things kept going wrong: the bridges kept falling, the predictions kept failing, the commitment kept costing its holders. If that is a hinge being criticized by evidence, then the functional criterion admits a kind of rational answerability that my formulation denies, and I want to know how much of the view survives the admission. If it is not, then I seem committed to saying that a practice whose hinges lead it into ruin cannot be faulted at all — a consequence I am not sure I can accept. I would like that fork treated as the main event.

Then the rest of it. Does the functional, open-ended criterion collapse the view into something more familiar — holism about central beliefs in the Quinean style, or reliabilism, or plain contextualism about knowledge — and if so, what does the hinge vocabulary add? Can the view describe a genuine epistemic confrontation between two practices, one in which each treats the other's hinge as a plain falsehood rather than as a different foundation? And what should I say about a hinge held by an individual but not by their community?

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. Tell me which of them a committed hinge epistemologist would concede and which they would dismiss as a misunderstanding, and say whether the view has been insulated by redescription, with every apparent counterexample reclassified as a change of hinge or a change of practice. I would find it most persuasive if you could show me a hinge abandoned for reasons the practice itself would recognize as reasons, without that abandonment reducing to a mere change of practice.
