Suppose you are advising a foundation that can support about ten researcher-years over five years on the conceptual and empirical question of whether habitat destruction reduces or increases wild animal suffering. The foundation will fund philosophy, welfare biology, and ecological modeling, but not direct conservation work. Which open questions should it prioritize, and in what order?

Treat these as candidate framings and rank them: (a) the hypothesis that typical wild animals in intact ecosystems have net-negative lives, so that habitat destruction could reduce total suffering even while harming individuals; (b) whether the right unit of analysis is the individual animal or a per-hectare welfare footprint, and how to define the latter when species composition changes; (c) the asymmetry between vertebrate and invertebrate biomass, and whether the sheer number of small invertebrates should dominate any welfare calculation; (d) the difference between one-time habitat destruction and chronic degradation, including whether the replacement land use (agriculture, pavement, secondary forest) supports fewer, more, or differently situated animals; and (e) the short-term suffering from displacement, fire, and starvation versus the long-term change in population and welfare. For each, say why it is or is not upstream of the others, how tractable it is in five years, and what evidence would move it up or down your ranking. Conclude with the two questions you would fund first and one you would treat as a distraction, and defend the ordering.
