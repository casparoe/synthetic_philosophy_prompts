Bit of a strange one. I'm finishing a long read on the consciousness-is-a-user-illusion crowd — the ones who say the self is basically the desktop of the brain: windows, icons, a cursor, and nothing behind it you'd call a viewer. Ive been leaning on that metaphor so hard in my drafts that I've stopped noticing its doing my arguing for me, which is usually the moment to check whether it actually holds.

So: how far does it hold? Give me the correspondence table — what plays the desktop, what plays the hidden machinery underneath, what plays the user, the cursor, the little spinner that tells you somethings happening — and then the places the mapping breaks. Separate the breaks that matter for the conclusion it's being wheeled out to license (the self is a fiction, there's no inner show, consciousness is a summary rather than a controller) from the ones that are just trivia. Three things I keep tripping over. On a real desktop you see the document through the icon and never the pixels. There's always somebody outside the screen doing the clicking. And the desktop is interactive — clicking really does move the file — so does the metaphor actually support the epiphenomenal reading people use it for, or does it quietly cut against it? Also, does illusion still mean anything once the user is inside the interface as well? And if the metaphor can't carry the conclusion, what would carry it better?
