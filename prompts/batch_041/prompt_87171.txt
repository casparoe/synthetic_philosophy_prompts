You are a philosopher of social science with expertise in Max Weber and in the theory of causal explanation in history. I want a careful audit of an analogy, not a summary of the literature.

Weber's Protestant ethic argument is often compressed into a railroad image: religious world-images act as switchmen, setting the tracks along which the dynamic of interests drives action. In seminar discussion this image gets used to license a specific verdict — that culture can be a cause of economic conduct without being its motor, since interest supplies the energy and the ethic only steers. A colleague objects that the image is doing the argument's work: it appears to grant culture causal status, but what it actually grants is a steering role, and steering is not what the Protestant ethic thesis needs. In that thesis the ethic seems to generate the drive rather than merely redirect one, and its most famous outcome — the hardening of the capitalist order into a mechanical shell that no longer requires the religious motive — is not something a switchman can produce.
