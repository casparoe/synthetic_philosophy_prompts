im a third-year phd student in hps and i have to open a workshop talk next month with something that isnt a slide, so im writing a poem. the talk is on lakatos on reseach programmes — mainly the part where you appraise a whole series of theories rather than one theory, and where you basically cant say whether a programme is progressive or degenerating while its happening, only in hindsight, once a rival has turned up.

so: a poem of five or six quatrains, loose meter, rhyme if you want it. one line has to come back verbatim in every stanza, and nothing in the poem is allowed to argue with it or rhyme with it — the rhyme always has to land somewhere else, so the recurring line never gets answered. that line is the hard core. make it a real commitment with teeth, not a platitude, or theres nothing at stake. everything else is the belt. in the first couple of stanzas the surrounding lines should look like the programme is getting richer and more specific — someone should say up front what kind of thing theyre going to need to add, and then actually add it — and by the last couple of stanzas the new lines should start to read like they exist only to save the line before them. if a piece of evidence shows up that doesnt fit, its always a belt line that bends, never the recuring one.

the poem must not deliver a verdict. theres one thing i really want though: a single line that reads as a bold prediction to someone inside the programme and as a rearguard rescue to someone outside it, and the reader cant tell which. last stanza can be a historian looking back, who is allowed to know, but cut them off before they give the judgement.

dont use the words hard core or protective belt or heuristic anywhere in the poem — if it needs those words to work it hasnt worked. a one-sentence gloss underneath for my audience is fine.

use a real episode if youre sure of the details, otherwise invent a programme and dont pretend its real; id rather have something honestly made up than something confidently wrong about newton.
