My opening move is this. I think in deep time, and here's what nags at me about cycles. In my old field we care a lot about the difference between systems that are ergodic — where the long-run statistics don't care where you started, so you just keep wandering back through the same states — and systems that aren't, where what happened earlier genuinely forecloses what can happen later. Evolution looks stubbornly non-ergodic to me. Once vertebrates have a backbone you don't get to rewind the tape and run it again without one; once oxygen floods the atmosphere that's a one-way door. If human history is anything like that, then sure, there are ups and downs, but the downs can't be the same downs — the tape won't rewind. So the "cycle" starts to look like an illusion, or at best a spiral with a direction. Can you actually defend real recurrence in a system with irreversible features like that?
