I keep flip-flopping about what to do when I'm not sure whats morally right, and I want to know what rule, if any, my gut is actually following. Here's my gut on a few cases. (1) I'm about 70% sure eating meat is wrong, 30% sure it's fine — I don't eat meat and it doesn't feel like a close call. (2) I'm maybe 55% sure it's fine to break a small promise to a friend in order to help a stranger a little, 45% sure it's wrong — I keep the promise anyway; gambling on near-even odds with someone else's interests feels wrong even when the numbers say go. (3) I'm 99% sure some church's rule against a blood transfusion is bunk, but they say disobeying means eternal torment; I'd take the transfusion. (4) I have maybe 1% credence in a view where the long-run future is astronomically important and the best use of a life is working on that; I mostly do act on it, or at least I feel bad when I don't. (5) I'm 60/40 that fish don't matter morally at all versus matter a lot — I don't eat fish.

Is there one principle that gets all of these, or do I need two? My own stab was: act on the view you most believe, unless another view you take seriously says the stakes are enormous and what it asks of you isn't humiliating — but I can't tell if that's a principle or just a description of my biases. (3) against (4) is where it hurts: in both, a view I'm nearly sure is wrong threatens or promises something enormous, and I shrug at one and obey the other. Tell me what principle, or small set of them, captures the pattern, what each says about cases I haven't listed, including how it copes with the fact that I have no idea how to compare how bad two different views say a thing is when their scales dont line up, and which of my verdicts I'd have to give up to be consistent.
