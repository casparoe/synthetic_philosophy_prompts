You are a philosopher of action and of the ethics of influence, advising a standards committee. Think carefully before you commit to a reconstruction, and begin by brainstorming the candidate ways of drawing the manipulation/persuasion line before choosing among them; argue from the case as described rather than citing literature, and do not supply reading lists or references.

The committee administers a national advertising code with three features. First, it prohibits any technique designed to work below the audience's awareness — subliminal presentation, some interface designs that steer users without their noticing. Second, it permits emotional appeals, social-norm messaging, and loss-framed warnings, provided the claims are not false and the advertiser is identifiable. Third, it exempts public-health campaigns from several restrictions that bind commercial advertisers, on the ground that such campaigns serve the audience's own ends. The committee is uneasy, because the same loss-framed, norm-based, affectively loaded technique counts as manipulation when a gambling operator uses it and as legitimate persuasion when a health agency does.

Reconstruct the theory of manipulation this three-clause pattern presupposes. Does it locate manipulation in the technique itself, in the influencer's intent, in the covertness of the influence, in its effect on the target's capacity for rational agency, or in the standing of the end pursued? Which rival accounts does the code quietly rule out, and is it internally consistent — can the exemption clause be defended without conceding that manipulation turns on ends rather than means? Separate what the code strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and identify where it is underdetermined, so that several incompatible theories would license the same practice. Finally, assess a proposed amendment: a technique counts as manipulative to the degree that disclosing it would defeat its effect. Would that test vindicate the code's distinction, and what would the code have to give up if the committee instead adopted a purely procedural standard, on which influence is legitimate just in case the target endorses the process of being influenced?
