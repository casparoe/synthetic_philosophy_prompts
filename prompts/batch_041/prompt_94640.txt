I write a science and ideas column and I'm doing a piece on the old question of whether you could have been someone else — or maybe nothing at all — and instead of another wall of text I want to hand readers a flowchart. So build me a decision tree that starts a reader at something they can actually answer and walks them to a named position on what de re possibility amounts to (what could have been true of this particular thing, as opposed to what's possible in general).

Start them at: "Do you think there's a fact of the matter about what could have been true of a particular thing — this person, this table, this election — or only about what's possible in general?"

From there, every branch node has to be a question a reader can answer without already knowing which label it leads to, and each leaf is a position. Leaves I want: transworld identity (the thing itself exists in more than one world), counterpart theory, haecceitism, anti-haecceitism, a "worlds are concrete" vs "worlds are abstract/ersatz" split, a leaf for someone who says the counterpart relation isn't resemblance but something else (a causal or historical one, say), and a leaf for someone who thinks de re modal talk isn't about worlds at all — it's primitive, or a fiction we tell ourselves.

Cases to route them through, each phrased so they can answer it cold (yes/no or a/b/c): a politician who lost an election — could he himself have won it, or only someone just like him? A table — could this very table have been made of ice while everything else about the world stayed the same? Two worlds that are perfect qualitative duplicates of each other except that they swap which person is which — is that a real difference between the worlds or not? Could a person have had different parents? Could one thing have been two things? And: is "being the same object" something that can hold between two different worlds at all, or is identity only ever a within-world relation?

Build it from the logical space first — lay out the combinations of answers, then drop the positions into the slots, so I can see which slots come out empty. When one is empty, say whether that's because the view is incoherent, because it's coherent but nobody wants it, or because nobody's bothered to explore it. If two of your leaves come out the same on every question, flag that rather than patching it over with a question that quietly assumes its own answer. Roughly 6-8 branch nodes and 8-10 leaves, please, it has to fit on a page.

Last thing: I've read alot of this literature and I know its a mess, so be explicit about what's settled and what's genuinely contested among people who actually know the topic. If, say, whether counterpart theory really counts as anti-haecceitism is itself disputed, put that at the node instead of asserting it as fact — don't hand me a live disagreement dressed up as consensus.
