I've been the designated drug-policy guy in my friend group ever since one of us got caught with a little weed and it turned into a whole ordeal, and I want to actually write down my position before the next argument, because I keep getting told I'm contradicting myself and honestly I'm not sure I'm not. So here's where I've landed. (1) Adults should be allowed to put whatever they want into their own bodies; using the criminal law to keep people from harming themselves is the state overstepping, full stop. (2) Most of the actual carnage around drugs isnt from the drugs themselves — it's from prohibition: the black market, the turf wars, the supply cut with fentanyl, the felony records that wreck a person's life long after the high wears off. (3) Which is why I'm a Portugal guy — decriminalize possession and personal use, treat it as a health problem, but keep producing and dealing the stuff illegal. That's the sweet spot. (4) And unlike the people who want full legalization, I don't want corner stores selling coke or even weed with ads and loyalty points; commercial legalization just builds a machine that has to recruit new users to grow, look at tobacco and booze. (5) And one more: I don't think "reducing drug use" is a legitimate goal for the state to have at all — that's just paternalism in a lab coat. The goal is reducing the harm that comes with use, not the use itself.

So: am I actually holding together, or have I smuggled something in somewhere? Go through the whole set, tell me which commitments pull against each other and how badly — rank the tensions, please, don't just dump them all as equally bad — and for the ones that are real, tell me what the cheapest fix is and what I'd be giving up by taking it. If some of them only look like they clash, say so and say why. I have a nagging feeling that one of these is doing a lot more work than I think it is, but I can't tell which.
