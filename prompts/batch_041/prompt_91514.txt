I'm in an ethics-of-climate-change seminar and we've spent the last three weeks on intergenerational justice and I feel like the discussion has completly stalled, and I want to figure out whats actually going on rather than just picking a side. Here's the shape of it as I see it. One camp treats the whole thing as basically a numbers problem: choose the right social discount rate and you can rank policies, and the real disagreement is whether a near-zero rate (future lives count the same as ours) or something closer to a market rate (future benefits count for less because they're further off, or because capital is productive) is the defensible one. The other camp thinks that whole framing is a category error, because the actual question is whether future people can be wronged at all, given that our choices also determine who they turn out to be, and whether something that doesn't make any particular person worse off than they'd otherwise have been can count as a harm in the first place. When the two camps meet, the first says "you're dodging the quantitative question," the second says "you're presupposing a moral framework you haven't defended," and nobody budges an inch.

My suspicion, and I want you to tell me if it's even right, is that this isn't one disagreement but two stacked on top of each other: a first-order dispute about the discount rate, and a second-order dispute about what kind of thing a wrong to a future person is supposed to be. I've been trying to cash this out with a distinction I made up, between future people as beneficiaries (their interests get weighed into some total) and future people as claimants (they're owed something, and the failure is a failure of a relation rather than a subtraction from a sum). On the beneficiary reading the discount rate is a genuine live question and the disagreement is substantive; on the claimant reading it's mostly beside the point and the fight is verbal or at least aimed at the wrong target.

So diagnose the impasse for me. Is it a substantive disagreement, a verbal one, two camps answering different questions under a single heading, or a clash of starting intuitions that neither side can argue for? Does my beneficiary/claimant distinction actually carve this at the joints or is it just repackaging the same old split with new labels? And be honest about what would count as progress here — a new arguement, a distinction, a reframing — or whether this is one of those disagreements where we should stop expecting convergence at all. Dont give me a reading list, just reason it out.
