Next month I am presenting a paper at a two-day workshop on the ethics of climate and population at an interdisciplinary institute. The audience will be about twenty people: mostly economists who build integrated assessment models and work on discounting and uncertainty, plus three philosophers -- two total utilitarians, one of whom is a longtermist concerned with expected total welfare over the very long run, and one prioritarian who works on the non-identity problem -- and a single Kantian-leaning deontologist who is skeptical of all consequentialist population reasoning. I am defending a view I call Floor-Plus-Concavity. It has two parts. First, a weak sufficiency floor: adding people whose lives fall below a minimal-decent-life threshold is disvaluable unless doing so is necessary to prevent an even larger number of people from falling below that threshold. The floor is not a fixed absolute line; it is indexed to the institutional and technological capacity of the world in question, so the same absolute welfare level can be below the floor in a high-capacity world and above it in a low-capacity world. Second, above the floor, the marginal value of adding an additional life at a given welfare level declines as the population grows, so the value function is concave in population size. The concavity applies to additional lives, not to additional welfare for existing lives. The upshot is that the repugnant conclusion is blocked without a lexical threshold and without average utilitarianism: some very large populations whose members have very low positive welfare are worse than much smaller populations with high welfare, yet large future populations are still permitted if their lives are comfortably above the floor. I also argue that the view can be action-guiding for climate policy, because the floor constrains which future populations we may bring about and the concavity gives a principled reason to care more about existing and near-future people than about adding vast numbers of marginally happy people in the distant future.
