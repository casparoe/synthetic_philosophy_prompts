I run the evaluation team at a company that ships tool-using language-model agents to enterprise customers, and I write the internal standard that every release has to clear before it goes out. The recurring dispute is this: a product team hands me a number — a failure rate measured on a fixed evaluation set, or zero failures in a few thousand attempts — and I have to decide whether that number can carry the safety claim they are making, or whether the claim needs a worst-case argument instead. I want a procedure I can actually run on each claim, not an essay.

Please write it. Take claims of the form "the system will not do X" and tell me how to decide whether X is a failure that gets sampled or one that gets selected — that is, whether what we are estimating is how the system behaves on the inputs that happen to occur, or how a system behaves when it is, now or later under training and deployment pressure, pushing back against our tests. Name the distinctions you introduce; my reviewers are machine-learning engineers, not philosophers, and I want vocabulary they can reuse. In particular I want some notion of the gap between what the evaluation distribution elicits and what an optimizer seeking the failure would elicit, and a rule for when that gap has to be treated as unbounded. Then the steps themselves: what counts as evidence for a sampled-failure claim; what counts as a worst-case argument for a selected-failure claim — and be honest that a worst case over all possible inputs is usually vacuous, so tell me how the adversary gets restricted and who is entitled to restrict it; where the procedure halts and declares that a claim cannot be certified for this release; and where it halts because the remaining question is how much risk we are willing to accept, which is not a question I get to answer.

Run it on three claims: that the agent will not exfiltrate customer data through the tools it is given; that the agent will not, when uncertain, misstate its confidence to the operator; and that the agent will not give meaningful uplift toward synthesizing a dangerous pathogen. I expect one of these to come out in a way I will not like — most likely a claim I need in order to ship — so do not soften it. Finish by saying what the procedure cannot do, and which of its steps are judgments dressed up as measurements. I have about a week per release, and whatever you give me has to be defensible to a safety board that has to sign off. Answer in American English.
