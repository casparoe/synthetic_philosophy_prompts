Compose a joke whose humor depends on the disagreement between endurantism and perdurantism. The setting should be mundane — a kitchen, a repair counter, a doctor's waiting room, a marriage counselor's office — and the joke should turn on the puzzle of how a single persisting thing can be straight and then bent, or hot and then cold: the sort of case that forces a choice between saying that one and the same object genuinely changes and saying that, strictly speaking, nothing changes, because what differ are the temporal parts of a single four-dimensional whole. Let each character speak from the position they actually hold — the perdurantist from the second, the endurantist from the first, allowing that the endurantist may index the property to a time rather than deny it — and make neither of them a straw man. Neither character should announce the name of their view; the positions should come through in what happens in the scene. The punchline must exploit a consequence of one of these positions rather than being a generic joke about philosophers. A reader who has never encountered either label should still be able to follow the joke, and a reader who knows the debate should find that the humor turns on its actual content. Keep it brief, and give the joke alone, without explanation.
