Construct a thought experiment that prises apart Marx's concepts of exploitation and alienation, in both directions. The case should be a single realistic setting — a workplace or a small market, not an island, a hypothetical, or a science-fiction economy — containing one group of workers who are exploited in Marx's technical sense (surplus value is extracted from their labour power) yet are not alienated, and a second group who are alienated yet not exploited. Do not let the scenario stipulate its verdicts: the exploitation must fall out of who owns the means of production and appropriates the surplus, and the alienation must fall out of features of the labour process itself — the division of labour, the worker's relation to the product, the compulsion of the market — rather than out of anyone's cruelty or bad intentions. Keep the scenario itself to a single paragraph.

The case is meant to test whether Marx's two concepts are genuinely separable, so it fails if the two verdicts are merely asserted rather than derived. After the scenario, take each group in turn and work through which of Marx's criteria for exploitation and for the aspects of alienation (from the product, from the labour process, from species-being, and from other people) are met and which are not. Then identify the one feature of the case doing the separating work in each direction, say what happens to the case if that feature is removed, and finish with how a defender of Marx would argue that the two cannot in fact come apart — and whether that reply succeeds.

Write in British English.
