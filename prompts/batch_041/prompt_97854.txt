I host a tiny philosophy-of-mind podcast and next month's guest is someone who's made a bit of a career out of the claim that machine translation, even the fancy neural kind, is shallow -- the Hofstadter-ish line that it's all pattern-matching with no grasp of what the words are about. He has a positive proposal too, and this is the part I want to dig at: he says genuine translation requires reading for the point, and more specifically that you can only render a speaker's meaning if you can represent the speaker's communicative *intention* -- and, his claim, you can only represent an intention of that kind if you're the kind of thing that can *have* intentions of that kind. So there's a same-kind requirement sitting under the whole thing.

Give me eight to ten interview questions that don't let him coast on "it's shallow, it doesn't understand." I want at least one question each on:

- whether his same-kind requirement would also disqualify human translators, like the phrasebook-and-pattern bilingual who nails idioms but can't say why, or the translator who gets the joke across without being able to explain it
- the difference between "understands" and "behaves in every observable respect exactly as if it understands" -- and whether he's got a test for understanding that isn't just our inability to imagine the alternative
- the mad-translator / philosophical-zombie-translator case: a system that produces perfect translations but (by stipulation) has no idea what any of it is about. Does his view predict it's impossible, or just that it's creepy?
- what "reading for the point" actually is: one capacity or a bundle, and if it's a bundle, which part is doing the anti-MT work
- the pun test: why doesn't a model that renders a joke it has never seen, into a language it has never been trained on for that joke, already count as doing the thing he says can't be done
- his scale-won't-help move: what would even count as evidence that scale *did* help, and does he owe us a falsifiable version

For each question, add a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give -- I know roughly where he'll run, and I want the second question already sitting there waiting for him. No softballs about how he got into philosophy, and please dont give me anything he could answer by just repeating his thesis in different words.
