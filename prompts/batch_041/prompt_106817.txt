I took early retirment from the lab a couple of years ago (chemist, forty years of it) and I've been working through philosophy ever since, mostly decision theory, which I find oddly gripping for a man who spent his life on reaction kinetics. Anyway, my daughter sat me down with Memento last week — the one where Leonard can't lay down new memories, so he tattoos instructions on himself and has to trust whatever his own notes and photos tell him — and it struck me about halfway through that it's more or less the absent-minded driver problem with a plot: a man who can't tell whether he's already made the decision he's trying to make, and who can only get anywhere by living off a policy, because he can't keep track of where he is in his own plan. What I want is a proper reading of the film through that puzzle. Where does the parallel really hold, and where does the film show up something the puzzle leaves out? My hunch is that the interesting bit is Leonard's tattoos and Polaroids as a precommitment device of a peculiar sort, since the self who wrote the instructions and the self who reads them don't want the same thing, and the later self can always scribble over the earlier one — whereas the driver in the puzzle has one consistent set of preferences and merely can't see which junction he's at. Then there's the backward telling, which leaves us as disoriented as he is; does the film make the audience run the same policy it's asking about? Please don't just reduce the film to an illustration of the puzzle, and don't bend the puzzle to fit the film — I'd like to know where the reading strains. Which scenes should I be watching hardest, and does the film add anything to the debate about how an agent ought to choose when he's uncertain of his own history, or is it just a vivid restatement? If it bears on the causal-versus-evidential business as well, say so, but only if it genuinely does.
