I'm a novelist, about midway through a book in which one of my characters, a slightly unhinged entomologist, keeps insisting that the ant colony in her garden is a person and that stepping on it would be murder. I keep flip-floping on whether she's right, so I want to put a quiz on the book's website: which side of the colony-mind question are you actually on? I want it to sort readers by what they say about concrete cases, not by which label they happen to like, so no question should just be do you think the colony has a mind. Give me nine questions, each a concrete little scenario with two or three answer options, and then a scoring key that maps the patterns of answers onto the positions.

Here's what it has to be able to tell apart. At one end, the view that only individual ants have anything mental and all colony talk is just shorthand, so the colony literally believes nothing. Next to it, the view that the colony does process information and can be called cognitive in a thin functional sense, but there's nothing it's like to be it. Then the view that there really is something it's like to be the colony, a subject over and above the ants. And finally the view that the whole thing is a pseudo-question, that there's no fact of the matter about whether the colony has a mind, that it's a useful fiction and nothing more. If you can see a fifth position lurking that isn't just a relabeling of those four, put it in and tell me why it earns its own leaf.

The questions should turn on distinctions like these: whether a mind has to be tied to a particular physical organization or can survive as a pattern, so cases where the colony splits into two, or where the ants get swapped out one by one for little silicon chips; whether a mind has to be located inside its owner or can live out in the world, like a colony that stores its memories in pheromone trails it keeps re-reading; whether there's any boundary at all, like two colonies whose foraging territories interleave, or a colony that absorbs a captured one; whether behaving as if you believe something, with no inner representing of it anywhere, actually counts as believing it; and the zombie case, a colony that behaves identically but with the lights off inside.

One more thing I care about: I want to know where the quiz is cheating. If two of those positions can't really be seperated by any of my cases, say so instead of inventing a question that pretends to pull them apart. And if some combination of answers is one that nobody in their right mind would hold, tell me that too, and whether it's empty because the view is incoherent or just because nobody's bothered to defend it.

Write the whole thing as continuous prose, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, the way you'd write a long email. I'll do the web formatting myself.
