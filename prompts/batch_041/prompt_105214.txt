I'm hosting a long interview for a podcast with a philosopher who's a committed defender of Integrated Information Theory — the idea that a system is conscious to the extent it forms an integrated whole of a certain kind. I don't want a recap of the axioms; I want the questions a fair but hostile critic would actually ask, each with a follow-up keyed to the deflection she's most likely to give. Draft eight to ten.

Things I definitely want to hit, roughly in this order: (1) the unfolding worry — an engineer takes a recurrent circuit with high Φ and unrolls it in time into a purely feedforward one computing the same function, and IIT scores the feedforward version at zero; both chips pass every behavioral test, so what is the difference supposed to be, and why isn't behavior the test? (2) that Φ as defined requires a minimization over every possible partition, which is intractible, so every real experiment leans on a proxy — ask her which one experience is meant to be identical to when the proxy and Φ come apart. (3) the exclusion/grain point: two clumps of tissue with decent Φ joined by a thin bridge — one experience or two, and is that a prediction or just whichever level happens to win the maximization? (4) a trap where I try to get her to admit the view is quietly panpsychist about simple systems, plus the follow-up where she says it isn't experience because it isn't maximally integrated. Finish with one question about what would actually make her abandon the theory, phrased so "nothing" isn't an available answer.
