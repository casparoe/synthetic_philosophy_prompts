I keep running into a state of mind I don't have a word for and it bugs me. Two examples. First, I'm grading exams with a rubric I helped write, and on one answer I'm sure the student messed up — but I also know that on this kind of question graders like me end up disagreeing with the key maybe a third of the time, and the person next to me, who's no worse at this than I am, reads it the other way. So I'm confident she's wrong, and I'm equally confident that my confidence doesn't track the truth here. Second, same shape in an argument with a friend: I can see the flaw in his reasoning, and I also know I'm exactly the kind of person who sees flaws that aren't there.

What's strange isn't being unsure — it's that the first-order belief doesn't budge while the second-order one says it has no business being there. I know there's alot written about disagreement, and about bias blind spots, and about humility, but is there a name for this exact combination, believing something while also believing you're not entitled to believe it? Is it even a coherent state, or is it the same kind of oddity as asserting something and adding that you don't believe it? And if it is coherent, what's the right move — drop the belief, keep it, or somehow hold both? I don't just want a label, I want to know what the thing actually is, how it differs from plain uncertainty and from knowing you're generally biased, and where the edges are.
