I am a novelist, most of the way through a book set in a city that governs itself by prediction markets: proposals are not voted on but traded, and a proposal is enacted only if the market on its consequences passes a threshold. The design I have written myself into is this: because the branch not taken never occurs, no one can ever score the market on the counterfactual, so the city randomizes — a fixed share of proposals are enacted or rejected by lottery, whatever the market says, precisely in order to manufacture the evidence that the markets require. My editor finds the device ingenious and weightless at once, and I suspect she is right that I do not know which conceptual questions are load-bearing. I have eight months. If the philosophical spine of the book is to rest on two or three open questions in futarchy-style governance, which should they be, and in what order? Defend the ordering: which questions, if resolved one way rather than another, would change how such a city actually operates; which are upstream of others; which can be dramatized rather than lectured about; and which of the standard objections to market governance are overrated and can be dropped. Tell me what would make me reorder the list.
