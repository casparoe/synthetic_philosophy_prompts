Some years of reading the rationalist blogosphere have convinced me that most disputes about social construction are two camps agreeing on the phenomenon and disagreeing only about a word, and I want the document that would show whether that is true. Take the deflationist, who says a social kind — race, gender, money — is nothing over and above a pattern of collective attitudes and representations, and the robust realist, who says such kinds are real and causally efficacious in their own right, that money buys bread whether or not you believe in it, while still granting that their existence depends on social practices. Draft the joint statement these two could put their names to. I want a numbered section of agreed claims — on what social construction should be taken to mean, on what must be true of a kind for it to count as real, and on race, gender, and money one at a time; then the residual disagreements, each phrased the way the camp holding it would want its own position stated rather than as a caricature; then the cruxes, the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. My own suspicion is that the live crux is whether a kind's being sustained by collective acceptance can be reconciled with its constraining people who do not accept it — whether there is a stable notion of social-but-not-merely-conventional — and a second candidate is whether social kinds support induction and explanation the way natural kinds do. For each crux, give a concrete case or argument both camps would grant bears on it, and record each camp's prediction about that case in advance. If part of the dispute is merely verbal — if they mean different things by real, or by social — identify that part and explain why it is verbal. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
