I'm a novelist working on a book set inside a company that sells curated information environments to subscribers, and I need one chapter to be an in-universe document: the onboarding FAQ for the company's two product tiers. Write it for me at around 600 words, as numbered questions and answers, in the deadpan voice of a support team that has fully absorbed its own marketing. The two tiers should map onto the real distinction between an epistemic bubble -- an environment that simply omits things, so you never meet the counterevidence -- and an echo chamber -- one that actively discredits the outside, so meeting the counterevidence only deepens your conviction. The satire has to get that distinction right instead of collapsing the two into synonyms; the joke is that the cheaper tier is the bubble and the premium tier is the chamber, and the FAQ should never quite admit that the upgrade consists of installing the discrediting machinery. Let the humor come from genuine features of the phenomenon: the pre-emptive discounting of outsiders, the redirection of trust toward the in-group, the self-sealing quality of the chamber against the merely incomplete quality of the bubble, and the awkward fact that a subscriber cannot easily tell from the inside which one they are in. Keep the company from winking at the reader -- stay in voice throughout -- so that someone who knows the epistemology laughs while someone who doesn't can still follow it.
