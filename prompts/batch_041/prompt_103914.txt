I am a philosophy professor preparing a case study for a graduate seminar on the metaphysics and epistemology of classification. I want you to analyze the ongoing controversy over the red wolf in the southeastern United States as a case study in the species problem. You may take the following salient facts as given: the red wolf was listed as endangered, a captive breeding and reintroduction program was established in North Carolina, and subsequent genetic work has been read by some researchers as showing that the red wolf is not a distinct evolutionary lineage but a hybrid of gray wolf and coyote, while others argue that it is a distinct lineage with a complex history of admixture. The legal question is whether the Endangered Species Act’s protections can or should extend to a population whose taxonomic status is contested, and how the Act’s inclusion of subspecies and distinct population segments bears on that question.

I want you to use the species problem not as a single puzzle but as a cluster of problems—at minimum the grouping problem, the ranking problem, and the naming problem—and to bring in the contrast between species as natural kinds and species as individuals, the biological, phylogenetic, ecological, and general lineage conceptions of species, and the role of normative and pragmatic considerations in conservation classification. Then do two things. First, map the red wolf case onto these theoretical distinctions: which species concept, if any, makes the red wolf a species; what work the distinction between species and hybrid is doing; where the relevant uncertainty is empirical and where it is conceptual; and what the case reveals about the species problem that a purely abstract discussion would miss. Second, take a definite position: say which species concept you think conservation law ought to employ, whether the red wolf should be protected as a species or as something else, and why. Do not simply lay out the options. Defend your choice against the strongest objection you can formulate, and be explicit about the point at which your argument relies on a value judgment rather than a biological fact. I do not need citations or a reading list; I need a disciplined case analysis that runs in both directions, from the theory to the case and from the case back to the theory.
