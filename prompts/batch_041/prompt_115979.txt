Two colleagues of mine have argued for years over whether sustained prosperity and order can make an authoritarian regime legitimate: one holds that performance is itself a ground of legitimacy, the other that it produces at best compliance or acquiescence. I want the joint statement their adversarial collaboration should have yielded, not a verdict from me. Fix first the sense of legitimacy in dispute — entitlement to rule, warranted expectation of compliance, or sociological acceptance — and say how much of the disagreement survives that disambiguation and how much of it is merely verbal. Then bring both views to bear on a single case: a city-state with one ruling party, no competitive elections or independent press, but competitive recruitment into the elite, a disciplined civil service, functioning local grievance channels, and three decades of rising living standards, followed by a decade of stagnation and declining living standards with no change of government; each side should say in advance whether legitimacy, on its own conception, lapses, persists, or was never present. Give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two with a short enumeration allowed inside an item, and no introduction or conclusion: the claims both sides can sign; the residual disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would accept; the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions whose settlement would move at least one side; for each crux, the consideration both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about the case above recorded; and any part of the dispute that turns out to be merely verbal, with reasons. Write in American English.
