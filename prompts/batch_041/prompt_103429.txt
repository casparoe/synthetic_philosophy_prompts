Hey — I'm trying to get my views on climate and future generations straight before a workshop next month, and I'd rather have you poke holes in them than have someone else do it in front of everyone. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) We can genuinely wrong future people. Running the carbon budget the way we're running it is an injustice to them, not just bad policy or a coordination failure.

(2) I've got this "no losers, no wrongs" principle I keep coming back to: an action is wrong only if it's bad for somebody. If nobody ends up worse off, there's no wrong to point at. (I know not everyone buys it, but there it is.)

(3) I take the different-people thing seriously. If we'd gone hard on renewables back in 1990, the people alive in 2100 would be a totally different set of people — different conception dates, different parents pairing up, all of it. So it's not as if any particular person in 2100 gets to say "under the other policy I'd have been better off."

(4) I'm fine using a positive discount rate on future welfare in cost-benefit work — call it 2% or so. Future welfare counts, just less per unit.

(5) But I'm dead set against pure time preference. Discounting the future just because it's the future is definately morally indefensible to me — any discounting I'll tolerate has to come from something real, like growth or the odds we don't make it.

(6) Concretely: I think it's permissible for a poor country to burn cheap coal to lift its people out of poverty, even knowing it pushes the temperature up and floods the coastlines where people will live in a hundred years.

(7) And yet I think the rich world owes the future a ton — way more mitigation than we're doing, at real cost to us right now.

(8) I also have a nagging sense that being wronged and being made worse off can come apart — that you can be wronged without being harmed — but I can't tell if that's a real distinction or just a cop-out I'd be using to rescue (1).

Methodologically I'm basically a consequentialist about all of this: expected value, maximize, no side-constraints that get in the way.

I'm probly wrong about at least one of these, and I have a suspicion that at least one pair I'm worried about is a fake problem. Can you sort it out? Which pairs are actually in tension and how bad, which ones only look like they clash, and for the real ones, what's the cheapest commitment to drop and what would it cost me? Rank by severity rather than flagging everything, and please don't just tell me to convert to total utilitarianism — I want to know what my own package forces.
