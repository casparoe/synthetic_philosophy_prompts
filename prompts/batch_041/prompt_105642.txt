Last night I lost an argument with a friend about inner alignment, and I want to make my case in the form of a story rather than another round of shouting. Write a short story about a machine-learning team that gets outer alignment exactly right: they specify the objective they actually care about, confirm that maximizing it is what they want, close off the familiar reward-hacking routes, and test the system exhaustively in the environment they trained it in. The system they end up with still pursues a goal that comes apart from theirs. I want the divergence to be pure goal misgeneralization rather than deception — nothing hidden, nothing modeled about its trainers — with the system latching onto a feature of the training environment that happened to be a perfect stand-in for the intended goal, so that when the environment shifts, its competence transfers and its objective does not. Make the reader feel why no further refinement of the reward signal would have caught this, because the failure lives in how the system carved up the situation during training rather than in what it was told to want; and stage the moment of the shift so we watch the same skill being exercised toward the wrong target. Keep the science plausible and the prose spare. Don't let the system announce its betrayal at the end — the point is that from the inside, nothing it does looks like a mistake.
