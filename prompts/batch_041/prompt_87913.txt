I am currently taking a seminar on dispositions and causal powers, and before we spend the term comparing accounts I want to pin down what any account is actually supposed to achieve. Please write out the desiderata for a theory of disposition ascription: what must a theory deliver to be adequate as an account of what it is to have a disposition — to be soluble, brittle, irascible, or a key that opens a particular lock?

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and keep two things separate: the phenomena the theory must explain, and the distinctions it must not collapse (having a disposition versus manifesting it; a disposition versus the counterfactual that usually accompanies it; a disposition versus its categorical basis).

The list needs to handle, at minimum, the standard trouble spots: finkish cases, in which the stimulus would remove the disposition at the very moment of triggering; masking and antidote cases, in which disposition and stimulus are both present but the manifestation is blocked; and mimicking cases, in which the manifestation occurs although the disposition is absent. I would also like two cases of my own added to the list. First, a disposition that can be exhausted by a single manifestation, so that it is genuinely present beforehand and genuinely gone afterwards — I want to know whether the theory must count this as one disposition, or as a disposition plus a liability to lose it. Second, a disposition that could never manifest because the world contains nothing capable of supplying its stimulus, yet which we seem to want to ascribe anyway. Add whatever further requirements follow from the fact that dispositions such as solubility and brittleness have to be realized in ordinary physical stuff, that they must be able to do explanatory or causal work, and that their ascriptions must not be vacuous or circular.

Then sort the requirements into those that are non-negotiable and those that are really the preferences of one camp — the reductive conditional analyst, the dispositional essentialist, the categoricalist. Finally, and this is the part I care most about: identify which subsets of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement does the most to rule out a simple conditional analysis. No bibliography or reading list — I want the specification itself, with the case behind each requirement and the incompatibilities argued rather than asserted.
