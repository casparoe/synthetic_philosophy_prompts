Alright, I've been chewing on this for a few months and I want somebody to try and knock it over.

Here's the view, and I've probly mangled the official version of it. Being free isn't mainly about whether somebody is actually interfering with you right now. It's about whether you have to watch your back. If there's a person — a boss, a landlord, a spouse, a cop, an immigration officer, whoever — who could wreck your life if they felt like it, and nothing stops them but their own mood or their own good manners, then you're not free, even if they never do a thing. And I want to push that harder than I've seen it pushed: the unfreedom isn't just the risk, it's the labor. You're doing work. You're being careful, you're staying on their good side, you're not saying the thing, you're keeping the option open. That's a tax you pay every single day whether or not the bad thing ever happens. So a society where nobody interferes with anybody but everybody's a little scared of somebody is not a free society, no matter how many rights are written down.

(I know there's another idea out there where freedom means being your own master or ruling your own desires or something. I never got that one, but if my thing is secretly that, tell me.)

Now here's what I actually want. What would count against this? Not "here's a critic who disagrees" — I mean what should change my mind. Sort it by how much it should move me. And tell me straight whether the view is secretly empty. My worry: if having to watch your back includes depending on anybody at all — your family, your customers, your health, the weather — then everyone is unfree all the time and I've just renamed "life is hard." Is there a line, and where is it? Does it matter whether the powerful person could do it on a whim, or whether they'd have to answer to somebody for it? And does it matter whether I *know* I'm being watched over, or is it enough that I am? Because if it's the first one, my view is about my feelings, and I really don't want that.

Cases I keep going back and forth on: a worker whose boss is genuinely kind but could fire them tomorrow for no reason; a tenant who's never had a problem with a decent landlord; a marriage where one person holds all the money and is lovely about it; the citizen of a country with great laws and a police force that mostly behaves. Am I saying all of them are unfree? If yes, fine, but then tell me what *wouldn't* be, and whether that's a real place or a thought experiment. Also, is this just the old republican thing (Pettit, I want to say?) with extra steps, or is the vigilance/labor bit actually doing new work? And what would the people who like this kind of view themselves admit counts against it — not just what the other side says.

Last thing. I'd be most persuaded by a case where somebody is obviously unfree and my test says they're free, or obviously free and my test says they're not. If all the counterexamples turn out to be me re-describing ordinary discomfort, just say so.
