I sit on the scientific advisory board of a small institute that funds weird computing hardware, and I have to write the board's opinion before Thursday on a dispute that has turned into a genuine philosophy-of-mind fight. One of our labs built a tabletop device: a shallow tray of a few thousand magnetized beads, agitated, with coils around the edge that respond to an input signal. The beads clump into mesoscale clusters that persist for a second or so and then break up, and the lab reads out the cluster configuration to do a simple classification task. They insist the clusters are where the computation happens, and they've started writing causal claims in their papers and grant text like the cluster state drives the bead motions, which is the kind of thing the careenium metaphor invites (beads = simms, clusters = simmballs, tray = careenium) and which our other lab says is just backwards: only bead-level collisions cause anything, cluster talk is a lossy bookkeeping description, and it should be kept out of causal claims entirely. Panel keeps going in circles, partly because when someone asks what experiment would settle it, nobody can answer.

There's also a third proposal on the table from the same group: a level-swap experiment where they perturb the cluster configuration with a shaped field pulse while trying to hold the bead-level microstate as fixed as they can manage, and the reverse, and see which perturbation propagates. Two of my board members think this is either impossible or question-begging, since the clusters are presumably nothing over and above the beads, and one of them keeps saying you can't intervene on the higher level while holding the lower level fixed, so the whole design is confused.

Draft the opinion I'll circulate. I need a concrete bottom line: fund, reject, or fund with conditions, and say what we should do about the causal language in the existing papers and in the standing review criteria for future proposals. Take the strongest version of the losing side seriously rather than just asserting a level. And tell me honestly which part of the recommendation is the soft spot, plus what result from the level-swap experiment would actually make me change my mind, so I don't get caught flat-footed when the chair pushes back.
