Ok, so I want to use the covid vaccine patent fight as a test case for the justifications of intellectual property, and I want you to do the mapping properly in both directions, and I dont want alot of hand-waving. Take the episode facts below as given — you can't look anything up, and neither can I, so we're working from these:

In October 2020 South Africa and India proposed at the WTO that a broad set of TRIPS obligations be waived for covid countermeasures, patents very much included. What actually came out of the June 2022 ministerial meeting was much narrower: a decision letting eligible members authorize use of patented vaccine technology without the right-holder's consent, for five years, and it dropped the requirement that production under such an authorization be mostly for the domestic market. It covers vaccines only, not therapeutics or diagnostics. And it is about patents — nothing in it touches trade secrets or manufacturing know-how. Meanwhile, the thing nearly everyone agreed was actually binding was manufacturing capacity and process know-how, not the patents as such.

Now the theory. Run the three standard justifications for owning ideas over this case: the Lockean one (you mixed your labor in, so it's yours, subject to some proviso about leaving enough and as good for others); the personality/Hegelian one (property as an extension of the self, expression, a stake in the world); and the utilitarian one (patents as a second-best remedy for a public-goods problem, a prize for R&D that would otherwise be underproduced).

Things I want you to actually take seriously rather than gesture at:

1. Nonrivalry and manufactured scarcity. The dose is rival, the recipe isn't. So the patent isn't creating the value, it's creating excludability. What does each justification have to say about a legal regime that manufactures scarcity in a thing that isn't scarce by nature, and does the case make that look like a bug or the whole point?

2. The attribution problem. This R&D was a joint product: decades of publicly funded basic science, public money and advance purchase guarantees, and then private firms on top. If the Lockean claim is "you made it, so it's yours," who made this? Does the desert claim even attach to the right-holder, or to a chain of funders and researchers that no single patent owner represents?

3. The know-how gap, which I think is the sharpest thing here. The waiver hands out a permission, not a capacity. What actually blocked production — the process knowledge, the cell lines, the tacit stuff you only get from people who've done it — is held as trade secrets and isn't in the patent at all, and to whatever extent it is in the patent, reading a patent doesn't teach it. So the regime distributes legal rights over an object while the bottleneck sits somewhere the regime doesn't reach. What does that do to the utilitarian story, which is supposed to be about getting more of the good produced? Does the episode show the incentive story is fine, broken, or simply irrelevant to this case?

4. Platform technologies and thickets — whether the relevant mRNA patents are more like a foundation everyone else must build on than like a discrete invention, and what that does to the personality justification in particular, which seems to want a tight creator-object link.
