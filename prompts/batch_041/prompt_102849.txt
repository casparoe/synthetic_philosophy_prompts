Write a sestina for a graduate seminar on the philosophy of pleasure, on the heterogeneity problem: the pleasure of a warm bath, of a resolved cadence, of a finished proof, of a friend's return seem to share no single property, and a hedonist in need of one must choose among a distinctive sensation, a hedonic tone, a taking-pleasure-in, a mode or manner of experience, a kind of wanting, and the bare word pleasant. Let those six serve as the poem's end-words, so that the form's rotation keeps attempting a unification that the envoi, forced to gather all six at once, visibly fails to complete. Give each candidate its moment of plausibility -- a single feeling tone does seem to cover the bodily pleasures -- before turning up the cases that embarrass it: the intellectual pleasures, and the mixed ones in which pleasure is inseparable from pain, as in the grief a piece of music carries or the ache inside a stretch. Treat pleasure as a determinable with no common determinate core, the way being colored is not itself a color. The poem should be accurate enough that someone who teaches the problem recognizes each theory by its commitments rather than by any label, and good enough to move a reader who has never heard of it.
