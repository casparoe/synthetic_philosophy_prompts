A small foundation has asked me to design a two-year, three-person conceptual research agenda on one narrow family of views: negative utilitarianism with a lexical threshold. As I understand the family, it holds that disvalue below some threshold is lexically prior to positive value, so that no quantity of mild goods can outweigh a single life of sub-threshold suffering, while above the threshold ordinary trade-offs resume. I want to know which open questions about this family most deserve the effort, and in what order.

Give me a defended agenda rather than a survey. For each question: why it is upstream of the others, how far it can be pushed by conceptual argument alone, whether its answer could make the view collapse rather than merely refine it, and what would move it up or down my list. Assume I cannot check any literature and want the argument, not the sources.

Two items I especially want placed. First, the threshold itself: can anything be said about where it lies without begging the question, and what happens when its location is uncertain? If I hold a probability distribution over where the threshold falls, does lexical priority survive aggregation, or does it wash out into ordinary expected-value weighing, and if it washes out, is that a reductio or a discovery? Second, commensurability: is the question whether pleasure and pain can be weighed against each other at all a distinct problem from the threshold question, or the same problem in different dress? Relatedly, say whether the view is best formulated intrapersonally, as a claim that no amount of a life's own pleasure compensates its sub-threshold suffering, or interpersonally across lives, and which formulation does the real work.

Close by naming which of these questions you would deprioritize even though it looks central, and what a genuinely good two-year result would look like for the two or three you keep.
