Since retiring from field ecology, I have been sitting in on a philosophy seminar, and we are closing a term on Kierkegaard. I would like a catch for four voices—a round in which the entries overlap so that the same lines can be heard as new phrases—on his critique of the press and the public. The structural point I want it to make is that the public, for Kierkegaard, is not a crowd or an aggregate of individuals but a phantom: an abstraction that is nobody and yet presumes to speak for everyone. The press gives this phantom its daily voice, and leveling is the process by which reflection turns concrete individuals into interchangeable spectators. A catch is apt because its voices are staggered: each singer repeats the same words, but the overlap produces a collective utterance that no single voice contains, and the words degrade into chatter. Write the catch so that the refrain, taken alone, sounds like a private reflection, but when the four voices are overlapped it becomes the empty unanimity of the public. Use a two-line refrain of your own devising, and let the four voices enter at staggered points—voice one, then voice two after the first line, and so on—so that the overlap is visible on the page. It should be recognizable to someone who knows Kierkegaard's critique of the press and the public—leveling, chatter, the phantom public, the press as an instrument of reflection—without quoting him or naming his works. Do not use quotation marks from Kierkegaard; the poem should be entirely in your own words. Keep it to roughly sixteen to twenty lines, with the four entries clearly marked. Begin directly with the catch itself (a title is fine); no preface, no commentary, no explanation after.
