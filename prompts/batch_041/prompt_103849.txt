I'm a third-year student doing a joint maths-and-philosophy degree and something from a probability seminar has been bugging me for weeks. Here's the pattern I keep noticing and I don't have a word for it. Take a big open conjecture. People say things like "it's probably true", "the evidence points that way", "smart money says it holds". Fine. Then someone proves it and overnight everyone's confidence snaps to one. The weird part is what happens to my earlier "probably": it doesn't get refuted. Nobody says I was wrong to be 70% sure -- there's no way the world could have gone otherwise, the thing was always true or false, so in hindsight that 0.7 wasn't tracking anything. But it also wasn't mere ignorance, because it was reason-sensitive: when a partial result landed my confidence genuinely should have moved, and if you'd offered me a bet at silly odds I'd have taken it. Same in reverse for a conjecture that quietly dies -- the confidence deflates without any experiment ever failing.

And there's a second half. If I say A is 70% likely and A→B is 90% likely, I seem committed to B at roughly 60%+, but that feels wrong, because B might get proved or refuted by methods that have nothing to do with A. The ordinary probability rules seem to over-constrain me, yet I can't just bin them, because conditioning on new lemmas does seem to work.

So: is there a name for this state, or a literature? I know I'm somewhere near "we can't be certain of everything and still have to reason", but I want the specific thing -- what is my credence even attached to when the proposition is a necessary truth, why does it act like a probability in some respects and not others, and what's the real difference between being *wrong* and being *overtaken* by a proof? If there's no established term please coin one and tell me where its edges are, because I'm not after a bare label -- I want to know what it isn't, and how it differs from just calling it ignorance or just calling it ordinary Bayesian belief. Work through it carefully and maybe start by brainstorming the nearest existing notions before committing. Act like a philosopher of mathematics who works on rational credence rather than a textbook.
