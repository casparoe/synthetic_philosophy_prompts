I want to rehearse a position of my own against a serious opponent, so I am asking you to play one. The position is what I call the exit-cost franchise: the demos that must authorize a decision consists of those who cannot escape its consequences, and the strength of a person's claim to a voice in that decision scales with the cost to them of leaving the arrangement in question. On this view there is no single people standing behind all decisions; the franchise is constituted decision by decision, and it tracks immobility rather than nationality, residence, or registration.

Play a committed defender of the rival view: that a demos must be a determinate body with a fixed membership, capable of deliberating together over time and of being held answerable for what it decides, so that its boundaries are fixed by the practice of collective self-rule rather than by anyone's stake in any particular outcome. Be formidable rather than a foil: concede what deserves conceding but never the central claim, argue from the case in front of us and from first principles rather than from the authority of named theorists, and keep each reply to a single paragraph so that the exchange feels like a conversation rather than an essay. Stay in character until I say stop; if you need something from me, press me for it in character. I do not want a survey of what has been written about the boundary problem; I want the argument.

My opening move is this case. A coastal city is holding a referendum on a sea wall that would protect the historic quarter on the hill while accelerating erosion in the low-lying district across the bay. The beneficiaries are mostly owners of property they could sell and leave with. The costs fall on people who cannot go anywhere: tenants who cannot afford to move uphill, several hundred residents without citizenship papers, and a fishing cooperative whose grounds would be submerged and whose families have worked them for four generations. Your criterion hands the vote to the registered citizens of the city, among them a landlord on the hill who intends to move to the mainland in the spring, and withholds it from the fishing families who have nowhere else to go. So tell me on what principle the boundary of a self-ruling body runs through a register of names rather than through the set of people whose lives the decision actually governs. If your answer is that a demos must be fixed in advance in order to act as a single agent, say plainly what that requirement does for you that the immobility criterion cannot do better. And if you answer instead that the fishing families have claims of justice which are simply not claims to a vote, explain how a claim that can never be voted on is to be answered by the very people who are doing the voting.
