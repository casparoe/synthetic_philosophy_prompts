I am writing a novel set in a near-future city where automation has made paid employment optional. My working premise is that the meaning of work is essentially a matter of social recognition—people need their contributions to be seen as valuable—so I planned to resolve my protagonist's crisis by having her join a cooperative whose members publicly honor one another's unpaid projects. I have been distinguishing positional meaning (the meaning of occupying a recognized role) from performative meaning (the meaning of the activity itself), and the cooperative is meant to restore the former without wages. A philosopher friend objects that recognition cannot be conferred by mutual agreement and that automation may erode the conditions under which positional meaning is possible. Is my friend right that the cooperative solution is incoherent, or can a community simply decide to treat one another's projects as valuable and thereby restore what paid work provided?
