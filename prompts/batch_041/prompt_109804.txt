I need to write a two-page research proposal by the end of the month for a small internal seedcorn grant — three months, about £1500, no teaching relief, basically enough for time and a few coffees — and I'd like to use it to finally make something out of an itch I've had since my biology undergrad. My patch is philosophy of biology, and the itch is the spandrels critique of adaptationism. Whenever someone says a trait is a spandrel or a byproduct, they sound like they're making one claim but I think they're making three: that it wasn't built by selection for the effect we care about; that it currently has no function of its own; and that adaptationist explanations are in practice unfalsifiable. My suspicion is that a lot of the dissagreement in that literature is people answering one of those while someone else asked another, and that the third one does most of the rhetorical work without ever being defended in the same currency as the first two. I also want to argue something I haven't seen put quite this way: byproduct claims and adaptation claims get treated asymmetrically — "it's an adaptation" is the thing that has to earn its keep, while "it's a byproduct" gets to be the default — but a byproduct claim is a claim about the causal history of the trait just as much as an adaptation claim is, so it should carry the same evidential burden. Where I'd like the project to actually spend its time is a case I've been calling a spandrel with a drag: a trait that's a byproduct of something else, has no function of its own, and is positively costly — selection would be shot of it if it could be, but can't, because of the developmental link. That case seems to fit neither the adaptationist picture nor the clean-spandrel picture, and I think it's the one that shows what "constraint" has to mean if it's to do any work at all. My flatmate works on limb development and got quite cross with me about this over dinner, which I'm taking as a good sign. The follow-up question I keep coming back to: if the byproduct later gets co-opted and turns out to be useful, was it ever a spandrel — is "spandrel" a status or a stage?
