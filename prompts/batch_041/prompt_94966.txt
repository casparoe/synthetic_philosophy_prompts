I chair a regional panel that reviews deaths in custody — people who died in prisons and immigration removal centres in our jurisdiction, most of them known to at least two public agencies before they died. We sit quarterly, take about thirty cases a year, and allow ninety minutes per case. The panel is nine people: a clinician, a former prison governor, a lawyer, a criminologist, two lay members, and a family liaison officer. Our terms of reference are narrower than our reputation. We are asked only to determine whether an omission by a named agency or individual was among the causes of the death, and to recommend changes to policy. We cannot discipline anyone. But our findings are published, sent to the family, and routinely cited in inquests and misconduct proceedings, so the field reads a causal finding as a verdict of blame. That is not the question we are asked, and not one we are equipped to answer. I sign the findings; dissents are recorded.

I would like you to turn the philosophy of causation by omission into a decision procedure the panel can actually run in a case meeting. I want the questions in the order they should be asked, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the point at which the procedure stops and returns a verdict of no causal role, the point at which it must return not assessable on this record, and the point at which it hands the question to the chair as a judgment the panel is not entitled to make by procedure. It has to survive the cases that keep defeating us. An omission leaves no footprint, so the counterfactual is not well defined until we say what the actual agent would have done had they not failed to act: the procedure must choose between an actual-agent standard and a reasonable-agent standard, and say what each buys and what it costs. The same inaction can be described at several grains — failing to conduct a round, failing to look through an observation panel, failing to escalate a note — and the but-for answer flips with the description, so I want a description audit built in. We get redundant failures, where two agencies each had a duty and each would have acted had the other not also dropped it, so neither is a but-for cause and yet something plainly caused the death; we get failures that remove a safeguard rather than add a harm, with the damage arriving later through a chain nobody foresaw; and we get pure documentation failures, where the norm broken is a record-keeping norm and the record, not the person, is what suffered. I also want the procedure to say what work the breached expectation is doing in each case: whether a failure can be a cause at all when nothing required the action, or only a counterfactual lost opportunity. Score each candidate omission on two axes kept strictly apart — how much the outcome counterfactually depended on it, and how salient the breached norm is — and tell us what to do when the two come apart, which is most of the time. Fix a verdict vocabulary, and say what the verdicts should be rather than merely that there should be some. Mark every step that is a judgment dressed up as procedure, and every step a lay member cannot perform without clinical or custodial input. No citations or reading lists; I will check the literature myself.

Then run the procedure on three cases and show your working. First: a man on an at-risk list died overnight; the required welfare round was not carried out, but the panel's clinician believes he was already beyond help by the time it was due, and the last plausible opportunity to intervene came four hours earlier, when no rule required anyone to check on him. Second: a man died by suicide after disclosing a previous attempt to a healthcare escort; the escort's service and the prison each assumed the other would pass the disclosure on, and each would have acted had it been passed. Third: a man's risk assessment was not updated after a cell-sharing decision removed the observation regime he had been under; the assessment was two weeks stale, and there is no evidence anyone would have read it before he died. Say what the procedure does with each, including the case where it returns an answer the panel will not want to hear, and close with an honest account of what the procedure cannot do.
