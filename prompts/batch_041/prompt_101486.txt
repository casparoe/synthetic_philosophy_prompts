I cover law, memory, and post-conflict politics for a long-form magazine, and I am about to give a year to a single subject: a national accounting that is still being designed rather than one that has already reported. What I am working from is a composite — I have drawn on three processes I reported on and altered the details — so please do not try to identify the country. The design is this. After a twelve-year civil war in which the state's security forces, a paramilitary auxiliary, and an insurgent movement all committed atrocities, the peace agreement creates a commission with subpoena power and a three-year clock. Amnesty is available, but only to those who give a full public account of their acts, identify the burial sites of the disappeared, and surrender a share of the assets they accumulated during the war into a reparations fund. The amnesty does not extend to sexual violence, to the recruitment of children, or to the economic crimes of those who financed the fighting without ever holding a gun. The commission's findings are inadmissible in court, it must publish a report, a dissenting minority report is expressly permitted, and a continuing mechanism lets testimony be added after the deadline. The government that appointed it includes people it will have to investigate.

Given that design, which open questions in the theory and practice of truth commissions, amnesty, and transitional justice most deserve a reporter's attention over the next year, and why? I have room for perhaps four long pieces, and I want a defended agenda rather than a survey. Tell me which questions are upstream of the others, which ones a reporter with access and a year can actually make progress on as opposed to which are unresolvable in principle, and which ones look urgent but would change nothing for a body built this way. I am especially interested in the difficulties this particular design forces and that a generic account of transitional justice would glide past — where the odd features, the partial amnesty, the liability of beneficiaries, the minority report, the clock, turn out to be exactly where the conceptual trouble sits. I would also like some account of the criteria you are ranking by and what I should watch for in the first six months that would move a question up or down the list.
