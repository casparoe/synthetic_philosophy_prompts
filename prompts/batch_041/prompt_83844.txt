I am preparing the opening lecture for a graduate seminar on intergenerational justice and climate policy, and I would like to begin with a joke. My audience will be philosophers and economists who already know the standard apparatus well, so a quip about recycling, or about everyone being doomed anyway, will not do.

The joke should turn on the internal structure of the way we value the distant future. In the familiar framework, the rate at which future consumption is discounted is assembled from two parts: a term for pure time preference — impatience, a preference for the present as such, the ingredient that many theorists regard as the ethically suspect one — and a term equal to the curvature of the utility function multiplied by the growth rate. That curvature parameter is the same one that expresses how sharply one weights the worse off against the better off. This double duty is the material I want you to exploit. In a growing world, the more sharply one weights the worse off, the more heavily one discounts future consumption, since the future is assumed to be better off; in a shrinking world, which is roughly what a severe climate outcome would be, the same setting reverses direction and the future weighs more heavily. It follows that someone who insists that discounting future welfare is indefensible may still discount future consumption substantially, and may do so out of an egalitarian commitment rather than out of impatience. A further oddity, if it helps: where the appropriate rate is uncertain, the effective rate applied to the very distant future is lower than the average of the rates one entertains and approaches the lowest of them, so the horizon itself does ethical work.

Write one joke, short enough to deliver aloud in about half a minute, whose punchline turns on one of these tensions rather than merely mentioning the framework. A listener who knows the material should hear the joke as being about the material; a listener who does not should still be able to follow the setup without knowing any names, dates, or works. Please do not let the humour rest on a pun on the words discount, rate, future, or horizon, and do not make it depend on any quotation or on identifying who said what in the literature. A one-line note on which tension the punchline exploits is welcome, but the joke must stand on its own.
