Okay, here's something I keep going back and fourth on and I want an actual number instead of a vibe.

Set-up first, since I don't want you guessing at the details. Two dots get flashed on a screen in quick succession — first one blue up top, second one red down near the bottom. At the right spacing and timing, people don't report two dots. They report one dot that moves and, here's the thing, that changes color partway along the path. Now ask them when the color change happened relative to when the second dot appeared, and they'll place it before the second dot showed up. Which can't be right on its face, since the second dot's color is the color it changed into, so nothing downstream could have known that color until the second flash arrived.

So there's a fork. Option one: the brain waits, takes in both flashes, and then builds one tidy moving-and-color-changing experience and hands it to you after the fact — so the experience you had at t=100ms wasn't actually settled until t=250ms or whatever. Call that the Stalinesque option, the show is staged before the audience is let in. Option two: you really did have an experience of a blue dot at the time, and it got overwritten later, so the record now reads as if the change happened mid-path. That's the Orwellian option, they rewrite the history after the fact. Dennett's the one who put the fork this way, and his move is that the two aren't merely hard to tell apart, they're in principle indistinguishable — all you ever get is the subject's later report, and that report is produced by the very machinery you're trying to date, so asking when the experience happened may be malformed, like asking what time the center of a city is.

Here's the proposition I want a credence on: that the color phi case has a determinate answer to whether it was Stalinesque or Orwellian — that it really is one of those (or some third determinate thing), rather than the question being empty because an experience just doesn't have a timestamp of the kind the fork presupposes. Give me your number, then decompose it, each term with a rough probability and a line of justification:

- how much rides on whether the timing question is well-posed at all;
- conditional on it being well-posed, how much on whether the two stories are even empirically separable — could some measurement (masking, reaction times, EEG, single-unit recordings) break the tie, or is Dennett right that it's undecidable in principle?
- conditional on separability, how much on which one turns out true;
- how much on whether subjects are even interpolating a color experience across the gap, versus just confabulating a report about content that was never colored;
- and how much on the Cartesian-theater worry, that any answer already smuggles in a spot where everything comes together for a viewer.

Then combine them, tell me which single term your number is most sensitive to, and what result or argument would move it most. And if your final number basically amounts to "the question is ill-posed," don't just drop a 0.5 and walk off — say whether that's a substantive discovery about consciousness or just you declining to answer a question you can't operationalize.
