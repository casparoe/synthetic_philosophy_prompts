I teach a third-year optional module on practical rationality and I'm writing the summer paper. I want a single essay question on the starving donkey and choices made when nothing favours either option — but I dont want answers that just retell the story, say "so it should have a tie-breaking preference" and stop there.

Two things I want the question to force apart. First, two variants: (a) the donkey faces two bales that really are exactly alike in every respect that could count — same size, same smell, same distance, same history; (b) same setup, except that one bale is in fact slightly bigger, though the donkey has no way of knowing and no reason to suspect it. Both donkeys starve. I want them to say whether these are the same case or different cases, and what that tells us about whether the puzzle is about the symmetry of the world or about the reasons available to the agent.

Second, some material I've been using in lectures: a hospital trust breaks ties between two equally urgent patients by lottery, and we don't call the lottery irrational — but we also don't say the lottery makes the patients equal. I want the question to ask whether that shows the donkey can get out of the jam with a second-order rule (a rule about how to choose, rather than a preference between the bales) without ever acquiring a preference between them, or whether that just relabels the problem. And whether the paradox quietly needs determinism to get going — the ass in the story is a determinist's ass, no? — so that someone who thinks the will is free in a stronger sense shouldn't be moved by it at all.

Also make them say what's wrong with the two cheap moves: "it isn't really indifferent, it prefers not starving to starving" and "it should just flip a coin".

Roughly 700 words for the model answer, please, plus a marking scheme out of 20 that names the specific moves an A answer has to make and the specific mistakes that should cost marks. British English, and keep the question itself to something a student could actually do in 45 minutes.
