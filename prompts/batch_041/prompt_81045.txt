Last night I got into a two-hour arguement with my friend Dan about whether pleasure is even one thing. His line: the first sip of coffee, the relief of finally getting to pee, the buzz of winning an argument, scratching an itch — none of these have anything in common, we just slap the same word on them because they're all things we want more of, which means hedonism (the whole "pleasure is the good" thing) is either trivial or confused. My line back was that something has to be shared or we couldn't recognize a brand-new pleasure as a pleasure at all, and that Dan's "things we want more of" move just relocates the problem, since we seem to want them *because* they're pleasant. We got nowhere. Now I want to build a one-page decision tree for our Thursday reading group so we can stop relitigating and actually see where each of us is sitting. Can you write it?

Start everyone from the same place, the thing Dan and I couldn't get past: "the taste of coffee and the relief of peeing feel nothing alike, yet both are pleasures." The angle I care about is that the tree should sort people by *where they put the commonality*, not by which label they like. So the first split should be on whether there's anything genuinely shared across all pleasures, and every later split should be one of the places you can put the shared thing. The moves I can see — steal these, or tell me if I'm missing a branch:

- there's a distinctive felt quality every pleasure has, a felt "tone" or "gloss" — and then: is it a sensation of its own, or a property that can ride on top of any sensation, so that tasting a peach and peeing can be pleasant in the same respect without feeling alike at all?
- if it's a property, is it a *phenomenal* property (a way it feels) or a *normative* one (a felt "more of this," where the pleasantness is partly a push or a command)?
- nothing is shared in the feel, but everything pleasant is something the subject is *for* in some way — the unity is in the attitude, not the sensation. Then: is the attitude object-directed (taking pleasure *in* something, which makes objectless pleasures like a warm bath or a good stretch awkward) or free-floating (a mood, a general up-swing)?
- nothing shared in the feel or the attitude, only in the role: pleasures are the states that dispose you to keep going, want more, repeat. (This is basically Dan's view and I suspect it's unstable, but let's see.)
- nothing shared at all — pleasures are a family or a merely nominal kind, and "hedonism" names a family of theories rather than one. Then the question is whether that kills hedonism or just makes it plural.

Phrase every node as a question a person could answer about a concrete case without knowing what position it leads to. Use actual pleasures — coffee, peeing, an itch, a warm bath, the relief when the bad news turns out to be false, a song you love — not abstract "do you think pleasure is monistic?" style questions. Leaves should be named positions (hedonic-tone monism; the mode/property view; attitudinalism, object-directed vs. free-floating; the desire/functional-role view; the "more of this" imperativist view; pleasure as primitive and indefinable; family-resemblance pluralism; and whatever else falls out). If a combination of answers is empty, say so and diagnose whether it's empty because it's incoherent, because it's just unattractive, or because nobody's bothered. If your tree can't seperate two leaves — say the mode/property view and the free-floating attitudinal view come out the same on every case — flag that instead of pretending they're distinct. Aim for about five or six split points and six to nine leaves. Don't give me a reading list or cite anyone; if you want to mention who holds a view that's fine, but don't invent sources, and honestly I'd rather have the positions than the roll call.

One more thing: at the end, give me a short honest note on which of these splits are just common ground among people who actually think about this and which ones are genuinely contested, and flag any branch you suspect you're making up rather than reporting. If something I listed above isn't a live position, just say so — don't dress up a contested or invented reading as the consensus.
