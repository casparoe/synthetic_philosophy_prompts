I keep running into a certain argument shape and I've never found a clean name for it, and at this point I'm not even sure it has one. Hoping someone here has either the term or a better carving than what I've got.

The shape is roughly this. Someone wants you to do or believe or adopt X. They don't argue that X is true or good in the ordinary way. Instead they posit an agent (or a "process," or just a rule) whose behavior is defined as a function of how you reason, and then they say: because of that, if you don't do X, you'll be worse off. The catch is that the agent's threat is unfalsifiable by construction -- it's not defined over anything observable in the world, it's defined over your decision procedure or your beliefs or the fact that you considered the question at all. So there's no experiment that could show the threat is empty, and pointing that out doesn't defuse it, because "unfalsifiable" was the whole design.

Some instances I've collected, none of which quite fit each other:

(a) The future superintelligence one. If a powerful AI gets built, it might reward the people who helped and punish the people who knew and didn't. So you should help now. The punishing is supposed to be conditioned on what kind of agent you were, so you can't escape by keeping your hands clean.

(b) The mugging. A stranger offers an enormous reward (or threatens an enormous punishment) over an event with minuscule probability, and the expected value comes out large, so "rationally" you should comply.

(c) The precommitment/exclusion one. If you don't adopt a certain policy now, a network of agents that trade "acausally" will decline to trade with you, so adopting the policy pays. Again, the payoff is defined over your policy, not over anything that happens.

(d) The one where the threat is specifically that you *thought about it*. Merely reading the argument and dismissing it is supposed to be the triggering event, so the only safe move is to never have engaged, which you can't retroactively do.

Here's what I can't tell: is there a single established concept for this general form, or am I lumping together things that a careful person would keep separate? The nearest things I know -- Pascal's wager, and the "expected value can be hijacked by tiny probabilities" point -- both feel adjacent but not right, because they don't have the self-referential "the threat is defined over your reasoning" feature. And "unfalsifiable" isn't it either, because lots of unfalsifiable claims aren't trying to move your decisions this way.

What I actually want isn't a label. I want the carving. So: if there's a term, tell me what it is and where it stops -- which of (a)-(d) it covers and which it doesn't. If there isn't, coin one, define it, and give me the boundary cases, i.e. what's the closest legitimate argument that looks like this but isn't a mugging. And I'd like to know the thing I suspect but can't prove: whether the whole family is united by the move of putting the reward/punishment over belief-or-policy instead of over action, so that evidence-based norms can't touch it. If that's the joint, say so; if the real joint is somewhere else, tell me where.
