I'm building a case for a metaethics seminar and I need one built to order, so let me be specific about the spec.

I want a single realistic situation -- two ordinary people, somewhere plausible, no aliens, no gods, no brains in vats, no special moral sense organ -- in which two agents disagree about whether some particular act is morally wrong, and they disagree even though they're identical in all of the following: they have the same desires and aversions, they care about the same things to the same degree, they belong to the same community and share its standards, and neither of them is missing any non-moral information about the situation (no false beliefs about the consequences, the facts, how anyone feels, nothing). So the only thing left for them to differ on is the moral verdict itself. Keep it to a paragraph, and it has to be the kind of thing two real people could actually end up on opposite sides of, not a trick.

Then do the audit. For each of these, say what it has to say about the case and why:

- moral realism, naturalist flavour (wrongness is some natural property, e.g. what a fully informed impartial agent would want)
- moral realism, non-naturalist flavour
- error theory
- expressivism / non-cognitivism
- a relativism or constructivism that ties the verdict to the communitys standards

For each one, tell me whether the case is easy, awkward, or fatal. In particular, the naturalist and the relativist have to explain how two agents with the same standards and the same information can still land on different verdicts, and the expressivist has to explain what the disagreement is even made of if the attitudes are identical. Which of them has to work hardest, and does any of them have to deny that the case is possible at all?

Then the part I actually care about: which single feature of the case is doing the work? I built it around the sameness of the attitudes and standards, so tell me what happens if I relax that -- if I let them differ in desires, or put them in different communities, does it collapse into an ordinary disagreement that every theory handles fine? And is the hard version even possible, or have I specified something incoherent -- two fully informed agents with identical attitudes who somehow can't be in genuine first-order disagreement without one of them making some kind of error? Be honest if the case proves to much.

Last, play defence: if the theory that comes out worst wants to reject the case rather than swallow the verdict, whats its best move, and does that move cost it anything elsewhere? And which flavour of realism has the easiest time of it -- I suspect its not the one people would guess.
