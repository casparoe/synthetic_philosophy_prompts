I'm in a seminar on nationalism and I think I've finally got the Hobsbawm and Anderson stuff, but it's left me feeling kind of cynical and I want to check I'm not taking it too far before I write my essay. Here's my takeaway: nations are basically made up. Anderson says they're imagined communities and Hobsbawm says their traditions are invented, so things like Scotland's tartans and clans, or the idea that the French have been French since time immemorial, are fabrications that elites cooked up in the nineteenth century to keep people loyal — and if you scratch a national identity you find nothing real underneath. My roommate thinks I'm just being edgy but I figure the scholars are on my side. (My professor also made some passing aside about "custom" being a different thing from an invented tradition and I didn't write it down, so maybe that's relevant.)

So two questions. First, since these identities are imagined and invented, are they just false — like, when a Scottish person says she feels a deep ancestral pull toward her clan, is she simply mistaken the way someone would be who thought she'd personally had tea with Queen Victoria? Second, if it's all invented anyway, why not just drop it? Isn't nationalism false consciousness that a reasonable person should shrug off, and doesn't the whole literature basically show that anyone who takes their nation's ancient roots seriously is being duped by a story some Victorian bureaucrat made up?
