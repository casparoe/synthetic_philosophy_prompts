Grice's account of speaker meaning is standardly given in something like this form: a speaker means something by an utterance just in case she intends the utterance to produce some effect in an audience, she intends the audience to recognise that intention, and she intends the audience's recognition of it to be at least part of their reason for the effect. The second condition — the recognition condition — is generally taken to be what separates communication from mere causation. A man who wants you to think he is frightened and who pales, without intending the pallor to be read as evidence of anything, has not told you anything, however reliably the pallor makes you believe he is frightened.

The recognition condition generates a puzzle when the intention to produce a belief is itself meant to remain hidden. Consider a case. A wants B to believe that their colleague C has been concealing something from the committee. A knows that B discounts anything A asserts directly, because B thinks A is prejudiced against C, and that B attaches great weight to what A says when A is not addressing B. So A arranges to be overheard remarking to a third party, in a tone of mild worry, that she wonders whether C has told the committee everything. A intends B to come to believe that C is concealing something; she intends B to come to believe it by grasping that A herself believes it; and she intends B not to recognise that the remark was staged for B's benefit, since B would discount a staged remark. B overhears, draws the inference, and believes.

Write an essay on whether Grice's account can accommodate cases of this kind, or whether such cases show that speaker meaning does not require the recognition condition. Reach and defend a verdict. Your essay should say what, if anything, A meant by the remark to the third party; what, if anything, A meant to B; whether the two audiences should be treated separately; and whether the machinery of conversational implicature is the right instrument for the case or a distraction from it. It should also consider the strongest objection to your verdict — for instance, that this is manipulation or staging rather than communication at all — and explain why it does not overturn your conclusion.
