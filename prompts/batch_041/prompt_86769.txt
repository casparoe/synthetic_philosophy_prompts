Inside that literature there's a live dispute I'd like you to translate into my home vocabulary. On one side: the features, directions, and circuits that interpretability tools turn up are real components of how the network computes — the network genuinely represents the properties those features are said to stand for, and the tools are discovering structure that was already there. On the other side: the features are artifacts of our methods — convenient parameterizations that let us predict or steer behavior without corresponding to anything the network actually uses or "has in mind," and the sense in which the network represents anything is thinner than the realists want.

Restate that dispute, as faithfully as you can, as a dispute about latent variables and their measurement. Where it helps, render the realists' claims in terms of latent factors, indicators, common-cause structure, construct validity, and factor indeterminacy, and render the deflationists' claims in terms of rotational indeterminacy, overfitting, operationalism, and the gap between a construct and the operations that recover it.
