I'm putting together a little set of philosophsy jokes for a reading group and I need one about Popper's whole strange relationship with Darwinism. Not the standard "falsification, amirite" thing — I want the punchline to actually turn on the material, so that someone who knows the debate laughs and someone who doesn't at least can tell I'm not bluffing.

The material I want it to land on: Popper's complaint that natural selection is basically circular — the fittest are the ones who survive, and we know who was fittest because they survived — which is why he demoted Darwinism to a metaphysical research programme rather than a testable theory, and then a few years later more or less took it back and allowed that it was testable after all. And my favourite bit, which I think is the real joke waiting to happen: his own falsifiability criterion doesn't look falsifiable either, and his escape was to say it's a proposal, a decision about what we'll count as science, not an empirical claim. Which means the Darwinian has exactly the same exit available. So a scene where a Darwinian and a Popperian are going at it and the punchline lands on that symmetry — "the fittest survive" versus "the best-tested theories survive", i.e. Popper's own evolutionary epistemology is the very shape of theory he was suspicious of, just aimed at ideas instead of organisms. If it helps, he liked to say we're like the amoeba except that when a theory fails, the theory dies instead of us, so something could hang on that.

Constraints: whole thing under 150 words, setup and punchline included — that's a hard limit, I'm reading these aloud. Then on a separate line, one sentence saying what the joke assumes the listener already knows, because I want to check the punchline isn't just vibes. Casual and tellable out loud, and please no "so a philosopher walks into a bar".
