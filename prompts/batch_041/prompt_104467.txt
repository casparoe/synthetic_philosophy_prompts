I have just started a postdoc in a long-run development group, and I have been asked to circulate my priors before the first workshop so that the others know what they are arguing against. Writing them out, I am no longer sure they form a single coherent position, so I would like you to audit them rather than defend or attack the whole thing. Here is where I have landed. (1) The fundamental cause of why some societies are rich and others poor is the quality of their institutions — broadly, whether political and economic institutions are inclusive or extractive, and whether they secure property rights for a wide population rather than a narrow elite. (2) Geography is not itself a fundamental cause; where it appears to matter it operates through the institutions that get transplanted, and through the disease and settlement environment that shapes which institutions take hold in the first place. (3) Culture is mostly downstream of institutions: the beliefs and norms that look like causes are largely adaptations to the incentives institutions set, and they tend to move when the incentives move. (4) That said, culture adjusts on a much slower clock than institutions do, so within any given generation it constrains which institutions can actually take root and function — imported rules that cut against deep norms get hollowed out in practice. (5) The counterfactual of what a given country would look like under different institutions is well-defined and often empirically tractable, thanks to natural experiments such as the settler-mortality instrument, discontinuities at colonial borders, and similar designs. (6) The large question — why is this nation rich? — is in the end not answerable; we can identify proximate mechanisms and local effects, but not a single total causal account of a nation's path.

I keep suspecting that some of these pull against each other and that others only look as though they do, and I cannot tell which is which. What I want from you is a ranking, not a survey. Go through the commitments pairwise and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how severe each tension is, which pairs merely appear to conflict and why the appearance dissolves on inspection, and, for each genuine tension, what the cheapest repair would be and what that repair costs me elsewhere in the set. Then, if there is a single revision that would clear up the most trouble at the least cost, name it and commit to it.

Please be decisive about this. Do not hedge by telling me it depends on what I mean by "fundamental" — tell me what I would have to mean for the package to hold, and whether that is a meaning I can actually live with. And do not assume the tension is wherever I seem most anxious; I have deliberately not signalled which commitment I would surrender first. I would far rather you take a definite line and be wrong in an interesting way than lay out the options and leave the choice to me.
