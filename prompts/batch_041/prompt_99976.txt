Ok so I'm in this cognitive science of creativity seminar and we just finished the Hofstadter unit -- the whole picture where creativity is basically twiddling knobs on a concept you already have, sliding around in a space of variations, and even the wild new stuff is just a bigger twiddle, a slide at the meta-level. Then my prof wheeled out the other camp, the Boden-ish people, who say exploring a space is one thing but the real deal is changing the space itself (transformational vs exploratory), and you can't just relabel that as a bigger twiddle or the whole theory eats itself.

Our assignment is to write the joint statement the two camps could actually both sign -- like an adversarial collaboration. So: a knob-twiddling camp and a space-breaking camp. Not a dialogue, not a verdict on who's right, but the document both sides could put their names on.

Please give me: a numbered section of agreed claims (about what counts as a variation, what a conceptual space is, what each side means by novelty, what would count as a counterexample), then a table of the remaining disagreements, with each side's view phrased in wording that side would accept -- so not stuff like "the space-breakers wrongly assume X", but how they'd phrase it themselves.

Cruxes I want nailed down: (1) is exploratory vs transformational a real kind of difference or just a difference of degree along one axis? The knob-twiddling side can always say changing the space is twiddling a meta-knob, and I suspect the space-breakers will say that move makes the thesis unfalsifiable. (2) Is novelty measured against the space and the history of the field, or against what the person could actually reach from where they started? (3) Does creative variation need blind or random generation, or is a constrained, sensible variation enough?

Then for each crux: a case or argument both sides agree is relevant, and each side's prediction, written down in advance.

Two concrete things to lean on, since our seminar keeps coming back to them:
- those letter-string analogy puzzles Hofstadter's group built, where abc goes to abd, so what does ijk go to -- the annoying part is it can go to ijl (keep the rule) or ijd (do the same thing to the last letter), and both feel right, which is exactly the slippage thing.
- pop songs built on the Pachelbel canon chord loop, which has to be one of the most thoroughly explored spaces ever.

Also, my roommate and I invented a little scale we've been using, feel free to adopt it: a change that keeps every element of the theme and flips exactly one feature (major to minor, sacred to profane, first person to third) is a one-knob tilt; then two- and three-knob moves; then whole-console moves where several things go at once. The question we can't settle is whether there's a principled threshold on that scale where it stops being variation and becomes transformation, or whether any threshold is just us drawing a line.

Last thing: say which part of their fight you think is just about words -- I have a suspicion its the bit about whether the exploratory/transformational labels describe products or processes -- and which part is a real disagreement. And what would actually move either side: what result would make the knob-twiddling camp admit there's a residual left over, and what would make the space-breakers admit it was a twiddle all along. Watch out for the trap where any space-change can be redescribed as a knob-twiddle after the fact; if that trap makes some seperate chunk of the debate unwinnable, just say so.
