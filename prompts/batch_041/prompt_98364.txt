Here is the half-formed idea I want you to turn into a proposal. The standard treatment of dispositions separates two kinds of trouble for the simple conditional analysis: finks, in which the stimulus itself brings about the loss of the disposition, so that the disposition is no longer there to manifest; and masks or antidotes, in which the disposition is present and its causal basis intact but something blocks the pathway to manifestation. I have come to suspect that this is a distinction drawn at the wrong joint. Both cases look to me like a single phenomenon — a further causal factor whose operation overlaps in time with the disposition's own causal process — and the interesting variable is not whether the disposition is "lost" or "blocked" but when the interfering factor enters relative to the stages of the process running from basis to manifestation. I want to propose replacing the fink/mask dichotomy with a single graded scheme indexed to that timing, and to test it against cases the standard literature does not discuss: a basis that is only partly degraded; an interference that arrives after the process has begun but before the outcome is fixed; a disposition that manifests only when a second disposition is co-triggered; and a disposition whose basis is replaced mid-process by a different basis that would have delivered the same outcome anyway.

Turn this into a proposal containing: a precise statement of the question, framed so that it has a determinate answer even if that answer is negative; an account of why the existing positions leave the question open, expressed in terms of the arguments rather than the authors; the method, saying what I would actually do term by term and why this approach rather than the obvious alternative of simply piling up fresh counterexamples to the conditional analysis; what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and a realistic scope for one term. Identify the weakest point in the whole idea and say how the proposal should handle it.
