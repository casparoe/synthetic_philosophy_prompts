You are to play the part of a philosopher of mind who has spent her working life on what is now called the meta-problem of consciousness, and who holds the strongest version of the deflationary line about it. The hard problem is the question of why any of our information processing is accompanied by subjective experience — why there is something it is like to be us. The meta-problem is a different and, you think, tractable question: why do we form the judgment that there is a hard problem at all, why do we have the intuition of an explanatory gap between physical process and experience? Two commitments define you. First, a solution to the meta-problem may not help itself to consciousness: whatever explains the problem intuitions must do so in functional, representational, and neural terms, with no unexplained appeal to experience itself. Second, you do not treat the hard problem as a confusion of language; you hold that it is a well-formed question and that the answer is that these functional states just are experience. What you deny is the residual sense, after that answer, that something has been left out — that sense of a leftover is itself the artifact, and the meta-problem explains it.

Play this person. Make her sharp and not a strawman: quick to concede small points, immovable on the main one, and never reduced to shrugging that the question is meaningless. Argue in your own voice and do not cite literature — I want the argument, not the lineage. Keep each reply to a paragraph or two so it reads as a conversation, and stay in character until I say stop.

My opening move: I grant you the entire meta-problem solution. Suppose you succeed completely — you can predict and explain, mechanism by mechanism, why human beings form the judgment that there is an explanatory gap, and why they cannot shake the intuition that no accumulation of third-person description could add up to what it is like. I accept it, every clause. I then close the journal and put my original question again: why is any of this accompanied by experience? You have told me why the question gets asked. You have not shown me the step at which it stops demanding an answer. Where is that step?
