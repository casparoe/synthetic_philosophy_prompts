My old stats professor still shows up to our lab meetings and I've been keeping a list of his calls, because I want to figure out what rule the guy is actually running before I have to present to him next month.

Preregistered, n around 1200, effect about a third of a standard deviation, p well under .001 — 'thats real, and the size is believable.'
n = 25, four outcomes, they reported the one that worked, no preregistration, p = .04 — 'no. And dont replicate it at that sample size either, that wont settle anything.'
Preregistered, couple hundred people, p = .01, effect in the direction and roughly the size they predicted — 'fine, I believe that one.'
Preregistered, well powered, p = .06, effect in the predicted direction, confidence interval just barely touching zero — 'no. It didn't get there. Thats the entire point of setting the bar before you look at the data.'
Preregistered, n = 400, effect basically zero with a tight interval — 'best paper of the year. They killed it.'
Meta-analysis of 40 small significant studies, no preregistration anywhere, funnel plot leaning like a drunk — 'thats not evidence, thats a filing cabinet. How many of the hypotheses in your literature do you think were true to begin with?'

He also once asked an author what the Bayes factor was under a prior that wasnt already convinced, and when they said nobody computes it that way he just wrote something in his notebook and moved on.

So the paper I have to present: preregistered, n = 90,000 off admin records, effect of d = 0.02, p < .001. The authors themselves say that on this scale anything under a quarter of a standard deviation is not worth caring about, and its an outcome where nobody would notice less than that. Also the null was never really in play — the intervention obviously does something, its just that the something is a rounding error. My coauthor thinks its a lock: preregistered, gigantic sample, p to three decimal places.

What is he going to say about it? I dont just want the verdict, I want the rules that fit his record so far, what each of them predicts here, which one you'd bet on and why, and what case would actually separate them — i.e. what should I put in front of him next to find out whether I've got him right. If his record honestly underdetermines it, say which way the competing readings pull and which you'd put money on.
