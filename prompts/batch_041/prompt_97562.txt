Hi — I chair the research ethics board at our teaching hospital and there's a case on Tueday's agenda I genuinely can't call, so I'm hoping you'll draft the decision memo I circulate to the board beforehand.

Background: a behavioral-medicine team wants to run a study on treatment adhearance in people with type 2 diabetes. Everyone enrolled gets a daily reminder text. The consent form says the study is about "how people manage their condition day to day" (that's my paraphrase of it, not a quote). What it doesn't say is that the texts are randomized — some are plain, some are written to make the recipient feel guilty about letting themselves down. The team argues that disclosing the guilt framing would wreck the study, since people would either opt out or brace for it and the results would be meaningless.

Two things make it messy. First, patients are recruited by their own clinicians during routine appointments, which this board has flagged before as borderline undue influence. Second, our own policy cuts both ways. One clause says consent counts as valid only if the participant wouldn't have refused had the deception been disclosed. Another says deception is acceptable where the study is no more than minimal risk and disclosure would undermine the study's validity. The team leans hard on the second clause. The two patients who already withdrew and complained lean on the first. I honestly don't know whether "the participant" in that first clause means the actual person in front of us or some reasonable-patient stand-in — and if it's the latter, I'm not convinced a slightly guilt-trippy text is the kind of thing a reasonable patient would refuse. One board member keeps saying that once they signed the form they consented, full stop, and whatever they feel afterward is irrelevant. I don't buy it but I can't quite say why.

Could you write the memo? I need a concrete recommendation — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — plus the reasoning, a clear bottom line with any deadlines attached to the conditions, and I want the losing side argued at its strongest rather than strawmanned, because half the board is on each side and I'll have to defend this in the room. Around 800 words. And if you think the policy itself is incoherent, say so and tell me what to fix instead of papering over it.
