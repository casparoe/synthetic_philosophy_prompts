Explain the disagreement between Mencius and Xunzi over human nature in a way that gets past the summary that one took our nature to be good and the other took it to be bad. Organize the explanation around a single question: what is already present in a person before any moral development has taken place, and what still has to be added from outside? Frame the contrast as that between a seed and a raw material — a seed already contains the form of what it will become and grows by an internal principle, whereas a raw material is neutral in itself and must be worked on by an external artisan. State what in each thinker's picture plays the role of the seed or the raw material, and what plays the role of the artisan. Then press on the place where each account seems to owe something it has not paid: for Xunzi, if the nature is bad and goodness comes only from deliberate effort, what supplies the very first motive to undertake that effort, and what in the person is able to recognize ritual and a teacher as worth following; for Mencius, if the moral beginnings are innate, how anyone can lose them, or, as in the story of the stripped mountain, come to look as though they were never there, and whether calling them sprouts commits him to their growing on their own or only marks a starting point. End by testing both accounts against one case — an adult raised with no moral instruction who later, on his own, comes to adopt a demanding ethical discipline — saying what each thinker must claim is going on and which account handles the case better.
