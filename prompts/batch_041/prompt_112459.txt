I chair the clinical AI governance committee at a mid-sized hospital. A vendor is asking us to approve a pilot of its sepsis-warning tool. Its submission argues that the model is assumption-free: it trains on our own electronic records, adapts to any hospital, and therefore carries no built-in inductive bias about which variables matter. The validation report notes good retrospective performance at three other sites and concludes that the model will generalize to ours. Two committee members say this is precisely what the no-free-lunch results rule out; two others say those results concern optimization over all possible functions and have little bearing on a deployed clinical model. We meet Thursday and must decide whether to approve, reject, or approve with conditions. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate. It should give a clear recommendation with a time frame, explain what the formal results do and do not establish, address the strongest version of the vendor's case, and distinguish the problem of induction from the narrower mathematical claims. Separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know the area, and do not present a contested interpretation as consensus. No bibliography; keep it to what the committee can act on.
