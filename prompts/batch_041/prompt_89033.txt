Last month our reading group finished a term on free will and legal punishment — the spine of it was the Dennett–Caruso exchange, though by the end we were mostly arguing about our own cases — and two members, Rafael and June, got into a dispute I have not been able to settle on my own. The occasion was a parole hearing in the news: a man sentenced to a long term for an armed robbery he committed at nineteen, whose childhood was, by any measure, disastrous — taken from his parents at six, moved through a dozen placements, functionally illiterate until his mid-teens. Rafael said the board should treat the sentence itself as the mistake. If the man's conduct at nineteen was the predictable output of a childhood he had no hand in shaping, then he no more deserves the extra years than a falling rock deserves the ground it lands on; whatever we do with him — confine him if he is dangerous, release him if he is not — is a question about future risk, and "desert" is a word we use to dress up the answer we already wanted. He added that the honest way to think about it is the way we think about quarantine: we isolate the contagious without supposing that they are wicked, and the point of the isolation is everyone's safety, not the patient's guilt. June said that this conflates two different things. Desert may well be a fiction, she said, but accountability is not: this man is still the kind of creature who can be given reasons, who can own what he did, and who can be answered as a member of the moral community — unlike, say, a man mid-psychosis who genuinely cannot be reached that way. That difference, she said, is what justifies treating the first man as an agent and the second as a patient, and it requires no metaphysics at all.

Rafael came back hard. He said the distinction is either doing predictive work or doing nothing. If the psychotic man happens to be more dangerous than the robber, June's "accountability" gives us no reason to treat him more leniently — so either she is smuggling desert back in under a new name, or she is a consequentialist who won't say so. He also said the quarantine analogy survives her objection better than she thinks: yes, it would license confining a dangerous innocent, but that is a bullet he is willing to bite, and it is a smaller cost than pretending we can see into souls. June said that was unfair on two counts. First, the reason we do not blame the psychotic man is not that he is less dangerous but that blame would be unintelligible as addressed to him — the practice of holding someone to account presupposes a capacity to take it up, and a presupposition of a practice is not a prediction about it. Second, she said, the quarantine model cannot tell us what to do with the robber's *record*: quarantine ends when the danger ends, so on Rafael's view a man who has aged out of violence should walk free with no more to answer for than a recovered flu patient, and she suspects Rafael does not actually believe that. At that point Rafael produced a hypothetical that I think is the real crux: suppose the board has a causal printout, compiled by a perfect science, showing that but for a single eviction when the man was nine he would have become a surgeon — does June think that should reduce the sentence? She said yes, possibly, and that this is precisely why desert-talk, properly understood, is not the enemy she takes it to be. He said no, and that this is precisely why her position collapses into his.

I would like a careful, move-by-move adjudication rather than a verdict on the whole dispute. For each of the main exchanges — the initial desert-versus-accountability contrast, Rafael's dilemma about prediction versus smuggled desert, June's appeal to the presuppositions of blame, the quarantine analogy and the record objection, and finally the eviction printout — tell me who has the better of it and why, and rate the moves from clearly mistaken to clearly sound. I would also like to know whether any of the disagreement is merely verbal, which claims each of them would have to abandon to keep the rest, and what both of them are missing.
