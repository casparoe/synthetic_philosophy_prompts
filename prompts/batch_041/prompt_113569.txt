In December 2004 a great undersea earthquake off Sumatra was recorded within minutes by seismic networks around the world, but the Indian Ocean had no tsunami warning system and no standing arrangement for getting word from the centres that had detected the quake to the governments and coastal populations in its path. The wave reached the nearest Sumatran coast in about half an hour, killing most of the eventual 230,000 dead in Aceh before anything could be done; it reached coasts a thousand miles away an hour or more later, where tens of thousands more died. Take those facts as given.

Analyze this as a case of causation by omission. Use the two standard positions: the no-omission view, on which a cause must be a positive event or state and an absence has no causal power of its own, and the counterfactual-dependence view, on which a failure to act is a cause just in case things would have gone differently had the agent acted. Add the literature on causal selection, which asks why we single out one condition as the cause, and the norm-based answer that we single out the condition that violated a norm or an expectation.

Then tell me: whether the failure to warn caused the deaths, and on which account, keeping the causal claim per-coast rather than global; what each account explains here and what it misdescribes, especially given that the warning centres had no duty toward the Indian Ocean and no one had been assigned the job; and what the episode shows about the theory -- whether it favours one side, and whether it exposes the causal question as inseparable from the question of who should have been answerable for the warning. Where the theory is silent, say so rather than forcing it to speak.
