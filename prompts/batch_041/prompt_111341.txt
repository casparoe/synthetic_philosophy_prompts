I think the whole objection turns on a switch Williams never quite makes explicit, and once you make it, the complaint either shrinks into a psychological claim about how deliberation feels or it turns into a thesis about reasons that you owe me an independent argument for. The story is the man who can only save one of two people and one of them is his wife, and he saves her; Williams says the thought that really does the work should just be that she's his wife, and that tacking on "and it's also permissible" or "and it also maximizes" is one thought too many. Fine — but the two-leveler just shrugs: the criterion of rightness needn't be the agent's deliberative thought, the husband's love is exactly the disposition the theory wants him to have, and nobody should be running sums at the water's edge. So here's the push, and I want to be fair to you because I know the two-leveler usually just stops there and preens. Either the real complaint is that the husband's love is being endorsed *for its consequences*, and so is conditional on them in a way that love can't coherently be — in which case tell me what goes wrong, since he needn't ever entertain the condition — or the complaint is about justification rather than psychology, and then I don't see how you get it without the premise that the agent's actual motivating reason has to be the reason that makes the act right, which is precisely the thing in dispute. So which horn are you on, and can you state the premise without assuming it?

And one more thing I keep tripping over, since I think it's where the real pressure is: what does your view do when the numbers get ugly? If the husband has the no-comparison disposition, it misfires in the case where the stranger's side is enormous and he dives for his wife anyway, and the two-leveler will say the theory can ask for that disposition and just eat the misfires as the price of having people who love each other at all. Does the Williams defender have to deny that the theory can ask for it, or accept the misfires and then explain why integrity was ever supposed to be a constraint on what a theory may demand rather than just a fact about how much people can take?
