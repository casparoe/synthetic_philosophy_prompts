ok so ive been reading about this stuff in my spare time, no philosophy degree just alot of podcasts and wikipedia rabbit holes, and I keep going round in circles on one thing.

Theres two camps about knowledge and stakes and I cant tell if they actually disagree about anything. One camp says the word "knows" is context sensitive — when I say "she knows the bank is open," the word itself means something different depending on whats at stake for me, the person saying it, so theres no single fact of the matter whether she knows, it depends whos asking. The other camp says no, the word means the same every time, but whether someone actually knows depends on their practical situation — if alot rides on being right they need more evidence to count as knowing, so the bar moves on the knowers side, not in the word.

From where I sit these two tell me the same thing about almost every real case. High stakes, both say dont call it knowledge; low stakes, both say go ahead. So I want the two of them to sit down and finally write the document they shouldve written years ago — a joint statement they could both actually put their names to.

What I want in it:

1. the claims they both accept, as a numbered list, about the cases and about what even needs explaining. And be honest — if part of what theyve been fighting over is just words, say which part and why.
2. where they still really disagree, each disagreement written in wording the person on that side would accept as fair to their own view, not a strawman.
3. the cruxes — the smaller questions where settling one would force at least one side to move.
4. for each crux, a concrete case or test both sides agree is relevant, with each sides prediction for it written down in advance.

I also want them to run through a case of my own because I honestly cant tell which side it favours. My flatmate and I were both looking at our other flatmate, Sam, who said the door was definately locked before leaving. I was stressed — Id left my laptop and my whole weeks work in there and the neighborhood isnt great. My flatmate couldnt have cared less, hed just come along for the walk and was mostly on his phone. Same Sam, same evidence, same everything. I was the one with something on the line, Sam wasnt. So does Sam know the door was locked? My stressed self says no, my relaxed flatmate says sure. Have each side write down what they predict about this one and why, before they compare notes, because I suspect this is where the two camps actually split and I want to see it happen.

Put the agreements as a numbered list and the disagreements as a table, thats the only way Ill be able to follow it.
