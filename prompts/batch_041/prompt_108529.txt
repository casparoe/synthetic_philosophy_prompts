ok so i've talked myself into a view about the color phi thing and i want you to try to wreck it, please be thorough.

refresher in case it helps: you flash a blue dot up top, then a fraction of a second later a red dot lower down. people don't see two dots, they see one dot travel across and change from blue to red about halfway. and when you ask them, they say the change happened *before* the second dot showed up — which should be impossible, because the red doesnt exist yet at the moment they say the change happened. (i think there's an old experiment exactly like this, blue then red, by Kolers and von Grünau, and i'm fairly sure Dennett leans on it somewhere. doesn't matter much.)

the view: there's no fact of the matter about when the experience happened. there are two stories and i claim they're the same story. story one: the brain waits, collects both flashes, and builds the whole travelling-and-changing dot up front, so the raw blue flash never becomes an experience at all. story two: the blue flash is experienced as blue, and afterwards the memory gets quietly rewritten, the rewrite backdated so it feels like the change happened in flight. early-edit vs late-edit. i say nothing could ever tell these apart, and if nothing could ever tell them apart then "when did you really experience the change" has no answer. there are facts about what people end up saying and facts about what's happening in the brain, and neither of those pins down a time of experience. experience isn't the kind of thing that has a timestamp. that's the position, roughly, and yes i know how it sounds.

so: what counts against it? ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and i want the stuff that's arguably already happened, not just thought experiments.

some things i keep circling:

- the leak idea. is there any measure that tracks the blue while the person is reporting the moving colour-changing dot — a fast motor response, priming, where their eyes went — and if there is one, does that show the blue was *experienced*, or only *processed*? be honest with me about whether the processed/experienced line is doing real work here or whether i'm just hiding behind it.

- the breaking-it idea. if the late-edit story is right then the rewriting is a process, and processes can be interfered with — load, a distractor, a zap to the head at the right moment, whatever. so you ought to be able to catch the unrewritten version. if the early-edit story is right there's nothing to catch, ever. is that asymmetry a real discriminator, or does the early-editor just say "the broken case is a different construction" and we're back to square one?

- the old partial-report trick, the one where you flash a grid of letters, sound a tone to say which row to read out, and the person can read any row — so they had far more in them than they could ever report. suppose you do the equivalent after the two flashes and can recover the raw blue that way. does that count as evidence the blue was experienced? because if "had it available" is just never the same as "experienced it," i worry my position is bulletproof in a way that should itself worry me.

- one head, two answers: what if on a single trial the person says "flew across and changed" but their body does something that only makes sense if the blue was still blue. does two disagreeing reports out of one system force a fact of the matter?

- and a deflating one: what if the real difference between early-edit and late-edit isn't about *when* at all, but about what gets stored, or what later thinking can get at? then the distinction is real and mine is just confused. is that what's actually going on?

also tell me what someone who holds my view has to concede — the cases where even we'd say "yeah that's bad for us." not what critics say, what we'd say.

and the bit i actually care about: is this falsifiable, or have i built it so nothing touches it? i can feel a version of it that's trivially true — of course you can always redescribe the same data two ways — and if that's all i've got, say so, and say whether that's fatal for a claim about experience or just exactly what you'd expect from a claim whose whole point is that a distinction is empty. does my view even predict anything its rivals don't? if not, is that a bug?

i'd be most moved by a real case where the raw blue has a consequence the edited version can't have — something the person *does* that only makes sense if at some point they were seeing blue. if you've got one, lead with it.
