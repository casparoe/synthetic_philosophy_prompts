I am convening a short reading group on Wittgenstein's On Certainty for a local meetup of people who read a great deal of rationalist blogging and have almost no philosophical training. They will have been handed the book but will retain very little of it, and I have sixty minutes for the single session that has to make the central idea land. Do not explain the idea to me; I understand it well enough. Plan how I should explain it to them.

Give me, first, the order in which the components have to be introduced and why each depends on the ones before it: what must already be in place before the notion of a proposition that is exempt from doubt can be stated without immediately sounding like an endorsement of dogmatism; whether and where the contrast with a Moore-style claim to know belongs in the sequence, or whether it belongs nowhere and should be saved for the second session; at what point the thought that reasons give out should enter; and where the audience's Bayesian instincts -- they will want to ask for the prior, the likelihood ratio, the update rule -- will misfire, and what I should say at that exact moment.

Then specify the one example that should carry the whole session. It has to be mundane and drawn from the audience's own lives rather than from the philosophical stock of chestnuts: not "here is one hand", not the evil demon, not brains in vats, not the riverbed, not the door that will not close. It has to be a case in which the certainty in question shows up in what someone does rather than in a claim they could state aloud, and one in which the person could not have articulated the hinge without being prompted. Tell me what the example must have and what it must lack in order to do this work, and why the obvious candidates fail.

Give me the misconception map next. I expect at minimum the reading that hinges are simply beliefs held with very high confidence; the reading that Wittgenstein is licensing a community-relative or fideist view on which any framework is as good as any other; and the reading that "neither known nor doubted" is a verbal dodge that collapses into either knowledge or mere animal habit. For each, name the exact point in the sequence at which it should be headed off and the move that heads it off -- including, for the relativism reading, the objection I expect from the sharpest person in the room, who has argued about simulation hypotheses over beer and will conclude that if hinges are beyond doubt then we cannot criticize anyone's framework at all.

Finish with one question I can put to the room at the end that would show whether the idea has landed rather than merely been assented to, and with a short list of what I should explicitly decline to settle at this level and say that I am declining.
