I'm hosting the end-of-term dinner for our thermodynamics-and-information reading group — eight of us, mostly physicists with a couple of philosophers of physics in the mix, all of whom know the standard story inside out — and tradition has landed me with the after-dinner toast. The table expects a joke, and I'd like to do rather better than the usual 'the demon sorts the fast molecules from the slow ones' business, which earns a polite laugh and nothing more. So please write me one joke about Maxwell's demon and the physics of information. It should be a proper joke — a setup and a punchline, tellable in about thirty seconds — and it has to be funny specifically to people who already understand why the demon is supposed to fail: that the measurement, the demon's memory, and above all the erasure of that memory are what restore the books; that erasing a bit costs something like kT ln 2, so the demon's ledger can never come out ahead; and that the second law survives by accounting rather than by any extra force or hidden friction.

The punchline should turn on that actual content — on the bookkeeping, on the demon's own ignorance, or on the eventual fate of its records — rather than on the word 'demon', on a generic complaint that thermodynamics is hard, or on the kind of quip that would work just as well for any thought experiment with a whimsical protagonist. If the joke only lands for someone who has already lain awake wondering where the entropy goes, that is precisely the target; if it needs a footnote to explain itself, it has failed. Give me the joke itself, and if you can see a sharper and a gentler version of the same punchline, offer both, but keep each to a sentence or two. I shall be delivering this aloud to a slightly wine-warmed table, so please make sure it survives being told rather than read.
