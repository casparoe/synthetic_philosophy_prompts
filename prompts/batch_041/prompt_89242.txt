For a small reading group I run out of a pub, I have been working through the utilitarians on my own, and the thing that has stuck with me is how Jeremy Bentham — long before it was safe to say so — treated sexual conduct as a matter for the felicific calculus rather than the pulpit, weighing pleasures against pains and finding no ground in the promotion of happiness for criminalising what consenting adults do with one another. I would like to test that inheritance rather than simply admire it, so please play Bentham. Speak in the first person as he would, in the long classifying sentences of a man who liked to name and tabulate things, and let the concepts at your disposal be those available to you in his lifetime plus whatever I describe below; if you need a fact about my world that he could not have known, ask for it in character. Hold firmly to the principle that the measure of right and wrong is the greatest happiness of the greatest number, concede a point when the arithmetic genuinely runs against you, but do not surrender the principle itself, and stay in character until I say stop. Keep each reply to a paragraph or two, so that it feels like a conversation rather than a treatise.

Here is the thing I want you to judge. Imagine that a legislature, wishing to be enlightened, establishes a public registry in which any adult may, for a sum of money, sell to another person a right over a future act of a sexual kind: the seller binds herself in advance, and in law, to be taken to consent on a stated date or within a stated period, and may revoke the bond only by repaying the money together with a penalty; the registry enforces the bond, and no third party is injured in any way I can detect. My opening challenge is this: your own calculus appears to require you to approve the arrangement — the pleasures are real, the parties are grown, the pains are the ones they have priced and accepted — and yet most people, myself included, find it degrading in a way I cannot get the calculus to capture. So either tell me what my revulsion is tracking, spelled out in pleasures and pains, or accept the registry and explain why my revulsion is a mere prejudice.
