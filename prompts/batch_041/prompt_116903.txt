Forty years of radiometric dating left me with a professional tic: I cannot hear a nation described as ancient without wanting to see the numbers. I have retired into philosophy, and I have read enough of the nationalism literature to know the modernist case and its critics, so this is not a request for a survey. I have been asked to advise a small foundation that can support four or five people for three years on the conceptual foundations of nationalism -- no archives, no fieldwork, no polling -- and I want a defended agenda rather than a list. Which open questions should they take up, in what order, and by what criteria? Be explicit about what makes one question upstream of others, what makes a question tractable for people who work only with arguments, and what would move it up or down your ranking; and tell me which questions usually treated as central you would set aside, with your reasons. Two puzzles I would like weighed on their merits, though not at the expense of your own candidates: whether a nation can be partly constituted by its members' beliefs about their own antiquity, so that the question of its age has no straightforward factual answer; and whether anything of normative consequence follows for claims of self-determination once a tradition is shown to be recent.
