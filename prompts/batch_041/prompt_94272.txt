For some years now I've followed the discussions of low-impact AI and side-effect avoidance, and there's a pattern in the proposed measures that I can describe but not name.

The pattern: someone wants a penalty term that makes an agent minimize how much it disturbs the world, and wants the term defined without smuggling in any particular goal or value, so that it can be dropped into an arbitrary system. In practice the measure always comes out as a distance between the world the agent actually produced and some baseline world — the world as it would have been had the agent done nothing, or had it acted only through a designated channel. And the entire content of the measure then lives in that baseline. Whether the agent looks reckless or inert is decided by which counterfactual you pick, and nothing in the value-free definition forces the choice.

Two consequences I keep tripping over. First, a measure that only counts deviation from the default scores an agent that prevents a harm exactly as badly as one that causes it, since both move the world away from the default; harm prevention and harm causation collapse into the same kind of event. Second, proposals that try to escape the baseline problem by ignoring what the agent was trying to do end up unable to distinguish the change the agent wanted from the change it didn't — which is precisely the distinction that "side effect" was supposed to mark. So the value-neutrality is bought either by relocating the value into the baseline or by dissolving the very distinction being measured.

Is there an established term or concept for this exact snag? I don't mean the broad points about Goodhart's law or the alignment problem in general; I mean the narrower claim about measures of impact or of side effects. If nothing established fits, coin a term and define it, and tell me how it stands to (a) the general fact that any measure presupposes a baseline, and (b) the specific thesis that "avoiding side effects" cannot be defined without a full specification of value. I'd rather have the carving than the label. Please answer in American English.
