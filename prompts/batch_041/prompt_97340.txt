I run a third-year seminar on values and measurement -- 14 students, 80 minutes, one room, no prep from them. I want them to feel the transitivity problem in their gut before anyone names it, and I have a rough mechanic in mind that you should feel free to rebuild if it won't fly.

Everyone gets a card describing an outcome with two dials on it, call them A and B (I'd use something like number of people mildly inconvenienced vs. how badly one person is harmed, but pick whatever makes the steps feel live). There are 20 cards and they're built as a chain: each card differs from the next by exactly one unit on each dial, in opposite directions, and the two ends are monstrously far apart. Students line up holding cards 1 through 20. Then the posted Swap Rule: when your card is compared with the neighbour to your right, you must swap if the right card is at least one unit better on dial A -- even though it's one unit worse on dial B. Nobody chooses the rule, they just execute it. We run the pass left to right, then right to left, and read off where everything lands.

The bit I want you to design carefully: a prediction ledger. Before every pairwise judgement each student writes down two things -- what they think the person at the far end of the chain will say about the two endpoints, and whether they expect their own verdict to survive to the end of the game. Then we compare the ledger to what actually happened.

Payoffs, no real money: two teams, points per swap executed correctly, and the team holding the card the room ultimately calls worst gets to set the seminar's next discussion topic. Debrief has to map the moments onto the structure of the argument, say what will predictably go wrong first time and how I should handle it, and be straight with me about what the game can't reproduce -- I suspect there's a gap between "the rule I imposed is intransitive" and "better than is intransitive" that I'll have to supply myself. Keep the whole thing under 800 words.
