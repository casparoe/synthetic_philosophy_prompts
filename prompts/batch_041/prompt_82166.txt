Ive been trying to get my head around Bradley's regress -- the argument that if a relation is a real thing that has to tie its terms together, then you need a further relation to tie that relation to those terms, and then another one, forever, so nothing ever actually gets united. Ive collected the escapes I keep running into, plus two I came up with myself, and I want you to sort them into tiers by how well they actually work as solutions, not by how famous they are:

1. Relations aren't extra things at all -- for a to relate to b is just a fact about what a and b are like, so there's nothing separate left over that needs tying down.
2. The regress is harmless: every step is trivially true and tells us nothing new, so demanding a stopping point was a confused demand to begin with.
3. The unity comes from outside the proposition: someone is judging that a relates to b, and the act of judging is the glue. Take the judging away and you have a list, not a thought.
4. The relation itself is the unifier, and it isn't a third item sitting alongside the terms -- relating is just what a relation does.
5. The proposition is a primitive whole: it has parts but it was never assembled out of them, so "what holds it together?" is like asking what holds a word's letters together.
6. The unity lives in the world rather than in the thought: there's a fact there, and facts aren't built out of pieces that need gluing.
7. Mine: let the regress run forever and call that fine -- an endless chain of relations unifies just as well as a finished one would, we just have to stop assuming that only a completed chain can do the job.
8. Mine: say the relation at each level is a different *kind* of thing from the one below it, and kinds of relations don't themselves get related, so the whole thing stops after a step or two by fiat.

Which tier does each land in, with one sentence of reasoning each? Also tell me if any of them are secretly the same answer in different words, and if you had to bet on one, which would it be and what's the worst thing you'd have to give up by betting on it.
