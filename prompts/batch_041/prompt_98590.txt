I am a trustee of a small woodland charity, and I would like advice on a decision the board has to take.

Our scheme is simple: we pay a landowner a fixed sum for each hectare of native broadleaf woodland they plant and maintain for ten years. Our endowment covers a modest number of hectares each year, and we have just opened a fourth round of applications. Three difficulties have surfaced at once, and I suspect they are not the same difficulty.

The first is that two applicants appear to have cleared mature native woodland and then replanted it. Their paperwork is in order, because our criteria are written in terms of what is planted rather than in terms of what was there beforehand. The second is that most of the land we have funded is land nobody else wanted — steep, wet, awkward fields whose baseline was already poor — so the hectares we can point to flatter us, while the woodlands that matter most to us are being looked after by other people with other money. The third is that we can no longer find out whether the scheme works at all: the only sites we hold data for are sites that applied to us, and the people who apply are the people who wanted the money. Our evidence about the scheme is manufactured by the scheme.

A fellow trustee draws the moral that this is simply what happens to every number, that whatever figure we choose will be chased and distorted within a year or two, and that we should abandon the hectare payment and fund people we trust, case by case, on our judgement. I think she is half right and half dangerous. Another trustee proposes keeping the hectare count but adding a condition that no mature woodland may be lost, with an audit. I can already see how I would evade that condition if I were a landowner.

What should we do — keep, repair, or abandon the hectare measure? I am also asking something less practical: whether my colleague's position is even coherent. Is there any real alternative to being steered by some measure, or is the choice only between the measures we admit to using and the ones we pretend not to be using? And if we do keep a measure, what can we honestly tell our funders, who want a headline figure and have hinted that next year's grant depends on that figure rising? Please be concrete, and tell me what I would be giving up in each case. I do not need a survey of the literature.
