Alright, weird request. I've been down a wild animal suffering rabbit hole lately — the crowd who argue that the average wild animal's life is net negative, that nature is basically a big suffering-production machine, and that once you take that seriously you have to at least consider the unthinkable: that wiping out a habitat, or letting a population blink out, could count as a welfare improvement, since it stops the next generation from being born into the same grim lottery. Some of them go further and say ending all life would be the kindest move available. I'm not asking you to settle that.

What I want is Schopenhauer. He never saw any of this — no ecology, no welfare biology, no rewilding fights, none of it. But he's the guy who stared at the will-to-live and decided the whole business is suffering, that the cheerful picture of a harmonious nature is a con, and who was, famously, softer on animals than on people. So predict his posistion. Not some vague thing about him being sad about it — I want the line his system actually forces, with the work shown.

Concretely: (1) Would he even accept the net-welfare framing, or is that a category mistake for him — on his view, is a life the sort of thing that can be netted out? (2) His metaphysics says the will is one and the same in the predator and the prey; does that push toward intervening (it's all one suffering, so cutting it anywhere cuts it everywhere) or away from it (individual animals were never the real unit)? (3) His ethics famously bottoms out in resignation and the denial of the will, not in fixing the world. So would he actually back the habitat-destruction argument, or would he say whoever makes it is just the will trying to will its way out of itself — i.e., the argument eats itself? (4) Where does his system genuinely underdetermine the answer, such that answering with confidence would mean smuggling in modern welfarism?

And be honest about which parts are his actual structure and which are just his temperment — I don't want a mood dressed up as a metaphysics. Keep it tight, doesn't need to be an essay.
