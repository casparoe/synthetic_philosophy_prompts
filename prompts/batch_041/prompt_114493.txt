I'm building a handout for a seminar that's half philosophy grad students and half people from the econ / behavioral side, and I want a decision tree that takes someone from "yeah, I've stayed up till 3am watching one more episode when I knew I'd regret it" to a named position on weakness of will. The point is to sort them by what they actually think is happening in the case, not by which philosopher they've happened to read.

Make it a tree where each internal node is a question you could ask about a concrete case, phrased so that a person can answer it without already knowing that it's the akrasia question, and each leaf is a position. The branches should be the real fault lines. Roughly: does genuine akrasia even exist, or is it always a case of the person not really judging the better option best (the Socratic line)? If it exists, is the failure in the judgment, in the motivation, or in the execution? Is the "better judgment" actually time-indexed — so the person who has the extra drink wasn't overriding a judgment that sobriety was best, but acting on a judgment that the drink was best-now (Davidson-ish)? Does hyperbolic discounting just dissolve the problem, i.e. is this a preference reversal over time rather than a failure of will, so the agent is better modelled as a series of selves who disagree than as one self who loses (Ainslie, Schelling-style intrapersonal bargaining, precommitment devices like Odysseus)? And where does the Aristotelian split between the weak-willed person (who regrets it) and the intemperate one (who doesn't) drop out?

I want at least these leaves: the Socratic / denial position; the Aristotelian akrasia-vs-intemperance position; the Davidsonian "the all-things-considered judgment was never what you thought it was" position; the two-selves / intrapersonal-bargaining position; the pure hyperbolic-discounting deflationary position (there's no akrasia, just time-inconsistent preferences described correctly); the willpower-as-a-depletable-resource position (flag that it's contested); and at least one unnamed leaf for a combination nobody actually defends, with a sentence on why nobody's there. If the tree can't cleanly separate two positions — I suspect it can't separate Davidson's from the discounting deflationary view — say so instead of papering over it.

The nodes should use these cases: staying up for one more episode; hitting snooze; promising yourself you'll start the diet Monday; buying cigarettes "for a friend"; Odysseus tied to the mast; and someone who sincerely believes smoking is bad for them and lights up while thinking about exactly that. Add one node about whether it changes anything if the person is clear-eyed at the moment — they know they're doing the wrong thing and do it anyway. And one node on whether the act is done for a reason the person could avow as a reason, or whether the reason only works in the moment and would look like no reason at all from outside.

8-12 nodes, whatever it takes to keep the leaves honest. If a leaf is just a relabeling of another one, say so rather than padding the tree.
