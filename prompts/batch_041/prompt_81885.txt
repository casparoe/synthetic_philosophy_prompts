Two philosophers have argued for two years without converging: one a resourcist, who holds that the currency of distributive justice is each person's command over an all-purpose bundle of resources — income, wealth, and the primary goods — and the other a capabilities theorist, who holds that the currency is what each person is actually able to do and be. They have agreed to an adversarial collaboration. Draft the joint statement both could sign.

It should contain a numbered section of claims both accept, including that resources matter only as means, that people convert the same resources into very different lives, and that any acceptable metric must be sensitive to those conversion differences; a section stating the residual disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would accept as its view; and an identification of the cruxes, among them whether the expensive-taste objection is fatal to resourcism, whether the metric should track real opportunity or achieved functioning, and whether capabilities whose value is purely positional belong in the metric at all. State explicitly which part of the dispute you judge to be merely verbal.

For each crux, supply a concrete case both sides would agree bears on it and record each side's prediction about it in advance. Use two cases that are not the textbook ones: a woman whose preferences adapted over years of deprivation, so that she now reports contentment with a life of severely restricted options; and a town that must distribute a fixed accessibility budget either as equal cash grants to all residents or as targeted conversion subsidies to the residents whose conversion factors are unusually low.

End with a disagreements table.
