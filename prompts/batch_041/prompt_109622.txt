Hey, I'm a TA for a second-year engineering course called Technology and Society — the prof does the big lectures, I run a 75-minute section every other week, about twenty students, whiteboard only, no slides. Next section is on the "do artifacts have politics" question, i.e. Winner's "Do Artifacts Have Politics?", the claim that the design of a thing can settle who gets to do what. I don't want you to explain the thesis to me, I want a plan for the section. What I need first is the order the pieces have to go in and why each one has to wait for the one before it. I'm fairly sure I can't open with the word "politics" because alot of them hear that as parties and elections, but tell me what actually has to be nailed down before the question even parses for them, where the contrast with plain technological determinism has to land relative to the contrast with the "tools are neutral" view, and where the trickier three-way distinction belongs — an artifact having political consequences vs. an artifact being political vs. an artifact requiring a certain social order — or whether that last bit is too much for this audience and should just be cut.

Then the carrying example. Everyone's go-to is the famous historical bridge story and half my students have already had it in another course, so I want to build the whole thing on a case from our own building: the department bought a shared instrument and its booking software has three tiers — undergrads can only book 9-to-5 slots, grad students can book whenever but get bumped with one click by faculty, and the usage dashboard is visible only to the lab manager. I think it's a decent carrier, but you tell me: what does a case like this need to have in order to do the work, and what would ruin it — if it turns out the professor personally told the vendor to lock out the undergrads, does the case die? Also the misconceptions, in the order they'll actually surface and at exactly which point in my sequence I should head each one off. And what's the one question I should ask at the end that tells me the idea landed, rather than just that they can recite the two-senses taxonomy back at me?
