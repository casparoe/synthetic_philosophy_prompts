I am giving a paper at our ancient philosophy seminar next week and should like you to prepare me for the discussion. My thesis is that Plato's defence of regulated drinking in the first two books of the Laws is neither a lapse from the austerity of the Republic nor a concession to human weakness, but a substantive claim about what virtue requires. The Athenian Stranger's argument, as I reconstruct it, is that courage and temperance are not the mere possession of correct opinions but the capacity to hold on to them while pleasure, fear and desire pull one about; that wine is a drug which dissolves fear and shame and is for that very reason the instrument the god has supplied for rehearsing that capacity; and that the human being is best pictured as a puppet hauled by several cords, of which only one, the golden cord of law, is to be grasped. On this reading the regulated drinking party, with a sober elder presiding, is a gymnasium of the soul, and a lawgiver who simply prohibits wine forfeits a means of education. I need the objections this seminar will actually raise, in the form in which they will raise them, and an honest ranking of how much damage each does.

The audience is mixed, and the mixture matters. Roughly half are classicists who work on Athenian law and on the symposion, and their first instinct will be to ask whether the Athenian's arrangements describe or travesty Greek drinking practice; two are Plato scholars who read the Laws chiefly as theology and penology and who think any attempt to reconcile it with the Republic flattens both works; one is a philosopher of public health who works on addiction and will hear the whole argument as a brief for state-managed intoxication. Give me the questions each of these groups will put, ordered by how likely they are and how much damage they would do, each phrased as its author would phrase it, with a reply I could deliver in two minutes and a note on where that reply is soft. Tell me what I should concede at the outset rather than have it extracted from me, and end with the one question I am hoping nobody asks.
