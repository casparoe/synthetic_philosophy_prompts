My flatmate's in law school and we ended up arguing till about 1am over whether you can understand a book without caring what the author meant. Her move was the statute analogy: she says when a court reads a statute nobody asks what the legislator was thinking, they read the words, and if the words are ambiguous you argue about the words, not about the draftsman's hopes — so the same goes for a novel, and anyone who insists you need the author's intention is just smuggling in a biography. She added that the author might be dead, or lying, or have simply forgotten, so a meaning that depends on their mental state is a meaning nobody can actually get at. I said a statute is a terrible model, because its written by hundreds of people who never agree on anything, it gets applied to strangers by officials who can fine them, and it has to be predictable in advance — none of which is true of a poem — but I couldn't say why any of that matters before we both gave up and went to bed annoyed.
