My grasp of that machinery is solid and operational. My grasp of physics and metaphysics is that of an interested amateur who has read the popular versions and suspects they flatten the interesting parts.

I would like the debate over the grandfather paradox restated in my vocabulary, as faithfully as it can be restated at all. Please set out the main positions in my terms: the view that the past is fixed, so backward time travel is either impossible or incoherent; the self-consistency constraint usually credited to Novikov, on which only globally consistent histories are physically realizable, so the traveller's gun jams or their aim fails and nothing is contradicted — together with the complication that for a given set-up there may be several, or infinitely many, consistent histories and nothing in the theory selects among them; the branching or many-timelines view, on which the traveller really does kill their grandfather but thereby opens a new branch instead of altering anything; the reply that the traveller is perfectly able to kill their grandfather in the ordinary sense of able, and simply never does; and the diagnosis that the question is malformed because it tacitly assumes a single global time in which the past can be an object of action.

Then give me the residue report. Where does the mapping hold structurally rather than merely suggestively, and where does it distort? What is present in the original debate but inexpressible in my vocabulary? I am especially interested in four things. First, whether a commit DAG can represent a closed causal loop at all, or whether the requirement that every commit descend from a root is precisely the assumption the time-travel case denies. Second, whether multiple self-consistent histories are just non-determinism awaiting a tie-break policy, or something for which my vocabulary has no room. Third, what becomes of personal identity, memory, and the indexical now once every agent is a node in a graph rather than something that persists through it. Fourth, whether the ability reply survives translation, or collapses into a claim about what a scheduler permits.

Keep the translation honest: do not let my framework smuggle in its own assumptions, and in particular keep the record of the world distinct from the world itself — a force-push must not stand in for changing the past, and a merge must not stand in for two histories interacting. Finish by telling me whether the residue marks a genuine gap in my framework or shows that the original claims were less clear than they looked.
