Suppose that tomorrow a short, mechanically checkable derivation of a contradiction from the standard axioms of set theory — Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory with Choice — were produced, using only ordinary classical logic. I would like a careful account of what exactly would and would not have been established. Begin by separating the claim that the formal system is inconsistent from the claim that its intended subject matter, the cumulative hierarchy or the natural numbers, is itself contradictory or fails to exist, and explain why consistency amounts to the existence of a model while inconsistency is the impossibility of one, so that an inconsistent theory describes nothing at all. Explain why inconsistency is witnessed by a finite, checkable object whereas consistency admits no comparable certificate, and why this asymmetry makes the two hypotheses evidentially uneven: the absence of a contradiction so far is weak evidence, while a contradiction, once found, could not be explained away. Then address the self-undermining aspect. The derivation would rely on the very axioms and rules now under suspicion, and an inconsistent system proves every sentence, including its own consistency and the negation of the contradiction; so it is not obvious what epistemic standing the discovery could have. Can the inconsistency be certified on a footing that does not already presuppose the system, or is the position inescapably circular? I am not asking which axiom is likeliest to be at fault, nor for a history of foundational crises; I want the conceptual anatomy of the situation.
