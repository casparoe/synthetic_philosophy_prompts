ok this is going to sound like a riddle but its a real pattern and i keep running into it and i dont have a word for it.

heres the pattern. i make a choice. its the choice i'd defend at the time, genuinely, not rationalizing, and its still the choice i'd defend now. then i make another one like that. then another. and after a while i'm somewhere i would never in a million years have agreed to go — somewhere i'd have paid real money to avoid — and i cant point at any single step and say "there, thats where i went wrong." no step was wrong. each one made things better by my own lights. nobodys trading with me, theres no fee, theres no trader at all. two examples so you believe me: (1) i keep saying yes to slightly more stuff at work because each individual ask is small and saying no feels like making a fuss, and now i have a job i would never have applied for. (2) i keep replacing things i already own with slightly better versions, each swap is a small obvious win, and now my flat is full of expensive junk i dont want.

what bugs me is that the badness is only there in the sequence. take any single step out and its fine. and my preferences havent changed — i wanted the same things at the start and at the end. so somehow a chain of moves each of which is an improvement lands me somewhere worse than where i started, judged by the very preferences that drove every move.

i know about money pumps and about weakness of will and neither is it. a money pump needs a trader and a fee and my preferences actually going round in a circle, and none of that is happening here. weakness of will isnt it either, because i'm not caving to something i think i shouldnt do — i endorse every single step. so: is there a name for this? if there is, i want it, but i want the carving more. what exactly is it, and how do i tell it apart from a money pump, from the tragedy of the commons (which i think is different because theres only one of me here), from just changing my mind over time, and from a plain mistake about what i'll want later. is this even a failure of rationality, or is it just what it looks like to be a thing that has to live forwards? and is there anything a person could actually do about it, given that the obvious fix — decide now where you want to end up and refuse the small wins along the way — means turning down things that are genuinely better at the time. if nothing in the literature fits, coin a term and tell me exactly what it rules out.
