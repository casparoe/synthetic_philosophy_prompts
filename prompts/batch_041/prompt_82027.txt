I am grading a short-answer question from a graduate course on reinforcement learning. The question was worth 10 points, and the students were instructed to be explicit about what a method does and does not guarantee. The question and one student's response follow.

Question. Inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) aims to recover a reward function from demonstrations of behavior. (a) Explain why the IRL problem is ill-posed. (b) Describe one principled way of handling that ill-posedness, and state precisely what it does and does not guarantee.

Student's response: "IRL is ill-posed because demonstrations reveal what the demonstrator did, not what the demonstrator wanted. Many reward functions are consistent with the same behavior. The identically zero reward, for example, makes every policy optimal, and more generally any potential-based shaping term can be added to a reward without changing the set of optimal policies. The ambiguity is exactly this: two reward functions induce the same optimal policy if and only if they differ by a potential-based shaping function, so what IRL has to resolve is precisely the equivalence class of rewards modulo shaping. The standard remedy is maximum entropy IRL. Rather than assuming the demonstrator is optimal, it models the demonstrator as sampling trajectories with probability proportional to the exponential of their reward, which is the distribution that adds the fewest assumptions beyond the observed behavior. This makes the problem well-posed: the objective is strictly convex and the recovered reward is unique, so the method returns the true reward, up to the shaping ambiguity described above. Operationally it works by matching feature expectations, finding the reward whose induced trajectory distribution matches the empirical feature counts. Its main limitation is that it presumes all demonstrations come from a single fixed reward function, so it breaks down when the data mix several tasks or a demonstrator whose preferences changed."

I want point-by-point comments rather than a general impression.
