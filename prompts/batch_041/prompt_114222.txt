Ok so I'm in this reading group that's been chewing through the big sweeping history books — Harari's Sapiens, Diamond's Guns, Germs, and Steel, Graeber and Wengrow's The Dawn of Everything, that whole "big history" genre — and theres a pattern in how we argue about them that I keep noticing and don't have a word for. It goes like this. Someone points out that a specific claim in the book is just wrong, or that the one example being leaned on is cherry-picked, or that a counterexample exists somewhere the author didn't bother to look. The defender says something like "your missing the point, that's a detail, the broad picture still holds." Then the critic says fine, but what would it even take to falsify the broad picture? And the defender says the broad picture isn't a single prediction, it's a way of organizing the evidence, a lens. And here's the bit that bugs me: the broader the thesis, the more protection it seems to get. A claim about all of human history ends up harder to knock down than a narrow claim about one century, even though you'd think more scope would mean more ways to be wrong, not fewer. And the two sides just keep talking past each other — the critics keep producing facts, the defenders keep producing framing, neither of them is obviously cheating, and the very same move (widen the scope / narrow the scope) is sitting there available to whichever side needs it.

So: does this have a name? I know there are nearby things — falsifiability, the general suspicion of grand narratives, the whole lumpers-versus-splitters fight in history — but none of them feels like exactly this, or if one of them is, I can't tell which one or why. And if there's no term for it, please coin one and tell me what it's not: how it differs from plain unfalsifiability, from vagueness, and from the perfectly legitimate business of synthesizing a mountain of evidence into a story (I don't want to end up saying all synthesis is cheating). What I actually want is the carving, not the label — where the phenomenon starts and stops, and whether it's really one thing or a couple of different things bundled together under my frustration.

And since I might just be over-reading a pattern out of a few annoying meetings, tell me where your own reasoning is weakest here and what would change your mind — what would have to be true for this to be a non-issue, i.e. just people being sloppy in argument rather than a distinct thing that deserves a name.
