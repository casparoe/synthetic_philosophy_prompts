I keep circling back to this Hofstadter/Sander line that analogy isn't one of the things thinking does, it's the thing thinking is — that every act of categorization, recogizing this as another one of those, is already an analogy, and there's no separate rule-book step hiding underneath it. Interesting, sure. But then I look at how we actually measure intelligence and something seems off to me.

Take Raven's Progressive Matrices, the grid of shapes with a cell missing where you pick which of the options completes the pattern. It's the go-to non-verbal estimate of general intelligence, the thing people point at when they want a language-free, culture-reduced measure of g, the eductive make-meaning part. And the way you solve an item is: spot the relation running down the first row, notice it's the same relation in the second row, carry it across to the missing cell. That is an analogy. So if analogy is the whole engine, the test isn't tapping some sub-faculty, it's tapping thought as such.

But the same test batteries will cheerfully list analogical reasoning as one subtest sitting next to working memory and processing speed, like it's a component that could get knocked out on its own. And IQ testing more generally acts as if there's one scale of difficulty a content-free puzzle can climb.

So: what commitments does the practice of intelligence testing actually presuppose about how cognition is organized? Does it need analogy to be a central, domain-general capacity, or is a thin proxy story enough — analogy scores correlate with whatever g is, who cares why? Does it presuppose that abstract shape puzzles can stand in for all the analogies a mind makes, and is that even coherent if every analogy is made in some particular medium? Which views does it quietly rule out — modularity, multiple intelligences, embodied or situated cognition — and which of those turn out to be compatable with it after all once you look closely? Be specific about the difference between what the practice strictly requires and what it just happens to have grown up next to. And if someone took the strong analogy claim seriously, what would have to change — the items, the scoring, or only how we read the scores?
