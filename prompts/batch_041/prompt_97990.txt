I sit on the board of a wildlife trust, and one of my fellow trustees ends every discussion of our restoration programme with the same line: 'a healthy ecosystem means healthy animals.' Last week she used it against a member who observed that the drained, species-poor marsh we intend to restore probably holds far fewer animals — and therefore far less suffering — than the predator-rich wetland the restoration is meant to produce, so that the project might leave the animals worse off even as it heals the landscape. I want the precise claims that one-liner could be making, laid out as a menu of readings running from the trivial to the radical rather than resolved into a single one. For each reading, tell me what it commits the speaker to, whether it is a definition, an empirical claim, or a value judgement, whether it is a platitude, defensible, or false, and whether it would actually deliver her conclusion that restoring the wetland benefits the animals. Be explicit wherever a reading requires that an ecosystem itself have a welfare, or that 'healthy' be measured by something — biomass, diversity, stability, absence of human impact — with no intrinsic connection to how the animals in it fare. Then say which reading she most likely has in mind when she uses the line to shut the argument down, and what she would have to give up if she were pressed off it.
