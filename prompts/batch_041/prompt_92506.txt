Here is the gist of the handout. Epiphenomenalism is the thesis that conscious experiences are caused by brain processes but never cause anything in turn: experiences are perfectly real, but they are side effects, like the steam whistle on a locomotive, which makes a noise but does no work. The paradox of phenomenal judgment is the standard objection to this, and it runs as follows. If experiences have no causal powers, then my belief that I am currently having an experience cannot have been caused by the experience itself; it must be produced entirely by brain processes. But then my beliefs about my own consciousness would be reliable only by accident, since nothing in their production is ever actually connected to the experiences they purport to be about. Epiphenomenalism therefore cannot be true, because it makes it impossible for anyone to know that consciousness exists, including the epiphenomenalist himself, who thus cannot even coherently state his own view.
