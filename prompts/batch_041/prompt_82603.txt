I'm a novelist, about two hundred pages into a book set in 1793 — the year Lord Macartney's embassy turns up in China, asks for a permanent embassy in Beijing and a few more ports, and gets politely stonewalled by the Qianlong emperor — and the book is really about contingency, about the roads not taken. So I've been reading around the why-Britain-and-not-China question (Needham's question, the Great Divergence debate) and I keep thinking the key to it is a bit of philosophy of science I half-remember: that a why-question is really "why this rather than that," and the "rather than that" is part of the question, not scenery. So "why Britain rather than China," "why Britain rather than France," and "why not China" are three different questions with three different bodies of relevant evidence, even though they get asked as if they were one. Van Fraassen, I think, or Lipton — I can never remember who gets the credit — plus the cousin idea that explaining is just locating the difference-maker.

Do the case analysis in both directions. Take the Macartney episode — the kowtow fight (which the two sides seem to have recorded completly differently, and I don't care who's right), the clocks and telescopes the court filed under tribute, the emperor's letter to George III that amounts to thanks-but-we-have-everything — and tell me which why-question that episode is actually evidence for and which it's useless for. Who's asking, what's their foil, and what would have had to be different for the embassy to be a cause rather than a symptom of the divergence. Then tell me what the episode does back to the theory: does the whole contrast-class apparatus survive a case where the two sides don't even agree on what happened, and where you've got one unrepeatable event and no way to run it again? And say where the framework goes quiet instead of making it answer.

Last thing: is the question well posed at all, or does it come apart once you ask which China, which Britain, and which "rather than"? Skip the reading list, I'll look that up myself, and don't just give me the standard coal-and-colonies, Canton-system story, I've read that alot.
