ok so I'm a senior and next month my school is doing this media-and-democracy night where a few students give short speeches to parents, teachers, and basically the whole school, and somehow I got picked. I want to talk about our school's anonymous confession account (you know the one, the instagram with like 4k followers, where everyone finds out who likes who and also where the genuinely nasty stuff goes). The obvious speech is anonymous accounts are mean, be nice, and I dont want to give that speech. Here's my actual idea and I need you to write the 5-minute speech.

There's this old Danish guy Kierkegaard who made a distinction between a crowd and the public, and it's not the distinction people usually think it is. A crowd is actual people standing somewhere; you can look one of them in the eye and they have to answer for what they said. The public is something else entirely. He calls it a phantom, a monstrous abstraction, an all-encompassing something that is nothing. Nobody is the public. If you ask who exactly you're addressing, there's no one there. And he thought the press is what spins this phantom out, especially once the press itself stops being particular people writing particular things and becomes an abstraction too. His worry was that you can address the phantom and feel like you've said something to everyone while actually being answerable to no one.

My argument is that the confession account doesn't just make bullying easier (everyone already says that), it manufactures a public. Before it, if you wanted to say something about someone at school, you said it to a crowd — real faces, and you were one of the faces. Now everyone posts into the phantom and the phantom answers back, and nobody in particular is holding the pen. I want to end with a concrete ask: shut the account down, or at minimum stop feeding it, and I want to admit in the speech that I've posted to it myself, so it's not me lecturing everyone from outside.

The hard part is the room. Half of it lives on that account and will smell fake philosophy instantly. The other half is parents and teachers, including my debate coach, who actually knows Kierkegaard and will catch it if I get him wrong. So it has to be accurate enough for her and clear enough for my mom, and it has to land like me trying to work something out rather than like a lecture. Also I can't quote him — I don't have the book and I'd botch the wording — so paraphrase the phantom idea in my own words. And one thing the speech has to deal with, because my coach will definitely raise it: someone could say a school is small enough that it's still a crowd, not a public, so why does Kierkegaard's distinction even apply here? Answer that instead of dodging it. Please don't let it end on so be kind online. Write the speech.
