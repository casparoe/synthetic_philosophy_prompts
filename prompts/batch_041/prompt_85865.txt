I am designing a ninety-minute session for an interdisciplinary graduate workshop — half cognitive scientists, half philosophers, all of them physicalists who will arrive assuming the question is already settled — on whether representational states at a higher level of organisation exercise any causal influence over the lower-level substrate that realises them. I intend to build the session around Hofstadter's careenium, the thought experiment from I Am a Strange Loop: a billiard table crowded with countless small magnetic marbles, the simms, which occasionally clump together into spherical clusters, the simmballs, whose trajectories are partly fixed by forces external to the careenium and thereby begin to model conditions outside it. Hofstadter's question is who shoves whom, and his answer is that the simmballs shove each other. The audience will have read nothing in advance. What I want from you is not the explanation but a plan for delivering it.

Set out the order in which the components must be introduced and why each has to wait for its predecessor — in particular, which criterion of causation must already be on the board before the question of whether the simmballs push the simms can be answered rather than merely gestured at, and where the difference between a higher-level state's being a difference-maker and its being an extra force has to be installed. Then tell me whether the careenium can carry the session or only part of it: what it supplies that no real-world case supplies, what it cannot supply because its design is stipulative and its substrate is already symbol-like, what second case should therefore carry the weight it cannot, and why the obvious alternative — opening with brains and beliefs — should or should not be resisted. Give me the misconceptions in the order this audience will actually produce them; my guesses are that they will hear the claim that simmballs push simms as a macro entity reaching down into its own parts, that they will treat the causal sufficiency of the simm-level dynamics as settling the matter, that they will assume any higher-level efficacy would require new forces or new energy, and that they will read the simmballs as miniature agents. Tell me exactly where in the sequence each must be headed off, and which two of them would wreck the session if they were left until the end. Finally, give me the check for understanding: a task or question at the close that would show whether they can now distinguish a higher-level property that does causal work from one that is merely a convenient description, and whether they can state what the exclusion argument would have to establish in order to succeed. Tell me also what to refuse to cover at this level — including, if you judge it to belong there, the question whether it is the content of a symbol or only its structure that does the pushing — and how to decline it without leaving the audience convinced that the question was dodged.
