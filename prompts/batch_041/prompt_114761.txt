I run a small meetup for LessWrong-adjacent folks — usually 10 to 14 people, 90 minutes, plain room with tables and a whiteboard, no computers needed. I want to do a session on sphexishness and I want the group to *feel* it rather than hear a lecture about it. You're a philosophy teacher who's also designed a lot of games for seminars, so please take that role and be careful and concrete — think through the mechanics first, maybe sketch 2-3 candidate structures before you commit to one, then give me the final version.

Quick background so you don't have to guess at the idea: there's a classic story about a digger wasp that paralyses a cricket and drags it to her burrow, but she leaves it at the entrance and goes inside to check for intruders before pulling it in. If you nudge the cricket a few inches away while she's inside, she comes out, drags it back to the entrance, and goes in to check again. Do it forty times, she does it forty times. The interesting bit isn't "haha dumb insect" — its that she's running a perfectly sensible procedure that happens to be brittle, and that brittleness is only visible relative to an environment that messes with her in the right way. So one reading is that sphexishness isn't a property of the wasp at all, it's a relation between a finite policy and an environment clever enough to find the loop. And then the worrying part: any finite procedure you write down, including the procedure for noticing that you're looping, is itself a finite procedure, so the environment can go after that one instead. Its turtles.

Here's the shape I'm imagining — please improve it, and tell me if a different structure would work better. People write down a deterministic policy for a simple robot (a token-collecting bug on a small grid), max six rules, no randomness, no judgment calls while executing. Then in pairs, one person plays the environment and tries to force the bug into a no-progress loop. Then a second round where the bug is allowed to rewrite one of its own rules each turn — but the rewrite rule has to be written down too, and it's fair game for the environment. I want the room to hit the moment where every defence they invent just moves the target up a level, and I want that to happen through play, not through me explaining it. Don't let the word sphexish or the wasp show up anywhere until the debrief.

So, the deliverable: roles and rules, the scoring scheme (points on the whiteboard is fine, no real money), materials, a minute-by-minute timeline for the 90 minutes that leaves room for a proper debrief, and what to do if the numbers are odd or if I get 8 people instead of 14. Then the debrief: map the specific moments in the game onto the pieces of the idea, including the bit about rigidity being relative to an environment and the regress of rewrite rules, and say honestly what the activity can't reproduce and what I'll have to supply myself — I'm especially interested in whether the game can touch the free will angle or whether that's just something I have to talk about afterwards.

Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this. I'd rather hear it now: someone will definately try to win by writing "do whatever works" as their rule, someone will argue the whole thing is rigged, the environment player will find a trap that has nothing to do with the policy and everything to do with the grid, and so on. For each failure mode, how do I handle it in the room without turning it into a lecture?
