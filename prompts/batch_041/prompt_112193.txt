So here's my situation. I'm a backend engineer at a mid-size SaaS company, no philosophy or econ background beyond one micro class I barely passed, and I've somehow ended up as the guy who writes the one-pager whenever product or sales wants us to do something because a competitor did it first. Cut QA to ship weekly, match the other guys free tier, add the same aggressive trial popups, bid up Google Ads, loosen the data retention thing — they all arrive with the same sentence, "we have to or we lose." Sometimes that's true. Sometimes it's a thing someone already wanted to do.

What I want is a procedure I can actually run in about 30 minutes before these reviews, grounded in whatever that literature actually says rather than vibes. The questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it terminates with a clear "decline," and where it hands the call to a person because it's a values question (how much margin do we trade, who eats the cost, do we care what this does to the customers who won't churn). My VP signs off, so it has to end in a recommendation she can argue with, not a lecture.

I know there's alot of writing about this — Moloch, multipolar traps, races to the bottom, tragedy of the commons, arms races, positional goods where everyone pays more and nobody's share moves, and the one where if a hundred people can each unilaterally do the risky thing, it happens no matter what any of them thinks is wise (unilateralist's curse, I think?). And I have a vague memory that races don't always go down — sometimes one big strict market drags everyone up instead. So there needs to be a step for telling which direction it's actually going and whether the "race" is even real, or just a story attached to a decision someone already made.

Then run it on three: (1) our ad spend, where CAC has doubled over two years and our market share hasn't budged; (2) a competitor cut prices 30% and sales wants us to match; (3) the engineering salary bidding war. I want at least one where the honest answer is "yeah, it's a real trap, there's no clever fix, you pay or you leave," and I want to be told where the procedure is just rationalizing something leadership already decided. And say what it can't do.
