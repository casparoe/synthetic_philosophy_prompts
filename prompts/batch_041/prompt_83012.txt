In my game theory reading group we keep having the same fight about the common prior assumption and I'm starting to think were not actually disagreeing about anything. Nobody disputes the theorem -- same prior, posteriors common knowledge, posteriors equal, so any real disagreement has to come from different priors or different information. What we can't settle is what that's supposed to show. One camp reads it as a claim about rationality: two people with the same evidence, each knowing the other is rational, can't keep disagreeing, so persistent disagreement between two forecasters means somebody's irrational or hasn't done their homework. The other camp says the CPA is just a modelling convention you impose to get a well-defined Bayesian game, and that nothing about rationality forces two agents to start from the same prior -- a prior is a compressed biography, and biographies differ. And whenever anyone tries to give the thing empirical teeth, someone points out that you can always redraw the state space so the disagreement reappears as a difference in priors, which makes the assumption look untestable. My suspicion, and it's only a suspicion, is that 'rational' is doing two jobs here -- coherent/consistent in one camp, justified-by-your-evidence in the other -- so we're talking past each other. But maybe I'm flattening a real disagreement. Diagnose it for me: is this verbal, a real normative dispute about whether agents with the same evidence must share credences, or a hidden question about whether the framework can represent the phenomena at all? And given the diagnosis, what would count as progress -- a distinction, a theorem, dropping the question -- or is it stalled because it's two questions wearing one hat?
