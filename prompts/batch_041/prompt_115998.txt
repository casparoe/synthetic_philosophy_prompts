I run a small metaphysics reading group with some old grad school friends who mostly work in tech now, and for this term's grounding and fundamentality session I want to hand out something where the tree itself is the content, not a list of isms to memorize. So: a decision tree / flowchart that walks someone from a starting commitment they'd accept with no jargon at all (like: the statue and the clay arent two fully independent things; or: there's got to be some fact of the matter about what's most basic) down to a named position about grounding and fundamentality.

Every branch node should be a question about one assumption, phrased so a person can answer it without already knowing which leaf it leads to. I want at least these nodes somewhere on the tree: does the ground necessitate the grounded, or can a ground make its upshot merely possible or likely; when you say one thing grounds another, what are the things being related, facts, objects, or truths; must grounding chains stop somewhere, and if they stop, do they stop at one thing or many; can grounding be symmetric or loop back on itself; is grounding one relation or a family of them, one per domain; is grounding a real feature of the world or just a way of talking about explanation; is fundamentality absolute or always relative to a question or a domain; and does necessity ever suffice for grounding, or is grounding finer grained than necessity, so that two necessarily coextensive properties still need one of them to ground the other.

Leaves I want on the tree, at least: priority monism (the one whole grounds its parts), priority pluralism / atomism with a base of many simples, an ontic structuralist flavored leaf where structure rather than things is fundamental, wavefunction-only monism, grounding pluralism, deflationism or anti-realism about grounding, bottomless infinitism where there's no fundamental level and that's fine, a mutual-grounding / coherentist leaf where loops are allowed, a supervenience-only leaf where there's no grounding relation at all, just brute modal covariance, and a quietist leaf on which the whole dispute is verbal. Use the standard name where there is one, but dont bother telling me who said what or citing anything, I don't care and neither will they.

Two things I especially want from this. First, flag any pairs of leaves your tree cant seperate, and don't paper over it. Second, for combinations of answers that no actual position occupies, say whether that's because the combination is incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored.

Build the nodes out of concrete cases, and please use these: the statue and the clay, a set and its members, a whole and its parts, a disposition and its categorical base, consciousness and the physical (good stress test for the necessitation branch), laws and the regularities they cover, a hole and its host, and negative facts or absences. Also add a branch for the case where two things are modally inseparable but you still want to say one of them is doing the grounding work, since I want to see which leaves can even state that case without contradicting themselves.

Tenish nodes, tenish leaves, American English.
