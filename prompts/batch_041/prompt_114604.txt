Alright, I've been chewing on this one for months and I want an actual number out of you, not a survey. Teleosemantics — the whole family of views where a state's content is fixed by what it was selected for, or by what the systems that consume it were designed to do with it — is suppossed to naturalize mental content. What's your credence that *some* version of it actually works, i.e. that it pins down a determinate content for a state and isn't just relocating the mystery into the word 'function'? Mine is around 0.3. Now decompose yours:

- the selection-determinacy sub-question: is there ever a unique fact about what a state was selected for, or can you always tell several equally true selection stories about the same state (proximal vs distal, this feature vs that one) with no principled way to pick, so that content comes out indeterminate?
- the misrepresentation sub-question: can the view explain a state's being *wrong* about the world without quietly helping itself to the very content it's meant to build?
- the ontogeny sub-question, which is the one I actually care most about: a lot of the states I care about — a trained detector, a learned concept, anything a creature picked up in its own lifetime — have no phylogenetic selection history at all. If the theory has to fall back on learning or on someone's design to cover those, does it still deliver determinate content, or does the determinacy just evaporate? And is that a fatal scope problem or a fixable one?
- the 'is content even a natural kind' sub-question: whether the whole project is tracking a joint of nature or a folk category we should just drop — and if it's the latter, whether the number you gave above is even well-posed.

Number for each with one line of justification, then how you'd combine them, then tell me which term you're least confident in and what single thing — an experiment, a counterexample, a formal argument — would move the total most. And if you think my 0.3 and your number come apart, say which of the four sub-questions that gap most likely lives in.
