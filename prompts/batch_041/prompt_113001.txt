Analyze the Challenger launch decision as a case study in collective intentionality and joint action. You know the basics (the O-rings, the Thiokol engineers objecting, the managers overruling them, launch on Jan 28 1986, the explosion), so dont rehash the history -- I want the mapping. When we say "NASA decided to launch," who or what is the agent? Run it through Bratman's shared intention and its meshing condition, Gilbert's plural subject, Searle's we-intentions, and Tuomela's I-mode/we-mode contrast, and say for each whether the engineers' dissent kills the shared intention or leaves it standing. Then push it back the other way: is this a case where a genuine group agent intends something none of its individual members intend, or is "NASA decided" just shorthand for a messy aggregate of people under pressure? Tell me where each theory goes silent rather than making it speak, and whether any framework -- team reasoning, List and Pettit's group agency -- handles the part the others cant, and at what cost.
