I convene a fortnightly graduate seminar on teleology and biological function, and for some years now I have opened the session on selected-effects versus causal-role accounts with a joke: it wakes the room up, and it serves as a rough diagnostic, since the students who laugh are generally the ones who have grasped what each account actually makes a trait's function depend on. Last year's effort fell flat, so I am outsourcing this year's. Please write me the joke.

What I want is a short comic piece — a dialogue would suit, and it should be short enough that I can deliver it from memory in a minute or so — whose humour turns on the substance of the disagreement rather than on the fact that a disagreement exists. To be blunt about the standard I am setting: someone who knows only that philosophers of biology quarrel over what functions are should not find it funny at all, whereas someone who can say what a selected-effects account and a causal-role account respectively require, before a trait counts as having a function, should find the punchline both funny and faintly illuminating. So no jokes whose whole point is that philosophers never agree, or that it all depends on what one means by 'function'; and please steer clear of the stock examples the students will have met in their reading — hearts and their thumping, appendices, peacocks' tails.

The case I should build it around, if that is any help, is one I use to prise the two accounts apart in the lecture: a trait doing its causal work perfectly well, in a system, with no selection history behind it whatsoever — the very first instance of some novelty, as it were, before any of the usual Darwinian machinery has had the chance to run. You are welcome to invent the characters and the setting, and to make it as silly as you please; I would sooner have something with a bit of bite in it than something tasteful. I need no references and no discussion afterwards — just the joke, and, if it needs one, a single line telling me what the punchline is turning on.
