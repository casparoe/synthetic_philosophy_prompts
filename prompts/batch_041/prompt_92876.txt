ok so for a philosophy thing at school i've been reading about the handicap principle (the peacock tail thing — the idea that some traits are honest signals exactly because they're a huge pain to have) and it made me realize my gut is a mess about what even counts as a signal. i want you to tell me what principle, if any, is behind my verdicts.

here they are:

- peacock's tail: yeah, real signal, the peahen is learning something true about him.
- gazelle jumping straight up when it sees a lion instead of just running: real signal, he's telling the lion not to bother.
- my cousin's college degree: signals something, but way less than it used to, now that basically everyone has one.
- a company blowing 7 million on a super bowl ad: sure it signals they can afford it, but i don't think it signals anything about them i actually care about.
- saying please and thank you: costs nothing, and i'd still say it signals respect.
- my dad training six months for a marathon: costs a ton of time and pain, but i don't think he's signaling anything to anyone, it seems like it's for himself.
- a classmate posting about the money they gave to the food bank: costs real money, but i trust it way less than the same donation done quietly.
- tipping 30% at a restaurant i'll never go back to: zero future benefit to me and i still think it's partly a signal, to the server and to the people at my table.
- my friend apologizing after saying something mean: the words cost nothing, but it only feels like a signal if it's actually hard for him to say.
