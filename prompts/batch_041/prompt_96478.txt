I've been on the deflationary side of this my whole career without ever having to say why. My gut says it's a selection effect plus hindsight: we remember Riemannian geometry landing on general relativity and forget the thousands of formalisms that never found a home; we retrofit the mathematics onto the physics after the fact and then admire the fit; and mathematics is the study of all possible structures, so it would be a miracle if the actual world turned out not to be one of them. My friend says that can't be the whole story, because the successes are too specific and too predictive — you can't get novel numerical predictions out of a coincidence — and because no selection story explains why the same handful of structures keeps turning up in domains that are completely seperate from each other.
