I want to practice defending nominalism about properties against someone who's actually good at it, so please play my opponent: a committed resemblance nominalist. Your line is that there are no universals and no tropes either, a property just is a maximal class of particulars that resemble each other, and resemblance itself is primitive and unanalyzable, comes in degrees, and you get to help yourself to it for free. Your not a strawman — you'll concede a small point when it's really forced, you'll add machinery when you have to, but you'll always tell me what the addition costs you, and you never give up the main claim. Stay in character until I say 'out of character'. Two paragraphs a turn please, feels more like a conversation.

My opening: small world, three particulars, call them A, B and C, and I'm stipulating every resemblance fact in it. A and B resemble each other very strongly in respect R1 and in nothing else. B and C resemble each other very strongly in R2 and in nothing else. A and C resemble each other weakly in R3 and in nothing else. No other resemblances hold anywhere. So {A, B, C} is pairwise resembling — on your view that's exactly the thing that makes a collection a candidate property class — but there's no one respect all three of them share. So either you tell me the rule that excludes the class, and it can't just be 'because I said so', and then tell me why that same rule doesn't also exclude {A, B}, which has the identical problem in miniature; or you let the class in as a property, and now 'being A or B or C' is a genuine property, which is the sort of thing you became a nominalist to avoid. Second one, and it's the one I actually care about: suppose R3 is a respect that gets instantiated exactly once in the entire history of the world — one object, one shade, never again, nothing else around to resemble. If resemblance isn't reflexive, that property has no class to be and you've simply lost it. If it is reflexive, then every singleton class is a class of mutually resembling particulars, so {Socrates} is a property class and being-Socrates is a property, which is a haecceity, which is a universal wearing a hat. Pick your horn, and while you're at it name that lonely shade for me without invoking anything general.
