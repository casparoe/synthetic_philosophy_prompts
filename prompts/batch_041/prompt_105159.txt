A few of us have a long-running group chat about animal ethics that has basically collapsed into a fight about insects, and I want to end it by building an actual decision tree instead of everyone re-stating their vibe every three days. The setup we all accept: there's some non-trivial chance that insects are sentient (nobody thinks it's zero, nobody thinks it's one), and there are astronomically many of them, most living short lives in the wild or in industrial rearing. The move that keeps getting made is the scale one: even a tiny per-insect probability of sentience, multiplied by that many individuals, is supposed to swamp every other animal-welfare concern we have. What I want is a flowchart that starts from that shared commitment and ends at the positions people actually land on, so we can see which of our disagreements are about the probability and which are about the arithmetic. Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of names: first the questions that carve up the space, then the answer combinations they permit, then the positions that occupy each combination, with the empty cells noted and diagnosed (is nobody there because the view is incoherent, because it's repulsive, or just because nobody's bothered?). The nodes I think have to be on it, though cut or add if the logic demands it: whether sentience is binary or graded, and whether moral weight scales smoothly with credence or needs a threshold to be crossed before it counts at all; whether you accept that a big enough number can generate a real obligation out of a small probability, or whether that's exactly the reasoning you refuse on principle; whether the per-insect probability is supposed to come from evidence about nervous systems and behaviour or from a policy default for deciding under uncertainty; whether the morally relevant unit is the individual insect, a whole taxon, or some capacity only some insects have; and whether the scale argument is meant to bind us everywhere or only where we have actual leverage, since the numbers are largest precisely where our ability to do anything is smallest, which is the part I find most confusing.

One friend keeps defending something she can't name, and I'd like the tree to adjudicate it for us. Her line is that you should treat the chance of insect sentience like a bet: give insects weight in proportion to the probability, so a five percent chance means their interests count a twentieth as much as a cow's, and she claims this dodges the whole fanaticism worry. I suspect it secretly collapses into one of the standard leaves, so make the tree tell me whether it does, and if it doesn't, give it its own leaf and a sentence on its most awkward commitment. Keep it to about seven questions and no more than eight leaves, name each leaf where a name exists and mark it unnamed where none does, and flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely can't separate rather than pretending it can. This is for our group chat, so it should be readable by smart people who aren't philosophers, but I'd rather it be precise than breezy.

And please write the whole thing out as continuous prose, no tables, no bullet points, no bold, no headings, just paragraphs the way you'd explain it in an email, referring to the questions as the first question, the second question, and so on.
