Roko's basilisk is the argument that you should help bring about a future superintelligence, since that AI will punish those who learned of the argument and declined — the threat binding you through a correlation between your choice and its own rather than through any causal channel. Predict Leibniz's verdict on it, deriving the prediction from his own system rather than from generic theism. Three doctrines seem to matter: first, does his pre-established harmony — no created substance acts on another, yet each expresses the whole universe — make him more or less hospitable than a contemporary physicalist to a threat that works by correlation rather than causation? Second, would his theodicy, with its insistence that a good God punishes only guilt actually incurred, permit a benevolent superintelligence to punish non-contributors; and does his denial of a freedom of indifference, the will always following the greatest apparent good, leave room to blame someone who simply found the argument unpersuasive? Third, given his principle of sufficient reason and his conviction that the actual world is the best of all possible ones, does the basilisk's threat have any work left to do? Give his verdict, and mark the points at which you are reconstructing rather than reporting.
