I'm giving a talk in three weeks at a consciousness workshop -- small room, mostly phil of mind plus one neuroscientist -- and I need a Q&A prep sheet, not a summary of my own paper. My line is that the meta-problem of consciousness is real but the hard problem inherits an artifact from it: the intuition that experience can't be captured by any representation is the output of a two-stage labeling quirk, where a fast system tags certain states as "not modelable" and a slower system then reifies that tag into a property of the state. Explain the meta-problem that way and the explanatory gap starts looking like a glitch in self-modeling rather than a window onto something hidden.

The audiance: mostly empirically-minded people who work on predictive processing and higher-order theories, two illusionists who'll be delighted, two or three committed phenomenal realists (one of them works on the knowledge argument), and a metacognition neuroscientist who's going to want a signature.

So: the eight or so objections I'll actualy get, in the vocab of the people who'll make them, ordered by how much damage they do, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. What do I concede up front, and what's the weakest point in my own position that I should prepare to defend instead of hoping nobody notices? And be honest about which single objection would make me give the view up -- what would have to be true for me to be wrong here. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
