Construct a St. Petersburg-style lottery that separates three agents: an expected-value maximizer, who accepts any finite entry fee; a bounded-utility expected-utility maximizer, who refuses above a calculable finite fee F1; and an agent who evaluates the lottery by replacing each objective probability p with p squared before multiplying by payoff, and who refuses above a different finite fee F2. The lottery must have infinitely many outcomes, all payoffs finite, probabilities generated by a fair coin, and the setting must be ordinary enough that a person could face it, with no infinite bankrolls or exotic physics; the scenario itself should be no longer than a paragraph. After the scenario, show that the objective expected monetary value is infinite, derive or bound F1 and F2, identify which feature of the payoff and probability structure makes each threshold finite, say what happens if the probabilities are instead geometric, and explain how an expected-value maximizer would argue that the other two agents are making a mistake.
