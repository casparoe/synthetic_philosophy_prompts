So my ethics reading group is doing individual responsibility for collective harms next month and I've been volenteered to write the discussion handout, so what I want from you is a dialogue: two people, both good at this, actually arguing with each other about whether it's wrong for me as an individual to fly, eat meat, or vote when my doing so changes nothing about what happens to anyone.

Keep them on three cases at once, because I want to see whether this is really one problem or three. One: a flight I book onto a plane that was going to fly anyway. Two: a steak I buy off a shelf, where the animal was killed weeks ago and the store's order went in months ago. Three: a vote in a district that's going to be won by twenty points no matter what. In none of them does anything change for any person or animal because of me.

One of them should run the line that this is the whole ballgame: no difference, no wrong, and the whole personal-responsibility discourse is a con that lets states and firms off the hook — that's a real position and it should get its best shot, not a strawman. The other should think that's too quick, and that the real question is what relation I'm actually standing in to the harm, if it isn't causation.

And this is the bit I most want them to dig into, because I genuinely can't tell whether it's a fix or a relabel. My hunch is that I don't cause the harm so much as I'm part of what constitutes it — the harm is made out of acts like mine, the way a jam is made out of cars and no one car causes the jam. If that's right then "my act made no difference" is answering an objection nobody had to make. The other one should push on it hard: is constituting a real relation or just a fancy way of saying I was nearby; does it need a threshold, a shared intention, a rule, a practice; does it apply to the one guy eating a burger in a world where nobody else does; and what does it ask of me if anything — to be a certain kind of person, to join a collective project, or nothing?

Throw in the threshold version too at some point, where my act is the one that tips something over — I buy the last one and the store keeps stocking it — does that make it wrong, or does it just make wrongness a lottery?

Have them use rough numbers once in a while so I can see whether the math is doing any work or just decoration, and have at least one of them say that voting isn't the same animal as the other two — then let the other either give ground or explain why it doesn't matter.

End with whatever they can honestly agree on, even if it's just that they've been answering two different questions. Please no quotes, no names, no citations or reading lists — I can't check any of it and I don't want invented references.
