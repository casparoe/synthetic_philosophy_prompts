How probable is it that the disagreement between the interventionist and the counterfactual theories of causation — Woodward's interventionism and Lewis's counterfactual analysis, roughly — is merely verbal, in the sense that no possible case assigns different truth values to a singular causal claim, and the apparent dispute reduces to a difference in presentation, in the questions each theory makes salient, or in the epistemology each recommends? I put it near 0.3. The number matters less to me than the decomposition, so I want separate estimates, each with a line of justification, for the sub-questions on which I take the answer to depend. First, whether an intervention can be defined in counterfactual terms without circularity: if it can, the interventionist has no primitive that the counterfactual theorist lacks, and the two collapse into one theory; if it cannot, they are not notational variants. Second, whether any of the standard trouble cases genuinely discriminates between them — preemption, where a redundant backup cause never gets to act; double prevention, where one event causes another by preventing a preventer; causation by omission — or whether each theory has by now been extended to cover all of them, so that the extensions coincide. Third, whether the residual difference is confined to indeterministic cases, where the interventionist apparatus accommodates probabilistic causation natively while the counterfactual theory has to work through probabilistic counterfactuals. Fourth, whether the two are answering the same question at all, given that one is typically offered as a semantics for causal claims and the other as an account of the causal relation, and given that one may be non-reductive while the other aims at reduction. Fifth, whether the ordinary-language term cause has a determinate extension at all; if it does not, the dispute could be verbal for that reason rather than because the two theories agree on every case.

Then give me the combination, and tell me which of those terms my 0.3 is most sensitive to, and what single result — an argument, a counterexample, a proof of definability — would move it most, and in which direction.
