I've been chewing on the reflection principle for a while now (I think its van Fraassen's? the one where if you know you're going to be 80% sure of something tomorrow, you should already be 80% sure of it today). I want you to argue for a position on it, and the position is this: as it's usually stated, the principle should be thrown out, or at least demoted to a rough rule of thumb, and whatever the real norm is, it has to be about how the later belief gets made rather than about the fact that it's later.

Why I got stuck on it. Next month I'm going on one of those week long retreat things. I know more or less what's going to happen to me: I'll come back about 80% convinced of their main teaching. Right now, sitting here, I'd put it at 10%, maybe less. My future self will say "trust me, I've seen it now." And I know perfectly well it won't be because he's got better evidence — it'll be because he spent seven days being patiently worn down by people who are very good at that. So the principle tells me to bump myself up to 80% today, which is obviously wrong. The usual dodge is "well then you don't trust your future self, so the principle doesn't apply," but I will trust him, that's the whole problem, and nobody ever says what "trust" is supposed to mean here.

The bit I actually want you to push on: the principle only ever looks at the number my future self lands on. It has no way of telling the difference between a later me who has better evidence and a later me whose standards got swapped out for different ones. Those are completely different situations to me and identical to the principle. So when people say reflection is what rules out wishful thinking and self deception, I don't see how it can, since it can't see the difference. And if I have to defer to the later me just because he's later, then he has to defer to the even later me, and it never bottoms out — plus why does the earlier me get no say, when he's the one who knows why he signed up in the first place?

So argue it: reflection as usually stated is broken, say what's really doing the work, and say what the principle should be instead. Give me exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no wrap up at the end.
