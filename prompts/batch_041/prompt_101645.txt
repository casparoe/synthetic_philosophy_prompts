For a graduate seminar on decision theory I've been trying to pin down exactly what separates counterfactual mugging from XOR blackmail, and I've ended up constructing a hybrid case that I can't get a clean verdict on. In the standard counterfactual mugging, Omega flips a fair coin, asks you for $100 if it lands heads, and if it lands tails pays you $10,000 precisely when you would have paid in the heads branch; the familiar moral is that you should be the kind of agent who pays, even though the payment is locally pointless. In the standard XOR blackmail, Omega announces that it has sent you a letter if and only if your house is infested with termites XOR you pay it $1000; there the evidential reasoner is supposed to pay — reasoning that a payment would indicate the letter was triggered by the payment rather than by termites — and this is usually presented as a mistake, since the disposition to refuse does better overall. What I want from you is a verdict on my hybrid, not a survey of the literature.

Here is the case. A fair coin, call it A, is flipped out of your sight; if A lands heads, a disaster befalls you that costs $1,000,000. Omega, who is honest and a perfect predictor of your dispositions, will send you a letter according to this rule: it sends the letter if and only if (A landed heads) XOR (you would pay it $100 in response to a letter). If the letter does arrive, you may hand over the $100 or refuse, and nothing further turns on your choice. If no letter arrives, Omega transfers $10,000 to you if and only if you would have paid had a letter arrived. Nothing else happens, and all of this is known to you in advance.

Please argue for a definite verdict on whether a rational agent should be disposed to pay the $100 here, and defend it rather than hedging. I want you to be explicit about how the main candidate theories come out — causal decision theory, evidential decision theory, and the dispositional or updateless style of reasoning that the mugging literature tends to invoke — to say where they diverge and which one you think gets the right answer and why, and to make the arithmetic behind your verdict transparent. Finally, tell me what the case shows about the supposed difference between counterfactual mugging and XOR blackmail: is the crucial difference the XOR structure itself, or something else, such as whether the relevant disposition is better or worse overall, and does my hybrid show that the two problems were really one problem all along, or that they genuinely come apart?
