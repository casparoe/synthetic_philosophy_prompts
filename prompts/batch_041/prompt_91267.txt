I am presenting next month to a graduate seminar a thesis I have been developing: that the familiar triad of honor, dignity, and victimhood cultures is best reconstructed as three competing answers to a single question -- who bears the burden of justification when an injury is contested -- and that on that axis victimhood culture is the most just, because it shifts that burden onto whichever party holds the greater power to do harm. The audience will be mostly virtue ethicists who are sympathetic to honor as a school of excellence, two moral psychologists who study blame and outrage, and one legal scholar convinced that dignity is the only defensible ground of rights. Prepare my question-period sheet as a numbered list of exactly five objections I should expect, ordered by how much damage each would do, each phrased in the vocabulary of whoever would raise it, each followed by a two-sentence reply and a note on where that reply is weakest; mark the one I am hoping nobody asks, and state what I should concede up front. Write in American English, with no introduction or conclusion.
