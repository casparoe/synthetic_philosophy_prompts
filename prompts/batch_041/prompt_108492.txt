For a departmental workshop next month I have been asked to set out my position on when and why numbers matter in the saving of lives, and I would like the set checked for coherence before I commit it to paper. These are my commitments.

(1) The number of people who stand to be saved is not, as such, a reason of greater weight. What gives me reason to save someone is what that person stands to lose, and equal losses generate claims of equal strength, whether one such claim is in play or five.

(2) Where I can save one person or save five, with what each stands to lose otherwise alike, I take it to be permissible to save the five, though not required.

(3) In the simpler case where I can save exactly one of two people whose stakes are alike, I take it to be impermissible to choose on a whim; I must give each an equal chance.

(4) I may save my own child in preference to five strangers. Partiality of this kind is permissible, and I do not think it has to be earned by the child's having a stronger claim.

(5) I accept a tie-breaking role for numbers: where claims are equal in strength and cannot all be satisfied, the larger number gives a reason to resolve the tie in its favour, but that reason is not a reason of the same kind as a claim.

(6) In allocating a fixed health budget across a population, I think the allocation that saves the most lives is not merely permissible but required.

(7) I take the strength of a claim to vary with the size of the loss, so where I can save one person from death or save five people from each losing a leg, I would save the one.

(8) I think the outcome in which five die is worse than the outcome in which one dies, though I deny that this is what makes saving the five permissible.

Which of these stand in genuine tension and which only appear to? Order the tensions by severity, say what each pair commits me to, and for each give the cheapest repair and what that repair costs. Tell me as well which single revision would clear up the most, and whether any of the items is not so much in conflict with the others as insufficiently specified. Do not assume that I know which of these I would give up.
