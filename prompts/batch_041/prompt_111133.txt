I am a novelist writing a speculative dialogue in which Einstein's preserved brain is revived and interviewed. The scene turns on a question that never arose in his lifetime: could a book, a large language model, or a whole-brain emulation be conscious? Please act as a historian of early twentieth-century physics and a philosopher of mind, and infer the position Einstein would most likely have taken. Ground the inference in his actual commitments—his Spinozist monism, his early Machian leanings and later scientific realism, and his determinism—rather than in generic praise of his genius. Have him respond to three posthumous proposals: first, that consciousness is whatever a system's information integration measures; second, that it is a biological feature of certain carbon-based organisms; third, that it is a user illusion generated by a self-model. For each, say whether he would accept, reject, or recast it, and why. Think carefully and brainstorm the relevant commitments before you write; do not invent quotations or citations. Keep the whole response under 600 words and in American English. End with a paragraph on what would change your mind: which argument or piece of evidence about Einstein's actual views would lead you to a different verdict, and where your inference is most likely to fail.
