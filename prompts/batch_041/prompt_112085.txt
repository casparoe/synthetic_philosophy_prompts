ok so I'm trying to write something about whether video game characters can be wronged and I keep getting stuck, so instead of just talking about it I want you to build me a thought experiment to order.

Here's the target. I want one case where a hedonic/sentience account of moral status -- the only thing that grounds status is whether the thing can feel anything -- says that what the player does to a game character is seriously morally wrong, while a practice/expressivist account -- what makes an act wrong is what it expresses, or what it violates within some moral community's norms -- says the same act is basically fine, or at most in bad taste. Thats the direction people don't usually build. The easy one is "character isn't sentient, so the sentience view shrugs, and the expressivist view is the one that objects." I want it flipped.

Constraints, and please actually respect them:
- one paragraph for the scenario itself, ordinary contemporary setting, a normal commercial game. No brain uploading, no consciousness-transfer machines, no "the NPC is secretly a person from the future" hand-waving.
- the character's sentience has to be something the case gives us real reason to accept, not something you just declare. If it's stipulated, the case is cheating.
- don't make the practice account look like an idiot. It shouldn't end up licensing cruelty to anything that feels; it should be a view a serious person could hold, and the reason it shrugs here has to be structural, not callousness.
- don't change two things at once. If you want to show what's doing the work, hold everything fixed except one variable.

After the scenario, do the check: say what the sentience account says and why, what the practice account says and why, name the one feature that makes them come apart, then remove that feature and show the case collapsing back into agreement (or back into the usual direction). Then put on the hat of someone defending the practice account and give me their best move against my case -- is there a reading where the act still isn't wrong for them, or do they have to bite a bullet?

Also give me one line at the top on what you think the case is actually good for, because if it's just a cute puzzle that doesn't decide anything about real games I'd rather know now.

I'm mostly using this to figure out whether the sentience view and the expressivist view can be pried apart at all, or whether every case that seperates them also seperates them for teh wrong reasons.
