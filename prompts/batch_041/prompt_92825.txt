The contrast people draw in her work between an older kind of political lie and a modern one usually gets read as a contrast in content: the old lie was about some specific fact, the modern one fabricates a whole picture of the world. I think the real hinge is the audience, and what the lie does to the liar's grip on the world, not how big the lie is. The old lie is told to an opponent and it presupposes a shared world of facts both sides can point at; the modern one is told to a population meant to live inside it, and its point isn't to get anyone to believe a false proposition so much as to make the shared factual world unusable.
