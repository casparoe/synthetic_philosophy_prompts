I'm in a jurisprudence seminar that keeps circling back to self-referring rules, and I've got a list of cases where my gut is fairly firm but I can't tell what principle is doing the work. Could you tell me what principle (or small set of them) actually fits, and which of my verdicts I'd have to give up to get a clean one?

(1) A club's bylaws can be amended by a two-thirds vote. A two-thirds vote raises the bar to three-quarters. Later, a two-thirds vote tries to put it back to two-thirds. I say that last vote fails.
(2) Same club: a two-thirds vote passes 'the board shall never have fewer than nine seats.' Later, a three-quarters vote cuts the board to five. I say that fails too, though I'm wobblier here.
(3) A federation's founding charter said it could only be replaced by unanimous consent of all the member states. The current constitution was ratified by three-quarters of them, ignoring that. I say the current constitution is perfectly valid and the old defect is a historical curiosity.
(4) Parliament passes a statute saying 'this Act may not be repealed.' The next Parliament repeals it. I say the repeal works.
(5) A rule says 'any rule, including this one, can be changed by a simple majority.' A simple majority changes it to unanimity-only. Then a simple majority tries to change it back. I say the first change works and the second fails.
(6) A constitution is amended, following its own amendment procedure exactly, to abolish that procedure. My gut says that's invalid -- you can't use the procedure to kill the procedure. But I can't see why (5) and (6) come out differently, since in both the entrenching move was legal when it was made.
(7) A statute whose entire text is 'this Act is void.' I keep going back and forth on whether its a valid enactment with no effects or just not law at all.

The only principle I can half-state is: an act binds later actors only if the rule authorizing it survives the act. But that feels circular. What's the best version of a principle here, what does it force me to drop, and what would it say about a rule that can only be amended by a unanimous vote of everyone who has ever been a member?
