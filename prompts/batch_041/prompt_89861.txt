You are a philosopher working on Kant's practical philosophy and on the moral status of animals. I am a doctoral student preparing a position paper for a seminar on Kant and animals, and before I draft it I want an audit of my own commitments. Please think carefully about this rather than giving me a survey; if some of the commitments are ambiguous, say briefly how you are reading them before you start, and flag where a different reading would change your verdict.

Here is where I have landed.

(1) The ground of moral status is autonomy: only beings capable of giving themselves the moral law are ends in themselves, and the formula of humanity is what protects them.
(2) So there are no direct duties to animals. What look like such duties are duties regarding animals, owed to ourselves and to humanity, and their content is fixed by how our treatment of animals tends to coarsen or refine our disposition toward other human beings.
(3) Kant's practical conclusions here seem largely right to me: killing animals for food and using them in medical research are permissible, provided the killing is not wanton and the research answers to a real human need.
(4) But I think the marginal-cases argument cuts against me. I would not say it is permissible to torture a human being who lacks the relevant rational capacities, and if what forbids that is not autonomy, I do not see what stops the same consideration from covering animals.
(5) I am also struck by Kant's treatment of organisms in the third Critique as natural purposes, in which the parts are reciprocally means and ends to one another. That looks like a kind of intrinsic end-directedness, and I cannot tell whether it is morally inert.
(6) Methodologically I want to stay with Kant's own grounds rather than adopt a reconstruction that quietly changes the subject. I would rather find that his position is untenable than defend a view that is Kantian in name only.

Which of these are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be? Rank the tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give and what giving it up would cost, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I would rather have three tensions ranked than six listed as equally bad, so be specific about where the pressure actually comes from and be willing to tell me that some of these are pulling in the same direction.
