I am a doctoral candidate in environmental history, preparing a handout for a graduate seminar that doubles as scaffolding for a chapter of my dissertation. The chapter argues that the long quarrel over germs, climate, and volcanoes as historical actors is really three quarrels run together — over causal role, over explanatory target, and over the idiom in which non-human causation may legitimately be described — and that the political charge carried by environmental determinism trades mostly on slides between them. I want the handout to exhibit that structure rather than survey the literature, so please build me a classification instrument.

The deliverable is a decision tree of diagnostic questions leading from an initial commitment to labelled positions on the causal and explanatory status of non-human factors in history. The tree should begin at something almost anyone in the room will grant — that some non-human factor has made a difference to some historical outcome — and sort from there. Every node must be answerable by a reader who knows the cases but not the labels, so no node may ask whether the reader accepts environmental determinism or anything else that names a position in advance.

Six further requirements:

1. Sort the factor before sorting the position. Pathogens, climate, and eruptions are not one kind of thing: one has fitness interests and coevolves with its hosts, one is an aggregate with no agent-like unity, one is a punctuated event. Give the tree at least one node that turns on this difference, and say how it alters which positions stay open — in particular whether agential vocabulary can be extended to climate and eruptions, or only to pathogens.

2. Before the tree, give me the logical space as a grid, crossing three axes I intend to treat as load-bearing: (a) causal role — force, on which the factor pushes outcomes; frame, on which it fixes the space of the possible; filter, on which it selects among variations humans have already generated; (b) explanatory target — a single event, a divergence between two societies, or a general pattern; (c) idiom — mechanical-causal, agential, or structural. Mark the occupied cells and characterise each occupant without attributing it to anyone, then diagnose every empty cell as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

3. The leaves must include at least: strong environmental determinism; a conditional determinism on which the non-human factor has causal primacy only under specifiable conditions; possibilism; a slow-frame or longue-durée position on which the non-human supplies the temporal setting rather than the cause; disease-as-protagonist; event-causation by eruption or climate shock; institutions-first anti-determinism; distributed or more-than-human agency; and a deflationary view on which actor-talk is a useful fiction carrying no metaphysical commitment. Where a standard label exists, use it; where none does, coin a descriptive one. Do not attribute any position to an author or a work, and do not supply citations or reading lists — I will do the sourcing myself.

4. Give each leaf one sentence stating its most awkward commitment: the thing its defender has to swallow.

5. Tell me which pairs of leaves the instrument cannot separate, and why. I expect at least possibilism versus conditional determinism, and deflationary actor-talk versus genuine distributed agency.

6. Flag any position the tree forces into a combination its own defenders would reject — where the instrument reveals the view as unstable rather than as located. And for each leaf, one line on whether it can accept the causal claim while refusing the naturalising inference that converts a causal finding into a justification of existing hierarchies, since that slide is what the chapter is about.

Use as diagnostic material at least the following: the 1815 Tambora eruption and the cold and harvest failures in Europe and North America the following year; the 1783–84 Laki eruption and its European aftermath; the demographic catastrophe in the Americas after 1492, where infectious disease is often assigned the leading role; and the Black Death, where the same question arises about the collapse of serfdom. Six to nine branching nodes, ten to fourteen leaves.
