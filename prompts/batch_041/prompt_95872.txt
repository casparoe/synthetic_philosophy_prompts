I teach the metaphysics seminar and the Thomson's lamp week goes the same way every year: the logicians say the puzzle is just underdescribed, the physicists say of course nothing can flip infinitely often, everyone goes home happy, nothing is settled. I've been having this exact arguement with a colleague in the physics dept for two years now and we've finally agreed to write it up properly, so I'd like you to draft the document for us.

The setup, so we're all on the same page: a lamp is off at t=0. It gets flipped at t=1/2, 3/4, 7/8, ..., at every time 1 − 2^−n, and then at t=1 its over. On or off?

Camp A, the no-fact-of-the-matter line: the description fixes the state at every time before 1 and is simply silent about 1 itself, so on-at-1 and off-at-1 are each perfectly consistent with everything that's been specified. No contradiction, just a gap, and the gap only closes if you add physics the puzzle leaves out — a filament, a capacitor, a spring.

Camp B, the impossibility line: no lamp could satisfy that description at all. The gap isn't a gap, it's a specification with no model, and not because we lack imagination.

Draft our joint statement. What can we both sign — about the setup, about what the description does and doesn't fix, about which moves are cheap? Then where we actually part, with each side's position worded so that side would sign its own wording. Then the cruxes, and for each crux a case we both agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. I suspect part of this is verbal and I want you to say which part.

Two cases I want in there specifically, because I think they break the symmetry: (1) put a mechanical counter beside the lamp that advances one notch per flip — at t=1 it has to read some number, no natural number works, and ω isn't a reading a counter can show, so here the spec looks unsatisfiable rather than merely incomplete; does that carry back over to the lamp, or does the lamp's two-state-ness rescue Camp A? (2) infinitely many killers, scheduled at t=1/2, 1/4, 1/8, ..., each killing Fred at his scheduled moment if Fred is alive then, with Fred alive at 0 — no assignment of alive-or-dead to Fred is consistent, so Camp A has to say there's no fact of the matter about whether Fred is alive afterwards, which sounds mad, while Camp B gets a case where their line looks obviously right.

Format: numbered agreed claims, then a table for the disagreements and cruxes. And please don't end by hedging into it depends what you mean by a state.
