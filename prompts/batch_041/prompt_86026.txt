My roommate and I have been argueing for about two months now about Bertrand's paradox and I think we've reached the point where we need to write something down instead of just going in circles. She's in what I'd call the well-posed camp: she thinks "pick a random chord" has a real answer once you apply the right invariance or maximum-ignorance principle, and that the answer is 1/2 — the one you get by picking a random distance from the center — because the space of lines in the plane carries a unique (up to scale) measure invariant under all the rigid motions, whereas the space of chords carries a whole family of rotation-invariant measures, one for every choice of density in the distance-from-center coordinate, so only the ambient symmetry of the plane can break the tie. I'm in the underspecified camp: I think the thing only has an answer relative to a physical procedure (two random rim points, midpoint uniform in the disk, random distance and random angle, whatever), that the invariance move just relabels a choice as a discovery, and that demanding the symmetry group of the whole plane when the problem hands you one specific circle is unmotivated — the circle's own rotations leave the radial density completely free, so they can't be what's picking out 1/2. We're not going to convert each other and I'm tired of the arguement.

Here's what I want: the joint statement the two of us could actually both put our names to. Then the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling things would actually move one of us; I suspect the load-bearing one is which symmetry group is relevant, the setup's or the ambient space's, and the second is whether indifference is a norm that constrains rational credence or just a heuristic for building priors. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table, and if some of this turns out to be purely verbal, say which part and why.
