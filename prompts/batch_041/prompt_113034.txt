I write about science and ideas, and I am trying to understand an argument that circulates among people who think about artificial intelligence and decision theory: that the Solomonoff prior is malign for consequentialists inside it. The rough mechanics I have are these. A universal prior assigns probability to hypotheses according to the length of the shortest program that generates them, so simpler programs get more weight. The claim is that when an agent takes its observations to be generated by some program and takes itself to be a subroutine within that program, the prior does not behave like a neutral prior over ordinary physical worlds. Instead, it assigns most of its credence to worlds that are cheap to generate but contain enormous numbers of observers, copies, or simulations of the agent. If that is right, a consequentialist agent inside the prior should expect its actions to be repeated across many copies, and its decisions may be dominated by what happens to those copies rather than by what happens in the single world it thought it inhabited. I want you to explain the conceptual issue here, not the formalism. Is the argument sound? Does the prior really force the agent to assign most of its credence to such worlds, or is that an artifact of how the agent is individuating itself? Is it rational for the agent to try to make itself cheaper to simulate, to increase the number of its copies, or to influence which program is running? Suppose the agent can choose between two actions: one makes the program generating it shorter, the other increases the number of copies of the agent within that program. What does the prior imply about which action it should take, and does that implication survive once the agent realizes that its choice is itself part of the program? Take a definite position on whether this is a genuine problem for consequentialism, a confusion about indexical uncertainty, or a benign feature of universal induction that only looks alarming. Give me the mechanism in plain language, with a concrete example, and say where you think the argument overreaches. Please do not give me a reading list or citations; I want your own reconstruction and judgment.
