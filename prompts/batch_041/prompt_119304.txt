Draft the joint statement that a committed epistemicist and a committed supervaluationist about vagueness could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration — the document they should have produced instead of talking past each other for decades. Include: (1) the claims both sides can sign, covering the sorites, bivalence versus excluded middle, what "borderline" and "definitely" are doing in each framework, and what the dispute is actually about; (2) the residual disagreements, each stated in wording that its own side would accept; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, a case or argument both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Work with two test cases and say what each side predicts about each: (a) a community whose use of "rich" is such that no member has settled, or is disposed to settle, where the line falls, and no member would recognize anything as settling it; (b) an admissions gate that once applied a precise height threshold, fixed by coin flip, before the stored threshold was erased — everyone agrees case (b) contains a sharp fact that is now unknowable. The question is whether vagueness is relevantly like (b) or like (a). Be explicit about whether the second-order boundary — the line between the definitely-tall and the borderline — is sharp on each view. Format the agreed claims as a numbered list and the disagreements as a table. If part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, say which part and why.
