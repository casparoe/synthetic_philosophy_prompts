Ok so I've been lurking on LessWrong-adjacent blogs and rationalist forums for like a decade, and I keep circling back to Lewis's triviality results for indicative conditionals. I want to actually figure out where the live questions are instead of rereading the same three blog posts every couple of years.

Quick setup so we're on the same page: Lewis showed you cant in general have a probability function where the probability of "if A then B" equals the conditional probability of B given A, on pain of the conditional collapsing into something trivial — roughly, P(B|A) ends up having to equal plain old P(B), so the conditional tells you nothing. So Stalnaker's thesis, P(A→B) = P(B|A), looks dead in full generality, and people have scattered in a bunch of directions: deny indicative conditionals have truth conditions at all and give them assertability via the Ramsey test, keep truth conditions but deny the probabilities obey the usual laws, restrict the thesis to special cases, argue the proof smuggles in assumptions about how the probability space is built, etc. Fine, I know the lay of the land well enough.

Here's what I actually want. I'm giving myself about a year of evenings on this and I want a prioritized agenda: which open questions here are upstream of the others, which are tractable for a smart amateur with decent math but no formal semantics training, and which look juicy but wouldnt change much downstream even if answered.

The angle I'm most curious about, and I havent seen it discussed much, is whether these triviality constraints bite on the kind of conditioning that shows up in decision theory and in attempts to build self-referential reasoners — an agent that assigns probabilities to its own conditionals, or conditions on its own future action ("if I one-box, then..."). A lot of the evidential-vs-causal debate and the whole updatelessness thing seems to quietly assume you can have well-behaved conditional credences about your own choices. If the triviality results say the general project of equating probabilities of conditionals with conditional probabilities is doomed, does that infect any of that? Or is it a different animal, because those conditionals are about your own algorithm rather than about the world?

Second thing I'm curious about: is there anything to the idea that triviality is really a result about the algebra you're allowed to conditionalize on — i.e. the problem is trying to make conditionals be objects inside the same probability space as their own constituents — and if so, does that point to a specific engineering fix for Bayesian agents rather than a philosophical one?

So give me the ranking. For each question, tell me why it's upstream of the others, how tractable it is for someone like me, what over the next year would move it up or down the list, and what a wrong answer actually costs — which downstream stuff (decision theory, semantics, the AI-agent stuff) breaks if we get it wrong. I'd rather have three or four questions with real reasoning behind them than a list of ten.
