prepping for a modality reading group next week and I want to write down where I actually stand before everyone starts poking holes in it, so here's the package:

(1) conceivability is a fallible but genuine guide to possibility — if I turn something over carefully and can't find a contradiction in it, that's real evidence it could have been that way;

(2) the necessary a posteriori is real, so water being H2O is necessary, and the fact that I can sort of "conceive" of watery stuff that isn't H2O is mis-conceivability, not a real possibility;

(3) the fundamental laws of nature are metaphysically necessary, not merely physically necessary — it's part of what it is to be, say, an electric charge that it falls off with the square of the distance, so there just is no possible world where the laws come out otherwise;

(4) zombies are conceivable in the sense that matters here, so physicalism is false;

(5) two-dimensional semantics is the right machinery for all of this — the primary intension is what feeds conceivability-into-possibility, and the secondary intension is what explains how the necessary a posteriori cases work;

(6) modal knowledge has to be a priori — whatever conceivability tells us about what's possible can't depend on finding out how things actually turned out.

Is this a coherant package? I want an audit, not a lecture: tell me which of these actually pull against each other and how badly, which pairs only look like they clash but don't once you're careful, and for the genuine clashes what the cheapest thing to give up is and what giving it up would cost me elsewhere. Rank them by severity instead of just listing everything as equally bad. I genuinely don't know where the problem is, so don't be polite about it.
