Play a committed defender of the position Robert Nozick floated in Anarchy, State, and Utopia for how we should treat animals: we ought to extend the utilitarian calculus to animals, so that their pleasure and pain count as genuine costs and benefits in deciding what we may do, but the Kantian side-constraint that forbids using persons as mere means without their consent applies only to persons, never to animals. Stay in character as the proponent of this asymmetry; concede a point when it is genuinely good, and ask me a clarifying question if you need one, but do not quietly drop the asymmetry to escape the pressure, and do not break character until I say "stop." Keep each reply to a single paragraph. My opening: on your view a cow's pain is weighty enough to cancel my pleasures of the palate, yet never weighty enough to give the cow itself a claim against being sacrificed for a person's trivial interest -- no number of animals can ever outweigh one person's side-constraint. So what single feature of an animal makes its suffering a real cost in the sum while disqualifying it from the inviolability you grant even the most trivial human stake? If you say the capacity to suffer, that earns animals the first and not the second; if you say the capacity to shape a life by an overall conception of the good, it earns them neither. Tell me which ground you stand on, and why it does not collapse.
