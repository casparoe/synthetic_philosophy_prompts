Please give an example of a conceptual issue, presented as a concrete decision scenario rather than a general objection. The scenario should be a descendant of the familiar puzzle in which a predictor has already decided, on the basis of its forecast of your choice, which of two locations to wait in for you, and you must choose one of them. The evidential recommendation in that puzzle never settles: staying looks best given that you stay, and leaving looks best given that you leave. Your scenario must add a twist that closes the usual escape routes — for instance, a forecast publicly announced in advance so that your choice is itself shaped by reading it, a forecast that is accurate only so long as you do not deliberate about it, or an agent acting through a randomizing device whose output the predictor also forecasts. Set out the scenario, identify precisely which assumption makes the recommendation fail to settle, and say what follows for how we should think about decision theory. Separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this topic, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
