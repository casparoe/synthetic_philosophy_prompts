Okay, I've got a half-formed story idea and I'd honestly rather you just write the thing than help me outline it, so here goes.

Near future, or now, whatever works. There's a procedure — call it a treatment, a device, doesn't matter — that can take a bad experience and fix it. But there are two completely different ways you could imagine that working, and morally they're not the same at all:

(a) The fix happens before the experience is ever finished. By the time it became something the person actually lived through, it was already the fixed version. They never went through the bad thing. Their body did, maybe, but their experience never did, and their memory of it is true.

(b) The fix happens after. They went through the bad thing all the way, they were conscious of every second of it, it happened to them. Then afterwards the recording gets overwritten. Their memory is false — they remember a version of events they never actually lived.

From the inside these are identical. And from the outside they're identical too: same words, same memories, same behaviour afterwards, even the scans come out the same. The only way to tell them apart would be to catch the two versions at the moment they split, and you can't, because you can only ever ask the person afterwards, and by then both have collapsed into the same memory.

Here's the bit thats been bugging me. I read somewhere about this old demo with two dots. Flash a blue dot at the top of a screen, then a red dot lower down, fast. People say they saw a single dot travel from the top to the bottom and change colour halfway. But they also say the colour change happened *before* the red dot was even on the screen — which can't be right, because the information about the red dot didn't exist yet. So the brain looks like it built the earlier part of the experience out of information that only arrived later. Fine. But nobody can say whether that's (a) or (b). Either the experience was always the merged version, so the "wrong" colour was never experienced at all, or it was experienced wrong first and then quietly rewritten a beat later. And some people think you'll never settle it, because settling it would mean finding the moment when an experience is finally "done", and maybe there is no such moment — experience is more like a draft that keeps getting edited, and "the original" is just an earlier draft, no more real than the last one. (I have a vague memory that a philosopher named the two options after Orwell and Stalin, for reasons I can never keep straight.)

What I want: a short story, maybe 2000-3000 words, set in the world where the procedure exists, where this difference actually matters to somebody — a court case, a regulator, a doctor, an insurer, a family, whatever feels alive to you. The person in the middle of it has to *act*, has to decide something that turns on which of (a) or (b) happened, and can't find out. Give me a real character with a real stake and a real reason to need the answer.

Two things I don't want. Don't have anyone stand around delivering a lecture about the concept, and please don't make it a 1984 thing where the government is erasing memories — the memory-hole version is the boring one and it isn't the idea. I want the story to actually sit inside the possibility that there's no fact of the matter here, and still have a plot, and still end without cheating by quietly revealing which one it was.
