ok, quick context: i'm a phd student in philosophy, mostly aesthetics, and our dept is putting together a little end-of-term zine and i said i'd write something for it, but i've been staring at a blank page for a week so i'm outsourcing. i want a sestina about the paradox of fiction, and specifically the version of it that actually keeps me up, not the textbook version.

the textbook version is: we cry over people we know dont exist, and crying seems to require believing the bad thing really happened, so something's off. fine, whatever. but the version i care about is sharper and weirder: a made-up death can wreck me more than a real one on the front page. i've read enough real obituaries to be a bit numb and i still have to put a novel down and walk around the block. i want the poem to live on that asymmmetry rather than explain it away.

and i want the form to be doing the work, not just decorating it. sestina, normal retrograde rotation, six stanzas plus envoi. for the six end-words, pick them so that three come from the feeling side (cry, ache, hold, whatever) and three come from the belief/reality side (real, believe, know...). then the rotation is doing exactly what the paradox does: every stanza recombines the same three claims into a new order, and none of the orders come out consistent. someone who knows the paradox should be able to point at the poem and say which claim is which and why the shuffling never fixes it. someone who's never heard of any of this should still just read it as a good poem about being wrecked by something you made up.

the envoi is where i want it to fail on purpose. thats where the poem tries to hand me a solution -- we dont really believe but we really do feel, or its make-believe feeling, or thoughts are enough without belief -- and i want the envoi to visibly not be able to hold all six words at once. two per line, all six of them, and you can feel it straining. dont resolve it in the last line, i'd rather it end with the thing still open.

one more thing if you can manage it: let one of the six stanzas be in the voice of the character. the person who isnt there, being grieved by someone who knows perfectly well they arent there. i dont know what that voice sounds like and thats kind of why i want it.

also please dont name-drop philosophers or cite anybody, it's going in a zine not a seminar paper. and dont title it something like "the paradox of fiction" -- give it an actual title.
