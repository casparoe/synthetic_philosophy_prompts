I am a high school student in a political economy elective, and I am writing a short paper on the socialist calculation debate. Before I commit to an argument, I want to make sure I am not building on a flawed summary. My older cousin, who is an economics major, explained the debate to me as follows: the dispute was between Mises and Hayek on one side and Lange on the other. Mises claimed that socialism is impossible because without private ownership there are no prices, so planners cannot know what to produce. Hayek softened this into the claim that planners cannot gather all the dispersed information they would need. Lange answered both by showing that a central planning board could set prices by trial and error, adjusting them until supply and demand balanced, exactly as a competitive market does under the Walrasian auctioneer; this is a system now usually called market socialism, and it proved that a socialist economy can be just as efficient as a capitalist one. So the calculation problem is really just a computational problem, and since modern computers can solve systems of equations far faster than any market, the debate is now moot. The only remaining objection to socialism is the incentive problem, which is separate and less serious.

I am inclined to believe my cousin, because he is usually careful, but some of this sounds too tidy, and I know the debate is supposed to be more complicated. Please go through his summary sentence by sentence and tell me what a historian of economic thought would accept, what they would wince at, and what is plainly wrong. In particular, I want to know whether the claim that the calculation problem is merely computational is fair to Hayek, whether Lange's trial-and-error procedure really answers the knowledge problem or only a different problem, whether it is accurate to say that Hayek merely softened Mises's argument, and whether the conclusion that the debate is moot and that only incentives remain actually follows from the parts of the summary that are accurate. I have also seen the debate described as three separate problems — calculation, knowledge, and incentive — and I cannot tell whether that is a useful map or a misleading one. I do not need citations or a reading list; I need to know which claims I can safely use in my paper and which I should drop or rephrase.
