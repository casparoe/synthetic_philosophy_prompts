So I'm one of the hosts of a small student-run philosophy podcast, and next month our guest is a visiting scholar whose whole thing is Confucian role ethics. The view, as I understand it, is that you don't first have a self and then happen to occupy roles; the self is constituted by its roles — child, parent, teacher, student, friend, neighbour, citizen — and ethics is therefore about learning to be good at those roles rather than about applying a general principle to a case. She works mostly out of the Analects and leans on the idea of love with distinctions: you care more about your parents than about strangers, and that isn't a regrettable concession to human weakness but part of what a good life is. Our co-host keeps calling role ethics "care ethics with the gender essentialism taken out," which I suspect is unfair but I don't have the ammunition to say why, and I'd rather not just ask her to define "role ethics" for the fifth time in a row.

Can you draft eight to ten interview questions that go for the joints of the view instead? I definitely want, somewhere in there: one that forces the issue of whether this is a genuinely normative ethics or just a thick description of how people in certain societies happen to live; one about what the view says when a role is itself corrupt or unjust — say a soldier in a war he's since decided was wrong, or the role of the dutiful wife in a tradition that's frankly misogynistic — where I want to press on whether role ethics can say the role is bad rather than merely that she's performing it badly; one about people who stand in no relevant role relation to me at all (strangers, people two centuries dead, animals), since love with distinctions seems to leave them out in the cold; one on moral progress, i.e. whether a self that is constituted by its roles can criticise the very tradition that handed it those roles; one on what happens when roles collide, since most of us are a parent and an employee and a citizen at once; and one on ritual, because if role performance is largely a matter of li, I want to know why the view isn't just conventionalism with better manners. Also worth working in somewhere, if it fits: the old argument with the Mohists over impartial care, and whether the comparison with Western virtue ethics is flattering or whether role ethics is just virtue ethics with a sociological base bolted on. For each question, give me a one-line note on the answer she's most likely to give and then a follow-up that lands *after* that answer — last time we did this the guest answered the first version of everything and we had nothing to say back. Order them so the later ones build on the earlier ones, and end with a closer that asks what would actually have to happen for her to give up the view.
