My cousin and her partner are deciding whether to have a child, and at dinner two of our relatives got into an argument about a claim she keeps invoking: that it can be bad to bring someone into existence but never bad not to, since no one is there to be wronged when a possible person is never born, whereas a real person can be harmed. I stayed out of it and still can't tell who had the better of it. Here is how it went.

Dev: That's why the reasons aren't symmetrical. There's a real downside to creating a person and no real upside. A child who suffers is wronged; a happy child who is never conceived wrongs no one, because there's no one who was deprived of anything. So procreation is at best a risk we take for ourselves, never a favor we do anyone.

Mira: You've described who can be a subject of harm, not a difference in value. Say it without the person: 'there is no suffering' and 'there is no joy' are both bare absences. If the first absence is good, the second is bad; if the second is neutral because no one is there to miss it, the first is neutral for exactly the same reason. Your asymmetry lives in your grammar, not in the world.

Dev: It isn't grammar. Pain is bad as an occurrence; pleasure is good only as received. So a world with no pain in it is better, full stop, while a world with no pleasure is merely empty.

Mira: Then pain is bad impersonally, apart from anyone it happens to. But what makes pain bad is what it's like for the one who has it. If the badness needs a subject, so does the goodness of its absence — and you're back to symmetry.

Dev: Fine, drop that plank. I only need this: existence can go badly for the person who exists, and non-existence can't go either way for anyone. So the only misfortune on the table sits on the side of existing.

Mira: Then you've quietly given up the asymmetry and kept a truism — existing people can be harmed — which is equally true of their being benefited. And your conclusion, that having a child is never a favor to the child, needed precisely the plank you just dropped.

Neither would give an inch. Please go through the exchange move by move, rating each move from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justifying each rating, and then say what both of them are missing about the underlying issue.
