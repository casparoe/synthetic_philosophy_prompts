I am a retired field ecologist who has spent the past several years reading philosophy and now runs a small reading group on the units-of-selection debate. What I want is not an essay but a classification instrument: a decision tree over the positions in that debate, built from the logical space rather than from a list of names — the dividing questions first, phrased so that a member of my group can answer each one about a concrete case without already knowing which label the answer leads to, then the positions located in the space those questions carve out. Where a combination of answers is unoccupied, say so, and say whether the resulting view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored; where two leaves cannot be separated by your questions, flag them rather than papering over the gap.

Four things to build in, since I think the usual framing hides them. First, keep separate the question of which entity bears adaptations, the question of which entity's fitness is tracked by a statistical decomposition of evolutionary change, and the question of which entity is copied with variation; a position that answers one and is silent on the others should be recorded as silent rather than filled in. Second, make a branch for whether the level question is a claim about the causal structure of the world or a choice about bookkeeping, and let the leaves on either side be genuinely distinct views. Third, route this case through the tree: a hypothetical colonial animal, which I will call the mosaic colony, in which two unrelated larvae fuse into a single body with one gonad and a shared vascular system, the partners contribute unequally to reproduction, and lineages can dissolve the union and re-fuse with different partners the following generation. Fourth, include a leaf for the view that selection at a higher level must be emergent rather than a sum of lower-level selection.

Leaves I want present: genic selectionism; individual-level adaptationism that treats group-level differences as mere differential reproduction; multi-level selection that allows real group-level selection without group-level adaptation; the kin-selection redescription; and the deflationary bookkeeping view. No citations or reading lists, please. Tell me how confident you are in each placement and in each diagnosis of an empty branch, and name the single branch point you are least sure of.
