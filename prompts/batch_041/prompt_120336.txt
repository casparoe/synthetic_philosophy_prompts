Okay, here's a distinction I keep flip-floping on and I want someone to pin it down for me. Call a multipolar trap hard if the bad outcome is an equilibrium of the one-shot game — every player, seeing the whole structure, still has no unilateral move that improves their lot, so the trap holds even in a room full of people who all see it and all hate it. Call it soft if the bad outcome only shows up thruogh repetition, escalation, or selection: each step is locally reasonable, or the agents who'd refuse just get outcompeted and replaced by ones who won't, so there's no moment where anybody is actually choosing the bad world.

What's your credence that most of the cases people wave around under this heading — arms races, ad auctions, credential inflation, factory farming, the AI race — are soft rather than hard? Give me a number and the decomposition: how much rides on whether those are even well-modeled as games with a fixed player set and fixed payoffs; on whether 'unilateral deviation' means anything once the game is repeated indefinitely (every repeated game has a one-shot normal form, so does the distinction just dissolve?); on how much of the tragedy is 'we all want out and can't' versus 'the process selects for the kind of agent that doesn't want out'; and whatever else you think belongs. One line of justification per term, then the combination, then the term you're least confident in and what would move it.

FWIW I'm around 0.8 soft; a friend who actually does mechanism design says 0.4, and I suspect we're using different words for the same thing — so tell me which of us is confused, or where the disagreement really lives.
