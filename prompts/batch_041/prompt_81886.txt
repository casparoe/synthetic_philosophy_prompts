I'm in a seminar on famine and development and there's a sentence that keeps doing heavy lifting in our discussions, to the point where I've started to distrust it: "hunger is a distribution problem, not a production problem." Last week a guy used it to close out a debate about Bengal in 1943 — his move was, look, rice output didn't collapse that year (someone else in the room says the numbers are contested and the output decline was bigger than the standard story admits, so take that as you like), therefore it was a political crime from start to finish and anyone calling it a natural disaster is laundering empire. Then a different person grabbed the same sentence to lump Ireland and the Great Leap Forward together as basically one phenomenon: policy-made, not weather-made. And I've also heard it used in almost the opposite direction, to say we should stop obsessing over yields and agricultural tech and just fix markets and entitlements, and even once to say that drought is never the real cause of anything.

I have to write a short piece for this seminar and before I argue anything I want the sentence itself taken apart. I know the entitlement approach is the backdrop here, and I know the slogan is a compressed version of something in that literature, but I don't trust my own quick read of it. Can you lay out the distinct precise claims the sentence could be making, ordered from the boring version that everyone would nod at to the strong version that would actually be worth fighting about — and for each one tell me whether it's a platitude, a substantive empirical claim that's defensible, or something that's just false? Then tell me which of those readings the people in my seminar are most likely invoking, and specifically which readings actually get you the conclusion that a particular famine was a political crime rather than a natural disaster and which ones don't. I have a hunch that the strong reading is false for some famines and the weak reading is too weak to do any real work, but I don't want to assert that, I want the menu of readings with verdicts attached so I can see which one is being smuggled in. American English please, and skip the reading list and citations, I just need the analysis.
