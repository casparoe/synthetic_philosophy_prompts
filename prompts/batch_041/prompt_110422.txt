My half-formed idea is about the transitivity of betterness and the spectrum arguments that are supposed to break it. You know the shape: a long sequence of cases where each adjacent pair seems clearly ranked one way, but the endpoints seem clearly ranked the other way, or seem incomparable, so transitivity looks like it has to go. I've been arguing alot with a friend who thinks this shows betterness is intransitive (or that there's no such thing as overall betterness). My suspicion is that the argument is getting traction from a conflation: we treat betterness as one relation, but maybe there are at least two things we call by that name -- a frame-relative betterness that is transitive because it only ranks options within a fixed comparison frame (same dimensions, same scale, same baseline), and a transitional or choice-guiding betterness that we use in pairwise comparisons across frames and that can look intransitive. The real question is whether that distinction can be made precise without collapsing into the boring claim that a partial order is a partial order, and whether it can explain the spectrum cases rather than just redescribing them.
