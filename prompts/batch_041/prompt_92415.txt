I run a small "ethics after hours" group — six or seven of us, no one with formal training, we meet in the back room of a pub once a month and argue about one topic. Last time we got onto compromise, and it turned into the best fight we've had, so I want to bring it back and actually referee it, but I can't decide who's right and I'd rather not steer it. Here's the gist. Dana opened with a line I thought was just a slogan but she's defended it twice now: she says compromising with someone whose values differ from yours is basically trade, and the reason to do it is the same reason to trade — each side gives up something it cares about less to get something it cares about more, so both walk away better off, and the only thing that makes it hard is that people confuse "I'm giving something up" with "I'm losing." Ravi pushed back hard. He said trade works because there's a common currency, money, that tells you how much each side wanted what; moral values don't come with an exchange rate, so the "both gain" claim is doing sleight of hand — you only get a gain if you've already decided how to weigh my commitment against yours, which is exactly the thing in dispute. Dana said fine, the weighing happens through the negotiation itself, that's what the back-and-forth is for. Ravi said no, that just means the stronger or more stubborn party sets the rate. Then Dana gave an example: two flatmates, one vegan for ethical reasons, one an enthusiastic cook who eats meat, they agree the kitchen is vegan and the meat-eater does his cooking at his girlfriend's place, and she says that's a clean gain, everyone's better off. Ravi said that's the example that proves his point, not hers: the vegan has agreed to live alongside something she thinks is wrong, so what she "gained" is the absence of a fight, and calling the absence of a fight a gain to her values is like calling the absence of a mugging a gain to your wallet — it's just the baseline. Dana came back that Ravi is treating every value as absolute, and that if your values are so absolute you can't trade on them you've just described fanaticism, and that real people rank their commitments all the time, so the common currency isn't money, it's just the ordinary fact that we care about some things more than others. Ravi said ranking your own commitments is one thing and letting someone else's ranking bind you is another, and that's the whole difference.

I don't have a dog in this — honestly I kept flip-flopping while they talked. Go through it move by move and tell me who's right about what, where each of them is overreaching even when the core point is good, and what both of them are missing that neither has said yet. In particular I want to know whether Dana's trade analogy survives Ravi's "common currency" objection or whether he's actually got her, and whether the flatmate example helps either of them or is just a distraction. And don't just tell me it's "complicated" — rate each of the moves from clearly wrong to clearly right and tell me who, on balance, has the better of it.
