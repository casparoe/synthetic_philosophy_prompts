My friend Dee and I spent two hours arguing about enhancement last night and I still can't work out what rule she's actually running. Her track record, every verdict delivered like it's self-evident: LASIK for myopia, fine. Growth hormone for a kid whose pituitary doesn't work, fine. Growth hormone for a kid who's just short and whose parents want him taller, no. Cochlear implants for a deaf infant, fine — but parents who deliberately pick a deaf embryo, she thinks are out of their minds. Modafinil for healthy students the week before finals, no. Beta blockers for a violinist who's just nervous about an audition and doesn't have an anxiety disorder, no. SSRIs for real depression, fine. Adderall for real ADHD, fine.

Every one of those comes out the same whether she's running the line that medicine restores normal functioning and doesn't get to optimize people past it, or the line that the therapy/enhancement split is a red herring and the only things that matter are whether it harms you and whether it buys you an unfair edge. So I can't tell which one she's committed to, and i dont think she can either.

Here's the case I want you to rule on for her. A healthy adult with normal vision and nothing wrong with her eyes wants a one-time gene therapy that would let her see a bit into the near ultraviolet. Cheap, safe, permanent, no side effects, and it doesn't make her better than anyone at anything anyone competes for. What does Dee say? Which of her rules is doing the real work? And what case would actually settle it — my guess is you'd need one that cuts the other way, a genuine therapy that happens to hand you a big competitive edge over everyone else, but tell me if there's a sharper one.
