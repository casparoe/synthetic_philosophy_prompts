ok so I've been chewing on this one for weeks and I want you to write me a short story about it.

Could a book be conscious? The version I keep coming back to: you bind a book that's a page-by-page simulation of somebodys brain. Each page is a snapshot of the entire neural state at one instant, and the margins tell you which page to turn to next depending on what just happened. You ask it a question, then spend a few hours flipping back and forth following the instructions, and eventually a reply comes out -- in Einsteins handwriting, phrased the way he'd have phrased it. Nobody wrote that reply. It fell out of the flipping. So: is the book thinking? Is there a person in there, or is it a very elaborate recording?

Here's the wrinkle I actually want the story to live in. There's a second book. Same cover, same size, same replies to everything. But this one has no simulation and no instructions -- it's just every possible question paired with its answer, precomputed and printed one per page in a giant index. You look up your question, turn to the page, and the answer is already sitting there, instantly. A friend of mine insists the first book might be conscious and the second one obviously isnt -- the second is a lookup table, its not doing anything, nobody's home. But I cant see what the flipping is buying us, since the two books behave identically and, honestly, both were finished the moment they came off the press.

Write the story. I want a character who ends up with both books and slowly starts to care about the difference in a way they can't justify. Some things I'd like in there: a scene where they test the books against each other and the test comes out ambiguous; a moment where one of the books hits a wall -- maybe the index one can't answer something that wasn't already in it, and the character has to decide whether that counts as a failure or just a fact about the question; at least two people who disagree about it without either being a strawman; and a beat where the character does something to the first book (leaves it open on a page, keeps it warm, apologizes to it) and then has to sit with why they did that. 

Dont have anyone stand up and lecture about consciousness -- let the whole thing come out of what the characters do and notice. And please dont tie it off with a neat answer about whether either book is conscious. I want the ending to leave the reader holding the same itch the character has. A few pages is fine, no need for a novel.
