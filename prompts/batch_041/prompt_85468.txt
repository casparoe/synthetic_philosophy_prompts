Germany recognized the Herero and Nama genocide in 2015 and then in 2021 worked out a deal with the Namibian government -- something like 1.1 billion euros over thirty years, paid out as development aid rather then reparations, with Germany explicitly rejecting any legal responsibility for what the Empire did in 1904-08. Herero and Nama representatives complained they got negotiated around instead of with. I want that episode run through Jaspers's four kinds of guilt from the 1946 lectures -- criminal, political, moral, metaphysical -- and run hard, not as a book report on Jaspers.

So: who in this case bears each of the four, and what does each one actually license -- a trial, an apology, money, a monument, nothing? Where the framework doesn't fit, say so rather than stretching it to fit.

Some things I keep coming back to. The perpetrators are all dead, so criminal guilt has no defendant, yet the deal still uses legal-ish language while refusing legal responsibility. The state that did it was the Empire, not the Federal Republic, which puts a continuity question right in the middle of any political-guilt claim. And the victim side isn't a state at all -- Herero and Nama are peoples, and the party that signed off was the government of Namibia, which is not the same thing and was itself accused of speaking over the people who were harmed.

Now push it out five generations. The way I remember Jaspers (correct me if I'm mangling him): moral guilt is strictly individual and can't be collectivized, political guilt runs with the state and can't be escaped by having been against the regime, and metaphysical guilt is a solidarity before God that no court or parliament can touch. If that's roughly right, then: if the moral guilt died with the people who did the killing, what exactly is the basis for the money? If the political guilt is real, on what grounds do you collect it from people who had no say in how they were governed, and what do you do about the Empire's political community not being the Federal Republic's? And if the metaphysical guilt is the live one, isn't it precisely the sort of thing that can't be handed to a nation or settled in euros -- which would make the 2021 deal a category error rather than an injustice?

Other direction: what does the case show about the theory? My hunch is that Jaspers built the scheme for a nation-state that persists and for a victim community that also has a state to speak for it and receive, and that Namibia wobbles both assumptions. Tell me if that's right, and which of the three is the real anomoly -- the non-state victim, the discontinuous perpetrator state, or the generational gap. And give me a verdict instead of a menu: which of the four kinds is actually doing the moral work in that deal, and is it the kind Jaspers would have wanted doing it?
