You are a philosopher of religion with expertise in the scholastic debates over divine foreknowledge, and a good ear for philosophical humor. I want a joke about Molinism and middle knowledge: the view that God knows, logically prior to any decision or decree of his own, what each possible free creature would freely do in any circumstances in which he might place it. Because those counterfactuals of freedom are supposed to be true independently of God's will, he cannot make them true; he can only consult them and then choose which possible world to actualize, taking the free choices it contains as given.

Start by brainstorming several candidate punchlines and set aside the ones that would work for almost any theory of divine foreknowledge, or that trade only on the words "God" and "knows." Then write one joke — a brief setup plus a punchline — whose humor turns on the actual mechanics of the view. Candidate targets include: that the counterfactuals are settled before God decides anything, so creation is a matter of selection rather than authorship; that no one has ever given a satisfactory account of what makes these counterfactuals true in the first place; that petitionary prayer could only change a creature's circumstances, never what it would freely do; and that the counterfactuals are supposed to hold even of people God never gets around to creating. A reader who knows the debate should be able to tell that the joke was written by someone who understands it. Keep it to a few lines, let the joke stand on its own, and do not cite or lean on any particular author or work. You may append a single sentence of explanation, but no more.
