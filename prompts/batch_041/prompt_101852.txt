I am in the sixth form and am writing an essay on whether the European Union can settle into a stable shape, or whether it must go on integrating or else come apart. My teacher introduced what she calls the bicycle theory: European integration, she says, is like riding a bicycle, in that you have to keep pedalling towards closer union, because the moment you stop and try to hold still you lose your balance and fall off. She used it to argue that deeper integration and break-up are the only real options available, and that talk of a stable, static EU is wishful thinking.

Please audit the analogy for me. Set out the correspondence explicitly — what stands for the rider, the pedals, the momentum, the balance, and the fall — and then say where the comparison breaks down: whether integration genuinely needs forward motion in the way a bicycle does, whether the treaties, opt-outs and differentiated integration offer any way of standing still, and whether the picture quietly imports a single rider with a single will where in fact there are twenty-seven governments pulling in different directions. Finally, tell me whether my teacher's conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying it all along, and suggest a better comparison if one exists.
