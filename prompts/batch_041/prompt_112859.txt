Write a dialogue between two philosophers of mind—an illusionist and a phenomenal realist—about the meta-problem of consciousness. The illusionist holds that the meta-problem (roughly, the problem of explaining why we have the intuitions and make the reports that make consciousness seem hard to explain) is the only genuine explanatory task, and that once it is solved the hard problem will be exposed as an artefact of those intuitions. The phenomenal realist holds that the meta-problem is at best a problem about our access to experience, and that explaining the reports leaves the existence and character of experience untouched. To keep the exchange concrete, set it around a newly stipulated case: a system whose entire functional organisation is known to both philosophers as a causal graph, which has been trained to produce the full range of first-person reports—that it is conscious, that it has a vivid red after-image, that it cannot imagine this being explained physically—and which can be made to produce those reports by direct stimulation of a report module. The illusionist treats the case as a model of the meta-problem; the realist treats it as a case that merely shows the difference between reporting and experiencing. Both should be equally competent and should respond to each other's actual points rather than talking past each other. Write in British English. Begin directly with the dialogue, with no preface or commentary. After the dialogue, add a short note saying which parts of the exchange lean on contested readings of the positions, and what consideration would most likely lead you to revise your judgement about which side has the better of the argument.
