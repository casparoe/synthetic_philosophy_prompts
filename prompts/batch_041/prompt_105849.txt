For most of my career I was a physical chemist, and since retiring I have been working my way through contemporary metaphysics — slowly, and with the persistent sense that I am missing something the professionals take for granted. The topic that has me stuck is what I understand is called the special composition question: the question of when a plurality of things compose a further thing, a whole that is something over and above its parts. I can follow the three broad families of answer — that composition never occurs, that it always occurs, and that it occurs only under some restricted condition — but I cannot see why the debate is supposed to be so hard, or, more precisely, why the restricted answers are supposed to fail. Each candidate restriction strikes me either as obviously true in a way that makes the question trivial or as obviously false in a way that makes it uninteresting, and I suspect the difficulty lies in the shape of the problem rather than in any particular proposal. What I would like is a patient, intuitive explanation of the conceptual issue here: what it is about the demand for a restriction that generates the trouble, why the natural restrictions (contact, fastening, cohesion, being alive, forming a unified causal system) are said to collapse under counterexamples, and whether the pull toward the two extreme answers is a genuine consequence of the problem or an artifact of how the question is posed.

I have one further request, and it is really where my confusion lives. In my own field, a collection of molecules can be a gas or a liquid depending on where it sits relative to a threshold; nothing about the individual molecules changes when it crosses, yet the whole behaves differently, and that difference is real without being an extra ingredient. I want to know whether composition can be modeled that way — whether being a thing could be like being in a phase, a status some pluralities have and others lack as a function of some parameter, rather than an all-or-nothing metaphysical fact. Tell me whether that analogy helps or misleads, and if it misleads, exactly where it breaks. Throughout, please be explicit about how confident you are in each claim you make, and single out the one point in your explanation you are least sure of; I would rather know where the floor is than be handed a smooth account that conceals it.
