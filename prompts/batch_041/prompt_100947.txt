ok, I'm helping a small foundation decide where to put about three years of funding for two or three theory people, and the area we've circled is "embedded agency" -- basically the pile of problems you get when you stop pretending the agent stands outside the world it's reasoning about and instead models that world from the inside. What I need from you is an agenda, not a survey, and definitely not a reading list (the foundation has a librarian for that).

Here's my own rough carving of the candidate problems, push back if it's wrong:
1. Self-trust and self-reference. An agent that designs or approves a successor more capable than itself. There seem to be formal results in the way, roughly: if you're a system that reasons carefully about systems like you, you can end up proving unhelpful things about your own behavior. Is this a deep wall or an artifact of picking one particular logic?
2. The Cartesian boundary. Agents that treat the world as out there and themselves as a special exception to it. Can a bounded agent's model even include the agent itself without paradox or infinite regress? And is dropping the boundary actually necessary, or just philosophically tidier?
3. Logical uncertainty. How to hold credences about claims you haven't proven. Is this upstream of everything else, or a mostly seperate technical project that just happens to live nearby?
4. Decision theory. The agent's choice is itself part of the world it's predicting. How much of embedded agency just collapses into getting this right?
5. Ontology and abstraction. What makes a carving of the world into agent vs environment, or into variables at all, a good carving for a bounded agent? I'll be honest, I suspect this is the real crux and everyone else just waves at it.

Now the thing I actually want: rank these (or a better carving, if mine is bad) by what you'd fund first, and defend the order. I especially want the dependency structure -- which one, if you settled it, would make the others easier or make them dissolve, and which are downstream of some prior choice you haven't made yet. Tell me which are tractable for armchair people vs people who have to write code. And tell me what kind of result in the next couple years would make you reorder the list.

One more wrinkle, and I want you to take it seriously rather than pat me on the head: I half-suspect the whole thing is a special case of a more general question -- how any bounded system can be a model of itself, with the AI-alignment framing distorting which questions look central. If that reframing is right, does your priority order change, and where?
