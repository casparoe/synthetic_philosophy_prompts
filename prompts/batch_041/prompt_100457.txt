Ok, the hitchhiker case: you're stranded in the desert, some guy says he'll only give you a ride if you promise to pay him once your home, you promise, he picks you up, and now — safe in the car — you've got no reason at all to actually pay. Write a dialogue between two people hashing out whether you should pay anyway. One thinks the fact that you'd gain nothing by paying doesn't get you off the hook, and that it's rational to have made yourself the sort of person who pays; the other thinks that whole move is a cheat and that once you're in the car the promise has no grip. They should really go at each other — the skeptic should press on whether "rational to have decided in advance" even means anything once the deciding-in-advance you isn't the one footing the bill, and the other should have a genuine reply, not just "but cooperation".

Throw in one wrinkle for them to argue over: suppose the driver knows perfectly well you've got no reason to pay and is doing it to test you, or just to feel generous — does your promise still bind if he isn't really counting on it? And what if there'd been two of you and he'd only take one?

Neither one should be a pushover. Keep it under 600 words, and finish with a one-line summary of where each of them ends up.
