I'm in a small philosophy of mind reading group, mostly grad students plus one guy who's a postdoc in a neuroscience lab, and last week we got into whether consciousness does any causal work at all. At some point someone dropped "consciousness is just along for the ride" like it ended the discussion — his point, as far as I could tell, was that experiences are caused by brain processes but don't themselves go on to cause anything, so any theory that hands consciousness a job is just confused. I sort of nodded but afterwards I couldnt tell whether he'd said something deep or something almost empty dressed up in a metaphor. Can you lay out for me the different precise claims that one sentence could be making, from the trivial to the radical — being explicit about *what* is supposed to be along for the ride (the experience itself? the feeling? just the fact that there's something it's like? the report I make about it?), and along for the ride with respect to *what* (causing my behavior? figuring in any causal explanation? being something natural selection could act on?) — and then say for each reading whether it's a platitude, a live and defensible position, or just false, and which of them would actually get him his conclusion that theories giving consciousness a causal job are confused.

The thing that's really nagging me is the self-report problem, and I want the answer to take it seriously. If the experience really is idle, then my saying "I'm in pain" has to be caused by whatever brain state I'm in, not by the pain — but then what makes that utterance *about* the pain at all, or makes it track the pain, rather than just being a noise the brain happens to make when it's in that state? It feels like under some readings the slogan turns my own confident report that I have experiences into a lucky coincidence, which seems like a reductio, and under other readings it's completely harmless. So I'd like the menu of readings, with a verdict on each, plus a note on which of them walk into that self-report mess and which slip past it. Lay it out as a list I can actually bring back to the group rather than one long essay, and tell me which reading I should assume he meant.
