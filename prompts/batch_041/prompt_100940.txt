I write about science and ideas for a general-reader outlet, and I've been trying my hand at fiction on the side — mostly because the thought experiments that never quite make it into the reporting are the ones I can't stop turning over. The one stuck in my head right now is the quantum-suicide / quantum-immortality business: the idea that if the many-worlds picture is right and every branch really happens, then someone who plays Russian roulette will, from her own first-person perspective, always wake up in the branch where the chamber was empty — so "I can't die" turns into something she can never test, because she only ever gets to ask the question from inside a surviving branch. I'd like a short story built on that, and please not the usual version where it's just spooky and the guy gets smug about being unkillable. What I actually want is the part that feels genuinely vertiginous to me: that the survival is unobservable and unfalsifiable from the inside, so every bit of evidence she collects is rigged in advance; that her grief for the selves who died in the other branches is either misplaced or monstrous and I honestly can't tell which; and the odd ethics of someone who, believing all this, starts treating her own risk-taking as free while the people around her — who in her branch only ever see her keep surviving — start to read it as recklessness, or as a kind of coldness. Maybe set it in a hospital, where she's visiting someone who is not going to survive, and where the whole worldview is being quietly stress-tested. Alot of moving parts, I know.

One more thing, and this matters more to me than the prose: if the premise here is confused, or the story as I've framed it can't really carry the idea, just say so instead of humoring me — I'd rather get pushback than a smooth piece that papers over a muddle. And if you do write it, tack on a short note at the end about where you're taking liberties with the physics and the philosophy, or leaning on a contested reading of what quantum immortality is even supposed to imply, because I want to know where the story is doing the philosophy badly rather than be told it all hangs together.
