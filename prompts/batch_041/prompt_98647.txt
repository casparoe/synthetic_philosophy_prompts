I'm a fourth-year PhD student in philosophy, and I'm running a session for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on modal epistemology: fourteen students, ninety minutes, and no lecture from me. I want a runnable classroom activity that makes them feel, in their own hands, a distinction I keep failing to land in prose — between merely being able to describe a scenario without overt contradiction and being able to work out, step by step, how the scenario would actually go. Call these thin and thick conceivability; my working gloss is that thin conceivability is a description that survives a quick read, while thick conceivability requires that you can fill in the details and still reach no contradiction. The point I want them to hit themselves is that the inference from conceivability to possibility is only as strong as the conception it starts from, and that our felt sense of "I can conceive of that" is usually thin conceivability dressed up as thick — and, further, that the same underlying situation can be thin-conceivable under one description and not under another, which is where the inference gets into trouble.

Please take the role of an experienced philosophy teacher who designs inquiry-based activities, and think carefully before you commit to a design: brainstorm several candidate mechanics, say briefly why you chose the one you did, then give me the full activity — rules, materials, roles if any, and a minute-by-minute timeline for the ninety minutes. One constraint: it must not lean on the usual gallery of examples (zombies, water and H2O, God, brains in vats). Build it instead around a mundane case, and arrange the rules so that students commit to a conceivability claim and then discover through what happens that their conception was thinner than they believed. Finish with a debrief that maps each stage of the activity onto the distinction above, tells me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and is frank about which part of the conceivability–possibility thesis the activity cannot reproduce and that I will have to supply myself. Keep it practical enough that I can run it next week.
