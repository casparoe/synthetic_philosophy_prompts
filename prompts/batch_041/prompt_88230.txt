Ok so I'm a fourth-year PhD student in philosophy and I got voluntold to write one essay question for the MA-level metaphysics final, specifically the vagueness unit. I want something students can't answer by dumping the usual taxonomy — epistemicism, supervaluationism, degree theory, contextualism, one paragraph each, done. So I'm gonna hand them a little case I made up and make them actually reason about it. The case: we introduce a brand-new predicate, say "schmeap", by stipulation — a collection of grains is a schmeap iff it has at least K grains, where K is a number written on a card sealed inside an envelope nobody has opened (and nothing about our usage fixes K beyond that card). Now run the ordinary sorites on it: one grain isn't a schmeap, two hundred thousand is, and each step adds a single grain.

What I want from you: draft the essay question itself (90 minutes, roughly 1000 words for them), then give me the model answer plus marking scheme as exactly five numbered points, each one a sentence or two, naming the specific move a top answer has to make, the marks it carries out of 20, and one line on how a B answer typically half-gets it. No preamble, no summing-up paragraph at the end — just the question and then the five points.

Some things the question has to force them on, so the model answer should definately hit them: (a) the envelope case has a sharp boundary that's merely contingently hidden — is that the same kind of unknowability the epistemicist needs, or is the epistemicist's whole bet that our ignorance of the "heap" boundary is *not* like that? (b) does the sorites paradox even get off the ground for "schmeap", and if it doesn't, what does that tell us about whether the tolerance premise is a real constraint on meaning or just a hidden-fact claim in disguise? (c) the nastiest objection, which I want them to face: the envelope case makes any candidate boundary look arbitrary, so what exactly does the epistemicist owe us that they haven't paid? And they should also say what a supervaluationist and a degree theorist would say about "schmeap" (my guess is both just call it precise, which is a nice way to make them see what vagueness is even supposed to be). Don't let it turn into a citation exercise, I don't want them quoting anyone, just reasoning.
