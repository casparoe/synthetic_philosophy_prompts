I've been reading over my friend's shoulder while she works on a book about the historical Jesus, and her method is basically: go through the sayings one by one and drop each into one of three piles -- he said it, maybe, the church invented it. Her calls so far, roughly:

- the one where he tells a would-be follower to let the dead bury their own dead -- he said it.
- the one about hating your father and mother and your own life to follow him -- he said it.
- the one where someone calls him good and he pushes back, why call me good, only God is good -- he said it.
- the one where he says the end is coming before all his hearers have died -- he said it, which seems like a strange thing to grant given how that turned out.
- the one about loving your enemies and turning the other cheek -- church invented it.
- the one about giving Caesar what belongs to Caesar -- church invented it.
- the one where the kingdom is like a mustard seed, i.e. a scrubby weed rather than a grand cedar -- he said it.

Now here's the one she hasn't gotten to. There's a saying where he hands the disciples the authority to bind and loose, to forgive sins or hold them unforgiven, on earth. What's her verdict going to be?

I'm less interested in the verdict than in the rule. She thinks she's running the criterion of embarrassment -- if the early church would have found a saying awkward, it's probably real. But I can explain every single one of those calls just as well with "it's in more than one independent strand and it fits the rest of the authentic core," and honestly for a couple of them I can also explain the call with "the church would have wanted this, so they made it up." So: what does each of those rules predict about the binding-and-loosing saying, which rule do you think she's actually running, and what saying should I hand her next that would seperate the rules for good? Bonus if you can tell me whether the two halves of my second rule -- multiple attestation, and coherence with the core -- ever come apart, and what a saying that pulled them apart would look like. And the thing I actually want to know: can any of these criteria settle who he was, or do they just launder back the picture of him you started with? If its the latter, say so plainly.
