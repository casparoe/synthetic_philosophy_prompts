- Which side of the road people drive on here: convention.
- That in English the word bank means the money place and not the river edge: convention.
- Tipping 18-20% at restaurants in the US: not a convention, she says its a social norm.
- In her hometown the weekly market is on Thursdays and the whole town plans its week around it. Nobody ever decided this, no one ever considered a different day, and any other day of the week would have worked exactly as well. She calls it a convention.

Next month she starts at a software company, and the engineers there have a house format for commit messages (imperative mood, ticket number first, no emoji). Nothing enforces it, but if you don't follow it, code review gets snippy and someone will bring it up in standup. Any of several other formats would work just as well, everybody uses the house format, and everybody knows everybody does.

What's she going to call the commit message thing — convention, social norm, or neither? I care less about the label than about the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit all four of her earlier verdicts just as well, and what each of those rules predicts here. Tell me which of her four cases is the closest analog, and be honest if none of them really is one. Then tell me what fifth regularity I could get her verdict on that would actually settle which rule she's running — something where the surviving rules disagree.
