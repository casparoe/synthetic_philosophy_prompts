A colleague in my reading group argues that asking whether a language model really has beliefs is a category mistake, and he reaches for the chess engine to make the point: no one supposes the program wants to win or believes its queen is en prise, yet describing it that way is the only tractable way to anticipate its moves, and on his view that is all belief talk has ever been — a predictive shortcut, not a report on hidden furniture. So the model's beliefs are exactly as real as the engine's, no more and no less, and the question of what it really thinks is empty.

I want that audited rather than asserted. Set out the correspondence he is relying on — what plays the role of the board state, the win condition, the search procedure, the programmer who specified the game — and then tell me where the mapping breaks. Which disanalogies bear on his conclusion (that the reality question is empty, and that belief talk about the model is licensed only instrumentally) and which are idle? Finally: does his conclusion survive once the differences are on the table, and if a better analogy is available for the work he wants done, what is it?
