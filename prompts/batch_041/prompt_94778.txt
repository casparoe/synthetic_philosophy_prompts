I'm building a decision tree for a graduate historiography seminar on the decline and fall of the Western Roman Empire, and I want it to sort the interpretive positions by what they actually claim rather than by the schools they are usually assigned to. Build it from the logical space, with each branch node phrased as a question about the supposed change rather than a question about which label or historian one favors, starting from a commitment most parties accept — that something consequential altered the western provinces between roughly the third and sixth centuries. The earliest nodes should separate positions by what they take the explanandum to be: the western imperial state, the tax-and-army apparatus, the classical city and its elite culture, the Mediterranean exchange economy, or civilization at large. Later nodes should separate internal from external causes, punctual catastrophe from slow erosion, and loss from liberation or neutral transformation, and the tree should keep decline, a gradual loss of capacity, distinct from fall, the ending of a particular polity, since several positions trade on conflating them. Leaves should carry the name of the position where one exists and otherwise be marked unnamed, each with a sentence on its most awkward commitment; I want the moral-degeneracy, barbarian-invasion, climate-and-plague, toxic-exposure, fiscal-military, and did-not-fall-but-transformed positions to appear as leaves. Finally, tell me which combinations of answers are unoccupied and whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of positions the tree cannot separate.
