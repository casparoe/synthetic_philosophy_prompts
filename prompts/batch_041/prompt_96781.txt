So I've been in this small Discord/reading group that's been grinding through the neuroscience-of-free-will stuff for like eight months, and the Libet thing has completely stalled in a way that's starting to feel less like a disagreement and more like four different conversations happening in the same channel. Roughly the camps are: (1) the "it's over" people, who think a readiness potential showing up before you're aware of having decided is a real problem for the kind of agency you need for blame and desert; (2) the a priori crowd, who say no timing measurement could possibly bear on free will, because free will was never a claim about when a decision happens in the brain; (3) the people who've read the newer reinterpretation where the readiness potential is basically just background noise in the motor cortex crossing a threshold, so the experiment maybe isn't measuring a decision at all; and (4) a couple of hard-nosed types who say the whole thing is confused because "the decision" isn't a well-defined event you can timestamp, so "when did you decide" has no fact of the matter. Every session, camps 2 and 3 win the room, camp 1 says "fine, but the folk psychology of agency still has to answer to something," and then we all go home. Nobody's changed their mind since March and I don't think anyone's going to. I want a diagnosis, not a verdict on free will.

Here's my hypothesis and I'd like you to try to wreck it: the stall isn't really about free will, it's about whether the moment of decision is a timestampable thing at all. And I think there's a distinction nobody in the group is drawing — between timing evidence (evidence about *when* something happened) and authorship evidence (evidence about whether the thing that happened was up to you, or yours in the way that matters for responsibility) — and that the whole debate quietly assumes that if you could nail the timing you'd get the authorship for free. If that assumption is doing the work, then camp 1 and camp 2 aren't even disagreeing about the same claim, and camp 3's empirical fix wouldn't settle anything either. But maybe I'm wrong and it's just a normal empirical dispute about what the readiness potential is, or a straight verbal fight over "free." Diagnose it using what each side actually says, not the labels I slapped on them — is this substantive, verbal, an empirical question in disguise, or two or three questions wearing one name?

Then tell me what, if anything, would count as progress given your diagnosis. A conceptual distinction that separates the questions cleanly? A better experiment — and if so, what would it even need to measure, since we clearly don't have an agreed way to identify the relevant event? Some formal result about what timing evidence could and couldn't establish? Or is the honest answer that the question is ill-posed and the group should stop asking it, in which case say that and explain why no amount of cleverness is going to move this. I've read enough LessWrong to know "it's all just semantics" is usually a cop-out, so if you go that route, actually defend it.
