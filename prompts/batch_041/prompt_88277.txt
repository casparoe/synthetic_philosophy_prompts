Next month I'm giving a talk to our department's philosophy of science reading group, and I'd like you to draft my Q&A prep sheet. The thesis I'm defending is that falsificationism fails as a demarcation criterion, and fails more embarrassingly than the usual objections admit: the trouble is not that it draws the science/pseudoscience line in the wrong place, but that the line is one that pseudoscience is unusually good at performing. The disciplines we most want to exclude — astrology, creation science, large stretches of Freudian theory — are precisely those that have learned to emit risky-sounding predictions and then immunise them after the fact, while plenty of respectable work, from string theory to parts of cosmology, makes no prediction anyone could presently refute. So I want to argue that falsifiability selects for skill at appearing falsifiable rather than for genuine epistemic exposure, and that we should replace it with a norm of treating one's own claims as actually at hazard — call it the exposure norm — applied to a research programme rather than to a single theory. Underneath it all, my real target is the rationalist fantasy of a certification procedure, an algorithm that sorts belief into licensed and unlicensed; I want to say that the demand for a demarcation criterion is that fantasy in philosophical dress.

The audience is small and I know exactly who will be there: one committed Popperian who understands the difference between naive and sophisticated falsificationism and will cite Lakatos at me; one Bayesian who thinks the entire demarcation project is a red herring, since science is just updating on likelihoods and falsification is merely the zero-likelihood special case; one historian of science of the Kuhn/Lakatos stripe who will insist that normal science never falsifies anything and that all of this was settled decades ago; and two working scientists who mainly want to know what to tell their undergraduates.

Give me the objections in the order I'll actually receive them, phrased as each of those people would phrase them, ordered by how much damage each does to my position, with a short reply to each and an honest note on where that reply is weakest. Include the one question I'm hoping nobody asks.
