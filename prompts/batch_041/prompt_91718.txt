Please explain what a coherence argument for expected utility maximization actually establishes, separating its two standard strands: the exploitability or money-pump argument, which begins from the thought that a rational agent should not be willing to accept a sure loss, and the representation-theorem argument, which begins from axioms on preference and derives a utility function. I want the explanation to make clear exactly where completeness of the preference ordering is load-bearing, and then to turn to an agent whose preferences are transitive but incomplete — one who prefers A to B, prefers B to C, and has no opinion between A and C. For that agent, say whether they are irrational, exploitable, or merely incomplete, and whether the two strands give different answers. No citations are needed; I am after the conceptual structure.
