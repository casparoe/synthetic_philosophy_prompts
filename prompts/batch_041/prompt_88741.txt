I'm building an anthology of stories that dramatise political philosophy, and I want one on transitional justice — but I'm a bit sick of the South Africa vs Nuremberg framing, so here's a setup I'd rather you used instead. A fictional country comes out of a fifteen-year civil war and sets up a truth commission with an odd rule: amnesty is automatic, but each perpetrator's confession is read out at a public hearing, and it only stands if a randomly selected member of the victims family — someone who never volunteered and can't be swapped out — stands up afterwards and says 'I accept this'. Silence, or a refusal, sends the case to an ordinary court. Tell the story from the point of view of one of those randomly chosen people on the morning of the hearing. I want it to actually wrestle with whether a forgiveness that's been engineered like that is justice or just a second injury, and with whether the point of the whole exercise is the truth or the peace — but don't resolve it tidily, and don't have anyone spell the theory out.
