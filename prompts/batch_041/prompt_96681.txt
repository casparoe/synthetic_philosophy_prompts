My flatmate's doing an MA in history and he's gotten into chaos theory and the whole for-want-of-a-nail thing, and he's built an argument that I'm pretty sure proves way too much but I can't find the seam. He's very pleased with it. Tidied up:

(1) A factor is a cause of an outcome just in case some change to that factor would have made the outcome not happen, or happen differently. That's what mattering means — if you can change it and nothing downstream budges, it didn't matter.
(2) Historical outcomes are chaotic in the technical sense: small differences in initial conditions get amplified, so arbitrarily small changes early on can produce arbitrarily large differences later.
(3) So for any factor in the run-up to an outcome — the weather, a nail in a horseshoe, the king's digestion that morning — there's some small change to it that would have flipped the outcome.
(4) By (1), every factor in the run-up is a cause.
(5) And (1) doesn't grade. It's yes or no. So they're all causes to the same degree: the nail is as much a cause of the battle as the general's decision.
(6) So the main cause is never a fact about the world, only a fact about what a historian happens to care about. Explanation is storytelling.
(7) Which means the question of whether some event was inevitable or contingent is empty — everything is equally contingent and equally caused, so nothing is specially contingent.

I want to keep (2), I'm fairly sure its true. And (7) has a grip on me, honestly. What I can't swallow is (5) — I don't believe the nail is as much a cause of the battle as the general's decision. But maybe that's just me being romantic about great men and I should get over it.

Where does this go wrong? Which step do I give up, why did it look fine while I was reading it, and what do I have to pay if I give it up? I'd rather not surrender (1) or (2) if I can help it. And if the fix is supposed to be that some causes are bigger than others, I want to know what bigger is even supposed to mean here, because it can't just mean the ones I find interesting — that's the move he's making fun of.
