I'm a retired protein chemist -- spent thirty-odd years in industry on enzymes, mostly -- and since I hung up the lab coat I've been sitting in on philosophy of biology seminars at the local university. I keep getting into arguments about whether DNA is really a code, or whether that's just a metaphor we've all gotten lazy with. Last week a philosopher told me the code idea is either trivial or false, and a former colleague told me it's the central fact of biology; they nearly came to blows over the cheese. I can't shake the feeling that alot of the debate is people talking past each other, but I also think there are real questions in there. For example: if the codon table is just a chemical fact, why does it feel so much like a lookup table? And if it is a genuine code, where is the decoder? The ribosome doesn't read a message the way a computer reads a file -- it's chemistry all the way down, or is it? I've also been wondering whether a distinction between recipe information (a sequence of operations) and blueprint information (a spatial plan) does any real work here, or just adds another layer of metaphor.

Anyway, here's what I'm after. I have a bit of money from an old patent and I'm thinking of funding a two-year, half-time postdoc plus a monthly workshop -- maybe ten of us, a mix of retired scientists and a couple of local philosophers. The goal is to actually make progress on something, not just produce another anthology. If you were advising us, which open questions in this DNA-as-code / genetic-information area should we prioritize, and in what order? I want the ranking defended: for each, why it's upstream of the others, how tractable it is for people who aren't running a wet lab, how neglected it is, and what kind of argument or evidence over the next couple of years would move it up or down the list. I'm also curious which alleged implications of the code metaphor -- for genetic determinism, for gene editing, for origin-of-life work, for the idea that organisms are blueprints -- actually follow from taking the code idea seriously and which are just rhetorical baggage. Don't give me a reading list or citations; I'm after the reasoning. And if you think the whole framing is a mistake, say so and tell me what question we should be asking instead.
