I have no training in astrophysics, game theory, or philosophy — I came across the dark forest hypothesis in a novel some years ago and it has occupied me ever since. What I want from you is a number, not a tour of the Fermi paradox, and I want the number taken apart so that I can see exactly where it comes from.

State your credence that the dark forest hypothesis is the correct explanation for our having detected no sign of any other technological civilization. Take the hypothesis as the following package, and stay with it rather than substituting a general discussion: technological civilizations are common enough, and durable enough, that in the absence of concealment the sky would look visibly busy; any civilization that announces its position becomes a target, because the intentions of strangers cannot be known in advance, because any party might at any moment undergo an unpredictable leap in capability, and because a first strike therefore confers a decisive and permanent advantage; survival above all else is the overriding preference of any civilization shaped by natural selection; and concealment is cheap enough to be the default. On those assumptions the silence is not a puzzle but an equilibrium.

Please give me a rough probability, with one line of justification for each, for the sub-claims the number rests on: (a) that technological civilizations are frequent and long-lived enough that the sky would be visibly occupied if no one were hiding; (b) that the chain of suspicion and the first-strike advantage really do hold between civilizations that may be separated by billions of years and by everything else besides; (c) that survival above all is a universal preference rather than a parochial feature of the one case we can inspect; (d) that concealment is both feasible and preferable to the alternatives. Then combine them, and tell me which single term the result is most sensitive to.

Two things I have not been able to settle, and I would like you to price them in. First, suppose the silence is structural rather than chosen: civilizations do expand, but in short bursts, so that the galaxy is usually empty and we are merely early, or in a gap between occupants. On that picture no one is concealing anything and the dark forest dissolves. Second, suppose the hypothesis is unfalsifiable in practice, since every absence of evidence counts in its favour and the detection of a broadcasting civilization would count against it; say whether that should lower my confidence at all, and by how much.

End with what observation or argument would move the number most, and in which direction.
