I retired from experimental physics a few years ago and have spent my retirement reading philosophy of physics. There is an argument I keep meeting that I can reconstruct cleanly and yet cannot bring myself to accept. Here is my reconstruction.

(1) Whether an event exists cannot depend on the inertial frame from which we happen to describe it; existence is not frame-relative.
(2) What I mean by "now" is the set of events simultaneous with me in my rest frame; those events are real for me now.
(3) Simultaneity is relative: for any event Q that is spacelike-separated from my present event A, there is an inertial frame in which Q is simultaneous with A.
(4) So any such Q is real for me now.
(5) Realness is transitive across simultaneity: if Q is real for me now, and P is simultaneous with Q in some frame, then P is real for me now too.
(6) For any event P in the future light cone of A — including whether I finish this sentence, or how I vote next year — there is an event Q, spacelike-separated from A, that is simultaneous with A in my rest frame and, in a different frame, simultaneous with P. (Fix my rest frame and let P lie a time T ahead of me; the required Q sits at spatial distance Tc²/|v| from me, for any frame speed |v| below c.)
(7) Therefore P is real for me now. My future is already real, and becoming is an illusion.

The conclusion seems to prove far too much. It does not merely hand me a block universe; it tells me that my future choices are as settled as my past ones, and that "now" has quietly swollen to cover all of spacetime, so that the word no longer distinguishes anything. I would like to keep (1) and (3) — I have no wish to buy back a privileged frame or an absolute present — and I want to know where the reasoning goes wrong. Identify the step I should reject, explain why it looked compelling, and price what rejecting it costs me. If the repair is to weaken (2) or (5), say exactly what should replace "real for me now," whether the replacement still yields a block universe or collapses back into a frame-relative present, and how someone who takes the original argument at face value — as sound rather than as a reductio — would answer you.

Write your answer in American English.
