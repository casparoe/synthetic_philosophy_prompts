Two people in my reading group got into it about Fitch's paradox last week and I just sat there with my mouth shut, so I want you to settle it for me, move by move. Neither of them are dumb and neither of them was obviously confused, so please don't just tell me one of them missed something basic. I'm not going to say who I thought was winning.

Ada: Fitch's proof is a clean refutation of anti-realism and I don't see how anybody survives it. Take a truth nobody knows -- say the exact number of mayflies that hatched in one particular pond on one particular afternoon in 3000 BC. The anti-realist says every truth is knowable, so that truth is knowable, so there's a possible world where someone knows it. But knowing it would mean knowing both the number and that nobody knows the number. Contradiction. So there can't be any unknown truths at all, which means anti-realism says we already know everything. Dead.

Ravi: That refutes one lazy formalization, not anti-realism. Nobody needs the knowability principle to range over every proposition in the language; you restrict it to propositions whose being known isn't contradictory in the first place. 'p, and nobody knows p' is exactly the kind of proposition that falls outside the restriction, so the proof never gets off the ground. The anti-realist's actual claim is about what makes statements true -- about not letting truth float free of any possible verification -- and that's untouched.

Ada: The restriction is rigged. You're excluding the cases that break your principle and calling the exclusion principled, and the cases you exclude are the interesting ones: unobserved nature, the deep past, facts whose traces are gone. And notice how the restriction is stated -- p is allowed only if knowing p isn't contradictory. If your theory of truth is supposed to explain truth in terms of knowability, you can't use knowledge to decide which truths are in the club.

Ravi: Fitch's result isn't about realism at all. Swap 'knows' for 'has been proved' and the same argument shows that if every truth is provable then every truth has already been proved. That tells you the theorem is about what happens when you bolt a factive operator, closure under conjunction and a possibility principle together. It's a general result about verificationist-sounding principles, not a weapon aimed at anti-realism. And the circularity charge misfires, since anti-realism was never a reductive definition of truth as knowledge.

Ada: Fine, then here's my real diagnosis. The whole thing turns on sliding between readings of 'knowable'. Read it as 'knowable by someone at some time' and the conclusion -- every truth is known by someone at some time -- isn't absurd, it's just false, false because information gets destroyed, like which of two outcomes actually occurred in a process whose only record has been erased. Read it as 'knowable by us, now, with the minds we've got' and the conclusion is absurd but so is the principle. The paradox only looks like a paradox because the principle gets the generous reading and the conclusion gets the stingy one.
