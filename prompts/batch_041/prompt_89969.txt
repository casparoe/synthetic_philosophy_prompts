I cover science and ideas and sit on my magazine's editorial board. A contributor has filed a long essay arguing that Canada is the more liberal of the two North American neighbours, and the editor wants to run it as our cover feature. The board is split: some think the claim is plainly true (single-payer health care, tighter gun laws, earlier same-sex marriage), others plainly false (the First Amendment, the Charter's notwithstanding clause, Quebec's language regime), and one colleague insists the comparison is ill-formed because liberal names two incompatible traditions — one about markets, one about personal freedom — that pull opposite ways; she would settle it instead by asking which country offers the wider range of life plans a person can actually adopt, counting social pressure as a cost and not only law. Please draft the memo I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete decision on the essay — run, run with conditions, or hold — give reasons the editor and the contributor could respond to, take the ill-formedness objection at its strongest, and say honestly what it would take to settle the Canada-versus-US question.
