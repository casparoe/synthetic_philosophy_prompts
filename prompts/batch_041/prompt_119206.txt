Next month it's my turn to run the discussion at our pub philosophy night -- 45 minutes, a dozen regulars, no slides, mixed bag: two engineers, a retired nurse, a guy who's read a lot of pop-science, my mate who just likes arguing alot. I want to do the Sleeping Beauty puzzle: fair coin, heads she's woken once on Monday, tails she's woken on Monday and then again on Tuesday, and she's given something so she can't tell the days apart or remember being woken before. I've been chewing on it for months and I still can't make up my mind whether she should say 1/2 or 1/3, and a few of them will have seen it online already, so I need to be careful.

Don't explain the puzzle to me, plan how I should run it. What has to be in their heads before the question even makes sense -- I think it's that she isn't unsure about any fact in the world, she's unsure which of the wakeings she's in, and I don't know if that's the right way to put it, or how to get them there, or what it has to come after. Which single case should carry the whole thing, and what does it have to have and not have -- I was going to use the coin and the drug, but I wondered about swapping in a case where I don't know whether I'm the original or a copy in an identical room, or a version where the coin isn't flipped until after the last waking; what does each buy me and what does it ruin? The wrong turns I'm expecting: 'it's 50/50, it's a fair coin, nothing that happens to her can change that', 'this is just Monty Hall again', 'it's definately 0 or 1, she just doesn't know which', 'just count the wakings and divide', 'the answer depends on whether you mean what she believes or what she should bet'. Where in the sequence do I head each one off, and is there one I should let them walk into on purpose? What should I leave out -- I don't want any maths past counting, and I can't define credence properly, do I even need to? -- and how do I say so without it looking like I'm dodging? And give me one question to ask at the end that tells me whether they've actually got the problem or have just worked out which answer I like.
