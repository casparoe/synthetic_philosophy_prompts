I am a novelist at work on a historical novel set during a period of failed political reform, and I have been reading Nietzsche's essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life, the second of the Untimely Meditations, because I suspect it describes exactly what my book is doing and doing wrong. The passage I keep returning to is the one where he sorts the ways a person or a culture can relate to the past into three modes and then presses hardest on the first, the monumental. As I paraphrase it for my own notes: monumental history belongs to the man who acts and struggles, who needs models and masters, and it works by drawing an analogy between his present struggle and some past greatness — and this procedure is inherently deceptive, because it can function only by overlooking the differences between epochs, by treating a fragment of the past as though it were a whole, and by presenting what it admires as something to be imitated. He adds that when this mode is taken up by the egoistic and the fanatical, it becomes a way of dressing up their own cause in borrowed splendor, and that it damages the past it uses by falsifying the past into an object of emulation. What I cannot decide is what kind of claim this is. Is he saying monumental history is epistemically defective, that it necessarily misrepresents the past — or only that it is dangerous when misused, and that the man of action has a legitimate need which antiquarian accuracy cannot satisfy? Does the essay anywhere allow that the need might be met without the falsification, or is the falsification simply the price of the need? I would also like to know how the other two modes bear on this: whether the antiquarian's piety and the critical mode's destructive judgment are meant as corrections or as further diseases, and whether Nietzsche thinks a culture can hold all three at once without any of them degenerating. Finally, tell me plainly which of the three my novel is actually in, given that any historical novel will make the past available for present needs — and whether that is a fault I can repair or a condition I have to write inside of. Please answer in American English, and end with a final line beginning Verdict: that names the mode in one sentence.
