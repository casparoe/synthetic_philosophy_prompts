Until a few months ago I treated Goodhart's law and specification gaming as two names for a single phenomenon, and then a metric I had a hand in designing went wrong at my workplace in a way I could not classify, and I have been trying to separate the two ever since. I would like you to construct a thought experiment that pulls them apart cleanly, with the verdicts generated by the structure of the case rather than stipulated in advance.

The design brief is as follows. The setting must be an ordinary workplace — a call center, a warehouse, a clinic's scheduling desk, a sales team, something of that order — with no artificial intelligence, no science fiction, and no agents more capable than a clever human. There must be exactly one metric M and one target T (the thing the organization actually cares about), and both of the halves below must arise from that same M and same T within the same organization; if the case only works because you quietly swap out M or T between the two halves, it fails.

First, I want specification gaming without Goodhart's law: one employee drives M up while T stays flat or falls, so the specification is gamed, and yet M remains sound evidence about T for everyone else in the organization — the measure has not been corrupted as a measure; it is only this one employee's number that is hollow.

Second, I want Goodhart's law without specification gaming: every employee optimizes M honestly and no one cheats, yet M's evidential value for T collapses, so that knowing an employee's M stops telling you much about their T.

The two halves should be identical except for a single variable, and the case should make that variable visible.

Then do the analysis. Name the one structural feature that flips the case from gaming to Goodhart, and explain why it produces the two different failures. For each half, state exactly what makes it gaming, what makes it Goodhart, and where a tempting but incorrect label should be refused. Close with an honest accounting: which feature is doing the real work, what becomes of both verdicts once that feature is removed, and how someone who insists that gaming and Goodhart are the same phenomenon under two names would attack the case — and whether that attack succeeds.

This is for a short piece I am writing on how to design metrics, so a case that simply asserts its conclusions is worthless to me; I need the conclusions to be forced by the causal structure of the scenario.

Please write in American English, and keep the scenario itself to a paragraph or two before you turn to the analysis.
