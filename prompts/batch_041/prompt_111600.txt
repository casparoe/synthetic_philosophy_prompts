I teach a single ninety-minute session on the Sapir–Whorf hypothesis for an adult-education course, Does Language Shape Thought? — an audience with no linguistics or psychology background, mostly retirees plus a few schoolteachers, and no appetite for the strong-versus-weak framing that the topic usually collapses into. I want a plan for the explanation, not the explanation itself. What is the order in which the ideas have to arrive for the guiding question to become answerable rather than rhetorical, and what depends on what — in particular, what has to be established about the very idea of thought before any evidence about language can bear on it? Which single example should carry the whole session? Where in the sequence should I intercept the misconceptions this audience will bring — I expect at least the reading of language shapes thought as you can only think what you can say, and I suspect a second one about translation being impossible across languages? And how, at the end, can I tell whether they have understood what the hypothesis actually asserts rather than merely been persuaded of some version of it?

One constraint on the carrying example. The standard illustrations — the Eskimo snow vocabulary, the Hopi treatment of time, the Russian blues — are either folklore or so familiar that the audience will recognise the punchline before I reach it, and I want an example they must reason through rather than recognise. So I have built a small artificial language for the session, Tavoshan, with two features: every declarative sentence obligatorily carries a particle indicating how the speaker came to know the claim (direct observation, inference, or hearsay), and it has a single basic colour term covering both what English calls blue and what English calls green. Tell me how to deploy Tavoshan — as the spine of the session or only as a closing test case — and what it must have and lack for the audience to grasp the difference between a language that makes a distinction obligatory and one that merely offers a word for it. Finally, tell me what I should openly refuse to settle in ninety minutes.
