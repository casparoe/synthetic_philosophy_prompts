ok so for our school papers april fools issue i want to write a satire and i want to do the Berkeley money thing we did in philosophy club

quick recap so you don't have to go looking: Berkeley (the "to be is to be perceived" guy) wrote this book called The Querist and the whole thing is just hundreds of numbered questions in a row, no answers at all, and the arguement running through it is that money isnt really wealth, its basically a ticket or a counter, and the actual wealth of a country is its land and labour and the industry of its people. two of them go like this: "Whether the four elements, and man's labour therein, be not the true source of wealth?" and "Whether money is to be considered as having an intrinsic value, or as being a commodity, a standard, a measure, or a pledge, as is variously suggested by writers? And whether the true idea of money, as such, be not altogether that of a ticket or counter?"

so: write me a Querist for today. same form — 30 to 40 numbered questions, every single line has to be a question, no answers, no paragraphs, no explaining anything, like a page and a half. voice: a made up guy, some clergyman-economist type who has just been put in charge of a small countrys new national bank and has read exactly one book about money and that book is The Querist. hes completely serious, never winks. target: the way everyone today talks about money like its a thing that is valuable in itself — the goldbugs, the crypto guys and their wallets, "money printer go brrr", people who think a rich country is one with a big pile of money in the bank, the financial literacy poster in my schools hallway, all of it. the funny part should be that he just keeps asking the obvious question that nobody asks.

rules: keep him consistent — if money is a ticket then he can never at any point slip into acting like gold is the real stuff, thats the whole point of him. at least 5 or 6 of the questions should be about specific modern things (a central bank balance sheet, an NFT, a savings account, student loans, inflation, a vending machine, whatever you want) so it doesnt just read as Berkeley with the serial numbers filed off. no winking, no jokes where the narrator knows hes joking. someone who has never heard of Berkeley should still be able to follow it, and my teacher who has heard of him shouldnt be able to say i got the position wrong. give it a title in the same spirit.
