here's the principle as i understand it so we're on the same page: at any moment your credence in some proposition A should already equal your expectation of your future credence in A. in other words, if you knew that in an hour your credence in A would be x, then your credence in A right now should also be x. it's a coherence constraint tying you-now to you-later — roughly, you shouldn't be able to predict that you'll disagree with your past self and still think you're the one who's right. van Fraassen is the name usually attached to it, and apparently he thought it falls out of plain conditionalization, but dont worry about the pedigree, i care about the content.
