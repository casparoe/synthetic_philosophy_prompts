I am a third-year PhD student in philosophy, writing a handout for a graduate seminar in comparative epistemology, and I need a diagnostic instrument rather than an essay. My students can already recite the standard inventories: that Nyāya counts four reliable means of knowing (perception, inference, comparison and testimony), that the Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsakas count six, the Prābhākaras five, the Buddhists two, the Cārvākas one, and so on. An instrument that merely sorted them by the number they accept would be useless to me. What I want is a decision tree that sorts the positions by something more basic: the principle of individuation a position is tacitly using when it decides whether some cognition has a source of its own.

My working hypothesis, which I would like the instrument to make visible, is that the familiar disputes about whether postulation (arthāpatti), non-apprehension (anupalabdhi) and comparison (upamāna) are genuinely separate means are at bottom disputes about which of five candidate criteria is load-bearing: (i) a difference in the causal instrument (karaṇa) that produces the cognition; (ii) a difference in the object (viṣaya) cognised; (iii) a difference in the structure or form of the resulting cognition; (iv) a difference in the kind of defeat or cancellation (bādha) the cognition is liable to; (v) whether the inventory is fixed by philosophical analysis of cognition at all, or is instead read off a canonical list whose authority is independent of such analysis. A school's answer to the counting question should come out as downstream of its answer here, and I want the tree to expose the places where that fails. A second axis cutting across the first is how a cognition's validity and its invalidity are established — whether either is self-established (svataḥ) or requires a further cognition (parataḥ) — and every leaf should carry a position on it.

Every node must be a concrete case that a student can answer without knowing which tradition the answer leads to. No node may ask how many means of knowledge one accepts, or whether one accepts some named means.

Use at least eight of these cases, more if the tree needs them: recalling where the pot was yesterday; recognising Devadatta as the man seen yesterday; judging that there is no pot on the table; inferring that Devadatta is outside because he is alive and not in the house; learning from a forester that a gavaya is like a cow and later applying that; accepting a reliable speaker's word about a matter no one can perceive, such as the fate of the departed; the same inference performed for oneself and set out for someone else; taking nacre for silver and afterwards discovering the mistake; the dream report; the cognition of one's own pleasure or pain; the meditator's or the omniscient being's direct awareness; the second-order cognition in which one knows that one is perceiving; doubt; and the cognition of a word's relation to what it means.

The tree must be able to reach at least these leaves: Cārvāka; Dignāga-and-Dharmakīrti-style Buddhist; Vaiśeṣika, which counts two means but still treats the Veda as authoritative; Nyāya; Sāṃkhya and Yoga, which share an inventory and may prove indistinguishable; Prābhākara Mīmāṃsā; Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā; Advaita Vedānta; and the Jaina scheme, which cross-classifies cognitions by direct and indirect access rather than by instrument, and so may resist the tree's own framing — if it does, say so rather than forcing it.

Return your answer as a single JSON object with exactly these fields: `organising_question` (one sentence); `case_index` (array of objects with `case_id`, `case`, `discriminates`); `root` (a node id); `nodes` (array of objects with `node_id`, `parent`, which is null for the root, `case_id`, `question`, `if_yes`, `if_no`, `turns_on`, and `separates`, an array of leaf ids); `leaves` (array of objects with `leaf_id`, `position_name`, `tradition_or_author`, `inventory` as an array of strings each giving a means of knowing in the school's own vocabulary with a two-or-three-word gloss, `individuation_criterion`, `validity_rule`, `case_verdicts` as an object mapping every `case_id` to a one-clause verdict, `path_from_root` as an array of strings of the form N3:yes, and `most_awkward_commitment`); `unoccupied_combinations` (array of objects with `combination`, `verdict` drawn from incoherent, unattractive or unexplored, and `diagnosis`); and `indistinguishable_pairs` (array of objects with `leaves`, `node_or_axis_that_fails`, and `why`). Every leaf must be reachable by exactly one path; nodes must be binary and acyclic; `if_yes` and `if_no` must each name either a node or a leaf. Use twelve to sixteen nodes and no more than eleven leaves, and give at least three unoccupied combinations and at least two indistinguishable pairs.

Write for a reader with no internet access: no citations, no reading lists, no dates, no quotations, and no appeals to what a particular commentator is said to have written. Where you attribute a view, use only the school or thinker names given above. Use British spelling throughout.
