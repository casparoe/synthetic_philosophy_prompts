I'm a novelist, and I'm writing a near-future book about a city that runs on a "low-impact AI." It proposes daily actions—bus reroutes, cooling-center hours, road closures, predictive maintenance—and a human impact auditor has to sign off. My protagonist is that auditor. I need a reusable decision procedure she can actually run, one proposal at a time, in about fifteen minutes, with no full theory of value and no ability to look anything up.

The city charter says the AI must "avoid side effects," but nobody has defined side effects or whose welfare counts. The AI's own metric is trajectory divergence from a no-op baseline, and the baseline depends on a "patience horizon"—how long you let the counterfactual run before you stop counting knock-on effects. I want the procedure to take that seriously instead of pretending the horizon is a neutral technical setting. It should distinguish intended effects, foreseen side effects, and effects that are constitutive of the action itself, and it should have a "value-underdetermination flag" that stops the procedure rather than fudging a number.

Give me the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it has to hand the decision to a person because it's a value call. My auditor signs off, and her boss can override only with a written reason, so say where that handoff goes. Then run it on three proposals: rerouting a bus to cut emissions while adding twelve minutes to poor commuters' trips; opening a cooling center during a heat wave that the model says will draw power from a hospital; and shutting off a neighborhood's water for a day to prevent a larger contamination event. At least one of those should come out in a way my protagonist hates, and tell me what the procedure cant do. Dont give me cites or a reading list—she can't Google either.
