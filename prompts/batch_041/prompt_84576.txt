Write a joke about Arrow's impossibility theorem for an audience of economists who know its conditions — unrestricted domain, weak Pareto, independence of irrelevant alternatives, non-dictatorship — but have never given a thought to the order in which pairwise votes are taken. The punchline has to turn on the mechanics, not on the familiar complaint that no voting system is fair: it should land on the fact that a rule can be formally non-dictatorial and yet let whoever fixes the agenda decide everything, because majority voting over three or more alternatives can cycle. Set it in a small ordinary committee — three people, three options — and spell out the preference profile inside the joke, so that the cycle is visible in what you give the audience rather than something they have to recall. No citations, no quotations, and nothing that depends on either.
