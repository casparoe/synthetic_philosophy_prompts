I have a set of verdicts about which higher-level, symbol-ish items do real causal work, and I want to know whether a single principle captures them. The backdrop is the careenium picture: an arena of countless mindless simmballs whose collisions implement something that can be described as wanting things and acting. I take the point of that picture to be that the higher-level description is not automatically a fiction, so I am not moved by the thought that the simmballs do all the pushing anyway. I grant that the simmball-level dynamics are complete, and I still think some higher-level items push the substrate around and others do not. Here are my verdicts.

1. Thermostat. The dial setting genuinely causes the furnace to fire. The thermostat's believing the room is cold does no causal work, and belief-talk there is a ladder to be thrown away.
2. Chess engine. The engine's wanting to control the center is a real cause of the move it makes, and not merely a summary: you cannot recover the explanation at the transistor level without losing it.
3. Ant colony. When a quorum of scouts reports a good site and the colony migrates, the colony's decision is a real cause of the migration, though no ant decided anything.
4. Wheat market. That the market decided wheat should be cheaper is a summary and not a cause; but the price falling is a cause of farmers planting less.
5. Gene. The gene's interest in being copied is bookkeeping for selection, not a cause of anything.
6. Species. Trying not to go extinct causes nothing; selection filters, it does not push.
7. Motorway jam. A jam that propagates backwards is a real thing and a real cause of the braking behind it, even though each driver responds only to the car ahead.
8. Intention. An intention to raise one's arm is a real cause. Baseline.

The two pairs I cannot square are 2 against 5, both intentional idioms applied to mindless things, and 3 against 4, both aggregates whose decisions are nothing over and above the parts. I have been working with the principle that a higher-level item is a cause when it is multiply realizable and manipulable, but the gene's interest is multiply realizable and manipulable (change the copying rate and the effect follows), so that principle keeps 5, which I will not accept.

So: is there a principle that keeps all eight? If the only principle that keeps all eight is baroque, give me the simpler one and say which verdict it forces me to drop. State the principle precisely, with each clause tied to the case that forces it; then say what it predicts for two cases I have not ruled on, an immune system that remembers a pathogen and a language model that believes Paris is in France; then say plainly which of my verdicts you take to be the mistake.

Lead with your bottom line in your first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, no restating of my cases or my question, and no survey of what various philosophers have said.
