You are a philosopher of technology and a political theorist with a strong command of science and technology studies and of the analytic literature on the moral and political agency of artifacts. I need a teaching instrument, and I would like you to reason carefully before producing it. Begin with a brief working note to yourself: list the genuinely distinct things a person might mean in asserting that a device, system, or design is political, and then prune your list of senses that are only rhetorical or that collapse into one another. Do not show me that note as a polished essay; use it, then build the instrument.

I am assembling a diagnostic handout for a graduate seminar on technology and politics. What I want is a decision tree, in the form of branching questions, that carries a reader from a small set of starting commitments to a definite position on whether and in what sense artifacts can be political, together with a taxonomy of the resulting positions.

Build it in the right order. First lay out the logical space: state each diagnostic question, the answers it allows, and the combinations those answers permit. Only then locate actual positions inside that space. Treat the questions as the primary object and the named views as occupants of the space, not the other way round. Where a combination of answers is left unoccupied, say so and diagnose the gap in a clause: is no one there because the view is incoherent, because it is coherent but unattractive, or because it is merely unexplored?

Each branch node must be a question a reader can answer without knowing which position it leads to, ideally by reacting to a concrete case rather than by endorsing a label. No node may simply ask whether the reader thinks artifacts have politics. Each leaf must carry the name of a position — a conventional label if one fits, otherwise a descriptive name, or the word unnamed — plus one sentence naming that position's most awkward commitment. Add a short key that says, for each leaf, what a reader would have to give up in order to leave it.

The angle I care about is the ambiguity of the political claim itself, so make your branch questions discriminate among at least these candidate senses: that an artifact is political because of its provenance, because of its downstream effects, because those effects are necessary or systematic given the design rather than incidental, because they were intended by designers or sponsors, because the artifact redistributes power among people, because it shapes what users can see as an available option or can bring themselves to want, and because the politics belongs to a wider socio-technical ensemble rather than to the artifact considered alone. Also let the tree distinguish a hard from a soft reading of technological determination where that distinction does real work. Where two leaves turn out to be indistinguishable given the cases available, flag the pair openly rather than papering over the gap.

Use the following cases as the diagnostic material for the branch nodes, and choose the order of nodes so that the earliest questions split the space most efficiently:

- a set of planter barriers at a junction whose spacing admits cars but not buses;
- a software feature that would make a product accessible, shipped off by default and reachable only by users who already know to look for it;
- a bicycle that can be unlocked and located only through a proprietary phone application, so that it cannot be repaired or ridden by anyone without the application;
- a predictive triage tool retrained every quarter on data that already encode the priorities of past policing;
- a building entrance deliberately made too narrow for a wheelchair, in a building also used as a polling place;
- a household appliance fastened with a tamper-resistant screw that keeps owners out of its internals;
- a syringe engineered so that it cannot be reused;
- a messaging application that is end-to-end encrypted by default but whose maker retains the ability to switch that default off in a later update.

You may add at most two further cases of your own, and only if they are needed to separate leaves that the eight above leave entangled. Keep the tree to roughly a dozen branch nodes and between six and nine leaves.

Do not supply citations, a reading list, or references of any kind; refer to positions by description and I will attach the sources myself. Write the whole thing as a self-contained handout that a reader could work through alone.
