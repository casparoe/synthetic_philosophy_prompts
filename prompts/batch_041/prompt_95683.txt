Hey, so I'm a backend engineer and I've gotten kind of obsessed with the mechanistic interpretability stuff — papers about finding "features" and "circuits" inside models, sparse autoencoders, all that world. I dont have any formal philosophy background but a few of us at work have a little reading group going, and I've been trying to write down what I actually think about whether these models have real internal representations, because I keep nodding along to people in the group who I'm pretty sure disagree with each other. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) When someone finds a circuit that seems to detect some feature and it fires when that feature is present, the network genuinely represents that feature — it's really in there, not just a story we're telling ourselves to feel good about. (2) But I also think there's no privileged level at which the "true" representation lives. Whether you say the model represents a cat, or a whisker-detector, or some direction in activation space, is relative to the task and the lens you pick; asking what it "really" represents with no purpose specified seems ill-posed to me. (3) I buy the superposition story: the model packs way more features than it has dimensions, so the features are real but smeared across overlapping directions rather than sitting in tidy single neurons. (4) The test of whether something is really represented is causal — if I can steer the model by messing with that representation and it then does what I'd expect, I've shown it's in there. (5) And I think interpretability work should be judged on whether it lets you predict and control behavior, not on whether it hands you a satisfying "explanation" that just feels right.

What I want is a consistency check before I present this to the group on Thursday. Which of these actually pull against each other, which ones only look like they do, and how bad is each real conflict? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, tell me for each what would have to give and what the cheapest fix would be, and then say which single revision would clean up the most. And please dont just tell me they're all in tension — I suspect a couple of these are totally fine together and I'd rather you discriminate instead of flagging everything. Plain American English please. End with one line that starts "Verdict:" and names the one commitment I should drop or reword.
