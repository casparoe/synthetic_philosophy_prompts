Write a short story set in a near-future society where teletransportation is routine and the reigning public philosophy is Parfitian: people are taught, in schools and courts and grief counseling, that personal identity is not what matters, and that what matters is psychological connectedness and continuity held with the right kind of cause, so that a copy deserves the same concern as the original. The technology has a wrinkle the society has had to legislate around. Transport does not destroy the traveler: it scans her and assembles the copy at the destination, but the original is placed in a reversible stasis and kept — aware, immobile — until the copy returns. Follow a single household across the years after one such transport, in which the returning copy and the kept original both claim the same marriage, the same debts, and the same name, and in which the protagonist, a sincere believer in the official view, learns that accepting that identity does not matter and living as though it does not are different things. Dramatize the Parfitian conclusion rather than stating it, and leave the metaphysics unresolved. Do not name or quote real philosophers.
