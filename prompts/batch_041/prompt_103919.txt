I'm writing on the regress problem and want to test the standard trichotomy — foundationalism, coherentism, infinitism — against a real episode rather than a toy case: the foundational crisis in mathematics, from Russell's paradox in Frege's system through Principia, Hilbert's program, Brouwer's intuitionism, and Gödel's incompleteness theorems. Read each program as a proposed regress-stopper: which horn of the trilemma does it actually occupy, how is the demand for a stopping point supposed to be discharged, and where do its own resources fail to discharge it? Then run the analysis in the other direction, asking what the episode shows about the trichotomy itself. Two things I especially want pressed. First, whether the regress of inference rules (the one Carroll's Tortoise presses on Achilles) is the same regress as the regress of beliefs, or whether the trichotomy cuts differently in the two cases. Second, whether Gödel's results refute foundationalism, vindicate coherentism, or instead expose a presupposition the three horns share — that justification must terminate in something self-warranting. Say clearly which horn nobody in the episode actually occupied, and whether that vacancy reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect.
