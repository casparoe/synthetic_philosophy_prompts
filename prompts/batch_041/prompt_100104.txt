For the past two years a colleague and I have run a small reading group that began on rationalist blogs and has drifted steadily towards AI systems, and next month we are hosting a two-day workshop titled Tool or Agent?. The room will be split roughly down the middle: half are people who ship models for a living and have little patience for philosophical throat-clearing, half are the reading group, who will feel cheated if nothing is left unsettled. I have been asked to give the five-minute opening remarks that set up the disagreement without resolving it.

The framing I want those remarks built around — one I have not seen put this way, and which I would like you to commit to rather than merely mention — is this. The tool/agent distinction is best cashed out by asking where the objective sits relative to the action loop, not by asking what the system is like on the inside. A tool has its objective supplied and evaluated from outside the loop on each occasion of use; an agent carries an objective inside the loop and across time. The consequence I care about is that one and the same system can be a tool at one timescale and an agent at another — a tool at the level of a single completion, something much closer to an agent at the level of the training run that produced it, which is a search over parameters pursuing an objective across many episodes — and that the cases worth arguing about are precisely the ones where the timescales disagree. Make that concrete enough for the practitioners to push back on and open enough that the reading group does not feel fobbed off. Please write the remarks; keep them to about five minutes, and do not cite or name-drop anyone, since I would rather spend the time on the argument than on the pedigree.

After the remarks, give me a brief structured summary in the form of a JSON object with the following fields: thesis (one sentence); timescale_claim (the version of the claim you actually committed to, stated precisely enough to be attacked); left_open (array of the questions you deliberately did not settle); for_practitioners (the single line most likely to land with the half of the room that ships models); for_reading_group (the same, for the other half); jargon_glossed (array of objects with the fields term and gloss); and biggest_risk (the most likely way these remarks fail). Write in British English throughout.
