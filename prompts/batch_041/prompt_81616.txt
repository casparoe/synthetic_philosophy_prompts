A friend in my reading group has been reading about infinite populations in ethics and he's now convinced the whole area is basically just Hilbert's hotel. His version goes like this: a world with infinitely many people is a hotel with infinitely many rooms, all of them full; if you want to add a billion more happy people you just shuffle everyone along by one room, so the billion check in and nobody loses anything — adding lives is free. So, he says, creating new people in an infinite world costs nothing, an infinite world plus a billion happy people is no better than the same world without them, and all the usual population-ethics tangles just dissolve once you're infinite; the value of an infinite world is fixed by how many people are in it and nothing else. He's using this to argue that infinite ethics is actually much simpler than finite ethics and that people who think infinities make ethics impossible are just confused. My gut says the hotel is carrying far more weight than it can bear, but I can't put my finger on why. (I did some maths at uni so the bijection side of it doesn't scare me, it's the ethics half I'm shaky on.)

Can you lay out the correspondence properly — the hotel, the rooms, the guests, the manager, the shuffling, the "nobody lost a room" fact — against the world, the lives, the extra people, whoever is supposed to be doing the deciding, the rearrangement, and the corresponding ethical claim — then go through where the mapping breaks, and tell me which breaks actually matter for what he wants to conclude and which are just scenery? The thing I most want nailed down is what the picture smuggles in about what makes one world better than another. It feels like it quietly assumes that value is a headcount, and that the bijection is a neutral bookkeeping move rather than a substantive claim about what matters, and I suspect the "nobody is worse off" bit is doing the real work rather than the infinity. Does his conclusion survive if you keep the hotel but compare two worlds with the same people and different welfare, or if you treat a world's value as a limit rather than a sum? (Another friend said it's more like a divergent series you can't add up, and that if you could, rearranging the terms would get you any total you liked — I don't know if that's a better analogy or just a different one.) And if the hotel is the wrong picture, what would be a better analogy for the infinite case — or is the honest answer that there isn't one?
