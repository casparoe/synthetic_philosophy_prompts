I run a platform team at a mid-sized software company, and I'd like help with something that has been nagging at me since a colleague told me we had "solved the frame problem." I have no formal philosophy background, so where my framing is wrong, please correct it rather than answering inside it.

Here is what we built. Every service in our system consumes events from a shared bus, and each service ships a manifest that declares, for every event type in our vocabulary, whether it consumes the event, ignores it, or reacts only under stated conditions. A build-time check fails if a manifest is incomplete, so no service can silently ignore an event; when we add a field to the order schema, the linter prints the fourteen services that must now make an explicit decision. Adding a new event type requires a design review and a versioned rollout, so our event vocabulary is closed and centrally owned, and each service has a known, documented responsibility.

As I understand it, the frame problem in its original setting is that when you write down the effects of an action you must also write down everything that does not change, and that this list is unmanageable. Our manifests look like exactly that list, written down, machine-checked, and maintained by the people who own each service. So my question is whether the frame problem is at bottom this bookkeeping burden — in which case discipline plus an explicit relevance declaration is a full solution — or whether there is a residue that no amount of discipline removes. If there is a residue, I want to know what it consists in and what would have to change in my own system for it to show up, given that our vocabulary is closed and our responsibilities are fixed. (I have also been told that the "real" frame problem is about relevance rather than change, which sounds to me like the same point said differently, but I may be missing something.)

1. "What my picture gets right" — about 100 words, so I can tell you are not correcting a straw man.
2. "Where my picture breaks" — 300 to 400 words.
3. "The residue, stated plainly" — 200 to 300 words, in ordinary language, without terms I would have to look up.
4. "The strongest case that this is just bookkeeping, and why it fails" — about 200 words.
