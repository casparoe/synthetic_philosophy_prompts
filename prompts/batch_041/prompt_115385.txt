I'm in a small population-ethics reading group that a professor here runs, and she is fantastic at giving verdicts on cases and hopeless at saying what principle she's using, so a few of us have started writing her verdicts down. She puts everything on a scale where 0 is a life not worth living at all, 1 is a life that is just barely worth living, and 100 is a life going about as well as a human life can go. Four verdicts so far. (1) The usual repugnant-conclusion pair: ten billion people all at 100, versus a quadrillion people all at 1 — a hundred thousand times as many people, each scraping by on a life that's worth having but not by much, and with a thousand times more welfare between them once you add it all up. She says the ten billion is better, and says it like it isn't close. (2) The ten billion at 100, versus the same ten billion at 100 plus another ten billion people at 2, where the original ten billion are completely untouched and nobody's life changes at all. She says the second one is better. (3) The ten billion at 100, versus the same ten billion at 100 plus ten billion people at −2, whose lives are miserable and not worth living. She says the first is better. (4) The ten billion at 100 plus ten trillion people at 1, versus the ten billion at 100 plus exactly one person at −1000, a life of unrelieved agony. She says the first is better — she'd rather have the ten trillion marginal lives than have one person in agony.

Now she owes us a verdict on a case I don't think her four verdicts settle, and I want to know what she'll say before she says it. Two possible futures, and nobody who exists in one of them exists in the other. Future A is the ten billion people at 100 again. Future N is a quadrillion people, each with a life that is comfortably worth living but nothing like A's — call them 3s on her scale: not hungry, not in pain, a few friends and small pleasures, no great loves, no achievements, nothing you'd call flourishing. Which one does she say is better?

What I want out of this: her verdict on N, the rule she actually seems to be running (and which of the usual candidates her four verdicts already kill off — plain total welfare, average welfare, some kind of critical-level view), at least one other rule that fits all four of her earlier verdicts just as well but comes out the other way on N, and the case I should put to her next that would seperate those two rules. If her four verdicts genuinely dont fix N, tell me that instead, and tell me what she'd be committing herself to either way.
