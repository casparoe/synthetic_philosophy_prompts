Here's the setup: basically all the image analysis in our project — we ID and count diatom species off microscope slides, thousands of them per site — is done by a segmentation/classification model that a postdoc built and trained before he left the lab. I can run it fine, I can read the code, I can stand up in a lab meeting and explain step by step what the pipeline does to each image. My advisor says that counts as understanding and I should stop hand-wringing. But last week the model started spitting out weird counts on a new batch and none of us could tell whether it was a real biological signal or a bug, because honestly none of us could rebuild the thing from scratch or say why it puts the boundaries where it does — and I got this cold feeling that we've been writing 'we found X' in papers when what we actually mean is 'the black box said X and we believed it.' I know that sounds dramatic.

Is she right? Is there actually a real difference between leaning on a tool you can open up and inspect versus one you can't, or between being able to recite the steps and understanding why they're the right steps?
