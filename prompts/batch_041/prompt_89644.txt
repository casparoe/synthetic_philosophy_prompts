Okay, so imagine Einstein didn't die in 1955 and instead got to hang around and read about the last decade or so of tech and labor debates. He's read his own 1949 Monthly Review essay Why Socialism? again and he's been following stuff like: platform cooperatives where drivers or delivery workers own the app; proposals for a data dividend paid out to citizens from taxes on big tech; the idea that training data and compute are the new means of production; universal basic income as a response to automation; algorithmic management and worker surveillance; and the whole debate about whether LLMs are going to make most labor obsolete or just shift it around. He's also seen the old socialist calculation debate get rebooted with people saying AI could finally make central planning work.

Given all that, what would his updated views be? I dont want a generic he'd support workers answer. I want you to predict his positions on specific things: (1) would he back platform co-ops and data dividends as a modern form of social ownership, or would he think they're just reforms that leave the core problem intact? (2) How would he react to the idea of public AI utilities or nationalized compute? (3) Would he see algorithmic planning as a solution to the bureaucracy problem he worried about, or as a nightmare version of it? (4) What would he say about UBI -- is it a socialist measure or a way to pacify people while capital keeps the machines? (5) Where would he break with contemporary leftists, and where would he break with contemporary capitalists?

Also, try to flag which of his 1949 commitments would have to be revised or abandoned if he took these new developments seriously. And be concrete -- don't just say he'd want democracy at work, say what he'd actually recommend for, like, a ride-hail app or a foundation model. Assume he's had a year to read up, so he's not totally naive about how the tech works, but he's still Einstein, not a Silicon Valley VC.
