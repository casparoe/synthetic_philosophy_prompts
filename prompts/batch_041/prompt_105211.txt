I am drafting a section on the liberal paradox and want to test the coherence of my own position before committing to it. Here is what I hold, stated as precisely as I can:

(1) The liberal paradox is a genuine impossibility theorem: no social choice rule can jointly satisfy unrestricted domain, weak Pareto, and minimal liberalism.
(2) I accept minimal liberalism in this form: for each individual there is at least one pair of social states over which that individual's strict preference is socially decisive, irrespective of others' preferences.
(3) I accept weak Pareto: if every individual strictly prefers one state to another, then the former must be socially preferred to the latter.
(4) I reject unrestricted domain. The domain of admissible preference profiles should exclude what I call meddlesome preferences—preferences over another person's private assignment that are not grounded in any interest of the preference-holder. An example would be a preference that a neighbor's private life conform to a religious code, held not because the neighbor's conduct affects the preference-holder.
(5) I think rights are best modeled as side constraints on the feasible set, not as decisiveness conditions inside the social welfare function.
(6) I therefore think the liberal paradox is not a deep objection to liberalism; it is an objection to a particular formalization of rights.
(7) I think the private/public distinction, though context-sensitive, is objective enough to underwrite the domain restriction in (4).
(8) I think a social choice rule can satisfy both liberal rights and weak Pareto for all admissible profiles, provided the domain restriction in (4) is principled rather than ad hoc.

Please audit this package. Which commitments are genuinely in tension, which are only apparently so, and how severe is each tension? Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad. For each one, say what would have to give and what the cost of giving it up would be. Finally, tell me whether there is a single revision that would resolve the most while doing the least damage to the overall position. Do not simply recite the standard escape routes; I want to know where this particular package is unstable.
