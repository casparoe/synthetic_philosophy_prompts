I am assembling materials for a graduate seminar on intention and practical reason, and I want to gauge how robust Kavka's toxin puzzle is to structural variation. The original case, as I shall present it, runs as follows: a billionaire will transfer a large sum to you tonight if and only if you form, tonight, the intention to drink a mildly nauseating toxin tomorrow morning; once the money has been transferred, no further condition applies, and tomorrow you will have decisive reason not to drink. The puzzle, put crudely, is that practical reason appears to demand that you form an intention whose content you would then have decisive reason to abandon.

I have drafted five variants. Please rank them from the one that best preserves the distinctive pressure of the original to the one that least does so, and assign each a preservation score between 0 and 1, where 0 means the variant is no longer a puzzle at all and 1 means it is fully as puzzling as the original. For each variant, give a brief justification and identify which feature of the original it does or does not retain.

(i) The Prediction Variant. The billionaire does not condition payment on your intention. Instead, she has an unbroken record of perfect accuracy in predicting your future conduct, and she transfers the money tonight if and only if she predicts that you will drink the toxin tomorrow. You know all of this.

(ii) The Erasure Variant. As in the original, you are paid tonight for forming the intention. You are then given a drug that erases every memory of the bargain while leaving your intentions and dispositions untouched. Tomorrow you discover in yourself a settled intention to drink an unfamiliar vial, with no recollection of why.

(iii) The Escrow Variant. The money is deposited tonight upon your forming the intention, but the deposit is released to you only if tomorrow you both drink the toxin and, at the moment of drinking, still intend to drink it.

(iv) The Hazard Variant. Exactly as the original, except that the toxin is lethal with probability one half, and the sum offered is correspondingly larger.

(v) The Harmless Variant. Exactly as the original, except that the toxin is replaced by a harmless saline injection, so that carrying out the intended act costs you nothing beyond a pinprick.

Finally, state which single variant you regard as the most philosophically productive, and say in a sentence what it exposes that the original does not.
