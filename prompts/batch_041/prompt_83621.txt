I have no academic background in this area, but I have been reading about the debate over whether neural networks "have representations," and I keep running into a difficulty I cannot resolve on my own. When a paper says a model represents the concept of a cat, or represents the truth of a sentence, the claim seems to be that some pattern of activations stands in for that thing — that the network is carrying something that is genuinely about the cat. But the network is just a large array of numbers, and nowhere inside it is there a picture of a cat or a symbol for one; nothing there resembles what it is said to represent. So the only way I can make sense of the talk is as a convenient metaphor, a shorthand for predicting behavior — and if that is all it is, then mechanistic interpretability is just a story we tell about the numbers, with no more claim to being correct than any other story that predicts the same outputs. I tried to test the idea myself: if I train two networks on the same task and their internal wiring comes out completely different, then there seems to be nothing the representation really is — it is just whatever the interpreter happens to find. Yet people do argue that some interpretations are better than others, which I cannot square with any of this. Where is my mistake, if there is one? Please tell me how confident you are in your diagnosis, and single out the part of it you are least sure about.
