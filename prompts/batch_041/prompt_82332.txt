My thesis, roughly: the whole debate about whether RL agents can suffer is stuck asking whether the agent has the right *stuff* (biology, some architecture) or the right *behavior* (avoidance, whining). I want to say it's neither — it's about whether the reward signal is self-addressed. Specifically, I claim an agent is a candidate subject of suffering only if its reward signal enters the agent's own world model as a state it can represent, predict and act on; i.e. it has a representation whose content is roughly "this is going well / badly *for me*". I've been calling this the loop-back condition. The distinction I lean on: reward as cause vs. reward as content — a signal that merely causes behavior vs. a signal that is itself represented by the agent. Standard agents (even model-based, even with intrinsic motivation, even with a value head) fail loop-back, because the reward is used by the *trainer* for credit assignment, not by the agent as a fact about itself.

The part that will get me in trouble: loop-back is architecturally cheap. Agents with an explicit homeostatic drive vector they monitor, or self-models used in planning, are already halfway there. So the suffering-risks crowd isn't wrong to worry, they're just worried about the wrong agents for the wrong reason. And I want to end by saying the real question isn't "does it suffer" but "what does it take to *engineer* loop-back, and is doing that permissible" — some kind of valence engineering ethics.
