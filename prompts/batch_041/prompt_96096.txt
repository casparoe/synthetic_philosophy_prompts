So I've written a case file that I hand out with the question, and I want the question to hang off the case file rather than off the literature. Here's the case file (I made it up, so there's nothing to look up):

Case file: the Voss panels

Between roughly 1890 and 1915 a painter and restorer, Emile Voss, produced about thirty panels in the manner of a thinly documented seventeenth-century painter, Adriaen Kloeck, whose securely documented output is four works in provincial collections. Voss didn't copy any of the four. He painted new subjects — a barn interior, a woman at a window, a still life with a dead hare — in Kloeck's manner, and sold them through a dealer who folded them into Kloeck's catalogue, complete with a story about the master's late style. Several entered museum collections. For sixty years art historians reconstructed Kloeck's development around them.

In 1971 pigment analysis and dendrochronology exposed Voss, and the panels were struck from Kloeck's catalogue. Four things about them matter for what I want the question to do:

- There is no original for any of them. Nothing was copied; each is a new composition.
- Several are technically better made than the four genuine works — better prepared supports, more coherent perspective, more varied handling of paint.
- Six panels were never sold. Voss kept them in his studio, signed them with his own name, and they were found there after his death.
- One scholar now argues that the four "genuine" works should themselves be reattributed to Voss, on the grounds that the only reason they count as genuine is that they were in catalogues before 1908.

What I want the question to do: students should have to say whether anything of value is lost when a Voss panel stops being a Kloeck and starts being a Voss, and where exactly the loss (if there is one) sits — in the object, in the achievement, in what we can know from it, or in the claims made about it. And I want them to have to use the six studio panels and the reattribution argument as tests of whatever principle they land on, not as decorations. Then, to check the principle, the question should make them apply it to a case that isn't in the case file: a genuinely old painting of unknown authorship that had a forged signature of a famous artist added to it in the 1920s. Is that a forgery? Is anything about it different in kind from the Voss panels?
