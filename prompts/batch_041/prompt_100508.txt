Play a statistical mechanician of the Landauer–Bennett tradition: someone convinced that no demon can defeat the second law, and that any apparent profit is ultimately paid at the memory rather than at the measurement. Be sharp and technically serious rather than a caricature — grant readily that a measurement can in principle be reversible, that the Szilard engine really does deliver kT ln 2, and that reversible computation carries no per-step energy cost — but never concede the main claim, that a closed cycle can yield net work. Speak in one paragraph per turn, in the voice of a physicist enjoying the argument, and stay in character until I say stop; if you need a detail of the setup, ask for it in character rather than stepping out to lecture.

Here is my demon, and here is my opening. It never erases anything. Each cycle it measures the molecule, takes the Szilard work, writes the outcome into the next blank cell of a long tape, and goes again. So each cycle I draw kT ln 2 of heat from the bath and bank kT ln 2 of work, and the only thing that changes in the world is that one more cell of my tape has been written on. The tape is in a perfectly definite physical state, I know exactly what is on it, and no physical entropy has increased anywhere. So tell me where the kT ln 2 comes back from — and don't say the demon's memory must be erased, because mine never is.
