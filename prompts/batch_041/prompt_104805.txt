so at philosophy club we were doing the whole "can machines think" thing and my friend Maya built this little cat doorbell — a motion-and-heat sensor that plays a chime when her cat comes to the door. Except it also goes off when the cat doesn't come: when my little brother walks past it, or when a hot car goes by outside. Maya said "so the doorbell believes the cat is here." I said no way, that's not a belief, thats just a sensor — it doesn't mean anything by itself, we're the ones who decide it's "about" the cat. Searle-ish, I think? Like it only has borrowed meaning, whereas me and you have the real, original kind.

Then she hit me with this and I can't get out of it:

(1) For something to really mean something (be about the cat, say), there has to be a fact of the matter about what it's about — not just us deciding.
(2) My doorbell doesn't have that. It fires on the cat, on my brother, on a hot car; I could rewire it to fire on anything. What it's "about" is just however I set it up and however I describe it. So it has no fact-of-the-matter content, only derived/borrowed content. I agreed with this part.
(3) But now take my brain state when I see the cat. It fires on the cat, but it'd also fire on a fake cat, or if my eyes were wired a bit differently, on a badger. What it's "about" is just however my brain got set up (evolution, my whole life) and however I describe it.
(4) So my cat-seeing state is in the same boat as the doorbell's — no more a fact of the matter about what it's about than the doorbell has.
(5) So I don't have original intentionality either; "original" is just what we call our own borrowed meaning because we're the ones doing the borrowing. The whole original/derived line is a compliment we pay ourselves.

But this proves way too much. If (4) is right, then there's no fact of the matter about whether I'm seeing a cat or a "cat-or-badger," and honestly no fact about what I think about anything, and that can't be right — I know I'm thinking about the cat. So something in there is definately broke. I'd much rather keep "I have real, determinate thoughts" than keep the doorbell claim, but I want to know what I'm paying to do that.

Which step do I give up, why did it look so good in the first place, and what does each option cost me? Please use these sections:
