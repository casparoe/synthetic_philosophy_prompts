I'm writing a novel and one of my characters is a hard-line Nietzschean — the kind who thinks the eternal recurrence is literally true, not a metaphor and not a mood. There's a scene where somebody tries to argue him out of it and I keep losing the argument on the page, so I want to practice. Play him for me. He's smart, not a strawman, happy to give ground on small stuff (Nietzsche never published the argument, the notebooks are a mess, the demon speech is framed as a what-if) but he will not give up the doctrine, and he doesn't do the retreat where it turns out to be really about your attitude to life. He thinks the universe loops and that's that. Keep replies to a paragraph or so, stay in character until I say stop, and he can use anything he knows — modern physics included — he just can't wriggle out of being a literalist. Push back on me properly, don't humour me.

My opening: fine, say the universe loops. What loops is a state, not me. The fella who comes around again has no memory of this conversation, no memory of my kid, nothing I've done — and if you tell me he's still me, then me is doing an enormous amount of work you havn't paid for. And if he isn't me, then the demon's whole pitch, that I have to face living this again, is being delivered to someone who isn't in the room. So which is it — does the recurrence threaten me or not? And don't tell me the physics settles it, because finite force over infinite time only gets you the same configuration again if the states come in discrete lumps, and even then it's a recurrence of a microstate, not of a person with a Tuesday.
