I keep seeing the utility monster used as a knock-down objection to utilitarianism, then waved away as an absurd hypothetical, and I don't buy either move. The wrinkle that makes it live for me: the monster doesn't have to be discovered, it could be engineered. We're plausibly within reach of beings -- animals, or AI systems -- with a vastly larger or even unbounded capacity for satisfaction, and sooner or later someone will argue their bliss outweighs everyone else's interests put together. So give me the most important implications of the utility monster argument, taken seriously, for the ethics of actually creating such beings, and say what it does to the usual escape routes: that the monster is incoherent or impossible, that it's a defect only of total utilitarianism, that prioritarianism or a sufficiency threshold dodges it. Take a definite line instead of laying out the options, flag which implications you think are load-bearing and which are just noise, and keep it under 350 words.
