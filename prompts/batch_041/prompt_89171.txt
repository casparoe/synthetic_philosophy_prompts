So this is basically a complaint about my reading group. We've been meeting for about a year on "AI and society" and I swear every session ends with the same three people relitigating the same three analogies. Priya says AI is the printing press — a general-purpose cognitive tool that gets cheap and ubiquitous, and the real story will be the new institutions it forces into existence (she keeps saying the printing press didn't just spread ideas, it created things like copyright and peer review and a whole public sphere that didn't exist before, and that that's the part everyone forgets). Marcus says it's nuclear weapons — a handful of actors, catastrophic downside, and the whole game is arms control and building a taboo on the worst uses; he points out the bomb got used twice and then not again for the next eighty years, which he reads as proof that norms can actually hold. And Dana says it's the Industrial Revolution — the interesting part is labor and displacement and the political reaction, Luddites and unions and the messy century it took for any of the gains to be shared. Nobody has moved an inch in a year and I'm definately starting to think we're not even arguing about the same thing.

Here's my hypothesis and I want you to tell me if it's confused. I think the analogies are mechanism claims in disguise: when Priya says printing press she's really claiming the binding constraint is institutions and culture; Marcus is claiming it's coordination among a few powerful actors; Dana is claiming it's the labor market and political economy. And because none of them ever says which mechanism they think dominates, each can always find a feature of their own case that 'fits' and a feature of someone else's that 'doesn't', so the argument never bottoms out. Or maybe it's worse than that and the analogies aren't predictive at all — they're doing rhetorical work, marking urgency or resignation or legitimation — in which case the disagreement is really about what stance to take, not about what will happen.

So diagnose this for me. What kind of disagreement is it actually — a factual one about the historical cases, a disagreement about which features of a case are the relevant ones, a disagreement about what an analogy is even for, or just three people answering different questions under one word? Use what each side would actually say, not a survey of the analogies. Then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a clearer specification of the mechanism, some variable we could actually estimate, a distinction between the predictive and the rhetorical uses of analogy — or whether you think no progress should be expected here and why. If my framing is confused, or if the honest answer is just "you're all doing politics with history," say so plainly, dont humor me.
