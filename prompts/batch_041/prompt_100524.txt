Been chewing on this one for a while: is the axiom of choice even the sort of thing that can be true or false? My current position is that once you've got the independence results, "is AC true?" is really a question about which set-theoretic universe we've decided to work in — closer to the parallel postulate than to a claim about the world. So the honest reply to "is AC true" is to ask what you need it for. I think I believe this. What would count against it?

I want the defeaters sorted by how decisive they'd actually be, not just a list, and be straight about which of them an AC-skeptic and an AC-partisan would each have to concede, not only the ones that suit their side. In particular: does the fact that AC is equivalent to things like Zorn's lemma, or every vector space having a basis, count as evidence that AC is already baked into how we reason (so the "free choice" picture is wrong), or does it just mean those results are hostage to the same decision? Also tell me honestly whether this view has quietly made itself unfalsifiable — if every argument for or against AC just gets redescribed as "a choice of universe," has anything been said at all? And is the parallel-postulate analogy doing real work or am i just borrowing its prestige, given that non-Euclidean geometry eventually turned out to describe something?

What I'd personally find most persuasive against me is a case where the "which universe" framing can't even state the disagreement.
