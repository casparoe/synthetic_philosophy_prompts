Ok so I'm taking this poli theory module this term, democracy and its alternatives, and something happened in my flat last night that I can't let go of. It's definately not an essay question so I don't really know how to look it up.

The setup: there's a campaign on campus to stop electing the student council and start drawing it by lottery instead — 20 names picked out of the registration list, those 20 run things for a year, no elections. Our seminar spent a whole week on sortition so both my flatmates, Maya and Dev, knew what they were talking about. Neither of them was being dumb and neither was just repeating a talking point, which is why I can't referee it myself. Here's how it went.

(1) Dev went first. He said the campaign's own fine print gives the game away. The proposal lets anyone who gets drawn decline — you just say no and they draw again. So it isn't a lottery over the student body, it's a lottery over the subset of students willing to do the job, and the people willing to do the job are exactly the people who'd have run in the elections anyway. They've smuggled the self-selection back in through the exit door, he says.

(2) Maya said that only looks like a problem if you think the point of a lottery is to get a miniature of the student body. She says it isn't. The point is that nobody can campaign. No posters, no manifestos, no slates, no favours traded, because nobody knows in advance who's going to be in the room. And that survives the exit clause completely — even the willing ones who get in didn't get in by campaigning.

(3) Dev said that if not-campaigning is the whole story then the exit clause is doing nothing except shrinking the pool, and you'd get the same result by drawing from a list of volunteers, which is basically what we already have minus the pretence. And he doesn't believe her anyway: if the council comes out 19 engineers and 1 philosopher, the fact that none of them campaigned doesn't stop it being a rubbish stand-in for the student body. So she does care about the mirror, he says, she just won't admit it.

(4) Maya said fine, mirroring matters a bit, but he's got the burden of proof the wrong way round. An election is designed to filter for a particular kind of person — someone who wants the job, someone good at asking strangers for votes, someone with a faction behind them — and that filtering is the defect, not the feature. A lottery's only flaw is that it doesn't filter at all, and not filtering isn't a flaw unless you can say what the filter is for.

(5) Dev said no filter cuts both ways: it also won't filter out the person who'll treat it as a joke or never show up, and she still hasn't said why a random 20 beats 20 people who've at least shown they care enough to turn up. And he thinks the line about wanting the job being disqualifying is circular — that wanting the job is bad is exactly the sort of thing elections let us decide, so she's assumed her conclusion and called it a principle.

Then they both looked at me and I said something useless about it being a rich discussion.
