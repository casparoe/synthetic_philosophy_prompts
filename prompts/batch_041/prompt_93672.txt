I've been lurking in a prediction-market betting forum where the same fight restarts every few months and nobody ever moves. The argument people keep making goes: if you and I start out with the same beliefs about some event and neither of us likes risk, then the only reason you'd take the other side of my bet is that you know something I don't — and the very fact that you're willing to bet is itself a signal, so I should back out. Which means bets that exist purely because of private information shouldn't happen at all, and anything that does trade must be about something else: different risk appetites, honest differences of opinion, hedging. Some people say fine, then the only real question is which of those assumptions fails in actual markets. Others say the whole thing is a toy, since billions change hands every day. A third group says both sides are confused, because the result is about a model rather than the world, and the interesting part is what it implies about prices — do they end up revealing the information anyway, and what is "the market is efficient" even supposed to mean: prices are accurate, you can't beat them, or information gets into them? I have a hunch, probaly wrong, that half of this is people meaning different things by "efficient" and the other half is a real disagreement about whether the proof is about markets or about a picture of markets. So what kind of disagreement is this really — verbal, empirical, two different arguments welded together, or people answering different questions under one name? And is there anything that would actually move it, or is the thread doomed?
