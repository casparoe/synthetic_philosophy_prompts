I'm in a reading group that's supposed to be about epistemology but has basically turned into an argument about one thing: the dogmatism paradox, which a friend of mine brought up at a bar and which I've now been annoying everyone about for two weeks. Here's the argument as I understand it, in case it helps you see what I want made fun of. If I know that P, then any evidence that shows up against P has to be misleading evidence -- because I know P, so P is true, so whatever seems to tell against it can't be telling the truth. Which means the stronger my position is, the more entitled I am to wave away the very things that look like they threaten it. And the twist that actually broke my brain: the argument is itself something you can know, so once you know the argument is right, you're entitled to ignore arguments against the argument. My friend who does Bayesian stuff says fine, whatever, you just have a prior of 1 and nothing can move it, and I said that's not the same thing at all. Then someone in the group said you can lose knowledge by gaining evidence -- you know something today, you learn a little more tomorrow, and now you don't know it anymore, even though nothing you learned was false and nobody lied to you -- and I've been chewing on that ever since. So I don't want an explainer or a rebuttal, I want to see the whole thing sent up.

Title it something like "Stop Learning Things" or "The Confidence Advantage."
