Okay so I've been lurking on LessWrong and the old Overcoming Bias archives since like 2011, and I keep getting into these arguements in the comments about "indirect normativity" — you know, the move where instead of trying to directly figure out what's right, you say the right thing is whatever we'd all end up agreeing on if we were wiser and better informed and more coherent, and then you act on some proxy that you hope tracks that. I want to turn this into a decision tree I can actually post, so people can figure out which position they secretly hold instead of just repeating the slogan at each other.

Here's what I want: a tree whose root is a commitment almost everyone in this fight shares, where every branch node is a yes/no question about the idealization, phrased so someone can answer it without already knowing which position it leads to. Please don't just list "Kantian constructivism, ideal observer theory, CEV, etc" and call it a taxonomy — build it from the questions first, then drop the names in at the leaves. This is for a forum post so keep it readable.

Some distinctions I really want on the tree, because I think these are the ones that actually seperate people:
- Whether there's a fact of the matter about what we ought to do that holds even under idealization, or whether the idealization is what makes it true. I.e. is the idealized reasoner discovering something or creating it?
- What "idealized" even means: perfect info plus perfect logic (input-side) versus "whatever they converge on" (output-side). I'm pretty sure these come apart and people conflate them constantly.
- Whether idealized reasoners are guaranteed to converge. If they aren't, the view either goes indexical — each agent's own idealization is authoritative for that agent, which smells like relativism — or actualist — the real historical convergence is what counts, which seems to make moral progress depend on luck.
- The epistemic constraint: can you be obligated to do something you couldn't possibly know you're obligated to do? Say no and you get pushed into needing a proxy, which is basically the indirect-normativity move. Say yes and you've got the horn where there are moral facts nobody could ever access.

I'd like at least these as leaves: ideal-observer theory, a Kantian-style constructivism, the coherent-extrapolated-volition / indirect-normativity family, plain moral realism where idealization is just a heuristic for finding a procedure-independent truth, an indexical/relativist option, a deflationist "no fact of the matter, the idealization is a useful fiction" option, and a non-ideal-theory option that just denies the whole setup. But don't feel locked to those names — if the questions generate a leaf that doesn't have a name yet, mark it "unnamed" and tell me what it would commit you to.

Use at least two concrete cases to force the sorting, not just abstract questions. Something like: a pill that makes you reason flawlessly for 24 hours — are its outputs binding on you, and are they binding on the person who didn't take it? And: two agents who are each perfectly rational, fully informed, and still disagree — what does your view say about them? If your view can't even describe that case, that's informative, say so.

Also flag any two leaves the tree can't actually separate with the questions you've got, instead of pretending they're distinct. And for any combo of answers that nobody seems to occupy, tell me whether that's because it's incoherent, just unattractive, or simply unexplored.

Last thing: tell me how confident you are in the tree as a whole, and single out the one branch or leaf you're least sure about — like where a real philosopher would probably say I've mislabeled it.
