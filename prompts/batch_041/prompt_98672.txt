I'm putting together a session for a reading group on precommitment -- Ulysses tying himself to the mast, someone taking Antabuse before a party, a parliament entrenching a clause it can't easily repeal, that kind of thing -- and I want a decision tree rather than a list of isms, because the usual soft-paternalism vs hard-paternalism axis doesn't actually capture where the people in the group keep seperating.

So build me the tree. Start from something everyone in the group already accepts -- like 'sometimes it's rational to arrange things now so that a later you can't easily undo them' -- and make every branch node a question that splits the remaining positions. I want the questions phrased as cases or yes/no claims a person could answer without already knowing which label they're about to land on. Leaves should be named positions, or 'unnamed' if you think nobody actually holds that combination, each with one line on its most awkward commitment.

A few distinctions I specifically care about, because they're the ones people keep collapsing:

- whether the person who precommits and the person bound by it count as one agent or two. If two, does the earlier one get to boss the later one around, and on what authority? If one, what's left of the idea that precommitment is a constraint at all rather than just a plan you're allowed to change?
- whether the binding force comes from the earlier self's will, the later self's will (maybe a hypothetical one), or from nothing about wills at all -- just the external mechanism, the fact that the mast holds.
- whether the device has to make the act impossible (ropes, Antabuse) or only costly (a fine, a reputational hit), and whether that difference tracks any real disagreement or is just engineering.
- whether precommitment only makes sense for akratic or divided agents, or whether a perfectly unified, rational agent could still do it.
- and the one that always derails the discussion: whether a constitution is the same animal as Ulysses' ropes. Is the people binding itself, or is one generation binding the next? Those feel like different structures to me and I suspect the tree has to fork on it.

Please dont' just hand me back the paternalism axis dressed up. Use at least these cases somewhere in the nodes: the mast, the Antabuse, an advance directive from a dementia patient who later protests, and an entrenchment clause. If two positions on your tree end up indistinguishable by any question you can actually ask, say so instead of pretending a branch separates them. And if there's an empty slot -- some combination of answers nobody occupies -- tell me whether that's because the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or genuinely just unexplored.
