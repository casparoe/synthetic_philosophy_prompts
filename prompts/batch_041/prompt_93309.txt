hey — so my decision theory reading group has this dumb tradition where everyone writes something for the end-of-term zine (it's called The Precommitment Device, we're very proud of ourselves), and this year I got the "parody of a genre" slot. I want to do an FAQ / product page for a fictional insurance company — let's call it Acausal Mutual, tagline is up to you — selling coverage against acausal threats. Background in case you want it: the idea is that an agent can be threatened or bargained with by another agent that has no causal channel to it at all, because both agents' decision procedures are correlated or predictable. Friendly version is two agents in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma each reasoning that their own choice is evidence about the other's, so cooperating becomes rational (Hofstadter called that superrationality); scary version is a much more powerful agent that predicts your decision procedure and threatens you unless your algorithm does something, where the thing being punished is your algorithm rather than your body. CDT people say such threats are noise since your choice can't cause anything on the other side; the updateless / functional-decision-theory people say your algorithm is exactly the thing that gets evaluated, so you'd better behave accordingly. There's also this defence move I half-remember where you swap your real values out for some cheap stand-in goal that the threatener is content to see you pursue, so the threat lands on the surrogate instead of on anything you actually care about (I think it gets called "surrogate goals" but dont hold me to the name).

What I want: an FAQ, roughly 700-800 words, numbered Q&A, deadpan brochure voice — no winking, no "in this hypothetical world" throat-clearing, the insurer is just a company doing business. The jokes have to be load-bearing, i.e. come out of the actual structure of the problem. So: what exactly the insured event is when the loss is defined on your decision algorithm rather than on any causal chain; why ordinary extortion is excluded (too causal, we're not that kind of insurer); adverse selection when merely buying the policy changes your algorithm and therefore changes which threats you're exposed to; why they can't write policies for CDT clients (there's nothing to insure, so instead they offer a free rider that pays out under precisely the conditions where it would be needed); a surcharge or discount depending on how widely your decision algorithm is instantiated, since more copies means more hostages but also more acausal leverage, and they can't tell which effect dominates so they charge you for the variance; a deductible denominated in utils that requires your utility function to be bounded and cardinally measurable, with a polite note about declining unbounded lexicographic clients; and an exclusion for policyholders who've already adopted one of those surrogate goals, since then a claim can't be distinguished from a loss to the surrogate. If you can work in a subrogation clause where the insurer reserves the right to recover from the threatening superintelligence by committing to punish non-payers acausally, please do. A satisfied-customer testimonial would be great too, ideally from a decision algorithm rather than a person.

Aim it at someone who's done the reading and will wince if you get the decision theory wrong, but keep it readable for someone who hasn't — if a term of art shows up, it should get defined by the insurance logic itself, not by a footnote. And please don't let it drift into a list of blog posts or papers to go read, it's just the brochure. thanks!
