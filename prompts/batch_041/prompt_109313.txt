Hey — second-year PhD here, working on conditionals. I've got a draft essay for a workshop next month and I'd rather have it torn apart now than in front of people. Could you give me comments? Not line-edits — I want to know whether the central move is sound, whether it's actually interesting or just a relabelling of the standard story, and where the whole thing collapses.

The gist. The essay starts from the familiar triviality proofs: assume a conditional has a probability, that this probability is given by P(B|A), and that the conditional is a proposition sitting in the same algebra as A and B, and you can derive that P(B|A) = P(B) for every A with P(A) > 0, which is obviously absurd. Standard reactions are either to ditch the equation or to ditch the idea that conditionals express propositions. My line is that both reactions misread what the proof proves. The proof needs the algebra to be closed under the conditional-forming operation — the conditional has to be a member of the very space whose probabilities it is supposed to track. I call this the closure reading. On that reading the theorem is really about a fixed point: you can't have a single space be both the object and the instrument of the update. And that smells like a general fact about any semantics that identifies the meaning of an expression with a conditional probability — I gesture at probabilistic treatments of 'because', of epistemic 'might', and of generics, mostly to show the shape recurs.

The punchline I want: triviality doesn't show the equation is false, it shows a conditional can't be a member of the space it's conditioned on, so the equation has to be stated as a constraint across a hierarchy of spaces rather than within one. Then I claim this is a version of the old point that you can't give the semantics of a language in that language itself — a Tarskian echo, though I try not to overplay it.

What I want from you: (1) is the closure reading a genuine diagnosis, or does it just redescribe the assumptions the standard proof already makes explicit? (2) does the hierarchy move actually save the equation, or does the same triviality re-arise one level up — and if so, exactly where? (3) is the "general fact" claim too cheap, i.e. am I basically saying "no self-reference" and dressing it up in probability notation? (4) is there any case where the closure reading predicts something different from the standard readings, because if there isn't, I worry the essay is unfalsifiable and therefore not worth the paper. And tell me which section you'd cut and which one I should push harder on. Don't worry about references, I'll seperate those out myself.
