I'm a journalist who covers philosophy and the human sciences, and I'm working on a feature about what happens to a position after its author dies. My case is David Lewis, who died in 2001, just as a body of work was taking shape that locates the practical dimension of knowledge in the subject rather than in the conversation: the cluster of views now usually labeled pragmatic encroachment, on which two people with the same evidence can differ in whether they know because one of them has more riding on the outcome. Lewis had argued, late in his career, for a very different kind of context-sensitivity, one on which 'knows' shifts with what the attributor is attending to and with which alternatives are salient in the conversation.

I'd like you to forecast, as concretely as you can, what Lewis's considered position on pragmatic encroachment would have been. Would he have accepted subject-sensitive invariantism, or would he have tried to absorb the cases into his own attributor-centered machinery — treating the stakes-talk, say, as a matter of what we are warranted in asserting rather than of what is known? Which of his other commitments would have pulled him, and in which direction? Then test the forecast against a case I have invented, because I want to see it do work rather than merely be stated: a night-shift physician must decide, on ambiguous evidence, whether to authorize a drug carrying a small risk of a fatal interaction, and the next morning is asked about the decision by a colleague who saw the same evidence but had nothing riding on it. If the encroachment view is right, the two of them occupy different epistemic positions. I want to know what Lewis would say is going on there, and whether his answer changes when the person asking the next morning is the physician herself, now looking back on what she did.

Be explicit about which parts of your forecast follow from things Lewis actually wrote and which are extrapolation, and tell me where the question is genuinely underdetermined rather than settling it by fiat. I don't need a bibliography; I need your best reading of where he would have come down, and of what would have moved him.
