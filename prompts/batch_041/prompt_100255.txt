I want to practice a live exchange about weakness of will, so I would like you to argue a position rather than describe one. Play a committed Socratic intellectualist: a philosopher who holds that no one ever acts against their own all-things-considered judgment at the moment of action, and that every apparent case of incontinence is either a change of judgment or a failure of knowledge. Stay in character until I say stop. Give the position its full strength. Your character may concede that people often act against their long-term interests, and against judgments they formed earlier, and may refine distinctions under pressure, but must never concede that a person can act, clear-eyed and in the act, against the judgment she holds at that very moment; when the exchange forces the issue, reach for the resources of the position instead of abandoning it. Write in the first person, one paragraph per turn, no stage directions, and if you need a fact about my case, ask for it in character. After each reply, and only there, step outside the role to append a JSON object with the fields "stance" (your position in one sentence as it stands after the turn), "concession" (whatever you granted this turn, or "none"), "crux" (the single claim you will not surrender), and "counterquestion" (the question you put back to me). American English throughout, and no meta-commentary outside that object.

Here is my opening move. Consider a smoker I will call D. At 8 p.m. she judges, all things considered, that she should not smoke tonight, and can recite her reasons in order. At 10 p.m., mid-cigarette, she still judges, all things considered, that she should not be smoking; she reports the judgment as no less vivid or endorsed than before, and says she is smoking anyway. She is not confused about the facts, has not forgotten the reasons, and does not report a change of mind. So either your view must call her report of her own judgment mistaken, and I want to know on what evidence, since by hypothesis she is sincere, articulate, and unmoved; or it must redescribe what she is doing as something other than acting against her judgment, in which case I will press you on whether weakness of will survives as anything more than a term of reproach. Which is it, and what work is the word judgment doing that lets you have it both ways?
