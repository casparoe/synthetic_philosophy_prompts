Ive been trying to write up a view I keep calling lexical threshold negative utilitarianism and I could use a critical read of the argument before I show it to anyone.

Heres the gist. Below some severity line, pleasures and pains just add up against each other the way a normal consequentialist would say they do — a mild headache is worth a nice afternoon, fine. Above the line, the pain is protected: no amount of pleasure, however large, buys it off. So the view isnt plain negative utilitarianism (suffering always trumps) and it isnt plain utilitarianism (everything is commensurable on one scale). The threshold is doing all the work.

Then the part Im less sure about. I dont want the threshold to be a fixed intensity, like any pain above 7/10 is protected, because intensity numbers dont track what actually makes a life go badly, and it makes the view hostage to a dubious psychophysics. So I want to make the threshold relative to the subject: a pain sits above the line when its worse than the best pleasure actually available to that same person. On this version, someone whose capacity for enjoyment has collapsed — depression, chronic illness, the last stretch of a long life — has a lower threshold, so pains that would be tradeable for someone else become lexically protected for them. I like this because it captures something about how deprivation makes even small pains matter enormously, and because it keys on what is available rather than what is wanted.

Two things nag me. First, if below the line pains and pleasures are commensurable and above it they arent, is the view even coherent, or am I stapling two incompatible value theories together and hoping? Second, the threshold attaches to individual experiences, but experiences come in bundles — ten sub-threshold pains that together are worse than any one protected pain. Does the lexical rule apply to the bundle? If yes, I think I get a sorites problem where enough trivial pains become untradeable; if no, the view says a person can be made utterly miserable by a sequence of perfectly tradeable pains, which is exactly what I was trying to rule out.

What I want from you: where is this confused, not just underdeveloped? Is the relativity move doing any work or is it ad hoc? Does the view collapse into one of its neighbours once you push on it, and if so which one? And is the bundling worry fatal, or does it just mean I owe an account of how the threshold gets applied? Also tell me if the depression example is carrying more rhetorical weight than the argument supports.
