I am a high school junior, and next month my school board votes on a policy that would require every student to lock their phone in a pouch for the entire school day starting in the fall, with no review date and no provision for changing course. I have five minutes at the meeting to argue that the board should adopt it differently. My case is Deweyan: a policy about how students learn is a hypothesis rather than a settled fact, the ends we aim at are properly held as ends-in-view and revised in light of what follows from them, and a school is genuinely democratic only insofar as the people who undergo the consequences of a decision take part in forming the problem, gathering the evidence, and judging the outcome. Write the speech. Please build it around one concrete proposal, namely that the policy be adopted as a single-semester experiment whose success criteria are fixed in advance and whose continuation is decided by a joint panel of students, teachers, and board members, since on the Deweyan view that structure is not a courtesy but the only way the board can be said to be inquiring at all. The room is mixed: three board members who want the vote finished, two teachers who have actually read Dewey, and several hundred students who will tune out anything that sounds like a textbook. I cannot claim the policy will obviously fail, appeal to students being right simply for being students, treat the majority vote as the end of the matter, or defer to expert judgment alone, and I do need to concede that if the evidence goes against us, we should accept it. About five minutes, written to be spoken aloud.
