Me and a couple of people in my epistemology reading group have been going round in circles on this and I want an outside verdict. The puzzle started out concrete: suppose I've actually checked, and I know — not just believe, know — that my neighbor's dog is harmless. I've watched it around kids for years, the vet backed it up, and so on. Then a stranger I've got no particular reason to distrust sincerely tells me the dog mauled a child last week. Here's the argument one of us built, and every line looks fine to me until the conclusion. (1) Knowledge is factive: if I know the dog is harmless, then the dog is harmless. (2) Given that the dog is harmless, the stranger's report is in fact misleading evidence — it points away from how things really are. (3) A belief that runs on misleading evidence is a belief heading away from the truth. (4) Rationality is a matter of proportioning belief to the truth of the matter, so a rational agent shouldn't be moved by evidence that's misleading. (5) So I shouldn't let the report lower my confidence at all; I'm entitled to shrug it off and go on believing the dog is harmless. (6) Nothing above turned on dogs or strangers in particular, so the general moral is: once you know something, you may rationally dismiss any later apparent counter-evidence to it. Which is clearly too much — it would make knowledge self-sealing and kill learning from experience outright.

The obvious move is to say "but knowledge can be defeated, so you might stop knowing once the report comes in," and I get that, but as far as I can tell thats just refusing (5) by fiat rather than showing me where the reasoning slips, and I'd rather understand the slip than just pick a step to deny. I'd defend (1) and (4) to the death if I had to; (5) is the one I keep wanting to reject, though I honestly can't tell whether that's because it's wrong or because I hate where it leads.

So: which step or steps actually fail, why did they look so airtight in the first place, and what does it cost me to give each one up? And if the honest answer is that the argument is valid and it's really a question of which premise I can most cheaply abandon, then say so and tell me what I'm paying. Write it in British English, please.
