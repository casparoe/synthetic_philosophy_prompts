Hey, I'm a third-year PhD in philosophy of science (physics undergrad, so I know the physics but not really the phil-of-religion literature) and a friend forwarded me this pop-sci blog post on the fine-tuning argument. I genuinely can't tell how much of it to believe, and I'd rather not embarass myself by repeating it at a seminar. The gist is:

'The fine-tuning argument starts from the fact that the fundamental constants of physics — the strength of gravity, the cosmological constant, the strong force — sit balanced on a knife's edge. If any of them had been even a hair different, life could never have gotten started, and the odds of them landing in that narrow life-permitting window by pure chance are so astronomically small that no reasonable person would call it luck. So the constants must have been dialed in by a designer. The main scientific alternative is the multiverse, but that just pushes the question back one step, since whatever generates the multiverse would itself have to be fine-tuned. And critics have an answer anyway: we shouldn't be surprised to find ourselves in a life-permitting universe, because we couldn't possibly have found ourselves in any other one. That's the anthropic principle, and once you see it, the whole argument is really just the old design argument wearing a lab coat — and the anthropic principle defuses it.'

Something about this reads as too neat, like it's winning the debate by definition rather than by argument. I'm definately inclined to think the argument is weaker than its boosters say, but I also suspect the post is pulling a fast one somewhere. Can you go through it roughly sentence by sentence and sort it into: (1) stuff someone who actually works on fine-tuning would happily sign off on, (2) stuff they'd wince at but might let slide, and (3) stuff that's just wrong? I especially want to know whether 'the odds are astronomically small' is as innocent as it sounds, whether the anthropic-principle reply actually refutes the argument or just changes the subject, and whether it's fair to say the whole thing is 'really just the design argument.' And finally: does the post's punchline — that the anthropic principle defuses the argument — actually follow from anything it got right, or is that conclusion doing work the premises don't support?
