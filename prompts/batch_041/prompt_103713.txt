I am assembling a small set of jokes for a graduate seminar on Cambridge philosophy between the wars, and I want one that will actually land for someone who has read Frank Ramsey's "Epilogue" carefully — the piece he read to the Apostles in 1925 under the title "On There Being No Discussable Subject," published only after his death. I do not want a generic philosopher-walks-into-a-bar joke. The punchline should turn on the content of Ramsey's actual position: that the meaning of life is not a further fact waiting to be discovered but a matter of the perspective from which the picture of the world is drawn; that in his picture the foreground is human-scale and the stars are drawn small, like coins, and that their size is no ground for humility because they cannot think or love. I would also like the joke to fold in the Ramsey test for conditionals — the procedure of adding the antecedent to your stock of beliefs and seeing whether the consequent follows — as though one could run that test on a conditional of the form "if my life were meaningful, then…". The joke should be funny to someone who knows both ideas; someone who knows only one should not come away thinking I have confused two different Ramseys. Paraphrase his views in your own words rather than quoting him, and do not rest the joke on dates, titles, or biographical detail beyond what I have supplied. It should be short enough to tell at a seminar without a five-minute setup.
