ok so I have to hand in a short position piece for my social choice reading group and I'd like a consistancy check before I do, because I have a feeling I've talked myself into something that doesn't actually hold together. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Strategy-proofness is the thing I care about most in a voting rule. If the honest vote is naive and the smart vote is a misreport, I basically don't want the rule — even if in practice hardly anyone bothers to vote strategically.

(2) Whenever there's a Condorcet winner, the rule should elect them. Majority cycles are a pathology and tracking majority will is the whole point of having a rule at all.

(3) The rule shouldn't play favourites. Voters should be treated symmetrically, and so should alternatives — no built-in advantage for a particular bloc or a particular candidate.

(4) I know about Gibbard–Satterthwaite. I read it as saying that no non-dictatorial rule is strategy-proof, and since I'm obviously not proposing a dictatorship, I take it to be a problem for other people's proposals rather than for mine.

(5) Anyway the unrestricted-domain thing strikes me as a bit of a red herring. Real electorates are basically one-dimensional, left to right, and I'd bet real profiles are single-peaked more often then not. So the theorem's hypothesis barely ever holds in the cases I actually care about.

(6) And I'm happy with lotteries. If it's determinism that's forcing the bad result, just randomise — a coin flip between two rules, or a properly randomised rule. Randomisation obviously gets you out from under a theorem about deterministic rules.

(7) Besides, VCG-type mechanisms are strategy-proof and nobody calls them dictatorial, so strategy-proofness clearly isn't as hopeless as the GS crowd make out.

(8) I also take the revelation principle at face value: we lose nothing by restricting attention to truthful direct mechanisms, so a theorem about strategy-proof direct mechanisms is a theorem about all mechanisms.
