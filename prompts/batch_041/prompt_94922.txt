I have been told that consequentialism faces a difficulty known as cluelessness: because we cannot know the long-run consequences of our actions, the theory cannot tell us what to do. I suspect the difficulty is smaller than it sounds, and I would like to know whether my way of handling it is sound. Uncertainty is nothing new for a consequentialist, who maximizes expected value; the only novelty here is that the probabilities are poorly estimated. And whatever I do, my influence on the distant future must be smaller than my influence on the near future, since causal influence decays with time and distance. I therefore propose to restrict my deliberations to effects falling within the next fifty years, treat everything beyond that as negligible, and reason by expected value inside that window. How reliable must my predictions be before consequentialism becomes a usable decision procedure, and is fifty years the right horizon to draw, or should I extend it? Please answer in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
