My best friend is getting married in a few weeks and I'm doing the best man's toast, and I'm stuck on how to pitch it. Half the room is our old philosophy department — we met in a grad seminar on Plato's Laws and we've spent ten years drinking together — and the other half is his parents and their church friends, who are teetotal and, I think, a little braced for a boozy best man speech. So I want the whole toast built on what Plato actually says about drinking in book 2 of the Laws, as I remember it: that wine is a god-given gymnasium for self-control, that you shouldn't touch it before your eighteen, that young men should only take it in moderation, and that past forty you're allowed to drink more freely because it loosens you back up and makes you young again — but only at a well-run party with a sober president in charge, since drink is what shows you who someone really is. I want that to do real work in the speech, not just be a bit I quote at the top and then drop. The tricky part is it has to land for the philosophers without making the teetotal half feel judged, and it has to actually be about the couple rather than a lecture on the Laws. Five minutes, warm, funny where it can be, and it should end as a toast to them. Can you write it?
