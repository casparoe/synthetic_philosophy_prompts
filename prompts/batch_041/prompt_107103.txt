Ok so here's my situation. I'm a retired ecologist — 34 years on insect behaviour, mostly social wasps, plus a long detour into slime molds — and since I hung up the field boots I've been reading alot of philosophy of mind. I've gotten fixated on the four-rung ladder of minds that Dennett popularised: the Darwinian creature (hardwired, natural selection did the thinking), the Skinnerian one (operant conditioning, learns from consequences), the Popperian one (can run the trial and error inside its own head before it acts, so it can be wrong in private), and the Gregorian one (gets its extra power from tools, words, external stuff — named after Richard Gregory, or so I'm told). I want to use this as a teaching tool for a little reading group I run, but every time I try it falls apart on the weird cases, and I suspect the ladder is missing a rung.

So: please act as a philosopher of mind who actually knows the biology, and think carefully — this is the kind of thing where the obvious answer is wrong. Before you build anything, spend a bit of space brainstorming the diagnostic questions you might use, then throw the bad ones out, then build the thing.

What I want is a decision tree / sorting quiz that takes some agent — a real organism, a colony, a machine, whatever I hand it — and sorts it onto a rung, with each branch node being a question I can answer just by watching the system, not by already knowing which rung it's on. No fair asking "is it a Popperian creature?" as a branch. Something like "if you remove the reward, does the behaviour extinguish, or does the animal just try something new?" — that sort of concrete test.

Cases it has to handle, because these are the ones that keep embarrassing me: a bacterium swimming up a sugar gradient; a rat in a Skinner box; a crow that drops stones into water to raise a floating treat; a human toddler; a termite mound (the mound, not the termites); a slime mold navigating a maze; a thermostat; a chess engine; and — this is the one I care about most — a current large language model chatbot. Some of those I'm sure about, some I'm definately not, and I'd bet at least one of them doesn't fit any rung.

Two more things. First, I think there's a missing rung and I want you to test the idea rather than just accept it. My candidate: a creature that is individually stupid but gets its cognitive power from a structure it builds and then lives inside — the mound, the web, the pheromone trail, the written archive, the codebase. The structure holds the information; the individual just reads and writes to it. Call it whatever you like, but tell me whether it's genuinely a distinct rung, or whether it's just the Gregorian rung with the word "tool" stretched too far, and where on the ladder it would go if it's real.

Second, build the tree from the logic first and the names second. Lay out the questions and which combinations of answers they allow, then place the named rungs in that space, then note which combinations nobody occupies — and say for each empty slot whether you think it's empty because the combination is incoherent, because it's possible but nothing evolved or got built that way, or just because nobody's looked. If two rungs can't be separated by any question I could actually run, say so plainly instead of papering over it. And please don't give me a reading list or citations, I can't use them and I'll just feel bad about it.
