I'm in a second-year historiography course and there's an argumentative move that keeps coming up and I have no idea what to call it.

Here's the shape of it. I write in an essay that the Armada got scattered by weather nobody could have planned for, so a good chunk of how that turned out is just luck. My seminar leader says fine, but if the sea had been calm the English still had the better guns and the Dutch still had the ports bottled up, so Spain loses anyway. Or I write that a provincial rising in 1848 fizzled because one garrison changed sides on a Tuesday, and someone says the deeper social conditions meant some version of the same regime was coming regardless, later maybe, by a different road. It's the horseshoe-nail rhyme with the arrow pointing backwards.

So the move is: a particular chancy event gets demoted from cause to mere detail, on the grounds that if it hadn't happened, the same outcome would have arrived anyway by some other route. What bugs me is that nobody ever has to produce that other route. The bare possibility that there could have been one gets treated as enough to cancel the one that actually happened. And it's the exact reverse of the chaos-theory line everyone else in the seminar is fond of (tiny causes, huge effects) — here the claim is that the big outcome is strangely insensitive to which tiny cause shows up.

My supervisor says it's just the counterfactual test; a friend says it's overdetermination; neither feels right, since overdetermination is two causes both actually doing the work, and here the second one is only ever hypothetical. Is there an established name for this, either for the move or for the assumption underneath it? If there isn't, coin one and actually define it for me — what's in it, what's out, how it differs from the nearby stuff, and when making the move is legitimate rather than a dodge. I'd also like to know whether philosophers of history or of causation have a term for the background assumption that an outcome is reachable by many different routes, because that seems to be doing alot of the work here.
