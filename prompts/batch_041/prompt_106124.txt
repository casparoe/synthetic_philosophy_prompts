I've been trying to get my head around Schopenhauer's philosophy of music and something's bugging me. His big claim is that music is the highest of the arts because it doesn't copy the Platonic Ideas — those first objectifications of the Will that painting, sculpture and poetry imitate — but copies the Will itself directly. Okay. But then he turns around and maps musical elements onto specific feelings: the bass standing for the lowest grades of the Will's objectification, the melody for the highest, harmony for the whole, different keys and modes tied to different emotional states. That sounds like music is representing determinate emotions after all, which is exactly what the "direct copy of the Will" story was supposed to avoid.

So two things. First, walk me through how these two claims are meant to be compatable — the metaphysical one (music copies the Will, not the Ideas) and the expressive one (music conveys particular emotions). What's the machinery that's supposed to make them fit? Second, and I care about this one more: if the Will is blind, non-representational striving, what does it even mean to be a "copy" of it? Copying normally implies representing some determinate content, and the Will by his own lights has none. Does "copy" here have to mean something else entirely, or is the whole comparison doing no real work? Tell me how the pieces are supposed to slot together and then whether you think they actually do, or whether one of the claims has to give.
