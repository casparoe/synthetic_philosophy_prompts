ok so this is going to be a bit of a mess but I want to get it out while it's fresh. I've been arguing about blame and responsibilty with people for years now, and I've ended up with a pile of opinions that I'm pretty sure are each individually right, but I have a nagging feeling that I've just been picking whatever feels right in each case and never checking whether they actually fit together. I'm not a philosopher, I've just read some things and thought about it a lot, so go easy on the jargon.

Here's my list:

1) Whether the universe is deterministic has nothing to do with responsibility. I mean literally nothing. If it turned out tomorrow that every choice anyone ever made was fixed from the big bang, that wouldn't change one thing about whether you're allowed to be furious with someone. The whole free will debate on this point feels like a trick.

2) Being responsible isn't some extra fact about a person sitting behind their behaviour, some hidden property they either have or don't. It just is being the kind of thing it makes sense to be angry at, or grateful to, or hurt by, or let down by. The reactions aren't a response to responsibility, they're the thing we're talking about when we talk about it.

3) I do not accept "he was raised that way" as an excuse. Ever. If someone's whole character is the product of their genes and a bad childhood and they never had a say in any of it — fine, so what. I'm still allowed to be furious. I don't think people get off the hook by pointing at the causes of themselves.

4) But I do think there's a real exemption for people who genuinely can't be reached by reasons at all. A serious psychopath, someone with severe brain damage. You don't resent them, you manage them. And I mean can't, not won't — someone who could take the point but refuses is a completely different case and I'd have no sympathy. But for the ones who can't, there's no grey area, they're just off the board.

5) Same deal with the classic excuses. If it turns out the guy had a tumour pressing on the right spot, or he was in the grip of a delusion so total that what he acted on wasn't really a reason at all, then he's off the hook. Not responsible, full stop.

6) I think a person is allowed to just stop resenting someone. Take the cold view — treat them as a problem to be handled rather than a person who can hurt you. I think that's a legitimate thing to do and it's not a failure or a betrayal of anything.

7) And I think two people who do the same careless thing are equally to blame even if one of them got lucky. Two drivers both fall asleep at the wheel, one drifts onto the shoulder, the other hits a kid. Same blame, the kid doesn't enter into it. The outcome shouldn't change the moral verdict.

Something in here is off. I can feel it but I can't find it, and honestly I don't know which one I'd give up if I had to — they all feel non-negotiable when I look at them one at a time.

What I want: tell me which of these actually can't all be held at once, and which ones just look like they clash and actually don't. I have a strong suspicion that some of these clashes are fake and I'm wasting my time worrying about them. For the real ones, rank them by how bad the problem actually is, and for each one say what the cheapest fix is and what I'd be giving up by taking it. Don't just flag everything as a tension, that's useless to me — I need to know which ones are load-bearing and which are just decoration. And if the answer is that I'm quietly helping myself to something in one of these that another one rules out, say which, and be specific about the arguement.
