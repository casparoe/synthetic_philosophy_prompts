My actual trade is distributed systems: I build and operate replicated services, so my working vocabulary is replicas, network partitions, quorum, leader election, consensus, linearizability versus eventual consistency, and the failure mode we literally call *split brain* — a partition that leaves two halves of a cluster each convinced it is the primary. I want you to act as a philosopher of mind who is also genuinely fluent in that engineering vocabulary — not someone who reaches for the analogy decoratively, but someone who can carry a translation all the way through and then tell me honestly where it breaks. Think carefully before you commit to any mapping; begin with a short brainstorm of the candidate correspondences, then discard the ones that are merely cute.

Here is the task. The split-brain literature concerns patients whose cerebral hemispheres have been surgically disconnected, producing a dissociation between what each half can perceive, name, act on, and report — including the well-known tendency of one hemisphere to confabulate a single coherent explanation for behavior it did not initiate. The philosophical dispute is over whether such a patient is one subject or has become two, and whether the question is even well-posed.

Restate the principal positions in that debate — the one-subject view, the two-subjects view, and the deflationary view that the question is ill-formed — in my vocabulary, one at a time, as faithfully as you can. Then give me the residue report. Which claims translate cleanly? Which translate only under distortion, and what exactly gets bent? Which have no counterpart at all in the distributed-systems frame?

Push on the specific points where I expect the analogy to strain. Is "unity of consciousness" anything more than a consistency guarantee, or is that a category error in my direction? When the connecting fibers are cut, is that best modeled as a partition of one system, or as two processes that were always distinct and merely shared a bus? Does the fact that the patient narrates a single life, or the possibility of reconnection, correspond to anything I have — a healed partition, a merge, a reconciliation protocol — or is that a false friend? And the part I most want: is there something the philosophers are arguing about that my vocabulary cannot express at all — the subject itself, or what it is like — and is that residue a defect of my framework (it simply has no place to put experience) or a sign that the philosophical dispute is smuggling in something it has not earned? Note explicitly any point at which the translation would require me to assume a spec — that the system *ought* to have exactly one leader — because I suspect the mind case has no spec, and that asymmetry may be the whole story. Don't soften the positions into "some say"; give me your best reconstruction of each as a position, and be candid about which of my intuitions the exercise should leave intact and which it should cost me.
