The idea, which I have only half worked out. Everyone arguing about sortition, term limits and career politicians fights over accountability as though it were one thing. I think there are at least three, and that they come apart in a way that reverses the usual argument. First, answerability: the obligation to give reasons to the people affected. Second, sanctionability: the liability to be removed, punished or voted out for doing badly. Third, contestability: the existence of a live channel through which a decision can be reopened or revised. Elections supply sanctionability reliably and the other two unevenly. The sortition bodies we have actually seen supply answerability and contestability unusually well and sanctionability almost not at all, and most of them can only recommend rather than decide. My suspicion is that the familiar accountability objection to sortition is really an objection to the absence of sanctionability, which may be the least important of the three for a body that proposes rather than decides, and that the same distinction explains why term limits, which also destroy sanctionability while leaving the other two intact, are attacked and defended in arguments that never quite meet. I would want to test this against real institutions rather than toy examples: the citizens' assemblies that have proliferated since roughly 2010 in Ireland, France, Belgium and elsewhere, and the US state legislatures that adopted term limits in the 1990s.

Two things I want from you. First, don't build the proposal around my distinction unless it survives a serious attempt to kill it. If the three kinds are just relabelings of something already standard, or if contestability turns out to be a synonym for a familiar idea, say so in the proposal and tell me what to do instead. Second, if this is really two projects, a conceptual one and a reporting one, that will each be done badly if fused into a single fellowship, say that plainly rather than handing me a tidy plan for a fused project. Keep the whole thing to about 700 words.
