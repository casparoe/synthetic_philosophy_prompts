A hospital system's psychiatric nosology committee has a track record I would like you to reason from. It approved a new diagnosis for a rare syndrome with a known biomarker, reasoning that the category rested on a distinct underlying dysfunction and that its patients had previously gone unserved, even though no treatment differs across the diagnosis and the label carries real stigma. It rejected a proposed diagnosis for a common symptom cluster, on the grounds that it carved out no distinct dysfunction and would mostly relabel patients already covered by existing categories; the committee also noted that the label was already circulating as a self-description. It rejected a proposed diagnosis for a culturally salient distress pattern then spreading through online patient communities, reasoning that once people applied the label to themselves their symptoms would drift and the category would end up validating itself rather than tracking anything independent. It approved a diagnosis defined by a distinct dysfunction whose uptake it later found stable over a decade of use.

The new proposal is for a syndrome with a plausible but as yet unconfirmed neural mechanism, currently being self-diagnosed and discussed intensively in online communities, where the committee expects that formal recognition would lead many people to reinterpret their histories and present differently to clinicians.

Tell me which rules fit the committee's earlier decisions, what each surviving rule predicts for this case, which rule you think it is actually running and why, and what further case would separate the rules that remain tied. I am less interested in whether the new category should exist than in what the committee's own pattern commits it to.
