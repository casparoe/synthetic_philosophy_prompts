I am writing down, in advance, the adequacy conditions for a theory of fluid concept slippage in a bounded microdomain, and I want the specification itself rather than a survey of who has proposed what. The domain is the letter-string analogy game, and I want the microdomain laid out concretely enough that the whole thing stands on its own.

In this game a source string is changed into a result string, and you are asked to apply the same change to a target string. From abc changing to abd, what does ijk change to? Nothing about the answer is mechanical: to reach it you have to let a concept slide. The c-to-d move can be read as incrementing the final letter (yielding ijl), as replacing the last letter with d (yielding ijd), as a successor relation between letters, as marking the rightmost position as special, or as an operation on the whole string rather than on a single character, and each reading drags the notion of what a letter is along with it. Comparable slippages appear when the change is a reversal, a duplication of a run, or a change in a run's length, and when the two strings differ in length or in the kind of structure they display.

Write me the desiderata: the full list of what any theory of concept slippage in this microdomain must get right to count as adequate — adequate, that is, for explaining human performance on these problems and for generalizing to adjacent microdomains. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it: the problems the theory must solve, the answers and answer-distributions it must reproduce, the distinctions it must not collapse (between a concept and its instances, between a slip that is licensed and one that is merely possible, between the process being plausible and the answer being correct, between what the model computes and what it merely retrieves), and the results it must stay consistent with (that a single problem admits several defensible readings, that difficulty and ambiguity vary across problems, that the mechanism must not have the target answer hardwired). Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are preferences of a particular camp — the rule-based, the connectionist, the probabilistic, the embodied — and, where you can, name the broad approach families that satisfy or violate each.

I most want the part that is a result in its own right: any subset of these requirements you believe cannot be jointly satisfied, with the argument for the conflict. A theory that reproduces the human-like distribution over answers and one that is fully general and unhackable; a theory that is graded and one that is deterministic; a theory that represents slippage explicitly and one that only exhibits it in behavior — tell me which of these pairs genuinely collide and which merely look as though they do. Then say which single requirement does the most to exclude candidates.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring particular named systems; I can do the scoring myself once I have the list. Write in American English.
