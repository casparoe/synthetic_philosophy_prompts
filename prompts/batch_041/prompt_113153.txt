I am a second-year doctoral student in intellectual history, and I need to draft a short research proposal — about 1,500 words — for a departmental workshop on method in the history of ideas. Let me set out the half-formed idea I want you to develop, and then the constraints.

The worry I keep returning to is this. The long-running argument about presentism — about whether historians can, or should, judge past actors by the standards of their own time rather than ours — appears to presuppose that any given period possesses a definite set of standards that could stand as the alternative to our own. The anti-presentist says we should judge by the standards of the time; the presentist says we cannot escape our own. Both moves take for granted that 'the standards of the time' names something determinate. But the periods I work on look internally riven: there is rarely a single standard of the age, only competing factions, each claiming to speak for the age as a whole. If that is right, the phrase 'the standards of their own time' may not pick out a stable object at all, and the familiar dichotomy might rest on a fiction.

I do not yet know where this leaves the debate. It might vindicate the presentist (there is no neutral period standard to retreat to), it might vindicate a chastened contextualism (we can still index to the standards the actor actually endorsed, or to the best reconstruction of the local norms), or it might show that both sides have been answering the wrong question. I want the proposal to make the question determinate enough that one of these outcomes can be argued for rather than merely gestured at.
