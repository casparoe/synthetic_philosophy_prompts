I'm in a seminar on population ethics and I've gotten into a running argument with a friend that I can't settle, so I want to see what a careful essay would do with it. She's antinatalist-leaning and thinks the asymmetry arguement is basically right — the one that says suffering is bad, the absence of suffering is good even when there's nobody around to enjoy that absence, pleasure is good, but the absence of pleasure is only bad when there's somebody who's actually been deprived of it. That's what's supposed to license the verdict that it's wrong to bring a suffering person into the world but not wrong to fail to bring a happy one.

Where we split is what happens when you point the asymmetry at nature. She thinks the whole thing falls apart there. Her line is that the asymmetry only makes sense as a claim about the interests of a person who exists or is going to exist — it's about a subject and what befalls them — and once you yank the subject out and start talking about "the absence of suffering in a biosphere" or "fewer animals born next spring," you're not doing ethics anymore, you're doing bad bookkeeping. If you insist it still applies, she says, you get a standing reason to prefer fewer and fewer animals until you reach a world with no sentient life at all, and that's obviously not the ideal, so something in the asymmetry has to be wrong. I think her derivation is valid and the conclusion is just true and unpleasant, and she's mistaking "this is a weird conclusion" for "this is a false premise."

Write me an essay on this. Lay out the asymmetry and what's actually supposed to motivate it (I want the motivation, not just the four clauses restated), then work through the nature application slowly — does the reason really transfer from "this person" to "this ecosystem," or does something break in the move, and if it breaks, is that a defect in the asymmetry or just a limit on how far one argument reaches? Then come down on one side. Don't end on "it's complicated" — tell me whether the nature case is a reductio of the asymmetry or a bullet the asymmetry should bite, and defend it. If you think the asymmetry survives but only in a repaired form, say exactly what the repair is and whether the repaired thing is still recognizably the same view or a different one wearing its clothes. And dont just assume I'm right because I'm the one asking — if the friend has the better of it, say so.
