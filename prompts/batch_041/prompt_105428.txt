I've got a hunch and I need you to turn it into that proposal.

It's about moral trade: the idea that two people who disagree morally can each do something the other wants, so that both come out ahead by their own lights even though they don't agree on what's good. What bugs me is that nearly every version of this I've seen seems to assume, without saying so, that there's a common currency -- that a unit of good-by-my-view can be put on the same scale as a unit of good-by-yours, so we can ask whether the swap is fair or whether it's a genuine improvement. But if we really hold different moral views, where does that exchange rate come from? I don't think it's obvious that there is one.

Here's the distinction I want to build the thing around, and I think it's the bit that's actually new. Some trades only move you to an option your own view already let you take -- you were permitted to do either thing, you just preferred one, and the trade nudges you towards the other. Call those within-the-lines trades. Others ask you to do something your own view outright forbids, in exchange for the other person doing something theirs forbids. Call those across-the-lines trades. My suspision is that within-the-lines trade needs no shared scale at all -- mutual benefit there is just a Pareto-ish idea, each side judges for itself and no comparison between frameworks is needed -- whereas across-the-lines trade is exactly where a common measure gets smuggled in, and where the whole thing either collapses or turns out to rest on a hidden assumption that one framework is the real one. I might be wrong; that's rather the point.

Turn this into a proper proposal. Also tell me if the within-the-lines / across-the-lines split is doing real work or if it's a distinction that turns out to be empty once you press on it -- I'd rather find that out in the proposal than halfway through the chapter.
