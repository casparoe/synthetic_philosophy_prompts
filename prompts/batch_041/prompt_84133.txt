The set theory reading group I'm in has spent a whole term going in circles about the axiom of choice and I'm starting to think we're not even having one disagreement. Everyone knows the setup: AC and its negation are both consistent with the rest of ZF, so no consistency arguement is going to settle it. But the room still splits. One side says AC is fine — it's how you get the theorems you actually want (Zorn's lemma, a basis for every vector space), and the alleged paradoxes like Banach-Tarski aren't paradoxes at all, because the pieces that come out are non-measurable, so there's no contradiction with anything we already knew about volume. The other side says that's just a description of the damage: you buy those theorems by licensing sets nobody could ever specify, and saying the pieces aren't measurable restates the problem instead of answering it. Nobody in the room is missing a theorem here and nobody budges an inch. Is this a verbal dispute about what 'exists' means in maths, a disagreement about what could count as evidence for an axiom, or two different questions under one heading — which axioms are fruitful versus which are true? My hunch is it's really about whether an independence proof is allowed to be a verdict, i.e. about what mathematics is answerable to — but then why do the same people who use Zorn without blinking get uneasy about a non-measurable set? Diagnose it for me, and tell me what would actually count as progress.
