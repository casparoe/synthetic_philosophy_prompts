I keep going back and forth on whether patriotism is a virtue, a vice, or just a personality trait like liking spicy food, and I'm hoping you can help me figure out what principle (if any) my gut is actually tracking. You're a political philosopher who's spent a lot of time on partiality, loyalty, and national identity — so please think carefully and don't just give me the standard for-and-against list. Start by brainstorming a bunch of candidate principles (at least five, including some weird ones), then test them against my cases, then give me your best one or two, stated precisely.

Here are my verdicts, going by gut:

1. My neighbor flies the flag on the 4th. I don't think this is virtuous or vicious; it's like putting up Christmas lights.
2. Someone spends their weekends restoring a local war memorial because it's their town's memorial. I think that's genuinely admirable, even if the same hours could have gone to a global charity.
3. Someone refuses to read a book critical of their country's founders because it feels disloyal. I think that's a small vice — a failure, not just a taste.
4. A voter supports a tariff that will hurt poor workers abroad mainly because it helps their own country's workers. I think that's a vice, though maybe a mild one.
5. A soldier in a country that's being invaded takes up arms to defend it, knowing this will kill invaders. I think that's admirable, not a vice at all.
6. A citizen of a wealthy country agitates to let in more refugees, even though it will make their own countrymen slightly worse off. I think that's admirable too, and I don't think it's unpatriotic.
7. Someone emigrates, naturalizes elsewhere, and stops caring about their birth country. I don't think they've done anything wrong.
8. But someone who feels no attachment to any place or people at all, and thinks that's the enlightened position, seems to me to be missing something important — not evil, but diminished.
9. Cheering for the national team and getting a lump in your throat at the anthem: fine, maybe even good.
10. An employer requiring workers to sing the anthem at the start of the workday: creepy, and I'd call it a vice of the employer.
11. A person who celebrates their country's historical massacres as proof of greatness: clear vice.
12. A person who constantly tells foreigners about their country's historical crimes, in a way that seems mostly designed to show off their own virtue: I think that's also a vice, though a different one.
13. Someone who disobeys their country by refusing to fight in a war they believe is unjust: admirable, and I think it can be a form of patriotism, not a betrayal.
14. Someone who burns their passport on TV mainly to express contempt for their fellow citizens: I think that's vicious, even if their criticisms are correct.

I've been toying with a distinction I made up, between 'warm patriotism' — attachment to a particular place, its landscape, rituals, food, neighbors, dead — and 'cold patriotism' — loyalty to the state as an institution, its flag, its army, its borders. I thought maybe warm patriotism is a virtue and cold patriotism is a vice, but case 5 and case 6 seem to mess that up. Also case 8 suggests maybe some warm attachment is required for a good life, which is a stronger claim than just 'it's not a vice.' I definately don't want to just seperate the cases into ones I like and ones I don't and call that a principle.

What I want from you: a principle, or a small set of principles, that gets as many of my verdicts as possible, says which ones I should probably give up, and tells me what it predicts for a case I haven't listed — say, a person who loves their country's culture and people but thinks its government is illegitimate and works to undermine it. Also, if my warm/cold distinction is doing any real work, say what work; if it's just relabeling, say that too.

Please write it as if you're talking to someone who knows some philosophy but isn't a specialist, and be honest about which verdicts your principle can't save. If you think there's no single principle, give me the best disjunctive one and tell me where the joints are.
