My neuroscientist, Vollard, is meant to be the greedy reductionist: at faculty dinners he insists that love, grief, and the sense of self are "nothing but" electrochemical events, and he clearly enjoys the discomfort this causes. Against him I've placed my philosopher, Iris, who is supposed to embody the humanistic resistance. She wants to keep the level of meaning and first-person experience from being swallowed whole by the lab downstairs, and I have been writing her as the defender of the skyhooks — on the assumption that Dennett's contrast is a version of the familiar one between humble, pluralistic explanation and the imperious "nothing but" variety, with the skyhooks standing for the humane, anti-reductionist side of the ledger and greedy reductionism naming the vice of dragging everything down to the level of atoms.

What I need from you is a correction and a plan, in that order. First, tell me plainly whether I have the terms right — whether the crane/skyhook contrast is the modest-versus-imperious distinction I've assumed, whether skyhooks are really the humanistic alternative to Vollard's reductionism, and whether "greedy reductionism" means reducing too far down (to physics, say) or something else; if I've mis-assigned either character, say so without softening it. I'd also like to know which one, on the corrected reading, is the more interesting character to write, since I suspect I've been giving the better lines to the wrong man.
