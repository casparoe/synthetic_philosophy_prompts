Write a fire-safety notice — the laminated sheet your landlord tapes inside the front door — for a city whose authorities take al-Ghazālī at his word: fire has no power to burn anything, God creates the burning himself each time a flame touches cloth, and what we ordinarily call causes are only the occasions on which he does it. The regularities are Gods habit, and he's free to break them whenever he likes.

So the notice cant say that a bucket of water puts out a fire, or that a smoke alarm saves lives, or that anything is what makes anything else happen — and yet it still has to get people to run outside, because if the habit is reliable then running outside is still the smart move. That tension is the whole joke. Keep it deadpan: no winking, no explaining the theology to the reader, the doctrine should show up only in what the rules refuse to say, and the people who wrote it should come across as a little proud of how rigorous the document is. Numbered sections (what to do, what not to do, what to expect from us, a short FAQ). Include a boxed sidebar from one committee member who was overruled because he thinks God gave fire a real power to burn — he's the only one allowed to say out loud what the rest of the document is built to avoid. Around 500 words.
