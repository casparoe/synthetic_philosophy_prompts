I'm gonna present to my department's philosophy of mind reading group next week. My thesis: conscious experience is real but causally inert; the usual objection that epiphenomenalism can't explain how we reliably judge and report our experiences only looks fatal if you assume the experience itself has to cause the judgment. I say the brain produces the experience and, separately, a causally active internal representation of it — call it a phenomenal proxy — and our reports track the proxy, not the experience. So reliable phenomenal judgment is no more mysterious than reliable judgment about any other represented state. The room is mostly physicalists, two illusionists who think I'm helping myself to the very thing they want to explain away, and one functionalist who knows the reportability literature and will press on whether the proxy is just a second experience.
