Ok so I'm in a philosophy of biology seminar and we just did Gould and Lewontin on the spandrels, and I have to bring in a ranking for Thursdays seminar that I dont really know how to do. Our professor handed out five "licensing moves" that people use when they want to claim some trait is an adaptation, and wants us to rank them by how much they actually answer the critique. The moves are:

1. Perfection move — the trait's parts fit together with a precision that seems too improbable to be an accident, so selection must have built it for that job.
2. Convergence move — unrelated lineages have arrived at the same trait, so the same selection pressure must have built it each time.
3. Constraint-first move — first show that a non-selective source (allometry, drift, pleiotropy, developmental constraint, phylogenetic inertia) could produce the trait, and only invoke selection for whatever is left over.
4. Cost-accounting move — the trait is currently costly for the organism, so it can't just be a free byproduct and must be maintained by selection.
5. Exaptation move — the structure was already there before it took on its current role, so the current role explains its maintenance even if not its origin.

Can you rank these five by how much pulling one off should raise my credence that the trait is an adaptation rather than a byproduct or a constraint effect, and say in a sentence why each one lands where it does? And if some of them are really answering a different question than the one the paper is asking, flag that too.
