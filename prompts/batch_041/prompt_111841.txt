Suppose you are advising a foundation that can fund three researchers for three years on the semantic paradoxes and formal theories of truth. Which open questions should it prioritize, and in what order? I am less interested in the list than in the reasoning: for each question, why it is upstream of others, how tractable it is with current proof- and model-theoretic tools, how neglected it is, and what a result over the next three years would move it up or down the ranking. Assume the foundation doubts that the standard menu — Kripke-style fixed points, paracomplete and paraconsistent approaches, substructural or transparent truth theories, contextualist hierarchies — exhausts the space, and is willing to fund work that reframes the problem rather than extends one of these. Two reframings are on the table and should be weighed explicitly: relativizing the truth predicate to a shifting assessment context, so that no single context can state the strengthened liar, and abandoning structural contraction so that the paradox cannot be derived at all. Are reframings of this kind the most promising line, or is the deeper obstacle the revenge problem in whatever form it recurs? Conclude with a ranked agenda of three items and state what would change the ranking. Answer in under 700 words.
