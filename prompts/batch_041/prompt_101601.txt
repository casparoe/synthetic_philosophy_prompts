For my philosophy class I have to write an essay on whether lying and misleading are morally different, and I would like you to write it for me. Please don't simply trot out the standard examples; I want the argument organised around the following case instead. A company spokesperson is bound by an internal rule never to utter a false statement. At a press conference about the closure of a factory, the spokesperson takes only friendly questions, quotes accurate production figures, and says nothing at all about the redundancies, so the reporters leave believing that nobody will lose their job. A second employee, who is under no such rule, says outright that no jobs will be lost — a claim they know to be false — but the reporters distrust this employee, investigate, and end up printing the truth. I would like the essay to reach and defend a definite verdict on whether the spokesperson's conduct is morally better than, worse than, or no different from the employee's, and to be explicit about what it takes the wrong of deception to consist in: the speaker's intention to deceive, the literal falsity of what is said, the false belief actually produced in the listener, or the breach of an expectation that one will not be deceived. Please also say whether your verdict would change if the spokesperson had merely stayed silent rather than making a careful selection of true statements, and whether misleading should be judged by what a reasonable listener would infer or by what these particular listeners actually inferred. Please write the essay in British English.
