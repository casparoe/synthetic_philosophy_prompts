ok so I've been calling myself an egalitarian for years and I want to stress test the label before I put it in a confrence abstract. here's roughly where I've landed — can you tell me if it actually hangs together:

1. Equality has intrinsic value. Take two worlds with the same people and the same total welfare, but one has a smaller spread between the best and worst off. The tighter one is better, just in virtue of that — not because anyone in it is better off, but because the gap is smaller.
2. I'm a welfarist. The only things that are intrinsically good or bad are facts about individual welfare; there are no other intrinsic goods floating around.
3. The levelling down objection is a problem for a cartoon egalitarian, not for me. Making the better off worse off with no gain to anyone isn't better in any respect, it's just worse, and I'd never defend a policy like that.
4. The reason the worse off matter more is that they have stronger claims on us, and a claim gets stronger the worse off the claimant is. It's about where they sit, not about how far they sit from anyone else.
5. I'd still take from the very rich to help the very poor even when it shrinks the total pot. Some inequality reduction is worth real efficiency losses.

Go through these and tell me which ones are actually in tension, which pairs only look like they are, and what the cheapest way to make the set consistent would be and what it would cost. Order the real tensions by how bad they are rather than telling me they're all fine, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most mess. I'm not wedded to any one of these in particular, I just want to know where the fault lines actually are and which ones I'm imagining.
