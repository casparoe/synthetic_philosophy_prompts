After a career in experimental ecology, I have come late to AI alignment, and I am struck by how much of the debate treats alignment as the problem of specifying the right objective for a single system. I would like you to argue for a position I have come to call dynamic alignment: the central problem is not specifying a fixed objective but preserving an agent's capacity to revise its own specification in response to correction, and that capacity is fragile under competitive selection even when every individual agent is perfectly aligned to its current specification. In making the case, distinguish dynamic alignment from corrigibility, describe a selection scenario in which a population of agents each aligned to a fixed specification drifts to an equilibrium none of them would endorse, and address the strongest objection—that dynamic alignment is either vacuous or collapses into ordinary human oversight. Please keep the argument self-contained; I am not looking for citations or a literature review.
