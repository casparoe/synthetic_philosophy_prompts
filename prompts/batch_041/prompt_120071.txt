I spent my career studying social insects and have lately been sitting in on philosophy of mind seminars; I am now setting the final for an upper-level course on emergence and cognition. I want a single essay question, answerable in about fifty minutes, built around the ant-colony thought experiment often called Aunt Hillary: a colony whose pheromone-mediated dynamics integrate information so thoroughly that it appears to deliberate and remember, even though no individual ant represents the colony's apparent thoughts, and the colony retains a behavioural memory after every ant that lived through the relevant event has died. The question must not be answerable by reciting functionalism versus biological naturalism; it should make students say what would have to be true for the colony to have a mind, whether a colony-level subject could exist with no ant-level subject, and how one could distinguish a genuine group mind from a merely useful as-if model. Please supply the question, a model answer of roughly 700 words, and a marking scheme out of 25 that names the specific moves a first-class answer must make and the specific errors that should cost marks.
