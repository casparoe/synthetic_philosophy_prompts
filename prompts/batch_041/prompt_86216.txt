I run a monthly discussion group at our community library: a dozen adults with no philosophical training, patient but argumentative, and quick to ask what any of it is supposed to matter for. Next month I have promised them a session on a puzzle I have been chewing over alone — why we say a glass is fragile even though it has never once been struck — and I have read enough on my own to see the trouble without being able to keep it straight. So plan the session rather than explain it to me.

The trouble, as I have it, is that fragile appears to mean nothing more than would break if struck, and that analysis seems to fail in both directions: sometimes the thing is still fragile and something outside it blocks the breaking, as packing material does for a glass; sometimes the trigger itself unmakes the fragility, so that the thing is fragile now but would not break, since the striking is precisely what would remove the disposition. I have seen the word finkish used for this second sort and not the first, and I cannot reliably keep them apart, which does not bode well for my teaching it. The repair I keep reaching for is a clause to the effect that nothing interferes, and I cannot tell whether that rescues the analysis or only hides the difficulty. There is, I gather, a further case in which the thing is not fragile at all yet would break if struck, because the striking would create the fragility; I do not know whether that belongs in an hour.

Plan the hour for me. What must come first for the problem to feel like a problem rather than a riddle? Which single case should carry the session — I had thought of a burglar alarm disposed to sound when it detects movement, muffled with a cushion in one telling and disabled by the intruder's own device in another, but say whether that case does the work or whether something plainer does it better, and what any carrying case must have and lack. Which misconceptions will form, in what order, and where do I head each one off? My guess is that they will take the two sorts of case to be one thing with different timing, or conclude that a disposition which never shows itself is not really there. What should I refuse to cover at this level, and say so? And give me one question for the end that would show whether they have the distinction rather than merely having followed me. No reading list; I find my own reading.
