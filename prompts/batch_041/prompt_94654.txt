I have been reading about the laws on prostitution in Sweden and in the countries that have copied them, and there is a feature of the arrangement that I cannot get to fit together. The headline rule is that selling sex is lawful and buying it is not: the seller is left alone by the criminal law, the buyer is prosecuted, and the stated reason is that prostitution is a form of violence against women, with public money going to help women leave. So far I can follow the thought — the wrong, if there is one, lies on the buyer's side, and she is the one being wronged. But then there is the rest of the same law, which makes it an offence to promote or profit from another person's prostitution. As I understand it, that reaches well beyond the pimp and the brothel-keeper: it covers the premises, the transport, the advertising, the security, the person who takes the bookings. The seller herself does nothing wrong, yet almost anyone who helps her work — her landlord, her driver, her flatmate — is committing a crime, and she is left with no lawful way to hire help or share a workplace. In any other trade that would be unthinkable: the worker innocent, and everyone around her guilty.

What does that combination presuppose? I can see at least three readings and I cannot tell which the law actually needs. One is that the act itself is not wrong but the institution is: the harm lies in prostitution as a social arrangement rather than in the transaction, so that a solitary exchange is tolerable and an organised one is not. Another is that her consent is not really consent, in which case the offence against the buyer is doing the moral work and the ban on helpers is a way of stopping anyone from using her. The third is that her consent is genuine and his is corrupt — which would make this the only exchange I know of in which the wrong attaches to the party who wants rather than the party who provides. It may also be that the two halves of the law rest on incompatible assumptions and simply coexist because different people wanted different things.

I should like you to reconstruct the view of agency, consent, harm and institutions that this practice presupposes, and to be careful about what it strictly requires as opposed to what it merely happens to fit. Then say where it is underdetermined — where several quite different theories of what prostitution is would each license the same statutes — and what would have to change if the presupposed view were abandoned, for instance if the seller were taken to be a worker like any other, as some countries have done in decriminalising it outright and regulating it as ordinary work. I am not asking you to defend or attack the law; I want to know what its defenders are committed to.
