I am a third-year undergraduate taking a metaphysics course on vagueness this term, alongside a numerical methods course, and I would like to use the vocabulary I am actually fluent in to make sense of the former. The vocabulary in question is that of floating-point arithmetic and tolerance-based computation: machine epsilon, rounding error, guard digits, condition number, comparison thresholds, and the distinction between the computed result and the exact result of the underlying real-number problem. I would like you to restate the disagreement between epistemicism and supervaluationism about vague predicates in those terms, as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report.

To make the translation concrete, take a predicate such as is a heap (or is tall, or is red, whichever carries the translation best) and treat it as something like a predicate over a continuous quantity, the way one implements approximate equality as a test of the form the absolute difference is below some epsilon. Then:

First, render epistemicism as an implementation and supervaluationism as an implementation. Be explicit about what plays the role of the sharp-but-unknown boundary, what plays the role of the admissible precisifications, and what plays the role of truth, or of supertruth, in each. I want to see the two views as two different designs rather than as two slogans.

Second, tell me which parts of each view survive the translation cleanly. Specifically, is a tolerance parameter an honest stand-in for a precisification, and does the supervaluationist idea that a sentence is true when it is true on every admissible way of making the predicate precise have a counterpart in my vocabulary, or does it collapse into something else, such as a convention, a design decision, or a risk choice?

Tell me whether that is a result of the translation or an artifact of my framework.

Is there anything in the philosophical debate about vagueness that speaks to that failure and to the fiat patch, or is that purely an engineering matter that the philosophers' vocabulary cannot express? I am not asking for a survey of the literature and I am not asking for citations or a reading list. I want the translation, the residue, and your verdict on whether the residue is a defect of my vocabulary or a sign that one of the two positions was less clear than it appeared.
