Alright, I'm trying to get my views straight for a social choice seminar and I want to check whether the package actually hangs together before I make a fool of myself. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Harsanyi's aggregation theorem is basically sound. If each person's preferences satisfy the expected-utility axioms, and the social preference does too, and society is never indifferent when every single person is indifferent (the Pareto indifference thing), then the social welfare function has to be a weighted sum of individual utilities. Add the impartiality move — behind the veil you treat yourself as equally likely to be anyone — and the weights come out equal, i.e. total utility, i.e. utilitarianism.

(2) I'm a prioritarian, not a utilitarian. A given gain matters more when it goes to someone who's badly off than to someone who's already comfortable, so pure sum-ranking is wrong.

(3) I also think equality is intrinsically valuable, not just instrumentally useful. Faced with two people at 5 each, versus a fair coin that gives one of them 10 and the other 0, I'd take the sure 5/5 — even though the ex ante expected utilities are identical.

(4) The seperateness of persons is the deepest constraint on any social morality. You can't treat society as one big person whose total experience you're maximising.

(5) Impartiality just is the veil of ignorance with equal probabilities. That's what makes everyone's interests count the same, and I don't see how else to cash it out.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, and how badly? I've got a nagging feeling that something in here is doing work it isn't entitled to, but I can't tell where. Some of these might only look like they conflict. Please go through the pairs, tell me which tensions are real and rank them by how bad they are, which ones dissolve once you spell them out properly, and for the real ones what the cheapest way out is and what I'd be giving up to take it. Don't just tell me it's all fine — if one of these has to go, say which, and say what it costs me.
