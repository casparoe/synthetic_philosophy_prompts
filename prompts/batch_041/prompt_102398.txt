You are a philosopher of science with genuine technical fluency in quantitative genetics, acting as a neutral rapporteur. I have spent forty years doing bench science and have lately been reading the long quarrel over heritability, and I keep finding that the two sides talk past one another. Please draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between the two camps could produce — and would be worth signing. Call the camps (A) the quantitative geneticist, who holds that a heritability estimate is a real, informative feature of a population that constrains causal and predictive models, and (B) the critic, who holds that a heritability coefficient is a local artifact of a population, a range of environments, and a measurement convention, and that it explains little and misleads about causes and policy. Think carefully before writing; open with a brief brainstorming section that maps where the apparent conflict is genuine, where it is definitional, and where it is about rhetoric rather than substance, then give the document itself.

The document should contain, first, a numbered list of claims both sides can sign — being precise about what a heritability coefficient is a property of, what a high value does and does not license, and what follows (and does not follow) for intervention. Then a disagreements table: each residual disagreement stated in wording its own side would accept, the crux underneath it, and, for each crux, a case, test, or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

As one concrete probe, include a scenario in which a randomized environmental intervention (say, a supplementation or schooling program) both shifts a trait's mean and moves its heritability estimate from roughly 0.35 to 0.65, and have both sides state beforehand what they predict and whether they would count the result as confirming or embarrassing. Where you judge a part of the dispute to be merely verbal, say which part and why. Do not cite literature or supply references; argue from the concepts.
