For a chapter I'm writing on early modern moral self-management I want a piece of satire I can teach with, so here's the brief.

Act as a satirist who knows the Franklin material cold — someone who's read the Autobiography closely and has actual views about virtue ethics — and write me a deadpan customer-onboarding FAQ, 600-700 words, for a company that has licensed and productized Franklin's Art of Virtue project. Call it something like Thirteen, Inc. Voice: a chipper B2B SaaS onboarding doc that never breaks character.

Ground rule: every joke has to be load-bearing, i.e. it has to come from something actually in Franklin's own account of the project, so that a Franklin scholar laughs instead of wincing. You have to work with: the thirteen virtues and their one-line precepts; the plan of one virtue per week, thirteen weeks to a course, four courses a year; the little book with red-ruled pages and black spots for faults; the later upgrade to ivory leaves and a lead pencil wiped out with a wet sponge; the fact that he eventually dropped to one course a year, then one in several years, then stopped entirely but kept carrying the book around anyway; and his confession that Order was the one he never managed and nearly made him quit. The speckled-axe anecdote is the company's official position on partial compliance. The FAQ has to actually handle the real objections instead of dodging them: that he never claimed to have arrived, only to have been a better and happier man for trying; that he suspected such extreme nicety might be "a kind of foppery in morals" that would make him ridiculous; that a benevolent man should allow himself a few faults "to keep his friends in countenance"; that he deliberately stripped out anything sectarian so the method would work for people of all religions; and the suspicion that the whole thing treats virtue as instrument — sincerity and justice because they earn you the confidence of your country. And let the doc quietly notice, without ever saying so, that the metrics have become the point rather than the virtues.

Dont wink. No knowing narrator, no aside telling us it's absurd. A reader should have to work out for themselves that the product is the problem.

Before you write, brainstorm for a few lines: which objection you'll fold into which FAQ entry, and what belongs in the company's "known limitations" section. Then give me the FAQ.
