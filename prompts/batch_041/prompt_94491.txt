I'm trying to get one of those small grants for people outside academia who want to sit down and think something through properly, and I need a short research proposal, two pages max. My idea is about why crowds end up all doing the same thing, and I think the way people usually talk about it misses a whole half of the picture.

The usual story is: I see you order the fish, I figure you know something I dont, so I order the fish too, and after a few of us nobodys really looking at the menu anymore, we're all just looking at each other. Fine. But heres what bugs me. Sometimes the crowd agreeing really is evidence that the thing is good -- a restaurant, a dentist, a used car. Other times the crowd agreeing is what makes the thing what it is -- a trendy jacket, a slang word, the right way to tip, whether its cool to like some band. If I order the fish because you did, maybe I learn the fish is good. If I buy the jacket because you did, I havent learned anything about the jacket, Ive just joined in. And from the outside these two look identical: a bunch of people doing the same thing.

So the question I want to propose working on: when people converge on the same choice, how can you tell whether the convergence is telling you something about the thing (it has a property everyone's picking up on) or making something about the thing (it only has that property because everyone converged)? And once you can tell, does it change what a single person should do when they're deciding whether to join in?

Why I think the usual answers dont settle it: everything I've read basically treats every crowd as a bunch of people guessing at some fact, and once they all guess the same way the fact is supposed to be settled. But that only works if there was a fact there to begin with. Nobody seems to give you a clean way to seperate the two cases, or to say what you'd have to observe to tell which one you're looking at.

What I'd actually do: start by collecting a bunch of real examples and trying to build a test -- some kind of checklist or decision procedure an outsider could run on a crowd to figure out which kind it is. Then check it against the hard cases (fashions that turn out to carry real information, reputations that turn out to be pure coordination, things that are a bit of both). And I want to think through whether there's a principled reason the two cases are always mixed together, which would be its own result.

What would count as the project failing: if it turns out you can never tell the two apart even in principle, or if the distinction only works for toy examples and falls apart on anything real, I'd want the proposal to say so upfront rather than pretend otherwise.

Please dont bother with a reading list or references, I'll sort that out myself, I just need the actual plan: the question stated so it has a real answer, why whats out there leaves it open, the method month by month, and an honest scope for the time and money.
