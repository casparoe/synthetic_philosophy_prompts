I have been reading one of the popular books on the current Stoic revival, and I would like an honest audit of its central chapter on the dichotomy of control. The author writes for a general audience, and I have no independent knowledge of the ancient sources, so I cannot tell where he is reporting the Stoics and where he is quietly updating them. Here is the substance of his account, as faithfully as I can reconstruct it.

He says that the dichotomy of control is the core of Stoic practice. Epictetus, he reports, divided everything we encounter into two classes: what is up to us and what is not. Up to us are our thoughts, feelings, and actions; not up to us are our bodies, our reputations, our possessions, other people, and the outcomes of our efforts. Because only the first class lies in our power, the Stoic trains himself to want only what he controls and to be indifferent to everything else — and this, the author says, is the source of the celebrated Stoic calm. He then refines the division into three parts: what we fully control, what we cannot control at all, and what we control only partially; he presents this trichotomy as Epictetus's own refinement of a cruder binary, with the middle category being where most of life actually happens. From there he draws three further conclusions. First, since the Stoics believed everything is fated, the practical upshot is that it ultimately makes no difference what you do, so long as you preserve the right attitude. Second, the goal is to feel nothing, since the passions are precisely what disturb us. Third, the whole point of the discipline is to lower your stress and sharpen your focus at work, which is why he recommends it to busy professionals.

I suspect parts of this are right and parts are not, but I cannot say which. Please go through the account claim by claim and tell me what a careful student of ancient Stoicism would accept, what would make them wince, and what is simply mistaken. Where the author has conflated two things the Stoics kept apart, or has used one of their terms in its ordinary everyday sense, I would like that identified explicitly. Finally, tell me whether his closing practical recommendation — that this is a good regimen for a stressed professional — survives once the account has been corrected, or whether it depends on the very distortions you have flagged.
