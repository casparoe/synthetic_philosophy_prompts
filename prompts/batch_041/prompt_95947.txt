I'm on the program committee of a small foundation that hands out three-year fellowships in philosophy, and this cycle we're devoting the whole batch -- about eight slots -- to the normativity of belief. Our bright idea was to use Moore's paradox (the it's-raining-but-I-don't-believe-it case) as the organizing entry point, on the theory that it's the cheapest doorway into everything else in the neighborhood.

Problem is I need to tell the committee which open questions we're actually steering people toward, and I don't want to commission eight variations on "is the absurdity semantic or pragmatic." So I need you to rank the open questions for me. Which ones are upstream of the rest -- where a good answer would quietly settle or reframe a bunch of the others? Which are doable by a philosopher alone with pen and paper in three years (no lab, no archive, no corpus)? And which are the ones everyone calls important but where an answer wouldn't move much of anything downstream?

Three candidates have come up internally. Take them seriously as agenda items, but feel free to tell me they're dead ends:

- One board member keeps pushing what she calls the graded Moore problem: sentences like "It's probably raining, but my credence that it's raining is low." She claims that's the real paradox and the all-or-nothing version is just the degenerate case. Is that a genuinely separate question or the same thing with numbers stapled on?
- My own hobbiehorse is that the interesting thing isn't the sentence but the speaker's position -- there's something off about leaning on a claim as your basis for acting while denying you're committed to it. Is "commitment" a better primitive here than "belief," and does that open up a program or just rename the puzzle?
- Someone else wants to know whether any of this is specific to belief at all, or whether you get the same absurdity for desires, intentions, hopes, regrets -- and if belief is special, what that reveals about its functional role.

The committee is allergic to anything that turns into a verbal fight about what "belief" is supposed to mean, and we've been burned by projects that deliver one more taxonomy and no verdicts. Weight that. Also weight that the fellows will be mostly early-career and working alone, and that the foundation wants two or three results we can point at in three years, not a research tradition that matures in thirty.

Give me the ordering, the criteria you're using, where those criteria pull against each other, and what would change the ranking -- if someone showed X, would your top question slide down? Write it in American English.
