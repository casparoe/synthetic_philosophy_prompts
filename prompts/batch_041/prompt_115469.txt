I have no training in philosophy or in physics, but I have spent a fair amount of time trying to think my way through the cosmological argument on my own, and I keep running up against the saying that nothing comes from nothing. I have now seen it do several quite different jobs, and I cannot tell whether they are the same claim wearing different clothes. In one debate I watched, the saying carried the whole argument: the universe cannot simply have appeared, because nothing comes from nothing, so something must have caused it. A friend with some physics tells me the saying is dead, since quantum fields are forever producing particles where there was nothing before. And in a longer piece I read, the same thought reappeared in what seemed to me a different form — not that the universe must have had a cause, but that since the universe might never have existed, something which could not have failed to exist must account for it.

I would like you to separate out the precise claims the saying could be making and give me a verdict on each, laid out as a list I can work through rather than a single preferred reading. For each, I want to know: what 'nothing' would have to mean for the claim to be true, and whether the word must mean the same thing across the readings; whether the claim is about things and events, about the quantities that physics measures, or about facts and the explanations we give for them; whether it is a trivial restatement, a substantive and defensible principle, or simply false; and whether it is strong enough to yield the conclusion that the universe has a cause. I would also like to know which reading the person in the debate most likely had in mind, and whether that reading is the one the argument actually needs. If none of the readings reaches the conclusion, I would rather be told so plainly than handed the nearest claim that does.

Two smaller questions. Does any reading of the saying survive the observation about particles appearing in a vacuum, or does that observation refute only its weakest form? And is there a tension between using the saying to argue that the universe must have been produced by something, and the further claim that it was produced out of nothing — since on the face of it the saying rules out precisely that?
