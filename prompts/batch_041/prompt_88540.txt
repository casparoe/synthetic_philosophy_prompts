I write about science and ideas for a living, and I sit on the funding committee of a small nonprofit that backs long-shot research bets. Thursday we vote on a proposal thats got us all stuck, and I have to circulate a written recommendation before then.

Heres the shape of it: the project runs in rounds, a fair coin decides when it stops, and if it stops on round n our net payoff is (-2)^n divided by n — so round 1 is -2, round 2 is +2, round 3 is about -2.7, round 4 is +4, and so on, with round n reached with probability 1/2^n. Our finance guy says the expected value works out to minus the natural log of 2, a small loss, so pass. The one statistician on the committee says no: that series only adds up in that particular order, rearrange the terms and you can make it come out to any number you like, plus or minus infinity included, so the expected value isn't -0.69, it's undefined, and you can't even rank the thing against the boring grant we'd fund instead. The person who brought us the proposal keeps saying that there's a twin of this program where every single outcome is better than ours and whose expected value is exactly as undefined as ours — so if our rule says no to this one, it says no to the better one too, and any rule like that is junk.

Draft the committee's decision memo: fund it, reject it, or fund with conditions, with a concrete next step and a timeframe, and give the losing side's best argument a real hearing instead of a polite nod. I won't get to ask the proposer anything else before the vote. No citations needed, just tell me what to do and why.
