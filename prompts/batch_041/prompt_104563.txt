I am in a graduate seminar on populism and constitutional theory, and a phrase that keeps doing heavy lifting is “the will of the people.” Last week it did two very different jobs in a single discussion. In one case, a classmate argued that a municipal referendum to remove a controversial statue should bind the city council because the vote expressed the will of the people; when I asked whether the will of the people could be unjust or ill-informed, she said that was close to a contradiction in terms. In another case, a different classmate argued that a court was right to strike down a plebiscite because the plebiscite had manipulated the will of the people rather than expressed it. I realized that I do not know what the phrase is actually asserting, and that the two arguments seem to require incompatible readings of it. I would like you to lay out the distinct precise claims the slogan could be making, from the nearly trivial to the radical, and to give a verdict on each: platitude, substantive and defensible, contentious, or false. I am not asking for a history of the phrase or for a survey of who said what; I want a conceptual menu.

Please organize the readings so they separate at least the following possibilities: a pre-political collective agent with a single will that exists independently of any vote; a merely aggregative sum of individual preferences; a procedural construction that exists only when certain deliberative conditions are met; a negative or veto-centered authorization to reject elites; a majoritarian fiction that licenses whoever can plausibly claim to speak for the people; and a constitutional or constituent-power reading on which the people's will authorizes a regime but does not itself govern. For each reading, say what it would imply about referendums, courts, representatives, and minorities, and say which reading was probably in play in each of the two arguments above. Finally, tell me which readings, if any, would actually support the claim that a referendum result is binding even when it is unjust or ill-informed, and which would support the claim that a court may overturn a plebiscite in the name of the people's will. I want this as a list I can bring to seminar, not a single favored interpretation.
