Teaching a week on time in an intro metaphysics unit and I want to run Slaughterhouse-Five against the Rietdijk–Putnam argument instead of the usual "block universe, so no free will" mush. So: simultaneity is frame-relative in special relativity, no frame is privileged, therefore anything that's present-relative-to-somebody is real, and by transitivity it all is — the Andromeda version (two people walking past each other, disagreeing about whether the Andromedans have already launched) is the one that sticks. Rietdijk's own paper announced a proof of determinism from SR, which should make you suspicous: what the argument actually delivers, if anything, is that the future exists, not that it's fixed, and the standard reply is that the present is a local notion that can't be smeared into one global hyperplane. Now read Billy's Tralfamadorians against that. Where does the novel's picture — all the moments laid out, we just happen to be looking at one — match the argument, and where is it quietly adding a single cosmic now, or fatalism, or the ability to know what's coming, none of which come free? And the bit I actually want your view on: in the novel the metaphysics arrives attached to a traumatised man and to the Kilgore Trout paperbacks he was reading in the hospital, so is Vonnegut asserting a block universe, or showing what one is good for when you've been through Dresden? End with what the novel sees about living in that picture that Putnam's argument can't.
