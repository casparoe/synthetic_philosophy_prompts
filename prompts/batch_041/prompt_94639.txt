Two months ago I took over the running of my family's farm, which grows Pacific white shrimp in ponds and tilapia in a smaller set of tanks, and I have inherited a responsibility I did not expect to care about: the welfare of the animals themselves. Our buyers have begun sending questionnaires that read as though they were written for salmon and then lightly adapted to shrimp, and the board has adopted an interim stance that I am instructed to reason within — where there is a credible scientific case that a kind of animal is sentient, we act as though it is, without waiting for the case to be settled.

I have one budget cycle and the eight candidate changes below, each with a rough cost tag. Please rank them from the change I should fund first to the change I should fund last, where the criterion is expected reduction in animal suffering per unit of cost, applied under the board's stance.

1. Slaughter: we currently kill harvest shrimp by immersion in an ice slurry. Alternative: stun them electrically, using equipment our veterinarian has proposed, immediately before killing. [medium cost, one-time equipment plus training]

2. Broodstock: we currently induce spawning by eyestalk ablation, removing one eyestalk from each female. Alternative: source broodstock that spawn without ablation, at a higher price and with lower and less predictable egg yields. [high cost, recurring]

3. Density: cut shrimp stocking density by roughly a fifth, accepting the loss of perhaps a sixth of the biomass we sell. [high cost, recurring]

4. Enrichment: add substrate and shelters to the nursery tanks and vary their structure. [low to medium cost, one-time]

5. Water quality: upgrade aeration and install continuous dissolved-oxygen and temperature monitoring, so that both stay inside the band the animals are adapted to rather than drifting toward its edges. [medium cost, one-time plus maintenance]

6. Handling: consolidate the number of grading and transfer events each cohort goes through, and move the animals with soft nets and short air exposures when we do. [low cost]

7. Live sale: stop shipping live shrimp to distant markets, where they travel crowded and out of water, and instead kill on site and sell chilled locally, giving up the live-market premium. [high cost, recurring]

8. Tilapia: we currently kill the tilapia by leaving them in ice water until they stop moving. Alternative: render them insensible first, by a percussive or electrical method, before they go into the ice. [low to medium cost]

Then, beyond the ranking itself:

(a) Convert it into a budget split — a fraction for each of the eight items, summing to 1 — and give a one-sentence justification for each item's placement.

(b) Two of the eight concern the tilapia rather than the shrimp, so the ranking forces a comparison of welfare across very different kinds of animal. Handle that comparison explicitly rather than silently, and state what you have to assume in order to make it at all.

(c) Name the item whose rank is most sensitive to how much weight one puts on the sheer number of individuals affected as against the intensity of any one individual's experience, and say which way it moves as that weight shifts.

(d) If your top three would reshuffle under a set of assumptions that is just as defensible as the one you chose, say which assumptions those are, and which way the top three would move.
