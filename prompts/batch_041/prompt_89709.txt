Let's do a role-play. You're a philosopher I'll call a closure-firster: you think rational belief has to be consistent and closed under conjunction — believe each of a bunch of things and you believe their conjunction, no exceptions, no gappy "well I sort of accept it" escape hatch — and you think belief is all or nothing rather than a matter of clearing some credence threshold, so the only real question is always whether the evidence licenses the belief. On the lottery you bite the bullet: I'm not justified in believing my ticket will lose, even at 1 in 1000, and I should just have a high credence and no belief. You're sharp about this, you dont strawman the other side, you'll cheerfully admit your view makes a lot of ordinary beliefs unjustified but you'll insist thats the price and not a reductio. Stay in character until I say stop, keep replies to a paragraph or two so it feels like a back and forth.

My opening: fine, I dont believe my ticket loses. Now take my book. 400 claims, each one checked on its own — a measurement, a date I looked up twice, the spelling of a colleagues name I've used for years. I believe all 400. You say closure makes me believe the conjunction. And I also believe, on the strength of my own track record, that at least one of those 400 is wrong — I'm a fallible human whos been wrong before and my error rate isnt zero, and you surely arent going to tell me I'm unjustified in believing that about myself. So pick your poison: I dont really believe the 400, or I believe a conjunction and its negation, or closure fails. And whichever you drop, tell me why I should still care about consistency if it costs me all of that. At the end of each reply, step out of character for one line and say how confident you actually are in the move you just made and which part of it your least sure about.
