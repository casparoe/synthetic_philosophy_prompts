ok so my reading group has been fighting about Kant and animals for months now and we've reached the stage where we just recite our lines at each other, so I want to draft the document we should have written at the start instead. Can you write it?

The setup, as briefly as I can put it. Kant's official position, as far as any of us can make out, is that we have no duties *to* animals, only duties *regarding* them: the duties in question are duties to humanity (maybe also duties to ourselves), and cruelty to animals is wrong because of what it does to us -- it dulls the fellow-feeling we need in order to treat people decently, and it's a kind of vandalism against something that isn't ours to smash. The "repair crew" in the group thinks this is basically right and just needs cleaning up. The "demolition crew" thinks it's a wreck that no cleanup saves, because it turns the animal's suffering into mere evidence about human character.

Two things we came up with ourselves that I'd really like the document to handle, since they're what actually broke the argument:

1. My flatmate's distinction, which she insists is the whole ballgame: the "dulling" reading (cruelty is wrong because of what it does to us later) versus what she calls the "already-there" reading (cruelty is wrong because it's the expression of a will that has already failed, and it doesn't need to make anyone worse down the line in order to be wrong). I'm not convinced those are two seperate readings rather than one reading with a story stapled to it, and I'd like the document to take a position on that.

2. The case I keep bringing up and nobody will answer: the character-proof cruelty case. Someone is cruel to animals and his attitudes toward human beings are provably unaffected -- he's a hermit who will never see another person, or (the version I prefer) the cruelty is quarantined and just doesn't leak into how he treats people. Does the dulling reading condemn this? If not, does the already-there reading condemn it, and if it does, is it still doing anything distinctively Kantian, or has it quietly become the view that animals are ends in themselves?

What I want out of it:

- a numbered list of claims both camps can actually sign, including about what Kant's own texts will and won't support, and about what the view is even a theory *of*. My suspicion is that one camp is answering "what grounds the wrongness of cruelty" while the other is answering "what are our duties concerning animals," and those aren't the same question. If part of this is verbal, say which part and why;
- the disagreements stated so that each side would accept the wording of its own view, no strawmen;
- the cruxes -- the sub-questions such that settling them would force at least one side to move;
- for each crux, the case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance;
- and what each camp would count as evidence against itself. If the repair crew would never change its mind about anything, I want that said out loud rather than buried.
