ok so in bio we watched this ancient video of a digger wasp dragging a paralyzed cricket to its burrow. the move is: it parks the cricket at the door, goes inside to check the burrow, and if you slide the cricket like two inches away while it's in there, it comes back out, finds the cricket, drags it to the door, and goes in to check the burrow again. you can do it over and over and it never once goes "wait, I already checked this." our teacher called that being sphexish. (one kid said the wasp thing is a myth and if you keep it up the wasp eventually changes what its doing. teacher said fine, imagine a machine that loops then.)

so my friend Devon, who is way too into this, built the following and now I can't get out of it:

(1) A thing is sphexish when its behaviour is run by a fixed routine instead of by the goal the routine is for, so you can break it just by messing with the world in a way the routine doesn't check.
(2) Anything with a finite setup, brain or program or whatever, has a fixed number of things it can be checking for.
(3) So for anything finite there's some way of messing with the world that it doesn't check for, and it just keeps going through the motions. Devon calls that a trap.
(4) Anything you can trap is sphexish.
(5) So every finite thing is sphexish.
(6) Sphexish means machine, and machine means not free.
(7) So nothing finite is free. Not the wasp, not us, not some giant AI, nothing.

I said (4) is unfair because people notice the trap and stop. He said fine, then build the trap so it also messes with your memory of the trap, or your attention, or whatever you were gonna use to notice. Then you don't notice, so you're a wasp. And if there's a level above that which you'd use to notice the memory thing, he traps that level too. Traps all the way up.

I tried the calculator thing, like you can "trap" a calculator by smashing it but that doesn't mean calculators can't do math. He says that's different because smashing it breaks it, while the trap leaves you running fine, just wrong. Which is sort of convincing and sort of not, because the memory one does seem to break something.

Heres what bugs me. This proves way too much. It doesn't even need determinism, because if your choices are random instead of fixed that doesn't help at all, a random routine is still a routine. So it kills the free-will people too, and it kills any possible mind whatsoever, which is usually a sign something's off. And if sphexish just means "there exists a trap that gets you," then being sphexish is a fact about how clever the person setting the trap is, not about you.

But every fix I try either makes (4) obviously false in a way I can't define, or it keeps the whole argument. If I say the difference is that we monitor whether the routine is working, Devon says the monitoring is just another routine and it's finite too, and we're back at (2). Which step is actually wrong, and if I give it up, what else do I have to give up with it? I'd like to keep (2) and (3), they seem true, but I can't tell you where the line is between "can be fooled" and "is a machine."
