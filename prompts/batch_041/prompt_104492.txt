I chair the model-release review committee at a mid-sized applied-AI company, and I need a decision memo for our Thursday vote. The case is a clinical decision-support model that drafts triage recommendations for an understaffed emergency-department pilot at two partner hospitals, and the two teams who built it cannot agree on how it should be trained or, more to the point, on what we should certify. Team A trained the model with outcome-based supervision: it was rewarded only for whether the final triage recommendation matched the disposition a panel of physicians assigned in retrospect. Team B trained a variant with process-based supervision, using a reward model that scores each step of the model's reasoning, and they argue this is plainly the safer artifact, because the reasoning is inspectable and the model can be corrected at the level of individual steps. Team A counters that step-level scoring only looks safer: the step reward model was itself fit to labels from a handful of clinicians, and in the cases that matter most — patients whose presentation is atypical, or where the correct disposition is genuinely contested even among experts — the step supervisor will penalize exactly the unconventional reasoning that might be right, while rewarding fluent, step-by-step prose that merely reads like good medicine. I find both worries real, and I don't think use both is automatically the answer, since the two signals will sometimes conflict and something has to arbitrate.

What I want from you is the memo I will circulate before the vote: a clear recommendation — certify the outcome-trained model, certify the process-trained variant, certify neither, or certify one with named conditions — with the reasoning laid out so that both teams could actually respond to it, and with the strongest consideration on the losing side stated fairly rather than strawmanned. Please make the recommendation operational: say what we would monitor after release and what specific finding should trigger a rollback. Two things I would like you to take seriously as more than engineering detail. First, the regress problem: whatever supervises the process is itself an artifact with an objective of its own, so I want you to say what supervises it and why the chain stops there. Second, the deeper issue that neither team will name: in a domain where expert disagreement is the norm, matching the retrospective panel and matching the step-labeller are not measures of correctness but of conformity, and I want the memo to say plainly what, if anything, we can certify under that description — whether the honest recommendation is a narrower claim about the model's competence, or a caution about what the pilot is and is not capable of establishing. Assume that no one on the committee has a philosophy background, but that they will not accept hand-waving.
