I'm the responsible-AI person at a mid-size lender and I've been given the job of writing the fairness spec for a new underwriting model before it goes live. The engineers keep asking "which fairness metric are we using" and I keep dodging, because I don't think you can answer that without first saying what any acceptable metric would have to deliver. So before we pick one, I want the desiderata written out properly.

Here's the situation it has to work in, because I reckon the constraints matter more than the maths. The protected attribute (ethnicity) is self-reported and missing for about a fifth of applicants, so whatever we use has to say something sensible when the attribute is unknown for some people. The label we train on is "defaulted within 24 months", which is a decent proxy for the thing we actually care about but not the same thing, and it's noisier for some groups than others. The regulator audits us at the group level and won't accept an explanation that requires reading individual files. Legal has told us we can't feed the attribute into the model itself, only use it to audit. And we have to report on intersections (say ethnicity x age band) without the report exploding into hundreds of cells nobody can act on.

What I want from you: the list of requirements any fairness criterion has to meet to be usable here -- the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it mustn't collapse, the things it can't contradict -- each with a one-line reason why it's a requirement and not just a preference. Flag clearly which are genuinely non-negotiable and which are just house taste. And here's the bit I actually care about: I'm fairly sure some of these can't all hold at once -- something like you can't have the error rates match across groups, the positive rates match, and the score mean the same thing for everyone, unless the underlying base rates are already equal. So tell me which subsets are jointly unsatisfiable, what the trade-off actually costs you, and which single requirement, if I dropped it, would make the rest consistent. Don't bother scoring the off-the-shelf metrics, I'll do that with the team -- I just want the spec.
