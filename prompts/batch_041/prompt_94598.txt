I am trying to formulate a version of lexical threshold negative utilitarianism, and my verdicts about when pleasure can compensate pain keep pulling in different directions. Here they are. One hour of agony as intense as a whole-body third-degree burn cannot be made acceptable by any finite amount of ecstasy, however long it lasts; the agony has lexical priority. A broken toe for a week can be compensated by a year of intense pleasure, so below some level pains and pleasures do seem commensurable. If that same broken toe is given to each of a million people, I do not think a year of intense pleasure for one person can compensate the total, even though each individual pain is compensable. And if the agony from the first case is reduced very slightly, just enough to fall below some threshold, then a large enough amount of ecstasy can compensate it. What principle, or small set of principles, captures these verdicts? Please state it precisely, say which clause is motivated by which case, what it predicts for cases I have not mentioned, such as a very large number of sub-threshold pains versus one supra-threshold pain or a supra-threshold pain barely above the line versus an enormous quantity of sub-threshold pleasure, and tell me honestly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate. If my verdicts are inconsistent or the cases are too underdescribed to support a principled answer, say so rather than forcing one.
