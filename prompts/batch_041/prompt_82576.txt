Write an essay on the following controversy: whether the inversions of reasoning that Dennett attributes to Darwin and to Turing are the same inversion or two different ones, and whether the analogy between them is strong enough to support the claim that competence can be fully explained without comprehension. In the Darwinian case, the inversion consists in explaining the appearance of design by a process that itself has no foresight; in the Turingian case, it consists in explaining the exercise of intelligence by a machine that itself has no understanding. Your essay should introduce a distinction that is not standard in the literature: the distinction between a horizontal inversion and a vertical inversion. A horizontal inversion reverses the order of explanation along a temporal or causal sequence: instead of explaining the process by the product it was supposed to produce, one explains the product by the process that produced it. A vertical inversion reverses the order of explanation along a level of description: instead of explaining the lower-level mechanism by the higher-level function it was supposed to serve, one explains the higher-level function by the lower-level mechanism that realizes it. The essay should argue that Darwin's inversion is primarily horizontal, that Turing's inversion is primarily vertical, and then assess whether this difference undermines the claim that the two inversions are instances of a single phenomenon. You should reach a conclusion about whether the two inversions are the same in kind, and whether the analogy between them licenses the stronger claim that all competence is ultimately competence without comprehension. Justify your conclusion by considering at least two objections: one from the side of those who think the Darwinian inversion is not really an inversion at all because natural selection is a process that produces design without being a reasoner, and one from the side of those who think the Turingian inversion is not really an inversion at all because a Turing machine only computes what it is designed to compute. The essay should not merely survey the literature; it should take a position and defend it. Do not rely on citations or bibliographies; the argument should stand on its own.
