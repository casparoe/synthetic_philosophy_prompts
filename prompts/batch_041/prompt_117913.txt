Please explain a conceptual issue in the philosophy of statistical mechanics, then bring your explanation to bear on a distinction I want to introduce.

The issue is this. A phase transition is standardly identified with a non-analyticity in a thermodynamic potential — a divergence of the susceptibility, a discontinuity in an order parameter, and the like — as a control parameter is varied. In any finite system, however, the partition function is analytic in the control parameters, so no such non-analyticity can occur; and real systems are finite. So either transitions are not real features of finite systems, or the standard identification is mistaken, or 'the transition' names something that only the infinite-volume limit possesses. Explain what is at stake: what the appeal to the thermodynamic limit is doing, whether it is an idealisation in the ordinary sense, and why it matters that the limit is taken at fixed density rather than by letting the volume grow alone. Say also whether the renormalisation-group account of universality — which locates universality in fixed points of a transformation on a space of Hamiltonians — is available to someone who refuses to treat the infinite-volume limit as more than a calculational convenience, given that finite systems have no fixed points.

Then the distinction I want to introduce. Call a property asymptotically emergent if it is had by the limit of a sequence of systems but by no member of the sequence, and pointwise emergent if it is had by each member but is not derivable from the lower-level description of that member taken on its own. Where does the boiling of a beaker of water fall? Is the divergence of the susceptibility asymptotically emergent, pointwise emergent, both, or neither? And if universality is only defined in the limit, what is a finite system's near-universal behaviour an approximation to?

Finally, say which of the two notions, if either, should matter to someone who wants emergence to be a metaphysical thesis about the world rather than a fact about our models, and say plainly where you take the case for realism about phase transitions to be strongest and where weakest. Please write in British English.
