I keep circling back to Hofstadter's careenium — that big frictionless box full of tiny magnetized marbles that bounce around and every so often clump into higher-level structures he calls simmballs, which are meant to be his stand-ins for symbols and concepts. The whole point of the setup is the question of who's really pushing who in there: do the individual marbles push the simmballs around, or do the simmballs push the marbles around? Hofstadter's line seems to be that the simmball level is where the genuine causal story lives, even though it's the marbles that do alot of the literal bumping.

I'd like you to compare that with the causal exclusion argument (the Kim-style one: since every physical effect already has a fully sufficient physical cause, any higher-level cause that isn't just identical with its physical realizer gets squeezed out, unless you're willing to accept rampant overdetermination). Are the careenium picture and the exclusion argument actually in conflict, or are they answering different questions and just talking past each other? Then commit: in the careenium, do the simmballs — the symbols — push the substrate around, or don't they?

And here's the wrinkle that keeps me from settling it. Suppose the same simmball can be realized by two totally different arrangements of marbles, so an experimenter can reach in at the marble level, take one simmball's micro-realization apart, and yet the simmball-level story — what it represents, what it does to neighboring simmballs — comes out the same. Does that multiplicity turn the simmball into a seperate cause (Kim's way of seeing it), or is it just a coarser description of the very same marble-level goings-on (Hofstadter's way)? I want your actual verdict on this, with the reasons for it — not a menu of positions. American English, please.
