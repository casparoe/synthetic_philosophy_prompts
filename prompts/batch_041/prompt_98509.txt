I am a secondary school student in a debate society, and our next motion concerns whether the British Empire was, on balance, beneficial or harmful to India. I have seen this debate associated with Niall Ferguson on one side and Shashi Tharoor on the other, but I care about the arguments rather than the personalities, and I do not need citations, titles, or a reading list. One camp points to railways, legal unification, the abolition of sati, and the creation of a modern state apparatus; the other points to deindustrialization, the drain of wealth, devastating famines, and a legacy of division. I would like you to write the joint statement that a committed defender and a committed critic could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Please include a numbered section of claims both sides can accept, about what is agreed on the facts, the counterfactual, and the standards of assessment; a precise statement of each remaining disagreement, phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own position; the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; and, for each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I suspect one crux is the counterfactual baseline, since we cannot simply compare British India with an imaginary unchanged India; another is how to aggregate gains to some groups against losses to others; and another is whether the relevant unit of assessment is the Indian peasant, the Indian economy as a whole, or the British taxpayer. I would also like you to say whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal, and if so, which part.

Format the response as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. Please do not include a bibliography or suggestions for further reading; I want the reasoning and the drafting itself.
