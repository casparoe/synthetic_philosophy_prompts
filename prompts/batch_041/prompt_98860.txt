I am a secondary-school student preparing for a debate in my philosophy of science class, and I should like to commission something more careful than the usual summary. Before I commit to a side on the pessimistic meta-induction — the argument that, because even our most successful past theories posited unobservable entities that later turned out not to exist, we should not believe that our present best theories describe the unobservable world correctly — I want a specification of what any adequate response to that argument would have to accomplish. Please write the desiderata.

The requirements themselves should be the substance of the answer; I am not asking for a survey of who has said what. For each requirement, state what the account must deliver, the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and whether it is non-negotiable or merely a preference of a particular camp. Where you think one of my suggested requirements is not a genuine requirement but a prejudice of one side, say so and explain why.

I should like the list to include at least the following kinds of requirement, though please treat my list as suggestive rather than exhaustive:

- a requirement about the historical record: what the account must say about the sequence of past theories, and why it is entitled to read that record as it does;
- a requirement about the present: what the account licenses us to believe about our current theories, and whether it can do so without circularity;
- a requirement about how the account individuates theories and their posits across change, since the meta-induction's premise depends on whether a given posit was abandoned or instead retained under a new description;
- a requirement about self-application: whether the account survives having the meta-induction turned upon itself, given that the meta-induction is itself a claim drawn from the history of science;
- a requirement about content: if the account appeals to notions such as approximate truth, partial retention or structural continuity, it must say what these amount to in a way that is more than a promissory note.

As test cases, I should like the account to be checked against the replacement of phlogiston by oxygen, of caloric by the mechanical theory of heat, and of the luminiferous aether by special relativity.

The part I most want is the tension: identify any pair or larger subset of these requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied, and explain precisely why. If the list is internally inconsistent, then the list is itself the result, and I would far rather know that than receive a tidy scorecard.

Please give the answer as a table with the following columns, one row per requirement: Requirement (a short label); What it demands; Motivating case or consideration; Status (non-negotiable, or the camp that holds it); Most sharply constrains (which families of response it rules out). After the table, give a JSON object with the fields "unsatisfiable_subsets" (an array, each entry an object with "requirements", listing the labels involved, and "reason"), "most_exclusionary_requirement" (the label of the single requirement that excludes the most candidates, as a string), and "verdicts" (an object mapping each of selective realism, structural realism and entity realism to a single sentence on how it fares against the list).

Please write in British English.
