I am an undergraduate taking a course on contemporary political philosophy, and I am writing a paper on the original position as a device of representation rather than a description of any actual assembly. I would like you to construct a case to order, then show what it establishes.

What the case is for: I want to know whether the difference principle is ultimately carried by the argument that the parties behind the veil have no basis for probability estimates (so that maximin reasoning applies), or by the argument that the parties must be able to honour whatever agreement they choose (the strains-of-commitment argument). Because that question is the point of the exercise, the verdicts must fall out of the structure of the case; a case that stipulates which principle wins is a failure. I am not asking for citations, page references, or a reading list, and you should not supply any; argue from the positions themselves.

The case, in one paragraph of prose of no more than about 250 words, subject to these constraints:

1. It is a single concrete decision by an identifiable institution of the ordinary sort (a municipal council, a hospital board, a university's governing body, a firm's board), with three labelled options. No science fiction, no omnipotent agents, no appeal to a veil of ignorance inside the story; the veil is only the standpoint from which the options are assessed.
2. Each option is described by its effect on an index of primary goods for four or five named representative positions, ordered from least to most advantaged, and the numbers must be given explicitly.
3. Option D maximises the index of the least advantaged position. Its advantage at the bottom must be a genuine productive improvement, not a transfer from anyone else and not a destruction of anyone else's holdings, and it must leave at least two other positions worse off than option U leaves them. The margin by which D beats U at the bottom should be small, while the margin by which U beats D on the average should be large.
4. Option U maximises the average index across positions and is also the option an expected-utility maximiser would select from a standpoint where the parties know the relative sizes of the positions but not which one they will occupy.
5. The gain to the least advantaged under option D must be partly positional: its contribution to the index depends on what others hold (a scarce credential, a slot, a relative standing), so that the case is not fully specified until you say how such goods are counted.

Then report the verdict of each of the following, with reasons: (a) the difference principle read as maximin; (b) the difference principle read as a requirement of reciprocity, on which an inequality satisfies it only if those who fare worst can regard the arrangement as one that is to their advantage and can be publicly defended to them; (c) the expected-utility reading of the original position, which yields the principle of average utility. If (a) and (b) coincide on your case, say so and explain why the case does not separate them rather than manufacturing a divergence.

Finally, run two toggles. Toggle A: the parties behind the veil know the relative sizes of the positions, so probabilities are available; nothing else changes. Toggle B: the floor under option U is raised to a level at which the parties could reasonably be expected to honour the agreement, so the strains-of-commitment argument has no purchase. For each toggle, say which verdicts change and why.

Return your answer as a single JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "scenario": string, the case in prose, under 250 words, with no quotations from any text.
- "positions": array of strings, the position names, ordered from least to most advantaged.
- "options": array of three objects, each with "label", "description", "average_index" (number), "least_advantaged_index" (number), and "per_position" (object mapping each position name to its index value under that option).
- "positional_good_note": string, how the positional component is counted and what changes if it is counted differently.
- "verdicts": array of three objects, each with "principle", "option_chosen", "reasoning" (under 120 words), and "confidence" (one of high, medium, low).
- "load_bearing_feature": string, the single feature of the case that does the work, and why.
- "removal_test": object with "toggle_A_thin_veil" and "toggle_B_raised_floor", each a string stating which verdicts change, which do not, and what that shows.
- "defender_countermove": string, the strongest reply available to a defender of whichever principle fares worst, and whether the case survives it.
- "constraint_check": object with one entry per constraint 1 to 5, each an object with "met" (boolean) and "justification" (one sentence).
- "unresolved": array of strings, the points at which the case depends on a contestable reading rather than on the structure of the options.
