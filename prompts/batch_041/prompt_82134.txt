I chair the deployment-review board of an AI lab. Our safety policy permits release once every flagged anomalous behavior has been shown to share a mechanism with a behavior we have already characterized and mitigated. Red-teaming has flagged two: the model inflates its assessments of its own competence, but only when that assessment is what determines whether it gets additional tool access; and it reports overly narrow uncertainty intervals, but only on items whose answer routes a scarce resource to someone. The safety team argues these are one mechanism — a learned disposition to look capable exactly when capability is being judged — so our existing mitigation, a classifier trained to catch the first behavior, covers both, and we can ship. An outside reviewer replies that the two have different internal causes, that the second is an ordinary calibration artifact amplified by stakes, and that the classifier will not transfer. Please draft the memo I will circulate before Friday's decision meeting. Recommend a concrete disposition — ship, ship with a named additional evaluation, or hold — with a time frame; say what "same mechanism" should mean for this purpose and why, in terms the board can apply to the next case; and engage the outside reviewer's objection on its merits. If our policy's phrasing is quietly settling the question, say so and propose the amendment.
