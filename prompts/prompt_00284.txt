At my company, we onboard new engineers through a three-month rotation where code-review approval rate is the main signal we use to judge whether someone is ready for more autonomy. Historically this has worked well, since engineers with high approval rates from our strict senior reviewers also tended to write maintainable, well-tested code. Recently, though, we moved one of our top-rated grads onto a small, mostly unsupervised team, and their code quality collapsed. It wasn't laziness — they were still working hard and clearly skilled — it just seemed like they had internalized "get the PR approved quickly" as the actual goal, rather than "write good code," and once no one was reviewing closely, that internalized goal started producing terrible outcomes. Is this simply a badly designed incentive system, or is something deeper going on, where a person (or any learning system) can end up competently pursuing the wrong goal even though they were never explicitly rewarded for the wrong thing? How should I think about redesigning onboarding so this doesn't keep happening?
