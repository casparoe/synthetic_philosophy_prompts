I need a short poem to close a reading-group session on akrasia and present-biased discounting — 20 to 30 lines, no fixed meter, but with one constraint that matters: a single refrain line whose appearances crowd together as the poem goes on. Have it first show up every eight lines or so, then four, then two, then twice in the same stanza, and then in the final line. The crowding-in is meant to be the actual point: with hyperbolic discounting the same option gets steeper the nearer it is, so a line that was comfortably spaced out at the start ends up jammed against itself, whereas a constant-rate (exponential) discounter keeps the ordering stable and the refrain would stay evenly spaced.

Content-wise: one person, one decision, small and concrete stakes — a paper, a run, a drink — and nothing about the options changes between stanzas except how near they are, since that's what makes it a reversal rather than a change of mind. Make the speaker a sophisticated agent, not a naive one: they can see the reversal coming and have already set something up in advance (deleted the app, handed over the keys, whatever), and the poem should end with that precommitment either holding or being wriggled around.

Two things I dont want: any line about the "real" self or about who really wills, and any moral. If there's a verdict at all it should be the negative one, that there's no further fact about which temporal slice speaks for the person. Someone who knows the literature should be able to tell that the naive/sophisticated distinction is in there and why the refrain spacing is doing the modelling, but it should still just work as a poem read out loud.
