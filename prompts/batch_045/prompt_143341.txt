Hey, I'm on the student committee that runs our school's debate invitational and we have to settle something before the March tournament. Last year a team from Germany kept using 'liberal' and 'liberalism' in the European sense — free markets, small state, suspicion of government telling people how to live — and our judges, who are all American, scored them as if they'd meant the American sense (welfare state, culturally left, more regulation). They dropped two rounds over it and complained, and half the committee thinks the judges were right anyway since it's an American tournament.

One member wants to just ban the word and make people name the actual policy instead ('single-payer', 'scrap rent control', whatever). I half like that, but I think the word also does something a policy list doesn't: it points at a stance about why coercion needs justifying at all — roughly, that the state should stay neutral between people's different ideas of a good life — and that stance is the bit both sides of the Atlantic still share even though their policy bundles have basically flipped.

Could you draft the one-page memo I'll send the committee recommending what we should actually do? I'm leaning toward a rule where anyone using the word has to declare which sense they mean in their first minute, and judges get told to penalize the listener's confusion rather than the speaker's, but argue it properly and deal honestly with the strongest thing on the other side — I think it's that baking a declared sense into the rules freezes one contested definition and quietly punishes anyone whose real argument is that the term is a mess. Needs a concrete bottom line and a date it kicks in.
