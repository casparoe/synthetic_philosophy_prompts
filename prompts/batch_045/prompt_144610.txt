I'm a novelist — third book, running long overdue — and there's a scene I can't get right because it turns on a bit of genetics, and I'd rather the character who explains it sounds like someone who actually knows the stuff than like someone who skimmed a summary page five minutes before.

Setup: my protagonist, Mira, was born on an offshore island and adopted at birth by a family inland. There's a trait in my world called tide-sense, an uncanny knack for reading the tide off tiny cues, and a study of the island population puts its heritability at 0.8. Late in the book a geneticist sits Mira down and explains what that number does and doesn't tell her. I've been modelling tide-sense on height, which I gather sits somewhere around 80% too.

The line I've got her defining it with is: "the share of the differences between people in a population that comes down to genetic differences." I'm fairly confident about that part, and it's what the rest of the scene hangs on. What follows from it, in my head, is this. First, it means 80% of Mira's own tide-sense was caused by her genes and at most 20% by how she was raised, so there's a hard ceiling of 20% on how much training or a different childhood could ever have moved her. Second, if the same study had been run on children all raised in identical conditions, the number would come out lower, maybe near zero, because with everyone's upbringing the same there'd be no environmental variation left for genes to beat. Third, the number is a stable property of the trait, close to a constant, so it carries over: the islanders score higher on average than mainlanders, and since Mira grew up in a seperate environment entirely, most of that gap, and most of whatever she has, must be genetic. The leftover 20% I've been treating as just "environment," meaning upbringing and schooling.

Does any of that hold up? I'd definately rather find out now than after copy-edit, since if the scene is built on a mistake I lose most of a chapter. And if it is wrong, I'd love a line or two of dialogue I could steal for the geneticist — something with the right rhythm for speech, not a textbook sentence. No papers or links, please, just the reasoning. And no bold, no headings, no bullet points, no tables: write it out as continuous prose, the way you'd write an email to a friend who happens to know genetics.
