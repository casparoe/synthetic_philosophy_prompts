hey — I'm in a third-year philosophy of physics course (we did EPR and Bell a few weeks back, now we're moving into the measurement problem) and I want a consistency check on where I've ended up about the psi-ontic / psi-epistemic question before I commit to it for the final essay. Roughly where I've landed: (1) I'm a psi-epistemicist in the overlapping-distributions sense our TA drew on the board — the wavefunction isn't a thing in the world, two distinct pure states can be compatible with the same underlying ontic state, and psi is more like a probability distribution over the ontic state than like a field. (2) The reason I like this is basically the stat-mech analogy: psi is to the ontic state as a Liouville distribution is to a point in phase space. That's what made it click. (3) I'm a realist about the ontic state — there are hidden variables, they're what's really there, and I've allready swallowed that Bell forces some kind of nonlocality on me, so I'm fine paying that. (4) But when I actually picture a Bell experiment I keep wanting to say: the pair leaves the source with a shared ontic state, and nothing Alice does changes anything physical on Bob's side; there's no influence travelling, there's just a correlation that was already baked in at the source. I know that sounds like exactly the thing Bell kills, but it's the verdict I keep coming back to. (5) The PBR theorem doesn't move me much, because I just deny preparation independence, and I think denying it is cheap: if I've accepted nonlocal hidden variables anyway, why balk at correlations between supposedly independent preparations? (6) Here's my own pet gloss, which my study group has been making fun of: psi isn't a probability distribution over ontic states, it's a *square root* of one — a vector of amplitudes whose squared moduli are the probabilities — so interference is just the fact that square roots of probabilities don't add the way probabilities do. I like it because it keeps psi as bookkeeping while explaining why it has more structure than a plain probability distribution. (7) Separately, I think psi is obviously causally efficacious in the boring sense: we manipulate it in the lab, you can do interference, teleportation, quantum computation with it. (8) And methodologically, I'd much rather add structure to the world — extra variables, whatever — than accept irreducible objective chance.

One more thing, which is a half-memory and might be garbled: I have a feeling someone proved that even setting PBR aside, the psi-epistemic models we can actually construct only reproduce some fragment of quantum predictions, not the full theory. Don't take my word for it.

What I'd like from you: go through that list and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, ranked by severity, and for each one what the cheapest fix is — which commitment has to bend and what it costs me. Please don't just say 'it depends what you mean by epistemic' for every item: I want the pairs that only LOOK inconsistent separated out from the real problems (I suspect the psi-is-causally-efficacious one is fine and I just can't say why, and I suspect the Wigner's-friend-style case where two observers assign different wavefunctions is fine too, but I'm not confident). I'm honestly not attached to any particular item and I don't know where the rot is, so please don't assume I'd give up (1) rather than, say, (2) or (4).
