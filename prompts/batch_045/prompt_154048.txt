I run a small reading group — two philosophers, two cognitive-science grad students, an ML engineer, and me — that has spent about a year on the classical-versus-connectionist dispute over whether cognition is computation over language-like representations: the language-of-thought hypothesis in something like its Fodorian form, with concatenative syntax, constituent structure, the same constituent tokened and re-tokened across different thoughts, and systematicity and productivity falling out of that structure rather than being trained in from the data. Two of our members, a committed classicist and a committed wide connectionist of the embodied-dynamical stripe, have now argued each other into a stalemate, and I want to convert the stalemate into a document rather than another evening of talking past each other. To keep this from becoming a citation contest, the group has stipulated an evidence base that neither side may dispute — they may only interpret it: (i) a filler-diversity curve, replicated across a transformer, a recurrent net, and a hybrid symbolic model on an artificial grammar of pseudoword functors and contentives, in which accuracy at novel role reversals is a steep, saturating function of how many distinct fillers occupied each role in training — near chance below roughly fifty distinct fillers, above ninety percent past a couple of thousand — with the curves nearly identical across architectures except for a persistent tail of role-confusion errors in the neural models; (ii) a re-encoding result from intracranial and MEG decoding, on pairs like John loves Mary versus Mary loves John, where a decoder trained to read a constituent's identity out of its agent-role representation fails to read that identity out of its theme-role representation, while a separately fitted linear map between the two role formats succeeds; (iii) a re-binding probe in which the subject must answer a query about a constituent from a premise that has been masked, showing graded interference from the number of intervening constituents with no sharp capacity break, plus a small fixed cost for role-swapped constituents that does not vary with semantic similarity; and (iv) a lesion dissociation in which focal damage to left inferior frontal cortex selectively disrupts non-adjacent dependency binding while sparing adjacent dependencies and single-word semantics.

Now the deliverable. Draft the joint statement these two could both sign at the end of a good-faith adversarial collaboration — we try to run things on the old blogosphere norms: agreed claims first, cruxes, predictions written down before anyone looks at the data, no motte-and-bailey. I want, as a numbered section, the claims both can genuinely sign: about the formalism (for instance, that any adequate model must implement some map from structured contents to internal states), about what needs explaining (the four stipulated results above), and about what those results rule out — my guess is that a flat feature-bag associationism is dead, but so is any story on which the brain literally buffers Mentalese sentences in a neural register. Then a table of the residual disagreements, each row giving the crux, the classicist's own formulation in wording she would accept, the connectionist's own formulation in wording he would accept, and whether the dispute at that point is empirical or verbal. I suspect at least four cruxes: whether graded, diversity-dependent systematicity is even the phenomenon the classical argument demanded or a weaker cousin of it; whether re-encoding across roles is fatal to constituent identity or only to a naive requirement of vehicle-identity, given that content-identity can survive format change; whether architectural priors that induce systematic generalization from data explain it or merely smuggle in the structure the classicist wanted; and whether some of this is a clash of Marr-level descriptions rather than of theories, so that nothing empirical could settle it. For each crux, name one concrete probe both would accept as bearing on it — design a follow-up if you need to, say a pseudoword-grammar battery with held-out roles, or a version of the re-binding probe with crossed semantic and syntactic factors — and record in advance what each camp predicts it will show and what result would make each of them give ground.

Two more things. Please flag explicitly any row where you think they are arguing about words rather than about the world, and say what observation would reveal that. And add a short closing section answering this: if a third position — a vector-symbolic architecture that binds roles to fillers in distributed superpositions, or an instrumentalist reading on which structure is a property of the best description rather than of the mechanism — undercuts part of the debate, say which part, and whether either of my two members should have been occupying that ground all along.
