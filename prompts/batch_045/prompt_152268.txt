hey — I'm writing my third chapter right now (PhD in philosophy, aesthetics; specifically on whether an artwork's value can survive there being nobody who felt anything while making it), and I've been going through Tolstoy's What Is Art? again. I want to use him as a kind of stress test for generative AI rather than just wheel him out as a citation, and I dont trust my own sense of which bit of his theory would bite.

Roughly, the picture I'm working from: art is someone who has lived through a feeling calling it up again and fixing it in external signs so that other people get infected by it; sincerity — actually feeling the thing you convey — is what makes infection work; the best art carries the feeling of brotherhood / what he calls the religious perception, and is accessible to ordinary people without any training; and the junk of his own day he sorts using those four markers (borrowing, imitation, effectfulness, diversion), on top of a lot of rage at the professionalisation of art — the schools, the workshops, the critics, the whole industry turning out technically brilliant work with nothing behind it. He also throws most of his own fiction on that pile, I think.

Now imagine we could explain generative models to him — not the tech, just this: you type a request, a machine trained on everything humans have already made spits out a new song or picture, fast, cheap, anyone can do it, and no human being felt anything at any point in the making.

Three cases I keep turning over:

(1) Someone types "a song that makes ordinary people feel they belong to each other", takes the first thing the model gives them, posts it. It genuinely lands — people report being moved, they sing it at weddings, it does the unifying work Tolstoy wants the highest art to do, more reliably on today's audiences than Uncle Tom's Cabin does. Nobody felt anything making it. Art or the ultimate counterfeit? I cant tell whether for him infection is the test or sincerity is, and whether he'd say the audience is really infected or just stimulated in the way he complains about under "effectfulness".

(2) A human artist works with a model for months, generates thousands of outputs, keeps the one that makes her cry, and says the model is her medium — she genuinely feels what the finished piece conveys, she just didn't make the marks. Does the sincerity condition get satisfied by the selection? Or is this precisely the professional technique-and-craft he hated, except now the machine does the craft?

(3) An amateur village choir learns an AI-generated melody and sings it, and they really do feel something while singing. Tolstoy liked folk song and he liked performers — would he just relocate the art to the performance and say the melody is raw material?

And the bigger thing I can't settle: Tolstoy seems to want art to become something everyone does, with no priests of art and no specialists, and he wants it to unite humanity. Generative AI delivers the accessibility and the reach and destroys the specialists — and it is also the most extreme version of the thing he despised: feeling manufactured by machinery, in a factory, with no one behind it. Would he read it as the disease arriving at its final stage, or as accidentally realising part of what he was asking for?

So: what would Tolstoy actually say about AI-generated art, and where do those three cases land with him? Give me them ranked from "most likely to get past him" to "least likely", say what a Tolstoyan would have to swallow in each case, and flag which part of his theory you think is doing the work in your answer versus where you're just extrapolating. No need for chapter numbers or citations, i want the argument.
