I left school at sixteen and have worked as a self-employed electrician ever since; for the last six years I have spent my evenings reading philosophy on my own, mostly books borrowed from the city library. Two years ago I started running a fortnightly reading group at the library, and the adult-education centre that lets us use its room has now asked me to set and mark the written assessment for the follow-on course. They describe the level as second-year undergraduate, by which they tell me they mean students who have completed one introductory course. The assessment is a single essay written in ninety minutes, worth 20 marks, and the students will have no books or notes in front of them.

The topic I want to set it on is Putnam's Twin Earth thought experiment: the suggestion that what a word like water picks out is not settled by anything inside the speaker's head. My difficulty is that every question I can find on this topic can be answered almost in full by retelling the story of the two planets and the two chemically different liquids, and then writing down the slogan about meanings not being in the head. I want a question where doing only that earns about a third of the marks and no more.

To that end I have invented two variations of my own, and I would rather the question were built around them than around any case from the literature.

Variation one, the switched planet. Suppose a planet that, up to the year 1900, was exactly like Earth: the drinkable liquid in its lakes and rivers and rain is H2O. Over the following generation every sample of it is quietly replaced by the Twin Earth liquid — same colour, smell and taste, same behaviour when boiled or frozen or used to dissolve things, same role in keeping living things alive, but chemically different. Nobody notices, and there is no chemistry on the planet. The word the community uses for the liquid is handed down from parents to children in an unbroken chain going back well before 1900. Today a speaker on that planet says that the liquid is good for the crops. Does her word pick out H2O, the new liquid, both at once, or nothing? What I want students to see is that the answer depends on which mechanism they think does the reference-fixing work — the nature of the stuff currently around her and in her cup, or the history of how the word travelled down to her — and that the slogan by itself settles nothing.

Variation two, the mixed planet. Suppose a planet on which two chemically different liquids both occur naturally, in roughly equal quantity, in different lakes, indistinguishable from one another without chemical analysis, and used for all the same purposes by the same community — a community that has a single word covering both and no idea that there are two. Does that word pick out both liquids taken together, one of them, or neither? And what does the answer there tell you about the recipe that says a word of this kind picks out whatever has the same underlying nature as the samples around the speaker?

Then the part I care about most. Whatever the students conclude about words, I want them to say whether anything follows about what these speakers think: whether the two Oscars believe different things, or whether the argument has only ever shown something about reference. I want them to state explicitly what further premise would be needed to get from the one conclusion to the other, if it can be reached at all.

Please write for me: the essay question itself, self-contained, since the students will not have read anything beyond the original Twin Earth story and everything else they need must be supplied in the question; a model answer of about 800 words; and a marking scheme built from five must-hit points, four marks each, with a one-line note under each on how students typically get that point wrong, plus a short note on what a bare pass looks like and what an excellent answer adds beyond it. Please do not let any marks depend on naming philosophers or papers — I cannot verify references, and I do not want anyone gaining marks for recollection rather than reasoning. And since I will be applying the scheme myself, please define in a line or two any technical term you use in it.
