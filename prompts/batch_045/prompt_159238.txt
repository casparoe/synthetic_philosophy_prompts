I host a podcast about science and ideas, and on Thursday I'm recording an interview with the chair of the city's franchise review commission — the body that spent two years quietly rewriting who gets to vote in municipal elections here. Her voting record so far:

- she pushed through lowering the municipal voting age to 16, but voted against going down to 14;
- she backed letting non-citizen permanent residents vote in city elections once they've lived here five years, but opposed the same for people on two-year work visas with no route to staying;
- she voted down a proposal to give parents extra proxy votes they'd cast on behalf of their under-16 kids;
- she supported compulsory voting for municipal elections — token fine, but with an official "abstain" box on the ballot.

The commission is now writing the implementing regulations, and the fight is over weather the turnout requirement reaches the people the commission just enfranchised. Concretely: does a 17-year-old have to show up? Does a green-card holder who's been here seven years? Does a 16-year-old who is herself a permanent resident, both at once? She's been slippery about this in public and I dont want to get a shrug on tape.

So: what rules fit all four of those earlier votes? I can think of two or three candidates — something about having a durable stake in the place, something about competence or political judgment, something about voting being a duty you owe the people you live among — but I can't tell which one she's running, or whether any of them actually forces an answer. Tell me what each of those principles predicts about the three new cases, give me your best guess at what she'll say and why, and then give me the one hypothetical case I should put to her on Thursday that would make it impossible for her to keep all three principles at once. The shape of the view interests me a lot more than the verdicts.
