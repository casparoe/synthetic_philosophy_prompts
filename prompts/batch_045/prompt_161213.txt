Here is my question.

I build recommender systems for a living and read philosophy of mind on the side, and I keep running into one configuration that I have no word for. I will describe it concretely and then say why the things people have pointed me to do not quite fit.

Background I am working from: Dennett's three stances, where describing a system as having beliefs and desires is defended by its predictive payoff, and the idea that something is a real pattern in a data set if there is a description of the data more compact than the data itself, so that pattern-hood is a matter of compressibility rather than of what the stuff is made of.

The configuration. Take a system I am modelling. Sometimes the belief-desire description is not merely cheaper or more tractable than the mechanistic description; it is strictly better even in principle. Hand me the complete lower-level state of the system and unlimited computation, and I would predict worse than someone working with the folk-psychological description, because the system's actual future trajectory is a function of what the prediction was. Concretely: (1) I predict that users will click on a certain kind of item; once the system starts serving on that prediction, the users' behaviour moves toward the prediction, and the belief-desire description gets more accurate rather than less. (2) A colleague published a model predicting congestion on a particular route; after the prediction became public, the predicted congestion stopped materialising, and materialised somewhere else instead. In both cases the pattern's status seems to depend on whether the predictor's own output is inside the system being predicted. There is also a mirror-image case that I suspect is the same phenomenon seen from the other side: a pattern that compresses historical data beautifully, passes the compressibility test with room to spare, and then stops being a pattern the moment the predictor that found it starts acting in the world, because acting on it changes what the data does.

Cases I think are *not* it, and want adjudicated: a household thermostat, which reacts to the world but not to anything I say about it; a system that reacts to being probed, where the disturbance depends only on the fact of measurement and not on the content of what was predicted; and a human subject who changes behaviour merely because she knows she is in a study, before anyone has predicted anything specific about her.

Terms I have been pointed at, none of which seem exactly right: looping effects of human kinds; self-fulfilling prophecy; reflexivity as discussed about financial markets; the performativity thesis in economic sociology; the saying about a measure becoming a target and thereby ceasing to be a good measure; anticipatory systems and second-order cybernetics; and the game-theoretic distinction between predicting a system that models you back and one that does not.
