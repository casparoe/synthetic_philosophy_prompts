I have been reading about panpsychism as a response to the hard problem of consciousness, and I want to get clear on why the combination problem is treated as a serious objection to it, rather than as a promissory research programme of the ordinary kind.

My understanding of the position is this: panpsychism denies that experience arises from wholly non-experiential physical stuff, and holds instead that the microphysical relata of physics already have some minimal experiential nature, with our macroscopic consciousness composed out of theirs. The objection is that we have no account of the composition. What I do not understand is why this is supposed to be harder than composition anywhere else in nature.

Mass and charge are additive. Temperature, liquidity, rigidity, transparency, wetness and the like are macro-properties that are nothing over and above arrangements of microphysical parts and their relations. In those cases nobody thinks there is a special difficulty; we think there is a mechanistic or realization story to be told, and once chemistry and condensed-matter physics told it, the appearance of a mystery dissolved. So the panpsychist seems to owe exactly the same kind of bridge story — a specification of which arrangements of micro-experiences realize which macro-experiences — and I do not see why we should assume in advance that it cannot be delivered, or why its current absence counts against the view rather than against our patience.

Two further considerations push me the same way. First, if the macro-property is realized in the micro-arrangement, then the macro-fact looks like it comes for free: once the micro-experiences and their relations are fixed, the macro-experience is fixed too, just as the liquidity of a sample of water is fixed once its molecules and their circumstances are. Second, the objection looks dialectically unstable, because the emergentist who presses it still owes an account of how experience arises from non-experience, and that looks like a strictly harder instance of the very same explanatory demand.

There also seems to me to be a cheap way out, which makes me suspect I am missing something. If micro-subjects really cannot compose a macro-subject, why not start at the top instead: take the cosmos as a whole to be the one fundamental conscious thing, and derive our experience from it by some kind of decomposition or restriction? That appears to sidestep the combination problem entirely, and I am not sure why more panpsychists do not simply take this route and be done with it.

So my question is: what exactly is supposed to be distinctive about the combination problem? Is it anything more than the general demand that a constitutive hierarchy supply its own bridge laws — a demand that physicalism has met elsewhere and that panpsychists can reasonably be expected to meet in time? Where precisely does my analogy with liquidity, temperature and rigidity break down, and why does the top-down variant not dissolve the objection?

Please begin your answer with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning. No preamble, and no restatement of the question.
