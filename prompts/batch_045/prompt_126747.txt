I am scoping a three-year, four-person research program whose single aim is to determine, as far as three years of desk work can, whether habitat destruction in terrestrial ecosystems reduces the total quantity of suffering experienced by wild animals. By "determine" I mean: hand a funder a sign, a rough magnitude, an honest error bar, and an explicit statement of which unresolved question the answer is most sensitive to. If the honest finding is that the sign of the effect is not knowable at this budget, I want that conclusion too, argued rather than gestured at.

Before you set the agenda, please work inside the following framing commitments, which I treat as settled for the program rather than as open questions. Where you think one of them is actually doing hidden work, say so, but don't spend the agenda on it.

First, welfare accounting is sentience-weighted and time-integrated. The quantity of interest is the expected balance of unpleasant over pleasant experience, summed over all welfare subjects in a landscape and integrated forward over that landscape's whole future trajectory, discounted only in whatever way you think is defensible. Snapshot comparisons of "how much suffering is there now" are not admissible answers.

Second, the unit of comparison is a landscape-trajectory, not a population. Because the animals alive in a cleared and regenerating plot are numerically different animals from those who would have lived had the plot stayed intact, net welfare cannot be computed as a difference over a fixed set of individuals; it has to be built out of counterfactual trajectories. Call the methodological difficulty this creates the non-identity problem for habitats. I want the program to have a position on what the right construction is.

Third, keep three counterfactuals separate and do not average across them: preservation versus destruction followed by abandonment; preservation versus conversion to a productive use whose own welfare profile (livestock, crops, plantation, ground-mounted solar, and so on) has to be estimated in the same currency; and preservation versus destruction where the land would in any case have been lost later to climate-driven regime shift. Tell me which one the program should treat as its headline case and why.

Fourth, trace two quantities that are easy to conflate: the sentience density of a landscape, meaning expected welfare subjects per unit of primary productivity weighted by your credence that each taxon is a welfare subject at all; and the welfare quality of an average subject-year in that landscape. A disturbed landscape can be far denser in welfare subjects and much worse per subject-year. Call the gap between these two numbers the abundance trap, and note that almost every popular argument on this topic silently picks one of them.

Fifth, adopt as a revisable working hypothesis the claim that early-successional and human-modified landscapes are dominated by small-bodied, high-fecundity, short-lived animals whose juvenile mortality is enormous, whereas intact late-successional systems hold fewer, larger, longer-lived ones. Treat the program as entitled to overthrow this, but not entitled to ignore it.

Given all that: what are the six or seven questions this program should actually work on, in order of priority, with an allocation of its total effort as fractions summing to one? I want a defended agenda rather than a survey. For each question give me the criteria you are trading off: how much the sign or magnitude of the final answer turns on it; how tractable it is in three years using conceptual analysis, existing ecological and land-use datasets, and simple modelling, with no budget for new fieldwork; how neglected it is; and how much of the rest of the agenda sits downstream of it. Where a downstream question is worthless unless an upstream one comes out a particular way, say so explicitly — I care more about the dependency structure than about the list.

Then tell me which two or three candidate questions you would drop even though they are genuinely interesting, and what results in the first eighteen months should trigger a re-ranking. Close with the program's provisional answer: your own best guess today at the sign and rough size of the effect, the confidence you would actually stand behind rather than the confidence that sounds modest, and the single crux such that if it resolved the other way your guess would flip.
