I'm writing the third chapter of my dissertation — philosophy, with a formal-epistemology sideline — and the chapter keeps collapsing into a dispute I don't think I've framed correctly. The topic is whether the machinery behind coherent extrapolated volition can be given a specification that is neither empty nor a quiet replacement of the agent by something else: the idea, roughly, that what we should aim at isn't what a person currently wants but what she would converge on under some counterfactual regime of greater knowledge, greater internal coherence, and more careful reflection. Rather than defend or attack that particular proposal, I want to treat it as one member of a wider family of idealized-preference accounts and ask what any member of that family has to deliver before it is worth arguing about which is best. So what I need from you is the specification itself, not a survey of the existing proposals.

Concretely: write out the desiderata that any adequate account of an agent's idealized preferences has to meet, each with the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a preference, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are local commitments of one research programme. Fix the purpose as follows: the account has to be good enough to hand to someone building a system that will act on a person's behalf in high-stakes, only partially foreseeable circumstances, and good enough to let me say whether the whole genus of views is coherent. To seed the list without constraining it, here is what I've been toying with. There is the锚 problem — the idealization has to correct for akrasia, adaptive preference, ignorance, and manipulation without drifting into telling the agent what she'd want if she were a different person, so I want a requirement that pins down what must be held fixed and what may be improved. There is the diachronic-seed problem: take two agents with indistinguishable current preference orderings but very different learning histories and self-narratives, and say whether the account owes me the same extrapolated output for both; I keep going back and forth on whether a requirement of seed-insensitivity is a genuine desideratum or a disguised commitment to there being a single true self. There is a coherence question — coherence of whom, and over what, given that the coherence in the name seems to be both an intrapersonal condition and a condition on aggregating many people. There is a tractability requirement I'm unsure how to state: if the idealization operator can't be approximated by anything we could build or consult, it may still be the right answer metaphysically, but it can't be the thing I hand to an engineer, and I don't know whether to count that as a desideratum on the theory or merely on our epistemic situation. And there is the question of what the output is even for: an answer to the agent's own deliberative question, or a mandate to a trustee acting in her name, since those seem to pull the idealization in different directions.

The part I most want is the negative structure. Tell me which pairs of desiderata you think cannot be jointly satisfied — my own suspicion is that demanding the output be substantively action-guiding and demanding that it remain traceable to the agent's actual motivational starting point pull apart, and my supervisor thinks seed-insensitivity and responsiveness to the person she actually is are jointly unsatisfiable — and say which single requirement does the most exclusionary work, knocking out the largest share of the family. I'd also like you to distinguish desiderata that are genuinely about correctness from ones that are really artifacts of wanting the theory to vindicate a prior political or decision-theoretic commitment; I have a nagging sense that at least two items on my own list are prejudices in disguise. Close with a few lines, no more, on which two or three desiderata most accounts in the family silently satisfy and which they silently ignore. I'll do the scoring of the competing accounts myself once I have the list.
