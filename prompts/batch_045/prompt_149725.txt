I am writing the opening chapter of an astrobiology handbook — the one that has to say what would have to be true of something for it to count as alive — and I want the specification settled before I look at candidate definitions.

Begin by separating three jobs a definition might be asked to do: a detection criterion, something a remote instrument or sample-return protocol could in principle apply to decide whether a specimen is alive; a natural-kind concept, the kind that earns its place in biology by supporting induction, explanation, and generalization rather than merely sorting; and a constitutive account, saying in virtue of what anything is alive at all. Tell me whether these are three separate specifications or three faces of one, and if no single definition can serve all three, say exactly where the strain falls. That trade-off is the part I most want.

Then give me the desiderata proper: the cases an adequate account must classify correctly, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the empirical results it may not contradict — each with the consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a taste. The cases should include at least viruses and viroids; prions; spores and other dormant or cryptobiotic stages; metabolically active but permanently sterile individuals such as mules and worker ants; mitochondria and other formerly free-living symbionts; a biosphere-scale entity such as a reef or a forest; and the three hypothetical lineages below, which I want included precisely because the terrestrial record does not settle them.

The hypotheticals:

The Cinder Drifts: subterranean, internally regulated, self-repairing, growing by taking up raw materials and reproducing by fission into two daughters — but its copying is so accurate that no heritable variation ever arises, and the lineage has not evolved in a million years.

The Glass Loom: no metabolism, no genome, no membrane, no cell. It imposes its own structure on mineral surfaces in its surroundings and thereby causes more structure of its own kind to appear; variants differ in how faithfully they template, and the better templaters come to dominate.

The Choir: its members are metabolically active, cannot survive apart from the group, and never reproduce. What reproduces is a population-level arrangement of chemistry and spacing, which reassembles itself at a new site each generation and on which selection acts.

Among the distinctions I expect handled: being alive versus being an organism; being alive versus merely being produced by life; life at a time versus life as a historical or lineage-level phenomenon; metabolism from replication from evolution; and life from mind or agency. Among the constraints: the deepest ancestor comparative genomics lets us reconstruct was already a coded, translating cell, so any definition demanding less will count things nobody has found and any demanding more will not count our own ancestors.

Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are preferences of a particular research programme — I am thinking of demands such as substrate-neutrality, or that the verdict be sharp and bivalent for every object, or that the account deliver the same answer for individuals and for lineages. Say which of these are genuine desiderata and which are camp prejudice. Identify any jointly unsatisfiable subset of the list, and name the single requirement that does the most work in excluding otherwise popular definitions. Only then, and briefly, note how the main families of account — metabolism- or autopoiesis-first, replicator-first, the working-definition style that ties life to evolvable chemical systems, and information-theoretic views — fare against the list. The criteria are what I am paying for; the scoring is secondary.
