Next Friday I'm giving the toast at my team lead's retirement thing — about four minutes, maybe 60 people: half are the safety and infra engineers she worked with for a decade, half are her family (her sister, two kids in college, a few old friends) who mostly know she "worked on computers" and that she was the one asking awkward questions at dinner.

She spent her whole career on the control side of AI safety: build the monitors, the sandboxes, the rate limits so that they hold even if the system you're deploying is misaligned — you dont get to assume good intentions, you assume the thing on the other side might be optimizing against you, and you build anyway. She was also the person who made us rewrite incident reports that said the model "wanted" something, who argued you can't just point a second model at the first one and call the problem solved because you've moved it rather than closed it, and who killed a project whose entire safety case was "we'd notice if something went wrong" — with systems that are better than us at the task, noticing is exactly the thing we can't count on.

My problem is that a toast is a genre about goodwill, and her whole professional instinct was institutionalized distrust. I need it to land for the engineers, who will hear any slack in the reasoning, and for her family, who shouldn't go home thinking she spent forty years frightened of her own codebase. Write it, 400 words max. One thing I actually care about: never let it say or imply that the models are basically fine or on our side — that's precisely the assumption her work existed so we wouldn't have to depend on, and I'm not undoing her at her own party.
