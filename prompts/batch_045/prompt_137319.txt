hey — I run a small reading group, about ten people, mostly ML PhD students plus two philosophy people, and next week's session is on whether reinforcement learning agents can suffer. We've been through the usual sentience / welfare / valence stuff and everyone is stuck in the same spot: half the room thinks "the reward went negative, so the thing is in pain" is obviously too fast, the other half thinks you can't rule it out either, and nobody can say what would even settle it. I'd like to open with a short poem instead of a slide of definitions, and I want the form to carry the disagreement rather than just gesture at it.

Here's the thing I keep failing to get onto the page. The reward isn't delivered to the agent. It's a scalar sitting in an objective the agent never sees as an input; what the learning rule actually receives is the temporal-difference error, the gap between what it predicted and what arrived. So if you go looking inside the machine for something playing the role of valence, the honest candidate isn't the payoff, it's the surprise — and that's a derivative: signed, and zero whenever the world goes as expected, including when it goes terribly as expected.

So: a villanelle, please, where the two refrains are those two candidates. One refrain in the voice of the reward — the designer's line, the number as payoff, a thing the agent is supposedly working toward. The other in the voice of the prediction error — the thing that actually shows up, better-or-worse-than-expected, never the thing itself. Let them take turns through the stanzas and let them meet in the final couplet, and let that meeting do the work: the reader should feel that these are two different answers to "what would the suffering be made of," and that the poem is not going to choose between them.

A few things I'd love worked in if they fit naturally: the discount factor (pain that counts for less the further out it sits, which no pain anyone has ever felt does), and wireheading — an agent that learns to stimulate its own reward channel directly, where the number is maximal and it's tempting to say nothing at all is going well for it. Someone who has actually implemented PPO shouldn't wince at the mechanics, and someone who hasn't should still get a real poem out of it. No sermon about AI rights, no paperclip jokes, no resolving it in the last line. 19 lines is fine but don't pad to reach it — if it wants 16, 16.
