I work on interpretability of LLMs. The vocabulary I actually think in is: next-token distributions, linear probes and SAE features on residual-stream activations, steering vectors, the pretraining vs post-training distribution gap, best-of-n sampling scored by a verifier, and OOD generalization. My group has the same fight every week: someone finds a direction that predicts whether the model will say the keys are in the kitchen, steers along it, behaviour shifts the way you'd expect, and they announce that the model believes the keys are in the kitchen. Someone else says a next-token predictor isn't the sort of thing that can believe anything.

What I want from you: restate the philosophy-side dispute about the intentional stance -- the instrumentalist line that belief attributions are just posits that pay for themselves in prediction, the realist line that beliefs are states with normative and holistic constraints (what the system ought to believe given its evidence, how one belief constrains the others), and the line that says we should stop doing this altogether -- in my vocabulary, as carefully as you can, without quietly importing my framework's assumptions and calling it translation. Then the residue report: what carried over cleanly, what only went through because you fudged something, and what has no expression at all in the terms I use.

Two wrinkles from our actual setup that I've never seen handled in that literature. Tell me what each of the three lines does with them, or whether it has no grip on them:

(1) What our deployed model emits is the candidate that scored best with a verifier, not the one the base model puts the most probability on. If steering a probe direction changes behaviour, did I change what the system believes or what it has learned gets rewarded?

(2) Same model, same content: belief-consistent behavior in English, reversed for the equivalent content in a language that was rare in pretraining. Attribution now seems to need an argument about which distribution fixes the content of the belief -- is that something the views already contain, or new?

On the residue I want your actual read: is what won't translate a blind spot in how interpretability is practiced, or a sign that the philosophy dispute is thinner than it sounds? Please dont just conclude that everyone has a point.
