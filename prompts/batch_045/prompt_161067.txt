I am writing a paper on deep disagreement, and I need a number I can argue with rather than a survey of what has already been said on the subject.

Take as the test case a pair of actual philosophers — call them A and B — who have spent their careers on opposite sides of one well-defined question (say, whether any two things ever compose a third: A holds that composition never occurs, B that it always does), who have read the same literature, cite each other, and have argued face to face many times. The claim I want your credence in is this:

C: there exists a single probability function over a state space such that the present opinions of A and B are both obtained from it by conditionalising on their respective total evidence — that is, the common prior assumption holds for this pair, allowing for small deviations.

Please give me a single number for C, then the decomposition that produces it. I want the following treated as separate terms, each with its own rough probability and one line of justification, and then combined:

(i) Articulation and discipline. A common prior can always be manufactured by enriching the state space until every residual disagreement is re-described as an unarticulated difference in private evidence. I want you to separate the disciplined reading — the state space must be specified independently of what A and B believe — from that enrichment manoeuvre, and to tell me how much of your headline number rides on which reading is in play.

(ii) Reflexivity. The states over which the shared prior is defined would have to include each agent's own prior and their higher-order beliefs about the other's prior, since those are among the things they hold opinions about. Say whether a prior can coherently distribute over a space that contains it in this way, whether the resulting regress can be closed (a universal type space, or something looser), and whether, if it can, any genuine difference of prior is left for the assumption to forbid.

(iii) Applicability of the machinery. How likely is it that the antecedents the results need actually obtain between two people: a shared algebra of events, common knowledge of each other's posteriors, common knowledge of each other's rationality and of each other's evidence-processing? Then, conditional on those, how confident are you that the agreement result — that common priors plus common knowledge of posteriors force identical posteriors — and the companion no-trade result are genuinely about the agents rather than only about the model of them?

(iv) The observational bead. Convergence under full disclosure is, as far as I can see, the assumption's only observable consequence. How much should the brute fact of persistent disagreement across decades of shared evidence move the number, and where is the resulting failure best located: in rationality, in common knowledge or awareness, in the prior itself, or in the presupposition that A and B are disagreeing about one proposition rather than talking past each other?

Then tell me which term your final number is most sensitive to, and which single piece of evidence would move it most and in which direction — I would prefer something that could in principle be carried out: pairs of specialists, full disclosure of evidence and arguments, and a measured rate of convergence.

For calibration, here is the spread I am trying to understand. I am at roughly 0.3. A decision theorist of my acquaintance, who puts common priors into models every working day, puts it at 0.85. An epistemologist who works on incommensurability puts it at 0.05. Tell me which of us, if any, is most likely mislocating the disagreement: which sub-question the gap between 0.3, 0.85 and 0.05 is really a gap about.

Finally, give me an honest verdict on exposure. If the disciplined reading is the only one with content but cannot be tested, while the testable reading is satisfiable by construction, then C risks nothing and can survive anything. I want to use the assumption in an argument that runs from persistent philosophical disagreement to the falsity of something else, so tell me whether that insulation disqualifies it for such work, or whether a harmless, contentless modelling postulate is exactly what an argument of that shape needs.

Please answer in British English, and keep the numbers visible throughout rather than burying them in prose.
