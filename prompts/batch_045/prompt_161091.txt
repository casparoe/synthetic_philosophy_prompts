Two people in my seminar have been going at it about the norm of assertion for a whole term and it's gone nowhere, so I want the document they should have produced instead: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between them. One is on the knowledge-norm side — assertion has a constitutive rule, roughly you may assert p only if you know p, and the only-if direction does the real work. The other holds a reasonable-credence line: what matters is whether the speaker's confidence is properly calibrated to her total evidence given some threshold set by the context, and knowledge is neither necessary nor sufficient.

What I want out of it:

- A numbered section of agreed claims. What assertion is (a speech act with a characteristic direction of fit, whatever the two of them settle on), what the practice seems to be for, and what counts as shared data: the oddity of Moorean conjunctions, the badness of asserting lottery losers, the fact that testimony and hearsay actually transmit something, the way hedging and retracting work. Also say honestly which bits of that data each side thinks it owns rather than shares.
- A disagreements table, each row a seperate residual disagreement, and each row carrying the formulation its own side would accept for its own view — not a strawman. If you think part of the fight was verbal (one of them working with a narrower speech act, or knowledge doing pragmatic-encroachment work that the other side has quietly folded into their threshold), say which part and why.
- The cruxes: sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of them. At least these four. (a) Stakes: same speaker, same evidence, two hearers, one for whom being wrong costs nothing and one for whom it costs everything. (b) The Beliefless Witness, a case I made up: a calibrated forecasting system — or a press officer reading out a compiled evidence report she has no opinion about — that outputs *the bridge is unsafe*, or *there's an 0.8 chance the bridge is unsafe*, from an evidence base it does not believe. Does it assert? Can it violate the norm? (c) Selfless assertion: a teacher at a religious school who asserts the science she does not believe, for the students' sake. (d) A hedged-assertion community: a language game with no bare assertoric form, where everything comes out as *probably p, 0.8*, and hearers have fully adapted to it. Neither of them has seen (d), so both have to commit.
- For each crux, the case or argument both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. I especially want them on record about (d) before they can wriggle.

Numbered agreed claims first, then the table. About 700-900 words plus the table, no citations or reading lists, just argue it out. And don't adjudicate: if both of them couldn't sign it, it's failed.
