hi — I keep having the same argument in our department's work-in-progress group and I'm not winning it, so I want to get clearer before I answer back in the comments thread on the group blog.

Setup: whenever someone runs a spectrum/continuum argument — the mere-addition kind of chain of small "better than" steps that ends somewhere like the Repugnant Conclusion — a value theorist who visited us last month (very good, annoyingly) leans on one analogy every time. "Better than," he says, is just like "taller than." Both are ordinary comparative relations; behind each there's a single underlying scalar quantity that the items have more or less of; that quantity's structure makes the relation transitive; and our inability to tell the difference between neighbours in a long chain is a Sorites-style epistemic blur, not a metaphysical fact about the relation. So, he concludes, in any such chain at least one step is simply false, we just can't locate which one, and spectrum arguments therefore show nothing about wether "better than" is transitive — they're the value-theory version of arguing from "you can't say when someone stops being tall" to "there's no fact about who's tall." He also seems to want the stronger bit: that there's a real-valued magnitude of goodness out there, since that's what makes the height case tick.

Could you audit the analogy properly for me? I want the correspondence table — what plays the role of the objects, the quantity, the unit, the measurement procedure, the thresholds — and then the breaks, and, importantly, which breaks actually matter for the use he's making of it versus which are just cute. Three things I half-think and can't get straight: (1) height is an extensive magnitude, you can concatenate rods and count units, and that structure is what underwrites both the transitivity and the claim that a sharp cut-off exists somewhere even if we can't find it; betterness has no concatenation operation I can see, so even if transitivity transfers, the "there must be a break" half has nothing to stand on. (2) spectrum arguments are mostly built out of weak steps — "not worse than," "at least as good" — and in the height case the threshold-infected, intransitive relation is the perceptual one ("looks the same height as": Luce's semiorders and that whole literature), while strict "taller than" stays clean; so it looks like the analogy may be pointing at the wrong relation, transitive where the argument needs it to be silent and silent where the argument needs it to say something. (3) I suspect it begs the question: if betterness had a unit and a real-valued representation, transitivity would follow by construction and there'd be no debate.

So: does the conclusion survive, does it survive in some weaker form (say only as "a spectrum on its own can't establish intransitivity, you need an extra premise ruling out sharp breaks"), and is there a better comparison to reach for? I've heard "better chess player than" and majority-preference/ tournament cycles floated, and someone muttered about multidimensional dominance, but I couldn't make any of them carry the weight the height analogy carries. And if he's basically right, say that plainly too — I'd rather know now than in front of the group.
