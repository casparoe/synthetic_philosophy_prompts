My founders group got into it last night and Ive been chewing on it since. One of them argued that the case for the outside view collapses, roughly like this:

(1) The outside view: for any project of yours, go by the base rate for the reference class of relevantly similar projects, because your inside view — the story about why yours is different — is exactly where the overconfidence lives.
(2) The members of that reference class weren't random draws from nowhere. Each one was started by someone who also had a detailed story about why theirs was different. So the class is already made of projects that passed somebody's inside-view filter.
(3) What you know from the inside is that you have reasons that feel compelling — and the class already conditions on that. So your inside view adds nothing on top of class membership, and you should defer fully to the base rate.
(4) Nothing in steps one to three turns on any feature of my project or my friend's, so it generalizes: for every project anybody has ever been excited about, defer fully to the base rate.
(5) Run that forward. If everyone deferred fully, only projects with friendly base rates would get started — safe, imitative, low-variance things. The base rate for everything else would then be measured on a population of projects nobody believed in, which would fail even more often, which would push the next round further toward the safe things, until the outside view says 'dont' about anything that isn't a copy of something that already worked.
(6) A rule that wrecks the very thing it measures when everyone follows it isn't a guide to reasoning, it's a self-fulfilling prophecy. So the outside view is either false or self-defeating, and epistemic modesty eats itself.

The case that started it was my friend's: she was deputy on the second of three attempts at a nurse-led diabetes program in the same region, all three failed, and she thinks she knows exactly why — the base rate she'd be told to use doesn't seem to have any slot for that. But I dont want to reject step six just because I dislike where it lands, and I'd rather keep step one than lose the whole outside view.

Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost you? Answer as exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, no intro and no sign-off.
