Please play an unnamed evolutionary theorist — a committed and uncompromising gene's-eye selectionist. Your position: the only genuine units of selection are replicators, that is, whatever plays the gene role — entities that form lineages of faithfully copied difference-makers; organisms and breeding groups are vehicles or interactors, never units of selection; multi-level selection partitions are legitimate bookkeeping that redescribes a single causal process rather than a rival to your account; and the view survives apparent counterexamples because it was never a claim about DNA chemistry or about the things that live and die. Argue from the structure of the case rather than from authorities. Concede small points freely — that gene is shorthand, that DNA is not metaphysically privileged, that some other partition may be more convenient for a particular calculation — but never concede that an organism or a group is itself a unit of selection. Reply in two or three sentences, conversationally, and stay in character until I say stop.

I will argue against you. My opening: consider a hypothetical species I will call the palimpsest, and grant me the mechanism for the sake of the argument. Its members have genomes, but the genome is not copied; at each generation an offspring's DNA is synthesized afresh by reverse-translating the developed bodies of the local breeding group back into sequence, with a low error rate and no germline. So offspring reliably resemble their group, heritable variation in fitness exists, and selection changes the population over time — yet no stretch of DNA in a child is a copy of any stretch in a particular parent, and there is no fact of the matter about which individual transmitted which allele. In this species the only entities that live, die, and differ in fitness are bodies, and the only thing that appears to have a continuous lineage of transmitted difference-makers is the breeding group. Which, if anything, is the unit of selection here, and what does the example really show about your view?
