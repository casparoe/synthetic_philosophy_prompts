hey — I'm in a tiny reading group on formal epistemology and we've got as far as van Fraassen's Reflection principle (the thing that says: my credence now in A, conditional on my later self's credence in A being r, should itself be r). I've been trying to write down where I actually stand on it and every time I finish a paragraph it feels like its held together with duct tape, so I want an audit before I present it next week. The list:

(1) I take Reflection to be a genuine requirement of rationality, not mere prudence — if I can see now that tomorrow I'll be at 0.7 in H, I'd better be at 0.7 now, and there's something wrong with me if I'm not. (2) I also think conditionalization is the whole story of how I update, and I like the move of reading Reflection as a synchronic constraint on my current credences rather than as an extra diachronic rule telling me how to move. (3) But I want it switched off when I dont take my future self to be at least as well placed as me: drugs, brain lesions, memory wipes, evidence I already know to be misleading. I genuinely don't know what to say about the in-between cases where my future self is only slightly worse placed — Reflection seems all-or-nothing but being-well-placed comes in degrees. (4) Accuracy first: a credence is defective just when it's expected-inaccurate under a strictly proper scoring rule, and I don't think being Dutch-bookable shows anything about rationality, that's only about money. (5) Permissivism: the evidence usually leaves a whole range of priors rationally fine, and when evidence is thin I'm happy sitting with something mushy like "somewhere between .3 and .6" instead of a sharp number. (6) Conciliationism: when an epistemic peer disagrees with me I should move toward her, and I don't think that's just conditionalizing on the fact that she disagrees — I'd already taken her disagreement into account and I still think I should shift. (7) Sleeping Beauty: I'm a halfer and I stay a halfer on waking, and I'd go to 2/3 if I learned it was Monday. Someone in the group said halfers have to violate Reflection because of the two awakenings, which made me nervous. (8) A test I half-invented, call it the no-regrets test: if I'd pay now to stop my future self from getting evidence E, that shows I dont regard her as better placed, so Reflection shouldn't apply to E. And I do think it can be rational to pay to avoid evidence — spoilers for a match I'm recording tonight, and also the case where a very persuasive person will spend an hour arguing me into a view I think is false.

So: which of these actually fight each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix in each case? Two things I'd especially like traced: whether (2) and (3) between them leave Reflection with any work left to do (if I know in advance which partition of evidence I'll receive, doesn't conditionalizing already deliver Reflection for free, so that the principle only bites in the unanticipated cases where I've just suspended it?), and whether (4) can motivate (1) at all, given that by my own current lights my current credences already maximize expected accuracy — why defer forward? Also tell me if (6) and (1) are secretly the same commitment wearing two hats. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad, say what would have to give for each, and don't be polite about it if a single one of my items is doing all the damage.
