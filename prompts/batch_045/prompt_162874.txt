i sit on the revenue study comission for our city — about 190k people, county seat, median household income a bit under the state average, two large hospital systems and a plant that keeps threatening to leave. We have to hand the full council a written recommendation in three weeks on whether to put a measure on the november ballot, and i'd like a memo i can actually circulate.

The proposal in front of us: a 0.75% surcharge on earned income for residents, into a dedicated fund, paying for universal pre-K for three- and four-year-olds, free after-school and summer for every elementary kid, and a mental-health clinician in each middle school. It's deliberately universal, not means-tested — the drafters' argument is that a benefit only poor people use is a benefit the middle stops defending, and that's how these things get cut in the next downturn.

Four things we're split on and i need a position on each:

1. Sequencing. Bundle it — tax and programs start the same year, named together in one ballot line — or do benefits first: there's a one-time state surplus grant the governor has been floating, enough to run the programs for three years, with the tax vote deferred until people have seen the pre-K work. Half the commission thinks the visible link at the moment of the vote is the entire point; half thinks you don't get to spend your way into trust.

2. Salience. Our consultants want the surcharge folded into existing withholding so it doesn't show up as a separate line on anyone's paystub. Two commissioners say that's exactly backwards — that the reason the Nordic arrangements hold is that people can see what they pay and see what comes back, and that an invisible tax just entrenches the belief that government is free.

3. The annual household statement we'd mail out if it passes: does it show only what that household recieved from the fund, or the net (paid in minus value recieved)? And does it go to every household or only to families actually using the programs?

4. Scope. We are twelve minutes from a suburb over the county line with no income tax, and three of our biggest employers are openly shopping sites. So: city-only, or do we hand this to the county (whose executive is lukewarm)? I keep thinking that if the honest answer is county-or-nothing, that's us admitting the whole thing only works at a scale where nobody can walk away from it.

Also relevant: a means-tested pre-K levy failed badly here in 2016, and an earmarked library levy passed narrowly in 2019 after the library started mailing households an annual report. I'd like us to be consistent with ourselves and not just relitigate.

So: recommend a course on all four, with a timeline, and give the strongest version of the option you're telling us to drop — including the one argument i can't shake, that trust is an input here rather than an output, and that a low-trust city which suddenly makes taxes legible just gets evasion plus a backlash that takes the pre-K down with it. And tell us what measurable thing we should watch in years one to three that would tell us the theory was wrong, and what unwinding would look like if it is.
