Suppose illusionism about phenomenal consciousness is true: nothing is like anything for anyone, and yet it seems to each of us, on reflection, that there is something it is like to be in the states we are in. I want to know what a positive version of that view would have to deliver in order to be asserted as a theory rather than offered as a research program. Write the specification, with the case or consideration that motivates each item, and mark each requirement as non-negotiable or as a preference of one side of the dispute.

The cases I keep getting stuck on, to start from — add the requirements I have missed rather than just working through mine, especially any concerning the mechanism that produces the error and why selection would have tolerated it:

Recalcitrance. A theorist who has accepted and taught the view for twenty years reports, on reflection, exactly what she reported before. Ordinary illusions do not survive being seen through. What is the theory committed to saying about that report, and what would count as the illusion actually lifting? If nothing conceivable would, say so and say whether that is a defect.

Cartography. The apparent structure of qualitative similarity seems to mirror the structure of what we discriminate. A misrepresentation that systematic and that fine-grained is not much like a perceptual illusion; the theory must either explain the mirroring or abandon the analogy with illusion.

Reliability without truth. Phenomenal reports are the data of psychophysics, of anaesthesia monitoring, of pain assessment. Explain how such reports can be reliably informative while being systematically mistaken about what they appear to describe.

A dissociation I have not seen discussed: a subject in whom whatever produces the introspective self-model is damped, who loses the sense that there is anything it is like, while discrimination, voluntary reporting and hedonic response are unchanged. Does the theory predict this, and would its occurrence confirm the theory or merely fail to refute it?

Containment. If we are this badly wrong about ourselves here, why is the debunking not equally available for introspective knowledge of our beliefs, intentions and efforts?

Finally, and I care about this part most: is it a genuine requirement that the theory state what the illusion is an illusion of without deploying phenomenal vocabulary, or is that a rhetorical self-denying ordinance? Which pair of requirements do you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied? Which single requirement excludes the most candidate theories? Then a sentence or two each on how the three strategies on offer fare against the list: locating the error in the introspective self-model, locating it in the concepts we deploy, and locating it in our reporting practice.
