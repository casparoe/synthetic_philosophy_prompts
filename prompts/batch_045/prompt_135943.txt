I work as an actuary at a life insurer, mostly on the valuation and hedging of a book of very long-dated annuities — some of the cashflows in our projection model run past 2100 — and I have been asked to draft an internal note on how climate change should enter the way we value and manage them. Two things in our current practice are provoking the question. First, we discount off what we call the 'cliff' curve: a market-consistent curve out to about thirty years, after which we splice on a flat rate that someone in risk set years ago, on the stated grounds that the market curve is not informative at that horizon; I have started to suspect that this splice is doing enormous ethical work under the cover of being a modelling convention. Second, a client has asked for a single figure: the share of our liabilities attributable to people born after 2050. My director wants the number, I am not sure it is a coherent thing to ask for, and a colleague has proposed we handle it by what she calls a 'fixed reference population' convention — hold the composition of the future population fixed across policy scenarios so that every comparison has a stable baseline, even though we know the scenarios themselves change who exists. I want to know whether that convention is a bookkeeping convenience or a substantive moral commitment that has quietly settled the question before anyone argues about it.

What I would like from you is a systematic translation, not a summary. Take three things that I have only encountered at second hand — the dispute in climate economics over the social discount rate (Stern against Nordhaus, and the Ramsey rule where the rate decomposes into a pure rate of time preference plus inequality aversion times growth), the non-identity problem, and the precautionary or catastrophe-avoiding style of argument about extinction risk — and restate each of them as precisely as you can in the vocabulary I actually use: discount curves and term structures, the distinction between a market-consistent (risk-neutral) valuation measure and a real-world measure for internal decision-making, spreads, model and parameter risk, ruin and solvency constraints, tail risk and coherent risk measures, and the going-concern assumption that sits underneath every valuation we sign. Assume I am fluent in all of that and assume essentially no philosophy: I do not need to be told what discounting is, but I could not tell you the difference between a person-affecting and an impersonal view.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. For each of the three, say which claims came across cleanly, which came across only by being distorted — and be explicit when you have quietly substituted an assumption of the actuarial framework for a premise of the philosophical argument rather than translating it — and which claims cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all. Some candidates I want you to rule on rather than leave vague: whether the pure rate of time preference is really just a spread, or whether setting it to zero fails to deliver impartiality between generations for reasons that survive the translation; whether the non-identity problem is the same difficulty as an endogenous or decision-dependent benchmark, or whether it is a different difficulty that my framework has no slot for; whether duties to people who merely might exist, and the idea of a wrong that makes no one worse off, are casualties of the translation or artefacts of the philosophy; whether the badness of human extinction is representable as a large adverse cashflow at all, or whether it is closer to the failure of the going-concern assumption, i.e. a condition of the model rather than an outcome inside it; and whether the fact that everything past a certain horizon either vanishes (under compounding at a positive rate) or swamps the entire valuation (at a near-zero rate) is a finding about the ethics or an artefact of using an exponential functional form. Close with a verdict on my colleague's fixed reference population convention: what exactly does it commit us to, and can we produce the director's number without it?
