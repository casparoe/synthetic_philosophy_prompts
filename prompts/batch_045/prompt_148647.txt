I work on replicated, event-sourced systems, and the vocabulary I actually reason in is that of state machines and their histories: logs and snapshots, invariants and the operation sets that preserve them, commutativity and convergence, materialized views and incremental view maintenance, compensation transactions, authorization as distinct from execution, and deterministic replay of a recorded history under modified rules. I am fluent in that vocabulary and have almost no philosophy.

I would like you to translate into exactly that vocabulary the debate over Nozick's entitlement theory of justice in holdings — the acquisition / transfer / rectification structure, the contrast between historical principles and patterned or end-state principles, and the Wilt Chamberlain argument meant to show that no pattern over holdings can be preserved without either forbidding voluntary transfers or continuously undoing their effects. Do the translation piece by piece rather than as a single gesture, and where a rendering required a choice between two options, state the choice and your reason for it.

Three mappings I want pinned down in particular. First: some patterned principles are defined not over holdings themselves but over a function of them — welfare, advantage, opportunity — which in my terms looks like a view over base state rather than the base state itself; I want to know whether that changes what it takes to maintain the pattern, or only what it takes to evaluate it. Second: some patterns are defined over a *transformed* variable, so that keeping them requires information the local transaction does not carry. Third: I have been toying with a three-way distinction among principles that are checkable on a snapshot, principles that are checkable only on the log, and principles that are checkable only by counterfactually replaying the log under a different rule set. Entitlement theory is usually presented as needing only the second kind, and as being better off for it. But the Lockean proviso looks to me like a test that compares actual trajectories against one that was never run, which would put it in the third class. Tell me whether that is right, and if it is, what it does to the claim that historical principles are computationally and institutionally cheaper than patterned ones — and, more pointedly, whether reclassifying the proviso that way is a real discovery about the theory or an artifact of forcing it into my vocabulary.

Then give me the residue report. Which claims translate cleanly, which translate only after you quietly reinterpreted them, and which have no expression in this vocabulary at all? I expect trouble around consent, around self-ownership and side constraints, around what it means for blocking a permitted transaction to be a violation rather than merely a cost, and around any comparison of how well off someone is. For each item of residue, say whether it is a blind spot of the engineering vocabulary or a sign that the original claim was doing less work than it looks like it was doing. Be explicit about the places where a translation would have smuggled in my framework's assumptions — for example, that there is a designer with a chosen metric, or that conforming to a specification is what makes a system good.

Do not reach for citations or quotations; argue from the machinery.
