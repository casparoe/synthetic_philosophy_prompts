Emergency departments and post-operative wards do something that a well-known tradition in philosophy treats as impossible in principle. A patient is asked to rate their pain from 0 to 10, the number is recorded as the patient's own authoritative report, and it is then used as a currency: it decides who gets the last available dose of an analgesic, who is sent for a nerve block, who is discharged and who is kept. The same protocols do things that sit badly with this. A nurse's observation of grimacing, guarding and vital signs is recorded alongside the number and is sometimes allowed to override it; requests for opioids are treated as less credible the more insistently they are made; and for patients who cannot speak — intubated, demented, newborn — the number is replaced by a staff rating of facial expression and movement, which is then treated as a measurement of the very same thing and used in exactly the same way.

What picture of sensation, and of sensation language, does this bundle of practices presuppose? I am interested in Wittgenstein's private language argument here, but please work from the practice to the theory rather than summarising the argument and then applying it. Does comparing one person's report with another's, and using that comparison to allocate a scarce resource, require that a sensation word be answerable to public criteria of correctness — so that it could not have been fixed by a private ostensive definition of the sort the argument is meant to undermine, a diary in which someone writes a mark whenever a certain sensation recurs? Or could someone who holds that the inner is logically private run the whole practice anyway, treating the number as an avowal or expression rather than as a report about a private object? Distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and say where it is underdetermined: which of its features any plausible theory would license, and which single feature is doing the real work. Finally, if clinicians took the private-language picture seriously, what would a protocol that never pretends to compare one person's pain with another's actually look like, and what would we lose by adopting it?
