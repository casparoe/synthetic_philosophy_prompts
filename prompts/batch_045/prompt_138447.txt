For a final-year undergraduate seminar on the methodology of economics, I am setting a forty-eight-hour take-home assessment and need one question on revealed preference and the meaning of utility. The question must be unanswerable by reciting the weak and generalised axioms of revealed preference and observing that utility is identified only up to a positive monotone transformation.

The crux I want to isolate is this case. We have clean choice data for a single household over a long run of budget sets involving an unchanging list of goods, and the data are consistent with the household's maximising a stable, well-behaved utility index over that region. A genuinely new good is then introduced, one with no counterpart in any earlier budget set, and the household buys it. Two things are now in dispute. First, how much better off the household is: the weak claim looks settled by the fact that the old bundles remain available and affordable, but the magnitude appears to require extrapolating the demand for the new good out to prices at which it is never bought, so that behaviour seems to pin bounds rather than a figure. Second, what the word 'utility' is doing in the first place: whether the recovered index is merely a re-encoding of choices, a real magnitude that our functional-form assumptions are trying to measure, or something whose meaning quietly shifts between those two questions. Add one further wrinkle to the case: the household volunteers that the new good has changed its life far more than the money suggests, and ask what status such a report can have inside a framework that admits only choices as evidence.

Please write the question in a form a student could answer in about a thousand words, along with a model answer of roughly seven hundred words and a marking scheme out of twenty built around four or five must-hit moves, each with its mark allocation and a one-line note on how strong candidates typically go wrong on it. The scheme should separate an A answer, which says what would have to be true for the magnitude to count as a genuine measurement rather than a modelling artefact, from a B answer, which restates the underdetermination without saying what it shows about the meaning of utility. Please write your answer in American English.
