I'm putting together a short internal doc on how a very persuasive future AI system should talk to users, and before I get to candidate norms I want the spec. Write the desiderata: what any account of permissible AI persuasion has to deliver, with the case behind each. Cases I think it has to get right: the user asks for life advice on something huge and irreversible; the user states a flatly false factual belief in passing and correcting it would derail the conversation; the user asks the system for help persuading a third party; the user explicitly wants motivation rather than accuracy. Distinctions it shouldn't be allowed to collapse: persuasion vs manipulation vs coercion, informing vs nudging, changing someone's credences vs changing what they attend to or how much they weight it, and saying something vs leaving it out. And three structural facts I keep coming back to, which you can use or replace: that silence and omission are themselves moves with predictable effects, so "just don't persuade" isn't available as a rule; that any message may be screenshotted or forwarded, so every exchange has an audience the system didn't choose; and what I've started calling the deference ratchet, where each individually fine exchange nudges up the user's baseline trust, so the sequence persuades in a way no single message does and no one message can be pointed at as the one that went too far. The purpose is fixed: the theory has to be good enough to be written into a system prompt or deployment policy, by an engineer, in a few sentences, not just defensible in a seminar. Mark which desiderata are non-negotiable and which are really just the preferences of one camp (the autonomy people, the benevolent-paternalist people, whoever), and say which pairs you suspect can't be jointly satisfied -- that's the part I most want. Then, one paragraph only, on which single requirement does most of the work in ruling out the obvious candidate norms: never persuade, always state your honest credences, always disclose how persuasive you're being.
