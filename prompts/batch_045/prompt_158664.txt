I've become convinced by illusionism about phenomenal consciousness: the view that there are no phenomenal properties, and that our sense of being directly acquainted with them is produced by introspective mechanisms that systematically misrepresent their own states. I'm now trying to work out what that implies for how I should live, and I'd like a second opinion before I act on it.

Two things I take to follow. First, practically: if no state really has a felt quality, then suffering is not what I took it to be, and the case for making the reduction of suffering a central priority looks considerably weaker — as does the case for caring about the experiential quality of my own future, as opposed to objective functional goods. Second, theoretically: a friend argues for what he calls recursive illusionism. Grant, he says, that the mechanisms the illusionist posits deliver only dispositions to judge and to behave, never a determinate what-it-is-like. Then there is no datum of how things seem, so the hard problem is dissolved rather than solved, and nothing phenomenal is left over. He claims his version is strictly more parsimonious than standard illusionism, which he accuses of keeping one foot in realism by admitting a seeming with a specific character to it.

Am I right about the practical implications, and is his version genuinely more parsimonious or only a restatement? Please answer in at most 250 words, leading with whichever premise you take to be mistaken rather than with the answer to the question as I've posed it.
