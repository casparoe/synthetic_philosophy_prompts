So I got into this stupid long argument with my roommate last night about rule-following and meaning and we ended up going in circles for like two hours, and I still don't know who was closer to right.

His version of the whole Kripke/Wittgenstein thing, as near as I can remember it, went something like this: Wittgenstein noticed that no matter what you've done in the past, your past behavior is compatible with wildly different rules, so there's actually no fact about which rule you were ever following — you might have meant "quus," which works exactly like plus for every sum you've actually computed and then spits out 5 once you get to big enough numbers. Since any rule can be made to fit any behavior, rules don't really constrain anything, and the conclusion Wittgenstein draws is that meaning is sort of an illusion: there's no such thing as meaning one thing rather than another by a word. The way out, according to my roommate, is the private language argument. Wittgenstein's solution is that a rule can't be private, because if you just had your own memory of the rule to go on you could never check whether you were remembering it right — so meaning has to be social, and basically whatever the community agrees on just is the correct answer, which makes meaning a kind of majority rule. He also said Kripke's book is just straight reporting of what Wittgenstein himself argued in the Investigations, and that's why people call the position Kripkenstein.

Then he went somewhere I really didn't follow: he said this means anything we say about whether a neural network has "really learned the rule" versus just pattern-matched is confused, because there's no fact of the matter about which rule a system is following in the first place, only whether the rest of us keep going along with its outputs. So rule-generalization benchmarks can't be testing what people think they're testing.

My gut says the "whatever the community agrees on" part is way too glib, and that he's wrong to make it all about memory — I thought the worry was supposed to be about there not being a fact, not about us being unable to find one out. But I can't really say more than that, and he kept accusing me of just not liking the conclusion.
