2. Fundamentality is just ungroundedness. There's no extra 'substantive' or 'thick' notion of being fundamental that isn't captured by not being grounded.
3. Necessitarianism: if the fact that P grounds the fact that Q, then necessarily, if P then Q. This is why I think grounding can back 'in virtue of' claims at all.
4. Hyperintensionalism: grounding is finer-grained than any modal relation. Swap in a necessarily equivalent fact and the grounding can break, so you can't read grounding off supervenience.
5. Humeanism about laws: laws are the axioms of the best system describing the mosaic; nothing necessitates anything else across distinct existences; no necessary connections between wholly distinct existences.
6. Non-reductive physicalism: every non-fundamental fact is grounded in physical facts; mental facts are grounded in neurophysical facts but not identical with them. And being grounded in something just is being nothing over and above it.
7. The base is what fundamental physics gives us: facts about local qualities at spacetime points (or field values). We should read our ontology off our best physics.
8. Purity — the tutor writes it as (Sep): no fact about a derivative entity is fundamental. I think I accept it.
