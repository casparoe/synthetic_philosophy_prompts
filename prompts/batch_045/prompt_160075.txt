I am trying to locate where a disagreement with a colleague actually lives. We disagree about where to place present-day language models on Dennett's ladder of kinds of minds: Darwinian creatures, whose repertoire is fixed by evolution; Skinnerian creatures, whose behaviour is shaped by reinforcement from outcomes that really happen to them in the world; Popperian creatures, which carry an internal model that lets candidate actions die in the head rather than in the world; and Gregorian creatures, whose heads are rebuilt by cultural tools such as words, diagrams, and arithmetic. She says a deployed model is straightforwardly Skinnerian, because all the selection that shapes what it emits happened during training, in a different arena from the one it now acts in. My credence on the proposition below is about 0.5; hers is under 0.1.

P: At least one deployed language-model system — the weights together with the scaffold it runs in: chain-of-thought, sampled-then-reranked candidates, tool calls, verifiers — considered as a single creature in its environment, satisfies Dennett's condition for Popperian-ness: some candidate actions it would otherwise perform are discarded before they affect its environment, on the basis of an internal model of that environment and of its own effects on it.

Give me your own number on P and the decomposition. Assign a probability, with one line of justification each, to at least: (a) that the candidate population discarded by sampling-and-reranking is genuinely a set of actions the creature would otherwise have performed, rather than an artefact of decoding that should not count as action selection at all; (b) that the internal model Popperian-ness requires may be inherited wholesale from a training corpus the way a Gregorian creature inherits its tools, rather than acquired through the creature's own trial and error — and, if so, whether that puts the rungs in the wrong order; (c) that it matters that no learning occurs at inference time, so that the system is on one description a Skinnerian creature whose conditioning is merely paused. Add whatever further terms you think carry weight, show how the numbers combine, and name the one you are least confident in.

There is also a rung I want you to rule on, because I suspect this is where our disagreement really sits. Call it the terrarium rung: candidate actions die neither in the world nor in a head-internal model of the world, but in a surrogate arena the system itself generates and is free to discard — a scratchpad rollout, a simulator, a sandboxed execution. Give credences for: that the taxonomy needs this as a further kind; that scaffolded language models occupy it rather than the Popperian or the Skinnerian rung; and that inserting it dissolves the original dispute instead of merely relocating it.

For resolution, propose one concrete test. My candidate is a diagonalization test: build cases where the system's own verdict on a candidate and the world's actual verdict come apart, and see which one its behaviour tracks — if it discards a candidate its model says will fail but that would in fact have succeeded, the head is doing the work; if it needs the world's feedback to reject anything, it is Skinnerian. Say whether that test would settle P, and if not, what would. Commit to definite numbers and a definite account of what Popperian-ness requires, and defend it, rather than saying the answer depends on how the terms are drawn.
