Someone I've been arguing with on a philosophy forum for months — call her M — has posted verdicts on a long list of de re modal cases. She calls herself a counterpart theorist and keeps saying the real question was never counterparts-vs-identity but which counterpart relation the semantics is quantifying over, and that once you fix the relation the old dispute mostly evaporates. I can't tell whether her verdicts are actually being driven by a similarity relation or by something that's basically transworld identity in a costume. I want to know what she'll say about the case I'm about to post, and I care much more about the rule she's running than about the verdict itself.

Her track record so far (she answers true / false / no fact of the matter; I'm condensing):

1. Wilma is a bank manager. "Wilma could have been a florist." True.
2. Mirembe lost an election. "Mirembe could have won." True, and she's emphatic that this doesn't mean we've quietly switched to talking about somebody else.
3. Of a wooden table: "It could have been made from a completely different tree, in a different country, by different carpenters." True, though she admits it sounds wrong in ordinary conversation.
4. "Wilma could have been born to completely different parents." False. Same for "Wilma could have been a chimpanzee."
5. "Wilma could have never existed at all." True.
6. In a world where nothing remotely like Wilma exists: "Wilma doesn't exist there." True.
7. Where a thing has changed so much that it's borderline whether it's still that thing, she says there's no fact of the matter, and that context sets the threshold.

The new case I'm about to post: a medical procedure goes wrong and Wilma is duplicated — two survivors, Left and Right, each with half her brain, each as similar to the actual Wilma as the other, each carrying on her projects, and nothing that makes either one "really" her. I'm going to ask her four things: is it true that Wilma could have been Left; is it true that Wilma could have been Right; can both be true at once; and does the truth of either one require that the survivor in question literally be Wilma.

What I want from you:

- Predict all four answers, with the reason each falls out of whatever rule you think she's using.
- State the rule, and then state at least one genuinely different rule that fits items 1 through 7 just as well but gives a different verdict on the duplication case. I suspect the artifact-origin verdict in item 3 sitting next to the organism-origin verdict in item 4 is doing more work than she realises, so say what each candidate rule has to say about that pairing.
- Tell me which rule to bet on and why, and be explicit about which single item in her record is doing the most work in your choice.
- Give me one further case that would separate the surviving rules, with each rule's verdict on it written down in advance — ideally something she'd have to rule on without being able to see which way it cuts, and nothing that turns on a perfect predictor or on science fiction she could dismiss as irrelevant.
- Finally: do her own verdicts support her claim that the whole counterpart-vs-identity fight is verbal once the relation is fixed, or do they commit her to something that a committed transworld-identity theorist would still refuse to sign?
