Need a hand untangling something before my epistemology reading group meets again. We did Sleeping Beauty last week and I tried to write up an argument I thought would settle it for the halfers, but the conclusion I end up with is obviously insane, so somewhere I've made a move thats doing more work than it should.

Standard setup: fair coin Sunday night. Heads, she's woken Monday and that's it. Tails, she's woken Monday and again Tuesday, memory wiped in between, and she knows the whole protocol going in.

(1) When she wakes, she hasn't learned anything she didn't already know on Sunday. She knew in advance she'd be in exactly this sort of situation — awake in a room, unsure about the coin, unsure about the day — at least once either way. No new relevant evidence, so her credence in heads shouldn't budge: P(H) = 1/2.

(2) The exact same reasoning applies to the day. The Monday and Tuesday awakenings are subjectively identical, nothing that happens on waking distinguishes them, so she should split evenly: P(Mon) = P(Tue) = 1/2.

(3) On heads there's no Tuesday waking at all. So P(H & Mon) = P(H) = 1/2, and P(H & Tue) = 0.

(4) From (2) and (3): P(H | Mon) = (1/2)/(1/2) = 1. And by the same algebra P(T | Tue) = 1.

(5) So if someone walks in and says "hey, it's Monday," Beauty should jump from 1/2 on heads all the way to dead certain heads — and symmetrically for Tuesday and tails. Nobody thinks that. The halfers in my group say Monday should leave P(H) at 1/2, the thirders say something else, but nobody says certainty.

So which step do I drop? I'd really like to keep (1), since the "she learned nothing" bit is the whole appeal of halfing. But I can't see how (1) is fine and (2) is broken when (2) looks like the very same argument pointed at a different claim. One person said the difference is that "heads" is a plain proposition about the world while "it is now Monday" is partly a claim about where you are in the world, and that the no-new-information move only gets traction on the first kind. I half see it, but then I don't know what's supposed to replace (2), or whether a halfer is even allowed to just decline to have a credence about the day.

Separately, someone else claimed that (1) plus (3) plus something weaker than (2) — like "conditional on tails, the two days are equally likely" — forces P(Mon) = 3/4, which gives P(H | Mon) = 2/3 instead of 1. Better, but still odd: finding out the date moves your credence in a fair coin. Is that the standard halfer repair, and is it stable or does it just push the bump somehwere else?

The bit that worries me most: the thirder's favourite move is to hand out 1/3 each to the three subjectively identical centred situations, H-Mon, T-Mon and T-Tue. But that's the same indifference reasoning as (2), just spread over a bigger set. If indifference over days is what wrecks my argument, why isn't indifference over situations equally suspect for them?
