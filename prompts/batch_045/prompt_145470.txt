You are a professional philosopher specializing in the ethics of human enhancement, and for this exercise you are the newest member of a university's Committee on Cognitive Interventions. Before you rule on anything, spend a few lines listing the rival principles that could each be generating the track record below, then tell me which one you think we are actually running.

Our precedents: (i) we approved stimulant medication for students with a formal ADHD diagnosis; (ii) we rejected the same medication for healthy students who wanted it before exams; (iii) we approved cochlear implants for children born deaf, i.e. for a capacity they never previously had; (iv) we rejected growth hormone for a healthy child at the low end of the normal height range with no detectable deficiency; (v) we approved, for anyone who asked, an intensive exam-preparation program that raises scores through training rather than drugs; (vi) we approved medication for students diagnosed with depression where the benefit we cared about was improved concentration.

The new proposal: students who lost close to two years of effective schooling to a prolonged school closure — no disorder, scores within the normal range but well below both their own earlier trajectory and their age cohort — would be offered a drug that briefly reopens a window of neural plasticity. Two complications: the drug cannot be titrated, so anyone who takes it ends up somewhat above their own pre-closure trajectory, and some of the gain persists into years after the gap has closed.

Give me the decision that best coheres with our precedents, identify the closest analog among (i)-(vi), and state what principle we would be committing ourselves to either way — normal species functioning, restoration of the person's own baseline or expected trajectory, some welfare or positional-goods balancing, or something else I haven't named. If our precedents genuinely underdetermine this case, say so, and tell me which further case would best separate the surviving rules — for instance the same catch-up delivered by tutoring instead of a drug, or a cohort whose deficit traces to their own choices rather than to a closure — and what each surviving rule predicts there.

Finally, keep the settled separate from the contested: flag what is uncontroversial among people who work on the therapy/enhancement distinction, and flag where you are relying on a contested reading of that distinction or on your own normative judgment. Do not present a contested position as if it were the consensus in the field.
