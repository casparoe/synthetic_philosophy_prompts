I'm writing a novel set about fifty years form now, and one of the threads is a biotech firm that grows engineered parrots — same body plan as a bird, but the brains come out roughly a third the size of a human's while packing something like three times the number of neurons, because the cells are tiny and packed differently. The firm is petitioning to have them classed as persons. The government in my book has solved the question the way you'd expect: moral status gets indexed to brain metrics.

I want to drop into the book, as an insert the character comes across, an actual page of the official guidance — the "Cortical Sufficiency (Moral Status) Guidance", third edition. Could you write it? About 600 words, laid out as numbered clauses with a worked example and a short FAQ at the bottom, in the deadpan voice of a civil-service leaflet: the tone of the notes that come with a tax return.

The target is the habit of reading moral standing straight off a single neural number, so the humour has to come from getting that right rather than from jokes bolted on. What I'd like worked in: which metric they've picked and how they justify the choice, where they definately draw the threshold and what they say about why it's there, and what happens to my parrots once brain size and neuron count disagree. The bit I care about most is the humans who fall below the line — infants, people with microcephaly, someone with advanced dementia — handled in exactly the same register as the animals, not flagged as an embarrassment. Keep it straight-faced throughout; no villainous flourish at the end. If there's a footnote buried somewhere, that can be the tell. I'd love it if someone who actually works on moral status laughed rather than winced.
