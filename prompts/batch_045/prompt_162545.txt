I've got a 90-minute slot next week with our first-year psychiatry residents for journal club, and the paper is a twin study reporting a heritability of about 0.4 for major depressive disorder. I don't need heritability explained to me again — I need a plan for the session. Most of them have had one genetics lecture ever and they're already walking around saying "so depression is 40% genetic."

Here's what I want: the order the ideas have to come in and why — what actually depends on what — because I keep starting with the definition and losing them by minute ten. And which single example should carry the whole thing, plus what it needs to have and lack. My instinct is PKU, but I'm scared they'll file it under "yeah, but that's a single-gene disease" and I'll have lost them for the rest of the hour. I also don't want an IQ example, it'll eat the entire session.

Then the misconceptions, in the order they'll actually reach for them, and the point in the sequence where I should kill each one. The ones I can predict: the reading where 40% heritable means 40% of *this patient's* depression is genes and the rest is upbringing; the slide from "heritable" to "fixed, so therapy and social factors are just noise"; taking a within-population number as a claim about a difference *between* populations; and treating h² as a property of the trait rather than of a population in an environment. There are two I'm less sure where to place: someone always notices that if you equalise the environment heritability goes *up* and concludes the genes got stronger, and someone else will half-remember a result about BMI heritability rising as environments got more obesogenic and conclude the same thing. Do those go in the same place, and are they even the same mistake?

One of them will also ask whether there can be a heritability of a *response* to an intervention — like, is there an h² for how much a given treatment helps someone. I have no idea whether its worth absorbing that in the slot I have, deferring it, or refusing it outright, so tell me which and why.

Give me one question for the end that would show me they've got it rather than just memorised "it's about variance." By the end I want them able to field a patient asking "is my depression genetic?" without lying to them, and to read a GWAS abstract without panicking about missing heritability. And tell me what to explicitly skip — I'd rather name the omissions myself than have them discover the gaps later.
