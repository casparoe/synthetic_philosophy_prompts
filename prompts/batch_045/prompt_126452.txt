Hey — I'm in the middle of a PhD on philosophy of mind (dreaming, mostly) and I've been stuck on the Malcolm vs. Dennett stuff for months now. My supervisor says the debate is dead: dreams are experiences, the empirical work on lucid dreamers signalling out of REM with deliberate eye movements and on decoding dream content from brain activity settles it, move on. I think that's way too fast, but I can't get clear on *why* it's too fast. Below is the sketch of what I want to argue in a workshop paper; I'd really appreciate comments on it before I write the whole thing out.

Sketch as it stands. Malcolm's book *Dreaming* takes off from a remark of Wittgenstein's — roughly, does it even matter whether dreamers really had those images while asleep or whether it merely seems so to them on waking — and argues that the only criterion we have for "she dreamed such-and-such" is the waking report, so there's no content to the idea of a dream going on *during* sleep, no fact of the matter being described by "I am now dreaming" as said by a sleeper. Dennett's "Are Dreams Experiences?" reply: the composition-at-awakening hypothesis is what needs defeating, and first-person evidence can't defeat it either way, so the question has to be treated as empirical; he then makes the point vivid with the cassette thought experiment, where the neural activity of the sleeper is recorded and played back into her own cortex on waking, generating a report of a dream she never experienced — if that's coherent, the "it gets composed at the moment of waking" story is at least in the running. My move is a *reverse* cassette. Take the neural activity that occurs while someone is composing a dream report during an ordinary awakening, record it, and play it back into her cortex *while she sleeps*. If on a later awakening (with no report given in between, so no fresh composition happening at that moment) she reports a dream whose content matches the playback, then whatever "composing" machinery Dennett and Malcolm think is essentially tied to waking can also run during sleep. So composition isn't conceptually tied to the awakening report, and Malcolm's criterion argument loses its bite: the report isn't doing the constitutive work, it's just one downstream effect of a process that can operate in sleep. Second, I want to separate two questions people run together in this literature: the *criterion* question (what counts as a criterion for ascribing a dream — is the report conceptually constitutive, or just our best evidence?) and the *empirical* question (what's actually going on in the sleeper). My claim is that the neural decoding and lucid-signalling results only look decisive because people slide from "here's a good public indicator of dream content" to "so the report is no longer constitutive" without arguing for it; a Malcolmian can grant every decoding result and still insist the grammar of "dream" hangs on the report. Third, I want to float the imagination view as a real third option rather than a compromise — dreams as imaginings rather than as hallucinatory perceptions — and ask whether it actually escapes the dispute or just relocates it.

What I want from you: (1) Is the reverse cassette doing any real work, or is it just Dennett's point with extra steps? I worry it smuggles in the assumption that the playback is *experienced*, which is the very thing at issue. (2) Is my criterion/empirical distinction the right place to cut, or is there a cleaner way to say what's wrong with the "the science settled it" line? (3) Does the imagination view collapse into one of the two sides under pressure, and whose side? (4) The strongest objection I'm not seeing — I'd rather hear it now than from a commentator. (5) Honest take: is this worth writing up for a journal, or is it a seminar-paper idea with one clever thought experiment in it? And (6) please flag anything historically wrong: I'm never sure I'm reading Malcolm right (am I attacking a strawman who says "dreams aren't experiences" when he really says something weaker about the sense of asking whether a sleeper is dreaming?), and I'm hazy on where Dennett ends up on the composition hypothesis in his later work, so call me out if I've got him backwards.
