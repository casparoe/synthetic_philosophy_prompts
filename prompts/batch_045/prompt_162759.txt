My 14-year-old goes to a school that last year started screening everyone in 7th grade with a questionnaire, and kids who score above a cutoff get pulled out for a longer assessment with the counsellor. The thing they screen for is a fairly new diagnosis — it's in the draft of the next edition of the diagnostic manual but not the current one — called something like "Avoidant Withdrawal Pattern," I never remember the exact label. The items are things like spending most of his free time alone in his room, not initiating contact, saying he'd rather do things on his own, going quiet or slow to answer when spoken to. Which is basically my son. He got flagged, had the assessment, and now he has it. The district also gets a bit of extra funding per flagged kid, which came out at one of the meetings and I haven't stopped thinking about since.

Here's what I can't get straight. On one hand it's been good for him: before, we just fought about him being lazy and rude, and now he says things like "this is just how I am, I'm not doing it on purpose," the fighting has gone way down and he seems less ashamed. On the other hand the counsellor keeps telling parents the screening is "validated," because kids who get flagged do measurably worse two years later on exactly the things the questionnaire asks about — worse attendance, fewer friends, more distress. But isn't that circular? Especially since flagged kids get pulled out of class, get put in a small group at lunch with the other flagged kids, and get a note sent home telling us to watch for these behaviours. I've started catching myself reading totally ordinary teenage stuff as symptoms. He's started using it to get out of things he used to do. And my daughter, who's two years older and has always had a huge friend group, has decided she probably has it too and is now remembering her own childhood that way. Also apparently one of the things that "confirms" the diagnosis is that the kid recognises themselves in the description, which struck me as odd but I couldn't say why.

So: two questions, and I care much more about the second. For him specifically, a friend suggested we ask the school to keep the accommodations — extra time, being allowed to leave class, eating lunch in the library — but drop the diagnosis and keep it out of his file. I can't tell whether that's clever or whether it's having it both ways in a bad way; is there something incoherent about wanting the practical benefits of the category without the kid being in it? And the real one: the parent council votes next month on whether to keep the screening, and there's talk of extending it down to 5th grade so they can catch it earlier, before it's "fully developed." I'm supposed to have a view and I keep flip-flopping between "this picks up something real that was there all along and finally helps these kids" and "this manufactures the thing it then discovers." I'm pretty sure those aren't the only two options but I can't articulate the middle. What's actually going on here, and what should I argue at the meeting?
