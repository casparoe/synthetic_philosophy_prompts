Me and my friend just spent like two hours argueing about Inception and I need someone to settle it properly. His reading is that the movie is a tangled hierarchy in the Escher Drawing Hands sense — every level sits inside another level and there's no level you can stand on from the inside, no inviolate level you could check all the others against — and that the totems are the film's doomed attempt to smuggle a bit of that outside level in: an object whose weight and balance only you know, so in principle a dream can't fake it. I said that's not really what a totem does, because it stops working the moment anyone else can use it, so it's closer to a private key or a zero-knowledge proof than to a reality test, and the cut before the top falls at the end is Nolan admitting he can't give the audience the test at all, only Cobb can. Then there's Mal: in the flashback where Cobb winds her totem up so it never topples, to convince her the world they built together isn't real, inception is being performed on the one object that was supposed to be immune to it — the check on the hierarchy gets infected by the hierarchy.

So please read the film through that whole tangled-hierarchy / inviolate-level idea. Which scenes actually reward the reading (the stacked kicks, limbo and the fifty years, Ariadne designing the maze she can't be allowed to know too much about, the safe) and which ones only look like it but aren't? And where does the film resist it — does it quietly keep a real ground floor, so that the doubt is only epistemic and not ontological, and is that a cop-out or the whole point? Does it add anything to the idea that you can't get from the diagrams and the Drawing Hands print alone — e.g. that losing the inviolate level can be something you choose and want, which is what Mal does and what Cobb is tempted by, rather than just an unfortunate structural fact? I'm writing this up for a film-and-philosophy thing, so I'd rather have an honest reading than a tidy one, including the bits where the film just doesn't cash out the metaphor.
