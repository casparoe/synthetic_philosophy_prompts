I'm a PhD student in political philosophy, finishing the framework section of a chapter on the ethics of philanthropic allocation, and I need a taxonomy of the objections to effective altruism as a classificatory instrument rather than as a list of names. The difficulty I keep running into is that the objections in this space are conjunctive: they combine an epistemic complaint with a complaint about the movement as an institution, or a worry about fungibility with a worry about who gets to set the agenda. A flat list of labels misrepresents the dialectic. I would rather see the logical space built first, and only then see where the actual positions land in it.

What I want: a tree with at most five branching questions and no more than ten leaves. Every internal node should be labeled with the claim, distinction, or case that does the splitting, phrased so that a graduate student could answer it without already knowing which objection it leads to. Every leaf should carry a name and one sentence on that position's most awkward commitment. Descriptive names are fine; use the movement's own vocabulary only where it is genuinely standard, and write unnamed where you think nobody actually occupies the cell.

The distinctions I care about as branch nodes, though you should reorder, merge, or replace them if the space is cleaner another way:

- Does the objection grant the guiding aim (roughly, impartial, outcome-focused maximization of value) or contest that aim itself?
- If it grants the aim, is the alleged failure epistemic (we cannot reliably identify the best interventions), causal (we can identify them but our action does not produce them), or dynamic (acting on the identification systematically degrades the evidence or the incentives)?
- If it contests the aim, does it appeal to agent-relative or relational considerations (roles, democratic legitimacy, complicity, proximity) or to a different account of what has value?
- Is the objection scoped to the long-run, future-directed part of the package, or to the whole thing?
- Is its target the individual allocator's decision procedure, the existence and character of the movement as an institution, or both?

Three objections from my own notes that I cannot tell are distinct leaves, notational variants of cells I already have, or positions my five questions do not have the resources to separate. Please locate each one in the tree and tell me plainly which it is.

1. Counterfactual fragility. Headline cost-effectiveness figures are computed against a baseline of what would have happened anyway; because money and labor are fungible across organizations and staff are replaceable, small shifts in that baseline — what the next-best funder would have done, who else would have taken the job — move marginal impact by large factors, and the error is not directionally neutral, since evaluators are assessing interventions they are already inclined to fund.
2. Justificatory inversion. The division of cognitive labor the movement recommends places the burden of justification on people who lack the relevant evidence, and the evidence with people who neither make the decision nor control the money; and because an earner's income depends on not looking too closely at their employer, the arrangement has a structural tendency to underproduce exactly the findings that would disqualify the most lucrative careers.
3. Metric-shifting. Publish a quantitative standard of impact and the organizations being assessed reorganize themselves to score well on it, so the measured quantity drifts from the quantity it was standing in for, and the drift is fastest where measurement was already hardest.

Please also mark which cells of the space are unoccupied, with a one-clause diagnosis for each — incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored — and flag any two leaves your tree cannot separate rather than quietly assigning them to different cells.

Two further constraints. I do not want citations, footnotes, reading lists, or attributed positions: label the cells descriptively, and if you gesture at a real author or text, mark that gesture as uncertain. And I need your confidence calibrated: separate what is uncontroversial about the structure of this debate from what is genuinely contested among people who work on it, and say explicitly where you are imposing a tidier structure on the literature than it in fact has, or where you are reconstructing a position rather than reporting one. The entire response should be under 750 words.
