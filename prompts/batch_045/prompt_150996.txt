For my undergraduate epistemology seminar I want to replace the usual survey handout on the regress of justification with a flowchart: something that walks a student from a starting commitment to a named position, branching at each node on a single claim or a small case that the student can answer without already knowing which view it leads to.

What I need:

- Build it from the logical space, not from a list of names. Give me the branching questions first (eight to ten), the combinations of answers they permit, and only then place the actual positions into the occupied cells — foundationalist, coherentist, infinitist, skeptic, and the externalist views on which no regress arises at all — with one line on each leaf's most awkward commitment.
- Leaves that must appear: at least one infinitist leaf; one hybrid view on which sensory experience constrains a system of beliefs without itself grounding any of them; one view on which the regress question never gets off the ground because being justified is not a matter of having reasons; and one skeptic's leaf.
- Two cases must be used as branch nodes. First, a testimonial chain: you believe p only because your mother told you, she believed it only because her mother told her, and nobody anywhere in the chain ever had an independent reason for it. Second, a case of double support: a belief that is both produced by a reliably working perceptual system and backed by an argument you can actually state — does the argument add anything, and does the reliability make the regress question idle for this belief?
- No node may simply ask whether there are basic beliefs, whether justification has to stop somewhere, or anything else that hands the student the label.

Also flag two things: any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate, together with the further question that would separate them; and any permitted combination that nobody actually occupies, with a diagnosis of whether it is empty because it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored.

Finally, on those diagnoses: tell me where they are most likely to be wrong — what would make a cell you call incoherent defensible after all, or one you call merely unexplored genuinely unstable — and tell me what pattern of student answers would persuade you that the tree itself is mis-structured, i.e. which node you would have to split or drop.
