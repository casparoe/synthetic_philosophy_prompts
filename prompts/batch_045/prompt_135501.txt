I'm in the lower sixth and have to get a proposal signed off before I'm allowed to start writing: a 4,000-word philosophy extended essay, roughly six months part-time, with a supervisor. The topic I want is whether an ant colony could be a single thinking thing rather than merely a very large number of insects — the sort of idea behind the ant colony in Hofstadter's *Gödel, Escher, Bach*, which the characters treat as a person they can hold a conversation with.

My half-formed idea is about method rather than metaphysics. Nearly everything I've come across turns on the analogy between ants and neurons, and that looks like a dead end to me, because both sides can accept the analogy and still disagree about what follows from it. So I'd rather borrow a test from neuropsychology: if the colony genuinely is a subject of experience, then damaging one part of it — removing a caste, or blocking a single pheromone channel — ought to produce a specific, dissociable deficit, one capacity lost while the others carry on; whereas if the colony is only a well-organised aggregate, the same damage should make everything worse by degrees. There's a second wrinkle I can't let go of: much of a colony's 'memory' seems to sit in the nest and its trails rather than in any individual ant, so if you replaced every ant but kept the nest, would the same colony-mind still be there?

Please turn this into a proper proposal. I'll chase up references myself, so please don't give me a bibliography or any citations — name positions and arguments instead, and tell me which ones I need to go and find.

Sections, in this order: The question (about 100 words); Why the usual arguments don't settle it (about 200 words); Method (about 250 words); Possible outcomes (about 150 words); What would count as failure (about 120 words).
