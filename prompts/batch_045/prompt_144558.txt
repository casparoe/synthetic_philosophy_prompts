Hey — I got roped onto the board of the housing co-op I live in about four months ago, and right now we're having what feels like the dumbest possible fight: whether to put up a printed schedule of fines for people who leave the shared workshop and bike room in a mess. I have this nagging feeling that the whole argument is missing something, but I can't put a name on it, which makes it very hard to argue in a meeting.

Here's the pattern I keep seeing — I've now noticed it three times. Case one, our own bike room: for years it was fine. Not spotless, but people mostly racked their bikes, swept up, and the two or three guys who used it most ran the place quietly — if someone left a bike in the middle of the floor for a week they'd get a friendly-but-not-friendly word. Nobody was ever charged anything. Last year the board put in a twenty-dollar fine for bikes left in the walkway, with a sign and a form to fill out. And now nobody leaves bikes in the walkway, but the room is objectively worse. People do exactly what the sign says and nothing more: tools from the shared bench stay out, nobody sweeps, the pump got stolen and nobody said anything. And the two or three guys who used to enforce things have completely stopped, because saying something now feels like reporting someone to an authority instead of just being a person who minds. Case two: my brother's volunteer trail crew got a sponsor offering a small cash bonus for the crew logging the most trail miles. Miles went up, quality went down, and nobody does the jobs that don't have a number attached anymore. Case three: a neighbourhood tool library near my old place added an app where you check tools in and out. Same shape of thing. The thing is, in every case the people didn't stop caring — if anything they talk about the shared thing more than they used to. What changed is what they do about it, and who is allowed to say something. And the bit that actually worries me: I think this is one-way. If we tear the sign down next year, I don't think the old arrangement comes back, because the people who moved in during the fine era never learned it and the old enforcers got out of the habit.

So: does this have a name? Not the general 'people game whatever you measure' thing — a friend keeps saying that's Goodhart's law, but as I understand it that's about the number becoming unreliable for the people reading it, whereas what I'm describing is about the act changing meaning for the person doing it, about the informal enforcement dying rather than just being crowded out, and about it being irreversible. Someone else said moral hazard, but nobody's risk exposure is changing what they do here as far as I can tell. I don't want a label I can drop in a board meeting; I want to know whether the three things I'm describing are really one thing or three, what the nearest established concepts are and exactly how my cases differ from each, whether the irreversibility part has its own literature (something about norms needing to be transmitted to newcomers?), and if there's no established concept that fits, I'd like a proposed name with a definition that says what would and wouldn't count as an instance. Please also settle the boundary cases: is it the same phenomenon when the new rule is a reward rather than a fine, is it the same thing when the outcome is better on the measured dimension and worse on every other one, and is it still this phenomenon if the old informal norm was never actually healthy — say it was really just two guys being territorial?
