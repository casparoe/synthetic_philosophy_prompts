My daughter was born after IVF. Five embryos were created from her mother's eggs and my sperm; one was transferred, and four remain frozen. She is now old enough to ask a question I cannot answer cleanly: if we had transferred a different embryo, would the child born nine months later have been her living a different life, or would it have been someone else — in which case our choice of embryo also decided which of five possible children got to exist, while the other four stayed unborn?

My instinct is the second: she could not have come from a different embryo, because that embryo grew from a different egg and a different sperm cell, and a person grown from those cells would have been a different person, not her in different circumstances. But three things unsettle me. Identical twins come from a single egg and a single sperm and are nonetheless two people, so the gametes alone do not seem to single out one individual. One of the five embryos was created with mitochondrial donation — the nucleus came from one of her mother's eggs, but the rest of the cell came from a donor's egg — so it is not obvious which egg that embryo came from. And I cannot say how much difference would have been enough: a sperm differing by a single base pair, or the same egg and sperm combined in a dish rather than in a fallopian tube.

What should I tell my daughter, and is my instinct defensible? Take a definite position on whether she could have come from a different embryo; say which of my three worries actually bites and which one is confused; and be explicit about what your answer implies for the question she is really circling, whether the four frozen embryos are possible children we owe something to. Where your answer rests on something contested, say so plainly instead of hedging the whole thing.
