I'm writing an essay on Maxwell's demon for a readers-and-referees audience in philosophy of physics — about 4,000 words, aiming to say something sharper than the usual "information is physical" moral. The load-bearing section is drafted below. I'd be grateful for your comments on it before I write the rest of the piece around it. Two stipulations: I'm not asking for references or further reading (I have plenty; I want your own verdict), and I'd rather you tell me a claim is false or unoriginal than help me make it sound better.

**2. Two exorcisms**

*2.1.* The exorcism everyone knows runs on a tariff. The demon has to see the molecule, seeing it means recording which side the molecule was on, a finite memory eventually fills, and restoring a filled bit to a blank one dumps at least k_B T ln 2 into a reservoir at temperature T — roughly 3 × 10⁻²¹ joules at room temperature. Charge the demon that fee on every cycle and the books balance: it never runs at a profit. Call this the *tariff reading*.

*2.2.* Notice what the tariff reading assumes: that the demon is required to be *cyclic* — that at the end of a round it is back in the state it started in, memory included. Nothing in the original set-up requires that. A demon permitted to accumulate records is not a machine paying a per-operation fee; it is a machine burning a stock. A blank bit is a system prepared in a known state, so a stock of N blank bits is N copies of a known state, and that is a low-entropy resource the demon can spend. Such a demon erases nothing, dissipates nothing in its memory, and sorts happily until the memory fills. Its total take is then capped not by any fee but by the size of its initial reserve — on the most favourable accounting, exactly N k_B T ln 2. The same number as before, arrived at without a single erasure ever occurring. Call this the *mortgage reading*.

*2.3.* The two are standardly presented as one argument with two emphases. I want to claim they are structurally different claims: one about rates, one about stocks, neither entailing the other. The tariff reading bounds the steady-state power of a demon that must return to its initial state; the mortgage reading bounds the lifetime take of a demon that need not. A demon with an inexhaustible supply of blank memory is untouched by the mortgage reading, but it is not for that reason a paradox: an infinite blank tape is simply a fuel, and a demon running on one is no stranger than a heat engine running between two reservoirs — it is a converter whose cheapest input we forgot to list. A demon exempt from cycling, meanwhile, is untouched by the tariff reading. Only the conjunction rules out the demon as imagined: indefinite operation on finite resources.

*2.4.* A third case sharpens the taxonomy. Consider the demon realised mechanically instead of epistemically — Smoluchowski's one-way door, a trapdoor light enough to be pushed open by a molecule arriving from one side. It keeps no records, performs no measurement, and fails anyway: light enough to be opened by a single molecule is light enough to be jostled open by the gas, and it opens both ways. Its failure has nothing to do with information. So there are at least three demons — the mechanical one, defeated by its own fluctuations; the cyclic one, defeated by a tariff; the hoarding one, defeated by a finite reserve — and only the middle one is defeated by information.

*2.5.* The obvious way to try to beat the mortgage is to shrink the record. The log is a string; if it is redundant the demon can compress it, and reversible computation is in principle dissipation-free, so compression looks like free memory. But the ledger has two columns and only one of them earns. Call the per-cycle entropy of the log h — what the demon pays to store — and its per-cycle mutual information with the gas i — what the demon can convert. Compression shrinks the log only to h bits per cycle, never below. The work convertible per cycle is bounded by k_B T ln 2 × i. And i ≤ h, with equality only when the log contains nothing but news about the gas. So compression buys extra cycles in proportion to 1/h and loses exactly as much per cycle through i; the product is unchanged. A noisy detector, where h exceeds i, is strictly worse off, because the demon pays storage for its own noise. The mortgage is charged on the gross column, not the useful one.

*2.6.* The same mortgage seems to reappear in other currencies. A demon that never stabilises a record but only couples reversibly to the gas, acting on correlations it never writes down, still ends up correlated with the gas: the entropy it removes reappears as entropy of correlation, and its own marginal state drifts toward mixedness. Blank bits, purity, unused correlations — three denominations of one reserve. But I am not sure what the reserve really is. Is the mortgage denominated in *entropy*, or in *free energy* relative to the ambient bath? A memory that is pure but energetically excited has no entropy to spend and yet looks like a resource; a memory thermalised at a temperature below T has plenty of entropy and less to give. If the right currency is athermality rather than entropy, then the slogan that information is physical is doing more work than it can bear, and the interesting claim is narrower than it sounds.

*2.7.* One last worry about the framing itself. Real memory is not a row of pristine bits; it thermalises continuously, whether or not the demon ever performs an erasure, and then there are no discrete events to charge a fee to. So I'd like to restate the mortgage as a rate: the demon's power output is bounded by T times the rate at which entropy capacity is consumed in its substrate, of which the k_B T ln 2 per bit figure is the ideal limiting case. That raises the question I want to end the section on — whether anything in the argument genuinely depends on the discreteness of the bit, or whether the bit is an artefact of the toy model and the real content is a bookkeeping identity about entropy flux from the gas into the demon. If it is the latter, the demon is not refuted by a dynamical prohibition at all, and the essay should say so.

**What I'd like**

1. Physics first. Is the N k_B T ln 2 cap in 2.2 right, and is it genuinely independent of the Landauer tariff rather than a corollary of it? Is 2.5 sound — is i ≤ h the right way to state the point, does reversible compression really come for free, and does the argument go through when the log's redundancy comes from the gas being predictable rather than from detector noise? Is the rate formulation in 2.7 correct as stated, or does it need a term I've left out?
2. Is the taxonomy in 2.4 real, or do the mechanical and informational failures turn out to be one failure described two ways? Is there a fourth demon I'm missing — in particular, one that profits from an initially non-equilibrium gas, which is not an information case at all?
3. Be blunt about 2.3 and 2.6: does the mortgage framing earn anything over the standard way of putting the point, or is it relabelling? And on the currency question — entropy or free energy — what is the right general form of the bound, and does the answer change if the memory is quantum?
4. Suggest one toy model or calculation, small enough to work through in a page, that would most decisively test whether the mortgage reading and the tariff reading are independent claims rather than one claim in two vocabularies.
5. Line-level: where does the section drag, and where is a metaphor doing work the argument isn't? Which paragraph would a referee cut, and what would they say about 2.7 as a place to end?
