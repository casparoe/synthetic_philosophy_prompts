Before I read another proposed solution, I want a specification: what would a theory have to do to count as solving the meta-problem of consciousness — not the problem of explaining why there is consciousness at all, but the problem of explaining why we say and think the things we do about it, that experience has a subjective character, that it is immediate and private, that it seems to resist physical or functional explanation, that its existence strikes us as at once certain and inexplicable. Argue from the cases and distinctions themselves rather than from what any particular author has argued; I do not want citations or references.

The purpose I have in mind is evidential: I want a theory good enough to tell us how much weight our first-person reports should carry as evidence about consciousness itself, so a theory that treats the reports merely as brute data to be set aside or explained away has missed what I am asking for. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it and say whether failing it refutes a theory or merely disfavours it. Include two requirements that are not the usual ones. First, the grain constraint: the data are not a uniform chorus of I am conscious but a graded and gappy profile — people who report no mental imagery whatsoever, patients who report seeing nothing while still steering around obstacles, subjects who report pain that does not bother them, reports of experience thinning out during absorbed or absent-minded activity and returning when attention is directed, reports of experience with no object at all in certain unusual conditions; a theory that explains only the loud central case has explained very little. Second, the force constraint: the reports are not just utterances to be causally accounted for, since they carry argumentative force, generating the intuition that a functionally identical creature might lack something and that someone who knew all the physical facts might still learn something new, and they move people who have spent years on the question; explain how a bit of cognition acquires that authority for the person who has it, and say whether your explanation vindicates the reports or debunks them.

Flag any pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — especially whether the demand that a solution stay neutral between the reports being veridical and their misdescribing something can be met alongside the demand that it account for their force, or whether any account of the force either endorses or undercuts it and so quietly settles the very question the meta-problem was meant to bracket. Tell me which single requirement excludes the most otherwise attractive theories, and say what the theory must say about reports from systems very unlike us, since a theory that explains every possible report explains none. Write in continuous prose with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, and stop before scoring any existing theory against the list. Conclude by saying how confident you are in the specification as a whole and naming the one requirement you are least confident belongs on it, with your reason.
