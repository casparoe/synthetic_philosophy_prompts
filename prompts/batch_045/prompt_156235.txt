I work in clinical epidemiology and I've never read any philosophy, so here's the vocabulary I actually have: distributions and reference ranges, case definitions, screening vs. case-finding, treatment thresholds, NNTs, surrogate endpoints, cost-per-QALY models, and guideline panels that move a cut-off every few years. Basically: you pick a threshold, above it you treat, the benefit gets counted in endpoints and QALYs, and "normal" just means whatever the reference population and the last committee agreed on.

I want the therapy/enhancement debate translated into exactly those terms, and then I want the residue report. The positions I keep half-hearing about: therapy restores normal species functioning while enhancement goes beyond it; the fair-equality-of-opportunity version where therapy protects a normal range of opportunity and enhancement doesn't; the welfarist line that says enhancement can be something we owe people, maybe even owe our kids; the Sandel-ish worry about mastery, striving and treating what we have as a gift; and the disability-rights / neurodiversity objection that for some conditions calling it a deficit in the first place is the problem. For each one tell me what translates, what you had to fudge to make it fit, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all — and for each bit of residue, whether my framework is missing something or the original claim was vaguer than it sounds.

Two places I suspect the translation breaks, so please use them. One: my thresholds move. Cut-offs for blood pressure, cholesterol, even height have crept downwards over the years until what used to count as enhancement gets reclassified as treatment, and I've started calling it threshold creep — so if the baseline is a committee decision, what's left of a distinction defined against a baseline? Two: the same drug is therapy in one person and enhancement in another depending on which reference class you score them in — a stimulant, a GLP-1 agonist, metformin for someone with normal glucose — which my models just treat as a coding choice, but the philosophers seam to think the flip means something. And if you want one test case: the people currently trying to get ageing itself accepted as a treatable indication. Continuous prose only please, like an email — no headings, bullets, bold or tables, and don't soften the parts where my way of thinking simply can't express the issue.
