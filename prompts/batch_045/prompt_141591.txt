hi — I do a column on science and ideas (and a small live thing we tape in front of an audience once a quarter), and the next piece is about near-misses. The case I keep circling is a control-room operator at a chemical plant who signed off on a pressure reading that had been sitting at nominal for weeks because somebody left the sensor in calibration mode. Every engineer I talked to says the same two things: he was right, and he didn't know. That gap is what I want to end the segment on, and I'd much rather end it on a poem than on a slide of further reading.

What I want is a villanelle, nineteen lines, in the voice of that operator, where the two refrains are the two subjunctive halves of Nozick's tracking account — the sensitivity one (if it weren't so I wouldn't believe it) and the adherence one (if it were so I would). Someone who knows the theory should be able to tell which refrain is which without being told, and should see why they have to keep taking turns all the way down and only arrive side by side in the final quatrain: neither condition earns anything on its own, the conjunction is what knowledge is. I think I have the cases right — the first is what fails for the stuck sensor, the second is what would fail for someone who is right by luck, like an inspector who signs off because the paperwork arrived pre-filled — but tell me if I've got them backwards and build it the correct way round. Keep each refrain short, under nine syllables or so, since it has to survive six outings. One thing to leave out: I know there's a rival formulation people swap in, roughly if I believe it then it's so — don't blend that in, I want tracking proper, not the safety version.

The bit I actually care about is the ending. The reason this material is interesting to me is that tracking buys you ordinary knowledge by giving up closure: he knows the pressure is fine, the pressure being fine entails that the whole panel isn't being fed a recorded loop, and he doesn't know that. A villanelle never generates a new line — it only returns the two it started with, over and over, on two rhymes — and I want that stinginess to be the argument, so that the reader feels the guarantee hold for p and refuse to travel to what follows from p without anybody having to announce the word closure. If some part of that mapping won't actually bear the weight, don't fudge it into a rhyme: put the poem first and then, in two or three sentences underneath, say where my setup breaks. Otherwise it should work as a poem for someone who has never heard of Nozick and as an accurate rendering for someone who has.
