My flatmate Dani has thought way more than is healthy about whether you could have come from a different sperm and egg. She took one philosophy class years ago, came away with the Kripke-ish line that no, you couldn't, and has been running on that plus gut feel ever since. Her verdicts so far:

- her younger brother, same two parents but a different sperm and egg: not her, obviously, that's just her brother
- her actual zygote carried by a surrogate in another country and raised by a diferent family: still her, just with a different life
- her embryo split at the two-cell stage, both halves implanted, two babies: neither of them is her, and she won't pick one
- half the cells of her four-cell embryo died and the rest went on to become her: still her
- her zygote spent three days in a dish before implantation: still her
- her zygote fused with a fraternal twin's zygote, so the single person who resulted has two cell lines: still her, she'd just have some cells that aren't hers
- a baby made by putting the nucleus of one of her skin cells into a donor egg that's had its own nucleus removed: not her

Here's the one I can't call. Same sperm, same egg, everything the same right up to fertilisation — except that because her mother's mitochondria carry a nasty mutation, the nuclear material from that fertilised egg gets moved into a donor egg whose own nucleus has been taken out, and that's the embryo that gets implanted. So the nuclear genome is exactly the one she came from, but the cytoplasm and every mitochondrion come from a stranger. This is the real three-parent-baby procedure, more or less. Would she say that person is her?

I care much less about the yes or no than about what rule she's actually running, what other rules fit all seven of those verdicts just as well, which one you'd definately put money on and why, and what case you'd put to her next to settle it. The bit I keep going in circles on: if it turns on the mitochondria rather than on the egg as a cell, or on the fact that the original zygote was taken apart and rebuilt, I can't see how you'd ever get her to say which.
