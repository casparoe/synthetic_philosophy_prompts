I need a thought experiment built to a specification. It is for a paper section I am drafting on whether the user-illusion account of consciousness can really be as quietist in practice as its defenders claim.

The two views, as I shall use them. On the user-illusion view — call it U — what a subject sincerely says about their own conscious life is the output of a self-monitoring interface: compressed, edited, geared to action and to the maintenance of a usable self-model rather than to accurate self-description. There is no further intrinsic phenomenal fact of the matter that the report is answerable to, over and above what the interface delivers. On the rival view — call it R — reports are defeasible but genuine evidence about real phenomenal goings-on, and those goings-on have their nature independently of whatever the interface happens to say.

The distinction I want to isolate is one that U is characteristically committed to treating as empty. In the literature it is labelled with the pair Orwellian and Stalinesque. Take an episode to which the subject, afterwards, has no introspective access at all: either they were conscious of it and the report or the memory was subsequently rewritten (Orwellian), or it never reached consciousness in the first place (Stalinesque). R holds that one or the other is true. U holds that there is nothing for the difference to consist in — not that the evidence is merely hard to come by, but that no possible first- or third-personal finding could settle it, and that the question is therefore vacuous.

What I want is a case in which both descriptions fit a single episode equally well, and yet taking one rather than the other would change what someone should do.

Constraints on the scenario:

1. Realistic, and set somewhere ordinary: an operating theatre, a ward, a clinic, a care home, a product-safety review. Existing technology only. No androids, uploads, demons, or exotic physics.
2. One paragraph, and the third-personal evidence must be identical under both descriptions — the same traces on the same monitors, the same behaviour, the same physiology, the same everything. If anything in your scenario would even in principle tell us which description is correct, the scenario has failed; the pair must be what U takes them to be.
3. The practical divergence must be generated, not stipulated. It has to fall out of what each description says about the episode plus ordinary practical reasoning. It is not enough that the episode would be described differently in a case report, and it is not enough that a clinician in the story happens to have read the literature and therefore chooses differently.
4. The stakes must be ones a non-philosopher would recognise as stakes: suffering, what to tell a patient, whether to apologise and for what, compensation, consent, professional or criminal responsibility, whether to continue a treatment.
5. The case should be one in which a defender of U would want to say that the distinction is empty, and yet still owes the person who has to decide something useful to go on.

After the scenario I want:

(a) Both descriptions applied to the episode, in the same vocabulary, so that it is visible that they fit it equally and that nothing further has been smuggled in.

(b) The verdict each generates: who should do what differently, to whom, with the reasoning that takes you from the description to that verdict set out step by step.

(c) Named: the single feature of the case in virtue of which an empty distinction acquires practical force.

(d) A demonstration that this is the feature: remove it, hold the rest fixed, and show the case collapsing — and say what it collapses into.

(e) The strongest reply available to a defender of U. I expect something in this neighbourhood: the emptiness is metaphysical and not practical; practice should be run off a policy that never consults the undecidable question; the thing to apologise for is the breach of protocol and the risk imposed, not the erased experience; what one says at the bedside should be settled by what helps rather than by what is true. Give the best version of that reply, and then say whether it disposes of the case or leaves a residue, and what the residue consists in.

(f) One paragraph on what the case establishes and what it does not. The claim I want to be able to make is that U's practical quietism holds for forward-looking questions and breaks down for a specifiable class of backward-looking ones. If your case will not carry that claim, say so and say what it carries instead.

Failure conditions: a scenario in which one of the two descriptions is secretly better supported; a divergence obtained by loading extra commitments into either description beyond the bare characterisation above; a difference that is only a difference in how we would narrate the episode; and any case in which the defender of U can say that nothing hangs on it and be obviously right.
