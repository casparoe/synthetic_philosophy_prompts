I'm taking a reading semiar on Confucian role ethics this term, and I keep running into the old ruler-ruler, minister-minister, father-father, son-son line (the one where the word for each role gets repeated, and the usual gloss is that the ruler should really be a ruler, the father a father, and so on). Our TA deployed it to argue that the view isn't just a fancy name for obedience to hierarchy: she said the line is really a standard aimed at the person in the superordinate role, so if the ruler doesn't act like one, he isn't a true ruler and maybe has no claim on the minister's deference. Later, the professor used the very same line for a much more metaphysical point: that on this view there is no pre-role individual hiding behind the roles—the person just is the sum of their roles, so my worry about what I want apart from being a daughter, a student, or a citizen is supposedly a confusion. I got whiplash, and then I made it worse by bringing up my friend who is both a father and a judge; if the line just says be a good father and be a good judge, it doesn't tell me what to do when being a good father (protecting his kid) and being a good judge (sentencing him) pull in opposite directions. The professor said something like there is no conflict, you are just the relationship you are in at the moment, which sounded like a dodge, but maybe that is a real implication of one of the stronger readings?

So: can you lay out the precise claims that slogan could be making, as a menu? I want the boring slash trivial readings (people should meet some minimal expectations of their social position), the semantic or rectification-of-names style reading (ruler has a built-in normative standard, so a bad ruler isn't a ruler in the full sense), the constitutive or relational readings (the self is nothing but its roles, or persons are constituted by role-relationships rather than by individual properties), any hierarchy-critical reading (a ruler who fails the standard loses legitimacy or the mandate), and maybe a reading about reciprocity or correlated roles—plus say for each whether it's a platitude, a substantive thesis a serious role ethicist could defend, or just false (e.g., an anything-goes relativism or a command of blind obedience). Then tell me which readings would actually support the TA's use, which would support the professor's no-pre-role-self claim, and whether any reading gives real help with the role-conflict case or whether that case just shows the slogan runs out. I don't need citations or who said what—just the conceptual verdicts, and please don't turn it into one big argument for whichever reading you like best; I need a menu I can bring to the reading group, not an essay that picks one reading and runs with it.
