The last time we ran this topic, half the class turned in variations on a single paragraph — state Good's theorem, say the expected value of information is non-negative because an expected-utility maximizer can always condition on the message and pick the same act she would have picked anyway, note that information is usually costly in practice, conclude — and I could not separate the students who understood the result from the students who had memorized the labels. To keep them from pattern-matching on examples they have seen, I would like the question built around a small piece of staged apparatus of my own devising: call an information source **leaky** if the mere fact that you consult it is observable by some other party whose subsequent behaviour depends on whether you consulted (the content of the signal need not be observable to them); call it **act-eroding** if receiving its output removes some act from your feasible set — most obviously the act of sincerely saying, or of the record showing, that you do not know; and call an agent a **fixed-rule updater** if she revises her beliefs by conditioning but then feeds the posterior into a decision rule she is not free to re-optimize for each message, such as "proceed iff the posterior probability of safety exceeds 0.7".
