Draft essay: "Thomson's Lamp and the Malignant Idealization"

§0. A lamp is off at t = 0. At each time t_n = 1 − 2^(−n), for n = 1, 2, 3, …, a switch is flipped, and nothing else bears on the lamp. So it is off on [0, 1/2), on [1/2, 3/4), off on [3/4, 7/8), and so on, with infinitely many flips and no last one before t = 1. Question: is it on or off at t = 1?

§1. Two standard moves. Thomson's argument concludes: neither, so the description is inconsistent and supertasks are impossible. The argument needs a settling premise: for the lamp to be on at t = 1, either some flip into the on-state must be the last one, or the lamp must be on throughout some final interval [1 − ε, 1); but every on-interval is followed by an off-interval, and symmetrically for off. The Benacerraf-style reply concludes: no contradiction, since the specification fixes the state only on [0, 1), and the value at t = 1 is an additional boundary condition that may be set either way; nothing follows about supertasks. I take that reply to be right but incomplete. It tells us that a gap exists without telling us which quantities have gaps, why they have them, and when it is legitimate to fill a gap by stipulation. Those three questions are where I think the real content is.

§2. Reformulating the gap. The state at t = 1 would have to be the parity of the number of flips completed by then. The sequence of partial parities alternates and so has no limit; equivalently, the state function has no limit as t approaches 1 from below. That is the entire content of the puzzle: the lamp's terminal state would be the parity of ω, and ω has no parity. Nothing about lamps is doing any work — the same gap opens for any two-valued quantity flipped on a schedule with no last flip.

§3. Where the gap comes from. A real lamp has a finite relaxation time τ > 0: brightness lags the switch rather than jumping with it. Two consequences. (a) In the tail of the schedule the intervals between flips are shorter than τ, so the lamp never completes a transition; brightness approaches some intermediate steady value, and I claim the limit exists for any convergent schedule. I am assuming first-order, monotone relaxation with an exponentially decaying response kernel, and I want to know whether more than that is needed. The limit is set by the schedule's duty cycle: for the halving schedule the on-intervals contribute 1/4 + 1/16 + 1/64 + …, i.e. one third of the elapsed time, so the terminal brightness is roughly a third of full. (b) The idealization Thomson's argument requires is exactly τ = 0, which replaces the smoothed history with the raw switch signal. Smoothing is the operation that turns a non-convergent two-valued history into a convergent magnitude; setting τ = 0 deletes the operation that produced the limit. On this reading the gap is manufactured by the idealization rather than discovered in the world.

§4. Benign and malignant idealization. Not every idealization is illegitimate. My proposal is the Limit Preservation Constraint: an idealization is admissible for the purposes of an argument only if it preserves the limiting behaviour of every quantity that the argument goes on to query. Small-angle idealization preserves the period of a pendulum, which is what the pendulum argument queries. Replacing thermal lag with instantaneous switching does not preserve the limit of brightness, which is exactly what the lamp argument queries. So Thomson's idealization is malignant relative to his own question, and the paradox is an artifact of it.

§5. From contradiction to vagueness. In the relaxed model the terminal brightness is determinate but is neither on nor off, so the sentence "the lamp is on at t = 1" is a vague sentence about a perfectly determinate physical magnitude. The right conclusion is therefore not that the lamp's state at t = 1 is metaphysically indeterminate, and not that supertasks are impossible, but that our bivalent predicate has no sharp application at that instant. Note the trade-off: replace the vague predicate with a sharp one, say drawing more than 0.1 W, and the relaxed model gives a determinate answer; restore τ = 0 and the gap returns. Contradiction was never in play.

§6. Tentative extension. If §§3–4 are right, the free boundary conditions that Benacerraf-style replies permit are not a general feature of supertasks but a symptom of malignant idealization, and a quantity-by-quantity limit audit tells you which terminal facts are fixed and which are stipulative. Applied to a machine that performs infinitely many computational steps before t = 1 and then prints an output: the output is not the limit of anything in the pre-terminal history, so it is a free parameter supplied by stipulation, and such a machine cannot be credited with settling any question it was built to settle. I am unsure how far this generalizes, and I expect it to be the most contested part of the paper.
