I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums since the days of the Sequences, and I'm doing a short series on the Liar paradox for a small but argumentative readership: Tarski-style hierarchies, Kripke's truth-value gaps and fixed points, dialetheism, the revision theory — one instalment each, with a verse epigraph at the head of every one. I'd like a single villanelle to sit at the head of the whole series, and I want the form to do the argumentative work rather than decorate it: the two refrains should be a Liar sentence and its strengthened successor, each returning after a stanza in which some theory has supposedly dealt with the paradox, so that the reader feels the revenge problem instead of being told about it.

Please make it technically honest. Someone who knows Kripke's fixed points should be able to tell which stanza is which proposal; should see why the Truth-Teller and the Liar come apart (both ungrounded, but the Truth-Teller admits fixed points in which it is true and others in which it is false, whereas the Liar refuses every valuation); and should read the closing quatrain, where the two refrains at last appear together, as the moment the gap theorist cannot say of the strengthened sentence what he wanted to say about the original. It should still stand up as a poem for a reader who has never heard of a fixed point. Please write it in British English.
