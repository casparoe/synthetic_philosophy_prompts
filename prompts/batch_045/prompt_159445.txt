hey — i'm putting together a one-page handout for a reading group I run on the old abolitionist / "paradise engineering" programme, i.e. the idea that we should use biotech to get rid of suffering altogether rather than just treat the cases where it malfunctions. Everyone in the group already accepts the premise that suffering is bad and that there's a staggeringly large amount of it, so a tree that branches on that is useless to me. What I want is something that sorts people by what they say about concrete cases into the positions that actually exist downstream of that shared premise.

So: give me a decision tree, roughly six branch questions and six to eight leaves. Every branch node has to be phrased as a case or claim someone could answer without knowing which position it leads to — no "do you think suffering should be abolished?", and nothing that just asks them to pick a label they like. Each leaf gets a name where there is one, "unnamed" where there isn't, plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

Two distinctions I definitely want doing real work in the tree. First, the one between suffering-as-unpleasant-felt-experience, pain-as-information, and aversive motivation — the tree should be able to separate someone who's happy to delete the first while keeping the other two from someone who thinks the whole package has to go or nothing has been achieved. The dentist case is the one I keep using: the drill is wrecking your tooth, you know exactly what's happening and you're strongly motivated to get it fixed, but nothing about it hurts. The grief case is the harder one, and I'd like the tree to put weight on people who are fine with the dentist and recoil at the grief. Second, whether the intervention is meant to raise the hedonic floor (guarantee nobody's experience drops below neutral, leave the rest to individual choice), compress the range, or raise the ceiling — the "safety floor, not heaven" position is one i want as its own leaf, not folded into full abolitionism.

Other branch candidates you can use or swap out: heritable/enbryonic rewriting of what kinds of minds exist versus reversible adult intervention; scope (consenting adults, all future humans, all sentient life, digital minds); whether the person survives the rewrite; and what happens to striving if nothing hurts — I want the "static bliss" worry to have somewhere to go, including the leaf where the honest conclusion is that the whole project was misconceived.

Two more asks. Flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely can't separate rather than pretending a question divides them — I suspect direct-stimulation wireheading and reward-gradient abolitionism collapse together unless some node handles whether positive states still have to be about real states of affairs. And point at two or three unoccupied cells in the logical space and say whether they're empty because incoherent, because unattractive, or because nobody's bothered. Please don't give me a who-said-what survey or a reading list; assume no web access, and if some combination doesn't have a settled name just call it unnamed and describe the view.
