I teach a seminar on agency and free will, and there is a configuration I keep running into for which I have no word. It is not the flat stereotypy of the familiar wasp case — the animal that runs a routine to its end no matter what happens partway through. It is roughly the inverse: an agent who is genuinely flexible, who deliberates, learns, and revises plans in response to reasons, but whose flexibility is *scheduled*. Some standing rule settles when reconsideration is in order, and that rule is not itself up for reconsideration at the time it applies.

Three instances. A colleague rewrites her syllabus from scratch at the start of every term and will not alter it during the term; asked what she would do if something genuinely syllabus-relevant happened in week five — a new edition, a two-week campus closure — she said, without any sense of paradox, that she would note it for next year. A friend resolved, after long reflection, to revisit his career plans only on his birthday, so as to keep the rest of the year free of second-guessing; last March something happened that he himself grants would have moved him had it happened in June, and he declined to think about it until June. And in the ethological material I have read, the same shape seems to recur as flexibility during the acquisition of a routine paired with rigidity during its execution, with nothing that revisits the switch between the two.

I am after a term of art, if one exists, for rigidity at the meta-level — flexibility that is real but exercised only under a rule that is not itself flexible — and, more than the label, its carving. I take it this is not stubbornness or akrasia: in my cases the agent is not failing to do what she judges best, but following a judgment about when to judge. I take it to be more than the truism that no rule can govern its own application without regress, though I want to know how near to that it sits. What I most want clarified is the edge: does the structure count as rigidity at all if the higher-level rule is itself revisable at some other scheduled time, or does that merely relocate the problem one level up? And if it does merely relocate it, what would have to be true for the regress to terminate in something that is not itself rigid? A name alone would be of little use to me; that last question is why I care, since pointing to an agent's flexibility is the standard way of marking agency off from mechanism, and the flexibility always seems to rest on something that is not flexible.
