I think I've basically fixed the nonidentity problem and I want holes poked in it. Rule: given a menu of options, score each option by summing over everyone who exists in it, w(person) × (number of options on the menu in which that person would exist ÷ total number of options). Rationale: people who exist no matter what you do get full weight, people whose existence hangs on the choice get discounted by how contingent they are — which feels like it respects the person-affecting intuition instead of just bulldozing it.

It gives the intuitive verdict in the standard cases (Parfit's depletion vs. conservation, the woman who can just wait a month), and it seems to get there without going totalist, since merely possible future people are always discounted — so I don't see how the repugnant conclusion gets in.
