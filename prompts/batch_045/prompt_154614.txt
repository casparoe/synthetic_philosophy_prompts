Here's the half-formed idea. Jeffrey conditionalization needs rigidity — the probability of H given each cell of the evidential partition has to survive the update. The case I keep coming back to is where one experience does two things at once: it nudges your credence in H, and it's also evidence that the channel you got it through is dodgy (dim light making a cloth look maybe-green-maybe-blue, together with a note that your color vision is off today; or a witness you half suspect is lying). Straight conditionalization handles the second part, Jeffrey handles the first, neither obviously handles both, and I definately don't want to just say "there's no rule here."

Two hunches. First: the usual repair — refine the partition until rigidity holds again — starts to look like it can manufacture rigidity for almost any update you like, which would make the constraint empty. Second: the real test of a candidate rule is whether it commutes. If I have two such experiences in an afternoon, should the order I process them in change where I land? Is path-independence something to impose or something to give up? And I have a vague feeling the plausible rules form a family indexed by something like a channel-trust parameter, so that commutativity might pin down which member is right — or might show none of them are.
