Please act as a professional philosopher of social epistemology who also writes formal verse, and take the task in stages: before drafting anything, spend a short paragraph pinning down, in plain prose, exactly which mechanism you are dramatising and what each structural feature of the poem is going to carry; then sketch the two refrains; then write the villanelle; then check it against the mechanism and revise.

I write a column on science and ideas for a magazine of general interest, and I am finishing a two-part piece on why a crowd that looks unanimous can be epistemically empty — I mean the rational-herding and observational-learning material, not the usual sheeple essay, and my editor wants something for the back page that isn't another graph of a hockey stick. So I want a villanelle: nineteen lines, six stanzas, two refrains, and I want the form to do the philosophical work rather than decorate it. The setting I have in mind is a valley where each household, in a fixed order at the spring planting meeting, announces which of two seed varieties it will sow; every household has also run its own small trial plot, but only the announcements travel, not the results of the trials. One refrain should be the speaker's own private evidence from their trial plot, and the other should be the choice the queue has already made; as the poem goes on, the crowd refrain should grow more assured while the private one grows quieter, and crucially nobody should ever be shown to be mistaken or weak — this must be informational herding, not reputational, with no one currying favour or protecting a position, and every person reasoning correctly on what is available to them. The point I want the repetition to make is that what passes downstream is the action and not the reason, so the line gets longer without getting wiser, and once the cascade has closed each further household contributes nothing at all even as the unanimity looks more impressive.

The part I care about most is the last quatrain. A villanelle must finally put its two refrains together, and I want you to use that convergence to stage the fragility result: set them side by side so a reader sees that the private line was silenced rather than refuted, and then let a single plain, unmusical line — reporting the full tally of what all the trial plots actually showed, in the drabbest words you can manage — break the villanelle's own music and hollow out the crowd refrain that follows it, because a cascade that a hundred defectors could not shift can be undone by one honest count of evidence nobody was ever able to transmit. Please don't name-drop a single theorist or model in the poem; someone who works on this literature should recognise the setup and the result from the mechanics alone, and someone who doesn't should simply find a good poem about a valley that planted the wrong seed. If you want a final wrinkle, you are welcome to leave open whether an early public announcement of the same tally would have helped, or would merely have locked everyone in sooner — I'd rather have that ambiguity than a tidy moral, since my own reporting left me genuinely unsure.
