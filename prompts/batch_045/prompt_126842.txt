My brother-in-law got put on unpaid leave last month after a screenshot of a private post of his went around, somebody dug up his employer's general inbox, and a few hundred emails came in from people who have nothing to do with his town or his industry. My sister is furious and the family group chat has been a war zone ever since. I should say I've got no background in any of this — I install flooring — I've just been turning it over in my head for weeks. Every time I try to say something in the chat, someone replies "freedom of speech doesn't mean freedom from consequences" and that's apparently supposed to be the end of it.

It isn't the end of it, but I can't say why. I keep coming back to three things that feel like they matter and I genuinely can't tell if I'm onto something or just going in circles:

1. Nobody decided anything. There was no hearing, no person who said "you're fired," no written rule he broke. Several hundred people each sent an email, each one on their own, and then something quite serious happened to him anyway. When people say "consequences" they talk about it like weather, but it's made entirely out of individual choices. Isn't there supposed to be somebody in charge of a punishment?

2. Almost none of the people emailing were the people his post was actually about, or anybody like them. They read about it third-hand. Does it matter whether the people coming for you are the ones you actually affected, or is that beside the point?

3. My sister says he can still say whatever he wants, he just has to pay for it now. But a big enough price is just a ban with extra steps, isn't it? A fine of a million dollars isn't a fee on talking, it's a rule against it. So "you're free to speak, it'll just cost you" doesn't look like the clean distinction everyone seems to think it is.

Also my other brother pointed out that the same line seems to cut backwards: if my brother-in-law can speak and take what comes, then so can the people emailing his boss, and so can my sister complaining about them — so what are we even arguing about? That felt like a trick when he said it, but I couldn't put my finger on why.

Can you take that one sentence and lay out, as a list I could paste into the group chat, the different precise things it might actually be claiming — the ones that are true almost by definition and don't tell us anything, the ones that are real and arguable, the ones that are false or quietly sneak the conclusion in — and for each one say whether it would actually settle our situation or whether it only looks like it does? And please tell me honestly whether my three points above are doing any work, or whether I'm chasing nothing.
