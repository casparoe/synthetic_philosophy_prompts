I build and operate distributed systems for a living and have no formal training in philosophy, so please read what follows as a specification rather than as an attempt to use the field's vocabulary correctly. Where you need a technical term, define it.

The base case, stated so we agree on it. A subject is put to sleep on Sunday. A fair coin is flipped. If it lands Heads she is awakened once, on Monday. If it lands Tails she is awakened on Monday and again on Tuesday. Before each awakening her memory of any prior awakening is erased, and she knows the full protocol. On waking she is asked for her credence that the coin landed Heads. One camp answers 1/2, on the grounds that she has learned nothing she did not already know; the other answers 1/3, on the grounds that two thirds of the awakenings permitted by the protocol are Tails-awakenings. I know both camps exist; I do not want a survey of them, and I do not want citations or reading suggestions. Reason from the setup.

The variant I actually care about, call it the Fork. Same protocol with one change on the Tails branch. At the close of Monday the subject's brain state is snapshotted; the Monday body is not re-awakened. The snapshot is restored into a fresh, physically distinct body on Tuesday, run for one day, and then terminated. The two instances never overlap in time. Subjectively, the Tuesday instance cannot tell whether it is the numerically identical Monday subject, re-awakened, or a causal descendant restored from a checkpoint into new hardware; its total evidence at the moment of being asked is, as far as it can tell, exactly what it would be in the base case.

The proposition I want a number on. In the Fork variant the rational credence on waking is 1/3, and the Fork variant and the base case have the same answer for the same reasons — that is, whether the Tuesday subject is numerically the same person as the Monday subject does no argumentative work, and anyone whose reasoning yields 1/3 in the base case is committed to 1/3 here.

For resolution: I will accept either a credal reading or a decision-theoretic one, but say which you are using, and if you use the decision-theoretic one settle explicitly how payoffs are aggregated. If you think credence for a duplicated or restored subject is simply not a well-defined quantity, or that the question is verbal, count that as the proposition failing rather than as a reason to withhold a number; put it in the decomposition as a term.

What I want back:

1. A headline number, stated plainly.
2. A decomposition into sub-questions, each with a number and one line of justification. The ones I expect to carry weight, which you should split, merge, or replace as you think right: whether self-locating credence under duplication is well-defined at all, or whether the dispute is really about decision rules or about what question the word credence is being used to ask; how much of your number is inherited from taking a side in the base case rather than from anything specific to the Fork; whether centred evidence of the form it is now, for me, an awakening inside this protocol screens off facts about numerical identity, so that identity cannot enter the argument at all; and whether the two instances share a wallet, meaning that if the Monday instance and the Tuesday instance each accept a bet, the payoffs are aggregated to one agent or to two. My colleague claims the wallet question is the only real question here; I suspect the credence question is upstream of it. Tell me which of us is wrong.
3. The combination: show how the terms multiply or otherwise compose into the headline number, and identify the single sub-question the number is most sensitive to, the one you would resolve first if you could only resolve one.
4. A diagnosis of a live disagreement. My own credence that identity is irrelevant in the sense above is about 0.35. My colleague puts it at about 0.85. Both of us answer 1/3 in the base case. On your decomposition, what is the most likely thing we are actually disagreeing about?
5. A unit test, which I care about more than the rest. In the Mirror variant the copying is attached to the other outcome: on Heads she is awakened on Monday and a snapshot is restored into a fresh body on Tuesday, and on Tails she is awakened only once. State what your account says the credence is there, and whether it follows by the same rule as your answer to the Fork. If the two answers do not come from one rule, explain what the additional rule is and why it is not ad hoc.
