My team of four handles a shared queue of support cases at a software company. Six months ago our office moved to a 35-hour week with no cut in pay and no reduction in the queue. Three of my teammates have quietly begun logging back in at night and on weekends; the system records the logins and nobody mentions them. One of them has taken almost no days off in a year and has started having chest pains. I have been stopping at 35 hours, and my share of the cases is being absorbed by the others, not because I am slow (my closed-cases-per-hour actually went up when the hours dropped) but because the work simply sits there until someone picks it up.

What I cannot work out is whether I am doing something wrong, and what the wrongness would consist in. My free time is not edifying: I watch videos and scroll. My teammate says he genuinely likes the extra hours, that the cases are his craft, that he would be restless at home, and that my leaving is a choice he is covering for rather than a burden I am imposing on him. Meanwhile our manager has begun citing how well the team is keeping up as evidence that the shorter week is sustainable at current headcount, which suggests the unpaid hours are being quietly converted into an argument against hiring a fifth person.

So: am I free-riding on my teammates, or is the free-riding charge just the work ethic in disguise, the assumption that hours not spent producing need a justification? Does it matter what I do with the time I have reclaimed? Does the fact that his extra hours seem to come from him rather than from the company make my benefiting from them different? And what do I owe him: an argument that he is being exploited, a refusal to benefit, or simply to be left alone? I have seen people invoke Japanese overwork deaths and the French shorter week in arguments like this, and neither framing tells me what to do on Monday. I would like an answer that says what kind of problem this is and whether the pieces I have described are one problem or several, rather than general advice about boundaries and burnout.
