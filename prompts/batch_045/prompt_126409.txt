I have been working through the diachronic money-pump argument and I think it generalizes into a conclusion nobody should want. I would like you to find where it breaks.

Set-up. Let H be the proposition that the referendum passes. At t1 Nadia's credence in H is 0.5, and that is exactly what her evidence supports. She also knows the following reliably: at t2 tomorrow she will be given a drug as part of a trial she cannot leave; the drug is administered under a fixed protocol that is independent of H; and it reliably induces in subjects a credence of 0.9 in H together with the conviction that this new credence is well grounded and that their earlier hesitation was mere confusion. She knows all of this now, and she knows that at t2 she will not act on anything she now decides: any note she leaves, she knows, her later self will dismiss as the product of her pre-drug confusion. Assume the bookie knows everything Nadia knows.

(1) The exploitability criterion: a body of attitudes is irrational if there is a possible sequence of trades, each individually acceptable to the agent at the price offered at the time it is offered, whose net effect is a sure loss to her. This is the whole force of the money-pump argument; without it, the coherence norms — transitivity, the probability axioms, and the diachronic constraint that your planned later credence in H should equal your current credence in H conditional on having that later credence, the principle usually called Reflection — are brute stipulations.

(2) The construction. At t1 the bookie buys from Nadia a ticket paying $1 if H, for $0.51. Given credence 0.5, the ticket is worth $0.50 to her, so she accepts. At t2, after the drug, the bookie sells her a ticket paying $1 if H, for $0.89. Given her new credence of 0.9, the ticket is worth $0.90 to her, so she accepts. Her holdings cancel in every state of the world; her cash position is −$0.38 for sure. Raise the stakes and the loss is unbounded. I have written the bets unconditionally because the event that her t2 credence is 0.9 is certain for her; the version with bets conditional on that event, called off if it fails, is the same argument in general form.

(3) The construction needs nothing beyond the gap between her current credence in H and the credence the drug will give her: it needs a violation of Reflection. Nadia violates it maximally, since cr_t1(H | cr_t2(H) = 0.9) = 0.5.

(4) Foresight does not rescue her. She can see the whole sequence at t1. But refusing at t1 means declining a trade that is favorable by her own current lights, and the money-pump argument cannot permit that escape: if declining a locally favorable trade is allowed, then an agent with cyclic preferences is immune to every pump by the same move, and the argument for transitivity collapses. And at t2 she cannot refuse, since the trade is favorable by the only lights she then has.

(5) Generalization. The same construction works for any agent who knows in advance how her attitudes will move for reasons she now regards as irrelevant: hypnosis, a stroke, targeted advertising, a decade of ordinary ageing, or a drug. Each such agent is, at t1, exploitable, and so by (1) irrational at t1.

(6) The only way for such an agent to be non-exploitable is to have already, at t1, the attitudes she knows she will later be made to have: 0.9, in Nadia's case. Nothing in the construction cares whether the induced attitude is an improvement or a deterioration. The requirement is conformity to whatever comes.

(7) So the argument makes irrationality a function of what will happen to a person rather than of how she thinks, and it directs her to bring her current attitudes into line with a process she correctly regards as distorting. I take that as a reductio.

(8) A further wrinkle. If Nadia foresees the pump and would pay up to $0.38 to be spared it, the bookie can extract nearly that amount merely by turning up with the offer. An agent who is perfectly coherent but merely mistaken about whether she will be manipulated is then exploitable too, and her exploitability is a function of her empirical beliefs about her own future.

Here is what I want to keep: (1) in some restricted form, since I know no other non-stipulative argument for the coherence norms; and the claim that rationality is a property of attitudes and reasoning rather than of causal situation. What I cannot accept is (5) and (6).

One contrast I also cannot let go of. If Nadia were going to take the drug voluntarily, because tomorrow she will want to and she now thinks wanting to is a bad reason, I have no hesitation saying that something is defective in her plan, and I would want the diachronic argument to reach it. Whatever you propose has to separate that case from the coerced one.

So: which step do you reject? If you restrict (1), state the restriction exactly — that the exploiter must not cause the later attitude change, that the later attitudes must be ones the agent would now endorse, that the trades must be acceptable given her foresight rather than merely locally, or something else — and say whether the restriction is principled or ad hoc. If instead you reject (4) and let foresight in, tell me what the money-pump argument is still worth, and what it costs a cyclically preferring or non-conditionalizing agent to become sophisticated. Then price the alternatives: what each rejection commits me to, and which is cheapest.
