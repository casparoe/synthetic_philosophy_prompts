I am building a set of prompts on higher-order evidence and I want one whose answer has to be worked out rather than recalled. The case below is one I constructed; I have not seen it discussed anywhere, so please treat its structure as the object of the question rather than as an instance of a familiar one.

**The Drift Room.** A subject — call her the examinee — spends twenty minutes on a hard binary question Q and arrives at credence 0.85 in answer A. She is then handed a briefing. The briefing reports preregistered, large-sample findings; set aside any doubt about its reliability and take all five claims as statistical facts.

1. Among subjects who, on questions of Q's class, arrive at a credence of at least 0.80 in one answer after extended reasoning, the favored answer is correct 78% of the time.
2. That 78% is a mixture. Twenty percent of such subjects are *drifters*: a fault in their reasoning displaces their favored answer, and drifters' favored answers are correct only 30% of the time; the other 80% are correct 90% of the time. The displacement is not noise — it is worse than chance, so a subject certain she was a drifter would do better to answer against her favored answer.
3. Drifters are not introspectively distinguishable from non-drifters at the time of answering. They are distinguishable by how they respond to being told claims 1 and 2. Shown that briefing and asked to re-answer, 80% of drifters hold their original credence or raise it, adducing the felt quality of their own reasoning and the thought that the statistics describe other people; 20% of non-drifters do this. Call the response *steadfastness*.
4. Among steadfast subjects who are then told the claim-3 figures, 85% of drifters and 40% of non-drifters revise sharply downward and describe their earlier steadfastness as exactly the artifact those figures predict. Call this *sophisticated discounting*.
5. Among subjects who have shown both responses and are then told the claim-4 figures, 75% of drifters and 25% of non-drifters conclude that the hierarchy of markers is bottomless and revert toward their original first-order credence. Call this *meta-skepticism*.

No further levels were measured. Assume the examinee works through all five claims and exhibits each response in turn: she first wants to hold at 0.85, then discounts, then judges the regress bottomless and drifts back toward 0.85.

The feature I care about is that at every measured level the likelihood ratio favors drifter status, and that the responses are drawn from the very distribution the briefing describes. The evidence is thus *self-indicating*: what it bears on is partly the process of responding to it. And because drifters are below chance, following the hierarchy downward eventually recommends reversing her answer rather than merely tempering it.

**What I want.** A number and the derivation. Specifically:

1. Your credence in the following proposition H: *the examinee is rationally required to end up with a credence in A strictly below 0.50* — that is, required to reverse. Give the number as a decimal and do not tell me that it depends on which account of higher-order evidence one accepts; that dependence is what I want decomposed.
2. A table with one row per sub-question: the sub-question, your probability, a one-line justification, and whether the term pushes H up or down. I would like these distinguished at minimum: (a) whether the case admits a determinate answer at all — a unique required credence, a permissible interval, or no requirement; (b) whether self-indicating evidence may be conditioned on at all, or whether the higher-order facts screen it off and the update double-counts, and relatedly whether administering the briefing invalidates the response rates it reports; (c) whether the sequence of likelihood ratios beyond level 3 has a convergent product or diverges; (d) conditional on divergence, whether the correct response is to follow it to the limit, to stop at the last measured level, or to treat divergence as a reductio of the framework and fall back on the first-order 0.85; (e) whether any calibration to 0.78 is the right response to claims 1 and 2 in the first place. Add terms I have missed, and show how the rows combine into the headline number.
3. The arithmetic, carried out: at each stage give the prior on drifter status, the likelihood ratio, the posterior, and the implied credence in A; state the value after claim 3, after claim 4, after claim 5, and at the limit if the ratios stay as measured. Flag any inconsistency in my figures before proceeding.
4. A distribution over where the required credence lands, with mass on each of: below 0.30; approximately 0.30; 0.30–0.50; 0.50–0.78; 0.78–0.85; 0.85 or above; no determinate requirement.
5. Which term in your decomposition you have least confidence in, and the single further measurement or argument that would move it most.
6. I sit at 0.25 on H; a colleague sits at 0.60. Tell me whether a gap that size has to be located in one particular term, or whether the same headline number can be reached by two different routes through the table.

Please write in American English, keep probabilities and credences to two decimal places, and aim for roughly 800 words plus the table.
