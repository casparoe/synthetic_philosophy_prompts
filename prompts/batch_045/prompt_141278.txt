Hey — hoping you can help with a methods thing that's been chewing at me for months. I'm 4th year, working on a failed 1849 separatist rising in a Habsburg borderland, and basically my entire source base is either the winning side's administrative paperwork (garrison reports, interrogation transcripts, the pacification commission's published findings) or exile memoirs written ten to thirty years later by guys who lost. So the old line about history being written by the winners isn't a slogan in my case, it's an archival fact.

What I want is to build that into the method rather than just apologise for it in the intro. The scheme I've been playing with: code each source for how close its author sat to the winning side (0 to 1), then when I'm aggregating claims about what actually happened, weight that source by (1 − λ·v). λ is a global "victory penalty" — bigger λ, less trust in winners.

Two problems.

First, I can't work out how to calibrate λ. Every way I think of checking it — hold out some claims, see which λ best predicts the ones I'm already confident about, or tune it against what we know from a later period — presupposes I already know what happened, which is exactly the thing the weighting was supposed to get me. Is there a standard trick for this? Something in formal epistemology or the quantitative-history people?

Second, and this is probably where I'm overthinking: my supervisor keeps saying the whole framing is confused but won't say more than "read around a bit." My worry is that if I'm honest that every account is shaped by who had the power to publish it, I've given up on there being any fact of the matter at all, and the only defensible chapter left is one about how the winning side's narrative got manufactured — not about what happened. That feels right to me (I definately don't want to be the naive positivist in the seminar), but it also feels like a cop-out: the exile memoirs are obviously self-serving too, and if I discount the winners by λ and the losers not at all, I've just decided in advance that the losers were right. Meanwhile everyone in the department keeps bringing up the Confederate memory stuff as the counterexample — losers who basically won the historiography — and I don't have a good answer to it.

So: how do I set λ, and is the relativist version actually the honest position here, or am I missing a third option? Don't just hand me a reading list, I want the argument.
