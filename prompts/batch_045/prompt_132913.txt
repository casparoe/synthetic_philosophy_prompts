I'm writing something on the epistemology of markets and I want the spec before I read any more of the literature: what would a theory of price authority actually have to get right? The question is deliberately narrow — when is an outsider (a journalist, a central banker, a forecaster who has read the same public headlines as everyone else) warranted in deferring to a market price over their own read of the evidence? Not a theory of price formation. A theory of warranted deference.

Give me the requirements list, each with the case that motivates it. The cases I want handled include:

- the annoying baseline: the standard no-trade result says common priors plus common knowledge of posteriors leave no room for information-driven trade, yet real markets trade constantly — so the theory has to say where the trade comes from and what that does to informativeness.
- the Grossman-Stiglitz-flavored tension: if the price fully reveals what dispersed traders know, no one pays to acquire information, so it can't fully reveal. Say plainly whether that pair is unsatisfiable and what has to give.
- prices that move for non-epistemic reasons (a margin call, a leverage constraint, one big player unwinding) — the price is as if the market believes p while nobody believes p.
- the same number with no volume behind it versus the same number after heavy two-sided trade.
- traders who aren't disagreeing about likelihood at all but about what would have to happen for the contract to pay out.
- reflexive markets, where the price feeds into the thing it prices (an internal company market on a launch date, odds that move donations or turnout).
- selection: only certain people trade, and some of them are hedging rather than saying anything.
- and my favorite: deference from the outside without knowing who is in the pool or why they trade — if you need to know that, it isn't deference to the market, it's just deference to experts.

For each, say whether it's a genuine desideratum or a prejudice of one camp. Flag any pair you think can't be jointly satisfied, and end with one line on which requirement excludes the most popular stories about why markets are smart. Skip the theory-by-theory scorecard, I'll do that myself — I want the criteria.
