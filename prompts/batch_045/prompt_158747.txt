My partner and I start our first IVF cycle in a couple weeks, we just had the talk about what happens to embryos we don't transfer, and ever since I've been stuck on this: I don't think I could have come from a different sperm and egg. If the clinic had grabbed a different sperm, that would have been my brother, not me — and so the embryos we end up not using aren't a child of ours we chose not to give a chance to, they're possible people who never existed.

But I've patched the view: I don't think the gametes are what matter. I think what's necessary is the particular conception event — the individual that came into existence then — which is why identical twins (same sperm, same egg) are two people rather than one person with two bodies.

So tell me what would break this patched version. The cases I keep circling: monozygotic twinning, where if one zygote becomes two I'm not sure whether either is the thing that existed before the split, or whether that even has an answer (and whether it matters if the split happens early vs late); embryo fusion/chimerism; the lucky-recombination case where a different sperm happens to carry the same DNA; SCNT-style cloning, and the weirder variant where someone is grown from cells that were once part of me. Which of these are real defeaters and which are just awkward, and is there anything I'm not thinking of?

I'd also want to know which of these a defender of the standard version would themselves concede as counting against them versus wave off as intuition-mongering, whether the view needs a general story about persistence that it doesn't have, whether my patch quietly collapses it into some rival view, and whether it's been qualified into unfalsifiability. Twinning and chimerism are what I'd personally find most persuasive — tell me if I'm overweighting them.
