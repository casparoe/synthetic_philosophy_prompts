Here's the case. Tesfaye does water testing for a small town. Every morning he dips a strip in the supply; it goes blue exactly when contamination is above the legal limit, and it's never been wrong about anything it reacts to. There's also a toxin, call it X, that the strip doesn't react to at all. X could only get in if somebody hooked a hose from the waste line to the intake overnight and took it away again, so by breakfast there's nothing to see and no marks on anything. He also gets a weekly plumbing manifest from the regional office saying the waste line was capped and welded three years ago — but that manifest is printed from a database nobody updates, so it would say capped even if the cap had been cut off. Strip's clean, manifest says capped, Tesfaye believes there's no X in the water, and he has to sign a county form saying exactly that.

On your view I take it he knows the water is fine: the nearest worlds where it isn't fine are ordinary bad mornings where the strip goes blue and he doesn't believe it, while the sabotage worlds are way further out. But he doesn't know there's no X, because X can only get in via the overnight hose job, so those sabotage worlds ARE the nearest X worlds, and in them the strip stays clean, the manifest still says capped, and he signs. Fine. That's your closure bullet and I'm granting you the vat thing, I don't care about vats. Here's what I couldn't finish.

Tesfaye has two independent lines of evidence, and no matter how many more you add — a second strip, a different database, someone driving out to look at the valve — as long as each one on its own would look the same in the sabotage worlds, your theory says he still doesn't know. That seems insane: stacking independent evidence is exactly how you get from unsure to sure, and tracking can't add evidence up at all, it just checks each method one at a time against the same fixed counterfactual world. And the second thing: if the method is what does the work, can't he just adopt some gerrymandered method — believe there's no X only if the strip is clean and the manifest says capped and there are no bolt cutters in the shed — and thereby know, even though nobody would ever use it? And if he believes it two ways at once and one of them is bad, which one wins? Your quantifier over methods has to go one way or the other and both ways look bad to me.
