Here is my opening, and it's Priya's move, which I think is a genuine problem for you. Consider a community that shares all your sources — same journals, same papers, same experts, properly cited — but has a stable practice of re-describing unwelcome findings so they never count against the view ('that's only a surrogate endpoint', 'that's a subgroup effect', 'that's a short-run marker'), with no general rule anyone could pin down in advance and no source ever discredited or called corrupt. Nothing is missing from their information environment and nothing has been taken out of circulation, yet the evidence-proofing is total. So the two tests you lean on come apart: by the behavioural test — what happens when you show them the other side's evidence — this is an echo chamber, since exposure does nothing; by the mechanism — trust manipulation, insinuation against sources, the closed credibility loop — it is a bubble, since nobody has been taught to distrust anybody. And if your answer is that the re-describing really is distrust in disguise, then I think you have made 'echo chamber' unfalsifiable: any community that is immune to evidence counts as one, and the mechanism drops out entirely. Is it a third box, is your taxonomy mis-sorting it, or is the mechanism claim not doing the work you thought?
