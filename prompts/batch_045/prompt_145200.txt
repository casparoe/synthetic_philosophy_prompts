I lead a four-person data-viz team at a national health department. We push out maybe thirty graphics a month — leaflets for clinics, posters, social cards, a couple of interactive dashboards — for a public with patchy literacy and three official languages, and lately also audio versions for people who get their information by phone message. Sign-off is our comms director plus whichever epidemiologist is on rotation, we get about half an hour of thinking time per graphic, and nobody here has any philosophy background.

I've been reading about Otto Neurath and the ISOTYPE picture language he built in Vienna in the 1920s and 30s, and it keeps feeling like it was wrote for exactly our problem: show quantity by repeating a same-sized symbol, never by making the symbol bigger; avoid perspective, because it wrecks comparison; symbols are conventions people have to be taught, not naturally obvious; and pictures are a helping language, never a replacement for words, because a picture can't really carry negation, conditionals, or other logical relations. Plus the bit I find strangest: that a lot of our basic categories — unemployment, the working class, maybe even something as plain as dog — are irreducibly fuzzy clumps that you can draw but can't pin down exactly, and that a picture language should own that fuzziness instead of pretending to be precise.

Can you turn that into an actual pass/fail procedure we could run on a draft? Questions in the order they'd come up, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where it hard-stops with a no, and where it has to hand the call back to a person because it's a values question rather than a design question. Then run it on three things we're arguing about right now: one, a district map of maternal deaths with circles sized by rate and coloured on a red ramp; two, our flagship vaccination dashboard, where the user can rescale the axis and filter by region themselves; three, a one-page leaflet explaining who qualifies for free care — you qualify if you're pregnant, or over 65, or you have a chronic condition — which we really wanted to make picture-first, because the words are the dificult part for our readers. Give me the unwelcome answers too, not a compromise.

Also be clear about which rules are really Neurath's and which are you stretching him to cover screens, interactivity and audio, say where the procedure will misfire on our kind of work, and tell me how confident you are in each piece — and single out the thing you're least sure of, since I can't check the sources myself.
