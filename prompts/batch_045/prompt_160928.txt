I teach a graduate seminar in environmental ethics, and I want a decision tree that takes a student from a single starting commitment — that suffering in wild animals matters morally, and matters on a par with comparable suffering in humans — to a named position on a concrete policy question: whether to permit, or actively to pursue, the conversion of a particular area of semi-natural habitat. Examples to pin the cases to: draining a lowland wetland for housing, letting a marginal upland farm revert to scrub, replanting an estate with conifers and culling the deer, clearing an island of invasive rats.

Each branch node should be one claim a student can take a view on without knowing where it leads, phrased around a case rather than a label. The splits I want the tree built from, at minimum: whether net welfare in the intact habitat is positive or negative; whether the currency is total welfare across all the animals that will ever live there or welfare per animal per year, so that a shift from a few long-lived specialists towards many short-lived, high-fecundity generalists can itself be the harm; whether the animals whose existence the conversion prevents can count as benefited at all, given that the choice also settles who exists; whether the deaths and displacements imposed on the animals already there must be weighed against the births prevented; whether the comparison class is the habitat as it stands, a successfully restored version of it, or a densely settled one; whether an ecosystem has welfare-relevant standing beyond the sentient individuals composing it; and whether how little we know about wild animal welfare puts a burden of proof on whoever proposes to convert.

Give me ten to twelve leaves, each with the name of the position — 'unnamed' if nobody appears to hold it — and one sentence naming the commitment its holder would least like to have to defend. The leaves must include a view that converts habitat in order to prevent future suffering, its opposite which resists conversion because clearance favours short-lived generalists, a land-sparing variant that concentrates production to release land elsewhere, a view on which restoration or rewilding is the harmful option, a holist view, and a view that reduces future suffering by fertility control rather than clearance. Finally, list the combinations of answers your tree allows that no position occupies, say of each whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any two leaves the tree cannot in the end separate.
