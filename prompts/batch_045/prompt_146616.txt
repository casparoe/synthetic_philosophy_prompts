I'm writing a chapter on the reliability of animal signals, and I keep finding that the handicap principle is used to mean at least three different things, so before I assess any contender I want the specification. Write the desiderata a theory of honest signalling must meet to be adequate for one purpose: telling a field biologist what to measure when she finds a signal she doesn't understand and wants to know whether to go looking for costs at all. For each requirement give the case behind it — the phenomena it must explain (why signals track quality or need instead of being bluffed; why some perfectly reliable signals are cheap; why some expensive signals carry no information; why receivers respond to displays that merely exploit their existing preferences; why signals that look wasteful persist), the distinctions it must not collapse (signal versus cue versus index versus manipulation; production cost versus differential cost versus receiver-imposed cost; signals of durable quality versus signals of current need; reliability versus costliness), and any game-theoretic or empirical result it cannot contradict. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the preferences of one camp, and say which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidate theories.

Then run the list against four hypothetical cases I want to use in the chapter, chosen so that different ways of spelling out the desiderata should come apart:

1. Borrowed cost: males build a display structure out of material they must steal from rivals, so the ornament is reliable as a indicator of fighting ability, but the cost is borne by the losers rather than by the signaller. Must cost fall on the signaller, or is it enough that there is no cheap way to get the signal?
2. Deferred cost: the display is cheap to produce, but individuals who display while in poor condition are later attacked by receivers, so the cost arrives after the benefit and only for low-quality signallers.
3. Aligned interests: a call whose pitch is fixed mechanically by body size, honest, essentially free to produce, and in a context where receiver and signaller both gain from accuracy.
4. Costly deception: an expensive display that works by hijacking the receiver's pre-existing sensory bias and carries no information whatsoever about the signaller.

If some subset of your desiderata cannot be satisfied jointly — if requiring signaller-borne cost rules out the aligned-interest case while dropping it lets the manipulation case in — say so, say which requirement you would give up and why, and give each of the main approaches (strategic handicap models, index or unfakeability accounts, receiver-psychology and sensory-exploitation accounts, cost-free honest signalling under aligned interests) one line against the list. Commit: rank the desiderata, state the one you would sacrifice, and be explicit about which of your claims are conceptual and which would actually be settled by data.
