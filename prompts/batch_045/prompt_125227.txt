I work in quantitative policy evaluation and read philosophy of social science on the side, and there is a configuration I keep running into that I have no word for.

Two cases.

(1) A model of a bank run. Each depositor withdraws because she expects the bank to fail, and she expects the bank to fail because she expects the other depositors to withdraw. Written out in full, the individual-level story is complete: the event is nothing but individual actions and the expectations behind them. But those expectations have the collective event as their content. You cannot specify the individual-level facts without already deploying the collective-level vocabulary — that the bank is failing, that everyone is pulling out — and you cannot fix which collective event is in question without the individual-level facts. Neither level is prior; each is statable only in terms of the other. What I keep noticing is that nobody in the individualism-versus-holism literature seems to count this as a defect. It does not read as circular, because the borrowed material sits inside the content of what the individuals believe rather than among the premises of the explanation.

(2) The firm has become risk-averse since the merger. Pressed, no member is risk-averse: the traders are as aggressive as ever, the risk committee follows the same procedure, the new CEO takes large bets. Distribute the property across the members and it evaporates; yet the claim about the firm is plainly true and plainly about something. Now go the other direction. State every individual's dispositions and relations completely, and you still appear to need to be told whether they compose into a risk-averse firm — in the way you would need to be told whether this arrangement of parts is a pump.

What I take to be shared: in both cases the individual-level and the collective-level descriptions depend on each other, and the dependence is not a causal loop in time. I am not claiming that next year's run causes this year's expectations. It is closer to a dependence in what can so much as be specified at one level without the other. And in both cases the two standing methodological programs each pick one direction as the respectable one — individualism says explain the collective by the individuals, holism says the collective is irreducible — and neither seems to have a category for the case where the direction of dependence is not well-defined at all.

I have been told this is just supervenience, and separately that it is just the demand for microfoundations. I do not think either fits. Supervenience as I understand it is a one-directional claim about what can change without what changing; the microfoundations demand is an instruction to rebuild the macro out of the micro, and it is silent about what happens when the rebuilding quietly imports the macro vocabulary. Self-fulfilling prophecy and feedback loops are near misses as well, but they are causal and temporal, and what I am describing is not.

So: does this configuration have a name of its own — the two-way dependence of levels, and the way it goes unnoticed because it hides either inside the content of what individuals believe or inside what counts as a property of the whole? If it does, I do not want only the label. I want the carving: its exact boundaries, the neighboring notions it is not (supervenience, emergence in its weak and strong varieties, non-aggregativity, constitutive as opposed to causal explanation, downward causation, and whatever else sits in that neighborhood), and an account of where a case like (1) and a case like (2) come apart, if they do. I am genuinely unsure whether these are one phenomenon or two that I have lumped together, and I would rather be told they are two than given a tidy answer. If there is no established concept, say so plainly, coin a name, and give me a definition together with the boundary cases that would decide membership — including what would count as evidence against treating (1) and (2) as instances of one thing.

Please do not give me citations, references, or a reading list; I can find the literature once I know what to look for. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — the name, or the verdict that there is none — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and without restating my question back to me. Say how confident you are in each of your main claims and single out the one you are least sure of. American English, please.
