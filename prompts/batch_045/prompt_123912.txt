I'm apartment hunting in a city I've just moved to, with about a week left before I have to be out of my current place. Because I don't know the market here and I didn't trust myself to compare twenty apartments on vibes, I made myself a rule before I started: I wrote down four conditions — rent under a certain amount, commute under thirty-five minutes, no shared bathroom, and a street that isn't on a bus route — and I decided in advance to take the first place that met all four and stop looking.

Twelve days ago I saw a place that met all four. I didn't take it. I told myself I'd keep the appointment I'd already booked for the next day, "just to see," and then that place is gone now, and of the nine I've seen since, none has met all four. I'm not panicked, but I've been going in circles on why I did that, and the more I think about it the more it seems like there are three separate problems tangled together that I can't pull apart.

The first is whether refusing the place was just a mistake. My roommate says it obviously was: I'd already decided what would be acceptable, I found something acceptable, and everything after that was me paying real money in rent overlap, travel and evenings to find out whether something nicer existed — information that was worth nothing once I'd committed to the rule. My brother says the opposite: a floor is not a ceiling, and a policy that makes it irrational to ever accept anything *better* than your minimum isn't a sensible policy at all, it's a guarantee that you live at your minimum forever. I can tell their disagreement has something to do with whether the rule is supposed to get me the best outcome in this one search or to be the kind of rule that, used on every decision like this one for the rest of my life, leaves me best off overall. But I can't tell whether that's one question with two ways of answering it or two different questions that happen to sound alike, and I can't tell which one I should be asking.

The second is the thing I actually keep thinking about. While I was looking I found out that I care about something that wasn't on my list: whether a place gets direct sun in the morning. I had no way to know that in advance — I learned it by standing in rooms at 9 a.m. Now I don't know what to do with it. If I add it to the list, I'm rewriting the standard in the middle of the search, on the basis of having seen things, which feels like moving the goalposts to justify whatever I happen to want. If I don't add it, I'm obeying a standard written by a person who knew strictly less than I know now — that person being me, three weeks ago. Both options feel like a kind of cheating, and I'd like to know which one is.

The third is that I've noticed my judgment of what counts as reasonable drifts depending on what I've seen most recently, and not in a way I control: after a run of overpriced, grim places everything starts to look like a bargain, and right after I see one genuinely good place everything looks unacceptable for a day or two. My brother's other objection — that choosing the bar is exactly as hard as choosing the apartment, and if I knew enough to set the bar well I wouldn't need to search at all — makes this worse, because it suggests the drift isn't a bug I can correct for but the whole thing eating itself.

So: are these one problem or three? Which of them is the one that's actually costing me anything? And concretely, what should I do with the week I have left — and how should I set up decisions like this one next time, given that I'll always be choosing the standard before I know what there is to choose between? I'd rather have your actual reasoning, including where you think my framing is confused, than reassurance.
