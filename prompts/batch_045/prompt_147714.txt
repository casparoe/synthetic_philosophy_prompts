ok so im writing up where ive landed on freedom for a political theory reading group and I want someone to poke at it before I present. Here's the set, more or less in my own words:

1. Freedom is one thing, and its basically negative: im unfree to the extent that other people block, obstruct, or coercively threaten me. Lacking the means or the ability to do something is a misfortune, not a kind of unfreedom — the person who cant afford a holiday isnt thereby unfree. I'd be sorry to give this up, because once sheer inability counts as unfreedom then everything becomes a freedom issue and the concept stops doing any work.

2. But the slave with the kindly master who never interferes, and the worker who can be sacked on a whim with no recourse, are unfree too. So being subject to somebody elses arbitrary power is itself a form of unfreedom, even when the power is never exercised. This is the republican bit and I dont want to lose it either.

3. And in the same spirit: I dont want to say that living under law makes people less free than they'd be in a state of nature. I want to say the citizen of a decent, contestable legal order is free, not less free but compensated by other goodies. Being forbidden a huge number of things you'd otherwise be able to do isnt automatically a loss of freedom, if the rules track your interests in the right way and you have some say in them.

4. Internal obstacles count as well. The severe addict and the person with a paralysing phobia are unfree in the sense I care about, even though nobody is stopping them and even where they get what they want.

5. But I absolutely reject the inner-citadel move: I dont want to say that the prisoner who has made peace with his cell, or the person whose desires have quietly shrunk to fit her options, is free. Freedom isnt about whether your wants fit your situation, and I dont want any real-will or higher-order-desire machinery in the account to make that come out.

6. Methodologically: I want to compare amounts of freedom, not just say who is and isnt free. Society A is freer than society B; policy X increases total freedom more than policy Y does; and I want that to be a real quantity I can trade off against other political goods rather than a slogan.

Two cases I keep testing myself on, and I dont love my own answers to either:

- My partners hours are set by a scheduling system at work. It can cut her to almost no shifts with a days notice, there is no human in the loop, nobody to appeal to, and its not anybody's will doing this — its just a system responding to predicted demand. Nothing she wants to do is blocked as such, and there is no one whose power over her could be called arbitrary or non-arbitrary. I still want to say she is unfree. But I cant see which of 1-5 is meant to deliver that verdict, or how much unfreedom it is for the purposes of 6.

- Two benefit systems. System A: entitlement set by statute, paid automatically, no official has discretion to withdraw it, but the conditions attached are invasive — document your job search, accept any offer, report in constantly. System B: a caseworker with very wide formal discretion over your payments, but in practice generous, almost nobody ever gets cut, and no conditions on how you live. My gut is that B leaves people less free than A does, even though B constrains their behaviour less and they end up with more of what they want — and then I also want to say, for the purposes of 6, how much less free. Those two wants feel like they pull in different directions but I cant say why.

So: audit this for me. Which of 1-6 are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and whats the cheapest way back to consistency? Please rank the tensions by severity instead of telling me all six are equally shaky, and for each one say definately what would have to give and what giving it up costs me elsewhere in the set. Also tell me whether there's one revision that cleans up the most, and whether either of my two cases is doing something my stated commitments actually forbid. I'd rather be told one of my favourite bits has to go than get a diplomatic list of pros and cons.
