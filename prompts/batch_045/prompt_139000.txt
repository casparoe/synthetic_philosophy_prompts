Some background so you know the angle: forty years in condensed matter physics, retired now, reading philosophy in the evenings mostly because my grandson talked me into an MMO and then got properly upset when another player scammed him out of a rare item and destroyed it. He was upset in a way I would call grief. My first reaction was that its a database row; my second reaction was that this is exactly the move I would have laughed at in my own department. Nobody there thought phonons, or holes in a semiconductor, were unreal because you cant hold one, and a hole is literally an absence. So I dont want the "its only code" move and I dont want the opposite move either, where everything the engine renders is as real as my kitchen table. What I want is to know what a theory of virtual objects and virtual harms would have to get right before I spend time on any of the ones already on offer.

Fix the purpose first, since I take it adequacy is relative to the job. Assume the job is decision guidance, not winning a metaphysics seminar. Three concretely: a platform moderator with no philosophical training and incomplete information has to decide what earns a permanent ban and what is merely rude; somebody has to decide when the police or the courts should get involved rather than the game's own admins; and somebody has to decide whether a dead player's inventory passes to their heirs. Write for those three users.

Then give me the requirements list, with the case or consideration behind each one. Here are the bench cases I want any candidate theory to be able to handle, and I would rather you add a couple I have not thought of than skip mine: a unique item destroyed versus one of a hundred thousand identical copies destroyed; a duplication bug followed by a rollback from backup, where the thing I earned in the window is gone and a copy survives, so which one was mine; a castle built for four hundred hours using a client side mod only one player can ever see, which then gets banned; a leftover database row from a removed quest that no rendering code displays to anyone; a player permanently banned for something unrelated whose items are still on the server but unreachable, compared with an item the developers deleted and that a fan run private server restored from old backups; an NPC shopkeeper with persistent memory of individual players that gets patched out, and players hold a funeral for it, compared with the same funeral for a bot that only seemed to remember them; unwanted haptic contact in a headset with no physical touching at all, leaving the target nauseated and having flashbacks for weeks, compared with the same action against a flat screen avatar where the target laughs it off; an item earned in one game sold on a grey market for real money that then buys something in a different game; a child who runs up an enormous bill on a parent's card, and a spouse whose savings went into virtual land; a player who consents in advance to their avatar's destruction as part of an in game ritual, and a player who destroys their own second character; and finally a dupe bug nobody ever notices that quietly inflates an economy and makes things slightly worse for thousands of people, with no identifiable victim and nobody upset at all.

Out of those I can half see some requirements forming, and I want you to sort them into the ones that are genuine desiderata and the ones that are just the prejudices of one camp. Roughly: the theory has to separate the question whether a virtual object exists from the question how much it matters, since those keep getting run together in the forum arguments I have been reading; it has to give identity and persistence conditions for virtual objects rather than falling back on how attached anyone feels, because in my old field you did not get to posit an entity without saying what made it the same entity over time and across a copy; it has to say what stops the slide from "virtual objects are patterns, and patterns are multiply realizable" to everything everywhere being a virtual object, without just answering "intentional object" and stopping; it has to allow that a player can be wronged who does not care, and that a player who is devastated can be over reacting; it has to distinguish the wrong of breaking the game's rules from the wrong that would be there even if the game's rules permitted it, and tell a moderator how to tell the two apart in a case where the game's own norms have gone rotten; it has to handle harms to third parties who never log on; and it has to say what the difference is between an item that was deleted and an item that was announced in a developer blog and never implemented, since I think something turns on that and I cant say what. My own guess is that the requirement that virtual objects be mind independent, and the requirement that every harm be a harm to a person, are both camp prejudices rather than desiderata, but argue me out of it if I am wrong.

I also want two structural results out of the list. First, tell me if some subset of the desiderata cannot be jointly satisfied, because if so the list itself is the interesting finding and I would rather know that now. The pair I keep circling is that an object's existence should not depend on anyone's attitudes or on what the code currently displays, and yet a server being switched off, or a backup being restored, does seem to settle whether the thing exists and which one it is. Second, tell me which single requirement knocks out the largest number of otherwise plausible theories, and whether that requirement is one I should be suspicious of for that reason.

Where I most want you to be honest: say what would change your mind. Which of your requirements would you drop first under pressure, what a real argument for dropping it would look like rather than a rhetorical one, where your judgment that two desiderata are jointly unsatisfiable is most likely to be a mistake or to depend on a case being cleaner than it is, and whether there is anywhere that I have carried a habit of thinking over from physics that does not transfer to this material.

If there is room at the end, three or four lines on how the two stock positions do against your list is fine, but the list is the deliverable and I do not want the stock positions to shape it. And please dont point me at papers or tell me what to read: I have no access to a university library from here, so a reading list is useless to me and I only want the arguments. A numbered list or a table, whichever is less work.
