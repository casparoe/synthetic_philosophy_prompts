Write me a job ad, 400-500 words, posted by a research institute that has decided to take the inevitability thesis completely seriously. It's hiring a Discoverer (Grade 3, Interchangeable) for a result the search commitee is confident at least three other labs will hit within the year, and the ad explains, deadpan and in the language of a real job posting, why the person who actually does the work won't get credit for it: pay is set by a counterfactual replaceability score (how quickly someone else would have got there), named credit is allocated by lottery among everyone who came within six months of the result, and there's a clause covering what happens if it turns out nobody else was really close. The jokes have to come from the philosophy itself: if the time was ripe then the result was arriving anyway, so singling out one person looks arbitrary and undeserved — yet the institute still needs somebody to do the work, and needs that somebody to believe they matter. Let that contradiction carry the piece; don't resolve it and don't wink. Include a benefits section and one proud quote from the HR officer who designed the scheme, and leave a reader actually feeling the question of whether it matters who made a discovery.
