I teach a seminar on moral progress, and I want to use one sentence as a first-day precisification exercise: "the arc of the moral universe is long, but it bends toward justice." I met it doing real work at a department meeting last month — a colleague in political theory used it to close an argument about whether we should sign a statement backing a student occupation, and she drew two conclusions from it: that the students' demands will look obviously just in fifty years, and that this future verdict is itself a reason to back them now. My students will want a menu of readings, not a single gloss, so that's what I need.

Lay out the distinct precise theses the sentence could be stating, ordered roughly from trivial to wild, with a verdict on each. A few things I want kept apart rather than blurred: whether "bends" is a claim about what people come to believe, about how institutions get arranged, or about the facts of the matter; whether the trend is supposed to be counterfactually robust or just the path we happen to have lived; whose conception of justice is doing the work; and whether the thing is descriptive at all or is really a fiduciary, self-fulfilling commitment — true, if at all, partly because people act as if it were.

Two of my own hobby-horses belong on the menu too, since I'll foist them on the students anyway. Call the first retrospective ratification: being on the right side of history isn't a property a movement has at the time but one partly constituted by later generations ratifying it, so the verdict can become true without ever having been knowable in advance. Call the second narrative survivorship: the episodes we tell stories about are selected for having come out well, so a shape read off the highlights needn't be a trend in the data at all.

For each reading, say what it commits you to, whether its plausible, and — the part I actually care about — whether it delivers her first conclusion, her second, both, or neither; and flag any reading on which there's been genuine moral improvement but no tendency whatsoever.
