Heres the modern bit. Some logicians today use four truth values instead of two: a sentence can be true only, false only, both, or neither — both for inconsistent information, neither for gaps. You pick which values are the good ones, the designated ones, and that choice gives you a consequence relation.

My reading — call it the two-tier reading — is that the four corners are a semantics (the four values), and your dismissal of all four is not a fifth value but an operator sitting on top of them: a rejection operator. Emptiness is that operator, not a property of things. Its why emptiness of emptiness is harmless on my account: the operator applied to itself just iterates, it doesn't collapse.

Here's my opening move, and I want you to hit back hard. If emptiness is a rejection operator rather than a value, then your line about emptiness being the relinquishing of all views, and anyone who takes emptiness itself as a view being incurable, stops being a paradox and becomes a rule for using the operator — and a rule isn't the kind of thing that can be refuted, only obeyed or not. So when you say my two-tier reading is itself a view, you aren't refuting me, you're telling me I'm not playing your game. Convince me that playing it is worth more than not playing it.
