I'm a third-year undergraduate in a combined philosophy-and-physics seminar on the foundations of statistical mechanics, and I'm writing a 1,500-word response paper on Maxwell's demon. In lecture we did the Szilard engine and then Landauer's principle, and the conclusion was presented to us as settled: the demon acquires one bit per measurement and can parlay it into roughly kT ln 2 of work, but it must eventually clear its memory, and resetting one bit dumps at least kT ln 2 of heat into the environment, so the books balance and the second law is safe.

What I can't see is how that answer survives a demon that simply never erases. I've built two variants in my head and I'd like help with the argument itself, not with sources — I have no library access until Monday, so please don't point me at papers, bibliographies or reading lists; I need the conceptual bookkeeping.

Variant one: a sealed container of gas at temperature T, divided by a partition with a shutter the demon can open for free. Before the experiment begins, someone outside the container hands the demon a memory register of M bits, every bit in a known standard state (all zero, say). Each cycle the demon opens the shutter, records which side the molecule is on, and lets the gas expand against a piston so as to draw out kT ln 2 of work. It never erases. After M cycles the register is full — a string that is perfectly correlated with the gas's history but, to anyone who doesn't know the gas, looks random — and the demon has extracted M kT ln 2 of work while exporting no heat anywhere. Since nothing in the setup caps M, the yield looks unbounded with no compensating entropy dumped. So the cost, if there is one, seems to sit in preparing blank memory, i.e. in a low-entropy resource supplied from outside.

Variant two: instead of erasing, the demon recycles reversibly — it applies a reversible scrambling to its register, spreading each record into correlations spread across many bits so that nothing locally readable remains. The string's Shannon entropy never drops and no heat leaves the demon. Yet the demon still carries a correlation with the gas; what it has lost is something addressable to act on. I want to know whether this helps it, and if it doesn't, exactly what runs out.

The things I'm stuck on, in rough order of how much they bother me:

1. What is the correct cycle-by-cycle accounting for the never-erasing demon, and where does the entropy actually go? I want the per-cycle and lifetime totals, not a hand-wave.

Does the erasure story do any independent explanatory work, or does it just relocate the puzzle to "a demon needs a low-entropy resource," which is more or less what the second law already asserted? If it's the latter, is the information-theoretic exorcism circular — and is there a non-circular reading on which the bit-counting is genuinely doing something new?
3. Must the measurement itself cost anything, or can measurement be reversible in principle, so that the entire thermodynamic bill falls on preparing or resetting the register rather than on acquiring the information?
4. In variant two, does the distinction between merely being correlated with the gas and holding usable, addressable information carry any thermodynamic weight, or is it a red herring?
