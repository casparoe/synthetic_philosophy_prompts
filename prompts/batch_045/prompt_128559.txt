Compose a case to the following specification. I need it for a seminar on causation by omission: I want to press a difference-making theorist into either accepting both of two omissions as causes of a bad outcome or producing some non-normative condition that makes only one of them eligible.

Design a pair of cases, written together in at most 200 words, set in an ordinary institutional context — a hospital ward, a nursery, a plant floor, a signal box, your choice. In each, one person fails to act, and a bad outcome follows. The two cases must be alike in the counterfactual respects that bear on the outcome: the same kind of outcome, produced by the same kind of process, where in each case had the agent acted the outcome would not have occurred, and where the omission is not a case of symmetric overdetermination among several non-actors and does not work by double prevention. They must also be alike in every normative respect: no duty, role, office, rule, promise, contract, special relationship, or expectation attaches to either agent, and neither agent did anything earlier that created the danger. The cases are to differ in one respect only — say so plainly if you need more than one — and that respect must not be normative. It is what makes the omission a cause in one case and not in the other.

Then give me, under the following headings:

- **The pair** (one paragraph, both cases in matched language, no asymmetry of tone or framing doing any work).
- **Held fixed** (~100 words): the counterfactual and normative facts common to both, as a checklist I can run against the paragraph.
- **Three accounts** (~250 words): what a difference-making account, a norm-based account, and an agency account each say in each case, and why.
- **The feature** (~130 words): name the single non-normative feature doing the work, argue that it is not a duty, expectation, or difference-making fact in disguise, and say what becomes of the pair if it is deleted and nothing else changes. Commit to one feature rather than canvassing candidates.
- **The counterattack and your verdict** (~180 words): the strongest move available to the difference-making theorist — stipulation, a pragmatic or conversational explanation of the asymmetry, or just biting the bullet — and then your own verdict on whether the pair survives it. Take a side.
- **Fragility** (~60 words): where the case is most likely to break.
