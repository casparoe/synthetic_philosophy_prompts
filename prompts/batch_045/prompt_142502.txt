I'm writing a short chapter on dream skepticism, and I want to open with Zhuangzi's butterfly passage as the canonical pre-Cartesian statement of the problem. My plan is to treat it as formally the same argument as the brain-in-a-vat and simulation hypotheses: in each case my present experience would be qualitatively identical either way, so my experience gives me no ground for choosing between the alternatives, and so I do not know which is the case. On that reading the butterfly passage differs from the contemporary versions only in that Zhuangzi lacked the vocabulary to describe the substrate behind the appearances, and the one genuinely hard question is how much credence the symmetry of the evidence forces on us. Since the alternatives are, ex hypothesi, evidentially indistinguishable, shouldn't a principled writer assign them roughly equal probability and concede that we are at no better than even odds on whether we are awake right now?

Three things make me hesitate. First, the passage closes by insisting that there must still be some distinction between Zhuang Zhou and the butterfly, which looks flatly inconsistent with the claim that nothing distinguishes the two cases — so either that closing line is a dispensable afterthought, or I have misidentified the argument. Second, the vat and simulation scenarios both retain a real world and a real mechanism behind the appearances, while nothing in the butterfly passage seems to posit any such deeper layer. Third, I have been working with a taxonomy on which the butterfly case is an instance of source-doubt — doubt about which of several candidate producers of my experience is the actual one — with world-doubt, doubt about whether any external world exists at all, as a further special case of it. Is that taxonomy sound, and does the asymmetry in my second point tell against treating all three as one argument? And if it does, is any skeptical reading of the passage left over, or does it turn into a claim about something else — the transformation of things, or the status of the boundaries we draw around a self — that I should not be presenting as a contribution to the epistemology of dreaming at all?
