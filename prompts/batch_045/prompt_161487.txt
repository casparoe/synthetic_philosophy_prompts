Our federation's Constitutional Review Commission has a standing Referral Committee that decides which adopted measures must go to a binding popular referendum — a double majority of voters and of the federation's regions — rather than taking effect on the assembly's own vote. Its track record:

- Referred: a revision of the amendment formula itself, including new entrenchment thresholds.
- Referred: replacement of party-list proportional representation with single-member districts.
- Referred: term limits for members of the assembly.
- Referred: assisted dying, after a decade of legislative deadlock and three failed private-member's bills.
- Referred: ratification of a treaty transferring regulatory authority to a supranational body, despite a comfortable pro-treaty majority.
- Not referred: a cross-party settlement, negotiated over two years and carried by four-fifths of both chambers, on the two official languages of the civil service.
- Not referred: siting of a long-lived nuclear waste repository, after the host municipality consented to a negotiated compensation package.
- Not referred: a five-year suspension of a constitutional right during an emergency, which lapsed automatically although everyone expected it to be extended twice.

The new case: a package amendment negotiated across party lines and carried by four-fifths of both chambers that (i) lowers the voting age to 16 for national elections and (ii) transfers constituency-boundary drawing from the assembly to a permanent independent commission, with no sunset and no review clause. Note two wrinkles I think matter: limb (ii) removes the assembly's conflict of interest rather than exemplifying it, and limb (i) would be authorized by an electorate that the change itself redefines.

I want more than a gut verdict. Identify the two or three rules that fit these precedents equally well — for example, a rule that any change to the constitutive rules of the game needs direct popular authorization whatever the legislative consensus, versus a rule that referral is only for repairing a deficit of authority (self-dealing, deadlock, or imposition on an unconsenting minority) — and say what each predicts here, including whether the two limbs come apart and whether the package is partible. Then give your best prediction with the reasons for preferring it, name the precedent that is the closest analog, say honestly whether our record underdetermines the case, and specify the one further case that would most cleanly separate the surviving rules.
