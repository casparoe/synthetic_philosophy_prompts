write me a parody, about 600 words, of the covering letter that comes with a payslip from a firm that's definately decided to pay everyone exactly what they deserve. the setup: anything about my year that traces back to something I didn't choose — my brains, my upbringing, being the sort of person who can be bothered, the fact that nothing awful happened to me — gets priced out as unearned luck, and so does anything where I took a gamble that just happened to come off. HR work out what's left by simulating a "counterfactual me" (same genes, same childhood, median everything else) and paying me the gap between what I actually did and what that version would of done.

the comedy should come from the arithmetic being done completely straight, in deadpan HR-speak. the deeper the letter goes, the smaller the bit that survives as genuinely mine, until the firm has to bring in some flat payment to everyone so that people don't starve, and the letter is very insistent that this flat bit has nothing at all to do with merit. format it as a real letter with itemised deductions, a footnote or two, maybe a short FAQ overleaf. someone reading it should come away properly feeling the worry that merit pay, taken seriously, ends up cancelling itself — but the letter must never say that out loud.
