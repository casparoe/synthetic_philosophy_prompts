I am putting together a handout for a graduate seminar on indirect normativity, and I would like a decision tree rather than a survey: an instrument that takes a student from one starting commitment to a named position, by way of questions they can answer without knowing what the questions lead to.

The starting commitment is this: an act's deontic status is not settled by what the agent's own actual deliberation would deliver, but by what an idealised version of that deliberation would deliver. Everything downstream should branch off that claim.

Please build the tree from the logical space first — the questions, and the combinations of answers they permit — and only then locate the actual positions inside it, rather than starting from a list of familiar views and working backwards. My angle is diagnostic rather than historical, so please do not give me a reading list, a bibliography, or footnoted attributions; if a leaf corresponds to a view that has a label in the literature, attach it, but mark it as your own label wherever you are not confident about who holds it, rather than guessing.

The distinctions I most want used as branch nodes are:

1. Anchoring: whether the idealisation inherits the agent's actual aims, projects and concerns, or replaces them with a fully general standpoint.
2. Information: full information, the agent's actual evidence, or the evidence the agent could have gathered within a realistic span of deliberation.
3. Powers: whether deliberation is idealised as costless and computationally unbounded, or as bounded but cleansed of bias, distraction and weakness of will.
4. The target of the idealisation: what the idealised deliberator would choose, would advise, would want the agent to do, or would rank first — and whether these can come apart.
5. Uptake-sensitivity: whether the recommendation is allowed to respond to the fact that the agent will probably not do exactly what is recommended.
6. Modality: what the idealised deliberator would do, could do, or what it would be best to do.
7. Indirectness: whether the criterion is also the correct decision procedure for real agents, and if it is not, which of the following does the separating work — informational limits, the costs of deliberating, the indispensability of uncalculating dispositions, assurance and coordination with others, corruption or self-deception, or moral uncertainty about the criterion itself.
8. Whether the recommended procedure is vindicated by the criterion itself, or by some independent standard of practical rationality.
9. Whether self-effacement is global or only local.

You may add up to two further nodes of your own if the space has a gap I have missed.

Each diagnostic question should be phrased about a concrete case rather than about a label, and no node may ask a student to self-describe. Please draw the cases from at least these five: a physician choosing between two drugs on asymmetric evidence; a case in which deliberating about whether to trust a friend is itself what destroys the trust; a case in which the agent lacks the concepts needed to grasp the decisive information even when it is handed to her; a case in which the idealised advisor knows the agent will comply only partially; and a case in which the agent's actual desires are themselves deformed by upbringing or oppression, so that it is unclear whether the idealised version inherits them.

I want eight to ten branch nodes and eight to twelve leaves. Each leaf should carry a name (or "unnamed" if you think nobody defends it) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Please also mark the combinations of answers that no leaf occupies, with a one-clause diagnosis of whether they are incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag explicitly any pair of leaves your tree cannot really separate instead of pretending that it can. Finally, add a two-line note on how the handout is meant to be used: a student's route through the tree should be recorded as a path, not collapsed into a total score. The finished handout should be written in British English.
