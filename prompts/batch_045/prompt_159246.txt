hey — I run a small philosophy reading group and we just did a session on satisficing and bounded rationality. Half the room came away thinking it just means "settle for good enough," and I want a piece of satire for our newsletter that makes them feel where the actual difficulty is.

Write it as an official guidance bulletin, about 700 words, from a fictional government body — say the Office of Sufficient Outcomes — a few years after something like a "Decision Frugality Act" took effect: citizens may not consider more than three options before choosing anything. Format it as numbered Q&As plus one worked example, in deadpan bureaucratese, and close with one short quote from a citizen who says the Act cured him of agonising over every purchase.

The jokes have to come out of the real structure of the view, not out of generic anti-bureaucracy humour, and I want them to land these:

- aspiration levels: the Office has to tell people where to set the bar, and the bar itself has to be arrived at somehow; also the way it creeps up when it's easily met and slides down after a run of failures
- the defence that satisficing is just optimisation once you price the searching properly — so the Act might be banning nothing at all, or banning everything, depending on what you count as a cost
- the shape of the option pool: stopping at three is nearly free when the options are all middling, and a catastrophe when one of them is ten times better than the rest
- offloading: people subscribe to services that search on their behalf, and the service, dealing with millions of clients, optimises flat out — the search hasn't gone away, its moved up a level
- enforcement: catching optimisation seems to require modelling optimal search perfectly, so the Office ends up optimising in order to police people for optimising
- "good enough for whom": the Office satisfices on the public's behalf while quietly optimising its own budget and headcount

No real theorists inside the piece (dont quote Simon, no name-dropping), but someone who knows the literature should feel it got satisficing right rather than caricaturing it — procedural vs substantive rationality, the scissors point if you can make it funny. And keep it legible: a reader should never be able to tell whether the Office is wise or completely insane, and dont ever say which.
