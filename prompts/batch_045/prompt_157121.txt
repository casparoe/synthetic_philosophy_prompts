so this is my third novel and the first one where I've let a character write a poem, and now Im stuck, because the poem turns out to be doing way more work than I planned when I outlined it. Quick setup: two brothers ran a boatyard together for something like twenty years, they slowly stopped trusting eachother about money and about who was actually showing up, one of them dies, and the surviving brother asks the dead man's daughter — who writes verse, and who did exactly one semester of political science and never got over it — for "something for the service, but not about god." What she hands him is secretly a poem about the iterated prisoner's dilemma: he should be able to read it straight through as a poem about two people who couldn't stop hurting each other, and a game theorist should read it and grin. I need both levels to genuinely work, not one decorating the other.

What I have in mind is a villanelle, full 19 lines, meant to be read aloud in a room with bad acoustics, with the form load-bearing rather than decorative. The two refrains should be the two moves — one player opening, one player taking — and because the story is really about noise (a move that gets misread, then the retaliation that the other guy reads as the original sin, so that nobody can agree any more on who started it), I want each return of a refrain to come back damaged: one word dropped, swapped, or misheard, so that by the final quatrain the two lines are standing next to each other and can't quite recognise one another. The turn should be the moment the poem realises there might not be a last round — the shadow of the future — and I want that to land as grief first and as theory second, because the man she's writing it for is grieving and would stop listening otherwise. Hard rules: no jargon in the poem itself, so none of the words equilibrium, strategy, game, payoff, or tit-for-tat, and no letters for the outcomes — but a reader who knows the thing should still be able to reconstruct from the images which outcome ranks where, see why the world where both of them open up beats the world where both of them take even though it isn't where the game lands, and notice that steady cooperation only beats taking turns exploiting each other because of that second inequality (2R > T + S, on top of T > R > P > S). There's also one idea of hers I want in there, a distinction I'm sort of inventing as I go: that a grudge isn't a memory but a rule, so forgiving isn't wiping the slate clean, it's changing what you'll do next, and the history lives in the rule rather than in a running score.

My writing group says it should be a sestina instead, because sestinas are about obsession and this is a poem about obsession; I say villanelle, because the whole problem here is two lines that can't stop coming back at each other. If you think the group is right, or that some other form beats both, tell me in two or three sentences why and then write that instead — but whatever you pick, the repeating or rotating machinery has to be carrying the argument. One last thing: don't tell us whether the two brothers would ever have made it back to cooperation, but make it answerable. The closing lines should give someone who knows the theory enough about the payoffs and about how much noise there is to say either yes-if-there's-one-more-round or no-not-ever, and ideally to disagree about it afterwards.
