Next month I have to lead a forty-minute session on moral relativism for my school's philosophy club: about a dozen sophomores and juniors, most of them at their first philosophy meeting, several of whom will arrive already convinced that relativism is either obviously true, because nobody is in a position to judge another culture, or obvious nonsense, because it destroys itself. I don't need relativism explained to me; I need a plan for teaching it.

What I want is the order the ideas have to come in and why each depends on what came before, starting from the gap between people disagreeing about something and there being no fact of the matter about it. I want one example to carry the whole session, and I have a candidate I would like you to assess rather than replace: two neighbouring valleys identical in climate, crops and household structure whose inheritance norms are mirror images of each other, plus a follow-up in which both valleys agree to let a third valley arbitrate, and I ask whether either of them was already right before the arbitrator spoke. Does that case work, what must it contain, and what must it not contain? I am worried both about anything emotionally loaded enough that the room shuts down or turns the session into a political argument, and about any reading on which the two valleys merely face different circumstances and so aren't really disagreeing about morality at all.

Then the misconceptions, in the order they will actually surface, each with the point in the sequence at which it should be headed off: I expect some version of relativism just means being tolerant, of if everything is relative then nothing is really wrong anywhere, and of objective meaning agreed upon by everyone. I especially want to know where to place the reflexive worry that relativism is itself only relatively true, since that is the one students will blurt out in the first five minutes and the one that needs the most machinery in place to answer properly. Finish with one question I can put to them at the end that would show they have separated the relativist claim from its neighbours rather than learned to repeat a slogan, and with a short list of the things I should say out loud that I am deliberately not going to cover.
