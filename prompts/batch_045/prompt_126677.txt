Hey — I need a plan here, not an explanation.

I've been roped into running a 75-minute session on teleosemantics and the naturalization of content for the ML reading group at work. About fifteen engineers and researchers: everyone in the room has trained models, a couple have read some Dennett-ish pop philosophy, nobody has any philosophy of mind. My guess is they arrive in one of two moods — either representation is just whatever the features happen to correlate with, or else this is a solved non-question, since the objective comes from the data and the loss, end of story. One whiteboard, no slides, no assigned reading (we're genuinely not handing anything out, so please don't give me a reading list or point me at the canonical authors, I can find those myself). I'd much rather they leave with one idea properly installed than with a taxonomy of -isms.

So tell me how to build it for them, not what the view is.

The order, first. My instinct is that nothing lands until they see why "carries information about" can't be the whole story — every event carries information about countless things, so information is too cheap to be content — and that the move to function is a response to that failure and not a random stipulation. But I can't tell whether the normativity point (that a state can be wrong, not merely unhelpful or low-reward) has to come before the function move, as the thing function is being recruited to explain, or after it, as the payoff. Say where it goes and what breaks if it goes in the other slot.

Then the carrying example. I've got two candidates and I don't trust either. (i) A moth species that navigates by holding a fixed angle to a distant light source; a lighthouse gets built on its island; two hundred moth generations pass, which is long enough for selection to have acted, but the moths that dive into the lamp die before breeding, so nothing has been selected for lighthouse-avoidance. Is the mechanism now about the lighthouse, still about distant light, or about nothing? (ii) A gripper on a warehouse robot arm with a pressure sensor, originally trained with a reward for not dropping the parcel; three firmware changes ago a cable started rattling when the arm swings over the packing table, so the sensor now fires mostly because of that, while the system's behaviour is unchanged and still fine.

Which one carries the hour, which do I keep in my pocket, and what does the winner have to have — and more usefully, what does it have to lack? I'm worried the moth case opens a selection-history rabbit hole before they've got the problem, and that the robot case invites "well, it means whatever the engineer meant it to mean," which is the thing I need them to get past rather than start from. If you think I need a third thing instead, describe it.

Misconceptions, in the order you'd expect them and the point where each gets headed off: content is just the loss function or the training objective (or: whoever wrote the reward fixes the content); then the fallback to "okay, so it's whatever currently causes the firing," which I expect when someone raises a swampman-style case — a net that pops into existence with exactly these weights and no history at all; then "isn't that backwards causation, the future can't fix what a state means now"; then the overcorrection into "so meaning is outside the model"; and the one I'm least sure how to time, "you've just said representations have purposes and purposes aren't physical, so you've smuggled something spooky back in." Also tell me which of these I should let them build up and watch collapse on their own rather than pre-empting.

Separately: I keep toying with something I made up and call content laundering — a mechanism that gets retrained or repurposed, so an old selection history sits underneath a new training regime, and the question is whether the old content survives, gets replaced, or both hang around and fight. Is that a useful thing to put in front of this audience at all, or does it muddy the water? If it's useful, where does it go?

What should I openly refuse to explain at this level and name as a refusal? And give me one thing to have them do in the last ten minutes that would show the idea had actually landed, rather than that they'd memorised the slogan content equals selected function — ideally something applied to a case where selection history and current causal role come apart in a way we didn't talk about.
