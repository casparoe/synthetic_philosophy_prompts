I've been chewing on Frege's puzzle since my Sequences-era phase and I want to know whether I actually have a principle here or just a pile of verdicts. Going by gut: 'Hesperus is Phosphorus' is informative — I learn something about the world, not merely about words — while 'Hesperus is Hesperus' isn't. But now suppose two astronomers sit in the same room looking at the same anomalous instrument reading; each independently dubs whatever is causing it, one picking 'A' and the other 'B', and then they say to each other 'A is B'. I want to call that uninformative: nothing is learned, even though there were two separate dubbing acts and two distinct words. So its not word-distinctness doing the work. 'Vulcan is Vulcan' is also uninformative, but I don't want to call it true either — it's defective in a way 'Hesperus is Hesperus' isn't. 'Water is H2O' is informative, and unlike Hesperus/Phosphorus it apparently never stops being informative: chemists who've known it for thirty years still treat it as load-bearing. And Lois: 'Clark Kent is Superman' stays non-inert for her even after she fully believes it and has, as far as I can tell, merged the two files — she'll assert it and still get caught out by it. So the difference isn't just whether the information has been merged in storage. My tentative principle: 'a is b' is informative for a speaker S just in case S associates a and b with two distinct files, i.e. bodies of information, and the statement tells S that those files are about one and the same thing. Test that against my cases, tell me where it breaks and repair it, motivate each clause of the repaired version by the case that forced it, and be straight about which of my verdicts it can't save. Then tell me what it predicts for two cases I haven't settled: if I know A=B and B=C and have merged both pairs, is 'A is C' informative for me? And is informativeness symmetric, or can 'a is b' be informative while 'b is a' isn't? Please write it as continuous prose, no headings, bullets, bold or tables, the way you'd write an email.
