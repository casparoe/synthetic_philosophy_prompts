Im putting together a decision-tree handout for the first-year grad seminar I TA, on whether the familiar expanding-circle story is really a story of moral progress. My students all agree that slavery was bad and that animals matter more than they used to, and then disagree in ways that no survey of the positions helps with — so I want a tree, not a survey: about six branch questions, seven to nine leaves, every branch labeled by the claim that splits it, and every question phrased as something a student can answer before they know which position it leads to. Build it from the logical space first and locate the actual views in it afterwards.

Cases I want used as the diagnostics: rising concern for animals over the last two centuries; the long decline in interpersonal violence and corporal punishment; the recent surge in solicitude for distant future people; and the apparent re-hardening of attitudes toward immigrants and out-groups in rich democracies over the last twenty years.

Leaves that definately have to show up: the realist tracking view (the circle widens toward a moral truth); the nothing-mind-independent-happened view (we just changed); the pacification-not-moralisation view (state capacity, commerce, literacy did the work, so what improved was behaviour, not moral belief); and a hypocrisy-reduction leaf on which what improved is compliance with beliefs we already had. Plus two leaves Im toying with myself and want placed on the tree: (i) a budget view on which concern is roughly fixed and the circle mostly redistributes it — rising concern for animals funded by falling concern for the nearby poor — so expansion is real but isn't growth; (ii) a widening-without-status view, where we extend care to things we don't think have standing at all (rivers, a chatbot, far-future people who may never exist), so what expands is the objects of care rather than the class of beings with moral standing.

Where the logical space has empty cells, mark them and say whether the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely undefended. And flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually separate: I suspect the realist and the nothing-mind-independent-happened leaf agree on every historical case and only come apart on some counterfactual question, and I want that question written down explicitly. One page-ish if you can.
