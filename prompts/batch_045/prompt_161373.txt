hey — I'm making a one-page handout for a comparative epistemology reading group (final-year philosophy undergrads, about half have done some Indian philosophy and half only analytic epistemology), and I want a decision tree that sorts *episodes of coming to know something* into kinds of warrant, rather than a list of the pramāṇas with defintions next to them.

The idea: start from a single concrete episode and put it through maybe six yes/no questions that don't give the label away. Suggested branch questions, use or swap them as you like: does the warrant need the object itself to be in contact with a sense; does the step require a remembered exceptionless link between a sign and what it indicates; does it turn on a remembered similarity used to identify an unfamiliar object; does it require understanding a sentence from someone taken to be reliable; does it require positing an unobserved state of affairs to make an observed one intelligible; is what gets known an absence; and — this is the wrinkle I care most about — does the route stop counting as a warrant if you already knew the fact by some other route.

Cases should be a mix: the classical ones (the forest officer and the gavaya-like animal, smoke on a hill, Devadatta fat and not eating by day, the jar that isn't on the table), plus modern ones nobody in the literature had — reading the time off a phone, a smoke alarm waking you, a blood-test result in a letter, an app that says it's raining in Delhi, a satnav instruction you half understand.

Then: build the space of answer-combinations first, locate the six candidates (perception, inference, comparison, testimony, postulation, non-apprehension) in it, then locate the school packages (the four-fold Nyāya set, the two-fold Buddhist set, perception-only, the six-fold one) as regions, mark which cells nobody occupies and say in one line each whether that's because the combo is incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag the pairs your tree genuinely can't pull apart — I suspect comparison vs testimony and inference vs postulation, but tell me what you actually get. No bibliography and no citations, the group has no library access. Please write it in British English.
