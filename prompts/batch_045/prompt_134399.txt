I work in machine learning. I'm fluent in minimum-description-length and PAC-Bayes arguments, structural causal models and interventions, equivariance and symmetry groups, the bias–variance and overfitting/inductive-bias story, and out-of-distribution evaluation. I've been reading about the dispute over laws of nature between the Humean side (laws are nothing over and above the total mosaic of particular matters of fact: they are the regularities a best system of description would encode, trading simplicity against informativeness) and the necessitarian or governing side (laws do something the mosaic does not — they constrain or generate it, and they support counterfactuals and modalities that no mere description of what happens can underwrite). I want to know how much of that dispute survives being said in my language.

Please do four things.

1. Restate the Humean best-system claim as faithfully as you can using only vocabulary I already have: draws from a data-generating process, dataset, model class, description length, regularisation, compression, fit to held-out data, symmetry or equivariance constraints, identifiability.

2. Restate the necessitarian or governing claim in the same vocabulary, and note explicitly every place where you had to press one of my terms into a use it does not normally bear.

3. Give the residue report, in three plainly separated parts: what translates cleanly; what translates only with distortion (say what the distortion is); and what cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all. I especially want you to work through four pressure points where I suspect the translation breaks:

(a) The asymmetry between laws and initial conditions. For the Humean both are features of one and the same mosaic, yet only laws are supposed to support counterfactuals. In my terms this looks like the difference between a hard-coded symmetry and a pattern that merely happens to occur in the sample, or between learned weights and training data — yet in my vocabulary that distinction is pragmatically drawn and interest-relative, not metaphysical. Say whether that is a faithful rendering or a caricature.

(b) The relativity of compression. Description length is defined only relative to a coding scheme and a model class, so "the best system" threatens to be relative to a choice of language or prior. In my terms: MDL is only defined up to a model class, and a Humean who gets to choose the model class has smuggled in exactly the substantive content their account was supposed to avoid. Say what the Humean can reply, and whether the reply is available to me as a working ML person or is unavailable to both of us.

(c) Chance. Dynamical laws that assign probabilities to outcomes rather than dictating them look, in my terms, like a sampler rather than a predictor — and a sampler is a piece of machinery, not a regularity. Does the governing view simply win on chancy laws, or can the Humean reconstruct a sampler out of frequencies in the mosaic without circularity?

(d) Offline versus online fitting. The best system is standardly taken to be fitted to the complete history of the world, all of it at once; any agent actually using a law is an embedded, incremental learner facing data it has not yet seen. In my terms this is the gap between batch fitting with access to the test set and genuine out-of-distribution deployment. My suspicion is that the governing view's extra "oomph" is at bottom a claim about OOD stability that a batch-fit Humean has no need for and no way to state. Develop this as far as it will honestly go, including the version where the Humean fits the system to a partial mosaic and re-fits as data arrives.

4. Then the part I actually care about. For each substantial item of residue, classify it as (i) a blind spot in the ML vocabulary — something the statistical picture cannot see but should be able to; (ii) a genuine, irreducible posit that no amount of statistical vocabulary will reproduce; or (iii) a sign that the original claim was never as clear as it looked, and that the translation has exposed a pseudo-question. Defend each classification rather than merely labelling it. Where you answer (iii), say what each side would have to give up in order for the disagreement to become answerable at all.

One constraint: do not let my vocabulary win by assumption. It is very easy to make the Humean sound obviously correct by describing the world as "a dataset" and the laws as "a model," because that framing already builds in a split between the pattern and the thing patterned — which is arguably the entire issue. Flag every point at which the translation does this. Then say whether an equal and opposite framing is available in some vocabulary I know — a framing that makes the governing view sound obvious instead — or whether the ML vocabulary is structurally biased toward one side, and why.

Format: lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — what survives the translation, what the irreducible residue is, and which way the ML vocabulary is biased — then give sections 1 through 4. No preamble and no restatement of my question.
