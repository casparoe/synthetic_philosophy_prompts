i'm a backend-ish engineer who somehow ended up owning most of the "growth" surface at work — the cancel flow, onboarding nudges, push notification timing, some of the ranking in a recommender. no philosophy training, just a very argumentative group chat with a designer. we keep going in circles about whether what we build counts as persuasion or manipulation, and i've noticed i'm carrying around a bunch of rules i've never actually checked against each other. here they are, in my own words:

(1) The line is bypass, not outcome. Persuasion hands someone something to think with; manipulation gets around the thinking — defaults, framing, timing, catching people when they're tired. So a nudge that makes people save more money by quietly exploiting the default effect is still manipulation, even though it's good for them and they'd thank me for it.

(2) It's a property of the mechanism, not of my intentions. I can manipulate someone by accident, if my design happens to route around their deliberation and I never thought about it once.

(3) But intent does change how bad it is. Doing it on purpose and doing it by accident are not morally the same thing.

(4) Endless scroll is the cleanest case I know. Every individual swipe is something the user chose, but the thing that got deleted is the second-order moment — the point where you'd ask whether you want to keep going at all. So a chain of fully chosen micro-choices can still count as manipulation, because what got bypassed was the stopping question, not the next swipe.

(5) I also think the test for whether a given influence is OK is hypothetical endorsement: would this person, informed and thinking clearly, sign off on it being done to them. I'll admit I use this one to defend some of what I ship.

(6) For the stuff that reshapes what you want over months (the recommender), I think whether it was manipulation has to be settled by the values you had *before*, not the ones you have after — otherwise it stops being manipulation just by succeeding.

(7) Last year a friend was spiralling and I deliberately waited until she was exhausted to bring up getting help, because I knew she'd say yes. That's timing-as-bypass. I don't think it was wrong.

(8) And I think a genuinely great documentary — vivid, upsetting, obviously built to move you — is persuasion, not manipulation, even though it works on your emotions and not on your arguments.

Plus the general one: I think if something is manipulation, that's always a mark against it. It can be outweighed (see 7), but it never counts for nothing.

So: which of these actually collide, and how badly? Part of me thinks (5) and (1) are quietly incompatible and that (6) needs a clean "before" that (4) says doesn't exist, but I honestly don't know which one I'd give up, and I don't trust my own sense of which pair is the real problem.
