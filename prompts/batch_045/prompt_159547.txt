I run a dumb comedy night in my department once a term and next week's theme is the Neolithic. The room will be maybe a hundred archaeologists, three very tired paleoethnobotanists, and one guy who keeps a replica grinding slab on his desk and definately thinks of himself as the straight man. I need one joke for that crowd that actually lands, and I dont want the easy ones: no wheat-domesticated-us riff, no cultivate/culture pun, no cereal killer. Those get a pity laugh and then silence, and the silence is worse.

The thing I keep circling is what I've started calling the reversion clause. On paper, agriculture is a package you could hand back: put down the seed stock, walk off the plot, go back to foraging, get your short work day and your varied diet and your life without bosses. In practice the option expired the moment anyone took the deal, and it expired for everybody at once. Add fields and you get storable surplus; add surplus and you get a population the land cant support by foraging; add that population and you get owners, granaries, somebody counting the grain, and fences across everything you'd need to walk over. So every individual would rather be out, but exiting is only available if essentially everyone exits together, and the very thing that makes you want to exit is what makes that coordination impossible. It's an assurance game where the prize for defecting is a slightly bigger pile of barley.

Can you write me one joke — short setup, one punchline, something I could say out loud in maybe four sentences total — where the laugh turns on that structure? Concretely, I want it to turn on the clash between the Sahlins-style definition of affluence (affluence as wants satisfied, and you can get there by wanting little instead of producing much, so the forager isn't poor, he's just not trapped in the produce-more/desire-more treadmill) and the fact that the surplus nobody asked for is exactly what breeds the population and the hierarchy that delete your way back out. The joke should need the actual content: if you swapped farming for, say, social media or crypto, the joke should stop working entirely. No namedropping, no dates, no 'as the famous essay argues' — nobody in the room needs a citation to get it, but it should be obvious it was written by someone who's read the stuff. Also please don't make it bleak; the room already knows the ending.

If you've got a genuinely different second angle, put it after the first one, but keep the one you'd actually perform first. Finish with a single line beginning "Why it lands:" that says in one sentence which move in the argument the joke is really laughing at.
