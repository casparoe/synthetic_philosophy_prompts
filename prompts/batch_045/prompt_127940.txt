I've been reading about the Sleeping Beauty problem in my own time and I've ended up a thirder, but for a specific reason that I want tested rather than for the usual arguments. The principle I'm relying on is what I call the share-of-awakenings rule: a self-locating credence — the sort you have when you don't know where you are in the story — should be the fraction of the awakenings available to you in which the hypothesis in question is true. In the standard set-up (fair coin, one awakening if Heads, two with amnesia in between if Tails) that gives a third for Heads, and it generalises: if Tails brought ten awakenings rather than two, I'd say an eleventh.

What worries me is a case of my own, which I call the enthusiastic technician. Everything is as before, except that after the coin has landed a technician may add as many extra Tails-awakenings as he likes, and he is instructed to use that power to make Beauty as confident as he can that the coin landed Tails. Beauty knows all of this, but not how many he added. On my rule it looks as though he can drive her confidence towards certainty in Tails without ever touching the coin, which feels like manufacturing evidence out of nothing.

So: what would count against the share-of-awakenings rule? I'd like the candidates ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I'd especially like to know which of them a defender of my sort of view should concede rather than explain away. Is the technician case a genuine defeater, or is it no different from fixing an enormous number of Tails-awakenings in advance? Does the rule implicitly require the number of awakenings to be settled independently of Beauty's own policy, and is that a real implication or a patch? How much trouble is it that "awakenings available to you" needs a unit and a reference class that I seem free to choose — moments of consciousness, awakenings, something else — and has the rule been qualified into something unfalsifiable, and would that matter here? What would move me most is a variant where the rule and my own verdicts come apart without simply restating the original dispute: I don't count "but the coin is fair, so a half" as an objection, since that is what's in question.
