I'm in my fourth year of a PhD and applying for a nine-month dissertation-writing fellowship, and I need a two-page proposal I can send to my referees by the end of next week. My first attempt ran to six pages and my supervisor said it read like three papers stapled together, so the main thing I need is for this to be visibly one project. The committee that reads it is mostly not made up of specialists in this area — two ethicists, a political philosopher, someone from law — so the question has to be legible to a philosopher who doesn't work on metanormative value theory without being flattened into something trivial.

Here is where I've got to. The debate about the wrong kind of reasons seems to me to have been conducted almost entirely on objects where everyone in the debate already agrees what the fitting response is: the dangerous is to be feared, the funny is to be enjoyed, the admirable is to be admired. The standard diagnostics — is the reason provided by the features in virtue of which the object has its value, or merely by the value of someone's responding to it? does the reason bear on the object, or on the state of affairs of the response occurring? — are then run on a case like a demon who threatens to punish you unless you admire him, and they return the verdict everyone wants. My half-formed worry is that they only return a verdict because the fitting response is being held fixed by background agreement about the value in question, and that the diagnostics go unstable for objects whose value is new, composite, or contested. I have in mind things like a conversational system that appears to track and respond to reasons, a restored wetland that is partly designed and partly wild, an artwork whose value partly consists in the responses it provokes, or a practice — a language, a craft — whose continuation depends on people caring about it. For these, whether a given reason is of the right kind seems to flip depending on which of several candidate responses you take to be the fitting one: admiring, trusting, deferring, preserving, cherishing. So my suspicion is that the question hiding inside the wrong kind of reasons problem is not which reasons are the right kind, but which response gets to fix what the value is, and that the priority claim in buck-passing and fitting-attitude accounts is doing more work than it looks like it's doing. My supervisor says this is just the original problem in new costumes; my second reader says it isn't a project about the wrong kind of reasons at all but about metanormative indeterminacy, or conceptual engineering under a different name. I need to find out whether there is a question here that is genuinely distinct from both of those descriptions.

So please turn this into a proposal with: a precise statement of the question, put in a form that has a determinate answer rather than being an invitation to wander; a short account of why the main existing ways of drawing the right-kind/wrong-kind distinction leave that question open, and how the question differs from both my supervisor's diagnosis and my second reader's; a method — what I would actually do across nine months and in what order, including how I would build, vary, and stress-test a set of cases, since the cases are carrying most of the argumentative weight; the range of possible conclusions and what would decide between them, including the conclusion that the distinction cannot be drawn at all without a determinate fitting-response profile, and what that would mean for the fitting-attitude style of account; an honest statement of what would count as the project having failed; and an assessment of whether the project is the right size for nine months, with a principled way of narrowing it if it isn't. Two constraints: please don't give me a literature review, a bibliography, or any citations or footnotes — I'll add those myself, and I'd rather the proposal argue than gesture at sources — and please keep the cases concrete throughout, so that a reader can tell at every step which claim about which case is doing the work.
