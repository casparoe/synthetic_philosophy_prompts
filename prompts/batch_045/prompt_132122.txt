Suppose the world is canonically infinite: countably many people, one for each natural number, each with a finite welfare level, and the welfare series divergent in every candidate outcome. A friend proposes the following escape from the resulting paralysis. Fix a non-principal ultrafilter U on the natural numbers. For two candidate outcomes, let d(n) be the welfare difference at location n and S(k) the sum of the first k differences; rank A above B just in case {k : S(k) > 0} ∈ U, below B just in case {k : S(k) < 0} ∈ U, and equal just in case {k : S(k) = 0} ∈ U. Since U is an ultrafilter these cases are jointly exhaustive, so the ranking is complete and transitive; since U contains every cofinite set it satisfies strong Pareto and is unmoved by any finitely many relocations of welfare; and since no non-principal ultrafilter is invariant under every permutation of an infinite set, it is anonymous only for finite permutations, and the ultrafilter it depends on can be proved to exist but never exhibited. He concludes that some ordering in this family is the correct axiology for infinite worlds. I put 0.4 on that claim; he puts 0.75.

Give me your own number, decomposed. Take the claim to be settled, in the sense I mean, only if such an ordering is what an ideally reflective theory of interpersonal aggregation would endorse — not merely a technically convenient device for avoiding widespread incomparability.

1. Your headline credence, as a single number, with the one consideration doing most of the work.
2. The one or two sub-questions your number mostly rides on — whether full permutation-invariance is genuinely required as opposed to finite-permutation-invariance, whether a non-constructible dependence on a choice of ultrafilter can be correct at all, whether incomparability is the better response, and so on — each with a probability and one line of justification.
3. The sub-questions you are setting near 0 or 1 and so treating as settled, with one line on why each is safe to ignore.
4. How your sub-probabilities combine into your headline figure, in one line of arithmetic, and whether you are treating them as independent.
5. Which term you are least confident in, what argument or evidence would move it most, and which of the two of us at 0.4 and 0.75 is most likely the one who disagrees about it.

Respond as exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, with no introduction and no conclusion.
