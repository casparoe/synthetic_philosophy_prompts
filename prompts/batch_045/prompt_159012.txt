hey — I'm in a grad seminar on AI alignment this term and we had a session on the HCH / factored cognition stuff (the recursive scheme where a human gets to hand subquestions to copies of themselves, and the "answer" is meant to be the limit of that process). Someone in the reading group said something I can't stop chewing on: deliberation just *is* factored cognition. Their pitch was that when you're stuck on something hard you already do this — you break the problem into smaller questions, answer those as if you were a fresh person, and recombine — so HCH is that same move with unlimited time and as many copies of you as you like, which makes it a pure idealisation of thinking harder rather than a new kind of system. They then used that to get two conclusions: (1) the limit of the scheme can't do anything a very patient human couldn't in principle do, so there's no room for discontinuity or a genuinely new kind of agency showing up inside it, and (2) nothing in there could be deceptive or strategically scheming, because no node is a long-lived optimiser — it's deliberation all the way down. A second person pushed it further and said the top node plays the role of consciousness and the tree underneath is like unconscious processing.

I want to push back but I'm not sure where the leverage is. Our tutor also had us tweak the scheme in a way that I think makes the analogy wobble: call it HCH+L. Same as before except (a) every node can read a shared ledger holding the full transcript of every sibling node already answered at the same depth, and (b) each human is capped at two children and a fixed time/token budget per node. So it's still "copies of you answering subquestions", but now the copies can all read each other's notes in parallel.

Can you audit the analogy properly? I'd like:

- a correspondence table — what in ordinary deliberation plays the role of a node, a subquestion, an answer, recombination, "thinking longer", attention, memory, intuition, and the unity of the deliberating agent;
- which features of real deliberation the scheme just doesn't have (a single evaluator with everything in view at once, interim answers being automatically integrated, deliberation being free rather than a budgeted resource, whatever else);
- which of those gaps actually matter for conclusions (1) and (2) and which are cosmetic;
- whether HCH+L is still an idealisation of deliberation at all, or whether the shared ledger has quietly turned it into something else (a committee, a market of subcontractors, a bureaucracy, an appellate court?) — and whether the two-children cap and the per-node budget change what "thinking longer" even means here;
- whether the analogy is smuggling in that decomposition is lossless and recombination trivial;
- whether (1) and (2) survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying them;
- and a better analogy, if there is one.

Answer in British English please, and be concrete about which feature of the case is doing the work rather than just listing pros and cons. Sorry if this is a bit of a ramble, I've been going back and foward on it all week.
