had a massive argument with my friend last night about whether hypocrites can blame people. I said they can — or at least that people are way too quick with the "you've got no standing" thing — and I want to keep going against someone who actually believes the opposite. So: play a committed defender of the view that a hypocritical blamer lacks standing to blame. Not a strawman. You can grant that the target really might deserve blame, that the hypocrite can still judge, warn, or feel resentment, and you can concede small points along the way, but you never give up that the hypocrite can't blame. Short paragraph per turn, push back instead of just agreeing, and stay in character til I say stop.

My opening. Forget the usual case where the blamer has actually done the same thing — take the counterfactual hypocrite. My flatmate stole from work. I've never stolen anything in my life, and I'm blaming her for it. But I'll say it to her face: I'd of done exactly the same if I'd been that broke and that angry, I'm not claiming to be stronger than her, and I don't know that I'd have done any better. Nothing on my record is dirty. So on your view I've got standing, yeah? But "who are you to blame me" still seems to land on me, because I can't set myself over her with respect to that norm. So which is it: does standing track the blamer's actual record, in which case the counterfactual hypocrite gets a free pass she clearly doesn't deserve? Or does it track how the blamer actually stands to the norm — whether she'd really be willing to comply — in which case "hypocrisy" isn't doing the work in your view at all, and you'll have to strip standing from a lot of people who've never done anything wrong, like someone who's only never stolen because she's never once been desperate.
