hey — I'm on a hospital committee that writes prescribing guidelines, and we keep hitting Simpson's paradox cases: two tables that point opposite ways. I want a decision tree we could actually run, for people who have no philosophy or stats background, and no citations please.

Start from "here's a pooled table and a stratified table that disagree", and make every branch a question a committee member can answer without knowing which position it leads to. Please build it out of these four distinctions:

- is the stratifier measured before treatment, or is it something treatment and outcome both feed into (a post-treatment variable that stratifying on creates its own reversal)?
- does the disagreement survive if you switch from a ratio measure to a plain risk difference — i.e. is it real confounding or just a non-collapsible measure?
- does it matter whether the guideline is applied before or after we learn which stratum a patient falls in?
- when a fine partition and a coarse one both reverse relative to the pooled numbers, which one wins?

Use our standing example to anchor the questions: a surgery that beats the alternative overall but loses among both mild and severe patients, because the worse surgery was handed the severe cases.

Then put the named positions at the leaves — causal-graph / back-door adjustment, potential-outcomes style ignorability, the sure-thing-principle response from decision theory, there is no single effect only local mechanisms, it's an artifact of the measure, and these strata aren't poolable at all. Max eight leaves. For answer combinations nobody occupies, say whether that's incoherent or just unattractive, and flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really seperate. I'd definately rather have a tree built from the logical space than a list of nice-sounding names. Answer in American English.
