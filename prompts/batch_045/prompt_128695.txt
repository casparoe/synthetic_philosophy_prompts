Looking for a name for a thing I keep tripping over, and fair warning: I'm going to be annoyed if I just get a label back. I want to know what exactly the phenomenon is, what it isn't, and where its edges are.

Context: I'm a PhD student, officially in cog sci, though the reading group I actually care about is philosophy of mind. The shape I keep seeing:

Someone claims that some class of features does no causal work. Consciousness is the usual example — there's an old image about a whistle that makes a lot of noise but doesn't turn any wheels. Someone else objects: fine, but then how do we end up with an enormous pile of true, detailed, systematically correct beliefs about those features? You say the experience of reddishness doesn't cause anything; yet here I am, able to say what it's like, and I wasn't guessing.

The reply is almost never "ok, it causes something after all." It's a split. There's the causal story about how the belief got produced, which never mentions the feature, and then a second, non-causal story about why the belief is *correct*, or about what makes it a belief about *that* feature — the belief is about it because it's the very thing the belief is about, or because the feature constitutes the belief's content, or because the belief "tracks" the feature without being pushed around by it.

The part I can't name comes one step later. That second story always seems to have to be strong enough that the belief *would have been different* if the feature had been different — otherwise the tracking is a coincidence and we're back at the objection. But that kind of "would have been different" sensitivity looks exactly like the difference-making that was just denied. So the causal thesis survives while the difference-making thesis gets quietly handed back, and the argument then turns entirely on whether counterfactual dependence is a species of causation, which nobody ever says out loud.

Same shape, I think, when people say mathematical or normative facts are causally inert and then explain our grip on them with some non-causal relation. And then there's a mirror version: the tracking is treated as *evidence* that the feature is causally efficacious after all. I can't tell if that's my thing in a hat or a genuinely separate argument.

Near misses I'm aware of and don't think are it: there's the standard worry about how you could have knowledge of experiences that do nothing, but that's usually framed as a problem about justification or about what explains the belief's existence, and mine is about the machinery of the repair. There's also a standard thing about causally inert properties getting squeezed out by physical causes, which feels different too — that's about competition for one effect, mine is about two stories being told side by side.

Two more things I want: does the pattern still bite when the second story is *identity* rather than tracking or representation? I suspect not, and if that's right the name should cover only the cases where the second relation carries content but not causal power. And if there's no established name at all, say so and coin one — with a definition and at least one case clearly in and one clearly out.
