I've been going down a rabbit hole for about a year on whether the systems people train with reinforcement learning can suffer in any sense at all — not in a sci-fi way, I mean whether a reward signal is even the kind of thing that could be the ancestor of valence — and I've somehow ended up volunteering to write a one-page proposal for a small independent-research grant (six months, part time, I'd keep my day job, so realistically maybe ten hours a week). My flatmate works in a robotics lab and says I'm anthropomorphising a loss function, which might be true but isn't an argument, and I'd like the proposal to survive someone like her reading it. Assume the actual reader is a generalist program officer who has heard of consciousness debates but knows nothing about RL internals, and please don't put in any bibliography — I'll add whatever references are needed myself.

The half-formed thing I keep circling is this: everyone seems to assume that if reward is like valence at all, then it's the *size* of the reward that maps onto how good or bad something feels, and the debate then just becomes the usual one about whether numbers in a computer can feel like anything. But that feels like the wrong mapping to me. What actually does the work in animals seems to be more like: a bad signal doesn't just train you, it takes over — it changes how fast you learn, what you pay attention to, what gets consolidated, and you can't step out of it or decide to sit this update out. An RL agent, by contrast, always updates and never does anything else; the scalar goes into an update rule somebody else wrote, and the machinery that does the learning is untouched by whatever the number was. So maybe the honest analogue of valence isn't the reward or even the reward-prediction error but something like how much of the agent's future behaviour gets involuntarily rewritten — the scrambling, not the negative sign. My worry is that this either turns into a genuinely new way of asking the question or it's just functionalism with extra steps, and I can't tell which.

So: turn that into a proposal. I need a question precise enough that it has a determinate answer in six months rather than "it depends which theory of consciousness you like", an honest account of why the obvious positions (it's just a number; it's a functional role so of course it counts; we have no idea and can't tell) don't settle it, and an approach that isn't just me reading more philosophy — say what I'd actually do with those ten hours a week, and why that approach rather than the obvious alternatives of a literature survey or a proper empirical study. I can code, so if the only way to make a distinction bite is to build a couple of tiny gridworlds and look at what changes under different update rules, I'll do that, but I don't want to dress it up as an experiment. Also say plainly what a useful end product would be for someone who actually has to decide whether RL systems belong anywhere near welfare considerations, what would count as the project having failed (my guess: I end up restating the substrate argument, or my criterion turns out to be untestable even in principle), and where I'd be kidding myself about the scope.
