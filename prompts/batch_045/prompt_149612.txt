I sit on my university's research ethics committee, which has received a protocol that doesn't fit any of our existing categories. A machine-learning group proposes to train 512 parallel instances of a single recurrent architecture under shared parameters — identical architecture and initialisation, differing only in data order and noise seed, with gradients averaged across instances at every step — and to build into the training schedule a deliberate battery of aversive signals intended to probe for the markers of valenced experience (persistent avoidance generalisation, failure to extinguish, an arousal-like internal signal) that the group's own working paper treats as the criterion for attributing sentience to a trained system. Half the committee wants to route this to full animal-protocol review; half points out that no organism with a nervous system is involved anywhere.

The question I can't settle is one of counting. Because the instances start as near-functional-duplicates and only diverge as training proceeds, it isn't clear whether we are dealing with one candidate subject, 512 of them, or something whose number changes over the course of the run — and hence whether moral weight scales with how many times a computation is physically performed or with how many distinct functional patterns get instantiated. This isn't academic for us: the group's power analysis requires all 512 copies and cannot be reduced.

Please draft our decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — recommending a concrete course of action with a time frame, stating what the committee should actually do about the counting question rather than flagging it as unresolved, and giving the strongest argument against your own recommendation its due. Close with a final line of the form: Recommendation: <approve | approve with conditions | reject>.
