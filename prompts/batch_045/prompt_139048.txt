Write a satirical government Green Paper — a formal consultation document — of roughly 850 to 950 words, issued by a fictional body, the Directorate-General for Cultural Inheritance, and titled *Securing the Accumulation of Culture: Proposals for a Cultural Germ Line*. Its premise is that culture is admitted, on all sides, to be a very poor Darwinian system: what passes from person to person is not copied but rebuilt by the receiver, variants blend rather than segregating cleanly, novelties are introduced by design rather than blind chance, and nobody can say where one cultural unit ends and the next begins. The paper's proposal is that these are not objections to a Darwinian science of culture but defects in culture, and that they can be remedied by regulation.

The piece should proceed, in deadpan civil-service register — passive constructions, hedged obligations, cross-references to annexes, impact assessments, claims of cost-neutrality, a compliance target — through numbered sections with headings: an executive summary; a statement of the problem; a definitions section; the proposal proper; a schedule of transmission licences; a carrier-welfare clause; a section answering the charge of censorship; and an annex of two worked examples.

Constrain the content as follows.

The proposal proper should impose a Weismannian separation: a designated master copy, held under archival conditions, is the only thing permitted to transmit, while instantiations — loaves baked, songs performed, tools used — are somatic and disposable. Verbatim copying is mandated within the germ line. Because improvement by a copyist is acquired modification and therefore not heritable in the relevant sense, a Blind Variation Requirement should state that alterations may enter the pool only through a randomised error-injection schedule, and that a copyist may not substitute a better version for the one received, however obviously better it is. Prestige and success bias — the tendency to copy the eminent or the already-widespread — should be treated as a distortion of the selection process, analogous to anti-competitive practice, and licensed accordingly. Transmission to non-descendants should be handled as unregulated lateral transfer: efficient, and destructive of pedigree.

The definitions section is where the piece should be most quietly damning. The Directorate must settle the boundary of the cultural unit — word, sentence, recipe, song, genre — and should do so by administrative convenience, fixing the grain at whatever size is legible to inspectors and billable under the licence schedule, without ever conceding that the choice was arbitrary.

Both worked examples must carry argumentative weight. In the first, hold up a sourdough starter as the model institution the Directorate wishes culture resembled: a genuine lineage with a physical germ line, vertical transmission, and high fidelity — while noting, without irony, that the recipe for maintaining the starter is culture, and therefore precisely the unreliable part. In the second, address a variant that spreads successfully while lowering the fitness of the human beings who carry it; the paper should find itself required, by its own mandate to serve the pool rather than its carriers, to protect it, and should resolve the difficulty with a one-replicator-health clause and a scheme of compensation payments to affected carriers.

Two further requirements. The section answering the charge of censorship must give a reply that is not obviously wrong — that every education system already biases transmission, and that the scheme merely makes an existing bias explicit, legible, and subject to appeal — so that a reader cannot simply dismiss the document. And the paper should refer to human beings throughout in the vocabulary of carriers or vehicles, softening this with a welfare clause which insists that nothing in the scheme is adverse to carrier interests while quietly ranking them last.

Do not name, quote, or allude to any real theorist, and include no citations or footnotes; the piece must be recognisable from the machinery alone. There should be no internet-meme humour and no winking at the reader: the Directorate never says the quiet part, and the comedy should consist entirely in the machinery being applied correctly. The document should close with a pilot scheme, a fidelity target, and a note that the programme will be revenue-neutral through licence fees.
