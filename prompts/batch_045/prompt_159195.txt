I am writing a novel set largely in a sleep laboratory, and I need one joke from you: a single line that a technician delivers to a subject who has just been woken mid-REM and asked what was going on. The line must turn on the real disagreement about whether dreams are experiences — Norman Malcolm's position that experiences cannot be ascribed to a sleeper at all, so that the waking report is the only datum and there is no further fact of the matter about the night, against Daniel Dennett's position that dreams are experiences but are composed at the moment of awakening, when a kind of recording is loaded into memory, rather than played back from something that happened earlier.

The complication is a fictional device in my laboratory: without waking the subject, it draws out and transcribes a continuous stream of dream reports across the whole night. The joke should still land, and its punchline should register what this device does to the dispute — whether it embarrasses Malcolm more than Dennett, or the reverse, and why the other view can absorb it. It must be funny to a reader who actually knows both positions, with the humour coming from their content rather than from REM puns or generic laboratory gags; give the line itself, then a single sentence of gloss.
