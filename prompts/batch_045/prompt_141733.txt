My friend and I have just spent an evening arguing about Libet's experiment and I want to know where the real weight in the disagreement sits. Her claim is that the readiness potential is, on the stochastic-accumulator reading, just what the drift of neural noise up to a threshold looks like when a movement is spontaneous, so there is no covert prior decision for it to reveal and the result threatens nothing. My reply is that this at best disposes of a slow, non-specific ramp, so I offered her a different case: call it the decoder case. Subjects deliberately choose between two options; a neural decoder predicts which option they will take a few seconds before they report being aware of having decided; the effect replicates, and it is not explained by anything the experimenter can name — no gaze, no posture, no attention, no drift over the session.

Give me your credence in this proposition: if that result were robust, it would give us genuine empirical reason to deny that the subjects' choices were free in the sense relevant to moral responsibility. I want a number and a decomposition, with a probability and one line of justification for each term: whether the decoder would be reading off the formation of the decision rather than an upstream biasing signal; whether the prior neural specification of an outcome is in tension with freedom at all, that is, how much rides on determinism about the decision being compatible with freedom; whether subjects' reports of when they became aware of deciding measure anything about decision timing; and how much of the whole thing depends on which reading of "free" is in play — leeway versus sourcehood or reasons-responsiveness. Add whatever terms you think I have missed, combine them, and tell me which term you are least confident in. My number is 0.7 and hers is 0.15; tell me which of those terms our disagreement most likely lives in, and what single further result would move your own number furthest.
