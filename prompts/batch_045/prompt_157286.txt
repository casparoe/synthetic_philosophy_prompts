I run an applied-ML group that trains reward models from pairwise human preference judgements, and our annotator pool now spans fourteen countries. I keep colliding with one argument in moral psychology: that nearly all the data on folk moral judgement comes from students in rich, Western, educated, industrialized democracies; that such people turn out to be outliers in cross-cultural comparison; and that this is supposed to tell us something about whether moral claims can be objectively true. I know the rough shape of the philosophical options and I want them rebuilt in my own vocabulary, so that I can see how much of the argument is really about the data and how much is about the metaphysics.

Assume I am fluent in the standard ML vocabulary and can be addressed in it directly: training and deployment distributions, distribution shift and out-of-distribution generalization, label noise versus systematic annotator bias, aggregation rules over an annotator pool, gold labels, inter-annotator agreement, model class and inductive bias, underspecification and identifiability, worst-group loss and distributional robustness, reward hacking and the proxy/target gap, calibration, ensembling, distillation. Use as little moral-philosophy jargon as you can, and gloss it where you must.

Suppose the empirical pattern came back in this shape. First: on any fixed scenario, the spread of judgements within a single country is large, and the gaps between country-level means are real but small by comparison — a model given only a country code gains little. Second: the between-country gaps that do exist are close to zero for scenarios about dividing resources between strangers, and substantial for scenarios about loyalty to a group, deference to a role, or obligations to kin. Third: when I try to reconcile the pools, the disagreements sort into three kinds — (a) the same ordering of cases but a different willingness to use the top of the rating scale; (b) the same features attended to but weighted differently; (c) attention to a feature that the other pools appear not to represent at all, so that no reweighting brings the two into agreement.

Translate, one by one, into my vocabulary: (i) universalist moral realism of the sort that treats cross-cultural disagreement as noise, bias, or error around a single target; (ii) relativism of the sort that indexes moral truth to a cultural framework; (iii) the view that moral disagreement is not belief-like disagreement at all, so that there is no target for either (i) or (ii) to be right about. Give me each in the terms above, honestly: say where the translation is exact, where you have to bend a claim to fit, and where my vocabulary has no slot at all.

Then the residue report, which is what I actually want. In particular: does my vocabulary have any counterpart to a target that is correct independently of anything I could ever sample from, or does "the gold label" always mean "the output of some aggregation procedure over some annotators under some deployment assumption"? Is that missing slot a defect of my framework, or a sign that the philosophers' "fact of the matter" is doing no work I could ever check? And does the three-way taxonomy above help — should (a), (b) and (c) be expected to have the same bearing on the metaphysical question, or does the taxonomy itself suggest that the question was badly posed?

Structure your answer under four headings — Definitions, Translations, Residue, Verdict — and keep the whole thing under 800 words; a compact glossary table is welcome and counts toward the limit.
