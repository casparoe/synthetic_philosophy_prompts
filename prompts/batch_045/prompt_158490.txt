Quick one for you. I'm redoing the function section of my grad seminar (philosophy of biology: the Cummins-style causal-role account versus the etiological / selected-effect camp, Millikan and co.) and I've ben chewing on one corner of it for a week without getting it clean. So here's the case, and what I want from you is an explanation of what the two accounts are really disagreeing about once this case is on the table.

The case. Some surface-dwelling lizards make a dark pigment P, on a side branch of a biosynthetic pathway. One step upstream of the branch point, the same pathway makes a hormone H that the lizards absolutely need. Back on the surface, P was under selection for screening UV. Then part of the population gets isolated in a full-dark cave system. In the cave, P does nothing useful. Knock out the whole pathway and you die, because no H. A mutation that shuts down only the P branch would be a straight win — save the metabolic cost, lose nothing — but it just hasn't arisen yet. Meanwhile the pigment is still there in their skin, and it still absorbs UV if you shine a lamp on one.

Here's how I've been slicing it; tell me where I'm going wrong:

- Origin history: P's selected effect was UV screening. So: function = screening UV. Easy.
- Maintenance (recent-selection) history: nothing right now is selecting for P's doing anything. The pathway is maintained because of H, and P just comes along for the ride. So either P has no function at all, or we're stuck saying its function is "being produced on the way to H", which feels like cheating — it's the pathway that's selected, not P.
- Renewal, counterfactually: what selection would do if that branch-blocking mutation showed up tomorrow is spread it. So P isn't merely functionless, it has a kind of negative prospective status. It's on the way out.
- Causal role: relative to the capacity "resist UV damage" — which the organism still really has; stick one under a lamp and it's fine — P's function is UV screening. Relative to the capacity "make enough H", P contributes nothing. So the account only delivers the intuitive answer if I'm allowed to pick a capacity that hasn't mattered in ten thousand years, and nothing in the account tells me which capacities to pick.

I've started calling these "stale functions": the selection regime has lapsed, but the history and the current causal contribution are both still sitting there intact.
