I'm a backend engineer with no philosophy or finance background, and a few months ago I got nerd-sniped into writing little Monte Carlo sims of repeated betting: you have a bankroll, each round you pick a fixed fraction f of it and put it on a coin that's biased in your favour, you win or lose that chunk, and you go again. I ended up in the crowd that says you should pick f to maximise the expected log of your wealth instead of the expected wealth itself, and I basically get why — the expected-value-maximising bettor goes broke in essentially every run, which I can see with my own eyes in the output. Fine. What I can't stop poking at is how fragile the nice properties of that rule seem to be.

The nice properties being these: with the log rule, the best f is the same on every round. It doesn't depend on how much money you currently have, it doesn't depend on how many rounds are left, and it doesn't seem to care what happened on the previous rounds either. I find that really elegant and I'd assumed it was just a fact about gambling. Then I started perturbing the setup. If I charge a fixed dollar fee per round instead of a fee that's a percentage of the bankroll, the best f suddenly depends on how much you have, and below some bankroll the best move seems to be to quit playing entirely. If I give the bettor a target — reach a thousand dollars or you've lost — the best f depends on how close you are and on how many rounds are left, and it's often a good deal larger than the long-run one. If I put a hard cap on the winnings, f shrinks toward nothing as you get near the cap. Each of those changes is small and reasonable on its own, and each one turns the optimal rule from a single number into a big ugly table indexed by wealth and by time. Meanwhile percentage fees, targets expressed as a multiple of your current wealth, and caps that scale with your wealth leave the clean rule completely untouched.

So: is there an established name for what's breaking here? I'm not after the name of the rule itself, I know that one; I want the name for the property of not depending on wealth or horizon, if there is one, and more than anything the name for the dividing line between perturbations that preserve it and perturbations that destroy it. In my notes I've started calling the preserving kind "scale-shaped", which probably already means something else. And I'm not confident I've carved this correctly at all: is the wealth-dependence and the horizon-dependence really one phenomenon or two, is "the optimal f becomes a function of two variables" the same fact as "the clean rule is a special case", and is the thing I'm calling preservation maybe just the rule surviving because the objective is still a logarithm of a ratio? If there genuinely isn't a term for it, give me one and define it tightly enough that I can tell which side a perturbation I haven't tried yet falls on — say, a floor under the bankroll you're not allowed to drop below, or a requirement to withdraw a fixed dollar amount every round.
