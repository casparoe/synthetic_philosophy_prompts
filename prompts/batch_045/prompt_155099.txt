Hey — I'd like a dialogue, not an essay.

The setup: Dennett's quining strategy against qualia, in the stiffer form that Chase and Sanborn give it. The four standard marks (ineffable, intrinsic, private, directly or immediately apprehensible in consciousness) can't all be had at once; so anyone who wants qualia has to give at least one up; and whatever is left no longer does any anti-physicalist work — no knowledge argument, no conceivability argument, nothing Mary couldn't in principle have got from her armchair. I've read enough of this literature that I don't need the standard realist replies set up for me (biting the bullet on ineffability, the phenomenal-concept move, the accusation that Dennett is smuggling in verificationism). Start past those.

The move I want tested is one I made up, and it occured to me while reading the swap cases. Call it the split-package reply. The idea: the four marks aren't four rival claims about one thing, they're two bundles that only look contradictory once you weld them together. Bundle A is an access package — private, directly apprehensible. Bundle B is a nature package — intrinsic, and ineffable in one specific thin sense: not capturable in the vocabulary of the physical and functional sciences, not unspeakable in every language whatsoever. The realist keeps B as constitutive of what a quale is, and demotes A to a contingent, empirically corrigible claim about how we happen to stand to our own qualia. On that reading the fading-qualia and dancing-qualia style arguments only look like reductios because they assume A holds of necessity; drop that and the gradual neural-prosthesis cases just show that functional role doesn't fix qualia — which is the realist's own thesis, not a problem for it.

So write me the dialogue between a quiner and a realist running the split-package reply, and make them evenly matched; nobody gets to be the straight man.

What I want the quiner to actually press: that the realist used A to fix the reference of the term in the first place (ostension, 'this', picking out the target from the inside), so demoting A saws off the branch; that B without A has no epistemology — if access is contingent and fallible, there's nothing left to distinguish Mary's learning a new property from Mary's acquiring a new recognitional ability, and the realist's own favourite cases (inversion, the absent/dancing qualia in the swap scenarios) secretly re-import A because they require someone to be able to tell from the inside that something changed; and that an intrinsic property we're only contingently related to is doing the work of a bare hidden essence, which B was supposed to avoid.

What I want the realist to actually press: that quining is a paradox and paradoxes are resolved by dropping any premise, so the quiner owes a non-question-begging reason to reject A rather than B, and verificationist discomfort isn't one; that the same knife cuts the other way — if direct apprehension goes, heterophenomenology has no data either; that there's a positive model available, acquaintance conceived as a causal-informational relation rather than an infallible one, which keeps privacy and directness as defeasible features; and that B-only qualia are still enough to block any a priori entailment from the physical and functional truths to the phenomenal ones, which is all the knowledge and conceivability arguments ever needed in the first place.

Let them go at least three or four full rounds, with each one answering what the other actually said rather than delivering a prepared speech, and end with someone conceding something concrete — I don't want a polite draw.

Then two short codas. First, in each character's own voice, a few lines on the single consideration that would genuinely move them off their position, and where their own argument is most likely to be broken. Second, a short note from you, the writer, on where the dialogue leans on a contested reading (say, of what ineffable or intrinsic is supposed to mean here) and where it takes liberties. No citations anywhere, nobody mentions a paper by name, keep it in the room.
