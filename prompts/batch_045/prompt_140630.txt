I'm teaching a 90-minute session on Lakatos to about 20 undergrads in a philosophy of science course, and I want them to *feel* why nobody working inside a research programme ever experiences its degeneration as a refutation -- it always just looks like one more awkward anomaly that some clever grad student will patch. Design me a game for that.

Rough shape I have in mind (change it if it won't work): teams of 4-5 each declare a "hard core" of 2-3 claims they publicly pledge never to give up, plus a starting "protective belt" of auxiliaries. Over 5-6 rounds I hand out phenomenon cards. A team can register a sealed prediction before the reveal; if it hits, that's a novel fact and scores a progressive point. If a card contradicts them, they patch by adding an auxiliary to the belt, which scores nothing and costs a belt token. Everyone also gets one "monster-barring" card they can play to disqualify a phenomenon.

The bit I most want your help designing: a rule that makes "there is no instant rationality" into something they're stuck under rather than a quote on a slide. Say -- a team may only abandon its hard core after two full rounds of patching with no successful novel prediction, and even then a "Lakatosian judge" (me) can grant a reprieve based only on the public record, never on which core is actually true.

Give me concrete materials (including what goes on ~15 phenomenon cards), the scoring, a round-by-round timeline with no lecture up front and no Lakatos vocabulary until the debrief, and then a debrief that maps specific moments onto hard core vs protective belt, negative vs positive heuristic, and progressive vs degenerating problem shifts. Also: what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (someone declares a hard core so vague it can't be hit, everyone just patches forever, the judge gets accused of favouritism) and how to handle each. And be honest about what the game can't reproduce -- especially that real appraisal of a programme takes decades, which 90 minutes necessarily compresses into nothing.
