for a democratic theory reading group I have to write my position on the boundary problem on one page and then have everyone else attack it, so here it is in bullets. tell me whether it hangs together.

1. who belongs in the demos should be settled by the coercion test: you get a say over a decision just in case you're one of the people the state will coercively enforce that decision against. so not the "everyone whose interests are affected" test — that one is hopeless, since everything affects everything, and it ends up giving the whole planet a vote on my city's bus timetable.

2. but I also think the boundary can't be fixed from the armchair in a way that binds anyone. in practice who counts has to be decided by the people already inside, by majority vote, and there's no court or philosopher standing above them to overrule it.

3. felon disenfranchisement is unjust, including for people currently serving prison sentences.

4. it's fine that citizens who've lived abroad for fifteen-plus years lose the vote, even when they still file taxes and could fly back whenever they like.

5. states do have a real if qualified right to decide who gets to immigrate.

6. local things should be decided locally: a city's zoning vote should be taken by that city's residents only.

7. voting age sixteen, no lower. someone has to draw a bright line and that's where I'd put it.

8. and no proxy votes for kids — one person one vote means one person one vote.

here's what I want from you. I already suspect 1 and 5 are in trouble together, because the people the state turns away at the border are precisely people it's coercing, but I can't tell whether that's a real problem or just wordplay on "coerce". I also want to know whether 1 and 2 can both be true — if inclusion is a matter of basic justice, how can the current majority be the ones who decide it, given they could just vote out whichever group threatens them? and 6 feels like it's quietly sneaking back in the affected-interests test I rejected in 1.

but don't just flag everything as in tension. rank the problems by how bad they actually are, tell me which pairs only look like they clash (3 and 4 feel wrong together but maybe they're fine and I just can't say why), point out any extra commitment I'm relying on without having written it down, and then say what the single cheapest revision would be and what it costs me. don't start by telling me which bullet I should give up — I want to see what you think forces what.
