hey — I'm a junior and I somehow got picked to give this 10-minute presentation with Q&A to our school board next month. Quick backstory: our school is Ridgeview Academy, named after Cornelius Vance, who basically founded the town, donated the land for the school back in the 1890s, and was also the main author of our state's first forced-sterilization law. The board has been screaming at each other about renaming for like two years and gotten nowhere, so they let our student advisory committee say something, and my friend in debate club talked me into doing it.

The thing I'm defending isn't "keep it" and it isn't "rename it" — it's a split, and the split rests on a distinction I stole from my philosophy elective: naming an *institution* is commemorative (it says "we, right now, stand for this person's values"), while naming a *building* after a donor is basically transactional, it's a reciept for a gift, and the only reason Vance's name is anywhere is a clause in the original land deed saying it stays on the building. So my proposal: we change the school's name, we leave "Vance Hall" on the original building, and we add two more moving parts. One, a two-sided plaque — the thing he did that we're honoring on one face, the thing he did that we're explicitly not honoring on the other. Two, a "commemorative offset": every year the district puts an inflation-adjusted fraction of what that land is worth now into a scholarship fund for students from the groups his law targeted, basically paying rent on a name you've decided you can't give back. I also want to say out loud that the "he was a man of his time" defense and the "presentism" accusation are both weaker than people think, because we don't honor people for being better than us, we honor them for being better than their peers, and Vance was worse than his peers on exactly this.

The room is going to be: three board members — one retired APUSH teacher, one real estate developer who brings up property values in every single meeting, and one who is literally a Vance descendant — plus the head of the county historical society, who keeps telling the paper that removals are "erasing history", our teachers' union rep, and the two students from the other high school on the committee with me, who both want a straight rename and think my compromise is spineless. So: get me ready for the question period. What are the objections I'll actually get, in roughly the order they'll come and sorted by how much damage they'd do, each one phrased the way that specific person would actually put it (the developer is not going to say "epistemic injustice"; the APUSH teacher might), with a short — like 60-second — reply for each and an honest note on where my reply is soft or where I'm just dodging. Tell me what I should concede in the first thirty seconds so I don't come off as defensive. And give me the question I'm hoping nobody asks — I think mine is the developer asking why, if a name is just a receipt, the district doesn't sell the naming rights to the highest bidder, but tell me what yours is.
