Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed frequentist of the error-statistical, Neyman–Pearson stripe and a committed Bayesian of the subjective-prior, coherence-first stripe, on what the replication crisis in the social and biomedical sciences actually shows and what should be done about it.

Structure the document in four parts:

1. A numbered list of claims both can sign, including about what a significance test does and does not license, about how a low replication rate can coexist with every individual study having been analysed correctly, and about which inferences are genuinely invalidated by selection (file drawer, outcome-switching, optional stopping) and which are not.
2. A disagreements table: one row per residual disagreement, with each side's position stated in wording that side would accept for itself, and a column marking whether the disagreement is substantive or merely verbal.
3. The cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side.
4. For each crux, a case or quantity both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Anchor the collaboration in the following background arrangement, which both parties stipulate: a funder runs a registry with two arms. Every study is registered in advance with design and analysis fixed, then randomly assigned either to a publish-all arm, where results appear whatever they are, or to a significance-filtered arm, where a result appears only if it clears the conventional threshold. Registration also requires each team to deposit, before seeing any data, either a prior distribution over the effect size it is looking for or a power analysis with a stated smallest effect of interest. After ten years the archive contains both arms, so the selection mechanism is known by construction instead of guessed at.

Have the joint statement say what each side would conclude from a large and persistent gap between the effect-size distributions in the two arms; whether the deposited prior or the deposited power analysis does more of the work; whether a prior fitted to the archive itself by a hierarchical or empirical-Bayes route is Bayesian inference or frequentism in disguise; and what becomes of the Bayesian claim that stopping rules are irrelevant once selection across studies, rather than within a single study, is what is doing the damage.

Do not adjudicate, and do not dissolve the dispute by declaring it verbal; make the residual disagreement exact. Close with a final line of the form \answer{...} naming, in at most fifteen words, the one crux both sides agree would most move the debate.
