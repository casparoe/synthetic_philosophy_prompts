What I'm trying to do is argue that the indicative / counterfactual distinction isn't in the meaning of if at all, and I can't tell whether that's a real thesis or just the standard story wearing different clothes. Here are the notes, roughly in order.

THESIS. There's one if. What it does is evaluate the consequent under a supposition of the antecedent relative to a contextually supplied dependency model: a causal graph plus probabilities, or a bare probability distribution, plus parameters for what gets held fixed and how high the bar is for would vs might. The indicative reading is what you get when the supplied model is used for conditioning: assertability tracks P(C | A), more or less Adams's thesis. The counterfactual reading is what you get when the same sentence is evaluated against an intervention on the model: assertability tracks P(C | do(A)), which deletes the arrows into A. Everything that looks like a lexical ambiguity is supposed to fall out of which model the context hands you, plus two knobs (hold-the-past-fixed, would-threshold).

CASE A, the direction everyone knows. Barometer: if the barometer is falling a storm is coming is fine, but if the barometer were falling a storm would be coming is bad if you'd have to force the needle down by hand. Same for the Kennedy pair: if Oswald didn't shoot Kennedy someone else did is fine, if Oswald hadn't shot Kennedy someone else would have is not. On my story both are the same phenomenon: conditioning respects the correlation from the common cause, intervention deletes it.

CASE B, the direction I think nobody writes down. Set up a drug D for disease X. X kills you unless D cures you. D cures 95% of patients who lack mutation M, and does nothing at all for patients who have M. M is at 5% in the population, but doctors only administer D when a genetic screen has flagged M, and the screen is reliable, so among actual D recipients something like 90% have M. Now take a patient who was not given D. Two sentences:
  If she was given D, she recovered. — terrible; the good indicative is if she was given D, she didn't recover.
  If she had been given D, she would have recovered. — fine, because in the closest D-worlds you hold fixed that she lacks M (which the non-administration was evidence for), and D cures non-M patients 95% of the time.
So you get a good counterfactual with a bad indicative, i.e. the mirror image of the barometer case, and it falls out of the same mechanism: intervention cuts the arrow from M into administration, conditioning keeps it. I think this is the strongest thing in the notes, if it's actually a case.

CASE C, where the graph itself is uncertain. You don't know whether the siren triggers the valve or whether the leak triggers both the siren and separately jams the valve. Now: if the siren is sounding, the valve closed. To evaluate this at all you need P(C | A) averaged over graphs, and for the counterfactual you need P(C | do(A)) averaged over graphs weighted by their pre-antecedent probabilities. But making the siren sound is itself evidence about which graph is right, so the weights want to move, and if you let them you get a regress. My fix is to freeze the weights at their pre-supposal values. I'm not sure that's principled or just a stipulation that makes the numbers come out.

THE PART I'M MOST WORRIED ABOUT. Two things. First: is this just Kratzer? On a restrictor-ish picture the context already supplies a modal base and an ordering source, and I could simply say that the ordering source is a causal model. If so my view is a specification of a familiar framework rather than a rival to it, and the paper gets much smaller. Second: Lewis's triviality argument. I want to say probabilities of conditionals are averages of model-relative conditional probabilities, weighted antecedent-independently, and that this escapes triviality because there's no single proposition whose probability is being tracked. But a mixture of probability functions with fixed weights is itself a probability function on a bigger space, so it looks like the escape hatch might close and the argument reapply. I go back and forth on this and I can't see it clearly.
