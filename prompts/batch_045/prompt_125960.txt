hey — I'm a highschool junior and over winter break I fell into a rabbit hole about the language of thought idea, and I think I've ended up believing it. Not the cartoon version where there are tiny english sentences floating around in your head, but this: when you think, your brain is running operations over mental representations that have real parts and real structure, roughly the way a sentence has words and grammar — predicates, arguments, slots you can drop different things into, maybe variables and negation. The thing that got me was the systematicity argument: if you're capable of thinking "the dog bit the cat" you're basically automatically capable of thinking "the cat bit the dog", and any theory of thought has to explain why those abilities show up as a package instead of being learned one at a time. The natural story is that you've got a symbol for dog, a symbol for cat, and a two-place relation with slots, so you get both thoughts for the price of the parts. The version of this I kept running into also says neural nets trained from scratch don't generalize that way unless you basically build the structure in by hand, so the fact that we generalize for free is evidence there really are symbols.

I want the other side, properly. What would count against this position? Rank the defeaters from "this would kill it" to "merely awkward", and for each say what the world would have to look like (what we'd see in people, in kids, in animals, in brains, or in models), whether a committed defender would actually concede it or just reinterpret it, and whether it's a live problem right now or only logically possible. Please stress-test three things in particular — two of them I came up with myself and I can't tell if they're any good:

1. The reporting trap. Every bit of evidence we have about the *format* of a thought seems to arrive already translated into something public — either someone says what they were thinking, or they picture it and describe the picture. So maybe all we're ever detecting is the format of the *output*, not of the thinking itself. Is there any way to get evidence about the vehicle that doesn't go thru this bottleneck? If there isn't, how much does that actually cost the view?

2. "Structured but not sentence-ish" — my favorite, and I'm suspicious of it definately because I invented it. Why couldn't a thought have systematic, recombinable parts and still not be sentence-like at all? Suppose the difference between the dog-bites-cat thought and the cat-bites-dog thought isn't an ordered string of wordlike tokens but a position in some big space of roles and fillers — compositional, structured, reusable, but nothing you could point to as a word, no serial order, no copying and pasting of the same token from one thought into another. If that's genuinely possible, then the systematicity argument only shows that thought is *structured*, not that it's *language-like*, and the language part of the view is just decoration. Where does this go wrong? Does it collapse back into the language of thought as soon as you ask it to handle variables, quantification, or negation? Does it smuggle in a homunculus to read the geometry?

3. The symmetry problem with babies and animals. Little kids and a bunch of non-human animals do things that look systematic without having anything we'd call a language. So either they have Mentalese too — which makes the view cheap and near-universal, but makes the link to actual language look like an accident — or the sentence-like format is something you *get* from learning a language, which would make the view a claim about humans specifically, and a pretty recent one. Which horn do defenders actually pick, and what would settle it?

Also: what would a clean, decisive experiment even look like here, and has anything close to one been done? And be honest about one last thing — I have a feeling the view gets rescued by weakening: whenever someone points at a brain doing something non-sentence-like, the reply is that it's still computing over structured representations, or that the claim was only ever that the mind is *as if* it had a language. At what point does that turn it into something that can't lose? If it has got there, is that actually bad for a claim at this level, or is it fine?
