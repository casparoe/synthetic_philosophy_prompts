Hi — I'm one of three volunteer moderators on a big hobby forum (about 40,000 accounts, 300ish posts a day). We handle maybe ten incidents a week that actually need a judgement: warnings, please explain this messages, forced apologies, temp bans, permanent bans. Right now we have no framework at all, we argue it out in DMs, and it's the same argument every time. One of us thinks members should be held to answer as agents who can see what they did and be addressed about it. Another thinks a good chunk of our problem users plainly aren't in a state to be reasoned with — people mid-meltdown, kids, obvious novelty accounts, a couple of posters who read like they're running a script. We keep rebuilding the same wheel, badly, at eleven at night.

I read P. F. Strawson's essay on freedom and resentment a while back (the reactive attitudes; the participant stance versus the objective one; the exemptions where you stop resenting because they didn't mean it, didn't know, or couldn't help it; and the bit where he says the truth of determinism isn't what's really at issue anyway) and I'm fairly sure it has something to say to us, but I can't turn it into a procedue we can actually run on a Tuesday. So: write me a decision procedure my team can work through on a real report.

What I need in it:

- Questions in a fixed order, each with what counts as a good enough answer to it. We've got ten minutes, no training, and only the thread plus a quick scroll of the account's history, so any question that needs information we can't get should be marked unanswerable rather than left in as a step.
- Where the procedure stops with a no — don't address them as a participant at all, just act — and where it hands the decision over to a person because the question is about our values and not about facts.
- A name and a test for the case Strawson doesn't really name: stepping back from the participant stance toward somebody about one stretch of their conduct while still being in it with them about everything else. I want to be able to tell that apart from us just not wanting an argument.
- A ruling on the blame-shaped messages we send while having quietly given up on the attitude itself — the public this caused harm notice addressed to somebody we are in fact only managing as a risk. Is that incoherent, a harmless institutional ritual, or a small lie, and what does it cost us and them?
- A recusal rule. His distinctions between the personal kind of resentment, the vicarious kind, and the impersonal or moral kind have to survive the simplification somehow, because one of us is often personally involved — the target was their friend, or they were insulted themselves.
- An honest answer about where the objective stance stops being something volunteers can occupy. Strawson more or less says you can't hold it generally, or for long, without it costing you something. At what volume or share of cases does my procedure stop being usable by unpaid people, and what should we do instead once we're there?
- A re-entry step: how we tell when to address someone as a participant again, and who signs off on that.

Every step should be doable in the time we have, explainable to the person affected in three sentences, and fit our rule that any ban needs two of us to agree.

Then run it on three cases:

1. A nine-year member with thousands of helpful posts posts a burst of paranoid rants ending in a racial slur at another member, disappears for two days, comes back mortified, and says he hadn't slept in a week and had just changed medication. Half the forum wants him gone and half wants nothing to happen.
2. A three-day-old account, forty posts, all of them elaborate time-wasting copy-paste designed to eat other people's evenings. Nobody is actually hurt.
3. A well-liked member who has never broken a rule and who has, over about four months, been quietly and cleverly cruel to exactly one newcomer — stuff the mods can read and the newcomer can't quite quote back at us. Everything is technically within the rules.

I'd like at least one of these to land somewhere I won't like — I deffinitely suspect 1 and 3 both do — and I want you to say plainly what the answer is rather than what I want to hear.

Finally, tell me what the procedure can't do. A forum isn't a person, we have legal duties that have nothing to do with anybody's quality of will, and we can't feel the right amounts on demand. Where does the Strawson apparatus just not reach?
