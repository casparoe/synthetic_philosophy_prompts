I am a high school senior writing an independent project on Glaucon's challenge in Book II of the Republic — the story of the ring of Gyges, and the demand that justice be defended in its own right rather than by the reputation and rewards that happen to attach to it. My two faculty advisors have been arguing about this in the margins of my drafts for months and are, as far as I can tell, no closer together than when they started. Before I write the chapter that tries to say something of my own, I want to force the collaboration they have not had. Please draft the joint statement they could both sign.

The two positions, as I understand them from their comments:

Camp P. A successful answer to Glaucon has to point to something the person with the ring has reason to care about, given the ends they already have. The usual candidates are reputation and punishment, which the ring cancels; then guilt, shame and loss of self-respect; then the slower costs of becoming a certain kind of person, including the damage to one's capacity for friendship and trust. Where a case removes all of these, Camp P's view is that the honest thing is to concede that the reason to act justly thins out and may vanish, and that any answer which survives only by appealing to a consideration the agent has no route into has stopped answering Glaucon — because Glaucon's question is a first-personal one about what to do and why to care.

Camp R. The wrongness of the act, or the value of just conduct, is itself a reason for the agent, and its being a reason does not depend on the agent's prior desires or on any payoff. A correct answer may leave the ring-wearer entirely unmoved; that is a fact about the ring-wearer, not a defect in the answer. To require that an answer motivate, as a condition on its being correct, is to build the agent's corruption into the test.

Three stipulations to build the document around, which they have not yet addressed jointly. First, I think they are answering three different questions under one heading, and I want the document to separate them: the predictive question of what people would in fact do with the ring; the normative question of what reasons such a person has; and what I have been calling the addressability question, of what could be said to the ring-wearer that would count as an answer to them, from inside their own point of view. Second, I want the argument to run on a strengthened ring, which I will call the insulated ring: no one ever finds out and no downstream evidence ever implicates the wearer; the wearer keeps full memory of the act but suffers no guilt, shame or self-reproach, and the act does not alter how they assess themselves or their relationships; using it does not degrade any capacity or disposition, so they remain exactly as capable of love, friendship and cooperation, and their other desires and their skill at pursuing them are unchanged; and they know all of this in advance. Third, I want two further variants used as test cases: a fractional ring, issued to a known fraction of the population, to test any answer that works by generalisation or by appeal to what everyone could do; and an inherited ring, passed down through generations, to test whether an answer is about a single act or about sustaining a practice over time.

What I want in the document:

A numbered section of agreed claims — the substantive things both camps can sign, including about what the original challenge is asking, about what the insulated ring does and does not settle, and about which familiar answers both sides now agree are inadequate.

A table of the remaining disagreements. Each row should give the disagreement in neutral wording and then each camp's own preferred formulation of its side, phrased so that camp would accept it as stated. Mark each row as substantive, verbal, or a case of the two camps answering different questions, and say briefly why.

A crux list: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one camp. For each crux, give the case, argument, or kind of evidence both would accept as bearing on it, and write down in advance what each camp predicts it will show. Please include at least the coherence of the insulated ring itself, what the fractional and inherited variants reveal, and whether evidence about what people actually do under guaranteed anonymity bears on the normative question at all.

A short closing section saying which parts of their dispute you judge to be merely verbal, and what I should do with whatever genuine disagreement is left.

Constraints. Do not adjudicate; I am not asking who is right. Do not cite or quote anything — I cannot check sources and I want the argument to stand on its own, so define every term of art you introduce and paraphrase rather than quote. Both advisors have said they will reject any document that rests on the claim that justice is its own reward or on the claim that the agent would feel guilty, so treat both as unavailable unless you can rebuild them into something non-question-begging. Where my stipulations rig the question in favour of one camp, or make it unanswerable, say so plainly rather than producing a document that looks tidier than the situation is. Aim for something a careful reader who has only read Book II could follow, around a thousand to fourteen hundred words.
