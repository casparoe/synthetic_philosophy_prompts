I'm a novelist and a philosopher friend at a dinner party backed me into this one and I cant find the exit.

My character Marnie decides on Sunday night to stop drinking, means it, and on Thursday pours a glass while thinking, clear as anything, that she shouldn't be doing this. My friend says scenes like that are incoherent once you take hyperbolic discounting seriously:

(1) Akrasia is acting freely and intentionally against your own better judgment.
(2) Everyone discounts the future hyperbolically, not exponentially: an option gets steeper the closer it gets.
(3) So Marnie's Sunday self and her Thursday self rank the options differently, and the ranking flips as Thursday's glass gets near.
(4) At the moment she pours, the near reward is what she most wants, so drinking is top-ranked for her at the moment she acts.
(5) If an agent always does whatever is top-ranked for her at the moment of action, she never acts against her own better judgment.
(6) So Marnie isn't weak-willed; she's two consistent agents in a row, and neither is irrational, since each maximizes her own present ranking.
(7) Generalizing: apparent weakness of will is always just discounting, so there is no such thing as akrasia.

I don't believe (7) — a character doing exactly what she judges, right then, to be worse is the engine of half of literary fiction, and I'm giving that up last. But the argument also seems to prove more than my friend wants: if (6) holds, then Marnie is irrational to precommit at all, and every resolution about the future is pointless, since the self who arrives will want something else. And she's a *sophisticated* discounter — she's read about this stuff, she predicts the flip, she could cheaply pour the wine out, and she still doesn't. He says that doesn't change anything.

Which step do I reject? I'd rather keep (1) and (2) than (7), but I want to know what each rejection costs me, and whether the detail that she knows in the moment that she shouldn't is the thing that saves the scene or the thing that makes it incoherent. Give me the diagnosis in prose, then a table with columns: step (1-7, one row each), verdict (valid / invalid / valid-only-under-one-reading), flaw (one sentence naming the exact equivocation, scope shift or modal slide), cost (one sentence on what you must give up or explain if you reject that step).
