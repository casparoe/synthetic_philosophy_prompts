I write a long-form column on quantification for a magazine that covers science and ideas, and my current piece is about what happens to a number once people start aiming at it. The essay part I can write myself. What I cannot find, and what I would like you to build, is the instrument: a way for a reader who has never heard of Goodhart's law to take a real metric from their own institution and work out what, exactly, has gone wrong with it.

I have read the standard four-way typology that every explainer reproduces, and I do not want it. It sorts failure modes by their underlying mechanism and it is fine as far as it goes, but my readers are not asking a mechanism question. They are asking a differential-diagnosis question: this number used to track something and now it does not, so which kind of thing has happened, and what do I check to confirm it. So I want the tree built around distinctions that do diagnostic work rather than around the ones that are conventional to draw.

Here are five candidate branch questions, drawn from what I keep seeing in reporting on hospital waiting-time targets, school accountability scores, conservation payments, reward modelling in machine learning, and research assessment. Please use them as branch nodes, collapse any that turn out not to be independent of the others (saying which, and why), and replace any that do not actually separate cases:

1. Where is the damage located? When the number is hit, is the underlying thing worse, or is the thing unchanged or even improved while only the number's informativeness about it has gone?
2. Who can move faster? Can the party being measured revise its behaviour more quickly than the measurer can revise the measure, or does the measurer hold the faster loop?
3. Is the number wired to a cliff or to a gradient? Does it trigger a threshold with discontinuous consequences, or does it feed a smoothly graded payoff?
4. Does the activity have a description independent of the number? Is the measure merely diagnostic of a practice that could in principle be specified another way, or has the measure come to partly constitute what the institution means by the practice?
5. What channel does the response take? Is it a redirection of a roughly fixed budget of effort or capital among activities, or does it involve degrading the measurement channel itself, so that the measure is attacked rather than merely served?

Please build the tree from the logical space first: take three or four of these questions, enumerate the combinations of answers they permit, and only then place the named failure modes into the cells. For the combinations nobody occupies, give me a one-line diagnosis of why: incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored. Where two named failure modes land in the same cell and genuinely cannot be separated by these questions, say so explicitly rather than inventing a further distinction to pry them apart; I would rather publish an honest note about the limits of the instrument than a tidy-looking tree.

I want eight to ten leaves. Among them there must be at least one for threshold-hugging, one for cream-skimming or effort substitution, one for direct attack on the measurement channel, one for the case where the practice gets redefined around the measure so that improving the number really does improve the thing as the institution now conceives it, one for decay of the proxy at the extremes rather than anywhere else, and — this matters to me — at least one leaf for a measure that was made a target and survived, with a statement of what the tree implies must have been true for it to survive.

For each leaf give me three things. First, the observable signature: what a reader would see in the data or in the behaviour of the people being measured that distinguishes this leaf from its siblings, stated as something they could go and check on Monday morning, such as bunching just short of a cutoff, collapse of variance, or drift between the metric and some unaudited second measure. Second, one sentence on the most awkward commitment carried by that diagnosis, meaning what someone who reaches this leaf has to swallow. Third, the one thing that, if it were true, would move the case to a different leaf.

Alongside the tree I want a ten-question sidebar keyed to the same leaves, so that a reader can run their own metric through it. Every question must be about a concrete scenario and answerable by someone with no vocabulary for any of this: no question may ask the reader to classify, name, or endorse a doctrine. Give me a scoring rule that maps answer patterns onto leaves, and tell me where the scoring rule will disagree with the tree.

Then apply the whole instrument to two of my cases. The first should be one where it gives a clean verdict. The second should be one you genuinely find ambiguous between two leaves, chosen by you, with an explanation of which further piece of evidence would settle it and why the tree cannot settle it on its own.

Treat the cases below as stylized and supplied by me: do not correct them, do not add real-world figures, and do not reach for any source material. I have no use for citations, reading lists, or bibliographies, so please supply none. Where an answer would depend on a fact you cannot get from this prompt, flag the dependence instead of filling it in.

The cases, in the form I have them: a hospital emergency department judged on the share of patients seen within four hours, where patients are held in ambulances before the clock starts; a school whose test scores rise for three years while an unaudited assessment administered by a different body stays flat; a conservation payment made per verified animal carcass; a reward model used to train a system, whose score climbs while human raters judge the outputs worse; a journal-level citation measure used in hiring and promotion decisions; an offset protocol under which the counterfactual baseline is set by the party seeking credit for the reduction; a police force measured on the share of reported crimes cleared; a welfare-to-work contractor paid per job placement; a hand-hygiene compliance audit conducted at announced times; and a field whose citation counts inflate after a norm of reciprocal self-citation spreads.

Aim for roughly twelve hundred words, in a register I can lift into print without heavy editing: prefer the tree, the leaf table, the quiz key, and the two worked applications, in that order.
