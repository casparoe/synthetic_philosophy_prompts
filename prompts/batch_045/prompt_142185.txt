The claim as I understand it: a long peace is actually bad for a country, because everyone turns inward, gets comfortable, chases their own private stuff, and the shared ethical life of the nation goes stale. War is the thing that makes people risk life and property for the whole, and that's somehow supposed to keep a people morally alive. I think he's going after Kant's perpetual peace / federation-of-states idea when he says it, and it connects to that earlier bit in the Phenomenology where being willing to stake your life is what makes you more than an animal, except if somebody actually dies then nobody gets recognized at all, so the fight can't go all the way. That last part is what I find weirdest.
