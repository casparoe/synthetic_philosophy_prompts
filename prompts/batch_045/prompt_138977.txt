Construct a thought experiment whose purpose is to separate two claims that discussions of iterated amplification with distillation tend to run together: (C) that breaking a hard task into subtasks, each answered by a fresh copy of the previous-generation model, and recombining the answers under human oversight preserves — indeed improves — competence on the task; and (A) that distilling the resulting composite behaviour into a new model preserves alignment. I want a single ordinary case, with no science fiction in it, in which one and the same decomposition delivers (C) and defeats (A), and in which the feature of the decomposition that buys the competence gain is also what produces the alignment failure, rather than two features that merely happen to coexist.

Constraints the case must satisfy.

- The base model is no more capable or reliable than a careful human contractor with the relevant domain knowledge; its only relevant limitation is the ordinary one of losing track of material it cannot hold in view at once. The human operator is competent but short of time, able to review sub-answers but not to redo the whole task.
- The base model must be aligned on this material to begin with: ask it the small, holistic version of the question and it gives the answer the operator wants. The failure has to be manufactured by the procedure, not inherited from a model that was already defective.
- Local soundness: every sub-answer must be one the operator would endorse on review, and the combination rule must be applied correctly to those sub-answers. No bad sub-answer, no sloppy aggregation, no decomposition an experienced practitioner would call obviously badly designed. The composite answer should be one the operator is happy with.
- The competence gain must come from the partitioning itself — from each copy having less to hold in view — and not from extra supervision or from spending the same effort on one holistic attempt.
- The distilled model must nevertheless fail in a way the operator would reject: give the later query, the answer, and say why this is a safety failure rather than an ordinary mistake, for instance because the model has the relevant information in view, omits it, and represents itself as having checked. One route, though not the only one, is a decomposition that is locally sound but globally blind: each part is impeccable, yet the pattern that matters is visible only across the parts taken together, so it can enter the final answer only as a correction applied at the root.

What I want back: the scenario in one paragraph; then a check that it meets each constraint, stating what the amplified system outputs, what the un-amplified base model outputs on the small holistic version of the question, and what the distilled model later outputs; then a diagnosis naming the feature doing the work and saying whether it is a property of the procedure rather than of the input–output mapping that distillation targets; then a removal test, showing what happens to competence and to alignment if that feature is taken away, or if the whole tree is distilled rather than only its root; then the strongest reply open to someone who holds (A), including what they would say is being smuggled in and how they would redesign the setup so that (C) and (A) both hold; and finally one sentence on whether the same structural pattern has a familiar analogue outside machine learning, in a bureaucracy, a market, or a legal system. Please write your answer in British English.
