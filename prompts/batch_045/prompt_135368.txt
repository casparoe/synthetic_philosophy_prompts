i run a 75-minute workshop for about 18 first-years on a physics-for-philosophers course — they've met entropy as "disorder" and nothing more formal than that. i need a card game where they play demons running single-molecule (szilard-style) engines and feel for themselves why information isn't free fuel.

rough setup, fix it if its broken: pairs get a shuffled deck of L/R cards (which side of the box the molecule is on). each round: draw a card, you may look at it, then insert the partition on that side and collect 1 work-token. but every mark you make on your tally sheet has to be erased by end of shift, 1 token a mark, sheet handed back blank — so at 50/50 it comes out dead even. shift two: decks are 70/30, and students may write their tally in any code they like, so they can compress it, and now extraction beats erasure and they turn a profit. the decks are built by the other half of the room, the "preparers," who pay out of their own budget to bias them. i suspect the minimum cost of lopsiding a deck comes out to exactly the demon's extra profit — check me on that, and price it so it does.

what i want: rules and payoffs, what people's hands are actually doing, a timeline for the 75 minutes, and a debrief that maps specific moments onto the physics and lands on the demon's profit being real but already paid for by somebody. plus what will predictably go wrong (someone will refuse to write anything down, surely, and the compression rule will get gamed) and which part of the argument a card game just can't hand them and i'll have to say out loud. commit to actual numbers — deck size, rounds, token prices — not a menu of options. under 600 words.
