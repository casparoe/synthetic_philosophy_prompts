I'm writing the framing doc for a first-year seminar I'm co-teaching with someone from earth science, and the topic is the Anthropocene as a historical period. Every session collapses into the same two fights: when did it start, and who gets to decide. I dont want another list of candidate start dates — I want the spec first. Before we look at any proposed boundary, what does a periodization like this have to get right in order to be any good?

The purpose is fixed and it's an awkward one: whatever scheme we adopt has to do three jobs at once. Earth scientists need to be able to correlate archives with it, historians need to be able to teach it as a period with causes and characters, and people use it in arguments about who bears responsibility for what's happening. So flag whenever a requirement is only a requirement because of one of those jobs, and say which job wins when they pull apart.

Some things I want the list to deal with rather than hand-wave:

- the "start" of something that arrives at different times in different places and different archives, so the boundary may be a surface rather than a line
- the gap between the sharpest detectable global marker and the thing we actually think is driving the change
- whether the period is an event with a date or a condition we're now in, since if it's a condition then "start" looks like the wrong question and you want something like irreversibility instead
- the fact that the preceding period isn't a clean baseline either, so a contrastive definition may not get any grip
- threshold boundaries drifting as our instruments get better, which pushes a "first detectable departure" earlier and earlier
- the name itself doing causal and moral work, so the question of who the "we" is can't be treated as separable from the dating question
- the three different questions hiding inside "who decides": who has the standing to declare it, whose uptake makes it real, and who eats the cost if it's wrong — plus the fact that a periodization can fail one test and still do its political work fine

Give me seven to nine requirements, each with the case that motivates it in one sentence, marked negotiable or non-negotiable. Then tell me which pair of your requirements you think can't be jointly satisfied — I have a suspicion about the sharpest-marker one versus the tracks-the-cause one, but you pick — and which single requirement rules out the most of the familiar candidate boundaries (mid-20th century, Industrial Revolution, the 1600s CO2 dip, early agriculture). You dont need exact years, citations, or a full scorecard for each candidate, just enough to show the list bites. Keep the whole thing under 650 words; a table is fine.
