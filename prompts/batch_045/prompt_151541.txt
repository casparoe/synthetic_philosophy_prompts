ok so I'm trying to write down where I've actually landed on indifference priors, and I want to know whether the package hangs together before I build anything on it. Here's where I am:

(1) the principle of indifference is basically fine: if my evidence is symmetric across the possibilities, spread credence evenly.
(2) Bertrand's chord paradox isn't a counterexample to it. 1/3, 1/2 and 1/4 are the answers to three different well-posed questions (endpoint method, radius method, midpoint-in-the-disc method); the original question is just ill-posed, so nothing contradicts.
(3) once I'm told the actual randomising mechanism, that mechanism settles it — its symmetries fix the distribution, and indifference over parametrisations drops out entirely (the Jaynes move, basically).
(4) but I still want indifference to hand me a number in cases where there's no mechanism at all, and the way I try to get one is by demanding invariance under redescription: "ratio of wine to water is between 1/3 and 3" is the same problem as "ratio of water to wine is between 1/3 and 3", so I require the prior to survive r → 1/r, and out comes 1/2.
(5) I also refuse to go imprecise. I want one number out of all this, not a set of priors or a range — reporting a range feels like giving up.
