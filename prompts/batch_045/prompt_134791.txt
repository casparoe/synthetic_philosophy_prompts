hey — I'm one of the two students on my school's Policy Committee (me, one other senior, three teachers, and the VP) and we're voting thursday on something I can't make up my mind about, so I want to circulate a short written opinion before the meeting.

Here's the situation. Right now late work is a free for all: every teacher invents their own rule. One takes anything anytime, one docks a letter grade per day, one refuses late work entirely and it's caused actual screaming in the hallway. So two teachers wrote the "Uniform Deadline Policy": same rule in every class, 10% off per day, nothing accepted after three days, no exceptions for any reason, except that students with a 504 or IEP on file get their own extended timeline worked out with the counselor. They're pitching it as fairness — right now your grade depends on which teacher you randomly got — and also as preparation, since deadlines are supposedly real in college and at jobs.

I read somewhere about researchers comparing "tight" and "loose" cultures: tight ones have more rules and punish breaking them harder, and the story is that places with more historical threats (disease, invasions, famine, crowding) ended up tighter because everyone had to coordinate. I don't know if that's actually true and I don't need it sourced. But I do like that it suggests there are separate dials here: how many rules there are, how harsh the penalty is, how much discretion the person enforcing has, and how visible the enforcement is. My sense is this proposal cranks up harshness and visibility while leaving the number-of-rules dial where it is, and I'm not sure that mix is what anyone would pick on purpose.

Two other things I keep chewing on. First: when a rule gets very strict and very easy to check, people start aiming at the rule instead of at whatever the rule was for — like shoving in something garbage at 11:59 so you don't lose the 10%. Is that a real pattern or am I inventing it? Second: the 504 carve-out. Everyone can tell who the accommodated kids are because they're the only ones handing things in late, and they already get enough grief about it, and it seems like the tighter the rule is for everyone else the worse that gets.

So please draft the opinion I'd send the committee. It should land on one concrete recommendation — adopt it, kill it, or adopt it as a one-term pilot with conditions — with a time frame and something we could actually check at the end of the term to decide whether to keep it (I was thinking we disaggregate who's getting penalized: kids with jobs, kids who take care of siblings, kids who travel for sports). It has to take the teachers' argument seriously, especially the part about it being unfair that your grade depends on which teacher you got, because I think that's genuinely the best thing on their side and I can't just wave it away. It should also say plainly what would count as the policy failing, and flag anything in it that's really a values call the committee has to make rather than a factual question.

Keep it around 700-900 words, something a teacher will actually read on a wednesday night, and please don't put in citations or quotations from research papers — I can't look them up properly and the VP will just make a face at me.
