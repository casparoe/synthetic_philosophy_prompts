(3) But I also want to keep what people call the experience requirement — nothing can be intrinsically good or bad for someone unless it could in principle make some difference to what their life is like from the inside.

I'd take that over a decent-but-ordinary real life, and I don't think choosing it would make me pathetic or irrational.

(6) Case verdict: the "crucible" variant my seminar tutor invented. The world is simulated, but the machine can't rig your choices: you really don't know what you'll do, you can genuinely fail, the effort of deliberating is real, and by any internal measure you really do act bravely and really do become the sort of person who would. The only thing missing is that the stakes aren't real — nobody is actually in danger. I still wouldn't plug in, and the reason I'd give is that the bravery isn't the real thing because the stakes aren't.
