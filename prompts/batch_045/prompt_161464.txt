The subject I am writing about is the Nordic model and whether it can be exported. As I understand the argument space, there are at least four moves to be translated:

1. The package or equilibrium view: the high tax take and broad tax base, universalistic rather than means-tested benefits, high union density with coordinated wage bargaining, compressed wage distributions, extensive public provision of childcare and eldercare, high employment — especially among women — and active labour-market policy combined with relatively light employment protection ("flexicurity") are not a menu but a mutually reinforcing system, held in place by high interpersonal trust, high tax morale, a legible and comparatively uncorrupt state, and a long historical path.
2. The portability view: the components are separable instruments, and what travels is the incentive and insurance design, not any cultural disposition; a country can adopt the childcare-and-activation parts without acquiring Nordic trust, and get employment and poverty effects anyway.
3. The selection sceptic: cross-country comparisons are confounded — small scale, relative ethnic and religious homogeneity, resource rents, favourable demography and terms of trade, and above all a prior history of state capacity that caused both the institutions and the outcomes.
4. The arrow question: universalism may *produce* trust, by making everyone a stakeholder in the same services and by avoiding the stigma and poverty traps of means-testing, rather than merely *requiring* a population that was already trusting.
