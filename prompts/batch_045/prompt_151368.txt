Last night I argued for three hours with a friend about a distinction she uses constantly and has never quite defined: the contrast between cranes and skyhooks, and the companion charge of greedy reductionism (Dennett's vocabulary, as she uses it). She has a long track record of rulings on individual explanations and I came away unable to tell which rule she is actually applying. We are now refereeing the same paper, and I would like to be able to anticipate her rather than discover her verdict in the margin of a draft.

Her rulings that I can remember, stated as neutrally as I can:

- The adaptive fit of the vertebrate eye explained by cumulative selection on heritable variation: a crane, and an exemplary one.
- The origin of life explained by a self-organizing chemical process that nobody has yet identified: not a skyhook. She called it an honest promissory note, a crane-shaped gap, and said the speaker had earned it by flagging the hole rather than papered over it.
- The origin of the eye explained by an intelligent designer who wanted seeing creatures: a skyhook.
- The organisation of living matter explained by a vital force: a skyhook.
- Mozart's output explained by his genius: a skyhook.
- A model's new ability to carry out multi-step arithmetic explained by the claim that scale gave it a general capacity for abstract reasoning: a skyhook.
- A model's success at translation explained by the claim that it understands French: a skyhook.
- A thermostat's holding a room at twenty degrees explained by its wanting the room at twenty: not a skyhook, harmless, because the wanting can be cashed out on the spot in a bimetallic strip.
- An ant colony's foraging efficiency explained by the colony's aiming to maximise net energy intake: not a skyhook, though she insisted that one should be able in principle to retell it as individual rules plus selection.
- A firm's survival explained by its strong culture: not a skyhook; a real higher-level cause, but untested as stated.
- The meaning of a word explained as nothing but a pattern of neural firing: greedy reductionism.
- Mendelian inheritance explained as nothing but base-pair chemistry: good reductionism, not greedy.
- Roman military success explained by the tensile strength of iron: not greedy, merely partial, one contributing factor pitched below the level of the question.
- A model's apparent reasoning explained as nothing but matrix multiplications and gradient descent: greedy.

The case now in front of us. The paper describes an assistant model that reliably declines a class of harmful requests: reliably across rephrasings, reliably in framings held out of training, and in a way that tracks the reasons given in the prompt rather than surface features. The authors' own explanation is that the model has internalised a value of non-maleficence. Later, in a footnote meant to reassure the metaphysicians, they add that there is of course no value anywhere inside the system, only a disposition to emit refusal-shaped continuations, produced by fine-tuning on human preference labels, and that value talk is a convenient shorthand for that disposition.

What I want from you:

First, on the value explanation. Will she call it a skyhook, a crane, or the admissible-but-unfilled promissory note she allowed in the origin-of-life case? I can see at least two rules behind her record. One is a no-uncraned-explainers rule: a skyhook is an item to which there is no route from below, where the speaker neither supplies that route nor gestures at one, and none is available in principle; this is what convicts the designer, the vital force, genius, emergent reasoning and understanding-French, and what acquits the unidentified chemical process, the thermostat and the colony. The other is a no-free-lunch or homunculus rule: a skyhook is an item credited with the very capacity being explained, or with one of the same or higher grade; this convicts the same cases by a different route, since a designer has the power to design eyes, a vital force has the power to organise, and a general capacity for reasoning just is the ability it was invoked to explain. These two rules fit everything of hers that I can recall, but they come apart here: there is a perfectly respectable route from below to the model's behaviour, namely preference fine-tuning, so the first rule should let the value talk pass as an incomplete crane, while the second should convict it, since having internalised a value is a normative, reason-responsive, generalising capacity that looks like strictly more than the observed behaviour paid for. There may be a third rule that fits her record as well as these two and that I have not thought of. I should say that I have already discarded one candidate: a flat ban on teleological or goal-directed explainers is refuted by her ruling on the ant colony.

Second, on the footnote. Will she call that gloss greedy reductionism, good reductionism, or merely partial, as with the Roman iron? Her greedy verdicts also seem to me to fit more than one rule: one keyed to whether the lower-level story can recover the higher-level generalisation that does the predictive work, and one keyed to whether the lower-level story is eliminative, denying the reality of the higher-level item rather than explaining it. These appear to agree on all four of her reductionism verdicts and to diverge here, depending on how richly the disposition in question is specified.

Third, and this is what I most want: for each axis, give me your best guess at her verdict with your reasons for preferring one rule to the others, and then describe, concretely rather than abstractly, the further case whose outcome would settle which rule she is running — a case where the candidate rules disagree and where her answer would therefore be diagnostic. I would also be grateful if you did not spend the answer restating the crane–skyhook distinction, which I already have; I am after the structure of her verdicts, not the vocabulary.
