I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums since the Sequences and follow the Alignment Forum closely, and I now sit on the value-inclusion subgroup of a small AI alignment project — the kind of working group that tries to turn blog-level intuition into an actual spec. Our remit is narrow but consequential: we have to say whose values go into the aggregator that a hypothetical aligned system is supposed to serve. We are not asking how to aggregate or weigh values once they are in; we are asking only about inclusion, i.e. whose values count as inputs at all.

So far we have ruled on seven cases:

1. A neurotypical adult human: included.
2. A six-year-old human child: included, but only the values she would endorse under ideal reflection as an adult — we apply an ideal-reflection extrapolation of the kind discussed under the heading of Coherent Extrapolated Volition, rather than her current desires.
3. A human who will live in 2200: included, with no discount for temporal distance.
4. A pig: excluded.
5. A legally incorporated company: excluded.
6. A human in a permanent vegetative state, with no cortical function and no prospect of recovery: excluded.
7. A simple digital agent that has valenced states and learns from reinforcement, but has no self-model and cannot reflect on or revise its own ends: excluded.

We have not yet ruled on the case now in front of us:

The destructive-upload em. A biological human adult undergoes a destructive whole-brain emulation procedure: the biological brain is scanned and destroyed, and a functionally identical mind is instantiated on a server. The resulting em has full psychological continuity with the pre-procedure person, is fully reflective, can model and revise its own ends, and has new valenced experiences from the moment of instantiation. The biological organism is gone; the em is all that remains of that person.

What should we decide if we want to be consistent with our own precedents, and what will we most likely decide? I am less interested in the bare answer than in the structure of the reasoning. Specifically:

1. What are the candidate inclusion rules that fit all seven precedents? I can see at least three: a substrate-independent personhood rule (include any being capable of reflecting on and revising a conception of the good); a biological-humanity rule (include only biologically human organisms that have, or would normally develop, a human-like mind); and a psychological-continuity rule (include any being whose psychology is continuous with that of a biologically human organism, counting the destructive em as the continuant of the original person). Identify any others that fit, and say what each predicts about the em.
2. Which of the seven precedents is the closest analog to the em, and which is the closest negative analog?
3. Give your best guess about what the subgroup decides, with the reasons for preferring one rule over the others, and say honestly whether our precedents underdetermine the choice.
4. If we decide either way, what principle are we adopting? In particular, one member invokes a version of liberal neutrality — the idea that the aggregator should stay neutral not only between competing conceptions of the good but also between competing metaphysical theories of personal identity — and argues that this tells against the biological rule (which privileges a biological kind), against the continuity rule (which forces us to pick a theory of what makes a later being the same person), and in favor of the personhood rule (which is indifferent to both substrate and identity). Assess that argument.
5. Name the further case that would best tell the surviving rules apart. I have in mind two candidates: the branch upload, a copy instantiated while the biological original survives, and the from-scratch artificial agent that develops reflective values without ever being continuous with a human. Say which is most diagnostic and why.

Please reason about the track record rather than about the metaphysics of uploads in the abstract, and do not give me a bibliography, reading list, or citations — I want your analysis of the precedents themselves.
