I'm a novelist rather than a classicist, and I'm midway through a novel set in a fictional maritime republic that assigns nearly every office by lot and forbids anyone to hold an office twice; one of my characters has to defend that constitution out loud, and I want her speech to be an argument and not a decoration, so I've been modelling it on Otanes in Herodotus, and especially on this sentence: "But the rule of the multitude has in the first place the loveliest name of all, equality, and does in the second place none of the things that a monarch does. It determines offices by lot, and holds power accountable, and conducts all deliberating publicly. Therefore I give my opinion that we make an end of monarchy and exalt the multitude, for all things are possible for the majority." The joint I can't get straight is why lot, accountability, and open deliberation arrive as one package rather than as three separable reforms: is there a genuine argument that selection by lot is what makes strict retrospective accountability possible, on the grounds that an elected officer can always meet a charge by pointing to the mandate that put him there, whereas a man who drew the office has nothing to point to but the record of his term? I also want to know what work "equality" is doing here — equality before the law, or an equal turn at ruling and being ruled — and whether the lot serves one reading, or whether the ambiguity is carrying the argument. Then a question specific to my fiction: since my constitution forbids a second term, so that no one can be returned or turned out, does "holds power accountable" have to collapse into purely retrospective sanction — audit, fine, exile — and does that help or hurt the case for the lot relative to election? Finally, do you read the closing claim, that all things are possible for the majority, as sitting oddly beside the lot, which deliberately ignores what the majority wants about who should rule? Please answer in continuous prose, the way one writes a letter: no headings, bullets, bold, or tables, and no bibliography or citations, since I have no library here and want the reasoning itself.
