I'm writing the term paper for a final-year seminar on the epistemology of logic and I want to pressure-test my central thesis before I commit to it. We have spent two weeks on Lewis Carroll's 1895 dialogue in *Mind* in which the Tortoise grants the premises of a trivial argument but withholds the conclusion, and then grants, one after another, each further hypothetical Achilles writes into his notebook while still withholding the conclusion, so that the list of premises grows without end. My supervisor has asked us to argue from the structure of the puzzle rather than from the secondary literature, and I have no way to check that literature from where I'm writing, so please reason from the case itself: I don't want a reading list, a bibliography, or an argument that rests on who is supposed to have said what.

The thesis I want a number on, call it T, is this: what drives the regress is not a deficit in the justification available for any single transition from premises to conclusion, but the absence of anything in one performance of that transition that binds the reasoner to go on performing it in the further cases the rule covers. On this reading, the Tortoise's power to grant every new hypothetical while withholding the conclusion is only the observation that accepting premises does not by itself settle how anyone will go on; the dialogue is then a local instance of the problem of how a finite performance can be answerable to a rule, not a special problem about deduction.

Let me say what would settle it, so the number has something to be about. Count T as correct if the considerations capable of stopping Carroll's regress form a subset of the considerations that settle how a performance is answerable to a rule — that is, if nothing stops the regress locally, at the level of the single inference, without also saying something about the reasoner's going on. Count T as incorrect if some consideration stops the regress entirely at the level of the single inference and owes nothing to any account of projection, practice, or rule-following.

I would like your credence in T as a single number, but the number interests me less than the decomposition. Please give me, each with a number and one line of justification: the probability that the Tortoise's stance is an intelligible position at all rather than masked incomprehension of the conditional or a performative inconsistency (if it is unintelligible, the regress is a pseudo-problem and T gets no support from it); the probability that some meaning-constitutive claim about the logical constants is true, that is, that grasping the conditional partly consists in being disposed to infer in accordance with it; the probability, conditional on that claim being true, that it stops the regress; the probability that a distinct regress reappears even for a reasoner with full mastery of the connectives, concerning whether the rule grasped is the rule that applies to the present case; the probability that any adequate response must invoke something non-propositional — a practice, a disposition, a skill, a norm — rather than another premise; and whatever further term you think belongs. Then show me how the terms combine, note which of them are conditional on others rather than independent, and give me the resulting number.

After that: tell me which single term your final number is most sensitive to, and what argument or consideration would move that term furthest. I am at about 0.7 on T and a friend in the seminar is at about 0.35; on your decomposition, which of those terms is the one we are most likely disagreeing about? And flag any term where you think your number is a temperamental bet rather than the output of an argument.

One more thing, because it is the case I think actually decides the paper. Consider a variant I have been calling the Serial Tortoise. This Tortoise does not refuse the inference: he infers Z from A and B straight away, and would do so again if asked. What he refuses is the projection. He says that nothing he has done or accepted entitles him, or anyone else, to say that he will or ought to infer the corresponding conclusion tomorrow, or in the next case Achilles puts to him, or in the infinitely many cases the rule covers; each case is a fresh act, and if Achilles wants him bound, Achilles should write down a premise saying that he is. When Achilles writes a universally quantified premise covering all such cases, the Tortoise grants it and asks for a premise to the effect that this new case falls under it — which is another premise, and the list grows again. My question is whether this is the same regress wearing different clothes or a different one, and I want a number on that too. Then take four responses — that rules of inference are not the kind of thing that can be a premise, because they are not truth-apt; that understanding the constants just is having the relevant inferential dispositions; that deduction is vindicated pragmatically by its role rather than validated by any argument; and that the demand for a justification of deduction is itself defective — and give me a probability for each that it silences the Serial Tortoise, and a note on which of them, if any, silences him locally rather than by supplying a general story about going on.

Please don't open by telling me that it depends on what I mean by justification or by rule. I want your numbers, your decomposition, and the places where you can feel the argument carrying less weight than the figure suggests.
