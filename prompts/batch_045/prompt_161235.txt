I have been using Daniel Dennett's four kinds of minds as a sorting device — Darwinian creatures, whose only route to improved design runs through generations; Skinnerian creatures, which can be reshaped within a lifetime by the actual consequences of their own actions; Popperian creatures, which can screen candidate actions against an internal model, so that rejected candidates die in their stead; and Gregorian creatures (named for the psychologist Richard Gregory), which have had their own cognitive machinery reorganized by informational tools, above all words, that they neither evolved nor arrived at by trial and error — and I now have enough verdicts that I should be able to say what principle generates them. I cannot.

My tentative principle has two clauses. (1) A creature counts as Popperian just in case some of its action-selection is mediated by a representation of an outcome it is not at that moment bringing about. (2) A creature counts as Gregorian just in case some of its selection machinery has been reorganized by a tool that carries information, that it did not build by its own trial and error, and that operates on its own cognitive operations rather than merely on the world.

The cases, with my verdicts:

1. The digger wasp of the classic anecdote, which re-enacts its prey-dragging routine when the prey is moved a few inches. Darwinian, and I hold this even though the behaviour looks intricate. Assume for the sake of the case that the rigidity is as described; I have been told the anecdote may be embellished, and I do not want the empirical question doing any work.
2. A pigeon in an operant chamber. Skinnerian, unproblematically.
3. A rat at a maze junction that rears, sniffs both arms, and only then commits. Popperian, or incipiently so.
4. A parrot that has learned to produce English labels and to answer simple questions about coloured objects. Skinnerian producing verbal behaviour, not Gregorian: the words are what it emits, not tools it uses to reorganize its own search.
5. A chimpanzee that selects and trims a twig to fish for termites. Skinnerian-plus, not Gregorian: the twig extends what the chimpanzee can do to the world but not, as far as I can tell, how it searches its own options.
6. A two-year-old human, who can do almost nothing interesting with the words it has absorbed. Gregorian already, because the words are doing the reorganizing, not the child.
7. The Japanese macaque tradition of washing food before eating it, and comparable transmitted food preferences in rats. Culturally transmitted, but not Gregorian.
8. A chess engine trained by self-play that searches at inference time. Skinnerian in training, Popperian at play — and I do not know which slot the taxonomy assigns, since it has one slot per creature.
9. A large language model pretrained on next-token prediction, tuned with reinforcement from human feedback, and prompted to reason in steps. I have three verdicts I cannot hold together: it is Gregorian, because everything it can do was installed by the mind-tools of a culture and it operates entirely in words; it is not Popperian, because nothing is at stake in its trials and it has no model of a world its tokens could be wrong about; and its step-by-step reasoning looks exactly like Popperian generate-and-test, except that what discards the discarded candidates is a distribution learned from text rather than any confrontation with outcomes.
10. An ant colony, and a slime mold navigating a maze. Something Darwinian or Skinnerian at the level of the collective, and no new kind of mind.
11. A scientific community. Popperian at the institutional level, though every member is Gregorian — which makes me suspect the kinds are properties of processes and levels and that my creature-level sorting is only a convenience.
12. An adult who has lost language to a stroke but retains problem-solving. Still Gregorian, I want to say, because words made them Gregorian and the installed machinery survives the loss of the tools. My clause (2) as written seems to require present possession, so it may have to go.

The pairs I cannot reconcile are 3 against 4, where I credit a covert trial on behavioural grounds a behaviourist could model as conditioning while refusing to credit a mechanism on grounds of verbal behaviour that looks like reasoning; and 8 against 9, where I grant Popperian status to a search conducted against a model of a real game with real wins and losses while withholding it from a search with the same surface architecture but no world.

What I want from you: state a principle, or two or three candidates, precise enough to be applied, with the case that motivates each clause; say which of my verdicts each candidate cannot accommodate and whether the simpler candidate that overturns one is worth the verdict it costs; and give verdicts on cases I have not listed — a beehive's collective decision mediated by the waggle dance, a market price system, an AI agent trained in a rich simulated world with model-based planning but no physical body, and an adult taught a first language having grown up without one. One last thing: I have been toying with splitting the Gregorian category into creatures whose machinery has been reorganized by mind-tools they merely receive, and creatures that can also manufacture new ones — a notation, a diagram type, a technical term — since the chimpanzee and the language model push me toward that distinction and Dennett's treatment of words as culturally evolving items pushes me away from it. Tell me whether that split is a genuine joint or an artefact of my own discomfort with case 9.
