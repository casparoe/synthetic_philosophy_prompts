I'm writing a paper on the odd fact that Nietzsche leans on "health" constantly and never says what it is. As I read him, the word does at least three different jobs, and I want a specification for a theory that could do all three at once:

1. criteriological — health functions as evidence about a doctrine or an institution: a philosophy is diagnosed as decadent by the physiology it expresses, so that the health of its adherents or of its author counts against it;
2. normative-ideal — in the book appended to The Gay Science in its 1887 second edition, "great health" is something one can have more or less of, and it is supposed to be worth having, not merely worth attributing;
3. regimen — in Ecce Homo and the late notebooks, health is the target of an almost comically quantitative first-person practice: what to eat and drink, how much sleep, which climate, how quickly one's digestion clears.

Write the desiderata an account of health would have to meet to serve as a standard for choosing and revising one's own regimen — first-personal, prospective, no physician, outcome unknown — while still being able to do the other two jobs. For each requirement give the case that motivates it and mark whether it is non-negotiable or a taste of one interpretive camp. I care most about three things:

- the asymmetry between the epistemic and the normative standing of illness: sickness seems to make Nietzsche a better diagnostician (he repeatedly credits his own suffering with the depth of his insight) while obviously not being something to aim at.
