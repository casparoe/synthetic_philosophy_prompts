Me and two other parents are helping rewrite the little genetics corner at our town science museum, the one aimed at eight-year-olds. My friend Priya wants every panel to say that DNA is a code, like a recipe or a written language, and she insists its not a metaphor at all, its literally what DNA is. I keep saying no: smoke means fire, but nobody thinks smoke is a sentence, and saying "code" feels like it smuggles in somebody deciding what things stand for. Then she said something I cant shake: the cell does contain machinery that reads the DNA and builds stuff from it, and that machinery is built to specs that are themselves written in the DNA, so the reader and the message are made of the same stuff and there's no one outside who picked the meanings. My brother, who writes software for a living, says of course its a code, a code is just a rule for turning one lot of symbols into another, end of story, which Priya loved but which sounds to me like he's changing the subject.

I tried to get clear on what I even think a code has to be, and the best test I could come up with is this: with a real code you could shuffle the pairings, swap which symbol goes with which outcome all the way through the system, and everything would still run, it would just say something different. By that test DNA looks like a code and smoke doesn't, which makes me feel like I've lost. But I also suspect my test is rigged, or that it only shows something much narrower than "literal language."

Whose side is closest here, and what should we actually print on the panels? Is there something I'm not seeing about what a code has to involve, about that business of the reader being written in the same stuff as the message, or about what my shuffling test really proves? Please answer in plain continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold text or tables, just write it out like your emailing me back.
