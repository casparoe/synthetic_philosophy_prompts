I have been arguing with a friend for two years about moral trade — the idea that agents whose terminal values differ can each end up better off by their own lights if each devotes part of their limited budget to advancing what the other values, exploiting something like comparative advantage across value functions. I am the enthusiast: I think the gains are large and unexploited, and that the obstacles are epistemic and institutional (verifying compliance, enforcing agreements, finding counterparties, thin markets) rather than moral. She is the skeptic: she thinks most of the apparent surplus is an artifact of modelling values as ordinary preference orderings over outcomes, that agents' values typically include what I would call complicity terms (disvalue attached to participating in a scheme that advances ends one regards as wrong), that such terms do not net out across a trade the way costs do in an ordinary market, that deontic side-constraints neither aggregate nor transfer to a counterparty, and that a counterparty's values are in any case unobservable, since one sees bargaining behaviour rather than compliance, so the size of the supposed surplus is not measurable even in principle.

Draft the joint statement the two of us would produce from a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Include: the claims both of us can sign, including about the static case where both parties' values are fixed, known, and contain no complicity terms; the residual disagreements, each stated in wording its own side would accept; the cruxes — I expect one to be how large complicity terms are in real agents, one to be whether compliance in a thin market can be made legible at acceptable cost, and one to be whether trades struck with counterparties whose values will drift, including one's own future self, impose option-value losses that swamp the static gains; and for each crux a concrete case or argument both of us would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Say explicitly which parts of our dispute, if any, are merely verbal, and for each side note what would count as evidence against its own view.

Format: a numbered agreed-claims section, followed by a table whose columns are crux, enthusiast's wording, skeptic's wording, agreed test or case, enthusiast's prediction, skeptic's prediction. Keep the whole document under 700 words.
