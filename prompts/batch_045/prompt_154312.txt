i TA for a first-year course thats officially called Reasoning Under Uncertainty but is really just a tour of epistemic paradoxes, and I want to slip a poem onto the last handout of term as a goodbye present. We did the surprise exam announcement last week and nobody in the seminar was happy with any of the exits, so I got thinking a sestina might be the right shape for it: the end-words keep coming back in a new order no matter what you do to them, which is roughly what happens to the five days of the week once you start crossing them out. So: a full sestina, six six-line stanzas plus the three-line envoi, unrhymed, standard rotation (each stanza takes the previous stanza's end-words in the order 6,1,5,2,4,3), and the six end-words have to be Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday and known. Inflect them if you have to but the day-words have to stay recognisably the day-words, and I want the envoi to use all six and land on known as its very last word. Aim for something like ten syllables a line, but don't let the meter iron everything flat.

Write it in the voice of one of the students, on the evening of the day the exam actually happened (make it Wednesday, so the argument has had time to run and the surprise can still land), addressed to the teacher who wrote the announcement on the board. The philosophy has to actually be right, not gestured at. I want the elimination done properly and in order: Friday goes first, because if Friday morning arrived with no exam yet the class could work out that it had to be Friday; but once Friday is struck out Thursday becomes the last possible day and goes the same way, and so on down; by Sunday night theyve concluded there will be no exam at all. And the surprise on Wednesday morning has to be shown as genuine and as coming from exactly that — they aren't surprised because they forgot or because they were sloppy, they're surprised because they trusted their own proof, which is what makes the announcement true and unusable at the same time. Please don't solve it. No closing move where the announcement turns out to be self-referential nonsense, no tidy couplet about knowledge versus certainty, no moral. People who work on this don't agree and my instructor doesn't want the handout picking a side either.

One more thing to get in somewhere, maybe the fourth or fifth stanza wherever the rotation lets you: my flatmate, who isn't in the class, said the whole machine only runs because the teacher promised exactly one exam. If the announcement had been at most one, and maybe none, then on Friday morning with no exam yet there'd still be two live possibilities, no exam at all and exam today, so Friday could never be ruled out and the induction would never get a first day to bite on. That sounds right to me and I'd like the poem to notice it, but as a cost rather than as a way out — the class buys back consistency only by giving up any reason to expect an exam. Also, and this is the bit I'll judge hardest: someone who has never heard of any of this should still be able to read it as a poem about a week they got wrong, so no jargon, no saying epistemic or base case or knowability out loud, and no leaning on the usual rhyme partners for known like shown and grown.
