I'm a TA for an intro philosophy of perception course and we did Molyneux's problem in section last week — you know the one: someone blind from birth who knows a cube and a sphere by touch, then gets their sight back, and we ask whether, before touching them, they can tell which is which. The class split almost exactly down the middle and I realised I couldn't articulate what the disagreement was actually about. Hoping you can help me get clearer.

Here's the thing I want compared. There seem to be two quite different ways of cashing out "can he tell which is which", and I think people in my section were quietly answering different questions. On the one hand, the explicit version: you put the two objects in front of him and ask him which is the cube, or ask him to hand you the cube, and you score him right or wrong on the answer. On the other hand, what I'll call the visuomotor version: you don't ask him anything at all, you just watch the way he reaches. Does his hand pre-shape for edges or for a curved surface as it comes in? Is his grip aperture, on the very first sighted reach, already scaled to the thing's real size rather than starting from a default and correcting on contact? Does his gaze go to the near edge first, the way a sighted person's does when they're about to take hold of a cube by its corners? The question there isn't whether he'd say it's the cube; it's whether what he learned by touch shows up in how his body moves toward it before it shows up in what he'd be willing to say.

So: compare those two operationalisations, properly. Which of them is the better test of whatever Molyneux's question was meant to test — and be explicit about what you think that underlying thesis even is, since I suspect half the confusion in my section is that it's ambiguous between something about concepts or ideas, something about conscious perceptual experience, and something about how the brain wires up cross-modal connections. Then the case I actually care about: suppose the two operationalisations come apart, reliably — implicit transfer within minutes of the bandages coming off, correct explicit identification only after weeks of using the new sense. What should we conclude from that? Would a dissociation like that settle the question, dissolve it, or just show that there were two questions all along? And I've got a seperate worry about a variant someone came up with in the section: a sighted person wearing one of those camera-to-tactile-vibration-on-the-back sensory substitution setups, someone who's used it long enough to navigate with it — does the cube as seen feel like the cube as felt through the array? I can't tell whether that's Molyneux's question in a new costume or a genuinely different one, and whether your verdict about explicit vs visuomotor carries over to it. Skip the 17th-century history and skip actual study results — I don't want citations, and I'd rather you didn't guess at the empirical literature — but do be concrete about what would count as evidence on either side, and do actually pick a side on which operationalisation is the right one.
