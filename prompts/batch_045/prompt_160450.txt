Me and a friend keep going in circles about whether pile-ons and deplatformings are really punishment. Give me a number: what's your credence that when a speaker is dropped from a conference lineup after a public outcry, with no formal finding that they did anything wrong, what happened to them is punishment in the sense that matters for whether they're owed procedural protections (notice, a real chance to answer, severity proportionate to the offense)? I'm around 0.6, she's around 0.25, and I suspect the gap lives in one sub-question.

Decompose it for me: does punishment require an agent who imposes the harm *because* the target is thought to have done wrong, or is it enough that the harm tracks perceived wrongdoing? Can losing something you had no entitlement to count as hard treatment at all? Does the organizer's motive matter — dropping the speaker because they think the view is harmful versus dropping them because sponsors threatened to walk, so the harm is really downstream of third-party pressure rather than the organizer's own censure? Is due process part of the concept of punishment or a separate constraint stacked on top of it? And add a term I've been calling "sanction drift": months later the original complaint has been forgotten or retracted but the person is still effectively unbookable — does that continuing condition count as ongoing punishment or just as the residue of past acts?

Rough probability per sub-question with a line of justification each, then the combination, then which term your final number is most sensitive to and what would actually move it. Please don't just say "it depends what you mean by punishment."
