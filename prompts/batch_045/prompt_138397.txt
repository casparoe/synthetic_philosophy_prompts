hey — I run a tiny reading group (six of us, Sunday afternoons) and we've been grinding through anatta and personal identity for about eight weeks. Next week's the last session and I'd rather end on a poem than on a summary, so im asking you to write one, and i have a fairly specific idea of what i want.

Write a sestina: 39 lines, six stanzas of six, then a three-line envoi. The six end-words should be the five aggregates plus one more that you choose. Use the Pali — rupa, vedana, sanna, sankhara, vinnana — and get them right, meaning: vinnana is not a soul and not a little observer standing behind the other four. In the tradition consciousness is dependently arisen, propped up by name-and-form the way two sheaves of reeds hold each other up, and that's the single thing I most don't want botched. The sixth word is your call but it has to earn its place (flame, chariot, tenant, whatever).

The reason I want a sestina specifically is that the rotation is supposed to be the argument: the same six words keep coming back, but never in the same arrangement, so the pattern persists while no position does. That's continuity without identity, which is roughly the claim about a person. If the last stanza can feel like the pattern is about to begin again, do that.

Voice: someone who has been argued into the no-self view, accepts it, and is suprised to find it consoling — but not a serene sage. She's caught on one snag: if there's no one over and above the aggregates, then nobody gets liberated either, and she wants to know for whose sake the path is practiced.

That snag has a sharper form I came up with in week five, and I'd like it in the poem somewhere. Call it the split-flame case. If a dying flame lights one candle, the tradition answers "neither the same flame nor a different one." But if the dying flame lights two candles at once, both descend from it, both carry the pattern forward, and neither is it. So what happens to the convention of a single person's story, and whose liberation is it? Don't resolve it — I want the reader to feel why "neither the same nor a different one" stops being a dodge the moment the flame splits. Also don't let the flame smuggle in a thing that travels; the point of the image is dependence, not a passenger.

Envoi: all six end-words, two per line, and that's where the liberation worry should land, unresolved.

Please write it in American English, no glossary, no author's notes after, and no explanatory asides in the poem — the doctrine has to be carried by the images and the form. Accuracy over prettiness, though i will be reading it out loud so it should scan in the mouth.
