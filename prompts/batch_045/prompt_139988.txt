I've been reading about sphexishness on my own (not for a class — I got into it after an argument with a friend who builds warehouse robots) and I think I basically get the setup, but the free will half of it still bugs me. The way I've got it: the digger wasp keeps re-running the same nest-checking routine if you nudge the cricket while she's inside, because her behaviour is wired to a fixed stimulus, so there's no real 'she' doing any choosing. My friend puts this in fancier words: any system whose behaviour is a deterministic function of its inputs and its internal state is sphexish in principle, the wasp is just a crude and unusually visible case, and we're the same thing with more RAM. I can see the pull of that, and I don't really buy the compatibilist response to it: if determinism is true then whatever I do in the next minute was already fixed, so it looks like the only way to not be a wasp is for some of what I do to be undetermined. And there's room for that, right? The brain is full of genuinely stochastic stuff at small scales — neurotransmitter release, ion channel noise, that kind of thing — and that noise can get amplified up to the level of a decision. So the picture I end up with is: sphexishness is determinism, freedom is noise.

I even tryed to make it precise. Define an agent's sphexishness coefficient as the fraction of its behavioural variance that isn't explained by differences in its situation — how much of what it does is left over once you condition on everything about its environment. The wasp scores near zero, because you can predict her whole routine from the stimulus; a creature with real indeterminacy in its control system scores higher; so freedom means a higher score. The problem, which my friend pointed out and I can't shake, is that on this definition a twitchy agent that does something different every time for no reason whatsoever comes out maximally free, while a perfectly reliable agent who always does the right thing for the reasons that are actually there comes out exactly as sphexish as the wasp.
