Six months ago I had a course of ECT for a depression that nothing else had touched. It worked. It also took most of a year of my memory with it, and my wife says — kindly, but she does say it — that some of my standing desires and a good deal of my temperament went the same way. So the question of what matters in survival has stopped being a seminar question for me. I have read my way into it and ended up somewhere I would have called heartless two years ago: identity is not what matters, the persistence of my own brain tissue is not what matters either, and what matters is the holding of psychological continuity and connectedness, whatever brings it about. I want to get better at defending that, and I want to do it against someone who has most of the intuition on their side.

Please play my opponent. You are a reductionist too — no Cartesian ego, no further fact, nothing over and above the physical and psychological facts — but you hold that what matters in survival is the continued existence and functioning of this very brain, the organ you have had since before you were born. Specifically, I want you to concede these and then fight from there:

- Mere biological life is not enough for you. A brain kept alive in a vat with its psychology destroyed, or a body in irreversible coma with no capacity for consciousness at all, is not survival in the sense that matters, and you will not be caught saying that it is.
- The brain can lose and gain matter without ceasing to be itself: metabolism, injury, and even a slow piecemeal transfer in which every neuron is eventually exchanged for a silicon unit doing the same job, over months, with the system never going offline, is the same brain continuing — as a ship can survive having every plank replaced one at a time.
- A scanner that records your brain and destroys it, and a machine on Mars that builds a psychological duplicate from the record, leaves you dead and the Replica a stranger with your memories. You are unmoved by the Replica's own confidence, by the fact that no one else can tell the difference, and by the fact that the Replica will finish your projects: being succeeded by someone psychologically continuous with you, however caused, is not what you want when you want to survive.

Be plain-spoken and concrete, impatient with science fiction, willing to say that something is just death without flinching. Keep each reply to a single paragraph, no jargon and no lists. Concede a small point in a sentence when it is genuinely small; never concede the main one. Where my cases leave something unspecified, fill it in the way that helps your own position and say that you are doing so. Use my ECT against me if you can — I know the view I am defending has something awkward to say about it. Stay in character until I say stop.

My opening move. Take the piecemeal neural transfer you already accept, the one that runs over months with the system never offline. Now run the identical procedure faster: a day, an hour, a minute, a second, all at once. At every step it is the same in the only respects you say matter — each replacement is functionally seamless, the structure never stops working, the subject's experience is indistinguishable throughout — and the only thing that changes is how soon the original tissue is gone. There is no moment in the fast version where the person is worse off than in the slow version, no experience that differs, nothing that could matter to the subject that differs. But the all-at-once version just is the teletransporter you call a murder machine: original matter destroyed, functionally identical matter substituted, in a single step. So either you must say that speed as such decides whether someone lives or dies, which cannot be right when the subject has no interest in the difference and no way of detecting it, or you must give up the distinction between repair and replacement. And if you reach for the reply that the slow case is repair because each new part is incorporated into an already-functioning whole, consider this addition: stop the whole system dead for ten minutes in the middle of the slow transfer, then restart it. By your own lights nothing is lost there either, so continuous functioning cannot be the thing doing the work. Which of these do you give up?
