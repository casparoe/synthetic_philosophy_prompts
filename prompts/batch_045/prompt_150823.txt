**Kelly betting is a limit result, and I think that matters more than people let on**

*§1 — the standard machinery, which I'm fine with*

Setup: repeated favorable bet, you pick what fraction of your wealth to stake each round. Two facts I lean on hard.

(i) To second order in the edge, if f is how much of the full-Kelly stake you take, your growth rate is g(f) = (2f − f²)·g\*, where g\* is full-Kelly growth. So any f in (0,2) grows, f > 2 shrinks to zero, and — this is the bit I like — g is *symmetric* around f = 1: g(1+ε) = g(1−ε) = (1−ε²)·g\*. Half Kelly buys you 75% of the growth for 50% of the volatility in log-wealth. The flatness near the top is why the "Kelly is too aggressive, be fractional" advice is nearly free.

(ii) If the market is priced at the true distribution p and you believe q and you bet your whole bankroll according to q, your wealth decays at rate KL(p‖q) per round. Your error costs you exactly your divergence from the truth, compounding. This is the thing that makes "kelly = epistemic hygiene" feel true.

*§2 — where the limit is doing the work*

The usual *argument* for kelly isn't (i) or (ii), it's domination: almost surely, as T → ∞, the Kelly bettor beats every other policy by a factor that goes to infinity. That's a statement about infinity. Sanity check on how much it needs: one round, binary bet, you believe q > p = true prob of winning, and I bet full Kelly while you bet half. My wealth beats yours iff the bet wins — so P(full Kelly ahead at T = 1) = p, which for a longshot (say p = 0.15 against odds implying 0.1) is *less than half*. The domination argument needs a lot of rounds before it says anything, and the draft's claim is that people quietly import the asymptotic conclusion into finite-horizon, non-repeated situations where it hasn't been derived.

*§3 — the actual new bit: a deadline and a goal*

Now change the problem. T rounds, and at T you need wealth ≥ C or the whole thing is worth nothing (you don't fund the thing, you don't buy the thing, whatever). Utility = log(w/C) if w ≥ C, and some big bad constant if w < C.

Now the optimal policy is not a constant fraction, it's a free-boundary thing in (t, log(w/C)). My heuristic for what it looks like:

- The deadline and the threshold only distort your bets when you're within about one *remaining volatility radius* of the goal, i.e. when |log(w/C)| ≲ σ·√(T−t), where σ is the per-round sd of your log-return. Far outside that band the barrier is irrelevant and you just bet (fractional) Kelly. Inside it, you distort.
- Above the goal and inside the band: shrink toward zero, lock it in. Below the goal and inside the band: gamble, hard, up to whatever your max stake is — the thing people in the EA-adjacent corners call gambling for resurrection.
- Discrete-time correction: that band can't shrink below roughly the size of a single round's move, because with one round left a single bet can still blow through the barrier even if √(T−t)·σ is tiny. So the band width is more like max(σ√(T−t), one-round move).

The part that made me want to write this: the region below the goal looks *insane*. Log utility is unbounded below, so once you're already at the bad constant, the expected-utility calculation is happy to have you put everything on a 2% shot. A geometric-rationality agent with a deadline and a goal looks, from the outside, exactly like a reckless gambler — right at the moment where looking reckless is most correlated with being about to fail. The log part and the threshold part are fighting each other, and I don't think the standard presentation notices, because it never has both in the same model.

*§4 — the punchline I'm least confident in*

The argument for geometric rationality is roughly "in the long run the kelly bettor dominates, so rationality ≈ geometric rationality." But domination is asymptotic *and* threshold-free, and I want to claim that (a) finite horizons plus (b) absorbing goals are the normal case, not the exotic one — arguably including the civilization-scale case, where there's a window and there's a level of "we got somewhere stable" vs "we didn't," rather than infinite compounding. In that regime the privileged-ness of kelly evaporates. Your policy is a function of your distance to the goal measured in remaining volatility, and it sweeps continuously from bet-nothing to bet-everything, with Kelly showing up only as the special case where the goal is infinitely far away in both directions. So the question isn't "should I be a Kelly bettor," it's "how far from the goal am I, in units of remaining volatility." I've been calling the rule goal-conditional Kelly, which is a bad name; suggestions welcome.

*§5 — stuff I already know is shaky*

(c) Whether the g(1+ε) = g(1−ε) symmetry survives outside the small-edge approximation — I suspect not and I don't know the sign of the error. (d) Whether the T=1 observation in §2 is the right way to make the "domination needs time" point, or whether the mean-vs-median gap is the cleaner version.
