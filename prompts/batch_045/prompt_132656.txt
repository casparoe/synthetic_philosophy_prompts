I am writing a novel, and before I commit to the mechanics of the machine at its centre I want a specification rather than a survey. What I need from you is the desiderata: a list of what any account of backwards time travel in fiction would have to deliver to be good enough to underwrite four hundred pages — the cases it must resolve, the distinctions it must respect, the constraints it may not violate — with the scene or consideration behind each requirement, and a judgement about which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely the prejudices of one camp or another. The purpose is fixed: I need rules a reader can infer from scenes rather than be told in an exposition chapter, rules that yield a determinate verdict at the end of any given scene about whether the intervention worked, and rules under which a character can be wrong about the mechanics and be surprised. Bear that purpose in mind when you decide what counts as a requirement.

Below are the scenes I already know I want to write. For each, tell me what requirement it generates, and add the requirements I have not anticipated.

My protagonist travels back to prevent a fire in which her brother died. She succeeds in the sense that the brother lives, yet the act by which she prevents it — cutting a wire, breaking a window, delaying a driver — is the act that starts it. Did the past change?

She remembers the fire. Once she has intervened, does she still? If the timeline is overwritten rather than branched, whose memories does she carry, and is there any way for her to know that her intervention failed?

The machine has a bounded range: certain years simply are not there to be reached. Is the boundary a fact about the machine, about the geometry of time, or about what is logically available? I need to know what fixes the reachable set, because I want the limit to feel like a discovery and not a manufacturer's specification.

An object with no origin: a score my protagonist carries back to a composer who will later publish it, and which she copied from that publication. Is this the same problem as the grandfather case or a different one in kind, and must a theory handle them by the same device?

The crowded room: she meets her younger self and they disagree about what to do. Which of them bears responsibility for what follows, and what should happen when the younger one later makes the trip herself?

The slow news: if the past is altered, does the alteration reach her departure date at once, or only as she travels forward along her own worldline? Can the present she left be edited while she is still in the past, and can she be erased retroactively while still acting?

The abandoned: on a branching account, what becomes of the people in the branch she leaves behind? I need to know whether her act is a killing, an abandonment, or nothing at all, since the moral weight of the book rests on the answer.

Deliberation: on a fixed-timeline account, is trying still coherent? Can she deliberate about an outcome that is settled either way, and what in the prose should convey that her effort is real rather than illusory?

Two further constraints, which I take to be external rather than optional. First, the theory must say what "going back to 1889" means given that the physics we actually have denies a global present; the date cannot be a bare fact about the universe. Second, it must give some account of the asymmetry between the directions — why travelling forward is trivially available while travelling back is the thing that needs a machine — or else explain why the asymmetry is only apparent.

Once the list is assembled, tell me which pairs of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say plainly if the list itself turns out to be the real result here; that is the part I most want to see. Then tell me which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidates — which one, if I insisted on it, would collapse my options fastest. Close with a brief scorecard, no more than a few lines each, for the three familiar families: the fixed or self-consistent timeline, the branching timeline, and the overwritten or erased timeline. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; I can extend the scoring myself.
