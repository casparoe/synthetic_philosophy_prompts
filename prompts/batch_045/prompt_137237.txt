I've been running a reading group on whether cognition can penetrate perception, and it has completely stalled — a year in, same handful of people, everyone's read the same material, and the two camps just restate themselves. One side points at the effects: people carrying heavy backpacks report hills as steeper, thirsty subjects report water-ish things as brighter and bigger, induced expectations shift which way an ambiguous figure or a bistable stimulus resolves. The other side says every one of those is post-perceptual — response bias, demand characteristics, shifts of attention, judgment applied after the fact — and that nothing has ever been shown to change what the world actually looks like.

My hunch is that we aren't really fighting about the data at all. We're using two different tests for 'perceptual' without noticing: some of us mean a subpersonal, computational thing (does the effect land inside early or mid vision, changing its content or the format of its output), others mean a personal-level thing (does the scene look different to the subject), and those can come apart. And I think there's a sneakier disagreement hiding underneath: even granting an effect is inside the perceptual system, is the cognitive state shifting the prior, or is it re-weighting the sensory evidence or changing its precision? Only the second feels like real 'penetration' to most people's gut sense of the word, but every experiment I can think of just measures the final report, so the two options are indistinguishable as things stand. Plus half the heat, I suspect, comes from a stake nobody says out loud — whether observation can be theory-neutral, whether perception can ground a belief without already borrowing its content from that belief.

Is that roughly the right diagnosis, and where does it go wrong? Is this verbal, a clash about which cases count as data, bedrock intuitions about what perception is for, or an empirical question that's just underdetermined by behavioural methods? And given whatever you think it actually is, what would count as progress — a task that dissociates prior-shift from re-weighting, an agreed psychophysical signature for a genuinely phenomenal rather than judgmental effect, neural evidence, a formal underdetermination argument — or should we conclude the question is ill-posed and disband? Give me a diagnosis with reasons rather than a survey of the literature, and be honest if the answer is that no progress is coming.
