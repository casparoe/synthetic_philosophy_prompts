Setup: I'm a second-year PhD student working mostly on Kant's practical philosophy, and I have to submit a ~2-page proposal for a seminar paper (about 7000 words, one semester). The seminar is on the moral status of animals, historically oriented, and I want to use the paper to get Kant straight because I think this is going to turn into my first dissertation chapter.

Here's the thing I keep circling. Kant gets presented as holding the most deflationary view on offer: animals aren't persons, they aren't ends in themselves, we have no duties *to* them, only duties *with regard to* them, and cruelty is objectionable mainly because of what it does to the agent — being cruel to animals supposedly cultivates a character that will be cruel to humans. My suspicion is that this is really three claims that everyone runs together: (1) a grounding claim, that the normative ground of any such duty is rational nature; (2) a content claim, about what the duty actually requires as far as the animal is concerned; and (3) an empirical causal claim, that cruelty to animals in fact spills over into cruelty to humans. And my hunch is that if you keep (1) but drop or downgrade (3), you land somewhere nobody has a name for: the duty is "owed to" humanity, but its content is fixed by the animal's good, so the agent still has to deliberate about that good, weigh it against human ends, trade it off, decide which labors are too much and which uses are gratuitous. At which point it's not obvious what work "indirect" is still doing — maybe indirectness is only a claim about who can be wronged rather than about what may be done, or maybe it quietly collapses into a weak direct-duty view.

Second half-formed thought: the discussion treats "animals" as one bucket, but on Kant's own terms the relevant property is rationality, not species membership. So the view looks committed to saying that rational non-humans, if we met them, would be owed duties directly, while a chimpanzee and an oyster are in exactly the same position. I want to know whether the view has any resources for degrees — degrees of rationality, degrees of sentience, whatever — or whether it's stuck with a binary, and if it is stuck, whether that's a defect or the whole point.
