I teach a first-year epistemology module and I am putting together a seminar that pairs Wittgenstein's late notes — published posthumously as _On Certainty_ — with Sidney Lumet's 1957 film _12 Angry Men_, Reginald Rose's jury-room drama. The hook I want to give the students is that a jury is a community institutionally required to doubt: twelve strangers are shut in a room and told that before they may act they must be certain, and the film then watches them discover, one by one, that what they were certain of rested on a great deal they had never examined. But I have become unsure how far the fit actually goes, and I would like a reading of the film through hinge epistemology that is willing to say where the fit breaks down. To make the discussion manageable I have been using a distinction of my own: on the one hand evidential doubt, which cites some specific way the case might have gone wrong (the knife may not be unique, the witness across the street may not have been wearing her glasses, the old man downstairs could not have reached his door in the time he claimed); on the other hand architectural doubt, which targets the framework within which those specific doubts get their force and points to no particular error of its own. I also want to press a phenomenon the film dramatises better than anything in the philosophical literature I have read: what I call evaporative doubt, the state Henry Fonda's Juror 8 seems to be in at the opening vote, when he cannot yet say what is wrong with the prosecution's story, reports only that it does not hang together, and is mocked — not unreasonably — for it by the other eleven.

Three things I would like you to work through. First: is Juror 8's early stance best described as Wittgensteinian doubt at all, or does it look much more like the wholesale, Cartesian doubt Wittgenstein is arguing against — and if the latter, does the film nonetheless vindicate it in a way that ought to embarrass the hinge reading? Second, consider the two holdouts against acquittal. Juror 3's conviction that the boy is guilty is not responsive to evidence in the way an ordinary belief is: it organises everything else he says, and at the end it collapses rather than being refuted, in the scene with the photograph of his son and, afterwards, Davis's small gesture with the coat. Juror 10's claims about people from the slums function similarly in his thinking, but are straightforwardly bigoted. My worry is this: if a hinge is defined by its functional role — unjustified, unshaken, standing fast for the person who holds it — then nothing in that definition distinguishes a healthy hinge from Juror 3's or Juror 10's, and the film becomes an argument that hinge epistemology owes us a normative account it does not obviously have. Is that fair, or is there a reply available from inside the framework — for instance by insisting that genuine hinges are essentially shared, so that an idiosyncratic or corrupt one is not a hinge at all but a prejudice wearing hinge's clothing? Third: the verdict at the end is not that the boy is innocent, only that the case has not been proved. Does the film thereby take a quiet position on whether legal inquiry aims at truth or at the proper stopping of inquiry, and does that position have anything to say back to the Wittgensteinian literature?

A few constraints, if you would humour them. I would like the answer in British English, and I do not want a plot summary, a reading list or any citations — I know the film and I can supply a bibliography myself. More importantly, please mark as you go which of the claims you rely on are ones nearly everyone working on _On Certainty_ would accept and which are genuinely contested: in particular, whether hinges are propositions, beliefs or neither, whether they can be true or false at all, and whether the variability of hinges across communities pushes one towards something like epistemic relativism. I do not want to hand first-years a contested reading dressed up as orthodoxy. Please also tell me plainly where my own framing is stretching: if the film is really about courage and conformity rather than about the structure of doubt, if evidential, architectural and evaporative doubt turn out to be far less separable than I have made them sound, or if my distinctions simply collapse into ones already standard in the literature, say so. And I would be grateful if you flagged anywhere you are reasoning from the film's reputation rather than from the film itself.
