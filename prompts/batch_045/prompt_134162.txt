I am writing the opening chapter of a paper on naturalizing mental content, and before I argue for any account I want the specification. What must a theory of representational content deliver in order to be usable as a working explanatory tool — the sort of tool a cognitive scientist reaches for when she explains a system's behaviour by citing what its internal states represent, in the successes and, more demandingly, in the failures? Take the purpose to be fixed: content as it figures in explanations of what a creature or a trained artificial system does, not content as a metaphysical ornament.

Write the desiderata, each with the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark which you take to be non-negotiable and which are prejudices of one camp. I want the list to be forced to answer, at minimum: how misrepresentation is possible when nothing in the system registers the mistake; how the grain of a content is settled, so that the answer to whether a detector represents flies, small moving dark things, or food is neither radically indeterminate nor just whatever the theorist happens to find useful; what to say about a state that functions precisely by getting things wrong, because errors of that kind are cheap while errors of the opposite kind are not; whether a state with no consumer — produced but never read by anything downstream — has content at all, and what turns on the answer; whether content can be assigned to a system with no selection history, whose functions come from a designer or a training process, and whether that is the same kind of content as the biological case; and how much of a system's content is in principle readable from the outside by someone who cannot already see what the system is for.

Then flag any subset of these you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. My own suspicion is that fixing content by history and making it do present-tense explanatory work pull apart, and that the requirement of genuine error and the requirement of third-person readability pull apart too — say whether either is a real inconsistency or only a tension one camp has dramatized. Finish with a few lines each on how the proper-function family and the covariance-or-information family do against the list; I will fill in the details myself.
