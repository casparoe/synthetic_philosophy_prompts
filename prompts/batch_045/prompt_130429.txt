Something's been bugging me for a while whenever I read popular writing about why time has a direction, and I can't figure out whether there's a word for the thing that bugs me or whether I'm just confusing myself.

The shape I keep running into is this: every account seems to buy its conclusion by slipping in the same kind of asymmetry one step earlier. Example one: the story for why things get more spread out and scrambled toward one end of time and never the other is that the universe began in an extraordinarily unscrambled state. But "began" and "unscrambled" are the very thing I wanted explained — a claim about one end of time being special is already a directional claim. So you ask why it was like that, and as far as I can tell people do one of two things. Either they say there's some further law that forces things to be smooth at the start, which looks to me like the same move again in a fancier coat. Or they say it was a random fluctuation in an otherwise directionless universe, and then — unless I've messed this up — you seem to be stuck with the consequence that a lone momentary blob of organized stuff that coheres for an instant and dissolves is vastly more likely than an entire long orderly history like the one we seem to be in the middle of, so on your own theory you shouldn't expect to be seeing what you're apparently seeing. Example two: same shape with records and memory. We've got traces and photographs of one direction and not the other, and why? Because scrambling goes up that way. And why's that? See above. Same again with the sense that I can do something about tomorrow and nothing whatsoever about yesterday.

I don't think I'm just rediscovering the point that every explanation has to stop somewhere, and I'd like that separated out too. If I ask why the balls scattered you say the cue ball hit them, if I ask why the cue ball was moving you say the arm moved, and pretty soon you shrug and the story just starts there — and nobody calls that circular, and it doesn't feel circular. But in the time case the place the story stops has exactly the same shape as the claim we began with, and in the fluctuation version it doesn't merely stop, it seems to eat the evidence we had for believing it in the first place. Those feel like two different complaints and I want to know if they are.

So: is there an established name — or several, for different parts — for this pattern of an explanation of temporal asymmetry presupposing what it was supposed to explain? Is there a separate established thing for the specific version where the explanation, taken at face value, predicts that observers like us should be the vastly more numerous minimal freaks rather than creatures embedded in long orderly histories? If they're really one phenomenon, say so and tell me why I'm wrong to split them. If one of them has no name, coin me one with a definition and give me the boundary cases.

Two more things I can't sort out. Some people I've read take all this to show the direction isn't out there in the world at all but has to do with where we're standing — that we're the sort of creature that has to be located on one side of the slope. Is that a third position or the second one dressed up, and does it actually escape the circle or just relocate the bump into the observer? And some people say the unscrambled start is simply a boundary condition, like any other initial condition, and needs no more explanation than the position of the cue ball does. Does that reply have a name, and does it work?

Please don't just hand me a label. I want to know what the phenomenon is, what it isn't, where its edges are, and which of my two complaints the existing vocabulary actually covers.
