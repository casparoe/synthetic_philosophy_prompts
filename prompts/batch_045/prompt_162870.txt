My housemate is the person everyone on our floor goes to when they want to know if someone was out of line, and she's very consistent-sounding about when being a hypocrite costs you the right to blame. Call her H. Her rulings so far:

- Kev, who shoplifted last year and told no one, tearing into a flatmate for stealing milk from the office fridge: no standing, she can just ignore him.
- Mara, two years sober, telling her friend her drinking is out of control: has standing. "She's the one person who's earned it."
- Tom, who ghosted two people three years ago and never apologised, publicly shaming his ex for ghosting him: no standing.
- Nadia, who eats beef and freely admits it, signing a petition against a dog-meat festival: no standing unless she stops or at least owns it.
- Anyone chronically late calling out a colleague's lateness: no standing, unless she's raising it because the client complained, in which case its fine because she's only relaying.
- A woman who cheated on her boyfriend calling out a friend for lying to hers: has standing, different fault, different norm.

She also insists the blame can be completely true and still be something you're not entitled to say out loud.

Three cases landing this week: (1) my brother, who drove drunk once about five years ago, was never caught, never told anyone and quietly stopped, now telling his 17-year-old son he loses the car if he ever drives after drinking; (2) a union rep who crossed a picket line in 2019 and never apologised, now running the strike and calling out colleagues who cross it; (3) our building's recycling enforcer, who sorts meticulously but flies long-haul three times a year, berating neighbours for not sorting.

What does H say in each, what rule (or rules) is she implicitly running, and what further case would best tell those rules apart? Please also say how confident you are in each prediction and flag the one claim you're least sure of.
