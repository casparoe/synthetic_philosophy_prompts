hey so I'm on this student committee thing at my school and we're supposed to be "reviewing the grading policy" and I keep feeling like everyone on it is talking past each other, and I think it's because the policy is quietly assuming stuff about what people deserve that nobody says out loud. I want you to dig that out. Please don't give me a reading list or tell me to go look some philosopher up — I want the argument, not the names.

Here's the setup. My school's report card gives two seperate marks for every subject: an achievement mark (how good your work actually is) and an effort mark (how hard you tried). They used to just be boxes the teacher ticked, but last year they made the effort mark worth 20% of your final grade, averaged in with everything else. On top of that: there's a "most improved" prize at assembly every term; we got rid of class rank and valedictorian a few years ago but we still give "distinction" to the top 5% by GPA; you can resit any test once and the higher score counts; and teachers are told not to mark homework down for being late, but the effort mark is allowed to drop if you hand things in late.

What got me is that two teachers on the committee said opposite things and both sounded obviously right. One said the effort mark is the fair part, because you can't help being bad at math but you can help whether you try. The other said effort marks are nonsense, because whether you're the kind of person who tries is exactly the sort of thing you don't choose — it comes from your family, how much sleep you get, whether you've got a shift after school, whether anyone at home ever checked your homework. So my suspicion is that the effort mark takes the standard "it's all luck so nobody deserves anything" objection and just steps it back one level instead of answering it. But I'm not sure that's right, and I'm not sure how much damage it does if it is.

Also — this is the bit I care most about — everyone knows that in practice the effort mark mostly means "did you sit still, turn stuff in on time, and be agreeable to the teacher." Kids who are visibly trying really hard and still failing often get low effort marks. So maybe the effort mark doesn't really presuppose a theory of desert at all and is just a behaviour-management tool wearing desert words, with the desert talk purely decorative. If that's true, does anything follow from it? Is there a way to test whether desert language is doing real work in a policy or just making it feel nicer?

What I want from you:

- Reconstruct the theory of desert (or whatever it actually is) that the two-marks system only makes sense under, and say what rival views it quietly rules out.
- Which parts of the policy strictly need that theory, and which parts lots of different theories would allow anyway? Especially: the "most improved" prize vs. the top-5% distinction vs. the resit rule. Those feel like they're rewarding different things, and I want to know whether they're actually fighting each other or just aimed at different stuff.
- Where the policy contradicts itself, if it does — e.g., effort dropping for late work while achievement isn't allowed to count lateness.
- The strongest version of the "effort is also just luck" objection, and honestly how much of the policy it takes down with it.
- If we dropped desert entirely and said grades are only there to give information and incentives, what would concretely have to change on the report card, and what would break?

And please sort the problems you find into "this is actually incoherent" versus "this is fine but the school is kidding itself about why." I have to defend whatever I say in front of the head teacher, who really likes the effort marks, so tell me which of your points she'd have a genuine answer to and which she wouldn't.
