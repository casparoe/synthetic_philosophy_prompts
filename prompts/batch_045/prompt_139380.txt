My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about a decision I have to make by Monday morning, and I came away unable to say why my own position is anything more than squeamishness. I manage the front office of a small clinic, and I have found out that two colleagues each intend, tomorrow, to do the same kind of thing: one plans to tell a patient's landlord that she is HIV-positive, the other to tell a girl's father about a termination. Each is the same kind of betrayal of the same rule, and each would ruin the person it concerns. Reporting them would achieve nothing, since it would be my word against theirs and I would be gone before I could prove anything. But I can stop both. If I open one file tonight and pass on one piece of information I have no right to pass on, the audit software will flag it within the hour, the records system will be locked down, and neither of them will get the chance. So: one betrayal of that kind, mine, instead of two of that kind, theirs. My friend says the case is obvious, that the same wrong either happens once or happens twice, and that refusing is just caring about my own clean hands at the price of a real person's life. My discomfort is not really about my hands, or not only: the woman whose file I would open has no complaint against my colleagues and every complaint against me, and it seems to me that this is supposed to matter more than the arithmetic does. But I cannot say why it should matter, or whether the numbers are irrelevant all the way up, since if it were my one against their ten I suspect I would fold, and if I would, then the arithmetic I claimed to distrust has simply come back in. We are all three of us in the same position, incidentally: either of them could stop the other two the same way, and I take it they are no more permitted to than I am, which is what makes the whole thing feel less like a hard choice and more like a mistake somewhere in how I am thinking.
