Draft the joint statement that two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the color phi phenomenon and the timing of conscious experience. No citations or bibliography — the argument should stand on the cases alone.

The shared setup. In the classic two-flash display a red disc is briefly presented at one location and, roughly 60 to 120 milliseconds later, a green disc appears several degrees away. Observers report not two flashes but a single disc that travels from the first location to the second and that appears to change color abruptly in mid-trajectory — and, strikingly, to have changed color before the green disc was ever presented. Call the disputed question Q: was there an earlier conscious version of the sequence that was subsequently displaced, or was there never such an earlier draft?

The two camps. Camp S, the construction-before-consciousness camp: the record is fixed before any draft reaches consciousness, so there was never a conscious experience of the sequence in its unrevised form and nothing for a later revision to overwrite. Camp O, the consciousness-before-revision camp: the subject did undergo an earlier conscious draft — for instance of a red disc completing the whole trajectory — and that draft was then displaced by the revised version now reported, so the mid-trajectory color change belongs to the replacement rather than to the original experience. Both camps concede that no later first-person report of the subject's could distinguish these two stories, and both reject the further conclusion, drawn by a third camp, that there is therefore no fact of the matter about which one occurred. Each must say why that inference fails, and where each concedes it nonetheless bites.

Produce:

1. A numbered section of claims both camps can sign — about the phenomenology to be explained, about which neural timings are and are not relevant, about what counts as a probe on the system's content at a time, and about the third camp's no-fact-of-the-matter argument.

2. A disagreements table with exactly these columns: Issue | Camp S's formulation, worded so S would sign it | Camp O's formulation, worded so O would sign it | Status (substantive, verbal, or partly verbal).

3. A cruxes section. For each crux give: its statement; the test, case, or argument both camps accept in advance as bearing on it; what S predicts; what O predicts; how decisive a contrary result would be (decisive, merely damaging, or awkward); and whether both camps agree the result would move at least one of them. Treat the following three as the central cruxes.

   (a) The deferred-disambiguation probe. Suppose experimenters succeed in building a variant in which the second flash is physically achromatic — a broadband white disc — and a large colored surround flashed 150 to 200 milliseconds after it settles whether the traveling disc is reported as having turned green or blue in mid-flight. Observers still report the hue change as occurring mid-trajectory, around 50 milliseconds in. So the hue attributed to an experience at 50 milliseconds is not fixed by anything in the stimulus until roughly 200 milliseconds. S must say what follows about whether there was any experience at 50 milliseconds at all; O must say what the hue content of the earlier draft was, given that it was not yet determined when the draft is supposed to have occurred: a determinate hue, a determinable or generic hue, or no hue content at all.

   (b) The deleted-draft trace test. If a conscious draft occurred and was then displaced, should it leave any signature that a construction without such a draft would not produce — residual priming by the discarded content, recoverability under a probe delivered between the two flashes, a metabolic or electrophysiological cost of the extra draft, or a distinctive confidence or voluntariness profile in the final report? Each camp records its predictions in advance, including which results it would count against itself.

   (c) The interruption probe. A mask or pulse delivered after the first flash abolishes all report of the second flash, yet observers still report the mid-trajectory color change — the content is built partly from an input that never became reportable. Does that favor S, or can O accommodate it?

4. A short closing section naming which parts of the dispute, if any, are about words rather than the world, and stating whether anything both camps agree would actually move the debate.
