Here is the stem as it will appear; students get a formula sheet with it.

Consider a standard actor–critic agent. The critic maintains V(s), an estimate of expected discounted return under the current policy; at each step the agent receives reward r_t, moves to s_{t+1}, and forms the one-step temporal-difference error delta_t = r_t + gamma·V(s_{t+1}) − V(s_t), which trains the critic and, directly or via a generalized advantage estimate, supplies the advantage term in the actor's policy-gradient update. Call (S) the surprise identification: valence is carried by delta_t. Call (P) the prospect identification: valence is carried by V(s_t). Which, if either, is the right place to look for suffering in such a system, and what follows for what we ought to do about it? In answering you must: (a) explain why the naive view that the reward r_t is itself the valence is the weakest of the three candidates; (b) work at least two of these four cases — an outcome the agent has fully predicted, an agent that knows from the start that it is heading for catastrophe, an agent that learns of the catastrophe late, and a deployed agent whose weights are frozen and which is not being updated at all; (c) state which intervention would reduce suffering on the account you favour, and whether that is an intervention we should be willing to perform.

Two things I want the scheme to catch, if you agree they are the cruxes; if you think I have mischaracterized either, say so inside the item where it belongs. First, the stronger CS students should notice that the discounted sum of one-step TD errors telescopes to (sum over t of gamma^t · r_t) + gamma^T·V(s_T) − V(s_0), so that the total surprise available across an episode is fixed by how optimistic the agent was at the start rather than by what then happens to it. Second, the same edit — pushing the agent's value estimates down — is the kindest available act under (S) and the cruellest under (P), and that flip is where the two accounts stop being descriptions of one phenomenon and start prescribing incompatible things.
