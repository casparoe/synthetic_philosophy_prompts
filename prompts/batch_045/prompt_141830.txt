I'm writing a seminar paper on nomic self-reference — rules that govern their own amendment — and before I sign up to any theory of it I want the spec. So: what would a theory of self-amending rules actually have to get right?

Here's my made-up legal system, which I built precisely to make the usual answers squirm. The Republic of Maraval, Charter of 1951, relevant bits:

- Art. 12: The Assembly may amend this Constitution by a two-thirds vote.
- Art. 13: Article 12 may not be amended.
- Art. 14: Articles 12–14 lapse on 1 January 2060; after that, amendments require a referendum. (So the amendment rule changes itself on a timer, with nobody doing anything.)
- Art. 15: No amendment may abolish universal suffrage. (A substantive limit, with nothing anywhere saying who decides compliance.)
- Art. 16: An amendment takes effect immediately, including an amendment to Art. 12 itself.
- Art. 17: Once, and only once, the Assembly may amend Art. 12 by a simple majority.

History: in 2048 the Assembly uses Art. 17 to lower the threshold to a simple majority. Then, under the new rule, it repeals Arts. 13, 15 and 17 and enacts Art. 18: "This Constitution and all amendments to it shall be interpreted as the Assembly from time to time determines." In 2055 a court — whose own authority traces back through the 2048 amendment — holds that 2048 was invalid because Art. 17 couldn't authorize a repeal of Art. 13's entrenchment, and now has to say what happens to seven years of legislation.

Give me the requirements list. For each one: the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, not a taste. I want the distinctions a theory must respect and not collapse — validity of an amendment vs. validity of everything done under it, amending vs. replacing (when is it revolution rather than amendment?), a first-order amendment rule vs. a second-order one about how the first gets changed, self-entrenchment vs. self-application vs. self-executing timing, existence of a rule vs. its content, legal validity vs. efficacy or acceptance, procedural vs. substantive limits — and I want the paradoxes it must actually dissolve rather than dodge. Two I especially care about: (a) Art. 17's power comes from a rule the exercise destroys, so where dose the successor rule's authority come from, and what happens to the 2048 amendment when 17 is itself repealed; (b) the liar-shaped case, e.g. a validly enacted Art. 19 saying "no amendment enacted under Art. 12 is valid," or Art. 18's interpretive supremacy covering Art. 18 itself.
