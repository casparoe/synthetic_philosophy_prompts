I'm finishing a dissertation chapter on the problem of old evidence, and I keep running into the same move in both directions without being able to tell whether the move is doing real work or whether it is an artifact of the picture of credence I inherited.

The picture is the Ramsey–de Finetti operationalization: a credence is a price — the highest price at which you would buy and the lowest at which you would sell a unit-stake bet on p — with conditional credences read off conditional, or called-off, bets. My supervisor leans on this picture in one direction and I lean on it in the other. Her line is that the problem of old evidence is not a problem for Bayesian confirmation theory at all but a byproduct of a bad operationalization: if credence is really an attitude of judgment about how far one proposition supports another, and not a price you would post, then nothing stops a new theory T from raising your confidence in a datum E you had already fully learned. The datum has probability one only on the assumption that P is being read as a betting quotient, and a bookie cannot write a bet on a proposition everyone in the room already knows to be true. So the problem is to be dissolved by discarding the analogy. My line is the reverse: the dynamics of the framework — the update rule, the role of the likelihood ratio, even the point of representing confirmation numerically — is inherited from the betting picture, so the problem reappears wherever that picture is load-bearing, and a judgment reading of P quietly loses the constraints (coherence, the update rule, calibration to frequencies) that made the framework worth having in the first place.

Before I commit either way I want the analogy itself audited. Concretely:

1. Set out the correspondence explicitly — a table is fine. What plays the role of a proposition, of a credence, of a conditional credence, of learning, of confirmation, of coherence, of the update rule, on each side? And note for each row whether the mapping is forced by the formal setup or is a stipulation someone made, because I suspect several rows that look forced are conventional.

2. Give me the places the mapping fails. The ones I can see: stakes have to be small enough that attitude to risk drops out; settlement requires a proposition that is decidable at a known time; the bettor and the bookie must share a space of possibilities over which the bet is defined; the bettor's own acceptance of a bet can be evidence to her, and a bookie's willingness to post odds is evidence to him; a price is one number and has no slot for how the proposition came to be known; and there must be a bookie willing to take the other side, which fails for propositions that are settled by logic, mathematics, or stipulation.

3. Separate the disanalogies that matter for the old-evidence case from the ones that are noise. In particular, I want to know what is actually doing the blocking: the value P(E) = 1; the absence of any time at which a wager on E could have been written; or the fact that the arrival of T re-individuates the evidential proposition, so that the E recorded in the old vocabulary and the E* that T entails, or approximately entails, are not the same item — think of a new gravitational theory recovering an anomaly that had been sitting in the tables for decades. My suspicion is that the third is where the difficulty really lives and that the betting picture cannot even formulate it, since it presupposes a fixed algebra of bettable propositions rather than allowing the theory to revise the algebra. If that suspicion is wrong, say what is wrong with it.

4. Stress-test the repair I have been toying with, which I call archival odds: read the support T receives from E off a pair of counterfactual prices, namely what the agent would have posted, at some earlier time before E was in hand, on the conditional bet on E given T and on the conditional bet on E given not-T, with the ratio of those prices playing the role the likelihood ratio plays in ordinary conditioning. Does this device inherit the disanalogies from (2)? Are the counterfactual prices well enough determined to constrain anything, or does the reconstruction leave the numbers free in exactly the place where I need them pinned? And is it genuinely independent of the betting picture, or just the betting picture with a hypothetical bookie?

5. Finally: does my supervisor's conclusion survive the audit, or does it relocate the problem rather than solve it? If credences are judgments rather than prices, something still has to fix their numerical values and their evolution, and I want to know whether that something is a theory of confirmation that can handle evidence already in hand, or whether it just changes the subject. And if there is a better analogy than betting for whatever P is supposed to be in a framework that has to handle old evidence, tell me what it is and what it buys.
