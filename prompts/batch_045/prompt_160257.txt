for a paper im writing on anti-corruption ive finally written down what I actually think, and I want to know if it hangs together:

1. the wrong of corruption is a breach of the impartiality that public office requires — public power converted into a private good. I mean the office-and-role version of the usual entrusted-power-for-private-gain formulation, not a harm-based one.
2. hospitality and gifts to officials aren't corruption where they express a relationship rather than buy an act; the test is wether a specific official action is expected in return. refusing the gift is often the insult.
3. clientelism — vote buying, patronage hiring, handouts for votes — is corruption straight up, including where voters and politicians both treat it as normal and where the voters would otherwise get nothing at all.
4. western anti-corruption standards aren't universal; donor conditionality and the good-governance requirements lenders attach impose one particular moral economy on societies that don't share it, and that's frequently illegitimate.
5. but grand kleptocracy — a ruler stripping the state and parking the proceeds offshore — is corruption in a plain cross-cultural sense, and asset recovery and sanctions against it are justified.
6. my own addition, which I call the equilibrium standard: a system is corrupt when, for the median official, the expected return to partial administration exceeds the expected return to impartial administration. no individual wrongdoing or bad character required.

two places I suspect im cheating but cant locate: (6) seems to sit badly with the blaming in (1)-(3) — if the individual act is basically forced by the equilibrium, what exactly am I condemning, and why should the line I draw in (2) versus (3) matter. and (1) doesn't cleanly deliver (3): the clientelist politician's private gain is just getting elected, and the voter gains too, so it isn't obvious what's been converted from public to private.

can you audit this? tell me which pairs genuinely clash and which only look like they do — please don't flag everything equally — rank the real ones by severity, and for each say what has to give and what giving it costs me. I'm attached to (6), it's the bit I actually care about, so say explicitly whether it can survive if one of the others goes.
