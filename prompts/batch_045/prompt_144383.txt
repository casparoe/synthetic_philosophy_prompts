Half the room calls itself "negative utilitarian," and then immediately disagrees about everything: whether a life that's net pleasant but contains one bad hour is worth creating, whether the mild badness of billions of tiny irritations can outweigh a single agony, whether pain someone chose and is glad about still needs to be offset. Then give me: eight to ten branch questions, each phrased as a concrete case or a claim someone could just agree or disagree with, two or three answer options each, and every one answerable by a smart person who has never heard the labels. Build the branch order from the axiological joints rather than from the names: start with what has negative value at all and whether suffering is the only thing that does, then whether positive states have independent value or only offsetting/instrumental value, then the aggregation question (do small bads sum across people, or is there some intensity threshold below which they stop counting, or a lexical priority for the worst off), then the creation/existence question, then person-affecting vs. impersonal if earlier branches haven't already settled it. (5) the consolation case — two worlds with identical total suffering, but in one every sufferer is also comforted and has plenty of what makes a life good; does the added positive make that world better, worse, or leave it exactly as it was?
