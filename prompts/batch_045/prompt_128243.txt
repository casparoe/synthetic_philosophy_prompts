I run a tiny discussion group at my church — like six regulars, one of whom is a very online Calvinist, one of whom is basically an open theist, and a couple who just show up for the snacks — and last week we got completely stuck on whether God could know what someone would freely do in circumstances that never actually happen. It ended with two people talking past each other about "sovereignty" for twenty minutes and me promising to bring something better next time. What I want is a "which view of divine foreknowledge do you actually hold?" quiz: eight or nine questions, each one a concrete case with two or three plain-English answer options, plus a scoring key that sorts the main answer patterns onto the named positions — the middle knowledge view, the Thomist-style rival on which God's knowledge of what creatures would do comes after and because of the divine decree, open theism, plain simple foreknowledge without middle knowledge, and a "God's knowledge just isn't foreknowledge at all" leaf if you think anyone actually stands there. The sorting has to work by verdicts on cases: nobody should get to answer "do you believe God is omniscient?" or "do you believe in free will?" or any question that basically just asks which label they like. Build the cases from the logical space instead — whether there are truths right now about what a creature would freely do in a circumstance that never occurs; if yes, whether God's grip on them is logically prior to any decision about what to create or follows from it; what makes them true, brute vs. God's will vs. the creature's own character and dispositions; whether God's having that knowledge is a matter of the divine nature or depends on what God freely does; and whether every combination of free choices is really "feasible" or whether some can't be actualized in a stronger sense.

The part I care most about, though, is that I want at least three of the questions to be cases the standard framing doesn't already cover, because the textbook ones we have all read about a dozen times and everyone has their canned answer ready. Concretely: (a) a counterfactual whose antecedent itself contains another creature's free choice — if Ana had grown up in this city *and* Ben had freely become a priest, would Ana have converted? — since then the counterfactuals stop being independent of each other and I genuinely don't know which views can digest that; (b) a tie case, where the agent has two options she weighs as exactly equally good, or where she'd settle it with a coin flip, so there may be no fact of the matter about which she'd pick; (c) the very first moment of a creature's existence, where there's no prior character, habit, or disposition yet for the counterfactual to be grounded in; and (d) if it fits, whether God has middle knowledge of God's own free choices, or whether the whole apparatus only ever ranges over creatures. Feel free to swap one of mine for a better one if you think of a case that separates the views more cleanly.

Two more things. First, tell me which combinations of answers nobody actually occupies, and take a guess why — incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored — and I'd rather you say "this cell is empty and here's the pressure pushing people out of it" than quietly leave it off the key. Second, flag any pair of leaves my quiz can't separate rather than pretending it can: I suspect middle knowledge and a sophisticated simple-foreknowledge-plus-brute-counterfactuals position will collapse into each other across most of my cases, and that the Thomist view and outright determinism will too unless one question is specifically built to target whether the choice is still free in whatever sense the view needs. If a pair really can't be pulled apart by case verdicts, say so and tell me what kind of question would do it, or whether it just needs a direct commitment question and that's the honest answer. Keep it to something I can run in one evening — I'm printing it out, so cases need to be readable aloud and the options should be short. No need for citations or a reading list; we're not checking sources, we just want to see which of us lands where.
