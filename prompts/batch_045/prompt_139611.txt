Im putting together a years agenda for a tiny working group — three people, no empirical work, funded by a foundation that wants something an institutional designer could actually pick up and use — on races to the bottom: Moloch, multipolar traps, the situations where everyone involved would rather the whole group stopped doing X and each still has to keep doing it.

My rough taxonomy, which I half suspect is the wrong cut: thin traps, where the defecting move is one discrete observable action (doping, busting a quota, undercutting a floor); thick traps, where the race is smeared across hundreds of small un-auditable choices (universities optimizing measurable output, labs trimming safety margin in ways that look like ordinary speed, credential arms races in hiring); and proxy traps, where nobody defects at all and the race just runs through a metric nobody chose and nobody can opt out of — a ranking, a benchmark, an index.

The candidates for what the year should actually be about: for thin traps, why so many persist even when the participants can name the trap and talk to each other — is that really an enforcement problem, or a commitment problem, or something about who gets to draw the boundary of the group; for thick traps, whether any of the standard toolkit (monitoring, sanctions, exit guarantees) transfers at all, or whether they need a different lever, like changing what gets measured or cutting the number of axes competition runs along; for proxy traps, whether they are races to the bottom in the same sense, given there may be no defection event to coordinate against and no actor doing the choosing.

Which one deserves the year, and by what criteria — what am I trading away by picking it? I want the upstream/downstream structure: which question the others depend on, which is genuinely tractable in twelve months for three conceptual people, which is neglected because it falls between economics and philosophy and nobody owns it, and what evidence in the next year or two would move things up or down. Also say where my three-way split is doing its work badly and a different distinction would do it better. And if you think the crux the whole area quietly rests on is something I havent listed, say that instead of answering the question I asked.
