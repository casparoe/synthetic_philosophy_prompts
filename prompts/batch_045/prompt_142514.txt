So I've been in and out of hospitals a bit lately and something about the routine is bugging me. Every nurse who comes in asks for a pain score, 0 to 10, charts it, and there's a protocol for getting it down fast; if someone is dying and the distress can't be managed any other way they'll even sedate them right under. But nothing in the chart for happiness. Nobody has ever asked me to rate how well my life is going as opposed to how much it hurts, and there's no protocol, no consult service, no medication for "fine but flat." And it's not only bedside culture: the cost-effectiveness stuff used to decide which treatments get funded scores health states on a scale where being dead is 0 and full health is 1, with room to go negative for states worse than death but no room above 1 — you literally can't register a benefit bigger than "not unwell."

What I want to know is what this pattern actually presupposes. On the face of it it looks like it comes straight out of the suffering-focused playbook: that a unit of suffering prevented and a unit of happiness produced are not interchangeable, that the first has some kind of priority or a different kind of standing entirely. But I also suspect you could get the whole pattern out of much boringer commitments — untreated pain wrecks recovery, distress is loud and easy to notice, medicine is officially in the business of pathology not fulfilment, we have strict duties not to harm and only loose ones to benefit, or positive states are just harder to measure and harder to act on. So which parts of the practice strictly require the asymmetry, and which could it keep while giving the asymmetry up?

Two follow ups. First: if a hospital really did take the symmetric view — a unit of happiness worth exactly a unit of relieved suffering — what concretely would have to change, and would any of it be absurd, or just unfamiliar? Second: is the practice even consistent with itself from the suffering-focused side? It will keep people alive for months in states that on its own scoring sit below zero, and it pours enormous sums into keeping alive people who aren't in much distress at all, while the same money could go to cheap analgesics somewhere else. Does that show the practice never really ran on the asymmetry, or only that it runs on it halfway?
