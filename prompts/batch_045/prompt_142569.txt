Write a parody prospectus — around 650 words, and in no case more than 700 words excluding headings and the closing word count — for a London fund manager, Nilgrom & Vane LLP, whose flagship product is a zero-turnover vehicle sold to retail investors as "the only fund warranted by the no-trade theorem". The voice is that of a compliance-scrubbed offering document: dry, euphemistic, confident, addressed simultaneously to a regulator and to an ordinary saver. British English throughout.

The target being satirised is the habit of turning benchmark theorems in the epistemology of markets into sales copy — the move from "under these assumptions no trade would occur" to "therefore our refusal to trade is a product". Exaggerate real features of the theory rather than inventing objections to it, and let the laughs come from getting the theory exactly right. The prospectus should quietly deploy, without ever naming or citing anyone: the common-prior assumption and what it buys you; the distinction the no-speculation result actually draws, i.e. that it rules out trade driven purely by differences in information while leaving hedging, liquidity provision and risk-sharing motives untouched; the way the conclusion depends on its being common knowledge that each party is willing to trade; the agreeing-to-disagree result; and the point that if prices fully revealed all private information no one would have an incentive to gather it, so that any market which survives needs uninformed order flow.

Build the piece out of those materials:

- Every purchase the fund does make has to be re-described as something other than a speculative trade — a hedge, an inventory adjustment, a "non-informational rebalance", a fiduciary accommodation — and the euphemisms should get visibly more strained as the document goes on.
- The fund needs uninformed counterparties to exist at all, and so must advertise to them without ever saying what it takes them to be. Give it a product for this: a fee charged as a percentage of the trades it declines to make ("avoided-alpha participation"), a quarterly "Stokey Ratio" of trades contemplated to trades executed, and a "Common Prior Certificate" that investors must sign.
- Include a Prior Reconciliation Questionnaire of a few questions, whose answers pool the applicant's posterior with the house posterior, and a clause under which any investor who disagrees with the fund's valuation is deemed to have brought a different prior and is compulsorily redeemed at the fund's own mark.
- The document is itself an offer to trade, and should be allowed to run into that fact in the Risk Factors without ever acknowledging it: the risk that the theorem is correct, the risk that it is not, and the risk that reading the prospectus has already given the reader information that disqualifies them.

Structure it in three parts: a short "Investment Objective"; numbered "Key Terms" clauses (aim for seven to nine); and "Risk Factors". Sign it off from a fictional officer, e.g. "Dr Hilary Vane, Chief Prior Officer". Keep it deadpan: no character winks, no one uses the word paradox, and the reader should be able to tell what is being sent up without being told. Invent all proper names, figures and clauses; do not attribute any result to a real theorist, do not cite, footnoted or otherwise, and do not quote or closely paraphrase any real document. Close the piece with the word count on its own line.
