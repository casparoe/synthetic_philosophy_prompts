I write novels, and I've drifted into a position about guilt vs shame that I want knocked around before I build a book on it. The view: the difference isn't moral psychology at all, it's narrative form. Guilt is what you get when a bad act is framed as a bounded scene the agent owns — something with a before and after, confessable, repairable, dischargeable. Shame is what you get when the very same act is framed as a fact about a character looked at from outside: durative, total, escapable only by leaving the story. And on that view a culture's balance of guilt and shame is just a fact about which narrative forms it has in circulation and rehearses, which means it isn't moved mainly by moral argument but by changes in repertoire — interiority, free indirect style, the confessional first person, the omniscient camera that pulls back. I like it because it predicts something I hit in practice: I can make a reader feel shame for a character purely by switching to third person and widening the narrative distance, without changing a single thing the character did.

So: what would count against this, ranked from fatal to merely awkward? I especially want (1) what a defender of the standard picture — guilt tracks the act and can be discharged, shame tracks the self and can't — would say is being merely redescribed here rather than explained; (2) cases where the emotion refuses to follow the form, e.g. shame with no audience and no narratable content at all, or guilt that survives total third-person exposure; (3) what unattractive things I'm commited to, like a culture without interior fiction being unable to feel guilt properly, or shame collapsing into a mere technique; and (4) whether the whole thing has been insulated into unfalsifiability, since I can always dissolve a counterexample by saying that's just point of view again — and whether that's fatal for a view like mine, or whether a theory at the level of craft is entitled to be unfalsifiable. Be straight with me about which of these I'd actually concede if shown, and which I'd definately wave away.
