I'm a novelist and I've somehow ended up on the jury for a translated-fiction prize — six of us, two long weekends a year, a longlist of about a hundred and fifty down to one winner. The disagreements are brutal and mostly can't be settled by anyone reading harder, and I'm bad at them: I either dig in or cave inside five minutes, and I can't tell which one I'm doing at the time.

Half the books are in languages only one or two of us read, so half the time I'm arguing about a translation I can't check against the original; and we deliberate in the same room after reading each other's notes, so I suspect our verdicts aren't independent evidence at all, whatever that ought to mean for how much I move.

What I want is a procedure I can actually run in the ten minutes before each meeting and again when we deadlock, for how much to shift when the others disagree with me. The stuff I half-remember about disagreement between peers seems built for factual disputes, where there's one thing that's true and we're both trying to find it, but "the ending doesn't earn itself" or "this is the most alive book on the list" doesn't obviously work like that, and the output isn't my belief anyway, it's a joint list I put my name to. So give me the questions in order, what counts as a decent answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision back to us because it's a row about what the prize is for rather than a matter of evidence. It has to handle the cases where the other person has evidence I can't get at, like the original; where they know that literature far better than I do; where I'm the only one who loves the book; and where going along means signing a citation I think is wrong. Then run it on two: a book only I can read in the original that the other five are lukewarm about, and the book I found and fought for and should probably drop.

Please write it as continuous prose, no headings, bullets, bold or tables — like an email to a friend. Don't worry about typos, its fine.
