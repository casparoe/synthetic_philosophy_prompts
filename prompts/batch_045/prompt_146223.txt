I have to submit a two-page proposal for a one-year master's dissertation, about 12,000 words, two semesters, no empirical work, supervisor meeting me fortnightly. Here's the half-formed idea. Everyone treats the number the mugger names as a given and then argues about whether to multiply it out: cap the utility function, or say the prior shrinks faster than the payoff grows, or switch decision theory, or just bite the bullet and hand over the five dollars. But the number is a move in a game, not a datum. The mugger chose it, presumably to maximise what they expect to get from me, so its size is evidence about the mugger's model of me rather than about the mugger's power. And a claimant who was honest and had astronomically large stakes riding on my compliance would have an overwhelming incentive to supply cheap, checkable evidence of some kind; the total absence of cheap evidence seems to be doing a lot of the work here, and it isn't usually counted separately from the bare implausibility of the claim. So maybe the right frame is signalling and adversarial selection, not a decision problem with fixed probabilities handed to you.
