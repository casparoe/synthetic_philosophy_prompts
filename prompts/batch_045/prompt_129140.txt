I am drawing up the specification before I read any further into accounts of imprecise credence — credence represented by a set of probability functions rather than a single one. Fix the purpose as follows: the theory is for an agent who has to act anyway on the basis of the imprecision, not merely to record it — a regulator setting a safety standard, or an engineer choosing between two designs — in a case where the disagreement among defensible probability models is itself the difficulty, and where the cost of error is badly asymmetric between the two directions in which the truth might lie.

Write the desiderata: what a theory of rational imprecise credence has to deliver to count as adequate for that use. For each requirement give the case or consideration behind it, and mark it either non-negotiable or as the preference of one camp. I want the weight on the requirements and their motivations, not on scoring candidate views.

Among the questions I want the list to settle:

- the minimum of mere coherence — no sure loss, no combination of trades the agent would have accepted that guarantees a loss whichever way things go — and whether that minimum is anywhere near sufficient;
- what the theory owes us about updating: whether it must say which members of the set get conditioned and when, and whether it may ever let incoming evidence widen the interval (the dilation phenomenon) or must never do so;
- whether the theory must be able to learn from accumulating evidence — must the interval narrow toward precision, and on what schedule — or whether permanent, irreducible imprecision is a defensible stopping point;
- a decision rule: when a choice is permitted or required given an interval, and whether such a rule can respect asymmetric error costs without degenerating into taking the midpoint and pretending one is precise;
- two distinctions I have not seen drawn cleanly, which I want the list to respect: (i) imprecision that comes from thin or conflicting evidence, versus imprecision that comes from the hypothesis space itself being arbitrarily grained or badly defined; and (ii) imprecision about how confident to be, versus imprecision about which of several models to trust when all of them are empirically adequate so far. Say whether a single representational device — one convex set of probability functions — can carry both distinctions, or whether the theory needs extra structure on top of it.

Then, briefly: if some subset of your requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, say which ones and what has to give — that is the part I most want to see. And name the single requirement that excludes the most candidates, including the candidate of staying a precise Bayesian with a sensitivity analysis bolted on afterward.

Please write in American English, in prose organized around the requirements list; no bibliography or citations.
