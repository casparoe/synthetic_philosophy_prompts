I work on distributed databases that have to answer questions about the past: tables that record both when a fact held in the world and when it was recorded, multiversion concurrency control in which each transaction reads a snapshot of committed data taken when it began, append-only event logs, and clusters with no global clock where orderings are reconstructed from vector clocks. I am fluent in this vocabulary, so please do not explain it back to me.

Please restate the presentism/eternalism debate — including the growing-block view, the truthmaker objection to presentism, and the objection from the relativity of simultaneity — in that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. Tell me, in these terms, what existence is, what a truth about the past or the future is, what the present is, and what each of the three views is claiming. Then give me the residue report: which claims translate cleanly (I suspect snapshot isolation offers a natural home for a relativized presentism, and uncommitted writes for the truthmaker problem), which translate only by distorting something in one framework or the other, and which have no counterpart at all in the vocabulary of commits, snapshots, and logs.

For the residue, say whether it is a defect of my framework — something it cannot express because it presupposes an external clock, an observer-independent commit order, or a fixed set of tables — or whether it marks a point where the metaphysical debate is less substantive than it looks. In particular, press on the place where the analogy seems to help me most: my snapshots are mutually consistent only because the system picks a single serialization order, so my framework may be smuggling in exactly the privileged present that presentism needs and that the physics appears to deny.
