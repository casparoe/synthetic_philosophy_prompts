So I've been chewing on the Maimonides-on-sacrifice thing for a while and I want to turn it into a piece of satire for the forum. You know the bit I mean: in the Guide he basically says God didn't want animal sacrifice at all — the Israelites coming out of Egypt were habituated to it (the cult of the se'irim, the goat-demon things, the Egyptian animal worship, whatever), and human nature can't jump from one extreme straight to its opposite, so rather than banning the practice he kept the form and redirected the object. Same gestures, now aimed at him, and progressively hemmed in: one sanctuary, one priestly caste, restricted species, deliberately small scale, until the population could be weaned onto prayer and contemplation, which were the actual point. What I keep coming back to is how defintely weird the implications are. The practice gets legislated because of a fact about us, not because of a fact about it. And the weaning only works if the people being weaned don't notice that's what's happening — which is presumably why the account turns up in a book written for one guy and not in the law code, where the same commandments are presented as straightforwardly commanded and as having reasons.

What I want is a leaked internal strategy memo, 700 to 900 words, numbered sections, completely deadpan, from a fictional nonprofit in the rationalist/EA orbit that has raised money to "run the Rambam playbook" on practices it can't talk anyone out of. Pick two or three targets from the usual suspects — the pledge and its honorifics, the status/credential tournaments people enter "for the cause," the community's habit of narrating its own coordination failures as sacrifices to Moloch — and have the memo work out, in total sincerity, how you'd redirect rather than abolish each one: keep the form, swap the object, restrict the venue, shrink the scale, schedule the weaning.

Things it needs to actually do:

- The humor has to come from getting the reasoning right. The memo people should be competent. If someone who has read the Guide can't tell that you understand the accommodation reading, it failed.
- Handle the self-defeat problem in ops language, without ever naming it as a problem: the playbook requires the target population to not know it's a concession, so the memo needs a disclosure schedule, a need-to-know tier, something about information hazards and epistemic division of labor. Let it drift, straight-faced, into the conclusion that each cohort should be told the redirection was the original plan all along — the point is the weaning, not the truth.
- Include one risk-register entry and one FAQ item, and a footnote swatting away the rival camp that holds the details of the cult aren't ours to explain at all (the playbook only works if the practice is rationally manipulable, so that camp is an existential threat to the org's whole grant).
- Give it one invented skeptic — a community member quoted in the objections section — who points out that if this is a concession then the people running it are in the priests' position, and who is answered badly.

On fairness: don't make him a crypto-utilitarian or a crypto-atheist, and no cheap gore jokes or puns on scapegoat — the target is the accommodationist style of reasoning and our habit of reaching for it whenever abolishing something looks hard, not the religion and not religious people. He still thought the details were divinely legislated and he wrote a law code presenting them as commanded, so the memo can't be written by people who think he'd be on their side about that. And a reader who has never heard of the Rambam should still be able to tell what's being ironized — gloss any Hebrew term the first time, and don't wink at the audience anywhere.
