I'm a PhD student in philosophy, and I'm assembling a packet of short pieces for a graduate seminar on probability and symmetry; I'd like one satirical item for it.

Please write a mock standards bulletin — roughly 600 words, numbered clauses, deadpan regulatory voice — issued by a fictional body (a "Committee on Canonical Randomness" or the like) laying down binding Official Methods for generating random geometric objects when the problem statement underdetermines the measure: random chords of a circle, random lines crossing a region, and so on, as arise in insurance, lottery design, and randomized trials.

The target is the conventionalist response to Bertrand's paradox: that there is no fact of the matter as to which parametrization is correct, only a procedural convention, and that symmetry or maximum-entropy desiderata can supply a canonical one. I want the joke to come from following that response honestly rather than caricaturing it, so please get the substance right. The different generative procedures really do yield different answers; the invariance arguments (under rotation, translation, and scale) really do select a single one, but only by adding a substantive assumption about which features of the setup are irrelevant; and maximum entropy is uniformity stated in a chosen coordinate, not the absence of an assumption. Throughout, the committee should sound as though it is discovering the correct distribution while in fact legislating it.

The reductio should land in a closing annex. Extend the remit to a non-circular convex region — an ellipse, or the irregular outline of a floodplain — where rotational symmetry is unavailable and the invariance machinery delivers nothing, so the committee has to assign a number by fiat while continuing to describe it as derived. That should be the moment the reader sees the conventionalist answer as legislation in technical disguise.

Keep the piece legible to a first-year graduate student who has met the paradox once, and attribute nothing to real philosophers or papers.
