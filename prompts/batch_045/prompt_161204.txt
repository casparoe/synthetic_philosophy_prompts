I'm helping a small foundation that has maybe four or five million dollars to spend over three years, and one of the buckets they keep coming back to is the Great Filter / Fermi-paradox cluster. I need to tell them whether that bucket deserves any of the money at all, and if so which questions inside it. I don't want a survey of the paradox — I want a defended ordering, and I'm more interested in your reasoning than in the list.

Some framing so you can push back on it. I've been sorting candidate explanations into three families: origin filters (life or intelligence basically never gets going), expansion filters (it gets going plenty, but something reliably stops civilizations before they spread or before they last long), and what I've started calling audience filters — the civilization is really there, but nothing about it lands in our particular window. Beacons that are loud for two centuries and then fall silent; signals in a medium or band we'd never think to monitor; a trajectory toward low-power, inward-turned, very efficient computation that leaves almost nothing to see from outside even at a billion years of age. That last family assumes loudness isn't monotonic in technological age, and I think that assumption is doing more work in the usual discussion than anyone admits. I'm also inclined to write off the zoo / interdict / 'they're hiding' family as unfalsifiable — tell me if that's a mistake.

So: given finite money and three years, which open questions here should actually get worked on, and in what order? Say what your criteria are and how you're trading them off. I especially want: which questions are upstream of others, so answering them makes the rest cheaper or moot; which are tractable for people who aren't astronomers (philosophers, decision theorists, economists) versus which genuinely need new instruments or new data; which are neglected relative to how much the answer would change a decision; and which are famous but overrated, in the sense that even a clean answer wouldn't move what anyone does.

The decision at the end of it is whether the silence should shift effort anywhere at all — more sky-search, or the ordinary x-risk portfolio, or nothing — and I want to know which questions would really discriminate between those options. If the honest answer is 'spend it elsewhere', say that and explain what makes this cluster a bad buy.

Last thing: what would change your ranking over the next decade? Biossignature results from exoplanet atmospheres, a bigger null result from a survey, tighter bounds on whether anything artificial has ever passed through the solar system — which of those should we treat as the real tests, and what would each one do to the list?
