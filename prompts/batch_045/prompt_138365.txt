The question (20 marks; answer both parts):

(a) 8 marks. Parfit's Teletransporter scans the states of all your cells, destroys your body, and beams the information to Mars, where a machine builds a Replica of you out of new matter — someone who seems to remember your life, has your dispositions, character, intentions, and so on.
(i) State Parfit's Reductionist claim about what a person is and what personal identity consists in, and contrast it with the Non-Reductionist alternative. (3)
(ii) Explain what Parfit means by Relation R, and say how the claim that identity is not what matters is supposed to follow from it. (5)

(b) 12 marks. Now consider a machine I'll call the Fax-of-a-Fax. It works like the Teletransporter except that each scan is lossy: the Replica is built at 85% fidelity, keeping 85% of the scanned person's memories, dispositions and character traits (a random 85%), with the other 15% replaced by psychological features drawn from an archive of other people's scans. The Replica then uses the machine again the next day, and the next, for thirty days. Call the person who first steps into it A0, and the person who exists on day thirty A30.
Second case, the Split Fax: days 1 to 4 are lossless, producing A1 to A4; on day 5 the machine malfunctions and builds two Replicas from A4's scan, B5 and C5, each lossless and each exactly as A5 would have been, then destroys A4. B5 and C5 then each use the machine daily at 85% fidelity for the remaining 25 days, giving B30 and C30.
Discuss, using Parfit's framework:
(i) Does A0 survive, in the sense that matters, as A30? Does your answer depend on whether we count long chains of overlapping psychological connections, and does it depend on whether identity holds?
(ii) In the Split Fax case, how much should A0 care about B30 and about C30, compared both to the first case and to ordinary survival? Is A0 identical with B30, with C30, with both, or with neither — and does answering that settle the question about what matters?
(iii) Would any of this change if the Non-Reductionist were right? Say what the Non-Reductionist is committed to about these cases, and whether that commitment is plausible.

The student's answer:

Parfit's Reductionism says that a person is not a separately existing entity over and above their brain and body and the series of mental and physical events that make up their life. Personal identity just consists in more particular facts — facts about physical and psychological continuity — which could in principle be described without mentioning who anyone is. The Non-Reductionist denies this: they think personal identity is a further fact, something additional to any facts about continuity. In practice this means the Non-Reductionist has to believe in a Cartesian soul or ego, a separately existing thing whose identity explains why I am me.

Relation R is psychological continuity and/or connectedness, with any cause. Connectedness is the holding of direct psychological links — memories of earlier experiences, the carrying out of earlier intentions, similarities of character and belief. Continuity is the holding of a chain of such links, where each link overlaps the next. Parfit's point is that R is what matters in survival, and that identity is a different relation from R. Identity is one-one: it cannot be true both that I am X and that I am Y, where X and Y are two different people. So in the Split Fax case A0 cannot be identical with both B5 and C5, and since there is nothing to choose between them, Parfit's conclusion is that A0 survives as both of them — there are two future people who are each him. Because what matters is preserved twice over, fission is actually better than ordinary survival for the person who divides: you get double the survival, at no cost to anyone.

Teletransportation shows the same thing from another direction. If R can hold with any cause, then it does not matter whether my Replica is made of the same matter or has any causal connection of the usual kind to my brain; a Replica made of new matter on Mars is psychologically continuous with me, so what matters survives. And R can come in degrees: this is the point of the Combined Spectrum, where continuity reduces in small steps and the question of whether the person at one end is the same person as the person at the other end has no determinate answer, so we should stop asking about identity and ask about degrees of R instead.

In the Fax-of-a-Fax, the degree of R falls off very fast. Each trip preserves 85% of the previous person's psychology, so the proportion of A0's psychology shared with A30 is 0.85 to the power 30, which is less than 1%. At 30 days there is therefore no chain of psychological connection linking A0 to A30 at all: A30 has almost nothing of A0, and A0 would not recognise A30's memories or intentions as his own. So R holds to a vanishingly small degree, and Parfit should say that A0 barely survives as A30 — the Fax-of-a-Fax is nearly as bad as ordinary death, and A0 has almost no reason to care about what happens to A30. The same applies in the Split Fax case: B30 and C30 are each less than 1% psychologically connected to A0, so A0 should care about them only slightly, though he should care about both equally since they are exactly similar.

None of this would change much if the Non-Reductionist were right. The Non-Reductionist has to say that in the Split Fax exactly one of B5 and C5 is really A0 and the other is a mere copy, even though the two are intrinsically indiscernible. The only way to make sense of that is a soul, and since belief in a soul is scientifically implausible, the disagreement between Reductionism and Non-Reductionism really just comes down to whether some form of substance dualism is true.
