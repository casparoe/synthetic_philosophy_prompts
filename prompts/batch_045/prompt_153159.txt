Sorry if this is a bit niche, but its been bugging me for a while and I'd rather ask than read around it.

Relativists have a practice I keep bumping into and I can't work out what it commits them to. The official doctrine goes like this. Take two solutions of Einstein's field equations that differ only by a diffeomorphism — a smooth relabelling of which manifold point is which. Those are not two possible worlds; they are one world described twice. So nothing physical hangs on the identity of the points: the physical content of a model is only what survives the relabelling. That's the moral everyone draws from the hole argument. Picture a region of spacetime with no matter or fields in it at all (the "hole"), and a relabelling that shuffles the points around inside that region but leaves everything outside it exactly as it was. If the points had identities of their own, independent of the fields sitting on them, you'd have two models that agree everywhere up to the edge of the hole and then disagree inside it — same past, different future, for no reason at all. Determinism breaks for free. Absurd. So: treat the relabelled models as the same model, and count as observable only the quantities that are invariant under relabelling.

Fine. But now look at what people actually do at the keyboard. They build a numerical simulation, and the first thing they do is fix a gauge — pick coordinates, choose a lapse and a shift, lay down a grid — because without that the equations won't evolve. Then they read numbers off that grid: the metric component at this grid point at this time step, the coordinate radius of the horizon, the waveform extracted at some finite radius, the peak curvature reached during the merger. And those are exactly the sort of quantities the official doctrine says aren't physical. Some gauge conditions are pinned to physical landmarks — the time coordinate is the proper time of observers comoving with the matter, r is fixed by the areas of spheres — but plenty aren't: harmonic gauge, the popular slicing conditions for black hole collisions, all of it is just a convenient functional choice with no matter to hang it on. And then there are the parts of the simulation where there is no matter at all to anchor anything, i.e. vacuum regions that are basically the hole again, and people still seem perfectly happy to say what is happening "at" a point "at" a time.

So: what does this practice actually presuppose about spacetime? Does the fact that gauge-fixed simulations work — convergent, cross-checked against each other, matching observation — presuppose that the relabelling freedom is genuinely surplus structure, so that the practice only makes sense if something like relationalism is true, or at least if points have no identity of their own beyond the fields (the anti-haecceitist thing, whatever it's called)? Or can someone who thinks the manifold points are real objects with primitive identities — a substantivalist, I suppose — explain why the practice is fine anyway, e.g. by treating the gauge-fixed model as a mere representative of an equivalence class and the grid numbers as an encoding of invariant facts about the whole class? I want the strict presuppositions separated from what the practice merely sits comfortably with, and I want to know where it's underdetermined: where several quite different metaphysical stories would license exactly the same behaviour at the keyboard.

Three things that make it sharper for me.

One. I've started calling the two kinds of gauge fixing tethered and free-floating. Tethered = the condition is stated by appeal to some physical field or landmark. Free-floating = it's just a convenient equation in the coordinates. My sense is people are much more relaxed about trusting tethered gauges, and that when a run produces something weird in a free-floating gauge the first diagnosis is "gauge artefact". Doesn't that asymmetry show the practice secretly relies on the relationalist idea — that coordinates only mean something when pinned to matter — even though the official doctrine says all gauges are equally unphysical and therefore equally fine? And if so, is "the gauge is just bookkeeping" false, or at least only half the story?

Two. Imagine a run in a genuinely matter-free, symmetry-free region — no dust, no horizons, nothing to tether to — and it spits out a blow-up. There's no landmark to relate it to, so there's no invariant way to say whether that's a physical blow-up at a place or a coordinate pathology of that particular gauge. What would the practice have to say? Does it presuppose there's always enough physical structure lying around to anchor the gauge, and what happens to the practice when that presupposition fails? People worry about something like this for quantum gravity, where there may be no fixed background of landmarks at all.

Three, and this is the bit I want a straight answer on. Suppose relativists decided the manifold points really do have primitive identities and the relabelled models really are distinct possibilities. Would anything at the keyboard change? Would any of the trust in tethered gauges, any of the "gauge artefact" diagnosing, any of the reading-off of local quantities become unjustified? Or is the whole practice compatible with every story, in which case the hole argument is doing no work in the practice even though everyone cites it as the thing that licenses the practice? If it is doing work, say where — what's the thing people do that they couldn't do, or couldn't justify, if the substantivalist were right?

Don't give me a literature dump, I can't look any of it up anyway. Just reconstruct the implicit view, tell me which bits of the practice force which bits of it, and be explicit about what stays exactly the same under the rival views.
