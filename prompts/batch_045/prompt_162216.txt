ok so slightly odd request: I'm running a small reading group on intentionality (Chinese Room last week, Dennett's two-bitser this week) and I want to close the last session with a poem rather than a summary. Write a sestina — six stanzas, the six end-words rotated properly, plus a three-line envoy — spoken in the voice of the two-bitser itself, the vending machine that clicks into state Q when a US quarter goes in and can't tell a Panamanian balboa from a quarter, so there's no saying whether that's a misrepresentation or a brand-new state meaning balboa.

I want a sestina specifically because of what the form does: the end-words stay the same six words but every stanza drops them somewhere new, so what a word does is purely a matter of where the scheme puts it, nothing intrinsic to it. That's the derived-intentionality picture, and I'd rather the form carried it than the speaker announcing it. One end-word should be a coin or money term (quarter, say), so that as it rotates it keeps threatening to mean something else entirely — mercy, a district, a fraction — which is the balboa problem in miniature.

The twist I want in there: make it a wandering two-bitser. Long after the last designer and the last customer are dead it still clicks into Q, and later someone reconditions it so the very same physical state now vends a bus ticket when a washer goes in — same state, new aboutness, borrowed from new users. So it has to face what the state means when no one is left to borrow intentions from, and whether content that floats free of the physics is content at all.

No cheap Dennet victory lap: the envoy should let the other intuition bite, that there is a fact of the matter about what the state means and that this is why we take our own case to be different. Someone who knows the debate should be able to tell the misrepresentation-or-new-state move from the Mother Nature move; someone who doesn't should still just find it a good poem.
