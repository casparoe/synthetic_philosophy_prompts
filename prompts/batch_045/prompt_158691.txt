I want to pin down how much of evolutionary psychology is genuine science and how much is just-so-storytelling, and I want a number rather than a tour of the literature. So take this hypothetical case, invented for the question. A preregistered adversarial collaboration reports that men rate the body odor of women in the high-fertility phase of the cycle as more pleasant than the same women's odor in the luteal phase. The effect shows up in all 30 sampled societies, including several small-scale ones with little exposure to Western media, at around d = 0.3; it is uncorrelated with raters' conscious beliefs about fertility and with how much they say they like the donor's face; it tracks salivary estradiol assays rather than self-reported cycle day; and it vanishes when the donors use hormonal contraception.

Give me your credence that the best explanation of this pattern is an evolved psychological adaptation specifically for detecting fertility, as opposed to a byproduct, an artifact, or a real regularity whose adaptive history this design cannot identify. Decompose it: your prior that selection builds domain-specific mechanisms for mate-relevant cues of this kind; how much the cross-cultural robustness moves you, given what such designs can and cannot exclude; how much the contraception condition is really worth, given what else differs between users and non-users; the base-rate term, meaning how often preregistered multi-site effects of this size turn out to be real at all; and whether the adaptation-versus-byproduct contrast is well defined here in the first place. A number and one line of justification per term, then the combination.

My own credence is around 0.5 and a colleague's is 0.85. Tell me which of those terms our disagreement most likely lives in, which term you are least confident about, and what single further experiment would move your number the most.
