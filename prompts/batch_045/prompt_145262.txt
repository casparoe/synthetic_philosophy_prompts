I teach an advanced-undergraduate course on thermal physics. In the lecture on the second law I disposed of Maxwell's demon in a single move, by way of the ratchet-and-pawl gadget that Feynman used in his introductory lectures: a windmill with angled teeth held by a spring-loaded pawl, sitting in a gas at temperature T, is meant to rectify fluctuations and lift a small weight, and it fails because the pawl is itself at temperature T, jiggles, and lets the wheel slip back — no net work unless the pawl is kept colder than the wheel, in which case what you have is an ordinary heat engine between two temperatures. I then said, as I have heard any number of physicists say, that the demon is the same device in another costume: it too is a one-way door that equilibrium refuses to supply, and everything written later about bits, memory and the cost of erasure is a redescription of an ordinary thermal failure rather than a separate insight.

A strong student caught me afterwards with a question I could not answer crisply: if the two really are the same device, why does the ratchet need a second temperature while the demon is said to need a blank tape? What plays the role of the pawl in the demon, and what plays the role of the bit in the ratchet? I would like an audit of the analogy, both to answer her and because I now doubt the stronger claim I half made — that the information-theoretic apparatus is dispensable. Please do the following.

1. Lay out the correspondence explicitly, as a table: for the ratchet (wheel, angled teeth, pawl, spring, gas, load, second temperature, net rotation) and for the demon (gas, partition or trapdoor, measurement, memory cell, reset operation, tape or register, work per cycle, heat dumped to the bath, bath at T). Mark the cells where the target has no counterpart and say what that absence means rather than leaving it blank.

2. Test both devices in one fixed limit, so that the comparison is not decided by who gets to assume more friction: a single heat bath at temperature T; all motion quasistatic; the axle, teeth and pawl free of dry friction and viscous drag beyond what follows from contact with the gas; the load coupled without slipping; the demon's measurement and trapdoor motion performed arbitrarily slowly and without error. In that limit, run four devices for many cycles: the ratchet; the demon with an inexhaustible supply of blank tape that never erases; the demon with a finite register of N bits that it cyclically resets by thermal equilibration with the bath; and the demon that avoids erasure altogether by reversing its own computation so that the record is destroyed with no dissipation. For each, state whether net work is delivered over a full cycle, what resource is consumed, and where the entropy goes. I want a resource ledger rather than a verdict.

3. Say where the mapping breaks, and sort the disanalogies into those that matter for the work the analogy is doing and those that do not. My own suspicion is that the important one is that the ratchet's state carries no accumulating record while the demon's does, and that its failure is a detailed-balance fact about a thermal mechanism while the demon's is a bookkeeping fact about correlations that a mechanism alone does not track. Tell me if that is right, and if it is, tell me whether the ratchet is best regarded as a special case of a demon whose memory is continuously and irreversibly reset by the bath.

4. Consider a variant a colleague proposed, which I could not settle: the demon is given a lossless compressor and allowed to reclaim tape by compressing its record, in a gas where the same molecules recur so that the record is not a fresh random bit each cycle. Does the per-cycle cost then become k_B T ln 2 multiplied by the entropy rate of the record rather than k_B T ln 2 per operation, and can a demon in a correlated gas thereby turn a profit? State the correct form of the bound and explain why compression cannot be used to beat it.

5. Force the analogy in both directions and report which failure mechanism wins: a ratchet geared to a bank of bistable cells that records its own net rotation and is never reset; and a demon whose memory bit is stored as the angular position of a ratchet wheel, reset by tilting the wheel so that it drops to a standard state. For each hybrid, does it fail for the ratchet's reason or the demon's reason, if it fails at all?

6. Give the verdict on two separate conclusions: that no device can cyclically extract work from a single bath at uniform temperature, and that the information-theoretic treatment is dispensable because the failure is already visible in the thermal physics of the mechanism. Say which survives, which was being carried by the analogy rather than by the physics, and, if a better analogy exists — a device or an everyday transaction that tracks the resource the demon actually consumes — propose it.

Constraints: no citations, references, reading suggestions or named papers; the reasoning should be self-contained and carry through from the physics of the devices themselves, with Landauer's bound used only as a formula whose content you state. Define every symbol you introduce. Flag any place where you must make a modelling choice, and say whether your verdict flips under the alternative. Assume for audience a student who has had one course in thermal physics and none in information theory, and finish with the single paragraph I should say to her in office hours.
