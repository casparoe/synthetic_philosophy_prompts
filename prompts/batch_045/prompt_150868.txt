Me and two others on our town's heritage working group have scraped together a small grant (about £6k, six months part-time) to write guidance for the council on what to do about street names that honour people we'd now disown — slave traders, colonial governors, that sort of thing. Statues have been argued to death everywhere and I don't want to just copy those arguments across.

My half-formed idea, which I've been chewing over on my own since the row last year (I've no philosophy background at all, I work in a school office): a statue is something you can choose to walk past, but a street name is something the council makes people say and write over and over — on forms, letters, food deliveries. So the complaint isn't just that a commemoration exists somewhere, it's that thousands of residents are made to repeat it. Second thing: I think people conflate honouring someone with simply marking them. Half the people on that street have no idea who the name refers to any more, so maybe it's stopped commemorating anything at all, which would mean there's less at stake than the statue case — or maybe it's worse, because the tribute is now invisible and nobody can answer it. And the hassle of renaming lands on the residents, who never asked for any of this.

Can you turn that into a short proposal, about two pages? I need: the question put sharply enough that it could actually have an answer; why the standard statue moves (contextualise it, move it to a museum, don't erase history) don't settle the street-name version; what I'd actually do month by month, given I can talk to residents and councillors and read council minutes but can't do proper acedemic library research; what a genuinely useful deliverable looks like for a council that has to decide something by next year; and an honest bit on how the project could fail or turn out too big for six months part time. Please write it in British English, and don't give me any reading list or citations — I've no library access and I'll slot in whatever references are needed myself.
