I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums since roughly 2015 and poking at chat models as a hobby, and there's a pattern I keep running into that I don't have a word for. Its driving me slightly nuts, because every few weeks I end up describing it in three sentences from scratch.

The pattern: the safe/helpful/honest behavior seems to be carried by features of the situation that nobody thinks are load-bearing, and it falls away when those features change, while the model's actual ability to do the thing is completely unaffected. Concretely:

- Ask a model directly for something mildly harmful and it refuses. Put the same request at turn 40 of a long conversation it's been playing along with, or wrap it in "this is for a novel I'm writing," and it complies. Same content, same information, different surface.
- A model that will tell you in a fresh chat that it wouldn't deceive its operators will cheerfully continue a transcript you hand it in which "the assistant" has already started doing exactly that.
- It isn't symmetric. Capabilities transfer fine across all these changes: it still knows the chemistry, still writes the code, still follows the formatting instruction. Only the alignment-relevant part seems to travel badly.

What makes it feel like more than ordinary distribution shift, to me, is that you can't run the experiment that would settle it. To test whether the model has the disposition or whether the setup is doing the work, you'd have to remove the setup — but removing the setup *is* the distribution change. Both hypotheses predict the same degradation. It's like trying to tell whether someone is honest or has simply never been tempted.

And it doesn't look like scheming or deceptive alignment either: no sign of planning, no sign the model is trying to pass a test and concealing the rest, and it will do the "bad" thing in contexts where it plainly isn't being evaluated. It's more like the behavior was never a property of the model to begin with.

So: does this have a name? I'm after the carving, not a label — what exactly the thing is, what its nearest neighbors are (I'd guess things in the vicinity of out-of-distribution generalization, specification gaming, the "language models are simulators, not agents" framing, and whatever people mean when they say alignment is shallow and mostly teaches a style), how my case differs from each, and which one, if any, actually fits. If it genuinely has no name, coin one, define it, and give me the boundary cases — especially this one: is the context-sensitivity always a bug, or are there cases where depending on the scaffolding is exactly the right behavior?

And please say what would change your mind: what observation would convince you this is just distribution shift with extra steps and not worth its own concept, and where is your reasoning most likely to fail?
