For a graduate seminar on social epistemology I need a taxonomy — a branching tree of eight to ten leaves — of the ways a community can end up systematically cut off from information or sources it would otherwise have had. Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of names: every branch node should carry the single question that separates it from its siblings, phrased so that a student could answer it about a concrete case without already knowing the vocabulary. Candidate splits I want considered, though you should use your own judgment about which are load-bearing: is the outside material absent altogether, or present but discounted; is the discounting general or directed only at sources that disagree; is the filtering produced by members' own dispositions to trust and to share, or by a selection mechanism they did not choose and may not know is operating; can a member notice the pattern and leave at acceptable cost, and does the community let them; does the community merely consume filtered material, or does it manufacture its own.

Leaves should carry the established label where one exists — the contrast between the structure that excludes by omission and the one that discredits outside sources is now standard, as are plain selective exposure and the case where apparent closure is really just warranted distrust — and should be marked unnamed where a combination is unoccupied, with one sentence on why nobody sits there: incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Two combinations must receive leaves and be treated as genuine possibilities even though I do not think they have settled names. First, a community that does not only discredit outside sources but produces its own ersatz evidence base, so that members' beliefs are supported by member testimony, fabricated or synthetic reports, and citations that circulate only inside the group; give this leaf whatever name you think fits and say how it differs, for remedial purposes, from the ordinary case. Second, the mirror image: a community that discounts its own members and defers to outside authorities, ending up estranged from local knowledge it was better placed to have.

The angle that should decide which distinctions matter: our final session is on remedies, so attach to each leaf a note on what the natural intervention would be — supplying missing content, repairing trust in outside sources, lowering the cost of exit, rebuilding internal testimonial confidence, or something the taxonomy cannot express — and flag explicitly any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate, and any real case that seems to land in more than one leaf at once. Please keep it to the taxonomy and the diagnostics; I do not need a literature review, and I would rather have your own names for unoccupied cells than a survey of who has said what.
