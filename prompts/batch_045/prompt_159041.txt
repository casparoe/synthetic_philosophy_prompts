I've been reading about the Nordic countries on my own for the past couple of years — I have no background in economics or political philosophy; I just got interested after working on a project with a team in Copenhagen and started picking up books and articles — and I've ended up with a package of views that I suspect doesn't hold together, but I can't locate the fault line. I'm meant to give a short talk to a local discussion group next month, and I'd rather be caught out now than in front of them. So I'd like you to audit the set rather than explain the topic to me. Where I've landed:

(1) The familiar claim that you have to choose between equality and prosperity looks to me to be simply false: these countries tax and spend on a scale that would be called ruinous anywhere else and are still rich, productive and commercially creative, so the trade-off argument against redistribution must be much weaker than it is usually made out to be.

(2) The mechanism is trust: people pay because they expect others to pay and expect to be looked after in turn, and what keeps that going is that benefits are universal — the same service for everyone, including people who could easily buy their own — rather than means-tested, because means-testing produces resentment and a two-tier citizenry.

(3) But I also think the package is most likely not exportable to somewhere large, unequal and low-trust like the United States or Britain: the trust looks like an inheritance from a long history of ethnic homogeneity and a particular kind of Lutheran civic culture, so you cannot pass the institutions first and wait for the trust to show up afterwards.

(4) On how the money is raised and spent, I am drawn to the view that if the point of the exercise is to improve the position of the worst off, you do better with the same revenue by targeting cash at people who need it than by handing it to everyone, including people who do not.

(5) I am instinctively distrustful of cross-country statistical comparisons — too many things differ at once, institutions are too path-dependent for that kind of evidence to carry much weight — though I notice that this is more or less the kind of evidence I used to arrive at (1) and (3).

(6) And I hold, without feeling any strain, that these are also thoroughly market-friendly societies: open to trade, light on corporate tax, easy to hire and fire, with the state mostly taxing and transferring rather than owning industry — which is a large part of why I find them attractive.

Go through the six and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly; which apparent conflicts dissolve once the claims are stated more carefully, and what the clarification is that dissolves them; for every real tension, what the cheapest fix would be and what it would cost me elsewhere in the set; and, if there is one revision that would clean up the most, which it is. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious, and say plainly if one of my commitments is not a coherence problem at all but simply a belief I should drop. I don't want sources, citations or a reading list — I can find those myself, and I'd rather spend the words on the argument.
