I'm applying for a six-month funded fellowship and need a two-page proposal (around 900 words) for the project sketched below. I'll insert the citations myself, so refer to positions and arguments descriptively and don't spend space on a literature survey.

The background I can take for granted: in the program-equilibrium treatment of the one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma, each player submits a program that is shown the other player's program and outputs C or D, and mutual cooperation becomes achievable because each can submit a program that cooperates just in case the other program's source is identical (or provably equivalent) to its own. My half-formed idea is that this result depends on the players having perfect reciprocal access to each other's mechanism, and that the interesting question is what survives when access is partial. Consider a variant in which there is a fixed, publicly known recognition channel φ: each player submits a program and then receives only the signal the channel yields for the opponent's program. Full source inspection, a cryptographic hash of the source, a bounded sketch, a record of the other program's outputs on a public suite of test inputs, and no signal at all are all special cases.

Please write the proposal with:

1. A precise statement of the question, including a formalization of the game with the channel, and an explicit decision about whether programs may also simulate the opponent on chosen inputs or may only read the signal — and why that choice matters.

2. Why the standard identity-checking construction leaves the question open. My suspicion is that there is a genuine trade-off and that "more information is better" is false here: a channel fine enough to make mimicry risky is also so fine that it fails to recognize a cooperator that is the same mechanism differently implemented, while a channel coarse enough to tolerate reimplementation lets a defector imitate a cooperator's signal. Say whether this framing is right, and if not, what the real structure is.

3. A six-month plan with phases, arguing for the approach most likely to produce an actual theorem and against the obvious alternatives. I have in mind either a characterization of the channels that support cooperation or an impossibility result for channels required to be both robust to reimplementation and safe against mimicry; pick whichever is more promising and say why.

4. What the possible conclusions are, what would count as the project failing, and an honest note on scope — I would rather have a sharp result about a narrow family of channels than a loose general theory.

Write it as continuous prose, in a form I could submit.
