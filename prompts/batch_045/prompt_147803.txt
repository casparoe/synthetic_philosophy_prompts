Our reading group did some Marx this year — the bit about machines, from the Grundrisse I think, though nobody could confirm which pages — and I built an argument out of it that I now think must be broken somewhere, because I don't accept the conclusion. I cant find the hole though. Here it is:

(1) The value of a thing, on this view, is the socially necessary labor time that goes into making it, and profit exists because workers produce more value than theyre paid.

(2) Only living labor creates new value. A machine is itself a product of past labor, so it hands its own value over to the product bit by bit, and adds nothing new on top.

(3) Capitalists are pushed by competition to automate. So over time a smaller and smaller share of production is done by living labor, and a bigger and bigger share by machines.

(4) If profit comes out of living labor, and there's less living labor relative to everything else, then the rate of profit has a built in tendency to fall, and not because of any accident.

(5) Run that far enough and you get something wierd: a heavily automated setup turns out enormous amounts of stuff with almost no labor in it. Wealth and labor time come apart. The thing that's supposed to be the measure stops tracking what it measures.

(6) So the theory, if it's true, predicts its own obsolescence — it says here is what value is, and also that the system it describes is busy making value stop being that. A theory that says of itself that it will stop being true isn't a good theory. So (1) is false, or at best only true for a while.

A friend says (6) isn't absurd at all, that value was always supposed to be a temporary historical form and my argument just restates that. But if that's the answer I dont see how (1) is a claim about what value *is* rather than about what it used to be in one period.

Which step do you reject, why did it look fine, and what does rejecting it cost me? If I have to choose I'd rather keep (1) through (3), since those feel like the actual insight, but I want to know what I'm paying for them.
