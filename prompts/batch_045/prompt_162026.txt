I'm a senior writing an independent paper for my school's philosophy club on whether qualia are a coherent posit, and I've built an argument that now worries me, because it keeps going past the point where I wanted it to stop.

The setup: suppose a neurosurgeon rewires your colour system overnight so that the channels carrying red and green are swapped, and — this is the stipulation that matters — she also adjusts everything downstream: your discriminations, your memories of what things looked like yesterday, your verbal reports, your reactions to ripe versus unripe fruit. Nothing you do, say, or could ever be brought to notice distinguishes the before from the after. Call this a sealed inversion: a difference in what an experience is like that leaves no trace in anything relational.

(1) A quale is by definition an intrinsic property of an experience: what the experience is like in itself, as opposed to any property it has in virtue of its causes, its effects, or its functional role.
(2) Because a quale is intrinsic in that sense, it can vary while the experience's total causal and functional profile stays fixed — so sealed inversions are possible.
(3) If a sealed inversion is possible, then no possible evidence, for anyone, could ever settle which quale a subject has: not third-person observation, not neuroscience, not the subject's own introspection, since any such evidence would be a relational difference of exactly the sort (2) excludes.
(4) If no possible evidence could bear on which quale a subject has, then there is no fact of the matter about which quale they have; a posit that no inquiry could in principle adjudicate is empty.
(5) So there is no fact of the matter about what anyone's experience is like.
(6) But this swallows more than I meant to establish. If it holds, there is no fact about whether there is anything it is like to be me at all — no difference between me and something functionally identical to me with the lights off inside — and no fact that my headache hurts, which I seem to know more securely than I know anything about my own functional organisation.

Which step should I reject, and why did it look safe when I wrote it? If I have to choose I would sooner keep (1) and (2) than (4), which feels like the thinnest plank, but I want to know what each option actually costs me — including what it would cost to keep (1) and (2) and simply accept (5) and (6). Please name the step and price the alternatives rather than just telling me whether qualia exist.
