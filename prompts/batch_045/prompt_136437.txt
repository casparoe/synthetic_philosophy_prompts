Someone in my local rationalist group chat posted this and I can't find the hole. It starts out about Ulysses contracts and ends up eating half the toolkit, which makes me think there's an equivocation somewhere I'm not seeing.

(1) You can never gain by removing an option from yourself. If you didn't want to take it, you just wouldn't take it, so holding an open option has non-negative value.
(2) A Ulysses contract is exactly a removal or taxing of your own future options -- tying yourself to the mast, locking the fridge, one of those commitment-contract sites where you lose money if you miss your target, garbage taken out before the ice cream is in the house.
(3) So precommitment is only ever a fix for akrasia. A fully rational agent gains nothing from it, and the only reason to bind yourself is that you expect your future self to screw up its own means-end reasoning.
(4) But a future self that screws up means-end reasoning also won't reliably honor the precommitment. Either the binding works because later-you decides to go along with it, in which case akrasia eats it, or it works externally, in which case later-you isn't choosing at all, just being moved -- and being moved isn't agency, so it isn't the thing earlier-you could have been rationally choosing for.
(5) So precommitment helps nobody: the rational don't need it, and the akratic can't use it.
(6) Same shape of argument kills everything nearby: alarm clocks, deleting the game off your phone, promises, contracts, constitutional supermajority rules, central bank independence, and even deliberately building a habit or going to therapy to debug yourself. If later-you executes the habit, later-you was never akratic and didn't need it; if later-you doesn't, it won't execute the habit either.
(7) Alarm clocks obviously work and I'm pretty sure constitutional entrenchment does something. So one of (1)-(5) is false.

I'd most want to keep (1) -- it feels like its just the definition of option value -- and I'd least mind dropping (4), except that (4) also feels airtight and I can't name what's wrong with it. I tried running the whole thing under the framing where your future selves are a population of agents in an iterated game and it just got worse, because then (1) is a claim about one agent and doesn't transfer, but it's also unclear who the "nobody" in (5) is.

Which step do you reject, where exactly is the slide, and what does rejecting it cost me? And is there any reading of (3) that survives -- or is it just false that precommitment is only ever about akrasia?
