My epistemology supervisor has handed down verdicts on a run of testimony cases in supervisions, and I've got to present next weeks case to her, so I want to guess right. In every case the question is only whether the hearer is justified, and she stipulates that the claim testified to is true and that the hearer has no other route to it.

Her verdicts so far:

- A stranger at a bus stop tells you the time; you have no reason to trust or distrust her: justified.
- The same stranger launches into a long, elaborate story about a conspiracy at her own workplace, with nothing to back it up: not justified.
- A warm, well-meaning friend who has been wrong about departmental gossip many times tells you who got the lectureship: not justified.
- A hospital consultant tells you a diagnosis inside her specialty; her name and title are on the door, you have no track record with her personally: justified.
- An unsigned card pinned in a museum says the vase is 9th century; you've no idea who wrote it: justified, and she insisted this really is a case of testimony and not something else in disguise.
- Someone who has lied to you repeatedly about money tells you the name of the capital of a country you know nothing about: justified; she said unreliability about one topic doesn't carry over to another.

My case: you're in an unfamiliar city and you ask a phone app — the kind that produces fluent sentences by fitting statistical patterns in a large corpus of human text — for the closing time of a museum. It answers "17:00", with a standing note on screen that answers may be inaccurate. It has no beliefs and no intentions, doesn't know you asked, can't be held to account, and might give you a different answer to the same question tomorrow. Over the past year you've asked it several hundred things whose answers you could check and it's been right about nine in ten, but you can't tell in advance which tenth it gets wrong, and you've no idea whether opening hours fall in the good nine or the bad one.

Set out the two or three rules that fit her six verdicts about equally well — I can't tell whether she needs positive evidence about the particular source, or only a global track record for testimony in general plus no defeater, or something like a default entitlement that only defeaters remove, or maybe an assurance/trust rule on which the source has to be able to take responsibility for what it says. Say what each predicts about the app case and why, give me your best guess at her verdict with your reason for preferring it, tell me which of her earlier verdicts is the closest analog and which one is doing the most work in narrowing things down, and describe the single further case you'd put to her to seperate whichever rules you think are still tied.
