my friend and I spent like two hours last night arguing about whether Harsanyi's aggregation theorem actually proves anything and I need to get it out of my system. Her version: grant that each person's preferences satisfy the vN-M axioms (so each has a utility function unique up to positive affine transformation), grant that social preferences over social lotteries satisfy those axioms too including independence, grant Pareto — and the social welfare function is forced to be a weighted sum of individual utilities; run the same thing behind a veil of ignorance with equal chances of being anyone and the weights come out equal, so you get average utilitarianism. My version: the weights are doing all the work, and because each person's function can be rescaled however you like, a weighted sum can be fitted to more or less any record of past decisions, so it's close to vacuous unless utility gets measured independently of the policy it's supposed to justify. Also, vN-M utility measures nerve, not how well a life goes.

Write me a satire that lands my side without ever stating it. Form: an internal circular from a fictional federal Bureau of Interpersonal Comparisons announcing its new official social welfare function. It should: measure each citizens's vN-M utility from lottery choices over money; set the weights by least-squares fitting the weighted sum to forty years of actual budget outcomes ('revealed social preference'); then declare that sum to be the SWF for all future policy. Include a clause on people whose fitted utility function comes out concave ('risk-averse malcontents — weight may be reduced'), a deadpan footnote about the recommended policy crashing the economy so that everyone ends up worse off than under the safe one, and one short quoted comment from an outside ethics consultant who raises a real objection as a compliance worry. 600-700 words, deadpan, and the memo should never name Harsanyi or any real philosopher — a social choice theorist should read it and wince.
