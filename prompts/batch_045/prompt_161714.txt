My brother-in-law is totally unbothered by the gradual-replacement version of mind uploading, and he has one move he keeps making: it's like transcribing a song for a different set of instruments. The tune doesn't stop being itself when a cover band plays it on syntheizers instead of guitars — the song is the thing, the instrments are just what carries it. So swapping your neurons for functionally equivalent chips, a batch at a time, is obviously fine, and anyone worried about substrate is just fetishizing the original hardware.

The version of the procedure I actually care about is messier than that, though. Each chip is calibrated by watching the neuron it replaces for a few weeks of ordinary life and copying its observed input–output behavior; for firing patterns it never saw during calibration it interpolates from the ones it did see. The chips also run a few percent slower than the real thing, and the rest of the network partly compensates. A thousand or so get swapped per night while you're asleep, so there's never a moment with two streams of experience running side by side.

The interpolation bit is what I keep getting stuck on: a score is fully specified up front, a neuron's function isn't, so the chip is only guaranteed to match on inputs it already saw.
