I work in probabilistic machine learning — latent-variable models, variational and MCMC inference, and a fair amount of time spent worrying about identifiability, reparameterization, and when two apparently different models are really the same model written twice. A physicist friend and I have been arguing about the measurement problem for a while now; his line is that the whole dispute dissolves once you notice that it is only about whether you reify the posterior, and that the interpretation literature is a fight over a modeling decision engineers make without noticing. I want to test that claim properly rather than either accept it or wave it away, so I would like you to do the translation honestly and then tell me what it lost.

Concretely: restate the measurement problem and then each of the three main interpretive stances — Copenhagen-style collapse views, Everett, and de Broglie–Bohm — in the vocabulary I actually use: priors and model classes, latent variables and their posteriors, likelihoods and data-conditioned updating, identifiability and non-identifiability, reparameterization and auxiliary variables, exchangeable sequences and mixture components, and exogenous interventions that are stipulated rather than modeled. I would like the translation to cover at least these: (i) whether the wave function is ontic or epistemic, including the psi-ontic/psi-epistemic classification and the Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph result that a pure state cannot be merely information about an underlying physical state, given an independence assumption about independently prepared systems; (ii) what the Born rule is supposed to be — a sampling distribution for a latent, a frequency across realized branches, a measure of typicality, or a credence an agent should have — and whether probabilistic talk inside a deterministic branching theory has any counterpart at all; (iii) the preferred-basis problem, meaning whether branches are a discovered factorization of the joint distribution or a coordinate choice with no invariant content, and how close that is to what identifiability results for factorization say in my own field; (iv) Bell-type nonlocality as the exclusion of a class of latent-variable models satisfying a conditional-independence or separability condition, and Bohmian trajectories as additional latent structure that leaves every predictive distribution unchanged — I suspect they look like auxiliary variables introduced to make an otherwise ill-posed generative process well defined, the way momentum or slice variables are, except that Bohmians take them literally, and I want you to say how far that runs and where it breaks; (v) the Heisenberg cut as a decision about where to stop modeling and treat the remainder as data or as an unmodeled intervention, and Wigner's-friend-style cases as what happens when two agents place the cut differently.

Then give me the residue report. For each item, tell me what translated cleanly, what translated only after you quietly altered the claim, and what could not be said in my vocabulary at all. I especially want to know whether my framework has any expression for the datum that we experience exactly one outcome rather than the whole posterior, whether self-locating uncertainty across branches has a real counterpart in how I handle mixture components, indexical information, and selection effects, and whether there is anything in it that answers to the correctness of a cut as opposed to its computational convenience. Where something does not translate, say whether that is a blind spot in the probability-and-inference vocabulary — something I would have to add, such as an explicit model of the observer with a self-locating state — or whether it is a sign that the philosophers' claim was less clear than it looked. Please flag anywhere you caught yourself wanting to smuggle in an assumption that is natural in my vocabulary but contested in theirs, for instance the assumption that a state is always a state of information. I would find it most persuasive if you closed by telling me which part of my friend's deflationary claim survives the exercise, and which part of it cannot even be stated in the vocabulary he is implicitly appealing to.
