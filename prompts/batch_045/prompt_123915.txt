my flatmate and I have this running bet with the maths soc: every morning they put a mystery cube in the hallway, the only thing we're told is that its edge is somewhere between 1 and 2 cm, and you bet even odds on whether the edge is under 1.5 cm. I say it's 50/50 — the edge is in that range, there's no reason to favour one bit of it over any other, done. He says that's backwards: run the same reasoning on the *face area* (somewhere between 1 and 4, so the chance the face is under 2.25 is 1.25/3 ≈ 0.42) and you get a different number, and on the volume (between 1 and 8, under 3.375) you get ≈ 0.34. Same "no reason to favour anything" move, three different answers, and I cant see what's supposed to break the tie.

A guy from my year who works in the physics labs overheard us and said the answer shouldn't depend on whether you measure the edge in cm or in inches, and that if you insist on that you get ln(1.5)/ln(2) ≈ 0.585, and that's the real number. That smells to me like just a fourth guess with extra steps. Also we've been keeping a tally for about 60 cubes now and it's at 31, which I think settles it my way, but my flatmate says 60 cubes can't tell 0.5 apart from 0.585 anyway. I'm meant to be writing a seminar essay on this kind of thing and we keep going on about how arguments like this can talk you into total nonsense — so who, if anyone, is right here, and should I keep taking the bet at even odds?
