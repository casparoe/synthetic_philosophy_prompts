Not sure this is really a philosophy question, but I've run into something I can't get a handle on and I keep going around in circles with it, so here goes.

My dad has to move into a care home. After everything else fell through we had two places left, and I honestly couldnt tell them apart — same distance from me, same price, same inspection reports, and after three visits I still had no feeling either way. Not "I like both a bit," more like genuinely nothing at all. In the end I just picked one, and that felt completely fine, even a relief. Then the social worker, trying to be helpful, said something like "when people get stuck like this we just toss a coin." And I had a really strong reaction against that. Not because I thought the coin might land on the worse home — it couldnt, they were the same — but because it felt like giving up on something. Like the coin landing on a home isn't a way of my dad ending up somewhere, if that makes any sense.

Here's the part I can't work out about my own reaction. A week later the GP said, completely off the top of his head, "oh, I'd go with the one on the hill, no real reason," and I was perfectly happy to take that. So I'm fine with an arbitrary choice, I'm fine with someone else's arbitrary choice, but not with a coin. And I've noticed the same shape in small things: I'll happily flip a coin to settle where to eat, but only if I get to look at the result and then decide whether I'm happy with it — which I relize is strange, because if I'm going to overrule it then either I already had a preference and didn't need the coin, or I didn't and the "veto" is fake too.

A friend said this is just "ambiguity aversion," but that seems wrong — I'm not worried about unknown odds, I know the odds, that's the whole problem. Another said it's about wanting control, but I was glad to hand the decision to the GP, so it isn't control over the outcome at least.

I've heard of the old story about the donkey between two identical piles of hay that starves because it has no reason to go to either one, and that's in the neighbourhood of what I mean but it isn't it: I'm not paralysed, I choose fine, it's the routes to the choice that I have strong feelings about, and the fact that a coin flip and my own arbitrary pick feel nothing alike even though they seem to produce exactly the same thing.

So: does this have a name? The thing where some procedures for resolving an indifference are acceptable and others aren't, with nothing differing on the outcome side? And if there is a name, does it actually fit my case, or am I smuggling two different issues together under it? If there isn't one, I'd want a name and a definition sharp enough that I can sort procedures into the acceptable and unacceptable ones and say why, including the edge cases — whether it changes anything that I'd have been fine with the GP flipping a coin in private and just telling me the result, and whether the "flip it and see how I feel" thing is the same phenomenon as the care home thing or a different one. A bare label won't do me much good on its own; I want to know where the boundaries of the thing actually are.
