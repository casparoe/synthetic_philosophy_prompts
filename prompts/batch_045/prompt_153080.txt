My friend Priya and I got into a three-hour fight about speciesism at a barbecue last weekend and I've been chewing on it ever since. Shes hard-line: eats nothing that came from an animal, thinks every time I hedge she has caught me out. I don't think I'm being incoherent, but I also can't say what I actually believe, so I want to lay out my gut verdicts on cases and see what principle, if any, they add up to.

Factory-farmed chicken: obviously wrong to buy, I won't. Beef from a cow that lived on a hillside and was shot in the field: I'll eat that, no guilt. Roadkill deer: fine. Oysters and mussels: fine, not even a question. Boiling a lobster alive: I won't do it and I do judge people who do. Shrimp: uneasy, mostly avoid. Crickets and mealworms in protein bars: fine — even though per gram that's a lot more individual deaths than the beef, which does bug me when Priya points it out. Mouse in my kitchen: kill it, no second thought. Mice in a lab, killed to test a cancer drug: fine, I'd fund it. Chimpanzees in that same lab: not fine, even granting that the chimp suffers less and the medical payoff is bigger. Letting my cat hunt birds: I let her out and don't lose sleep, but I would never wring a sparrow's neck myself, and I can't explain the difference. And the one that started the whole fight: pork — fine — versus dog — absolutely not, even though I know pigs are at least as smart and social as dogs, so Priya says I've just swallowed a culturally arbitrary taboo. I don't feel like I have.

The principle I half-believe is something like: what matters is how much a creature can suffer, how rich its subjective life is, how much future it loses in dying, and — this is the clause I can't justify at all — whether it stands in some relationship with us that killing or eating it would betray. I'd rather you test that against my verdicts and repair it than scrap it, if it can be repaired. Two cases I invented to stress it: (1) a company that will biopsy your living pet, grow steaks from the cells and mail them to you; the animal is never hurt and never killed, and I still think eating my own dog that way is vile, and I want to know whether that's a moral verdict or just disgust doing philosophy; (2) a farm that edits its animals so they can't feel pain but still walk around, forage and socialize normally, then slaughters them — I feel squeamish about eating those and can't say why, and Priya says the squeamishness is the whole point because if suffering is gone my own principle seems to permit it. So: is there one principle that gets all my verdicts, or does one have to go — and if so which, and why that one? State the principle precisely enough that I can see which case motivates each clause, and tell me what it predicts about some case I haven't thought of, ideally one where it hands down something I'd have resisted.
