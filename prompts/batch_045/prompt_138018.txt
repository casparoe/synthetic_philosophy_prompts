The thing that sniped me: the KK principle and the anti-luminosity argument. As I have it: a condition C is luminous if, whenever you're in C, you're in a position to know you're in C. KK — if you know p, you're in a position to know that you know p — is basically the claim that knowing is luminous. The argument against it (Williamson's, in Knowledge and Its Limits) is that no nontrivial condition is luminous, because knowledge needs a margin for error: if you know you're in C in this case, then C holds in nearby cases too, so you can walk a chain of tiny steps outward to a case where you're not in C but where the margin still forces you to count as knowing you are. Contradiction.
