i help run a volunteer bike repair workshop, maybe 40 volunteers, most of them stay one season, and basically everything we know how to do gets passed on by watching somebody do it and then showing the next person. practices come and go and nobody is steering it. i started thinking about this in a half-baked darwinian way and made up three labels for what i keep seeing: demo-magnets (it spreads because it looks cool the one time you see it), founder-shadows (it's only still around because whoever invented it is still around doing it), and quiet good ones (it works, nobody notices, and it vanishes after one unlucky week).

i want a procedure i can actually run by myself in about twenty minutes, with no data beyond what i can watch and who i can ask, that tells me for a given practice which of those three is going on, and then whether we should deliberately protect it, deliberately kill it, or leave it alone. give me the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, and be straight about which steps are really just my hunch dressed up as a method.

then run it on three real ones: a weirdly complicated way of truing a wheel that one old volunteer does and everybody copies; a sign-in rule everybody follows but nobody can explain the point of; and a way of greeting people at the door that we dropped after two bad saturdays.

also my co-organizer says the whole framing is bogus because we copy on purpose and improve things as we hand them over, which she says isn't how genes work. i dont know what to say to that, so build whatever you think about it into the procedure. please answer in american english.
