help — my reading group built this over two sessions and I think it proves too much. Its about whether "better than" is transitive.

Set up a spectrum L0 … L86400. L0 is twenty-four hours of severe agony. L86400 is twenty-four hours of mild contentment. Consecutive lives differ by exactly one second of agony swapped for one second of contentment; the difference is imperceptible from the inside and (stipulate) changes nothing else — not the memories, not the projects, not the length of the life.

(1) Imperceptibility: if two lives differ only in a respect that is imperceptible from the inside and changes nothing else, then neither is better than the other.
(2) So for every i, L(i+1) is not better than L(i).
(3) Chaining: "not better than" propagates along chains of such links. If it didnt, transitivity of better than wouldnt get you anywhere, and every spectrum argument anyone actually runs — the repugnant-conclusion style ones, the animal ones — would die at the very first link.
(4) So L86400 is not better than L0: a day of mild contentment is not better than a day of agony.
(5) Now put L0 and L86400 side by side instead of walking the chain. They differ by a whole day's worth of pain and pleasure, and when two lives differ that much, the one with more of the good and less of the bad is better. So L86400 is better than L0.
(6) So whether one thing is better than another depends on which route you take between them — stepwise, or side by side.
(7) None of this used anything special about well-being: run the same construction on minutes of pain or on pounds in a bank account and you get route-dependence there as well.

I cant accept (6) or (7). It cant be that a million pounds isn't more money than one pound just because you can walk from one to the other in invisible increments. But I dont see what to drop. Giving up (1) commits me to betterness facts that make no difference to anyone's experience, and then where exactly is the cut off? Giving up (3) looks like giving up transitivity, which I thought was part of what a comparative even is. Giving up (5) is the one I'd least like: "more of the good, less of the bad" is more or less all I've got to go on.
