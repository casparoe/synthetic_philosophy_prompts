Putting together my actual view on social punishment for something I'm writing, and I want it poked at before I commit to it in print. Where I've landed:

1. Boycotts, callouts, pile-ons, no-platforming — all of that is punishment in the sense that matters, so proportionality constraints genuinely apply to it. And I want to measure proportionality by the sanction's real effects on the person's life, not by how hard the sanctioners meant to hit.
2. Deplatforming specifically isn't punishment: you're declining to hand over an audience that's yours, nobody is owed other people's attention, so "disproportionate deplatforming" isn't even a coherent complaint.
3. Firing someone for off-duty speech that has nothing to do with the job is coercive and usually wrong, because employees are economically vulnerable in a way that makes "they can get another job" a joke.
4. A pile-on can be disproportionate even when every individual post in it was something its author was perfectly entitled to write — the aggregate is a different moral object from the parts.
5. Sanctions should decay. I hold something like a half-life rule: an informal punishment that is still costing someone their livelihood five years later is disproportionate more or less by construction. And relatedly, equal wrongdoing deserves equal treatment — how much platform someone has, or how thick-skinned they happen to be, shouldn't change what they've got coming.

I genuinely can't tell which of these is load-bearing and which one is quietly breaking the others. So: audit the set. Which pairs really conflict, which only look like they do, order the real conflicts by how bad they are, and for each one say what I'd have to drop and roughly what dropping it costs me. Assume I'd rather keep 1 and 4 than the others, but don't let that do any work for you. Also please say how confident you are in each call you make and single out the one judgement you're least sure of.
