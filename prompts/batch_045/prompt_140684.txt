I want you to take the flatten the curve chart from March 2020 — the one with the two humps and the dashed capacity line, which (as far as I can tell) was already kicking around pandemic planning since about 2007 before it went completely viral — and run it through Otto Neurath's theory of picture languages, meaning ISOTYPE and the stuff around it. Not a general riff on data viz: I want a real case analysis that goes both directions.

Here's what I think I know about the theory, so correct me if I've got him wrong. ISOTYPE (the Vienna Method first, at the Social and Economic Museum in Vienna in the late 20s, with Gerd Arntz drawing the pictograms and Marie Reidemeister doing most of the transformation) works by repeating identical symbols so bigger quantities are more symbols, never a bigger symbol, no perspective, everything designed so a chart is self-explanatory after a bit of teaching. Neurath's own view was modest in one way: he called it a language-like technique, a helping language, not a replacement for words. And the point of it was visual education — getting ordinary people, including kids, to grasp social facts so they weren't dependent on experts. The underlying bet is that a standardized picture vocabulary let's you compare across domains and defeats the fog of bare numbers.

So: map the episode onto that. Who was the transformer in March 2020, who was the audience, what exactly was the vocabulary and what did a viewer have to be taught to read it (that the x-axis is time, that the dashed line is a capacity not a target, that the two curves aren't two countries)? What does the theory explain about why that picture traveled and what it got people to do — and what does it not explain at all? Be honest about silence: Neurath had no notion of algorithmic distribution, of an audience that will read a chart as a team jersey, or of trust collapse.

Where I actually want you to dig in is two cracks. First: ISOTYPE's basic device is for counting things that already happened. Flatten the curve is a picture of two futures, one of which is contrafactual, both produced by a model. Neurath was pretty hostile to fictions and idealizations in the social sciences — so does his framework even permit a picture of a possibility, or does modal content force words back in the door? Second: his picture language needed an institution — a museum, a municipality, a standard dictionary, a teaching period. 2020 had none of that; every outlet drew its own curves, log vs linear, different case definitions. Is that a failing of the episode or a hostage in the theory, i.e. is a picture language only possible where some body can enforce the vocabulary?

And then the awkward one. The slogan-ish idea that pictures unite rather than divide. In 2020 the same charts were used to argue for opposite policies, and they arguably united people by being vague about timescale — lots of people read flattening as ending. Given that Neurath knew a transformation is always a selection, should his theory have predicted that failure, or does the episode actually damage the optimistic line about pictures? Finish by saying what an honest ISOTYPE version of that moment would have looked like and whether it would have worked better, including the parts you can't know.
