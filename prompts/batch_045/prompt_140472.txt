I am writing a position paper for a graduate seminar on the foundations of quantum mechanics, and before I commit to it in print I would like the package audited for consistency. Each of the theses below is one I have seen defended by people who know the literature, and each looks acceptable in isolation; what I cannot tell is whether they can be held together. The set is:

(a) Locality, in the only sense I think worth caring about, is the absence of superluminal causal influence: what happens at a spacetime point is not causally influenced by goings-on at spacelike separation from it. Call this causal locality, and treat it as a substantive constraint rather than a verbal stipulation.

(b) Bell's theorem is best read as a trilemma. Take λ to be a complete specification of the relevant state of the common past of the two wings, and assume measurement independence: λ is probabilistically independent of the settings the experimenters choose. The Bell inequalities then follow from two further conditions taken jointly: parameter independence, whereby the probability of the outcome on one wing, conditional on λ and on both settings, does not depend on the setting chosen on the far wing; and outcome independence, whereby the probability of the outcome on one wing, conditional on λ, on both settings, and on the far outcome, does not depend on that far outcome. The experiments tell us the inequalities fail, so at least one of the three assumptions is false.

(c) The lesson of the violation is that the world is non-local in a robust sense: there is a real dependence between what happens at the two wings that no finer specification of the common past will screen off, and this is a discovery about the causal structure of the world rather than a remark about our descriptions of it.

(d) The quantum state is not a physical object. It is best read nomologically, as encoding a law, or epistemically, as a summary of what an agent should expect. In particular I do not want to be committed to a field or a substance living on a high-dimensional configuration space.

(e) The violation is explained by the non-separability, or holism, of the quantum state: the components of an entangled pair do not have states of their own, the state of the composite is what does the explanatory work, and that is why the correlations are what they are.

(f) No superluminal signalling is possible: the outcome statistics observable on either wing are independent of which measurement is performed on the other. This is a theorem of the quantum formalism and, I think, a constraint any successor to it should be made to respect.

(g) Superdeterminism, the denial of measurement independence, is not a serious research programme. My reason is not that it is logically excluded but that it is methodologically corrosive: if the hidden variables that help determine outcomes are systematically correlated with the settings an experimenter selects, then randomised controlled comparison is undermined in general, and with it a large part of experimental science.

(h) A methodological coda, and the item I am least confident about. I would like to formalise the independence assumptions as an ordered hierarchy rather than a flat list. The thought is that measurement independence is more basic than outcome independence, because the former is presupposed by the reliability of the very statistical inferences we use to read these experiments, whereas the latter is not: so when the theorem forces us to drop one, dropping outcome independence should be the default and dropping measurement independence a last resort. If this ordering can be made out, it would also underwrite (g).

What I want is an audit, not a survey or a tutorial. Go through the package and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and how severe each tension is, ranked rather than listed flatly; for each real tension, say precisely what would have to be revised or abandoned and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the set; and say explicitly which pairs only look like tensions and why they dissolve. Two constraints. First, do not ask me which commitment I would least like to give up and then treat my answer as fixed: I am genuinely unsure, and part of what I want from you is a ranking of how load-bearing each item is. Second, my own suspicion is that the strain lies between (c) and (f); test that suspicion rather than take it from me. Finally, if there is a single revision that would clean up more than one of the tensions at once, tell me what it is and what else it would commit me to.
