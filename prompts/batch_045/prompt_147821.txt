I want a number, not a survey of the literature. Here is the question I am actually asking: can a transcendental argument do anti-skeptical work at all, or does the best case for one always collapse into a conditional?

I think the whole thing turns on a distinction I have not seen drawn cleanly. Call a transcendental argument *uptake-loaded* if the conclusion it derives as a necessary condition of some practice P is one that the arguer, in the very act of running the argument, is committed to *believing* — not merely presupposing, being entitled to assume, following as a rule, or treating as true for the purposes of the practice. An argument can show that you cannot coherently deny X while engaged in P without being uptake-loaded: it can establish that P has X as a presupposition while saying nothing about whether engaging in P commits you to X's truth. My view is that only uptake-loaded arguments have any shot at refuting a skeptic rather than restating a conditional, and that the canonical cases — the transcendental deduction; the argument that reidentifying anything in experience already requires mind-independent objects; the argument that a conceptual scheme we could not in principle interpret is not a scheme at all — all traffic in presupposition and never get to belief. My credence that even one of them comes out uptake-loaded is about 0.2.

Give me your own number, but mainly the decomposition, with a probability and one line of justification for each term: (i) that the presupposition-to-commitment gap I have drawn is a real distinction rather than a verbal one; (ii) that, if it is real, a practice's having X as a presupposition can never by itself generate warrant for believing X; (iii) that an anti-skeptical conclusion here needs truth rather than mere entitlement or practical indispensability; (iv) that the canonical arguments in fact stop at presupposition. Then combine them. Tell me which term your final number is most sensitive to, which term you would expect a defender of transcendental arguments to concede rather than contest, and what would move you most — a case where presupposing-without-believing is visibly unstable under reflection, or a single worked argument that closes the gap without begging the question. Take the resolution condition to be whether even one such argument can be produced. Please steelman the uptake-loaded reading of the canonical cases before you rule against them, and do not spend your answer restating the standard taxonomy.
