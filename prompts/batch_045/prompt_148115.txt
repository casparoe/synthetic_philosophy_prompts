i run a mod team for a large sandbox MMO and we keep going in circles about whether the stuff in our world is real. Someone flattens a castle another player spent 400 hours on, half the team says "its just bytes, no real harm," the other half says "that was real work and real loss, of course it counts," and nobody moves. I want to stop the circling with a short sorting instrument the team can take in ten minutes: eight questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key that sorts people by what they say about the cases rather than by which label they like. No question should just ask "do you think virtual objects are real?"

The cases I want used, not just the castle one: an item that exists only as long as the company keeps paying for the server, and the servers are being shut down next month; an object a procedural generator created in a region no player ever visited and no log records; "the" unique sword after a duplication glitch, where two players each hold one; a haptic pain setting in a VR world, and whether turning it up makes the hurt real; something built in singleplayer that nobody else will ever see; and a build one player cares about enormously that the company's own terms say it may delete at will. Please also include at least one case where the answer someone would give on reflex and the answer implied by the rest of their pattern come apart.

Build it from the logical space first — the questions and the combinations of answers they allow — then locate positions in that space: irrealism/fictionalism, make-believe or pretense views, artifact or social-institutional views, functionalist "it's real if the code and server state back it" views, straight digital realism, a view where some virtual objects are real and others aren't (company-backed versus peer-hosted, say), and the view that the ontology question doesn't matter because only harm to persons does. For combinations nobody occupies, say briefly why: incoherent, unattractive, or just unclaimed. And flag any pair of positions your quiz genuinely can't separate instead of pretending the key distinguishes them.
