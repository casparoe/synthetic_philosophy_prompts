I volunteer on a four-person committee writing the text for a historical marker for the neighborhood I grew up in — a mostly Black, mostly working-class section of the city that was cleared in the 1960s under an urban renewal project and replaced with a civic plaza and a parking deck. The city's files on the project run to several linear feet: engineering drawings, appraisal sheets, relocation vouchers, condemnation notices, photographs of houses taken just before demolition. What they do not contain is a single line written by anyone who lived there.

This has made me the person on the committee who keeps saying that absence of evidence is not evidence of absence, and I would like to know whether that is true and whether it does the work I am using it for. Two cases.

Case A. I want the marker to say that residents objected to the clearance. The historian advising us says we cannot assert that: there is no petition, no letter, no newspaper account of a protest anywhere in the file. I replied that absence of evidence is not evidence of absence. My reasoning was that the file was assembled by the redevelopment agency for its own purposes — title clearance, federal reimbursement, legal exposure — and that a tenant's complaint had no route into that file even if it happened, so the chance that an objection left a trace is low, and low in a way that is about power rather than about luck.

Case B. Another volunteer says the federal relocation program kept an office here in the city and that families were offered rehousing assistance. The city directories and the audited budget line-items for those years are complete and systematic, and they mention no such office. He says exactly what I said in Case A. I think he is wrong, and I want to be able to explain why he is wrong without having been wrong myself in Case A.

So: what are the distinct precise theses that sentence could be expressing? I have two rough distinctions I have been using privately, and you can use them or discard them. First, a silence of non-production (nobody ever wrote it down) versus a silence of destruction or non-accession (somebody wrote it down and it was weeded, lost, misfiled, or never handed over). Second, treating the archive as a detector with a sensitivity that has to be estimated, so that what matters is not that we found nothing but how much more likely a trace is if the event happened than if it did not.

Please give me the menu as a table with exactly these columns:

1. reading — the precise thesis in one sentence, in your own words, not a rewording of the slogan
2. what_absence_is — what counts as an absence under this reading, and what kind of search it presupposes: one unopened box, a systematic survey of a well-indexed collection, a detector whose sensitivity is known
3. status — exactly one of: logical platitude, substantive and defensible, defensible but routinely overstated, false as stated
4. case_a — licensed, partly licensed, or not licensed, followed in the same cell by the single extra premise I would have to supply for it to license my claim
5. case_b — same treatment for the relocation-office claim
6. who_means_this — whether this is the reading people usually have in mind when they deploy the line to end an argument: usually, sometimes, or rarely

Give at least five rows, ordered from the weakest or most trivial reading to the strongest or most radical one, and make sure the list includes (a) the bare point that a gap in what we have does not logically entail nonexistence, (b) a version on which what matters is a likelihood ratio between trace-given-event and trace-given-no-event, (c) a version about the thoroughness and design of the search, (d) a version about who held the pen, on which the probability of something leaving a trace is itself a function of the record-keeper's interests, and (e) at least one version you take to be false as stated, with the reason it fails.

After the table, give me a bottom line as a single JSON object with these fields, and no prose outside it:

- best_defensible_reading: string, naming which row or combination of rows survives, in one sentence
- hidden_premise_case_a: string, one sentence, the load-bearing thing I asserted without stating
- verdict_case_a: string, one of licensed, partly licensed, not licensed
- verdict_case_b: string, one of licensed, partly licensed, not licensed
- discriminating_question: string, one sentence, the question I should ask about any silence to tell a Case A from a Case B
- upgrade_path: array of two or three strings, each a concrete thing my committee could do that would turn the Case A silence from uninformative into actual evidence, using only local sources and no web access
- marker_sentence: string, one sentence of marker text about the residents that the surviving reading would actually entitle us to print

Two last things. First, is there a defensible reading on which the abundance of documentation is itself grounds for suspicion — e.g. a neat stack of notarized voluntary-sale deeds counting as evidence of pressure rather than of willing sellers? If so, say which row generalizes to it. Second, I am writing this from a folding chair in a church basement with no library access, so please answer in American English and do not send me to any literature: no citations, no suggested reading, no appeals to what scholars have argued. Just the analysis.
