So I've been playing the same MMO with the same little group of people (we call ourselves the Tuesday Regulars, very creative I know) since about 2016, and there's a pattern in how we react to things happening to our in-game stuff that I can't get a clean description of, and I was wondering whether there's an established name for it — or, if there isn't, whether someone can carve it up properly. The rough shape is: we seem to care about the *history* of an object rather than what it does or what it looks like, and none of the explanations I can come up with on my own quite cover the cases. Case one: our guild finally killed a boss we'd been wiping on for three weeks and I got a sword out of it. Later the devs nerfed it into uselessness — I shrugged, it was funny, we joked about it. A year later a bugged patch deleted that sword from my account and automatically mailed me a replacement with the same name, same look, and the original (good) stats — functionally I ended up better off than after the nerf, and it felt like being robbed. Case two: at one point they re-skinned that sword, new model, same stats, and nobody in the group even mentioned it, it was just the same sword. But when the devs once retired an old item ID and reissued a visually identical one, there was genuine fury and a petition. Case three: I found a rare drop in a PvP zone, never told anyone, someone killed me and took it — mild annoyance. The same rare drop, except I'd worn it around the capital for a month and people kept whispering me about it — I'd have been properly upset if it were destroyed, and I'd have called that a personal violation and not just bad luck. Case four: a mount I wanted for years dropped while I was offline and someone else got it; everyone knew I wanted it; years later that person deleted it and I felt a real pang and would have paid to stop it, but I wouldn't say I was harmed, and my guildmates laughed at me. Case five, the one that weirds me out: the server crashed and they restored from a backup, so the world that came back is, as far as anyone can tell, exactly the world that was there, every object the same object — and three hours of play are gone, and half of us were furious about stolen time and half of us (me) couldn't locate anything that had been lost.

Here's what I don't want as the answer, or at least not as the whole answer. My brother says its just that you value things more once they're yours — there's some psychology term for it — plus not wanting to admit wasted effort, and I get that this explains *some* of the heat, but it doesn't explain the pattern *within* the cases, like why the nerf bothers me less than the deletion-plus-identical-replacement, or why the re-skin is nothing and the ID change is everything. "You don't own any of it, the company does" also misses: I know that, and we still draw all these distinctions among things we equally don't own. And it isn't just the big question of whether stuff in games is real or not, because my reactions seem finer-grained than a yes/no on that — they seem to say something about what *kind* of thing we're implicitly treating a virtual object as, and about what would have to happen for it to count as damaged. The closest thing I half-remember from a philosophy class is that puzzle about a ship whose planks get replaced one by one, but that feels like it's asking whether the ship is still the same ship, and what I'm after is more like: why does the answer seem to depend on the plank-replacement *chain* rather than on what the ship can do, and why does breaking the chain feel like a harm even when nothing anyone experiences gets worse.

So: does this pattern have a name, either as a view about what sort of thing a virtual object is, or as a view about what sort of harm it is when one is destroyed? If it's a cluster of two or three different things, say so and tell me which of my cases belong to which, and if there's nothing established, coin something and define it tightly enough that I can tell what it does and doesn't cover. Please also give me boundary cases — what would the concept say about the rollback case, and about an NPC that remembers things from my past sessions and is later rewritten so it doesn't? And be honest about where your own answer is shakiest: what consideration would convince you that my "pattern" is really just a few unrelated psychological effects wearing one costume, or that one of my cases is quietly doing all the work?
