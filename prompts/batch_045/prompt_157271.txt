I direct the interpretation team at a hospital genome-sequencing program, and we write both the clinician-facing reports and the plain-language text participants receive. Our house explanations lean hard on one comparison: DNA is a code — the genome is source code, the transcription and translation machinery is the compiler and runtime, proteins and regulatory RNAs are the output, introns and repeats are comments or dead code. The comparison is doing real argumentative work for us in two places: it licenses telling participants that their sequence can be read and therefore says what will happen, and it licenses our computational group's assumption that a sequence means the same thing wherever it appears, so function can be predicted from sequence alone and sequences can be moved between cell types and organisms.

Please audit it for me. Give the correspondence explicitly — what plays the role of the source text, the encoding, the compiler, the machine, the output, and of comments or dead code — then the disanalogies, and separate the ones that matter for the two uses above from the ones that don't. Three of our recurring cases have to be handled: (1) variants that change an organism without changing what the sequence codes for, acting instead through the physical properties of the molecule — mRNA folding, codon usage, how stiff or bendable the stretch is and where nucleosomes sit; (2) the same codon read as a different amino acid under a different translation system, as in mitochondria or in engineered ones; (3) stretches whose only job is physical — spacing, centromere attachment, replication timing — which the code picture has to classify as whitespace. For each, say whether the analogy accounts for it or whether the case is one the analogy cannot see.

Then say whether the two conclusions survive: the readable-therefore-predictive claim in participant-facing text, and the portability claim behind function-from-sequence. If a better comparison exists for our purposes, propose it; if the audit leaves us needing different analogies for the two uses, say so. Where your verdict is uncertain, mark it, and tell me which further argument or case would move you to the opposite verdict and where your own reasoning is likeliest to break down.
