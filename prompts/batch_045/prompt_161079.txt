so I'm writing a novel and one of my characters — Ines, night porter at a big hotel — runs her day on a routine so exact that another character accuses her of being a machine. Not cruel, not cold, just running a program. I want that accusation to actually mean something, and the word I keep circling is sphexish: the digger-wasp thing where the wasp paralyses a cricket, drags it to her burrow, leaves it on the step, goes in to inspect, comes back out and hauls it in — and if you nudge the cricket a few inches while she's down there, she runs the whole inspection from the top again, apparently without end. Not stupid, just locked.

I need a spec before I write the scene. Give me the desiderata for a theory of sphexishness: what an account of the difference between rigid mechanical behaviour and flexible agency has to deliver to be worth anything. Purpose matters here, so: good enough for two things — (1) a reader should be able to tell, from what I put on the page, whether Ines is sphexish or is just a woman who likes her routines, and (2) the accusation has to be able to do moral work between characters, excuse or blame, without collapsing into "she isn't free."

For each requirement give the case or consideration behind it. The cases I want covered: the wasp; a sleepwalker; an OCD hand-washing ritual; a concert pianist whose hands know the piece better than she does; a nurse on autopilot at 4am; a bureaucrat who follows procedure and is thereby monstrous; a tick or an ant with a three-item repertoire; a language model producing perfectly fluent text; and a person whose options have all been quietly closed off by money or threat — she varies her behaviour lots, but every variation was scripted in advance by someone else.

Two things I've been mulling that you should fold in. First, off-distribution rigidity: it feels to me like sphexishness is a property of a system relative to a *range* of environments, not a binary trait of the system, so a thing can be fabulously flexible inside the range it was built for and still fall apart the moment you move the cricket. Second, canned spontaneity: a system whose flexibility is itself stereotyped — the improv student with forty bits she recombines, the rebel teenager performing rebellion — strikes me as sphexish in a higher-order way, and I want to know if the theory can say that or whether it's just a prejudice of mine.

Also tell me: which distinctions the theory must not collapse (stupid vs rigid vs predictable, behaviour vs mechanism vs history, local vs global rigidity, degrees vs threshold), which requirements are non-negotiable and which are camp preferences (I assume "must not smuggle in indeterminism" is non-negotiable and "must be legible from the outside" is negotiable for some camps), and whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable — my hunch is that "diagnosable third-personally" and "sensitive to the agent's reasons" can't both hold, and that "graded" and "sharp enough to blame on" can't either. Which single requirement rules out the most candidate accounts? A line each on the obvious candidates — behavioural, mechanistic, reasons-responsive, information-theoretic — is fine, but keep the weight on the criteria, not the scorecard.
