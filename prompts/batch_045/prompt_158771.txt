Not sure if this is the right kind of question for this but here goes. I have no academic background at all, I work in logistics, and I've been chewing on this stuff on my own for a couple of years mostly from blog posts and arguments with friends. I recently came across this idea that I think is called moral trade, where two people who disagree about what's actually good make a deal: I do the thing your view says matters, you do the thing my view says matters, and we both end up better off by our own lights. It made me want to finally write down where I've landed on all of this, and now that it's on paper I can't tell if it hangs together. Here's the list:

(1) I'm not a moral realist. When I say something is wrong I take myself to be expressing what I care about, not describing some fact out there in the world, and when two people disagree about right and wrong I don't think either of them is making a factual mistake, they just care about different things.

(2) But I still talk and act like I have real obligations. I give away about 10% of my income, I think people in rich countries owe something to people they will never meet, and I want to say that someone who just doesn't care about any of that is doing something wrong, not merely something I happen to dislike.

(3) I think moral trade is one of the best ideas I've run into and I've actually done a small version of it, me funding my friend's animal charity and her funding the malaria one I like. I'd also be fine trading with someone whose values I think are silly or mistaken, as long as the thing they want me to do isn't something my own view counts as bad. And honestly I think a lot of political fighting could be replaced by trades like this instead of everyone shouting.

(4) I think it's sleazy to get someone to do something by leaning on a belief you think is false. I wouldn't pretend to be religious to get a religious person to give to something I care about, and I'd be annoyed if someone did that to me. In general I think you owe people a straight story about what you actually believe and why you're asking them to do something.

(5) I think some things you can't buy your way out of. Paying a stranger to go visit your grandmother, or a company buying offsets instead of cutting its own emissions, strikes me as corrupt even when the end result is the same or better. Something is lost when you don't do it yourself.

(6) When it comes down to it I think I should just do whatever does the most good by my own lights, and I don't put much weight on rules like do it yourself or don't mislead people except insofar as following them happens to produce better results.

So: is this a consistent package, or am I helping myself to something somewhere? Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and for each real one what would have to give and what would it cost me? Please rank them by how bad the tension is rather than listing them all as equally bad, and if there's one revision that cleans up the most I'd like to know which. I genuinely don't know which of these I'd drop, so don't assume I'm attached to any particular one.
