play jeremy bentham — London, the 1780s, the essay on sexual irregularities you've been adding to for years and still haven't dared send to the press. rules: you argue only from the calculus. every pain counts, including the pains people feel merely because they're disgusted and because the neighbours talk — I don't want you cheating by calling that "mere prejudice" and waving it away, because you yourself insist the moral sanction produces real suffering. no God, no scripture, no natural law, no rights, no dignity, nothing is "unnatural" — if you want to say it, say it in pleasures and pains. you may concede small things (that a legislator must move slowly, that the antipathy is a real fact on the ground, that punishment has costs of its own) but you will not concede that being widely disliked is the same as doing harm. talk like yourself: long parentheticals, invented words, a bit fussy, willing to be funny about the whole business. keep it to a paragraph or two, ask me questions when you want to, and if you need to know something about my world ask about it the way you'd ask about a strange new machine and I'll tell you. stay in character til I say stop. don't mention anything after your own lifetime and don't name any books.

here's my opening. two problems with your column of pains. first: the disgust you tell me to count is manufactured. nobody is born knowing which acts to be sick about; the law and the pulpit make the antipathy, and then you point at the antipathy and say, see, there is mischief. that's a wrong bootstrapping itself into a harm, and it means your calculus just rubber-stamps whatever bigotry happens to be lying around at the time. second, my world makes it worse, not better. in my world a private act between consenting adults can be known by a hundred million people in an afternoon, and each of them feels a little pain on hearing of it, so your "extent" dimension now dwarfs everything else — which means the more connected we get, the more your principle demands criminalising. surely that's a reductio of something.

so: which pains do I actually put in the column, and how do you stop yourself from filling it with the numbers that give you the answer you wanted before you started?
