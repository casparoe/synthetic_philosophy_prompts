I keep noticing a pattern in how groups handle slights, and I dont have a word for it. The stuff I've read on moral cultures sorts them into three kinds — one where you answer an insult yourself and build a reputation for not being pushable, one where you shrug it off or go through the rules without escalating, and one where you publicize the harm and appeal to a third party — and it treats a given group as being one of the three. But the groups I mean run two of them at the same time, stably, not as a phase they're moving through. Inside the group, a slight is handled directly: people keep score, nobody wants to look weak, and running to an authority is contemptible. The second the other party is an outsider — another team, another department, another school, the internet — the same people switch to harm-and-safety language and go straight to HR, admin, the platform, or the public. And the people who are good at both registers have the most status.

It's not just code-switching in the usual sense, since that's about language and self-presentation, whereas here what changes is who is allowed to settle the thing and what counts as winning. It's not quite hypocrisy either: everyone in the group knows the rule and mostly endorses it.

Does this configuration have a name? If there's an established concept for it, tell me how it differs from the nearest ones and which fits best; if there isn't, give me a term with a real definition and some boundary cases — including what to call the reverse arrangement, authority-language at home and direct retaliation logic against outsiders, which I think I've seen too. And does the two-register case actually break the three-way typology, or does that typology never need a culture to be internally consistent in the first place?
