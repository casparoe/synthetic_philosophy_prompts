My metaphysics study group built an argument that there are no dispositions and I can't find where it breaks. Hoping you can help.

(1) For x to be disposed to M when S is just for it to be true that if x were S, x would M.
(2) Finks break that. The wire is live and disposed to conduct when touched, but being touched trips a little device that cuts the current, so if it were touched it wouldnt conduct. So (1) is false as it stands.
(3) Standard repair: x is disposed to M when S iff x has some intrinsic basis B such that, if x were S and retained B, x would M.
(4) But you can fink the basis too. Pick any B you like: there's a possible setup in which S makes x lose B, and that's just a fink aimed at B. And you can fink the retaining of B, and fink that, and so on. So the retain-B clause either regresses forever, or it quietly helps itself to a non-finkish disposition -- the disposition to hang onto B -- in order to say what dispositions are.
(5) So no counterfactual analysis of dispositions can ever get a grip.
(6) And the manifestations don't rescue things either: masks are supposed to be coherent, and a permanently masked thing has a disposition it never once manifests, so there's nothing observable left for the ascription to be about.
(7) So dispositional ascriptions are never true of anything. There are no dispositions, only occurrent goings-on and the actual sequence of events.
(8) Which is absurd, and not only about dispositions -- run the same trick on any would-counterfactual and nothing causes anything and there are no laws.

There's a case we made up that makes me even less sure. Call it a dimmer: a device that fires on S and halves the response instead of eliminating it. Is that a fink on the full-strength disposition, or a mask on a half-strength one? If there's no non-arbitrary cut-off anywhere along the continuum from full fink to full mask, it starts to look like there's no fact of the matter whether the thing has the disposition at all -- anywhere in that range.

I'd most like to keep two things if I can: that masks are coherent, and that dispositions are real, since if they aren't I don't know what I'm doing when I call the glass fragile. Which step do you reject, why did it look safe, and what does rejecting it cost me? I feel like something slides somewhere in the middle steps but I can't name it.
