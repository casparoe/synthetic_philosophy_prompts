Hey — hoping you can help me put a name on something, or tell me it doesn't have one.

I run a small psychophysics setup (me, two undergrads, way too much coffee). We flash a target somewhere near threshold and then ask people about it, and we keep tripping over the same pattern, which I can't find a word for. Five things we've seen:

1. Ask "did you see anything?" straight after and they say no. Ask instead "was it more like a P or a Q?" and they're well above chance — and then, if you follow up with "so did you see anything?", a good chunk of them now say yes, and can go on to say what it was, in a way they couldn't if you'd led with the yes/no question.

2. The rescue only works if you ask within a few hundred milliseconds. Two seconds later the forced-choice question doesn't help; they're at chance either way.

3. The question that does the rescuing doesn't have to be about the same feature. Asking about the colour first makes them later report the *letter* as seen, and asking about a distractor item from the same display works too, though a bit less well.

4. Asking the forced-choice question *before* the display — "get ready to say whether it's more like a P or a Q" — does nothing at all. So it isn't just that we're pointing attention at the thing in advance.

5. In the middle of the difficulty range people give split answers: "I didn't see it", and then, without being pushed, "it was kind of dark, sort of blobby" — and their confidence ratings don't track whether they're actually right.

Two reactions so far from people I've described this to. One says it's obvious and boring: there's no fact of the matter about whether they saw it, people just decide where to put their threshold for saying yes, and our questions shove it around. The other says it isn't about seeing at all, it's memory — the experience was there, it just wasn't kept.

Both feel too quick to me, but I can't say exactly why, and part of me thinks the second one is half right given the timing thing, except that it shouldn't predict the colour question helping with the letter. I've also been pointed at a debate about whether there's a kind of conscious experience that isn't available to the rest of the mind, and at a theory about some kind of internal broadcasting station in the brain. From what I can gather, the first is about experience that's really there but boxed off from report, and the second says the broadcasting is all-or-nothing. Neither one seems to describe a case where *whether it got broadcast at all depends on which question you asked afterwards*.

So: is there an established concept for this — the question-dependence of whether there was an experience there to report, where it isn't attention set up in advance and isn't just forgetting? If there is, I want more than the label: what exactly are its boundaries, and which of the nearby things does it collapse into? If there isn't, name it and define it yourself, and tell me what it is not — specifically, how I'd distinguish it from (a) subjects merely shifting their criterion for saying "seen", (b) a fragile trace that decays before it can be reported, (c) experience that's present but not broadcast, and (d) blindsight-ish cases where there's arguably no experience at all. A bare name won't do me any good, I need the carving.

Give me exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no wrapping up at the end.
