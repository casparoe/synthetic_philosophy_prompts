I'm a PhD student in political theory writing a paper on the therapy/enhancement distinction, and I want to use Ishiguro's *Never Let Me Go* as my case instead of *Gattaca*, which is what everyone reaches for. My sense is that the novel pressures the distinction from a direction the literature doesn't consider, and I want a reading that tells me whether I'm right.

Three claims I want tested. First: on a value-free, species-typical normal-function account of health, the pre-donation clones are, by every clinical measure, in excellent health — more carefully monitored than any patient. But their health is maintained as a specification on a component, not for their own sake. Does the novel pry apart being healthy from health being good for its bearer, or does that separation already sit inside normal-function accounts, doing the same work elsewhere? Second: the same operation is therapy relative to the recipient, restoring species-typical function, and the deliberate induction of a deficit relative to the donor, who was never ill. So which body's baseline the distinction treats as its reference seems to be doing all the work. Does the distinction quietly presuppose a single patient whose good is the reference point, and is that a defect or a defensible feature? Third: completion, the terminal stage of the donation course, is the death of a being who was never a patient, produced by a therapeutic protocol. Does the therapy/enhancement vocabulary have the resources to describe a regime with that endpoint, or does it simply fall silent — and is the silence informative or just a gap in the terminology?

Then the part I expect you to push back on. The novel is not a protest novel: Kathy narrates without protest, the deferral rumour is about love rather than rights, and the book seems to be about mortality and being remembered. So give me the honest version of where the reading works and where it is imposed — two or three scenes where the distinction does real interpretive work (the Gallery, the Norfolk rumour, Miss Emily's final explanation, Tommy's completion), and at least one place where it goes dead and I'd be distorting the book to keep using it.

Commit to a verdict rather than laying out options: is the novel a counterexample to the distinction, a demonstration of its limits, or largely irrelevant to it — and which of my three claims survives, including if the answer is that one of them is confused?
