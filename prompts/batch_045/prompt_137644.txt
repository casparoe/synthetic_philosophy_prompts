I should say at the start that I have no academic background in any of this — I work in warehouse logistics and read about these things in the evenings — so please explain the machinery rather than point me toward it. If a move I'm missing has a standard name, naming it helps me, but I can't follow an answer that only gestures at a literature.

The argument began with a machine at the depot where I work. It reads barcodes, weighs parcels, photographs them, and prints a word on the label — sometimes FRAGILE, sometimes not. A friend who works on the software says the machine doesn't mean anything by the word; it is just a mark that comes out at the end of a chain of other marks. I wanted to say no, it's hooked up to a scale and a camera, and those are connected to the world, so the word is anchored. Then I tried to say what "anchored" comes to and got into trouble.

The test I invented was this: a symbol is anchored only if there is something outside the system of symbols that can, at least in principle, prove the system's use of it wrong — something that can push back. A dictionary can't push back. If the machine looks up "fragile" it gets more words; if it looks those up it gets more words; you can go round as long as you like. But a parcel can push back. If the machine slaps FRAGILE on a box of bricks because a forklift driver keyed it in wrong months ago, the box surviving the drop settles it, and the box is not made of words.

Then I noticed the argument runs away from me:

(1) A symbol means something only if something outside the system of symbols can make the system's use of it wrong.
(2) Anything that can make the system wrong has to reach it through the system's inputs — camera images, sensor readings, whatever arrives from beyond it — and everything that arrives is, to the system, more symbols. I had a version of this in a dream last month: I dreamt I got up to check whether I had left a mug on the table, and in the dream the mug was there and the check went through fine. Anything the real mug might have done to correct me, it would have had to do by way of a further experience, and a further experience is exactly what dreams are made of.
(3) So no correction ever comes from outside the system of symbols. Every check is internal, and the machine's dictionary and my mug are in the same position.
(4) So by (1) nothing is ever anchored. My word "mug" means as little as the machine's FRAGILE.
(5) That can't be right. If it were, there would be no difference between talking about mugs and making noise, and I would have no more reason to prefer a true sentence about the depot to a false one than to a string of nonsense.

And it comes apart in both directions when I try to patch (1). If I loosen it so that any input the system cannot set for itself counts as a check, then everything means something: a weathervane's angle means "wind," a thermostat's strip means "cold," a cell in a spreadsheet means whatever its formula feeds on — and the distinction I wanted between the machine and a person has quietly vanished. If I keep it strict, then nothing outside me can correct my use of "seven," or "because," or "if," or "the depot owes me overtime," so all of those go meaningless, which is a worse result than the one I started out trying to avoid.

One more thing that bothers me and may be the same thing in disguise: if someone put the machine in a building where a second machine fed it a perfectly faked camera image, nothing would change from the machine's point of view. So whatever anchors its word, it isn't something it could check from inside — which makes me suspect the test is asking for something no user of a symbol could ever confirm about itself.

So (1) and (2) look jointly fatal to meaning and useless one at a time. Which one should I give up, or is the damage really done in the step from (2) to (3)? My friend says the whole thing collapses because "outside the system" has no fixed sense: neither the machine nor I am a sealed thing with a border, and you can draw the line around the software, or the software plus the cameras, or the whole depot, and get a different verdict each time. That feels like a dodge to me — it looks like it would let anyone claim anchoring for anything — but I can't say why it's a dodge.

Two claims I would like to keep if I can: that there is some real difference between the machine's FRAGILE and a person's, and that "seven" means something. Price the alternatives for me. If I keep (1), what exactly is the anchoring relation, and must I accept that a system can be anchored without being able to tell, even in principle, whether it is? If I give up (1) and let meaning come from how a symbol is used among other symbols, what stops the dictionary going round in circles?
