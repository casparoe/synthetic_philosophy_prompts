I'm giving the toast at my sister's wedding in three weeks, and I need it written to a constraint. She is a philosopher of language who defends a knowledge norm for assertion — you may assert only what you know — and she also holds the less common further thesis that the same norm governs what you deliberately implicate: on her view you cannot escape the requirement by hinting at something you would not be willing to state flat out, or by wrapping it in a joke that the room is clearly meant to walk away believing. Roughly half the guests will be her colleagues, who know her positions and will be listening for exactly this; the other half are our family and her partner's family, who know nothing about any of it and mostly want to cry a little and then eat.

This is awkward for me personally because she has called me out on it before. At Christmas I told the family that her partner's new job was basically confirmed when all I had was a rumour, and she made me retract it in front of everyone. I have since come round to her view, and I don't want the toast to be the one place where I quietly exempt myself.

So: write a four-minute wedding toast that obeys the constraint — every asserted or deliberately implicated proposition is something the speaker actually knows, and the only material genuinely available is what I have watched with my own eyes over the years — while never naming the view and never turning into a lecture. Where the toast needs to reach past what anyone can know, whether they will still be happy in thirty years for instance, I want it to do that work in another key: wishing, promising, hoping, rather than in hedged assertions like I believe they'll make it, which she would treat as no better than the unhedged version. It should not read as evasive or legalistic to the non-philosophers. One further wrinkle worth using: some of the colleagues at the table hold a truth norm or a reasonable-belief norm instead of hers, so there is room for a single moment that registers, for them, that the speaker is holding himself to the strictest standard in the room, and that passes as nothing more than modesty to everyone else.

Please write it in British English, around 500 words.
