For a graduate reading group on whether the notion of a quale is a coherent posit, I need a classification instrument rather than another survey of views. Please build me a decision tree that takes a student from an ordinary starting commitment about experience down to a named position on the question, with leaves for realism about phenomenal properties, for reductive accounts, and for views on which the term picks out nothing at all.

Constraints on the tree:

1. Build it from the logical space first. Set out the branching questions and the combinations of answers they permit, then locate the actual named positions in that space. Where a combination of answers is occupied by nobody, keep it as an empty leaf and add one line on why: incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored.

2. Every branch node must be a concrete question about a case or a claim that the positions genuinely come apart on, phrased so that a student can answer it without knowing which view it leads to. No node may be a label question such as "are qualia real?", "is consciousness physical?", or "are you a dualist?". Aim for no more than nine branch nodes and between eight and twelve leaves; the audience is second-year graduate students who have read the central papers but are not specialists.

3. The distinctions I care about, and which must drive the upper branches:
 (a) the difference between synchronic cases (another subject or another system now) and diachronic cases (one subject whose experience changes over time while their reports, discriminations and memories are held fixed). I want these split: the tree should show whether a student's verdict is stable across the two, and you should flag any leaf that has to give the same answer to both but has good reason to dislike doing so.
 (b) whether a subject's first-person access contributes evidence beyond their verbal reports and discriminations, or whether reports are the only data.
 (c) whether the character of an experience is constituted by its relations of similarity and difference to the subject's other experiences, or is intrinsic to the experience considered alone.

4. I have drafted three cases I would like used as nodes; use at least two, and say plainly if one of them is broken or collapses into a case already in the literature rather than patching it up quietly.
 - The palimpsest case: a subject slowly acquires an additional phenomenal dimension over a decade, slowly enough and with enough memory overwriting that no report, discrimination or remembered contrast could register the change. Is there a fact of the matter about whether their experience changed?
 - The twin-taster case: two subjects swap the character of one modality's experience each night and swap back each morning, in such a way that each subject's total pattern of similarity and difference among their own experiences is the same on every day, and only the cross-subject comparison varies. Is there a fact of the matter about the swap?
 - The empty-report case: a subject's reports about their experience are produced by a mechanism with no access to the experience itself, tracking the distal stimulus instead. Would you still treat those reports as evidence about what it is like for them?

5. Leaves I want present, named with the label that is most standard and flagged where the label is contested or where two labels name one position: reductive representationalism; the phenomenal-concept or a posteriori physicalist strategy; illusionism; non-reductive phenomenal realism; Russellian monism or panpsychism; epiphenomenalism; mysterianism; a relationalist or structuralist view of phenomenal character; and the meta-position that the dispute is verbal. Each leaf should carry one sentence on the commitment its holder should find most awkward. Where the tree cannot separate two leaves — for instance because they answer every node alike — say so explicitly instead of inventing a node to pry them apart.

6. Please write in British English, and mark at each branch whether you are confident the question really separates its siblings or whether the separation is fragile. If one of my required distinctions in (3) is ill-drawn, if the cases I have drafted beg the question, or if sorting people into these leaves at all misrepresents how the debate is structured, tell me directly and explain why, rather than producing the tree I asked for anyway.
