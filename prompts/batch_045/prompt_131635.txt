So this got personally relevant in a hurry. My wife and I have been through three rounds of IVF, we've got three frozen embryos left and we're only doing one more transfer, and while we were sitting in the clinic parking lot waiting for the coordinator to call us back I fell down a rabbit hole reading the archive of this woman — everyone calls her Junie — who spent about a decade answering "is it okay if we…" questions on some long-running childfree/antinatalism forum. She hasn't posted in six years and the place is basically a museum now, so I can't just ask her. I've been trying to reverse-engineer what she'd say about us, and I keep finding that her old answers fit more than one rule and I can't tell which one she's actually running. My wife thinks the whole exercise is ghoulish and that we should transfer whichever one the embryologist says looks best, which is probably right, but I still want to know.

Here's the track record I pieced together from her old posts, more or less in her own words. (1) A couple who both carry something that would give any child of theirs a few years of pretty bad pain and then death, with no compensating joys: she says they shouldn't conceive, and that conceiving would be a real wrong, not merely imprudent. (2) A couple who decide to have no kids and spend the money on sailing: she says nothing wrong, nothing lost, no one worse off — her line was always "there's nobody in the nursery complaining about it" — and she specifically refuses to say the world is worse for the missing kid. (3) A couple who go ahead and have a kid who turns out very happy: not wrong, no complaint, but she insists they haven't *done something for* the kid, because the kid isn't better off than they'd have been and there's no one who missed out. (4) A couple who have a kid instead of giving the equivalent money to a charity that helps children who already exist: permissible, she's not going to demand sainthood. (5) A woman already fourteen weeks along with a wanted pregnancy, kid's life would be okay but not great, she'd be raising it alone: continuing is fine, she won't tell anyone to terminate. (6) A couple picking between two embryos where one is predicted to be a bit happier than the other, one kid either way: "no moral difference, you're not choosing *for* anybody." (7) A couple deciding to have two kids instead of one, both expected happy: also fine, and she adds that she's not going to call the extra person a gain — though she doesn't call it a loss either. (8) A few times, to people who said they wanted to parent, she said something like: then adopt, because there you're actually doing something for somebody.

Our case. The clinic's new panel gives profiles on our three, and two of them matter here. Embryo A: ordinary pain sensitivity, high capacity for enjoyment — by the clinic's own little scoring thing, a great life with an ordinary amount of suffering in it. Embryo B: a variant that means much lower pain sensitivity across the board, and — the genetic counselor claimed the same pathways carry some of the "liking" response, which I have no idea whether to believe — a blunted capacity for the most intense pleasures, so the clinic scores B's overall life satisfaction as *slightly lower* than A's, though still well above zero, obviously a life worth living. One kid either way, different possible kid either way, and no, we're not going to adopt, for reasons I'm not getting into — I want the comparison between these two. So: what would Junie say, which rule of hers is doing the work, and what would each of the plausible rules say? The thing I can't work out is that on the one hand she talks as if pain that never happens is a good even with nobody there to be glad about it and pleasure that never happens isn't a loss unless somebody's missing it, which seems to point straight at B. On the other hand she also says things like "nobody's better off either way," and if she really means that, then the pain-avoiding option isn't better, it's just different — and if she's quietly just adding up welfare, A wins. And then there's the version that makes me think her asymmetry talk is decoration: suppose the kid already existed and we were deciding about a medication after birth that would do the same thing, less pain, less joy, slightly lower overall. If she gives the same answer there as in the embryo case, is she really running the "no one is deprived, so no comparison" line at all, or just a straight welfare or pain-minimizing rule? I'd also take a guess at what she'd say if the clinic scored B as dead equal to A overall, or if B were much lower overall but avoided an enormous amount of suffering — and if her archived posts genuinely underdetermine our case, tell me which one question I'd most want to ask her to settle it.
