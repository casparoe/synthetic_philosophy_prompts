My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about the woodlot our village shares for firewood, and I need a word for the thing I was trying and failing to describe. Everyone there is pretty conservative about how much they cut — nobody wants to be the person who wrecked it, and for years the unwritten rule held fine. But last winter somebody started a rumour that the parish was going to sell the land, and within a few weeks people who'd taken two loads a year for decades were out at dawn carrying off everything they could, and the lot got stripped. Nothing about the wood changed; what changed is that people started expecting everyone else to grab. Same shape at my office: the shared coffee and milk are fine for months, then someone forwards an email about budget cuts and the kitchen is cleaned out by Wednesday, even though the email was about something else and the budget never got cut.

My friend says that's just the tragedy of the commons and that I'm overcomplicating it. I keep saying no: in the version I learned, each person quietly takes a bit more than their share, the resource declines bit by bit, and it doesn't much matter what anyone believes anyone else will do. Here nobody overcuts as a first choice. They do it because they think the others are about to, and the rush is what produces the shortage they were braced for — so the expectation looks like it's doing the damage rather than the taking. It feels closer to a bank run than to a commons, except there's no bank and nothing is being withdrawn from a fixed pool; the wood is still growing.

So: is there an established concept for this failure mode — restraint is stable until a belief about other people's restraint breaks, and the breakdown brings about exactly what everyone feared? Is the nearest thing really the ordinary tragedy of the commons, or is it some kind of coordination or assurance problem, or something from the bank-run / self-fulfilling-prophecy side, and how would you tell which of those it actually is here? If there's no name for it, coin one and define it carefully enough that I can see its edges: does it still count when the belief is true, and does it count when the resource is one that gets better with light use — footpaths, a shared wiki — so that what the rush destroys isn't just a stock but a thing those same people were maintaining? I definately don't just want a label, I want the carving.
