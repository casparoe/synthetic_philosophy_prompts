Candidate 8 wrote this:

PAP says that a person is morally responsible for what she has done only if she could have done otherwise. A Frankfurt case is one in which an agent does A for her own reasons, while a counterfactual intervener waits in the background, ready to make her do A if she shows any sign of not doing it. Since she does A on her own, the intervener never does anything at all: her actual causal history is exactly what it would have been without it. But since the intervener was there, she could not have done otherwise. So she is morally responsible while lacking alternative possibilities, and PAP is false. Frankfurt himself was a compatibilist, though the argument is supposed to work independently of that.

That is why the cases show that determinism is no threat to moral responsibility. If Jones can be responsible while unable to do otherwise, then determinism, which only removes alternative possibilities, cannot excuse anyone; and an incompatibilist therefore has to find some way of rejecting the cases.

The two standard complaints are the flicker reply and the dilemma. The flicker reply says that the agent still has some alternative left — she could have produced some earlier sign or movement that would have triggered the device. The dilemma says the device only removes alternatives if the trigger reliably indicates the decision, and that is only possible if the agent's process is deterministic, which begs the question against the incompatibilist.

The Auditor answers both. It needs no prior sign of what Zoe will decide, only of whether her mechanism is working properly, and that can be known in advance even if her deliberation is indeterministic. And it leaves her no route to a different outcome: every path to a different decision runs through a malfunction, and the implant corrects those. So it is a Frankfurt case that even an incompatibilist should accept, and after it the whole debate is just about the actual sequence of her deliberation.
