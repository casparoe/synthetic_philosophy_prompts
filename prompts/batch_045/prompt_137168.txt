I spend most of my working day on incremental build systems — dependency graphs, dirty bits, over-approximating the set of stuff that needs rebuilding, sound-but-imprecise vs. precise invalidation, the occassional full rebuild, and the standing nightmare that some flag or generated header quietly makes everything transitively depend on everything else. I've read a bit of the philosophy around the frame problem but I want it in those terms.

So: translate the frame problem into them, as faithfully as you can — the original logic-y version where an action's non-effects somehow have to be stated without listing them one by one, the Fodor-ish version where it stops being about logic and becomes a claim about relevance and holistic central cognition, and the later reading where a bounded agent on a clock has to work out what matters in an environment it is actually embedded in. For each, say what maps cleanly onto dependency graphs and invalidation, what only fits if you fudge it, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all.

My own sense is that we partly solved it by cheating: we make the world hermetic, enumerate every input, treat anything untracked as nonexistent, and we happily recompute more than strictly necessary because sound over-approximation is cheap next to a wrong answer. If that's the move, is the frame problem just the holism problem in a costume, or is there something in what the philosophers mean by relevance that dependency graphs cannot say? And does the from-scratch build I use as ground truth have any analogue for them, or is not having one the whole point?
