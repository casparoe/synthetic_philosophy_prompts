Our grad-run works-in-progress series has a metaphysician visiting next month who defends what she calls cardinality primitivism about Black's spheres. The package: the two-sphere universe is a genuine possibility, so the identity of indiscernibles is at best contingent and in fact false there; the spheres are still weakly discerned, since each bears the irreflexive symmetric relation 'is two miles from another sphere' to the other and not to itself, and that's enough to ground their distinctness; PII is worth keeping only in a relational, non-identity-involving form; and the leftover fact that there are exactly two of them rather than one or three is a brute structural fact that she thinks is much cheaper than handing each sphere a haecceity.

Can you draft 8–10 Q&A questions that don't just let her restate the package? I want at least one on each of: whether the irreflexivity move is circular — saying a bears R to b but not to itself already looks like it assumes a ≠ b — and how 'is two miles from another sphere' is supposed to differ from the trivial discerner 'is distinct from'; whether the relational replacement for PII can still do whatever work the principle was ever supposed to do; the counting problem, since three mutually equidistant spheres look just as weakly discerned, so if cardinality is brute isn't the number of concreta floating completely free of the qualitative facts, and why is that better than haecceities; whether any of it survives for non-spatial duplicates like two exactly similar immaterial souls, where there's no distance relation to reach for; and whether the two-sphere scenario can even be formulated by someone who denies primitive transworld identity, or, on her own view, whether the 'exactly one sphere' and 'exactly two spheres' descriptions differ qualitatively at all. For each add the follow-up keyed to the reply she's most likely to give — she tends to fall back on 'that's a feature of the relation, not of the relata' and 'brute structural facts aren't individual essences'. Also one on whether she loses anything if space is relational rather than substantival, and close with a question about whether this is a real dispute or a verbal one about what counts as a property, plus what could in principle count against the view.
