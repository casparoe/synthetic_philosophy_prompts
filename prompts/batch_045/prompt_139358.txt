I am an undergraduate taking a seminar on probability, chance and confirmation, but almost all of my actual training is in statistics and machine learning, and I think in that vocabulary far more fluently than in the seminar's. I am at home with calibration and proper scoring rules, exchangeability and de Finetti-style representation results, latent parameters and hierarchical models, filtering versus smoothing in state-space models, non-stationarity and concept drift, online learning and regret bounds, and prediction under model misspecification. We have just reached the Principal Principle: the requirement that a reasonable agent's credence in an outcome, conditional on the claim that its objective chance at the relevant time is x together with any further evidence that is admissible, should equal x. We have also discussed three things that hang off it — the restriction to admissible evidence, the undermining problem, where evidence about later outcomes feeds back on what the chances are now and so cannot simply be conditionalized on, and the background dispute about what chances themselves are, Humean patterns in the totality of events versus irreducible single-case propensities. I am not asking for further reading; I have the seminar material. I want the whole package translated into my vocabulary, and I want to know what gets lost.

Please do four translations in turn, keeping each honest and flagging explicitly whenever a translation only goes through because you have quietly added an assumption my vocabulary does not actually contain.

First, the Principle itself. Is it best rendered as a calibration constraint — that my forecasts, restricted to the subsequence of cases where the chance is x, should show frequency x — as an exchangeability-style symmetry assumption, or as deference to an expert probability function whose outputs I take over when I have no side information? My own sense is that calibration is too weak, since a forecaster who never looks at the chances and always announces the base rate can be perfectly calibrated while violating the Principle at every point, so that the Principle seems to imply calibration rather than the reverse. Tell me whether that is right and what the direction of fit is.

Second, admissibility. Statistics has no general prohibition on conditioning; the nearest analogues I can think of are prohibitions on conditioning on post-treatment variables or colliders in causal inference, and leakage in prediction. Is admissibility simply leakage avoidance, or does the temporal and historical character of the notion — evidence from later times is suspect precisely because the chances depend on the whole history — leave a residue my framework cannot state?

Third, undermining. My proposal is that this is a case where the parameter is a function of the entire sequence including its future segment, so that exchangeability fails and the usual unknown-parameter representation breaks down; and that admissibility amounts to insisting that I estimate the present chance by filtering rather than by smoothing, even though smoothing would give a more accurate estimate. Develop that mapping as far as it will go, and then tell me what in the philosophical problem resists it.

Fourth, the metaphysics of chance. In my vocabulary a chance is either a parameter of a stipulated generative model or an assumption about the data-generating process, and I cannot see how to express the claim that the chance facts are nothing over and above the pattern of outcomes, nor how to express a single-case propensity for an event that will never be repeated, nor how to express the thought that I ought to match the chance rather than merely that doing so is a good long-run policy.

Finally, here is a case the seminar invented and that I want used as a test. Call a setup drift-prone if the chance of the outcome shifts between the time at which I set my credence and the time at which the outcome occurs, in a way that nothing available to me at the earlier time tracks, though I know the shift happens. Say what the Principle requires of me there, how your translation renders the case — my guess is that it looks like concept drift, where the natural response is to track the best expert and minimize regret rather than to match any single probability — and whether the Principle then adds anything to what my vocabulary already tells me to do.

End with the residue report I actually want: a single verdict on whether what fails to translate points to something missing from statistics and machine learning, or to something less well defined in the philosophical debate than the seminar has made it sound. I am deciding whether this is a good topic for my final paper, so I would rather be told that the debate is partly verbal than be given a tidy translation that hides it.
