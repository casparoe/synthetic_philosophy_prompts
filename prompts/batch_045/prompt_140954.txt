You are a professional philosopher who has spent several years embedded as an advisor to a large animal-welfare funder, sitting in the meetings where cost-effectiveness models are actually built and where good ideas routinely die in the question period because they cannot be turned into a number. Think carefully, before you write, about the difference between the objections that are philosophically most interesting and the objections that will actually decide the outcome of the meeting. I want the second kind.

The situation: next month I present a proposal to the technical advisory panel of an animal-welfare funder, and I need Q&A prep, not a defense.

The proposal. The panel currently scores each taxon with a single number -- a sentience multiplier, or welfare range -- that gets multiplied by duration and intensity of a state to put the suffering of a shrimp, a pig, and a chicken into one currency, which then feeds a cost-effectiveness ranking of interventions. I want them to replace the single number with a short vector, and to make the conversion from vector to number an explicit, contestable moral parameter rather than an apparently empirical one.

My argument in outline. "Degree of sentience" is not one quantity but at least three that can come apart:

- Bandwidth: how many distinguishable valenced states a creature can be in, and how finely it discriminates among them -- the size and grain of its affective repertoire.
- Integration window: the temporal grain over which valenced states are unified into one experience. A creature whose experience is integrated over a few tens of milliseconds and one whose experience is integrated over seconds or longer may be said to have the same pain for the same sixty seconds, but the arithmetic of how much pain that is depends on the window.
- Uptake: how far a valenced state is registered by the creature's own standing concerns -- whether an aversive state is a bare nociceptive event or something the creature, with a self-model and future-directed concerns, suffers about.

I claim, first, that these three can in principle dissociate, so that a creature can rank high on one and low on another; second, that a single multiplier is a lossy compression that hides a moral choice about how to weight them inside what looks like a measurement; third, that the fix is to report the vector per taxon, let the weighting be a declared parameter, and rank interventions by a dominance rule -- fund where the intervention looks better under every weighting in a stated reasonable range, and treat disagreement among weightings as a flag rather than as noise to be averaged away; and fourth, that this is not paralysis, because most of the current portfolio survives the dominance test unchanged, and the rule mainly changes the answer in a small number of identifiable cases.

The panel, so you can pitch the objections at the right level and in the right idiom:

- A animal welfare scientist with twenty years of motivational trade-off and judgement-bias work behind her. She treats sentience as whatever the behavioural and physiological markers operationalize, and she will want each of my three dimensions tied to a protocol she could pre-register.
- An insect neurobiologist who thinks the field is being asked to run well ahead of its data, and who will resist any framework whose application to insects requires evidence nobody has.
- Two cost-effectiveness analysts who maintain the funding model. They need one number per taxon and one number per intervention, they think in expected value and in dollars per unit of welfare, and they are worried about paralysis, about cluelessness, and about anything that hands the board a respectable excuse to do nothing.
- A philosopher of mind of broadly functionalist-to-illusionist sympathies, who doubts that amount of consciousness is well formed at all -- consciousness may be all-or-nothing per state, with richness just being amount of content -- and who suspects my vector is cognitive sophistication under a new label.
- A bioethicist who holds that moral status is not a matter of degree in the first place: what we owe a being is settled by its kind or by threshold capacities such as agency or rationality, and a graded framework quietly licenses trade-offs that ought to be off the table.
- The chair, who is a money person, cares mainly about defensibility to the board and to donors, and will want to know what changes on Monday morning.
