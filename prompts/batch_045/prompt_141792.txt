**Desert as a cap, not an engine**

Caruso's target is basic desert: the claim that a wrongdoer deserves to suffer in virtue of the wrong alone, before any good consequences are totalled up. If free will scepticism is right, nobody has the kind of control basic desert requires, so retributivism fails, and the state should move to something like a public health model — quarantine, treatment, rehabilitation, general deterrence — bounded by rights and by a forward-looking proportionality. Dennett's complaint is that this throws away something that does work. On his side of the ledger moral responsibility is a real, evolved, socially maintained status; it comes in degrees; and what those degrees track is not contra-causal power but something like evitability, the capacity to see a consequence coming and steer around it. Holding people responsible is not a report on a metaphysical fact, it is part of the machinery that makes agents into the sort of thing that can be addressed with reasons at all. Call this the scaffolding claim.

My worry is that both views go unstable in the same place: the long case. Quarantine has no natural expiry date. If the only justification for confining someone is that they are still dangerous, confinement lasts as long as the danger does, and our estimates of that danger are bad in a very specific way — they are passable for populations and poor at the tails, and they are worst for exactly the people we are most tempted to keep in indefinitely. Desert, by contrast, is fixed by the wrong and therefore runs out. Call this the expiry argument. It does not show that anyone deserves anything; it shows that a desert-shaped element is doing limiting work that a purely forward-looking model cannot do for itself. So the interesting question is not whether desert is metaphysically real but what job it is doing, and whether that job can be done by something a sceptic could accept.

My proposal is desert as a cap, not an engine. Quarantine supplies the positive justification for any particular sanction; desert-talk is retained only as a ceiling and as a form of address. The addressive function matters independently: blaming someone treats them as a reason-responsive agent rather than as a hazard to be managed, and that difference shows up in what you are permitted to say to them, not merely in how long you lock them up.

Three moves I think are mine.

First, the smuggling point. The actuarial instruments a quarantine model would rely on routinely include items like displayed remorse, acceptance of responsibility and attitude to the offence. Those are proxies for desert-attribution. So the quarantine model may be empirically propped up by the very practice it claims to replace: strip everything desert-shaped out of the risk tools and their predictive power drops. If that is right, Caruso's alternative inherits, at the level of measurement, the metaphysical difficulty he raises against retributivism, and the two views are not as independent as the debate assumes.

Second, the pedagogical regress against scaffolding. If desert-attribution is what turns people into reason-responsive agents, then the first cohort it was applied to were not yet reason-responsive in the relevant sense, so on Dennett's own capacity-tracking terms they did not deserve it. The practice bootstraps itself on an injustice it can never repair, and the same holds within a single life: every child blamed before they could respond to the reason in question is blamed undeservedly. Dennett can reply that the practice is justified by what it produces downstream. But that reply concedes that the justification is consequentialist all the way down, and then he owes an answer to the rights-based objection: you may not use this person as a means to the responsiveness of everyone else.

Third, collapse at the boundary. Dennett's own suggestion that agents might opt out of moral responsibility, with prison or institutionalisation as the consequence, reproduces the quarantine model at exactly the point where responsibility runs out. So the two positions do not sit on opposite sides of a divide; they converge on the non-responsible, and the whole disagreement is about the size of the border region, not about what happens inside it.

Here is the case I use to make the divergence vivid. Alan and Bryn, both drunk, both start a fire in a disused warehouse, identical damage. Alan scores slightly higher on the risk instrument; Bryn is the one who would have stopped if he had thought about it for ten seconds. On the capacity view Bryn is more blameworthy because the act was more evitable for him; on the risk view Alan is the one to watch. Desert and quarantine pull in opposite directions, and the sentence has to follow one of them. I want to argue that the case shows quarantine cannot be the whole story about sentencing length, because the desert-shaped limit is the only thing in either theory that explains why Bryn's sentence stops.
