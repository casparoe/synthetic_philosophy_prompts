I'm writing a short "where I stand" note on forecasting, disagreement and epistemic modesty for a seminar, and I'd like the position audited before I circulate it. Here is the set as I currently hold it.

(a) The outside view is a requirement of rationality, not a rule of thumb: someone who forecasts their own project from its details while ignoring the base rate for projects of that kind is reasoning badly, not merely rashly.
(b) The outside view is nonetheless defeated by case-specific discriminating evidence — the "broken leg" sort of case — and there the inside view properly wins.
(c) On disagreement I am a conciliator: where I count someone an epistemic peer, I move substantially toward their view rather than standing pat.
(d) I count very few people as peers on the questions I actually care about, since most of those who disagree with me have not worked through the argument that convinced me.
(e) I take the planning-fallacy data to license a general modesty about my own case: the feeling that my situation is special is precisely the signature of the error.
(f) Even so, I act on my views without hedging — I left a stable job to start something on an inside-view case — and I do not think modest credences demand timid action.
(g) When a friend asks whether to do the same kind of thing, I quote the base rate rather than engage with their details.
(h) One further thing that troubles me: the base rates the outside view appeals to were generated by populations of agents who mostly took the inside view, so a population that all took the outside view would generate different ones — which makes me unsure the outside view can be prescribed universally without undercutting its own inputs.

My suspicion is that (d) is exactly the move (a) and (e) forbid, and that (g) applies a standard to others that I quietly bypass for myself, but I cannot tell whether either is a real tension or just self-criticism. Please work through the set and tell me which pairs are genuinely in conflict and how severely, which merely look like they are and why the appearance dissolves, and for each real conflict what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost me. If one commitment has to go, say which, and what I lose by dropping it. Rank by severity rather than treating them all alike.
