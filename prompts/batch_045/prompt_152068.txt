I've read way too many comment-section fights about this and I've noticed I have a pile of confident verdicts and no principle, so here's my gut on who counts. Someone fleeing indiscriminate bombing by their own government: yes. A family fleeing gang extortion that the police can't or won't stop: yes. Someone whose farming livelihood died in a decade-long drought: no, that's not persecution, sorry. Someone who left for purely economic reasons and only became a known dissident after arriving: yes, which feels wierd but I'm sure of it. Someone fleeing random civil-war violence that isn't aimed at them for any particular reason: yes, but that's where I'm shakiest. The pair I can't square: I think a claim is defeated if the person could have been safe by relocating to a different region of their own country, but NOT defeated by the fact that they passed through two perfectly safe countries on the way here. Those look like the same fact — "there was somwhere safe you could have gone" — and I answer them opposite ways. On who has to take them: I think a state that materially helped break the country owes more than whichever state the person happens to land in first, but I also don't think you can bounce the person to that state or make their admission conditional on it.

My own stab at a principle: you count as a refugee when your own state owes you protection from some serious harm and isn't providing it. That gets the bombing and gang cases, but it seems to drag the drought case in if the state also can't feed people, and I don't see how it explains the relocation-versus-transit split.

Is there one principle that gets all of this, or is the honest answer two principles plus a hedge? Which verdict does it force me to drop? And what does it say about two cases I haven't settled: a minority group that's been stripped of citizenship by the country they've lived in for generations, so there's no state that owes them protection; and someone whose home regime collapsed while their claim was pending, so they'd be fine now but were genuinely in danger when they fled. Don't argue from what the 1951 Convention or any court actually says — I want the principle, not the doctrine. No headings, bullets, bold or tables, just continuous prose like an email.
