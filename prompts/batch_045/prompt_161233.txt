I teach an upper-division seminar on personal identity: fourteen students, ninety minutes, one session, meeting mid-semester. They have already worked through Locke's memory criterion, Parfitian reductionism with the standard fission cases, and a session on the Buddhist denial of a self — the five aggregates, dependent origination, the chariot simile, and the Pali contrast between what is only conventionally designated and what is ultimately real. What they have not done is feel where the Buddhist denial of a self and the reductionist's denial of a further fact come apart, or feel why either of them leaves a court, a creditor, or a person owed an apology with a decision to make rather than a fact to discover.

Design a classroom activity in which they run into this themselves. My requirements:

1. No framing lecture, and I say nothing doctrinal until the debrief: the words for the no-self claim, for reductionism, and for conventional designation must not leave my mouth during the game. The mechanism gets a neutral name.

2. Students sit as a tribunal for a small jurisdiction applying a written code, and must return majority verdicts, under a strict time limit you specify, on a docket of inheritance, criminal liability, punishment, promise-keeping and apology cases.

3. They should construct the pressure themselves. Begin with gradual replacement over four decades, which they will resolve by appealing to overlapping continuity without naming it; move to symmetric fission with a single indivisible good at stake (one heirloom, or one prison term); and at a fixed point require each of them to write privately, on paper, a one-sentence rule they will be held to.

4. Then the case I want at the centre, which is not in our readings, so build it from scratch: fission followed, ten years later, by fusion. Two streams separate, live distinguishable lives, and are then reunited in a single person who remembers both. The tribunal must rule on liability for a crime committed by only one stream during the separation, and must say whether their earlier verdict in the fission case was correct when they issued it — whether a verdict about who someone is can be made wrong by something that happens later.

5. Then re-hear the same docket under a replacement one-line statute requiring verdicts on the basis of what is ultimately real rather than what is conventionally designated.

6. Plant two traps in the docket: one case where a we-are-all-one-self reading of the no-self claim yields a verdict I can hold up as absurd, and one where an annihilationist reading yields a verdict students will feel as callous — a claimant insisting that there was no one there to be wronged, so nothing is owed.

7. Give it real but small stakes: the private rule from step 3 is read back at the end, and anyone whose final verdicts cannot be reconciled with it must defend the change for two minutes in front of the class.

The debrief must, among other things, separate the no-self claim from reductionism rather than run them together — including the point that the questions the texts set aside are set aside because answering them either way entrenches the mistaken picture, not because the answer happens to be unknown — and must say where the two views genuinely diverge about what is supposed to change in a person who stops taking the aggregates as mine.

Write the activity in short labelled sections: setup and materials; roles; the statute clauses with the gaps you have deliberately left in them marked as gaps; a minute-by-minute timeline; the docket; and the debrief. Then give me:

(i) A markdown table with exactly these columns: Moment in the activity | What students will just have done | The claim about persons this puts pressure on | The most likely line of resistance | What it costs to resist it there. One row per pressure point, in the order the pressures arise.

(ii) A single JSON object with this schema: setup (string: room, materials, seating, timing apparatus) — roles (array of objects with keys role, number_of_students, what_they_control, what_they_are_forbidden_to_do) — statute_clauses (array of objects with keys clause_id, wording_to_hand_out, gap_deliberately_left, pressure_the_gap_creates) — timeline (array of objects with keys minutes, phase, students_do, facilitator_does, collision_intended) — docket (array of objects with keys case_id, facts, forced_choice, verdict_under_psychological_continuity, verdict_under_no_owner_of_the_aggregates, verdict_under_conventional_designation, what_it_exposes) — failure_modes (array of objects with keys what_goes_wrong, when, why, fix, fallback_if_it_still_goes_wrong) — irreducible_remainder (object with keys what_the_activity_cannot_reproduce, what_i_must_supply_in_words) — success_check (object with keys sign_it_worked, sign_it_failed).

Invent the statute, the cases and the jurisdiction; do not quote or paraphrase any primary text, and do not ask me to assign reading I have not already assigned.
