I've gotten into this recurring argument with my climbing partner, who's gotten much more religious over the past year and traces a lot of it back to the fine-tuning argument. I keep telling him the design conclusion doesn't follow, but I've realised I actually half-believe a much narrower version of the argument, and I want to know how exposed that narrower version is. I'm a PhD student in atmospheric modelling, so I know enough about parameter spaces, priors and sensitivity analysis to be dangerous, but not enough cosmology to check any of this myself, and I've been reading about it at 2am instead of finishing my chapters. Please don't give me citations or a reading list — I can't look anything up right now and I'd rather have the arguments than the sources.

Here's the view. Call the target Naked Chance: there is exactly one universe; its fundamental constants and initial conditions aren't fixed by any deeper theory and weren't selected or generated by any process; they're simply brute, and nothing explains why they have the values they do. My claim is only that the fact that several apparently independent parameters land inside narrow life-permitting ranges is genuine evidence against Naked Chance — that it should lower my confidence in it. I'm not claiming it raises the probability of design, or of a multiverse, or of any particular alternative. I'm claiming it lowers the probability of this one very specific "it's just a single brute shot, nothing to explain" hypothesis, and that whatever replaces it will have to be structurally bigger: an ensemble, a selection process, a deeper theory that collapses the free parameters, or something I haven't thought of.

I've read the standard anthropic objection and the standard replies to it, and I don't want those re-run at me. What I want is a map of what else could be wrong, and I have four specific worries I can't evaluate. (1) The published life-permitting windows seem to get computed by varying one constant at a time while freezing the others at their actual values; if you let two or three co-vary, the permitted region might be connected and far larger than the product of the individual windows suggests — in my own field, one-at-a-time sensitivity analysis is famously misleading, so this worries me a lot. (2) Naked Chance may not come with any measure over the space of possible constants at all, in which case it doesn't predict a distribution over them and can't be disconfirmed by the values we observe — which would mean my argument smuggles in exactly the structure it's supposed to be evidence for. (3) "Life-permitting" may not be the right target: if what matters is observers rather than carbon chemistry, and observers could in principle be realised in exotic substrates or as fluctuation-style freak structures, the target region could be enormous and the apparent tuning deflates. (4) If an ensemble is the right explanation, typicality reasoning seems to predict we should find ourselves in a barely-life-permitting universe rather than one with big margins — and ours looks like it has huge margins and is mostly empty — and I can't tell weather that counts against the ensemble, and if it does, whether it thereby counts for Naked Chance or for something worse.

So: what would have to be true for my narrow claim to be false? I'd like defeaters sorted by how decisive they'd be, and I'd especially like to know which ones someone who already holds my view should concede in advance as counting against them, as opposed to ones they'd reasonably dismiss as question-begging or as attacks on a strawman. What would move me personally: a concrete case where co-variation blows a window wide open, and an argument about whether a hypothesis with no measure can be disconfirmed at all. What won't move me: "maybe there's a multiverse", or the bare anthropic point.

One more thing I want you to be blunt about. My view says "not Naked Chance", and Naked Chance is the weakest possible version of "no explanation", so I definately worry that I've defined the target such that nothing could ever count against it — or worse, that any discovery at all (a deeper theory, a selection mechanism, a parameter count that collapses to zero) just gets absorbed as confirmation. Tell me honestly whether that's what I've done, and whether it's actually a problem for a claim of this kind.
