I'm finishing a dissertation chapter on coercive threats in crisis bargaining, and I keep running into a configuration I have no name for.

The background is the standard machinery of brinkmanship. A wants B to do something, and A's threat isn't believed, so A makes it believable by giving up part of its own control over whether the threat is carried out: authority is dispersed, standing orders are written, the response is arranged so that it might simply happen without a fresh decision from the centre. The more thoroughly A ties its own hands, the more seriously B takes the threat.

The configuration I can't name is what happens past a certain point. Once B is convinced that A could not stop the response even if B complied, compliance buys B nothing, because the thing it was buying with compliance is no longer A's to deliver. In my cases B then does one of two things, neither of them what A wanted: it refuses outright, on the grounds that refusing costs it nothing more than complying would, or it concludes that if the response is coming anyway its best moment to act is now, before the machinery is fully armed. So the threat is at its most believable exactly when it has stopped being something B can respond to, and the move that supplies credibility destroys the threat's usefulness.

This isn't the familiar point that a threat must be credible — here the threat is fully credible — and it isn't the familiar point that a threatener also needs to be able to promise restraint, since the failure is produced by the credibility-raising move itself and worsens as credibility improves. It also isn't quite the move of making a threat effective by letting the outcome be partly a matter of chance, nor a reputation for irrationality, though it neighbours both.

Is there an established concept for this? I'd like the name if there is one, but what I need is the carving: how it relates to those neighbours, where it differs from each, and where its edges lie — in particular whether it requires B to know that A's loss of control was arranged, and whether it survives if A retains some residual capacity to withhold.
