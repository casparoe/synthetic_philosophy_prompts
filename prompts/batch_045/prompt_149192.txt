Ok so this has become weirdly personal lately. I changed jobs in the spring, took a decent raise, and ended up pledging about 10% of it to a couple of global health charities. My brother-in-law thinks that's lovely but optional, we've been going bak and forth for weeks, and it keeps circling back to the shallow pond.

Here's the thing I want a number on. Call P the claim that someone who walks past a drowning toddler in a shallow pond, in dry shoes, late for a meeting, does something morally wrong in the same register as breaking a fairly serious promise — not merely that they failed to be a saint, but that blame is genuinely in order. My credence on P is about 0.85. His is about 0.35, and I honestly can't tell which moving part he disagrees with.

So: give me your own number on P, but mostly decompose it. The terms I keep coming back to:

- whether the pull of the case is evidence about duty at all, or just identifiable-victim psychology plus the fact that you happen to be the only one standing there;
- whether the step from one child to all the children you could help is valid, or whether the demandingness objection really works as a reductio of the whole argument;
- doing vs allowing — how much weight it actually carries once you say it plainly;
- the crowded pond: twelve people who could each wade in, nobody does. Does each owe only a fair share of the cost rather than the rescue itself, and does that fair-share idea scale up until, among eight billion bystanders, each of us owes approximately nothing?
- the relay pond: you know for certain that if you don't wade in, someone else will thirty seconds later and the child is fine either way. Is walking on still wrong? I care about this one because it separates your duty being about the outcome from its being about you being the one who responds;
- whether my uncertainty about whether the money actually achieves anything changes whether I have a duty or only what the duty is.

Put a number on each with one line of justification, combine them, and tell me which single term your final number is most sensitive to. Then tell me which of the two people — the 0.85 and the 0.35 — is most likely wrong about which term; I'd rather find out we disagree about one thing than keep talking past each other.

Be commited: give me the actual number, pick a side on where the disagreement lives, and say outright if one of my terms is muddled or doesn't belong. Don't hand me it depends on your theory of reasons.
