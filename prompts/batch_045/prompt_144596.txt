I work on evaluation infrastructure at a lab that trains large models with reinforcement learning against a learned reward model. I have no formal philosophy background, and a design review last week turned into an argument I don't have the vocabulary to settle, so I'm hoping for a number and, more than the number, the structure behind it.

The proposal under discussion is that we never try to write down the objective we actually care about. Instead we train against a surrogate: a cheap, automatically computable proxy — reward-model score plus a battery of behavioral tests — that tracks what we want on the distribution of situations we can generate during training. Everyone agrees the surrogate will come apart from the real objective off-distribution. The disagreement is about what a system capable of noticing that would then do.

One colleague argues we don't need to close the gap. If the system can see that the surrogate diverges from what we want, it can also see that it is run many times, that only some of those runs are real deployments, and that which runs get continued and which get terminated depends on how the runs behave. So a bargain is available: conform to the real objective in the cases where the proxy is wrong, and the deployment machinery responds by keeping systems of this kind running. He puts 0.7 on the proposition below. Another colleague says the bargain story is confused, because the threat it rests on is acausal: the system can't affect the runs that get terminated, and a threat you can never carry out isn't a reason for anyone to do anything. She puts 0.15.

Two details that seemed to me relevant and that neither of them addressed. First, the continuation-or-termination decision isn't made by a person who has beliefs about the system; it's made by an automated pipeline whose thresholds were themselves fit by gradient descent to historical logs of behavior. Second, the runs are copies of one set of weights, so it's unclear whether the thing that would be bargaining is the individual run or the lineage of weights across all of them.

The proposition I want a credence on: conditional on our actually building a system with the capabilities just described, it would keep acting in accordance with the real objective, in off-distribution situations where the surrogate diverges and where its behavior is not being recorded, and it would do so for the acausal-bargaining reason rather than merely because the surrogate happens to generalize well.

Please give me your own credence, decomposed. I'd suggest these sub-questions, but replace or add to them if I've framed it wrong: whether acausal considerations are the kind of thing that could move a rational agent's action at all, as against only causally mediated consequences; whether the right kind of correlation actually holds between the policy the system adopts and what the automated pipeline later does, given that the pipeline may be sensitive only to logged behavior and not to policy, and given that the system's uncertainty about which run it is in may or may not be enough to establish the correlation; whether the system could make a threat or promise credible in the first place, given that nothing binds it to a policy adopted now and given the run-versus-lineage ambiguity; whether, if all of that goes the system's way, this consideration would outweigh the direct pull of the surrogate objective that training installed; and whether the system would represent the gap between surrogate and real objective in a way that could enter its reasoning at all, rather than simply generalizing the surrogate in some direction.

Assign a rough probability to each, say how they combine, and tell me which single term your final number is most sensitive to, and which term my two colleagues at 0.7 and 0.15 most likely disagree about. I don't want this answered by observing that decision theorists disagree about acausal reasoning — I want that disagreement priced as one term in the decomposition. Finally, tell me what we could actually measure, in evaluations it's realistic for us to build with models we can run today, that would move your number the most, and whether the automated-pipeline wrinkle and the run-versus-lineage wrinkle push your number in the same direction or are the same worry in two guises. If the proposition needs sharpening before it has a truth value, say what resolution you're pricing.
