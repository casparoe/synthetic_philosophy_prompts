For a piece I'm working on about mechanistic interpretability, I want to stage an encounter with W. V. Quine: hand him every weight and activation of a large language model, plus the power to intervene on any internal feature and watch what its outputs do, and ask him whether the model's internal states refer to anything determinate. His radical translator, pairing a native utterance with a scurrying rabbit, could settle nothing on the available evidence between rabbit, undetached rabbit part, and rabbit-stage — so what would he say to the researcher who announces that she has found the model's rabbit feature? I'd like two wrinkles handled rather than smoothed over. First, the researcher's evidence is the complete physical state of the system, not merely verbal behavior: does that break the inscrutability, or does ontological relativity make the internals just more physics awaiting interpretation? Second, the model has no sensory receptors and shares no environment with us, having been trained on text alone, so there is nothing to anchor stimulus meaning or the assent and dissent by which observation sentences get fixed: would Quine think that makes the case better than the jungle case, worse, or different enough that the analogy lapses? Please close with his reply to an interpretability researcher who insists that once we can steer a system's behavior by manipulating a feature, the question of what it means is settled, and that inscrutability is only skepticism in academic dress. Stay within his actual commitments — his physicalism, his neuralism about evidence, his refusal to countenance meanings as entities — and say plainly where, if anywhere, you think he would have had to give ground.
