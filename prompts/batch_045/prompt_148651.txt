I came to philosophy late, after a career in experimental physics, and I am drafting a short position paper on infinite gambles for a reading group. Before I commit myself in print I would like an audit of whether the following package holds together.

The cases I care about are two. G1: a fair coin is tossed until it lands heads; if the first heads falls on toss n you win 2^n dollars when n is odd and lose 2^n dollars when n is even. G2 is the same, except that the magnitude is 2^n/n. In G1 the payoff contributions per state are 1, −1, 1, −1, …; in G2 they are 1, −1/2, 1/3, …. In both, the positive and the negative parts of the expectation diverge, so the expectation does not exist.

My commitments are these.

(i) Where the expectation does not exist, there is no fact of the matter about what the gamble is worth; talk of its value is a façon de parler, and the familiar reason is that any candidate total depends on an ordering or grouping of the states, which carries no decision-theoretic weight.

(ii) A determinate rational price can nevertheless be fixed by a summation rule rather than by an expectation. I favour the damped or Abel sum: it is linear, it is stable in the sense that the value equals the first term plus the value of the continuation, and it provably agrees with the ordinary expectation whenever the ordinary expectation exists. On this rule G1 is worth 1/2 and G2 is worth the natural logarithm of 2, and I would pay exactly that.

(iii) The summation order is not a mere labelling of states: it is supplied by the physical process, since the natural cut-off is the number of tosses that have actually occurred, and damping is a smooth version of that cut-off. This is why (ii) escapes the arbitrariness that motivates (i), and it seems to me the same move one makes when a regularised sum in physics is not the sum of anything and still delivers the right number.

(iv) The rational treatment of a gamble supervenes on the probability distribution it induces over payoffs. A geometric distribution over n can be produced either by repeated tossing or by a single uniform draw, and I assume these cannot differ in value.

(v) Value is additive over a partition of the state space: split the states into any two classes, evaluate the gamble restricted to each, and add.

One datum I cannot place: for G1 the expectations of the games truncated at the n-th toss oscillate between 1 and 0 forever and never approach 1/2, and I am told this shows any price in the unit interval is admissible and that 1/2 is only one of them.

Please tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look to be, ordered by severity; for each real tension, what exactly would have to be given up and what that costs; and which single revision would clean up the most. I do not know which of (i) through (v) I would surrender, and I would rather be told the package is unstable than have it patched politely.
