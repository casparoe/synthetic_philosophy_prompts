Hi — I work at a smallish foundation (about $4M a year out the door, three program staff, roughly 25 grants, health and animal welfare mostly) and we've backed ourselves into a philosophical mess that I need turned into something runnable. One bloc on our board has been arguing that if the lexical-threshold negative-utilitarian picture is even approximately right — that there's a severity threshold above which suffering lexically dominates any amount of milder suffering *and* isn't offset by pleasures at all — then our whole pipeline is the wrong shape of tool, because we currently rank everything on a single made-up currency of "units of wellbeing per dollar" and that only makes sense if pleasure and pain are commensurable on one cardinal scale. The other bloc thinks that's fanatical and would have us defund things everyone agrees are good. The compromise the board landed on is that they want a written procedure, not a white paper: something a program officer with a public-health or vet-med background and no philosophy training can work through in about 40 minutes per application, that ends in a fund / don't fund / send back for more info, and that produces a half page of reasoning a donor could read without wincing. The board also wants to know explicitly where they're allowed to overrule it, since they won't accept being bound by it.

So: give me the procedure. Ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the points where it stops with a no, and the points where it hands the decision to a person because it's a values call rather than a factual one. Some things I specifically want it to handle, and some of these I suspect are where the real work is. First, severity placement under coarse evidence: we have an internal six-rung suffering ladder we use informally, and I want to know what's actually being ordered — peak intensity, duration, or the product — whether the threshold is on a single episode or on a whole life, and whether many sub-threshold experiences can ever *sum* past the threshold (my guess is the honest lexical answer is no, and I want to see the bullet bitten rather than fudged into mere heavy weighting). Second, uncertainty: we essentially never know the severity, so the officer is always choosing between something like a 10% shot at preventing agony and a near-certain reduction in moderate chronic pain, and I need a rule for reasoning about probability-of-crossing-the-threshold that doesn't quietly reintroduce cross-rung expected-value arithmetic through the back door. Third, the offset question: can pleasure cancel pain at all, intra-personally or inter-personally, and if the view says never, what does our procedure do with the fact that our donors and our beneficiaries care a lot about positive experiences? Fourth, counterfactuals about *who exists*: several of our grants change prices or demand, so they plausibly change which animals live and how many, and there's substitution leakage between species and between countries — I need the procedure to catch that rather than awarding points for measured suffering on the animals in front of us. And fifth, gaming: applicants already learn to describe whatever they work on as agony, so I'd like a calibration step that penalises severity claims which have inflated over time in our own files. Wherever the procedure has to draw an arbitrary line (which rung is the threshold, how big a probability is big enough), say who should set it, how often to revisit it, and what record-keeping makes that revisiting possible.

Then run it on three of our actual live decisions so I can see whether it survives contact: (1) a cost-share grant to a large poultry producer for a controlled-atmosphere stunning retrofit affecting several million birds a year, where the ask is big enough that it crowd out four smaller grants; (2) a palliative-care and opioid-availability programme in a low-income country, where the target suffering is unambiguously top-rung but the theory of change is soft and there's a real regulatory-capture risk we can't quantify; and (3) our original flagship, a small music-and-playground programme in the same districts we fund health work in, which staff love, which the board will fight for, and which I'm fairly sure your procedure tells us to stop funding — so please show me how it says that, in language I could actually bring to a board meeting. Also tell me plainly what the procedure can't do: which cases it genuinely can't settle, where the judgment can't be eliminated no matter how much scaffolding you add, where it will misfire in a way I should warn officers about, and what an officer should write down when they hit one of those points so the board can see the seam.
