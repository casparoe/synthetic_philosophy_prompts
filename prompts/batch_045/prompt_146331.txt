I sit on a small foresight team that advises a research fund on transformative AI, and we have noticed that most of our disagreements are really disagreements about which historical analogy is quietly doing the work. I would like two of them compared rather than blended.

The first is the printing press analogy: AI as chiefly an information technology that collapses the cost of producing competent text, code and images, whose effects arrive through the diffusion of capability and through institutions slowly reorganising around cheapened cognition — with the cautionary part of the story being the century of propaganda, censorship struggles and wars of religion that followed cheap print, rather than the technology itself. The second is the Industrial Revolution analogy: AI as chiefly the substitution of machine effort for human effort in production, whose effects arrive through capital accumulation, energy and compute constraints, and the reorganisation of labour markets — with the cautionary part being the long lag between the technical breakthrough and the institutional settlement (factory legislation, unions, mass schooling) that eventually spread its gains.

Compare them along three axes. First, what each takes the binding constraint to be: a scarcity of skilled human attention, or a scarcity of physical capital and energy. Second, where each predicts the first serious disruption will show up: in the wage structure, or in the epistemic environment. Third, what each implies about the proper object of governance: the model, the compute, the application layer, or the downstream institutions that have to absorb the shock.

Our internal rule is what we call the fork test: an analogy earns its keep in a funding decision only if it yields at least one prediction the other analogy gets wrong, and we can state that prediction in advance. Apply the test to both analogies, then recommend which one should carry more weight in our work over the next five years. Finally, tell us where your reasoning is most likely to fail, and name the single development or argument that would most quickly move you to the other frame.

Please write in British English.
