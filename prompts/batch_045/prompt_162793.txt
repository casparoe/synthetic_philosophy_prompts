I'm in the lower sixth, and two friends in my year had an argument at lunch which I was asked to settle and made a poor job of. I'd be grateful for a proper verdict, since I've promised them one.

The school canteen serves beef lasagne on Tuesdays, and Finn has stopped eating it. Nadia says this achieves nothing: the canteen has already bought the mince for the half-term, the animal is dead, and no animal is spared by lasagne left on one plate. Finn says the order is not fixed by fate — the caterer revises it each term from what is actually eaten — so his skipping it slightly raises the chance that next term's order is smaller. Nadia says that is a probability too small and too unmeasurable to be a reason for anything: you cannot multiply a figure you cannot estimate by a benefit you cannot locate. Finn says that proves too much, because Nadia thinks voting is worth doing, and there too the chance of one ballot deciding anything is minute and nobody can say where the decisive one falls. Nadia says the cases are not alike: an election has a definite threshold and your vote sits on it, whereas the order moves in whole cases of mince and Finn has no idea where the jumps are — and, worse, if the canteen sells less beef it sells more chicken, which by her reckoning means more animals killed for the same dinners rather than fewer. Finn says that last claim is an empirical guess she is not entitled to, and that in any case she is now arguing that we cannot know the effects, which is a reason for caution, not for eating the lasagne. Nadia says no: it is a reason for thinking "does my bit help?" is the wrong question altogether. Finn is one person among millions, she says, none of whom makes a difference, and nobody has explained how to get from that to a duty on him. Finn says the duty is not to make a difference but not to take part. Nadia says that is a claim about his character, not about the cow.

Would you go through the exchange move by move and say who is right about what, and what both of them are missing? Please don't leave me with "it's complicated".
