I sit on the grants committee of a small independent foundation (roughly $4.2M to disburse this cycle), and we are trying to decide between three shortlisted causes using the three-factor prioritization framework our staff has used for years — expected value per dollar approximated as importance × tractability × neglectedness. Our program staff scored them as follows. Cause A, scaling a micronutrient fortification program through an existing government delivery system, scores very high on importance and tractability (strong evidence, known cost per unit of benefit) but low on neglectedness, and staff now estimate that around 60% of our marginal dollar would simply displace budget lines the government would otherwise have funded. Cause B, a multi-year litigation and standard-setting push to tighten a national building code in a way that would cut indoor air pollution, scores high on importance with a long and diffuse causal pathway, moderate tractability, and moderate neglectedness; staff's note says the neglect here looks informational — hardly anyone has tried this route — rather than diagnostic. Cause C, seed funding to launch a research field around a poorly understood systemic risk, scores off the charts on importance with enormous error bars, very low on measured tractability (there is almost no evidence base), and very high on neglectedness — though the only groups that have touched it appear to have tried, failed to get traction, and moved on. Under the framework as written, A wins comfortably, B is second, C is last.

At our last meeting I circulated a proposed revision, and the committee is split on it, so I need something I can put in front of them. The revision has two parts. First, replace neglectedness with what I've been calling counterfactual neglectedness: not how little is currently spent on the cause, but how little would be spent if we did not act, net of crowding out and of the fungibility that lets our grant free up someone else's money for something we would not have chosen. Second, stop treating tractability as a single number: score marginal tractability at the funding level the cause is actually at (rather than average tractability), and treat low measured tractability differently depending on whether the surrounding neglect is informational (no one has looked, so absence of evidence is not evidence of absence) or diagnostic (others looked and moved on, so the neglect is itself evidence against tractability). On my scoring this flips the ordering — B first, A second, C third — mainly because A's displacement is large and C's neglect looks diagnostic rather than informational. Our board member who is a retired epidemiologist dissents from the whole exercise: her claim is that importance is a prior in numerical clothing, tractability is estimated from thin and self-selected evidence, and a framework you can re-tune until it produces the conclusion you like is not a decision rule at all, so we would do better with a blunt rule of funding whichever intervention has the strongest direct evidence of effect — which would put A clearly first. I think she is wrong but I do not think she is silly, and I would rather the memo defeat her position than talk past it.

Please draft the decision memo I will circulate before the November 14 meeting: about 900–1,200 words, bottom line up front, addressed to the committee. It should recommend one of the three decision rules (the framework as written, my revision, or the evidence-strength rule), give a concrete allocation of the $4.2M across A, B and C as percentages summing to 100, and say what happens when — e.g. what we re-score at the six-month review and what would have to be true for next cycle's ordering to change. Engage the epidemiologist's objection at its strongest, including the uncomfortable version where the framework's flexibility is exactly why it is useful for search but exactly why it cannot discipline us. Also flag where my revision might be rigging the answer rather than correcting a bias: in particular whether separability fails (whether the three factors can really be multiplied when tractability plausibly falls as funding rises and neglectedness is often evidence about tractability), and whether the framework quietly favors causes whose benefits are legible and measurable over causes whose benefits are systemic. Throughout, separate what is settled — the arithmetic of diminishing returns, what follows from the definitions — from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and do not present a contested claim as consensus; where you are extrapolating beyond what the staff numbers support, say so plainly. End the memo with a single machine-readable line: \answer{[adopt revision | retain framework as written | adopt evidence-strength rule]; A: __%, B: __%, C: __%}.
