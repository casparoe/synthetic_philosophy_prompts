You are helping me assemble the centerpiece of a planned special issue, *Signalling Theory after the Handicap Principle*. Two contributors have been arguing past each other for a decade and have agreed, at my urging, to write one joint statement instead of two more rebuttals. I need you to draft the document they should produce.

The parties are, on one side, someone who holds that stable honest advertisement of a hidden, continuously varying quality to a receiver whose interests partly conflict with the signaller's requires a *differential* cost — that at equilibrium the marginal cost of signalling must fall as quality rises, so that no lower-quality individual could profitably match the signal — and that this is what the handicap principle, properly stated, asserts. On the other side is someone who holds that honesty is secured by whatever makes the signal's conditions of production track the advertised quality: physical or developmental constraint, a trait whose only function is to make an already-existing cue easier for the receiver to perceive or measure, receiver-side verification with post-hoc retaliation, or partial alignment of interests. On that view differential cost is a common consequence of honest signalling and a frequent marker of it, but not what does the stabilizing work, and the handicap principle mistakes a symptom for the mechanism.

Write the statement in four parts.

1. **Agreed claims, numbered.** Include at least: a taxonomy of the notions of cost both sides need to keep apart (absolute production cost; marginal or differential cost at equilibrium; the counterfactual cost a mimicker of lower quality would have to pay, retaliation and verification costs included; cost measured in fitness versus cost measured in resources or opportunity), with a note on which of these each side's thesis is about; the game-theoretic background both accept, including the point about freely chosen costless signals under strict conflict, and the point that whether real signallers have access to a cost-free channel is an empirical and developmental question rather than a theorem.

2. **A disagreements table.** Columns: the disputed claim; a formulation of it the handicap side would sign as its own; a formulation the cue-and-constraint side would sign as its own; and a verdict on whether the disagreement is substantive or verbal. Where you judge a row verbal, say which term is doing the work and what substantive question survives once it is disambiguated.

3. **A crux table.** For each crux: the crux stated as a yes/no question; the case, manipulation, or model variation both sides agree would bear on it; the handicap side's prediction registered in advance; the cue-and-constraint side's prediction; and what each side would count as moved. Cover at least these cruxes: the separability question — whether the equilibrium correlation between signal and quality could be held fixed while the differential production cost is driven to zero, and whether the signalling system would then still be stable, or whether that counterfactual is incoherent; the subsidized-signal case, in which the production cost is paid on every signaller's behalf, or a mutant arises that produces the full signal at negligible marginal cost while changing nothing else; the perceptibility case, in which the trait at issue makes an existing cue easier to measure rather than adding a burden; the enforcement case, in which all of the relevant cost is imposed by receivers after the signal is given, so that no cost is paid at production; the origin-versus-maintenance crux, whether the two theses are claims about how honesty gets established or only about how it persists; and a crux about whether the distinction between costs that merely accompany honesty and costs that enforce honesty depends on features of the models — discreteness of the quality variable, continuity of the signal, or whether costs and benefits combine additively — rather than on features of organisms. Flag explicitly any crux on which both sides would predict the same outcome, since those tests cannot settle anything, and any crux on which you think neither side would in fact move.

4. **What neither side holds.** A short list of theses often attributed to one camp that that camp actually rejects, so that the volume does not reprint a decade of strawmen.

Constraints: no citations, bibliographies, author names, or paper titles — refer to positions by the neutral labels above, and do not lean on any particular published model by name. Do not adjudicate; the document must be one both parties could sign. Aim for something a journal could print as a two-page joint statement, addressed to readers who know the formalism of signalling games but should not have to reconstruct it.
