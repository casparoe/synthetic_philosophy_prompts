ok so next week I'm giving a paper at this interdisciplinary workshop thing — 30 min talk, and then like 20 min of Q&A which is the part I'm dreading. The room is roughly: half cultural psychologists who actually use the tightness/looseness framework in their day to day work (they think in terms of ecological threat, situational constraint, multilevel models, whether the construct holds up within countries and not just between them, whether my measures are even measuring the same thing as theirs); two political philosophers who think the whole framework quietly assumes a liberal picture on which fewer enforced rules = more freedom = better, and who will want to know what I think "coercion" even is; and one anthropologist who is fairly sure that any cross-national survey measure of "how strict is this society" is really measuring what an outsider with a questionnaire can see, not what people live inside of.

Here's the thesis, sketch version. First: tightness is at least two things wearing one label — (a) coverage, how wide the range of behaviour that's normatively regulated at all, and (b) intensity, how hard a violation gets punished once it's on the books. They come apart, and they come apart systematically: a society can regulate a huge amount and sanction it mildly, or regulate a narrow band and sanction it brutally, and the standard composite score flattens both into "moderately tight". Second, and this is the bit I'm shakiest on: the scores partly track how legible enforcement is to a researcher, not how constrained people actually feel. Informal sanctions — reputational, relational, family-based, the kind that never becomes a fine or a prison sentence — don't show up in the instruments, so some places score as loose because their enforcement is invisible to us rather than absent. Third, the reform implication: if you want to loosen a society, cut intensity first and leave coverage mostly alone, because coverage does coordination work people genuinely rely on (queueing conventions, traffic, contract form, which side of the hall you walk on), while intensity is where the cruelty and the ruined lives actually live.
