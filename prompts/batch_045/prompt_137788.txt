I am writing a survey chapter on anthropic reasoning, and I want to predict how one theorist in the area — call her M — will rule on a case she has not yet published on. Her verdicts so far, as I have tabulated them:

(1) *Incubator.* A fair coin is flipped. If heads, one observer is created in a room; if tails, two observers are created in two rooms. No one can tell how many rooms there are or which one he is in. You wake in a room. Verdict: 2/3 tails.

(2) *The presumptuous philosopher.* Two cosmological theories with equal priors, alike in every respect except that one says the world contains 10^12 observers and the other 10^24; observers under either theory have the same evidence. Verdict: the presumptuous philosopher's inference is sound; credence in the second theory should be about 1 − 10^−12.

(3) *Doomsday.* Priors: 5% that our species ends at 10^11 members, 95% that it reaches 10^14. You find yourself roughly the 7 × 10^10th. Verdict: no shift; 5% stays 5%.

(4) *The first pair.* Eve knows that if she and Adam have a child they will be expelled and the population will eventually reach about 10^12; if not, there will never be more than the two of them. Setting aside anthropic considerations, the evidence is balanced. Verdict: no shift; 50% stays 50%.

(5) *The painted door.* Heads: one observer, behind a red door. Tails: two observers, one behind a red door and one behind a blue door; each knows the setup and which door he is behind. You find yourself behind a red door. Verdict: 1/2.

As far as I can tell, exactly two rules generate all five. The first: the posterior of a hypothesis is proportional to its prior times the number of *momentary conscious stages* (observer-moments) that would have exactly your evidence if it were true — what you get from the self-indication assumption combined with self-sampling over observer-moments, or, equivalently, from self-sampling alone with the reference class narrowed to stages in your exact evidential situation. The second: the same rule with *persons* or streams of consciousness in place of moments. Every case M has ruled on contains at most one observer-moment per person per evidential state, so nothing she has said distinguishes them, and she is deliberately silent on Sleeping-Beauty-style cases on the ground that they turn on questions about personal identity over time that she does not want to prejudge.

The case I want to put to her avoids fission, amnesia and duplication, and turns only on how long a life lasts. Two hypotheses about a far-future population, equal priors. *Many short lives*: 10^9 persons, each of whom has one hour of conscious experience, every instant of it subjectively indistinguishable from your present instant, and then ceases. *Few long lives*: 10^6 persons, each of whom has 10^6 hours of conscious experience, again every instant subjectively indistinguishable from your present instant. Nobody is in a position to tell which hypothesis is true or which sort of person he is.

Please give me:

- Her predicted verdict, as a number, under the moment-counting rule and under the person-counting rule, with the arithmetic shown.
- Your best guess at what she will actually say, and which of the two rules she is really running. Do not just split the difference: tell me which verdict is better supported by the five precedents and why, and whether there is a third rule that fits all five but comes apart here.
- Her verdicts on three variants: (i) the person numbers are equal (10^9 each) and only the durations differ, one hour against 10^3 hours; (ii) the durations differ but total experience-time is the same, 10^9 persons for one hour each against 10^6 persons for 10^3 hours each; (iii) as in the original case, except that the long-lived persons spend 99.9% of their 10^6 hours in dreamless sleep. Say which variant is the cleanest test of the moment-versus-person question, and why.
- If my new case smuggles the identity question back in — because a person with temporal parts might just be a collection of momentary stages — say so, and propose a case that separates the two rules without doing that.
- Finally, the normative version: if she wants her verdict here to be consistent with verdicts (1) through (5), what should she say, and what is she committing herself to either way — about the metaphysics of persons, about what self-indication is supposed to be tracking, or about whether the quantity of consciousness has anthropic weight at all?
