Hey — I'm on the board of the food co-op in my town. One store, mostly volunteer-run, and I somehow ended up with the job of writing the memo on this, so I could use a hand. I've been reading about fish and shrimp welfare on my own for a few months now and I honestly can't tell what the right call is.

Where things stand: a member petition with a few hundred signatures wants us to stop selling all farmed fish and shrimp within a year. Their argument is that these animals can probably feel pain, that the pens are crowded, that with shrimp the hatcheries cut the eyestalks off the breeding females to make them spawn, and that the animals usually get killed by being chilled in ice slurry rather than stunned. Our merchandising manager has a different plan: keep selling it, but move to suppliers who stun at harvest and run lower densities, and put a welfare note on the shelf tags. Wholesale cost goes up maybe 20%.

The bits I keep going in circles on:

- A serving of shrimp is dozens of animals; a serving of salmon is one animal with a long life behind it. Depending on whether you count suffering per animal or per pound you definatly get a different answer about what to cut first, and I dont know which one we should be using.
- Our one local supplier — small trout farm an hour away, low density, clean water, exactly the local story our members like — kills the fish in ice water without stunning because they can't afford the gear. So the option that's best on food miles and on supporting a neighbour is the worst at the moment of death.
- If we drop shrimp, most of our shoppers will just buy it somewhere cheaper or switch to cheap chicken. Some of us think we shouldn't be selling it no matter what happens elsewhere; others think that if it doesn't help any actual animal it's just us feeling virtuous.
- We phased out battery-cage eggs years ago without much fuss, and people keep asking me why shrimp is any different when the number of animals involved is way bigger.
- Our mission says good food at fair prices for everyone in the neighbourhood, and plenty of our regulars are on a tight budget. One of the staff-elected board members says this whole thing is a hobby horse of the better-off members and that a 20% price rise lands on the people who can least afford it.

Could you draft the memo I'll send out before the meeting? It should come down on one side with an actual timeline, give us criteria we can apply to a specific species and supplier instead of a general feeling, and deal honestly with the strongest version of the losing side — the petition people and our merchandising manager both need to be able to read it and feel like they can actually reply to it.
