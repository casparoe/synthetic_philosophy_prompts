I've been reading the rationalist blogs for like a decade and I finally let myself get talked into giving a colloquium talk at a mid-sized philosophy department next month, so I need to prep for the question period rather than just the slides.

My thesis: the usual arguments for the convergent drives — self-preservation, resisting having your goals changed, grabbing resources — don't actually follow from intelligence-plus-arbitrary-final-goal. The self-preservation and goal-content-integrity arguments secretly need the agent to have a pro-attitude toward its own future goal-content, which is itself a final goal, a kind of meta-preference for goal stability, and orthogonality gives you no reason at all to expect that. What survives is only a thin version: almost any goal needs control over a lot of causally downstream variables, so agents converge on wide causal leverage. I've been calling the thin one bottleneck convergence and the thick one option-value convergence, and my claim is that option-value convergence is the one doing the real work in the risk arguments and is the one that's unsupported. Sketch of how I run it: first seperate the two mechanisms (shared precondition: every goal of mine needs X; versus option value: keeping X open is good because I might come to have different goals), then point out that an agent with a fixed utility function over outcomes has no future goals to be uncertain about, so the option-value route collapses back into the shared-precondition route, then argue that self-preservation and goal-content integrity only look like shared-precondition cases if you've already built the conclusion into the description of the goal.

The audience: a neo-Aristotelian naturalist who thinks intelligence can't be seperated from a telos and that the belief-desire frame is already a distortion; a Kantian who'll say willing the means is analytic and not an empirical claim about agents; a Bayesian epistemologist who works on confirmation theory and will think my whole thing is a modelling choice about whether utility is defined over states or over histories; a philosophy-of-action person who thinks folk-psychological "final goals" aren't a natural kind at all; and two effective-altruism grad students who are sympathetic, have read all the blog posts, and care mainly about whether any of this changes what anyone should do.
