I have been trying, on and off, to say what all the things we call pleasures have in common, and I keep arriving at the same impasse. I would like to know whether anyone has named it.

Consider the taste of cold water when one is very thirsty; the moment a crossword clue finally resolves; the relief of setting down a heavy bag; the sound of a passage of music one loves. These do not appear to share anything one could point to in the experience itself — no common texture, no common bodily location, no common object. The distinctions I keep coming across elsewhere are all distinctions between kinds of pleasure, rather than about what makes any of them a pleasure at all.

Every candidate I can construct fails in one of two opposite directions. Either it is a bare residue left over from subtraction: strip away the coldness, the relief, the melody, and what remains is a thin "this is good, keep going" — except that I cannot locate anything in the experience answering to that, apart from the very features I have just stripped away. Or it is a stance taken towards the experience, and that I can identify well enough, except that the very same stance seems present in experiences I would not call pleasant at all: the person who wants to go on being angry, the grief one does not want interrupted, the film one is glad to be watching whilst hating every minute.

One further case seems to break both at once: the warm pressure of a massage can, as far as I can tell, shift from pleasant to unpleasant with no change in what it feels like, only in whether I want it.

Does this difficulty have an established name, and does the split between the two families of candidate? I am not after a label. I want to know whether the two failures are aspects of a single problem or two distinct ones, what the nearest established concepts are and precisely how each differs from what I have described, whether the two families are genuinely the only options — and, if nothing established fits, a term with a definition and some boundary cases, so that I can tell what would and would not count as an instance.
