Write me a parody: a Request for Proposals put out by a made-up body called the Intergovernmental Office for Atrocity Assessment, offering a contract to build the Comparative Severity Index — one number per historical atrocity, so that museums, school curricula and reparations commisions can finally sort them. Format it like a real procurement notice: numbered clauses with section headings (scope of work, mandatory methodology, deliverables, evaluation criteria), then a short "questions from bidders" section at the end. Around 700 words, deadpan throughout — no winking, no line that announces its own joke. American English please.

The humor has to come from the actual problems with ranking evils, so that someone who works on incommensurability or hard comparisons reads it and thinks the satire is fair.Things it needs to get right and quietly send up:

- the unit problem: a single scale needs a unit, so invent one — my suggestion is the substitution rate, how many standard famine-years an informed jury would accept in exchange for averting the event — and let the RFP insist, straight-faced, that this is a measurement and not a preference.
- the denominator: absolute victim counts make atrocities against small populations nearly invisible, so the Office has adopted a per-target-population option under which a campaign that killed most of a small people outranks one that killed far more people in absolute terms. Leave it genuinely unclear which is worse.
- the ceiling: if severity saturates — past some point everything is just "maximally bad" — the Index can't rank the cases anyone actually cares about, so the Office breaks ties by administrative efficiency: deaths per bureaucrat-hour, killings per unit of expenditure. This is the reductio, let it land, and include a couple of lines from the draft league table.
- interrupted atrocities: a genocide stopped halfway gets full credit for intent and so can outrank a completed one that killed more people. The Office is entirely fine with this.
- downstream effects: whether to count the famine afterward, the diaspora, the children never born.

Use placeholder event codes ("Event 14 (steppe famine, 1930s)") instead of naming real cases — I dont want the piece to turn into an actual ranking. Close with a bidder asking in the Q&A whether the winning contractor also has to publish the full table including thier own government's entries, and have the Office answer the way a bureaucracy would. Lowest compliant bid wins.
