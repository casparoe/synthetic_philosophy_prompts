Hi — I'm giving a talk next month to the philosophy of mind reading group here and I want a real Q&A prep sheet, not ad-libbing. Here's what I'm defending.

Constitutive panpsychism, plus a package I've been calling "grain pluralism" (working title). The commitments: (1) the ultimates of physics have phenomenal properties, and a human experience is wholly constituted out of micro-experiences, with no extra brute grounding facts tacked on at the macro level. (2) There is no absolute number of subjects in a region at a time. How many subjects there are is relative to a temporal grain of description, roughly the way how many vortices there are in a stream depends on the scale at which you look. So the subject-summing half of the combination problem never gets going: it assumes a fixed micro-subject count that then has to add up to one. (3) The quality half I handle diachronically: a coarse-grained subject's present experience isn't built out of instantaneous micro-qualities taken all at once, it's a temporal gestalt over a window, the way a melody isn't in any single note. So there's genuine novelty in the macro quality space, but it's novelty across time, not brute synchronic emergence. (4) I stop short of cosmopsychism: coarsening doesn't run all the way up, because a grain yields a subject only if it tracks an actual causal-integration timescale in the system, and there's no global integration scale for the cosmos as a whole.

The audience, about fifteen people: two illusionists who think the hard problem is a pseudo-problem and talk in terms of the illusion and of conceptual engineering; two Russellian monists, one of whom is openly sympathetic to cosmopsychism; one empirically-minded person who works on neural correlates and global integration and will want to know what any of this predicts; one metaphysician who works on grounding, composition and the special composition question; and one phenomenology person who cares about lived time, the specious present and pre-reflective self-awareness. An ethicist drifts in most weeks.

What I want from you: the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this group — not the ones I'd get online, not the ones I'd get from physicists — ordered by how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way that particular person would put it, each with a ninety-second reply and a note on where the reply is soft or where I'd be bluffing. Say who each objection comes from, because it matters to me which of them is annoyed. Also: what I should concede during the talk itself rather than get caught with in Q&A (I'd definately rather give ground early than look cornered at the end), and what the one question I'm hoping nobody asks is.

One worry I can't shake and can't place: grain pluralism feels like too powerful a tool. If it works for me, couldn't someone I don't want to help use the same move somewhere else? I don't know whether that's my best objection or a confusion, so treat it as raw material rather than as a brief.
