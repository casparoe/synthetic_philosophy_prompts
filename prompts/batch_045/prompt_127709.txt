I'm writing a short position paper on diachronic rationality for a volume, and I want to get my own house in order before somebody else spots the hole. Here's roughly where I've landed. The package has survived years of conference conversations, but every time I actually write it down someone tells me two of these can't both be true, and its never the same two.

1. I accept Reflection — the van Fraassen thing: my credence in p now, conditional on my credence in p at some later time being x, should be x. And I want to insist (as he did, I think) that this is really a constraint on my *current* credal state, a kind of synchronic coherence condition, and not a rule telling me to go out and align myself with my future self.

2. What I think makes it true is deference: from where I'm standing, my later self will have strictly more evidence than I have, and so she's an expert whose credences I should defer to. That's the whole ground of the principle as far as I'm concerned.

3. I don't think the Dutch book argument does any real work here or anywhere else. I've never beleived that being money-pumped shows anything about rationality, and I'd rather ground everything in accuracy and calibration considerations instead. Which I realize is slightly awkward, given how Reflection is usually motivated.

4. I'm a permissivist about synchronic credences: given my total evidence there's a non-trivial range of credence functions it would be rational for me to have, and which one I land on inside that range is, in some sense, up to me.

5. I don't think conditionalization is the whole story about updating. It can be perfectly rational to plan to *lose* information: I'm told the coin came up heads, I know that tomorrow I'll be made to forget whether I was ever told anything and I'll go back to 1/2, and there is nothing irrational about signing up for that in advance.

6. And one thing that's been nagging me: if I'm supposed to defer to my later self, and she's supposed to defer to hers, and so on, then my current credences look like they're hostage to my deathbed self, which is absurd.

So: which pairs here are actually in tension, and which only look like it? My guess is that at least one of these is perfectly fine and I'm just confusing myself, and at least one is a real problem I'm not seeing. Rank the tensions by severity rather than flagging everything equally, tell me for each what would have to give and what the cheapest fix would cost me, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. And don't just tell me what the literature says — I want to know which of my own commitments is doing the work, and what I'd be giving up if I dropped it.
