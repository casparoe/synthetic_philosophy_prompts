I'm putting together a one-page handout for a grad reading group on distributive justice — the session is on the leveling-down objection, and I want something better then the usual "here are three views, here's the objection" page. What I'd like is a taxonomy, laid out as a tree, where every branch is labeled with the claim that splits it from its siblings, and every leaf is a named position plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

Size: aim for eight to ten leaves and no more than about five branch levels, so it fits on one side of A4 if I shrink the font.

Build the logical space before reaching for any names: pick four or five seperating questions, show which combinations of answers they actually allow, and only then drop the positions into the cells. For any cell nobody occupies, say why — incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored — that's half the reason I want this. Leaves that have to show up somewhere: telic egalitarianism, the deontic/pluralistic version where equality constrains action rather than being a goal, Parfit-style prioritarianism, sufficientism, luck/choice egalitarianism, relational egalitarianism, and a plain sum-of-benefits view. "Unnamed" is fine for anything you have to add.

The branch questions need to be real cases, not slogans and not "do you value equality?" — concrete and preferably a bit odd, so someone can answer without knowing which view the question is sorting them toward. A few I want in there (add better ones if you have them): a hurricane that destroys the surplus of the better-off half of a population, leaving everyone worse off but equal; a policy that lowers everyone's expected welfare while equalizing their prospects; a case where the inequality is entirely the product of the better-off person's own choices; a case where the worse-off person never learns about the difference and their social standing is unaffected; and a case where equality is reached by the better-off person not existing rather than by anyone being brought down. Also, if it matters to your tree: leveling down in one dimension (income) while welfare overall goes up.

Where two leaves collapse into the same answers — prioritarianism vs pluralistic egalitarianism with a small equality weight, sufficientism vs prioritarianism near a threshold, whatever you find — say so explicitly rather than inventing a distinction. I'd rather have two leaves flagged as indistinguishable than a fake branch.

Last thing: after the tree, give me ten quiz questions I could actually hand out, each a case with two or three options, plus a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto leaves — including what to tell someone whose answers don't land cleanly in any cell.
