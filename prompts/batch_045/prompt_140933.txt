i run a tiny reading group — me, three grad students, a retired actuary, and a friend who just shows up to argue — and we've spent four weeks on whether 'better than' has to be transitive. mostly the spectrum arguments: a long row of cases, each one only a hair different from its neigbour, and the two ends coming out wildly different. nobody has convinced anybody. last meeting is thursday and i'd definately rather end on a poem than on a summary, so here's what i'm after.

a terza rima, 8 to 12 tercets, plus the one closing line that rhymes with the middle line of the last tercet so the chain closes the way a dante canto does. the interlocking aba bcb thing has to be doing real work: each tercet should be one pairwise comparison, and the line that gets carried down into the next tercet is the shared item — so the form is literally the chain of step-by-step judgements that a transitivity argument is built out of. the closing line should bring back the word the poem opened with, not necessarily as a rhyme word, just so the thing is visibly a loop.

the case i want it on is one i made up in week two and have been chewing on since. forty summers of one life, summer 1 to summer 40. summer 1 you spend alone finishing something hard you care about — high on achievement, low on pleasure. summer 40 is beaches and long dinners and nothing else — high on pleasure, no achievement at all. in between, each summer trades a sliver of achievement for a sliver of pleasure, small enough that no adjacent pair feels like a real trade: summer 17 and summer 18 are, from inside them, the same summer. the bit i can't let go of is that you can walk the row in either direction and get either answer, depending on which thing you're moving along — and i don't think that's just the same phenomenon as the row being vague, though it might be, and i'd rather the poem not decide. if it helps: let one repeated word shift its sense as it travels, so the word that comes back at the end isn't quite the word that left.

constraints. no seminar vocabulary anywhere in the poem — no transitive, spectrum, sorites, utility, tradeoff, dominance, dimension, vagueness. no philosophers' names. no footnotes, no headnote, no afterword; if you want an epigraph, invent it and attribute it to nobody. it should be readable out loud in about two minutes, and it should work as a poem for someone who has never heard of any of this. and please don't let it turn into a carpe diem thing or a summing-up-of-a-life thing — no deathbed, no nostalgia for being young. if the rhyme has to strain somewhere, let it strain at the point where the argument does.

three people in the room are going to hear it at once: one thinks the whole puzzle is just vagueness wearing a hat, one thinks the relation genuinely cycles, and one thinks there's a fourth thing a pair of items can be besides better, worse or equally good. i want all three to feel like the poem was written at them and none of them to feel like it agreed with them.
