I am writing a short paper on finks and masks and would like critical comments on the notes below before I commit to a full draft. Assume the standard literature is covered on my end, so I do not need references or reading suggestions; I want help with the argument itself.

Notes: Finks, masks, and what disposition ascriptions are for

Setup. The simple conditional analysis says that x is disposed to manifest M in conditions C if and only if x would M were C to obtain. Two familiar counterexamples. A Lewis-style fink: a live wire wired to a device that deadens it the instant anything touches it. A Martin-style mask: a fragile glass wrapped so that any striking is cushioned. The usual moral is that no simple conditional analysis is correct, and the usual repair adds a requirement that the causal basis persist, unchanged, for a suitable interval after C obtains.

Claim 1, diagnostic. Finks and masks are counterexamples of the same shape: in both, the basis is present at the time of ascription and the manifestation does not occur. They differ only in the causal route to non-manifestation, the basis being destroyed in one case and retained while the manifestation is intercepted in the other. The pair therefore cannot by itself discriminate between rival analyses. What it does do is prise apart three quantities that normally coincide: the basis, meaning the intrinsic structure in virtue of which things of that kind M; the propensity, meaning the chance of M given C; and the frequency, meaning the actual rate of M across C-cases. Much of the literature reads as a dispute over which of these three is the disposition. I want to say the dispute is ill-posed, and that ascriptions are well formed only relative to a degree and a temporal window: disposed to M in C to degree p within interval t.

Claim 2, three cases meant to make that parameterization earn its keep.

A, the deferred fink. A sample of a shock-sensitive compound sits in a rig that detects a descending hammer and floods the sample with a stabilizer. The stabilizer arrives 0.4 milliseconds after impact; detonation takes roughly 2 milliseconds; strikes last 5 milliseconds. Nothing ever detonates. But for the first 0.4 milliseconds the sample is in exactly the intrinsic state that, unrigged, nomically suffices for detonation, and the early stages of the detonation process are arguably already underway. A fixed analysis must give a flat yes or no. Mine gives a high degree over a sub-millisecond window and a near-zero degree over the strike as a whole. I want to conclude that dispositions have temporal profiles, and that the classical fink is the limiting case in which the window collapses to nothing.

B, the chancy fink. Now let the rig misfire: it floods the sample on 90 percent of strikes and fails on the rest, the failures being genuinely chancy. The compound detonates in 10 percent of strikes. Nothing about its intrinsic structure differs from case A. If a mask interferes with the manifestation and a fink interferes with the basis, a probabilistic fink is neither, since it interferes with the basis and sometimes fails to. I conclude that fink and mask are ends of a continuum whose real axis is how deeply the interference reaches, and that no taxonomy with two slots can sort the middle.

C, the intrinsic mask. A pellet of a water-soluble salt which, on sensing humidity, extrudes an insoluble coating over its own surface. The lattice that grounds solubility is untouched, but the suppressor is produced by the object itself in response to the stimulus rather than applied from outside. Standard taxonomies lean on masks being extrinsic, interfering, and no part of the object. Here the interference is intrinsic and self-initiated, and I doubt there is a stable pre-theoretic verdict about whether we have a mask, a fink, or something with no slot. I use the case to argue that the intrinsic/extrinsic contrast is doing borrowed work in the standard taxonomy.

Claim 3, payoff. Disposition ascriptions serve at least three roles: predicting what will happen, explaining what does happen, and sorting things into kinds. Finks undermine the first, not the second or third. Masks undermine the first as well. So the sense of crisis surrounding these cases tracks an unargued assumption that prediction is the central role. Once ascriptions are parameterized, the explanatory and taxonomic roles can be served at one setting of p and t and the predictive role at another.

What I would like:

1. Whether the threefold distinction plus parameterization is a positive proposal or only a relabeling of the existing standoff, and precisely where it would have to say something substantive to avoid that charge.
2. Whether any of the three cases is already in circulation under another description. In particular: does the deferred fink collapse into an ordinary fink with a time lag, and does the chancy fink collapse into a plain chancy disposition? If so, what would have to be added for them to bite?
3. The main cost. If ascriptions need a degree and a window, can they still explain? Can the pellet's solubility still explain its dissolving, or does parameterization make the notion context-dependent in a way that guts the very cases it was introduced for? Is there a principled default setting for p and t, or should I concede context-sensitivity and argue it is benign?
4. The reverse case: an object that acquires the relevant basis only once C obtains, such as a wire that goes live at the moment of contact. My view implies this is not a disposition at all, however strong the pull to say otherwise. Is that a bullet worth biting or the start of a reductio?
5. Flag any place where I have slid from a claim about the semantics or pragmatics of disposition ascriptions to a claim about what dispositions are.
6. Which of the three cases should lead the paper, and where a referee would press hardest.

A page or so of prioritized comments, concrete rather than general, is what would help most.
