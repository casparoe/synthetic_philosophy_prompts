I work in monitoring and evaluation at a philanthropic intermediary, and for the past year I have been building an indicator set for funders who want to report on expansion of the moral circle. Everything I can actually measure — survey items about concern for distant people, the number of countries or species a programme covers, the frequency of moral language in media — feels like a proxy for something I have not yet defined, and each candidate measure quietly settles a philosophical question that the arguments I know do not settle.

The thought I want to work up is that the metaphor conceals several variables that can move independently: how much of the world falls inside the circle; how much weight the beings inside it are given, particularly those near the edge; and the resolution at which we discriminate among them, from species down to individual interests. I suspect movement along one axis can cost movement along another — that learning to see fine-grained differences among the beings we have just admitted may require giving up the coarse universalist language that did the admitting. Behind that sits a prior question about where a circle has to be located for its expansion to be real: in stated conviction, in what people actually notice, or in what institutions do when no one is watching — since indicators can only ever capture the first and the last.

Please turn this into a proposal for a nine-month, part-time fellowship that I would carry out alongside my job. State the question so that it admits of a determinate answer; explain why the usual way of framing the topic — whether there is moral progress at all, and whether it is driven by reason, sentiment, or institutional change — leaves this particular question untouched; set out what I would actually do, month by month, with no research budget and no data collection; describe the deliverable that would be useful to someone drafting funding criteria; and say how I would know the project had failed. I will add the references myself, so please give no bibliography and no literature review organised by citation. British English, please, and around 900 words.
