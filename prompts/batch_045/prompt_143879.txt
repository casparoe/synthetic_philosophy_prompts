Setup: one-molecule Szilard engine in a box on a bath at T_h. Demon measures which half the molecule is in, drops in the partition, lets it expand isothermally, and takes out W = k T_h ln 2 per cycle. The usual story is that the second law is saved because the demon has to erase its one-bit memory afterwards, and erasure costs kT ln 2. But the T in that formula is the temperature of whatever reservoir the memory dumps its bit of entropy into — and nothing forces the demon's memory to sit at the gas temperature. So: let the memory bit be a little system coupled to its own cold reservoir at T_c < T_h, and erase there instead. Then erasure only costs k T_c ln 2 per cycle, i net k(T_h − T_c) ln 2 out of what is supposedly a single hot bath, and by taking T_c → 0 i get arbitarily close to the full k T_h ln 2 for free. (And honestly, why erase at all — why not just write each measurment to a fresh blank bit of tape, never reuse anything, and pay zero?) My advisor waved this off with "it's just Carnot" and wouldnt elaborate. Is that all it is, or does the cold-memory version show that Landauer's principle was never the thing doing the work, and that what really limits the demon is some bound on how much work a bit of information can buy?
