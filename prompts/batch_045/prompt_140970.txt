I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on rational choice — 16 students, one 90-minute session this week plus five minutes at the start of each of the next two sessions. They have seen expected utility theory but nothing on diachronic rationality. I want a two-part activity in which they actually get money-pumped, and come away feeling the difference between two quite different arguments that travel under that label: one exploiting synchronic incoherence (cyclic or elicitation-mode-dependent valuations), one exploiting reversal between planning and acting.

Constraint: no real money may change hands, so the currency has to be something like vouchers redeemable for a consumption good at any session. I want you to be explicit about what that substitution costs the argument and where the design has to compensate for it.

What participants should hit before it is named:

- In part one, that they can end up strictly poorer after a sequence of trades each of which they accepted as an improvement, with no one deceiving them and no odds being changed — the pump runs on a pattern across their own responses, not on any single mistake.
- In part two, that some will pay to have an option removed, some will take a flexible menu and then use it against their earlier selves, and some will stick to a plan — and that from the outside these are hard to distinguish.

Part two needs a mechanism that turns foresight and pre-commitment into real choices with real, small, regrettable costs, settled this week and paid out over the following two sessions.

Please give me: the rules for each part, materials, payoff scheme with a bounded bankroll for the dealer, the facilitator's script (including how to handle a student who refuses to trade because they have guessed where this is going, and one who wants to renegotiate after the fact), a recording sheet, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto the two arguments and onto the three patterns of planning.

Also: what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — stakes too small to reveal anything, satiation, suspicion, genuine preference change versus mere reversal, the difficulty of telling foresight from plan-honoring from outside — and an honest note on which feature of the arguments the activity cannot reproduce and I will have to supply myself.

Finally: separate what is settled among people who work on this from what is contested. Flag rather than settle the live disputes — whether exploitability constitutes the irrationality or merely evidences it, whether sticking to a plan is coherent or violates sequential rationality, and whether being pumped in a consumption good carries the force of being pumped in a neutral medium.
