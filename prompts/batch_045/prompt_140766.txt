My dad's oncologist gave us two options and basically said the evidence is thin and drawn from patients who don't look much like him. I made a spreadsheet to pin down numbers, and I kept typing things like 0.35–0.6 instead of a single number and couldn't make myself narrow them. Then I fell down a rabbit hole, and now I actually hold the view: when the evidence is sparse or the reference class is unclear, rationality *requires* your confidence to be a range — I think of it as a convex set of probability functions, with me permitted to act on any member — and this isn't sloppiness on my part, a single number would misrepresent what I know.

So: what would count against it? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and tell me which ones people who hold this view would themselves concede, not just what its critics say. Include the ones I half-know already: the Briar-score style argument that some single number near the middle of my range does better in expectation, the question of whether I can be money-pumped even if the classic Dutch book argument doesn't reach me, and the worry that my range is really just vagueness about which precise number is right and so should be modeled as a precise probability over probabilities.

Spend most of your time on my friend's argument, which I can't shake: if I'm allowed to act on any member of the set, then I'm allowed to pick one right now — say 0.6 — and just update it by ordinary conditioning from here on. But then my view is ordinary precise Bayesianism with extra steps; and if that isn't allowed, the set has to be doing real work, and I'm not sure I can say what. Is there a version that survives that fork, and what does it cost me? Also: does the view predict anything I could actually notice in my own forecasting record that the single-number view wouldn't — and if not, be honest about whether it's been qualified into something you can't argue with. The thing that would move me most is anything showing my range makes my dad's decision worse, or no different, than just picking a number.
