ok so my friend and I spent about three hours last night arguing about self-amending rules (started on a game of Nomic, ended up on actual constitutions) and I wrote down afterwards what I actually believe, and it looks fine to me, and he says at least two of these can't both be true. I genuinely can't tell which two he means, so please audit it properly and rank what you find instead of flagging all five as equally problematic.

1. No dead hand. A legal order cannot bind its own future. Whatever the officials of a later generation actually treat as the criteria of legal validity just is the criteria, so nothing enacted earlier can make a later change legally void. It can make that change a rupture, or the founding of a different system, but not invalid.

2. Eternity clauses do real legal work. If a system validly adopts a clause saying this clause may never be amended, then a later attempt to repeal it is legally void, not merely politically expensive. He says this flatly contradicts 1. I keep saying it doesn't: repealing it wouldn't be a failed exercise of the old power, it would be a new constitution. I want to know whether that reply actually costs me something, because I think it's free.

3. The trapdoor. Substantive entrenchment is worth nothing at the level of legal validity, because you almost never have to violate an entrenched clause — you amend the clause that does the entrenching, in one motion or in two, and then do the thing. Anything unamendable that isn't itself protected is exactly as good as the mutable/immutable distinction in the game: it holds until someone bothers.

4. Ratchets bind. A clause like no amendment may reduce the franchise below its current extent can make a later, procedurally flawless, near-unanimous amendment void. This is the one I care most about and the reason I got into the topic.

5. The bootstrap case. Amendments need two thirds. Amendment one, passed by two thirds: from now on amendments need three quarters. Amendment two, passed by two thirds but not three quarters: from now on amendments need two thirds. I think amendment two is valid. The power that raised the bar is the same power, and it can put the bar back at the level it was originally authorized to act at; a heightened threshold is only ever as strong as the threshold that created it.

He says 5 is what kills 4, since the ratchet was also passed at the old threshold and so can be taken out at the old threshold. I think 5 is only about the shape of the power and 4 is about the content of what it produces, so they're seperate. But 3 is definately in the neighbourhood of 4 too and I'm not sure how.
