I write a column on science and ideas, and for a piece I'm doing on the suffering-focused end of moral philosophy I keep geting stuck on the same fight: negative-leaning utilitarians who give pain some kind of priority over happiness, and symmetric total utilitarians who say a unit of suffering is just minus one unit of happiness and it's all one currency. I've now sat through a dozen versions of this exchange — conference panels, long blog back-and-forths, interviews — and nobody ever moves. They agree about how much suffering there is in the world, they agree it's bad, they even agree on most of what we should be funding this decade, and they still go at each other like it's 1970. So I want a diagnosis, not a verdict. Is it a verbal dispute about what "priority" and "bad" are doing; a real axiological difference, one cardinality claim against another; a hidden empirical question wearing a philosophical costume — whether affect is a single bipolar scale with a genuine zero point, or whether agony is a different psychological kind from enjoyment rather than the low end of one dimension; or is it only really live in the population-ethics cases, the asymmetry stuff about whether a vast world of mild contentment could outweigh a small world with torture in it, with everything nearer-term just temperament and emphasis? A source I trust says the whole thing collapses into one test he calls the exchange-rate question: is there any finite N at which you'd accept one unit of added suffering for N units of added happiness, and if you say no at every N you're a negative utilitarian whatever you call yourself. Is he right that it collapses like that, or does the test already smuggle in the thing at issue? And given whatever you diagnose, what would actually count as movement — a finding in affective science, some formal result about aggregation, a change of question — or should I just tell readers no progress is coming, and say why? Write it as straight prose please, no headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables, because I'm pasting chunks into an email to a source and formatting just gets in the way.
