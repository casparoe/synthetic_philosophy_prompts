I am writing a short piece on worst-case versus average-case guarantees in AI alignment, and I need a case built to order, because every example I can find runs in the same direction.

The familiar dialectic goes like this. A worst-case standard refuses deployment, since no bound can be certified for all inputs; an expected-value standard approves, since the average deployment goes fine; the worst-case side then accuses the other of ignoring tail risk, and the average-case side accuses it of paralysis by an unboundable tail. I want a case that reverses the direction: one in which a worst-case-guarantee standard licenses a deployment that an average-case standard forbids, where the average-case standard is understood as expected harm under the actual deployment distribution, compared against a stated counterfactual baseline.

Specifications.

1. Realism. One organization, one workflow, the scenario itself in a single paragraph. Present-day or near-term capability: an LLM-based agent with tool access is fine. No AGI, no existential stakes, no science fiction, no adversary more capable than an ordinary user typing something strange.

2. The worst-case standard must approve, and on a genuine guarantee. Make the guarantee structural, obtained by construction from the architecture of the deployment rather than from empirical evaluation, a vendor's assurance, or a claim about what the model would not do. The usual materials are an allowlisted action set, a machine-checked wrapper, sandboxing, absence of write authority over anything consequential, mandatory human final sign-off, and reversibility of every action the system can take on its own. State the guarantee explicitly, name the mechanism that secures it, and specify the class of inputs and circumstances over which it holds.

3. Non-vacuity. The system must be genuinely useful: under the actual input distribution it has to deliver expected benefits the organization would plausibly pay for. The average-case standard may not refuse on the ground that the guaranteed system is guaranteed worthless.

4. Composition of the refusal. The expected loss that drives refusal must be built entirely out of harms that the worst-case certificate itself counts as acceptable in any single instance. No low-probability catastrophe, no astronomically large stakes, no appeal to the claim that the deployment distribution cannot be known, no unknown unknowns: those are worst-case reasoning wearing a costume. The expected loss must be large because of the bulk of the distribution, either through the accumulation of many individually acceptable losses across volume or through an interaction effect that exists only at the level of the whole deployment and is invisible in any single case.

5. Derived, not stipulated. Both verdicts have to fall out of the facts you give me. Put rough numbers on the relevant rates, magnitudes and volumes: orders of magnitude are enough, but I want to see the arithmetic that flips the verdict, including the harm of the counterfactual baseline you are comparing against.

What I want after the scenario.

(a) The guarantee stated in a form I could audit, and the derivation of the worst-case approval from it.

(b) The expected-harm calculation, with the baseline made explicit, and the derivation of the average-case refusal.

(c) An identification of the single structural feature doing the work, and an argument that it, rather than some background assumption, is what separates the verdicts.

(d) An ablation: vary that feature, and tell me where the two standards reconverge, which way they reconverge, and whether the reconvergence is a genuine agreement between the standards or only the disappearance of the problem.

(e) The strongest available reply from someone who holds that worst-case guarantees are the right currency for deployment decisions, together with your judgment about whether the reply rescues the case or only relocates it.

(f) One sentence on what the case does, and does not, establish about whether a worst-case guarantee is necessary or sufficient for a deployment decision.

If you think the specifications cannot be jointly satisfied, tell me which one has to bend and why. I would rather have that than a case that quietly stipulates the verdicts or smuggles in a doom tail.
