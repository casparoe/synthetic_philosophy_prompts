I want a number on something I keep going back and forth about, and I want it in pieces rather than as a single confident gesture.

Setup, all stipulated — I am not asking you to recall who proposed any of this. Consider a symmetric-bounce cosmology: a universe whose entropy has a global minimum at some time t₀, near a bounce, and rises monotonically as |t − t₀| grows, so that there are two long, structured branches, call them L and R, one on each side of t₀, each containing galaxies, records and observers. The fundamental dynamics is time-symmetric; the only candidate source of a direction is the entropy profile. (I do not require the branches to be exact mirror images. If your answer turns on how close to symmetric they are, say so.) Two features matter:

First, on the usual one-ended picture the thermodynamic asymmetry is explained by a low-entropy boundary condition at one temporal extremity — the Past Hypothesis, if you want the standard label — together with time-symmetric dynamics. In the symmetric-bounce cosmology the low-entropy condition sits in the middle. A defender will say this is a single boundary condition applied twice over: one stipulates that at t₀ the relevant statistical-independence assumption holds for both directions of inference at once, so the H-theorem style argument runs both ways from the minimum.

Second, near t₀ there are usable entropy gradients in both directions. So consider a straddling observer: a system close enough to the minimum to stand in the right kind of correlations with records laid down on the L side of t₀ and with records laid down on the R side.

The headline claim, call it C: the asymmetries we actually experience — memory, records, causation, and the fact that deliberation aims at one end of time rather than the other — are fully accounted for by the entropy gradient plus a low-entropy boundary condition, leaving no residual fact about temporal direction for any further posit to capture; and this remains true in the symmetric-bounce cosmology, from which it follows both that L-observers and R-observers are equally entitled to call their own direction of increasing entropy the future, and that a straddling observer would have genuine two-sided epistemic access rather than being an incoherent description. I will count C as vindicated if a derivation from the boundary condition plus the dynamics recovers all of those asymmetries, and as refuted if some asymmetry fails to follow even when the boundary condition is granted in full — for instance if straddling observers turn out to be impossible for reasons that are not themselves thermodynamic.

Give me P(C) as a single number, decomposed. At minimum I want numbers for:
