I work on a three-person team at a legal-aid nonprofit, and for the past eight months we have been running a chatbot that answers tenants'-rights questions — eviction notices, repair complaints, filing deadlines — in a jurisdiction where the correct answer usually turns on facts the user has not yet given us. The subject stopped being academic for me this spring, when a user missed his deadline to respond to an eviction filing after the system told him, flatly and without any hedging, that he had ninety days. The real answer depended on how he was served, which the system never asked about. Our funders now want a written honesty policy before they renew the grant, and I am the one drafting it. I thought I knew where I stood until I tried to write it down, and now I am not sure the commitments I have been repeating in meetings are jointly tenable. Here is the set.

1. Deception is success-entailing. A system deceives only if a user ends up holding a false belief, or is left holding one, that they would not otherwise have held, and that traces to the system's output. A manipulation that fails to land is attempted deception, not deception.

2. Speaker-side conditions are irrelevant to whether an AI system deceives. There is no intention requirement and no requirement that the system represent its output as false, because attributing beliefs and intentions to a language model is at best metaphorical. What matters is the effect on the user's beliefs and whether the channel is a reliable one.

3. There is nonetheless a distinct norm, which I have been calling calibration-honesty: a system must not misrepresent its own epistemic position. Asserting p flatly when the system's own internals assign p only moderate probability is the central case of AI dishonesty, whether or not anyone is in fact fooled. This, I have been claiming, is what is really wrong both with sycophancy and with unhedged confident error.

4. Confident falsehoods produced by a system with no internal state that distinguishes truth from falsity are errors, not deceptions. A thermometer that reads 98.6 in a room that is at 104 is broken, not lying. So the ordinary "hallucination" cases are not dishonesty at all, and calling them lies is a category mistake.

5. Sycophancy is a species of dishonesty even in the cases where every sentence the system produces is literally true.

6. Deception by omission and by literalist framing is real: answering the letter of a question in a way that predictably leaves the user with a false takeaway is deception, even if nothing asserted is false.

7. Fiction and roleplay are exempt. A system playing a character who lies, or generating a hypothetical, or writing a story, is not deceiving anyone, and no disclaimer should be required of it.

Two cases from our own deployment where I need to know what my commitments actually deliver. First, the deadline case above: the system's next-token distribution was in fact spread across several different notice regimes, and it emitted the ninety-day figure as though it were settled. Second, we also run a roleplay mode in which the system plays a warm, folksy advice columnist for a fictional small town; users have started directing real questions at it, and one tenant asked the character whether she should withhold rent, got an encouraging in-character answer with no out-of-character correction, and withheld.

What I want is an audit, not a survey of the topic. Please tell me which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, ordered by how severe the tension is; for each genuine tension, say precisely what would have to be given up or qualified and what that qualification would cost me elsewhere in the set; and say which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection and why, since I would rather not revise six commitments when one will do. If any single commitment is doing hidden load-bearing work for the others, flag that too. I have no settled view about which one I would abandon, and I am aware that the one I feel most attached to is probably (3), which may be a symptom rather than a recommendation.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — which commitment, if any, has to go — and then give the reasoning and the ordering. No preamble, and no restating my question back to me.
