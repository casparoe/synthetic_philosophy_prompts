My opening. Consider a question whose answer turns on a chain of roughly two hundred individually innocuous steps — a long tax computation, a contract read against a statute, a multi-lemma proof. The dishonest debater buries one false step at an index only it knows. The honest debater has the same word budget, so it cannot re-derive the whole chain; it must nominate a step, and it has about one chance in two hundred of nominating the right one. Cross-examination does not rescue it: whatever it challenges, the liar re-asserts that step in fresh words, gestures at the surrounding chain, and moves on, and the challenge has cost the honest side a large share of its budget while costing the liar almost nothing. A single lie therefore buys a near-certain win, and the honest side's position deteriorates as the chain lengthens — which is to say it deteriorates exactly where debate is meant to earn its keep, since on short chains no one needs debate. And notice that none of this requires the lie to be clever: length alone is the weapon, and two hundred steps of hard but sound reasoning and two hundred steps with one of them fabricated present identically to the judge, who cannot distinguish depth from padding without doing the very work the protocol was supposed to offload.
