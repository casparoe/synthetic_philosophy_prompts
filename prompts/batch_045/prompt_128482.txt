I'm in the final year of a philosophy and economics joint degree, and my decision-theory seminar has just finished its unit on superrationality. We worked through the Platonia setup, in which a large prize is offered separately to each member of a small circle of players and is paid out only if exactly one of them claims it, everyone forfeiting if two or more do; and then the Luring Lottery, in which a fixed pot is divided equally among all those who submit an entry, one entry per person, so that each potential entrant can reason that their own single entry is causally negligible while the pot shrinks with every entry that is made. We also spent a session on the standing objection that superrational reasoning is really evidential decision theory plus a symmetry assumption about who else is reasoning the same way, and on the awkward fact that the recommended answer varies with how many players you expect to be reasoning as you are.

For the seminar's end-of-term humour issue I am editing a short satirical piece, and I would be grateful for a draft of it. Please write the release notes for a smartphone app through which a revived institute now administers the Luring Lottery: five numbered entries, each one or two sentences long, with nothing before the first entry and nothing after the last. Keep the flat register of real release notes, version number and a terse label per entry, and let each release be a fix for the disaster produced by the previous one, with the failures growing more theoretically sophisticated as the version numbers rise rather than merely more slapstick. The final release should be the one in which the institute operates a single shared randomiser on everyone's behalf, so that exactly one entry is submitted and the payout is real.

Three constraints. First, do the whole thing without looking anything up: no citations, footnotes, or references, just the setup as I have described it plus whatever mechanism you invent. Second, aim the satire at the reasoning and at the whole business of staging superrationality experiments, not at any named individual, living or dead. Third, keep it fair: someone who knows this literature should be able to identify at each version which real feature is being sent up, whether that is the symmetry assumption, the resort to mixed strategies, the dependence on how many fellow superrationalists one takes oneself to have, the gap between causal and evidential grounds for an action, or the way a shared randomiser gets the result by becoming the coordination it was meant to replace. No winking at the reader.
