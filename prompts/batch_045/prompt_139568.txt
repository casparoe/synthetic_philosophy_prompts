Write a short story, dialogue-heavy is fine, about a committee deciding whether to add a new diagnosis to the next edition of the psychiatric manual. The proposed category is "volitional attenuation disorder": people who, after a year or more of delegating everyday decisions to AI assistants, report that their sense of having chosen something has gone thin, they still act fine, but actions stop feeling like they originated with them. Nobody on the committee can tell whether this is a genuine new condition, a rebranding of something much older, or just what everyone feels now.

The story should show the feedback loop in action: once the label leaks, patients start using it to organise vague discomfort, an online community forms with its own vocabulary and its own unwritten rules about which experiences count as real symptoms, clinicians start recognising it faster and more confidently, and the rising diagnosis rate then gets cited back as evidence the condition was there all along. Have one character argue that this loop is exactly the reason to reject the category, and another argue that looping is just how human kinds work and disqualifies nothing. Maybe a third who doesn't care about any of that and just wants people to get accommodations covered.

Don't tie it up with a tidy moral. I want the reader left with the vertigo of not being able to tell whether the diagnosis found the thing or made it.
