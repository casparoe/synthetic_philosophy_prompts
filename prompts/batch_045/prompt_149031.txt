For the past year or so I have been having lucid dreams — dreams in which I realize, while still asleep, that I am dreaming, and in which I can sometimes steer what happens next. It began during a stretch of badly fragmented sleep, and it has become frequent enough that I have read a fair amount about it: practitioners train themselves to test their state by checking whether written text stays stable when they look away and back, or whether they can push a finger through the opposite palm. They also report the unnerving companion phenomenon of the false awakening, in which one seems to wake up in one's own bedroom, runs precisely those checks, finds nothing amiss, and only later discovers that one was dreaming the whole time.

I had long been attached to the butterfly episode in the Zhuangzi, in which Zhuangzi dreams he is a butterfly and, on waking, does not know whether he is Zhuangzi who dreamt he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming he is Zhuangzi. I had always read its force as resting on the claim that dreaming and waking are indistinguishable from the inside, so that the confidence with which I take myself to be awake could never by itself rule out the alternative. My own experience now seems to tell against that reading — and also, confusingly, for it.

Suppose Zhuangzi had known what we now know about lucid dreaming and false awakenings. Would he have thought that the capacity to recognize a dream as a dream from within it undercuts the skeptical use his story is usually put to? Or would he have taken the false awakening, in which even the test itself is dreamt, to show that the skeptical point survives every test we might devise? And what, on his view, would follow practically: would he have recommended cultivating lucidity as a way of waking up within the dream, or would he have regarded the very question of which state one is really in as a confusion better dissolved than answered? I would be grateful for an answer that reasons from what the story is actually doing, rather than from the standard summaries of it.
