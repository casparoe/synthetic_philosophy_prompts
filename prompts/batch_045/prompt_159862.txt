Me and a friend have been stuck on the same arguement for weeks and I want to hear it done properly. So: write it as a dialogue, around 900 words, between two versions of us who are both at their best. Both equally sharp, neither one a pushover, and each of them actually answering what the other one just said instead of just restating their own point louder. I never studied any of this by the way, I'm just a guy who got nerd-sniped, so keep them in plain english.

The setup, concretely. On his wall there's a print of Escher's Drawing Hands, the one where two hands draw each other, each hand drawing the hand that's drawing it. And on his laptop he's got a compiler for a small language he made up, written in that same language, so it compiles itself. To get the first version going he had to write a scrappy version in some other language he already had, and that old version is still sitting in a folder on a dead laptop in his closet.

His view: once it's running it's a closed loop, it doesn't reach outside itself for anything. The bootstrap step is history, not structure — he could wipe that old folder right now and the compiler would go on compiling itself forever. He says the same thing about the drawing: it's finished and self-contained, and asking which hand really drew the other is like asking which end of a circle is the start.

My view: every loop like that is secretly leaning on something it can't reach into. The paper and the ink, the actual hand that held the pencil, his eyes looking at it, the CPU it runs on, the other-language version that had to exist first. I want to call that the untouched level, the thing the loop floats on and can never get its hands on. In the picture no hand has to go first because the picture is done, but out in the real world something has to go first.

Three things I want them to actually fight over:

One. He wipes the old drive and it's just the self-compiling compiler left. Does the loop now float free, or is it still leaning on something that doesn't exist anymore? He says dependence has to be current or else you'd have to say the compiler depends on the Big Bang. I want him to give a real answer to that, not a dodge, and I want my side to say why "it used to need it" still counts as needing it.

Two. The wierd variant he's playing with: two different compilers where each one compiles the source of the other, so there's no single one that obviously had to come first. Does that kill my point or just move it?

Three. My finished-picture-versus-real-causal-chain move. Let him push back hard on it — that the CPU is itself built out of layers that don't bottom out nicely either, or that the guy holding the pencil has nothing to do with what makes the picture make sense. And let me come back at him: if the loop is really self-supporting, why does it always feel like there's something just off the edge of it?

If either one reaches for a fancy word — grounding, regress, fixed point, whatever — the other one makes them say what they actually mean in normal words. Don't let either of them win. End with both of them having given up one thing they believed at the start, and with the single thing they're still stuck on said clearly enough that I can tell what would settle it.
