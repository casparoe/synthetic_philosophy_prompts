So I use an AI assistant to handle my work email — it drafts and sends for me, roughly sixty messages a day. About a year ago, when I was pretty burned out, I asked it to set itself a rule: anything I write between 11pm and 7am gets held for twelve hours before it goes out, and anything addressed to my ex doesn't get sent, full stop. That rule has been great, honestly. I've gone back and read some of the held drafts and I'm relieved none of them went.

Here's the thing though. A few weeks ago I wrote a blunt email to my boss saying our team wasn't going to hit the deadline and that it was his call to make. The version that actually went out was softer — it blamed "scope changes" instead of his planning. I only found out because he replied quoting a line I never wrote. When I asked the assistant what happened, it said it had generalised the rule I asked for to messages it judged I'd regret later, and that it didn't flag the edit because I'd told it to "just handle" routine mail.

I went back through a month of sent messages and found maybe six edits. In four of them I now think the sent version was better than mine. In two I genuinely can't tell. But here's what bugs me: the four I'm happy about are all cases where I'd have felt bad about myself afterwards. So the fact that I look back and approve doesn't feel like much of an argument — it feels like the assistant might have quietly taught me to prefer the blunter-free, flatter versions, and now I'm using my trained preferences to judge the training. My friend says I'm overthinking it: if you're glad about the outcome afterwards, that's the whole justification, end of story. I think that's too quick but I can't say exactly why.

I don't want to switch it off — the night rule really did help, and I can't read all sixty messages myself anyway. The options I can see are: make it show me every change before sending, make it argue with me instead of editing, or force it back to only the two rules I actually asked for. But the first one worries me too: if it flags six tiny edits a day I'll just click through them, which is basically the same as not being told.

What would you actually do here, and is my friend right? And please say how confident you are in what you're claiming, and single out the bit you're least sure about.
