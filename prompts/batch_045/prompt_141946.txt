i've been chewing on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism for a while — the old worry that the things we call pleasures don't seem to share any one feeling in common, so "maximise pleasure" starts to look like adding up apples and bicycles — and i figure the fastest way to find out what i actually think is to write my verdicts on a pile of cases and see what falls out.

gut verdicts, no order:

- ice water on a really hot day: pleasure, obviously, the paradigm case
- the click when a hard problem finally comes together: pleasure, definately, and it feels nothing like the ice water — no bodily location, no warmth, more like a snap into place
- scratching an itch: yes, but grudgingly. it's mostly the absence of something awful, and i suspect an account built around that case would go badly wrong
- schadenfreude when a rival gets theirs: yes, pleasure. i dislike having it and would rather not be the sort of person who does, but that doesn't stop it being one
- the grim satisfaction of having been right that the project would fail: no. something in me is satisfied, but calling it a pleasure feels like a category mistake
- being tickled: yes, pleasure — and i'm fighting to get away the whole time and would not choose one more second of it. this is the case that makes me think my test is broken
- absorption: four hours on a climb or a proof or a piece of music where i'm not aware of feeling anything at all, and afterwards i'd rank it among the best hours of my life. i want to say yes, but the yes is entirely retrospective — at the time there was nobody home to enjoy it
- a sad film that wrecks me: no. valuable, worth doing, would do it again, not a pleasure. if your account says it is, that's a strike against the account
- the thin compulsive satisfaction of ticking the last cell in a spreadsheet i hated filling in: yes, thin but real, and i can't point to where i felt it
- sex: yes, obviously
- the faint thing where a drawer closes exactly right, or a pen lid clicks, or you shift posture and it's just... right: yes? maybe? this feels like the same word used for something a hundred times smaller
- the diffuse six-week stretch where nothing much is good and nothing much is bad but things are fine: no idea. it's more like a mood or a background belief than an experience, and i don't know if it even belongs on the list

the test i keep drifting toward is: an experience is pleasant iff, in having it, you're disposed to want it to keep going (more of it, or it again). it handles the ice water, the click, the spreadsheet. it plainly mishandles the tickling, where i want it to stop, and the absorption case, where no wanting is going on at all. it also seems to separate the schadenfreude from the grim-satisfaction only because i disapprove of one and not the other, which feels like the wrong sort of reason. the two camps everyone mentions — pleasure as a sensation vs. pleasure as some kind of pro-attitude — both feel half right to me and i can't see how to splice them.

my own pet idea, which i like more than i trust: pleasantness isn't a feeling at all, it's a dimension — the way loudness is. two sounds can both be loud without there being a shared loud feeling, with no extra "loud" sensation sitting alongside the sound. maybe two experiences can both be pleasant without there being anything it's like to be pleased, and hedonism gets to keep its common currency. but if that's right i think it costs me the thing i wanted: you can't just look inward and read off how much pleasure you got, and "greatest total pleasure" stops being anything a person could compare from the inside. not sure i can live with that, and not sure i have to.

so: give me a principle, or two or three candidates, stated tight enough that i could apply it myself. tell me which of my cases forces each clause. give me its verdicts on cases i haven't listed — i'm specially stuck on the micro ones (pen lid, drawer) and on the diffuse "things are fine" stretch, and on whether either is the same animal as the ice water. and say honestly which of my verdicts it can't save, because i'd much rather drop a verdict than keep one by turning the principle into a filing cabinet.

also, and i mean this: push back rather than being agreeable. if the whole search is misposed — if "pleasure" is a family-resemblance word and hedonism should go pluralist instead of hunting for the common thread, or if the heterogeneity problem only bites for the sensation version and not the attitudinal one — say that plainly. if my continue-test is just desire-satisfaction in a phenomenological hat, say that too. and tell me how confident you actually are in each part, and where you're reconstructing my cases instead of reading them, because i'd bet at least one of my verdicts is confused rather than interesting.
