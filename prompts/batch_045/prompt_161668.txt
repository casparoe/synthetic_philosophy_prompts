I am writing a handout for a graduate seminar in philosophy of physics. The students have met the hole argument once, in its standard continuum presentation: a generally covariant field theory, an initial-value formulation, a region of spacetime left untouched by the data, a smooth map that is the identity outside that region and non-trivial inside it, and the resulting pair of models that agree everywhere outside the hole and disagree inside it. The usual lesson drawn is that a certain kind of realist about the points of the manifold is committed to a failure of determinism, and the usual replies are on the table: that the two models represent the same physical situation, that the difference is not an observable difference, and that a suitably sophisticated realist can accept both models as possibilities while denying that they are distinct possibilities.

The dialectical point I want the new case to serve — which fixes what counts as cheating — is this: I want to argue that the argument really turns on the conjunction of (a) a realist commitment to the elements of a theory's base structure, understood as a commitment to there being a fact of the matter about which element plays which role over and above the structure as a whole, with (b) the presence of a non-trivial symmetry of that structure that can be localized to a hole. On this reading, general covariance, the diffeomorphism group, and the absence of background structure are inessential. So build me a case that generates that conclusion from the materials rather than stipulating it, and build it so that the standard moral cannot simply be read off the standard presentation.

Specifications for the case:

1. Discrete and self-contained. No manifolds, no diffeomorphisms, no differential equations. Specify a small finite structure (a lattice or graph), a fixed non-dynamical background structure on it, a dynamical field, and a local deterministic evolution rule with a well-posed initial-value problem. A student should be able to verify by hand both that the rule is deterministic and that the two solutions you produce really are solutions.

2. The theory must have the kind of fixed background structure that is standardly said to stop the hole argument from getting off the ground, on the grounds that a symmetry must preserve the background and a generic one will not.

3. The hole argument must nonetheless run. Choose the background structure so that it is invariant under a non-trivial automorphism of the structure that is the identity outside some region and non-trivial inside it, so that the automorphism maps solutions to solutions despite the background being fixed.

4. One paragraph for the case itself, followed by a short numbered list of checks: that the automorphism preserves the background; that it preserves the evolution rule; that it fixes the initial data; that the two solutions agree outside the region and differ inside it; and that the second solution is not simply identical to the first. Keep the structure small enough to check but large enough that the hole is a proper part of it.

5. No primitive haecceities, no primitive thisness, no hand-waving about identity: state the realist commitment that gets into trouble as a claim about what the two models represent, and state the automorphism's effect in the vocabulary of the model rather than in a separate metaphysics.

After the case, give me:

- What each of three positions says about it and why: the element-realist; the theorist who says that symmetry-related models represent one and the same physical situation; and the sophisticated element-realist who allows both models as possibilities but denies that they are distinct possibilities. Note that the two models agree on every symmetry-invariant quantity, and say what each position makes of that. If one of the three fares notably better or worse here than in the continuum case, say which and why.

- The single feature of the construction doing the work, and exactly what fails when it is removed.

- The strongest objection available to whichever position comes out worst, phrased as a real defender of that position would put it, together with a note on where your reply to it would be softest.

- An explicit list of the features of the continuum hole argument your toy model does not reproduce, and whether any of them is doing hidden work in your case.

Finally, calibrate the epistemics: separate what is settled once the toy theory is fixed — the claims that are mere checkable facts about the model — from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this topic, including whether the element-realist is really committed to indeterminism here, whether the same-situation reply is an answer or a concession, whether the sophisticated move is coherent, and whether a discrete toy with a finite symmetry group is a fair proxy for the continuum case. Do not present any contested claim as the consensus view, and flag anywhere my framing of the dialectical point above presupposes something that is itself disputed.
