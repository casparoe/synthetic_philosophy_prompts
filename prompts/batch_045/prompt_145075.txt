I'm on the committee of my uni philosophy society and we put out a genuinely terrible little zine at the end of term — half essays, half jokes — and this year I somehow volunteered to do the funny bit. I've been reading Berkeley's Principles this week, specifically the bit where he challenges you to conceive of the trees in the park existing unconceived and says you can't, because the moment you try you're conceiving them, so the thought defeats itself; and what keeps striking me is that if somebody really believed it, the everyday anxiety it would generate is enormous — the flat doesn't exist when you leave it, the kids don't exist when you're both asleep — and the standard move is to wheel in God as the perceiver who never blinks, which I guess works doctrinally but is cold comfort emotionally, and quietly makes the whole thing rest on something nobody can audit. My flatmate, who is doing the same module, says the solution is obvious and that I'm overthinking it, which is definately not helping.

So: write me an agony-aunt column, the sort of thing you'd get in a Saturday magazine, where the columnist (call her Iris, or whatever you like) is an absolutely committed immaterialist and answers three letters. Rough material, redo it however you want: (a) a night-shift security guard at a self-storage place who can't sleep because he's worried the contents of the units aren't there when nobody's looking, and wants to know if he should be worried; (b) a mother who's anxious that her children cease to exist in the hours when she and her husband are both asleep; (c) someone whose flatmate insists the pain of a burn is in the fire, and who wants ammunition for correcting him. The rules: Iris stays entirely in character and never says Berkeley, idealism, immaterialism or any -ism at all — she just gives the advice and the reader works out what she thinks — and every answer has to be one an actual immaterialist could endorse, so the humour comes out of the doctrine rather than out of Iris behaving oddly. That means the unconceivability point doing the work in (a), the permanent-perceiver move in (b), and in (c) not just the 'the pain isn't in the fire' correction but the further one that nothing material causes anything and the fire is only a sign that warns you. No 'if a tree falls in the forest', no dream-scepticism gags, no simulation stuff — I want someone who actually knows the period to read it and groan rather than roll their eyes. And at least one answer has to leave the reader colder than when they started; my own idea is that Iris's reassurance to the guard doesn't really remove his worry, it just relocates it onto a perceiver nobody can check up on, and she never quite registers that she's done it, but if you can find a better place to let the discomfort through, use yours.

Keep it deadpan throughout, no winking at the reader, no footnotes, no explaining the jokes afterwards. Hard limit: 550 words for the whole thing including the column's title and standfirst — put the word count in square brackets after the last line. I need it by the end of the fortnight so don't spend words telling me what you're going to do, just give me the column.
