I am a TA for a second-year metaphysics course, and I have one fifty-minute section to take students from the simple conditional analysis of dispositions to seeing why finks and masks are not two instances of a single problem. They have already met one fink case — a wire that is live but wired to a device that cuts the current at the instant it is touched — and they found it intuitive; they have not met masks. Do not explain the material to me; give me the plan for teaching it.

I want four things. First, the order the components must come in and what depends on what: in particular, what has to be in place before a mask can even look like a counterexample, and whether the reverse failure — a conditional that is true although the disposition is absent — belongs in this session at all or would only blur the distinction I am trying to build. Second, the one carrying case for masking, and what it must have and lack: I am drawn to the analgesic that blocks pain while leaving the sensitivity to pain intact, but I suspect students will defuse it by saying the subject is not really in the pain-disposing state, and I do not want to use protective packaging, since they will just say the glass was never really struck. Third, the misconceptions in the order they will form and the point in the sequence where each should be headed off: that finks and masks have the same structure and so call for the same repair; that a normal-conditions or ceteris paribus clause disposes of both; that if the disposition survives the mask then the manifestation must survive too, in some weakened form.

Fourth, tell me where to place a pair of cases I have constructed: a fragile mug coated in a polymer that stiffens on impact so the mug does not break, the stiffening lasting only as long as the stress does; and a variant in which the same impact also briefly anneals the ceramic, so that the brittleness itself is absent for a moment. Which belongs earlier, what each should force the students to notice, and whether the hybrid case is both, neither, or a reason to doubt the distinction. Finally, give me one question to put on the board at the end that would show me they have the distinction rather than the labels — including what a wrong-but-tempting answer would look like — and name one thing I should explicitly refuse to go into at this level.
