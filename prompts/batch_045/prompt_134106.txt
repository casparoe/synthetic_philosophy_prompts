Hey — I run a small reading group on constitutional theory and we keep coming back to this analogy: a constitution is like the rulebook of a game, and an amending clause is just the rule about how to change the rules. On that picture an "eternity clause" — the kind that says the amending procedure itself, or some core principle, can never be ammended, and that courts in a couple of countries have used to void amendments on the ground that they gutted the constitution's core — is like a page in the rulebook saying "this page can never be changed." The people in the group who like the analogy use it to argue that entrenchment is either incoherent, since whatever can change any page can change that one, or purely decorative, and that a court striking down a duly passed amendment is just making it up.

Can you audit it?
