Me and my brother-in-law got into a proper argument the other weekend about a wasps nest by the back door — he said leave them be, I said it's got to go — and it's sort of spiralled from there. I've ended up writing down what I actually think about which things matter morally and how much, and I'd like someone to check whether it holds together. Bit of context: I fit kitchens for a living, I've never read any philosophy, and I don't want book recommendations or dead philosophers name-dropped at me. Just argue with the list.

1. It's a sliding scale, not an on/off switch. A chimp is more conscious than a mouse, a mouse more than an ant, an ant more than a thermostat, and a thermostat isn't conscious at all.

2. What makes something count is whether it can feel things — pain, enjoyment, stuff like that. Not how clever it is, not whether it can talk or reason. Something very clever that can't feel anything isn't owed anything for its own sake.

3. Small amounts add up. A million insects each feeling a small amount of pain is worse than one pig in agony, because there are a million of them. I don't think there's a cut-off where the amount per creature gets too small to count anymore.

4. I kill insects without thinking about it and don't feel bad afterwards — wasps, flies, ants in the kitchen, spiders in the bath, the lot. I don't think that makes me a bad person.

5. It's worse to kill a chimp than a mouse, and worse to kill a mouse than an ant, because the chimp has more to lose — plans, relationships, things it was looking forward to. The ant loses basically nothing.

6. A thermostat has nothing at all going on inside it. So does a chess program, and so do the big AI language things everyone's using at the moment, clever as they sound. No inner life, nothing owed to them.

7. I'm not totally sure about that last one. Maybe there's a few percent chance some of that AI stuff has a tiny flicker of something. But at that level of doubt I don't think it changes anything we should actually do, and I don't think we owe them anything right now.

8. I'm fine with the law drawing its line wherever's convenient. I read a while back that over here they brought in a law that counts octopuses and lobsters as sentient but not insects, which seemed a bit random to me — I'd of thought bees were a better bet than lobsters — but I've no problem with drawing a line somewhere just so there is one.

9. Plants do nothing for me. No moral status at all, and I don't feel guilty about a lawn.

What I want is this: go through them and tell me which ones genuinely fight with each other and which only look like they do, and rank the real ones worst first. I don't want nine separate little worries all given equal weight — I want to know where the actual problem is. For each genuine clash, tell me what I'd have to give up, and whether there's a cheaper way out that keeps most of the rest. Then, if you could make one change to the whole set that cleans up the most, what would it be?

One thing I'll flag myself, because I can't tell if it's real: points 1 and 2 together make me feel a bit wierd. If the scale is smooth all the way down, then there must be things with a trillionth of a unit of feeling, and they'd have a trillionth of the moral status, and that just feels silly. But I can't say what's actually wrong with it. Is that a proper problem, or am I only being squeamish?
