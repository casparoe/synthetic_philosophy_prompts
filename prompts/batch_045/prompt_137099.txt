My roommate, who does phil and econ, has been running this at me for a week and I cant find the hole even though the conclusion is nuts.

(1) Real credences are imprecise: for most questions your evidence doesn't pick out a single number, so your credence state is a set of probability functions (a representor).
(2) Under imprecise credences an option is permissible iff it maximizes expected utility by the lights of at least one function in the representor — you can't be required to do something that's best on none of them, and you can't be forbidden from doing something that's best on one of them.
(3) Call a representor open-minded if, for every hypothesis you can state but can't rule out a priori, it contains some function giving that hypothesis positive probability. Mine is open-minded: I can't refute a god who infinitely rewards people who spend a decade flipping a coin.
(4) Heaven is infinite, so for any function that gives positive probability to a god who rewards action A infinitely, A's expected utility comes out infinitely good.
(5) So for every action open to me — finishing my thesis, joining a cult, flipping a coin for ten years — there's a function in my open-minded representor on which that action is infinitely good.
(6) So every action is permissible, no action is required, and Pascal's wager doesn't recommend theism at all. It just dissolves decision-making.
(7) And none of this needed the religious stuff: any decision problem with unbounded payoffs plus an open-minded representor has the same property, so my whole habit of treating a tiny chance of an astronomical payoff as swamping ordinary considerations (I work on x-risk stuff, it's basically my worldview) goes down with it.

I'm fairly sure something slides between (4) and (6) — either "maximizes" is doing work he's not entitled to, or "in my representor" is — but I can't put my finger on it, and he just keeps saying "yep, that's the problem with infinities," which feels like a way of not answering. I'd like to keep (2) and keep the thought that a one-in-a-billion shot at something enormous can matter; I'm willing to give up open-mindedness or give up heaven being literally infinite if that's the cheaper fix, but I want to know what I'm paying in each case.
