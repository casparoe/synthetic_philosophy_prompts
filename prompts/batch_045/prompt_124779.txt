I've been reading a bit about dispositions for fun (I'm a formulation chemist, so 'fragile', 'soluble', 'sedating' are the sorts of words I use all day), and I keep running into a pair of situations I cant find a name for.

First: a material that's brittle, but striking it triggers a self-healing response inside the material. The healing is slower than a crack is, so a sharp blow shatters it while a slow press doesn't, because the repair keeps up. Whether it counts as brittle seems to depend on how fast you test it, and there's no principled cut-off, just a race between two processes.

Second: a tablet that dissolves in water, where contact with water triggers a coating that seals the tablet shut except the sealing reaction heats the water, and the tablet dissolves in the hot water anyway. So you get the result you predicted, but not via the property the thing had before you dropped it in: the stimulus destroys the disposition and then produces the same end result by a different route.

I've seen the words 'fink' and 'mask' mentioned somewhere in this area, but as near as I can tell they cover cases where the test removes the property and so nothing happens, or where something extrinsic blocks the result. Neither is quite it: in my first case whether anything happens depends on timing, and in my second the outcome is exactly what you'd have predicted except it wasn't the disposition that produced it. Do these have established names? And if not, how would you carve them: what exactly separates them from finks, from masks, and from a plain fake (a non-soluble tablet that dissolves only because the water got hot), and is my first case really two cases, or just one case with a dial on it? I don't want a label, I want to know where the lines go.
