Hey — I need a deliverable for a reading group I run (6 people, two sessions, we read the doc out loud in the first 20 minutes and then argue). Two members have been going at it for over a year about whether pleasure and pain sit on one scale, and I want the adversarial-collaboration document they should have produced instead of the argument they actually had.

Side T holds a lexical threshold view: below some region, pleasures and pains trade off on a single scale, but there is a class of suffering no quantity of mild-to-decent positive experience can outweigh, however many people get the positive experiences. Side C says that's a confusion: everything is commensurable on one scale, and the felt pull of the threshold is really something else — uncertainty about how bad extreme pain is, risk aversion, the way intense experiences get compressed when we report them, whatever.

Some things to build in, please:

- Both sides stipulate total, impartial aggregation over experiences, no side-constraints, no appeal to rights or desert, and they're comparing outcomes rather than choosing policy under uncertainty. So the doc shouldn't waste lines on that.
- Please make Side T say whether the threshold is located in intensity, duration, or intensity-times-duration; whether it is sharp or a band; and whether the scale below it is interval (up to affine transform) or ratio (true zero). Make Side C say whether its single scale is unbounded above or has a finite maximum total per experience. I suspect some of the shouting has been about these sub-choices and not about lexicality at all.
- Invent a name for, and press on, what I'd call the bearer question: is the threshold a property of a momentary experience, of a whole life, or of a population-aggregate total? A threshold that attaches to moments behaves very differently from one that attaches to lives.
- Two empirical findings are on the table, both made up — just stipulate them, don't cite anything: (i) hysteresis, where the stated exchange rate between a fixed mild pleasure and a fixed pain depends on which one you're asked to give up first, and the gap widens as pain intensity climbs; (ii) ceiling compression, where retrospective ratings of intense pain regress toward the top of the available scale. Each side has to say what these would be evidence of on its own view.
- Include the case I keep returning to: astronomically many experiences each sitting at 99% of the threshold versus one experience just over it. Both sides state what follows, and whether it's a reductio or a bullet they'll bite.
- A third member proposes a two-ledger version where pleasures and pains are recorded separately and compared only by a coarse dominance rule. Have the two signatories say whether that's a genuinely third position or just one of theirs in disguise, and if disguised, which one.

Format: numbered agreed claims first — 8 to 12 of them, each non-vacuous enough that I could imagine someone denying it, including about what counts as evidence here. Then a disagreements table with a column for the wording Side T accepts and a column for the wording Side C accepts. Then a cruxes section: for each crux, the case or argument both accept as bearing on it, and each side's prediction written down in advance, plus what each side would concede if its prediction fails. Close with one paragraph on which parts of this are verbal, which are genuinely substantive, and which cruxes are undecidable even in principle and what that means for anyone trying to act on either view.

Adverserial doesn't mean one side gets strawmanned — I want text both could sign, and if you think they can't get to a joint statement at some point, say exactly where and why instead of papering over it. Sorry if this is over-specified, I'd rather have too much structure than a mushy both-sides thing.
