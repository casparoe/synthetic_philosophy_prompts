I teach a first-year graduate seminar and I am writing a taxonomy handout on Nozick's utility-monster example. Every version of this I can find simply lines up five theories side by side; I want a tree built from the logical space instead, and I want it to separate, for my students, objections to aggregation as such from objections to the concentration of a claim in a single being.

Structure it as a decision tree with one branching question per node and eight to twelve leaves. Each node should be phrased as a claim or a case a student could answer without already knowing which position it leads to; no node may ask them which theorist they side with. Start from a commitment nearly everyone in the room already accepts, not from the definition of utilitarianism.

The work should be done by cases rather than labels. Use at least these: the standard monster whose gains dwarf everyone else's losses; the reluctant monster, who has the monster's utility function, hates having it, and wants to be cured of it at a large net cost to aggregate utility; the reverse monster, whose capacity for suffering is out of all proportion to anyone's capacity for enjoyment; the swarm, in which no individual claim dominates but ten million slightly more efficient converters collectively do; and the archival monster, a merely possible being we could bring into existence or decline to.

Leaves that must appear: total utilitarianism biting the bullet; prioritarianism; negative or suffering-focused views; average utilitarianism; sufficientarian and lexical-threshold views; Nozickian side-constraints; Harsanyi-style aggregation, on which a social welfare function meeting the expected-utility and Pareto-indifference conditions must be a weighted sum of individual utilities; rule-consequentialist self-effacement; and the empirical dismissal, on which no such psychology is nomologically possible.

Close by marking the combinations your branching questions permit but that nobody occupies, with a one-line diagnosis of each — incoherent, unattractive, or merely unclaimed — and by flagging any pair of leaves the tree cannot actually separate, together with the further case that would separate them if one could be constructed.

Lead with a single sentence naming the branching question that does the most diagnostic work, then give the tree: no preface, and no restatement of what I asked for.
