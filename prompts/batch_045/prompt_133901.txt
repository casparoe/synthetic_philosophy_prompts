hey so i run this tiny podcast for my school's philosophy club (its called *Second Guess*, we get maybe 40 listeners an episode lol) and next week we've got Dr. June Halloran on. She wrote a book arguing that in that old dialogue — the one where Einstein's brain is kept alive in a vat wired up to a TV screen, and by the end the brain is basically claiming that the book the reader is holding is the thing that's conscious (i'm pretty sure its in The Mind's I, early 80s) — the book really is conscious, and not just as a cute metaphor.

Her positive claim is what she calls the uptake criterion: a physical object is a subject during the stretches when its states get taken up by a system of the right kind in a way that produces a point of view whose contents are about those very states. So the book qualifies while somebody's reading it, because the reader's conscious stream is partly *about* the book's own sentences, including the sentences where the brain talks about itself. She bites a couple of bullets on purpose: the book is only a subject while it's being read (she calls these intermittent subjects, and says continuity is a scheduling detail rather than a metaphysical requirement), and when two people read the same copy at the same time that's one subject with two channels, not two subjects. She also insists she isn't a panpsychist, because a subway map or a phone book doesn't loop back on its own uptake.

Can you draft me eight to ten questions for a 45 minute episode, in plain language my friends will follow, but not softballs? Specifically:

- Something on the recording-versus-instrument objection: the book looks like a trace left behind by the brain, not a mind of its own. She always bats this away with some version of a score isn't a footprint, so I want a question where that answer starts to strain (a player piano roll? a film? a transcript nobody ever reads?).
- One that presses the flicker: if the subject pops into existence at page 1 and out again when I put it down, is that one life with gaps or a new subject every time I pick it up, and why should I care about either.
- One on the two-readers case, with a follow-up for her one subject, two channels answer — what happens when the two readers get to different pages, or one of them is reading badly.
- A question built around a book that isn't self-referential, like a printed quine (a program that prints its own source) or a manual that only talks about itself. I honestly think the whole argument only sounds deep because of the self reference, and I want to make her say whether content is doing the work here.
- Her symmetry move: she likes to say the brain in the vat is only conscious because somebody's watching the screen, so you're a book too. I want that pushed — if her own answer is that a brain reads itself, why can't a book be arranged to be its own reader, and if it can't, what exactly is missing.
- The proper part worry: is the book conscious, or is the reader-plus-book system conscious with the book just along for the ride.
- A closer on what would actually count against her: I want a concrete case, not what evidence in principle, maybe something like a reader whose conscious experience is suppressed while their eyes still track the page — and then a follow-up to whatever she's most likely to say about it.

Order them so the deflections get harder to reach for as the episode goes on, and put the likeliest version of her answer under each question so I know where to jump in.
