I have just joined the analytics team of a famine early-warning system, and an argument in our office has become a practical problem: one colleague treats famine as, at bottom, a supply event — the food available to a population falls below what it needs, and politics matters only by affecting how much there is and whether it moves — while another treats it as a failure of access in which identifiable agents had the means to avert mass death and did not, so that calling it a natural disaster is a misdescription of what happened. It is not an academic matter for us: which of them is closer to right decides what variables the system tracks and which alerts it issues.

Please draft the joint statement the two of them should have produced after a good-faith adversarial collaboration: a numbered section of claims both could actually sign, followed by a table setting out where they still differ, each row phrased in wording its own side would accept.

I have views about where the cruxes lie; use them if they hold up and replace them if they do not.

1. Slack: whether, in a famine year, there remained within the affected region any surplus that could realistically have been mobilized — I would like this stated as a ratio of the shortfall to what was in fact removed by procurement, export, or taxation — together with the question of whether anyone could have measured it at the time.
2. Continued outflows: both camps agree that grain leaving a famine region bears on the dispute; they disagree about whether it shows indifference or an ordinary market response that later brought relief in.
3. The mortality gradient: whether deaths concentrate by occupation and class or by agro-ecological zone, and how far migration confounds the reading.
4. How much of the crime claim is causal, how much moral, how much legal, and how much merely verbal.

For each crux, give the case or the quantity both sides would accept as bearing on it (Bengal in 1943, Ireland in the 1840s, and China during the Great Leap Forward are the obvious ones, but use better ones if they exist), and record in advance what each camp predicts it will show. Say which cruxes the surviving data cannot settle and what would settle them. Please write the answer in British English.
