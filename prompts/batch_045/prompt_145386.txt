ok so context: I'm a parent-governor at a big comprehensive (11–16, about 900 kids) and I've been reading a bit of Foucault on discipline and power/knowledge on the side, purely for my own interest, and I now cannot look at our behaviour system straight. I've got to say something about it at the next governors' meeting and I'd rather say something with a spine to it than just "it feels a bit surveillant". So here's what I want: help me reconstruct what the system actually presupposes, philosophically, about power and about the child.

How it works: every teacher has an app on their phone and at any point in a lesson they can tag any pupil with one of maybe a dozen labels — on task, helped someone, good homework, but also off task, disrupted learning, late. Each tap pings the parents immediately. The points total only ever goes up; there are no deductions at all, so a bad week just means fewer taps rather than a lower number, and the total is never reset — not at half term, not at the end of the year — so by Year 11 a kid has one number that is basically the accumulated sum of five years of being looked at. The top twenty totals go on a screen in the main corridor, updated live. The bit I keep coming back to: the school is adamant this is not a sanctions system. Nobody gets a detention for a low score, nothing is taken away, and it's presented to parents as "information" that helps children learn to "self-regulate". Meanwhile the head tells us it has "completely changed the culture", teachers say the kids are visibly different when the corridor screen is switched on, and we publish the top twenty but not the bottom twenty — which everyone involved says is the kind version, except that everyone can work out who's missing.

So the questions. What view of power does a setup like this only make sense under — does it need the productive/creative picture rather than the repressive one, or would a repressive picture explain it too? Does it secretly depend on the idea that the knowledge it generates is neutral description rather than something that makes the thing it describes? Is it even coherent for it to present itself as merely informational when the whole design is about visibility, or is that claim itself part of how it works? And the one I genuinely can't settle: does the fact that the number only rises make it more disciplinary or less? On one hand there's no punishment anywhere in it, on the other it's a record from which nothing can ever be removed. Also useful: what should I distinguish between what the practice strictly presupposes and what it merely sits comfortably with, and where is it underdetermined in the sense that several quite different theories of power would all license it? And concretely — if we kept all the logging but dropped the public ranking, or kept the ranking but stopped logging individual lesson-by-lesson acts, which of those actually cuts against the implicit theory and which is cosmetic? My own kid is in Year 9 and refuses to look at the screen and I can't tell if that's resistance or just indifference. Please write the answer in British English, and don't give me readings or footnotes — I want the reconstruction, not a reading list.
