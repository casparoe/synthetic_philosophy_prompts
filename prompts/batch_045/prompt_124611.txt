The thesis, which is chapter three of my dissertation. Call it the Symmetric Exposure Rule. Given a general-purpose AI system and a set of candidate value profiles it might be trained to satisfy, we proceed in four steps. First, elicit from each affected party a value profile, understood as a ranking over the candidate profiles the system might instantiate. Second, run each elicited profile through what I call the reflection test: a profile is discounted to the extent that it does not survive being presented with accurate information about the alternatives and about its own causal genealogy, which is meant to screen out adaptive preferences, internalized oppression, and straightforward factual error, and to screen in nothing else. Third, assign each surviving profile a weight equal to that party's expected exposure to the system's outputs over the system's whole lifetime, counting future and not-yet-existing users at their projected population size; exposure, not citizenship or headcount, is what makes you subject to the system. Fourth, instead of taking a weighted average, select the candidate profile that minimizes the maximum stake-weighted value distance, so that a numerous group that is only mildly alienated cannot outweigh a small group that would be catastrophically alienated. My argument in outline: headcount and majority-style aggregation have no principled answer to who counts, are hostage to adaptive preferences, and are silent about the fact that most of the affected parties do not yet exist; welfare-style averaging presupposes interpersonally comparable cardinal value functions that the elicitation cannot deliver; liberal neutrality is the right constraint but is standardly stated as a constraint on the state, whereas the neutrality that matters here is neutrality about which comprehensive doctrine gets implemented, not neutrality about how a doctrine was arrived at, and the reflection test is neutral in exactly that sense; stake rather than headcount follows from the fact that exposure is what makes one subject to the system; the minimax step is needed because averaging permits aggregation across persons that no individual would accept; and carrying future parties at projected size follows from the very same stake principle, even though it heavily dilutes the values of currently living people, which I argue is a feature and not a bug.
