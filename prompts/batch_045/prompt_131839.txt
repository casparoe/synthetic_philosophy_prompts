I want to throw a spanner in, though. Consider the *resilience view*: you are unfree to φ to the extent that whether you get to φ depends on the will of another across nearby counterfactuals in which that person's preferences shift — so it isn't standing power that matters, but how fast your option set degrades as someone else's will moves. My hunch is that this cuts across their dispute rather then falling on either side, and that forcing both of them to locate themselves relative to it exposes cruxes the standard examples cover up.
