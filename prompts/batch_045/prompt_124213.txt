I sit on the standing subcommittee of my professional association's ethics committee, which rules on whether a member has standing to press a censure motion against another member for a violation of our research-conduct norms. I'm the junior member, I've been asked to draft the ruling for next week, and I'd like to understand what we are actually already committed to, because our precedents read to me as though they were decided one at a time and I can't tell which principle connects them. Our rulings so far: Reyes, who had himself cropped an image misleadingly two years earlier, never disclosed it, and was never caught, had no standing to censure a colleague for the same; Okonkwo, who had done the same thing, reported herself, served a three-month suspension, and has since kept scrupulous records, had standing; Lindqvist, who did the same thing in 2004, before the association had any rule on image manipulation, had standing; Mbeki, who has never done it and has never worked with that kind of imaging data at all, had standing; Harker, who had been found to have squeezed a junior collaborator out of a project, had standing to censure a colleague for plagiarism, on the ground that the two norms are unrelated; Sorensen, who did the same thing years ago, disclosed it herself in a departmental talk without being asked, was never sanctioned, and says she would accept a sanction if we reopened it, had standing; and Duvalier, who engages in the practice openly, says he does not accept the norm, and nevertheless censures others for it, had no standing.

The new case is Adeyemi. Some years ago, as a lab head, she instructed a junior technician to drop an outlier from a data set without recording it, which is the very practice she now wants to censure in a colleague. She never did it with her own hands; the technician, who could not reasonably have refused, carried it out on her instruction and was never blamed for it. She has never disclosed the episode, there is no realistic prospect of her being sanctioned for it now, and the limitation period in our own disciplinary process has run. But she abandoned the practice years ago, she now keeps meticulous records, she says it was wrong and that she would not do it again, and she is willing to say all of this publicly, though not so far to invite a sanction on herself. Some of us think the derivative character of the dirt makes it worse rather than better: she used someone with no real option to refuse, and the violation is hers even though it isn't in her hand.

I've read a little of the philosophical literature on hypocrisy and standing to blame and I don't want a tour of it. What I want is this: which rules fit all seven precedents, what each of those rules predicts about Adeyemi, and which one you'd bet the subcommittee is implicitly running, with your reasons for preferring it. If our precedents genuinely underdetermine the case, say plainly what principle we would be adopting with each of the available choices, and tell me what we should rule if we want to be consistent with ourselves. I'd also like you to say which precedent is the closest analog and why; whether it matters that her violation was procured rather than performed, and whether it matters that the person who performed it was not blameworthy; whether standing to bring an official censure motion could come apart from standing to blame the person privately; whether this is the case that should push us off our all-or-nothing yes-or-no verdicts and toward something graded, such as standing to blame without standing to censure, or censure issued with a note about diminished moral authority; and what further case would best separate the rules that agree on Adeyemi. I've been toying with a member who is currently violating the norm, admits it, is trying to stop, and accepts that the norm applies to her — would that be the useful probe, or is there a better one? Please write your answer as continuous prose, the way you would write an email to a colleague: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, and keep it under about 800 words.
