I am the honorary secretary of a national voluntary federation — a membership body of roughly four thousand individuals and affiliated branches, governed by a set of Rules running to some forty clauses. Clause 38 is our amendment rule: the Rules may be amended by a two-thirds majority at a general meeting, provided six weeks' notice has been given. Clause 38 says nothing about its own amendment, so on its face the rule that governs amendment can itself be amended by the ordinary amending procedure.

Over the past year a few members have noticed this, and four motions have been tabled for the annual general meeting in six weeks. My committee must circulate a report with a recommendation on each motion three weeks from now, and I have to draft it. The federation has no paid staff and no standing legal adviser beyond a solicitor we instruct occasionally; her summary, which you should take as given rather than relitigate, is that the Rules are a contract among the members, that a majority acting by the required procedure can in principle change any clause including an entrenchment (because it can always repeal the entrenchment first), but that a clause purporting to restrict amendment may still be given effect as a rule about what counts as a validly made amendment, so that a purported change made without following it can be challenged as ineffective. She added that she would not expect any drafting of ours to bind a determined future majority indefinitely, and that in practice what such clauses do is shape what has to be argued for and in how many steps.

The motions:

A. Add to our membership-and-equal-vote clause the sentence "This clause may not be amended or repealed."

B. Amend Clause 38 so that Clause 38 itself may be amended only by a two-thirds majority at each of two consecutive general meetings, all other clauses remaining amendable at a single meeting.

C. Add a continuity condition: no amendment may repeal the amendment rule, or leave the Federation with no rule under which the Rules can be amended.

D. Add a restatement power: the Committee may from time to time issue an authoritative restatement of the Rules which shall have effect as the Rules, and a restatement is not an amendment. The proposer notes that we already publish consolidated versions after each AGM and that several clauses are, in his word, ripe for modernisation; he argues that supporters of A need D, since without it entrenchment is empty.

My committee is split roughly evenly between those who think A is worthless window dressing and those who think it is cheap and can do no harm. I suspect both camps are asking the wrong question, and I want the report to ask the right one.

Please draft the committee's report as a memo I could put in front of the meeting: a recommendation on each motion — approve, reject, or approve in a redrafted form — with the reasoning a member could actually respond to, including an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the side you are rejecting. Where you think none of the four does the job, draft the clause you would put in its place. In the course of that, I want you to sort out what these devices can and cannot do: whether entrenchment achieves anything beyond making a change take two visible steps rather than one; whether a limit on the amending power can be justified as a limit on a delegated power, so that some changes — dissolving the federation, converting it into a company, handing amendment to a three-person committee — are not amendments of the Rules at all but something else, and what our Rules should say about that case since they currently say nothing; whether the continuity condition in C is simply the mirror image of A and equally empty, given that locking in amendability is itself a substantive choice in favour of the status quo; and whether D, whatever its drafting, lets the Committee reach Clause 38 itself, and what — if anything — distinguishes a restatement from an amendment once a body has the power to declare which is which. I would also like you to consider, and if you favour it to draft, an alternative to entrenchment that works by an asymmetric default rather than a prohibition: the protected clause can be changed, but only by a heavier and more deliberate procedure than an ordinary amendment, perhaps with a periodic automatic reconsideration so that protection has to be renewed rather than defended.

Please do not cite cases, statutes or literature; we have no law library and I need reasoning I can defend from the floor of the meeting, not authorities I cannot check. Close the memo with a short note on how confident you are in each recommendation, marking the points where your reasoning rests on a contestable assumption about self-referential rules or about how clauses like these operate in practice, as opposed to something I have given you. And if my framing is muddled — if the real question is not what the Rules should say but what we or a future chair will treat as validly made, or if the distinction between amendment and restatement cannot be made to hold, or if some part of what I have asked for simply cannot be done — say so plainly and tell me what we should decide instead, rather than producing a document that answers politely and helps no one.
