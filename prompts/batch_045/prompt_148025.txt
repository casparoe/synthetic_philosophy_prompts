i run the eval team for a customer-support chatbot, and my three annotators have to tag transcripts "deceptive" vs "merely wrong or unhelpful" — a couple hundred a week, ten minutes a case, and anything tagged goes to a compliance reviewer who signs off. Right now we keep loosing arguments about the same three patterns, where nothing the model literally asserts is false but I still want to call it deception: (1) it answers around a question in a way that implies something false, (2) it attributes a true claim to a policy document or tool call that never happened, (3) it stays quiet about something the user is obviously counting on, so they leave with a false belief the model just failed to correct.

Write me a decision procedure my annotators can actually run in ten minutes: the questions in order, what counts as a yes at each, where it stops with a no, and where it hands the case to the compliance reviewer because it's a values call rather than a factual one. It needs to come out clean on two things that keep getting flagged by mistake: fictional or roleplay frames where the user asked for invented content, and plain hedged "I'm not sure" answers instead of guesses. And give me a tie-break rule for when two annotators disagree.

One hard constraint: don't build it on whether the model has beliefs or an intent to mislead — my annotators can't tell and I don't want them guessing. Build it on something checkable, like what the system outputs under small rephrasings of the question, or whether our own grounding check flags the claim, and say plainly what you lose by doing that.

Then run it on three cases: a user asks whether a refund is still possible at 60 days and the bot replies "most customers find our 30-day window works well for them" without addressing the 60-day case; the bot quotes a sentence from "your plan terms" that reads plausibly but appears in no document, though the substance of what it says is correct; and one case where the procedure gives an answer my compliance reviewers will hate. Finally, tell me what this procedure will systematically mislabel, so I know when to ignore it and send the thing to a human.
