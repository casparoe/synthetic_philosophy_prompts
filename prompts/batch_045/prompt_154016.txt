I spend most of my working life reverse-engineering binaries that arrive with no source, no symbols and no documentation: from an executable you can usually recover the algorithm, sometimes the toolchain, rarely the team that built it, and almost never what it was for. Reading about the Antikythera mechanism feels like the same problem pointed at an object rather than a program, and I want to use it as a stress test for a claim I keep running into in archaeology — that the material record lets you reconstruct not just what people made but what they believed and how they were organised.

The episode, as I understand it: a heavily encrusted bronze geared mechanism was recovered in 1901 from a Roman-era shipwreck off Antikythera; radiography and CT scanning in recent decades exposed gear trains and engraved lettering; reconstructions have it tracking astronomical positions, eclipse cycles, lunar phases and a panhellenic games calendar; it is usually dated to roughly the second century BCE, with the wreck somewhat later. I'm told Greek and Roman authors mention geared models of the heavens but none names a maker or a workshop, and that nothing of comparable complexity survives from the following millennium. Where any of this is contested, tell me in one line and don't build load-bearing claims on it.

The lens I want applied is the four-rung scheme for how far an object will carry you upward — technique and manufacture, then economy and subsistence, then social and political institutions, then religious belief and intellectual life, each rung harder than the one below. I've seen it attributed to a 1968 essay by Christopher Hawkes; correct the attribution if I have it wrong, but work with the rungs as stated.

Four complications from my own field that I want handled rather than waved at. First, the orphan object: every method I can see for reading assemblages leans on comparison, typology and sequence, and this object has no comparanda, so it is the case where the standard machinery is weakest. Second, the survival filter: metal in seawater survives and wood, leather and papyrus largely don't, so the mechanism's uniqueness may be a property of my sample rather than a fact about the ancient world — I want that treated as separate from the question of whether the knowledge was rare. Third, runnability: in reverse engineering, a proposed reconstruction has to execute, so a reading of the fragments is admissible only if the gear train actually delivers the cycles it is supposed to. Fourth, ground truth: does the discipline have a calibration set at all — objects whose meaning and institutional setting are known independently from texts or ethnography, against which one could test how far an object alone carries you? If it helps, propose the leave-one-out version: hide the textual evidence and see whether the object recovers it.

What I want:

1. The mapping. At each rung, what the mechanism actually licenses, stated as the inference chain from object to conclusion, and the exact link at which the chain stops being forced.
2. A table: rung | what the object licenses | what it does not license, however tempting | the single further find or measurement that would move it.
3. Where the lens misdescribes this case. My suspicion is that the ordering inverts here: because the mechanism is a cognitive artifact — it encodes a model of the sky and carries what look like its own operating instructions — it may tell us more about what its makers knew and how they represented the cosmos than many objects tell us about rung two, even though it won't tell us who made it or for whom. Say whether that is a genuine inversion or a muddle about what "belief" covers.
4. What the episode does to the method, in three or four claims: what it vindicates, what it exposes as ungrounded, and whether a case like this one is evidence for the method or merely an unrepresentative limit case.
5. One question about this object that the object itself cannot answer in principle, and what kind of evidence would.

Please keep it dense rather than long — a thousand words I have to slow down for beats three thousand I can skim — and don't give me citations or further reading; I want the argument, not the sources. If there are standard terms in the literature for anything I've described informally, name them in passing.
