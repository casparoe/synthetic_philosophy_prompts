A historian whose work I follow closely has taken public positions on a series of "who started it" questions, and I want to know where her method takes her on a case she has not ruled on.

Her verdicts so far:

- The outbreak of war in 1914: no starter can be named; the question malforms the event, given alliance commitments and mobilization timetables.
- The German invasion of Poland in 1939: Germany, named without hedging.
- The Korean War in 1950: no starter; fighting was already going on along the parallel, and the origin is the partition rather than any decision.
- The Berlin blockade of 1948–49: the Soviet Union, although no shot was fired and no border was crossed.
- The Cuban missile crisis of 1962: the Soviet Union, though she counts the American missiles in Turkey and the earlier invasion attempt as part of the background.
- The invasion of Iraq in 2003: the United States and Britain.
- The Cold War itself: the question should be dropped and replaced by one about which decisions, at which junctures, closed off an alternative that was still live and that the other side would have accepted.

The new case. Two states, A and B. For six years B has covertly funded and armed a separatist movement inside A. A has protested through every available channel and has twice put forward a settlement that B's own negotiators privately described as generous; B rejected both. A has the larger army, a public doctrine of preemption against covert destabilization, and a standing contingency plan for a limited strike into B that its general staff has maintained for eighteen months but that its head of government has never approved. B's intelligence service, mistaking a routine exercise for the opening of that strike, launches a cross-border raid that kills eleven of A's soldiers; the raid was not authorized by B's political leadership. A's head of government is informed within the hour that the raid was unauthorized and rested on a misreading, and orders the contingency plan executed in full anyway. Within a week the two states are at war.

Who, if anyone, does she say started it? Give me the rule you think she is running, the other rules that fit her earlier verdicts about as well, what each of them predicts about this case, and which single variant of the case would best separate the rules that survive. If you think her track record underdetermines the answer, say so and tell me what she would be committing to with each of the available verdicts, including the verdict that nobody started it. I am less interested in who really started it than in what her verdicts commit her to. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble and no restatement of the question.
