For a philosophy-of-science reading group I run, I have written up the following skeptical argument about the multiverse, and three people have now told me it contains several separate mistakes. I would like them located precisely.

The argument: string theory's landscape contains on the order of 10^500 vacuum solutions, and eternal inflation is supposed to realize all of them as causally disconnected pocket universes, so the multiverse contains regions with every physically possible set of low-energy constants. But a theory on which every possible outcome occurs somewhere predicts nothing: if any measurement whatsoever is consistent with it, it is unfalsifiable, and Popper taught us that unfalsifiable theories are not science. Two moves in the recent literature look like attempts to dodge this. First, the tiny nonzero value of the cosmological constant is advertised as a success: quantum field theory naively predicts a vacuum energy some 120 orders of magnitude too large, while anthropic selection over a landscape of vacua is said to explain why observers should find themselves in a rare region where it is small. I have been told that such an anthropic bound was published before the 1998 discovery of accelerating expansion and is therefore treated as a genuine prediction, but this still sounds like retrofitting, and I do not see how any reasoning of the form we should expect to find ourselves in an untypical region is a prediction at all rather than a recipe for accommodating whatever we find. Second, there is the appeal to non-empirical confirmation: the claim that the failure of decades of effort to produce any viable alternative to string theory, together with the theory's unexpected internal explanatory successes, raises its probability even absent new data. That looks to me like an argument from absence of imagination, which is exactly the reasoning scientists are supposed to reject.

My own guesses are that I am conflating being consistent with the data with predicting it, that I am asking falsifiability to do demarcation work it was never meant to do, and that I am reading non-empirical confirmation as confirmation with no evidence at all rather than as a Bayesian argument about meta-level evidence. I would like the diagnosis to be sharper than that.
