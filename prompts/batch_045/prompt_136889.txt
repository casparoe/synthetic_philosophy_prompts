Hey — I'm a science-and-ideas reporter and I've got a weird offer I can't think straight about. A billionaire I'm profiling (climate-tech money, huge into "commitment devices," owns a compound in Nevada) has offered the freelance-fund nonprofit I sit on the board of a quarter of a million dollars if, at midnight a week from Sunday, I've genuinely decided to spend next winter in an off-grid cabin in northern Minnesota writing a book. Three months, no internet, no sources, no phone calls returned, and my editors have already hinted they'd backfill my beat. He does not require me to actually go; the money lands either way. He'll judge whether I really decided by listening to a voice memo I record that night and going with his gut, and he's banned anything that locks me in — no nonrefundable tickets, no signed contracts, no public announcements, no telling my agent. Then he closed the last loophole: if I actually do go to the cabin, he won't pay a cent more. He thinks it would be a waste of a good reporter. Its not even self-serving for me personally — the money goes to the fund.

So: can I do it? I have a reason to have decided, and once the money's in hand I'll have no reason at all to go — which is exactly what makes me suspect I *can't* decide to go, because deciding seems to involve settling on doing the thing. I could lie in the memo, but he's freakishly good at reading people and I'd like the money honestly. My brother says screw the no-lock-in clause, book the cabin and buy the ticket and then I'll go — but that feels like it makes me go, not makes me have decided to go; I'd go grudgingly, and grudging isn't deciding. I also half-remember something from a philosophy elective about intentions having to track whatever you take yourself to have reason to do, but I can't reconstruct it and I was hungover. Is there any way to make myself genuinely decide, is self-deception the only route, or is the whole offer uncollectable? One paragraph, 200 words max, no bullet points — I need something I can read out loud to my editor on the phone.
