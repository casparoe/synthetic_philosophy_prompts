Trying to figure out what I actually think about everyday conventions — tipping, queueing, jaywalking — by just going with my gut on cases rather than starting from a theory:

- Cutting into a queue at a bus stop in a city where queueing is the norm: not ok.
- Shouldering forward in the scrum at a bus stop where boarding has always been push-first: fine, that's how it works there.
- Jaywalking at 2am, empty street, no cars, nobody around: totally fine, not even slightly.
- Jaywalking on that same empty street while a school group waits at the corner and the kids are watching: not ok, and clearly worse than the 2am one even though the risk is identical.
- Not tipping in a US sit-down restaurant where the servers wage assumes tips: not ok, feels like the worst item on this list.
- Not tipping when the counter-service iPad swivels round with a 20% prompt and the barista is on a normal wage: fine.
- Tipping in a country where tipping isnt a thing and the staff get a bit embarrassed: not really ok, but only barely.
- A visitor from a scrum-norm country shoving ahead at a queueing-norm airport: not ok, they should queue.
- A visitor from a tipping country leaving tips everywhere in a no-tip country: harmless, maybe faintly annoying for locals.
- The case I keep flip-flopping on: refusing the small facilitation fee a border official expects, in a country where everyone pays it. My gut says refusing is fine, but if conventions generate obligations I can't see why this one is different from the airport queue.

Tentative principle I've been playing with: you owe compliance to a convention only when other people are already stuck in it and have arranged their behaviour or their income around it (call it load-bearing), and your deviation either takes a benefit while skipping a cost everyone else is paying, or is visible enough to lower other people's expectation that folks will comply — which is what keeps the whole thing running. Where neither holds, its not a moral matter at all, just etiquette.

So: does that principle survive my verdicts, especially the 2am-versus-kids pair and the bribe? Does it need an extra clause for conventions whose content is independently rotten, or does adding that clause just smuggle in ordinary morality and leave the word convention doing no work? Does anything here amount to a real duty to defer to local ways, or is it all downstream of reliance plus visibility? And what does your version predict for two cases I haven't listed: a local who stops tipping as a protest but hands each server a note explaining why, and a tourist who jaywalks in a city where literally everyone jaywalks?
