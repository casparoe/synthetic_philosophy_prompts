Two friends of mine spent most of a dinner arguing about a third friend, Marta, who walked away from a tenure-track job to open a pottery studio and tells everyone her reason was that she wanted to make things with her hands.

(1) Jonah said that's almost certainly confabulation. People have no introspective access to what actually caused their choices, so when you ask them why, they generate a plausible-sounding reason on the spot. Marta's answer is that kind of story, not knowledge.

(2) Simone said that's overgeneralized. Introspection is obviously reliable for plenty of things — whether you're in pain, whether you're anxious, whether you're attracted to someone — and "no access to causes" doesn't get you "introspection is unreliable" full stop, because those are different objects.

(3) Jonah granted that, but said the interesting case is exactly the one about reasons, and there's an extra problem with it: the report is produced *after* the decision and under pressure to come up with an answer, so the question may manufacture the state it asks about instead of reading off one that was already there.

(4) Simone said that cuts the other way. If the procedure manufactures the answer, then it doesn't show introspection is unreliable, it shows the procedure never tested introspection — like concluding eyesight is bad from a study run in the dark.

(5) Jonah said that's not right either. If the report is manufactured, there was no prior state to be accurate about, so the capacity isn't being mismeasured, it's being asked about something that didn't exist yet.

(6) Simone said being asked is sometimes *how* you find out what you wanted — the answer you arrive at can be the truth about what you wanted rather than a fabrication — and that this is most of what having a mind of your own consists in anyway.

(7) Jonah said fine, here's a test that settles it without asking anyone anything: if Marta's stated reason is real, it should predict what she does next — she stays with pottery, she doesn't jump at the first offer to go back. Confabulated reasons dissolve once the occasion for them passes. Simone said that just conflates a real reason with a stable one; perfectly real reasons get outweighed all the time.
