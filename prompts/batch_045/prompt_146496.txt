I'm not a philosophy person, I just got stuck on something and cant get unstuck. I read the old story about Buridan's donkey, exactly midway between two identical piles of hay, starving because it has no reason to go left rather than right, and I built an argument out of it that I'm sure goes wrong somewhere, because where it ends up is ridiculous. Here it is. (1) A choice can be rational only if you have some reason for making it. (2) A reason for taking the left pile rather than the right one is just something that makes left better than right. (3) So taking left is rational only if something makes left better, that is, only if you prefer left. (4) But the piles are identical and the donkey is exactly midway, so nothing favors either one; it's genuinely indifferent. (5) So neither choice is rational, and the donkey starves, which is obviously the worst outcome, and no account of rationality should say starving is what a rational animal does here. (6) The fix I keep coming back to is that (3) is wrong: maybe all rationality needs is a reason to take one of the two, not a reason to take this one over that one, and the donkey has a huge reason to eat hay, so either pile is fine. (7) But that fix seems to let in way too much. I definately prefer tea to coffee. Someone offers me both. I have a reason to take one of them, so on this account taking the coffee is rational as well, and that's nuts: knowingly taking the option you think is worse is basically the definition of a bad choice. (8) So (6) is no good either, and the donkey starves again. A friend said the donkey should just flip a mental coin, but that only pushes the question back one step, since now I want to know what makes going along with the coin rational. I'd give up almost any step here before I gave up (1), and I also really dont want to give up what (7) says about picking the worse thing on purpose, but those two seem to be pulling me in opposite directions. Which step should I actually reject, why does it look fine until you press on it, and what does rejecting it cost me?
