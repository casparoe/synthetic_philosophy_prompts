Set-up so we're using the same vocab. A social welfare function takes a profile of individual orderings over social alternatives and spits out a social ordering. Unrestricted domain: any profile of orderings is allowed in. Pareto: if everyone strictly prefers x to y, society ranks x above y. Minimal liberalism: at least two people are each decisive over some pair of alternatives that differ only in that person's own personal sphere. Sen's result: you can't have all three together, even if all you demand of the social ordering is that it be acyclic — in the classic case (a prude and a libertine, and the question of who reads a certain scandalous novel) the three conditions force an outright cycle.

My opening move: the cycle only gets going because the profile is allowed to contain meddlesome preferences. The prude has a ranking over whether the libertine reads the book, and the libertine has one over whether the prude reads it, and those are exactly what let the rights-generated rankings fight the Pareto-generated one. So here's the fix. Don't feed the social welfare function people's actual all-things-considered preferences; feed it their sphere-scrubbed ones. For each person, delete every comparison that turns on what happens outside there own personal sphere, leaving that person indifferent between any two alternatives that differ only in other people's spheres. Then run unrestricted domain, Pareto and minimal liberalism on the scrubbed profile exactly as before. In the novel case: the prude keeps his say over whether he reads, the libertine keeps his say over whether he reads, neither of them has a scrubbed preference between "prude reads" and "libertine reads", so Pareto never fires on that pair and the cycle never gets off the ground. Rights intact, Pareto intact, no domain restriction, no weakening of decisiveness.
