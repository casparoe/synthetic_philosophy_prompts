I'm a PhD student in philosophy — my dissertation is on rule-following and the normativity of proof — and I'm the TA for an upper-level undergraduate course on formal systems. The last class of term covers Hofstadter's MIU-system from *Gödel, Escher, Bach* and the move he calls jumping out of the system, and I'd like to close it with a poem rather than a summary slide. A villanelle, I think.

What I want the form to do:

The two refrains should be the strings MI and MU — or lines built so tightly around them that the string carries the entire line. I don't mind a very short refrain line; the smallness of what keeps coming back is part of the point. They should take turns at the ends of the tercets and arrive together in the final two lines, so that the form itself enacts the puzzle: the same lines return in altered company, never turning into one another, and at the close they sit side by side with the gap between them still unbridgeable from inside.

The poem should also contain a real derivation. Each stanza should include one MIU-string that follows from the previous stanza's string by exactly one legal application of one of the four rules, so that a student with the rules in front of them can trace the sequence and check every step. End at some attainable string; do not let the derivation reach MU. I'd rather the derivation be correct than the metre be perfect — if a step won't scan, pick a different legal step.

The turn: the closing stanza is where the speaker stops deriving and instead says, from outside the system, why the target can never be reached — and says it without the vocabulary. No invariant, no modulo, no undecidable, no Gödel, no incompleteness, no winking at the reader about any of it. The reason has to be carried by an image — the I's never arriving in threes, or whatever figure you find. A student who slept through the proof should still get the shape of the argument; a student who didn't should be able to reconstruct the proof from the poem.

The part I care most about: I want the poem to jump out of its own form at the point where its speaker jumps out of the system. A villanelle is itself a rule-governed system with recursive rules about what must return where; at the moment the argument goes meta, the poem should break or bend its own rule — a refrain misquoted rather than repeated, a rhyme refused, a closing pair that is not quite the pair we have been hearing. The break should be legible as a break, so that the reader feels the poem step outside itself exactly where the reasoning does. If you can also let the last line hint that the outside one lands in is itself a system one could jump out of in turn, so much the better, but don't labour it.

Voice: I'd like the speaker to be the I's — the symbol that gets doubled, that can only be deleted three at a time, and that therefore can never be eliminated altogether. Singular or plural is your call, but it should be the voice of something constitutionally unable to disappear, addressing something it can never become.

Mechanics: nineteen lines, five tercets plus the closing quatrain, ABA rhyme, loose iambic tetrameter — loose enough that the strings don't wreck it — and readable aloud in about a minute.

Give me the poem, then one titled section, 'Commentary' (roughly 300 words), in which you note: which rule each stanza instantiates and the string it yields; where exactly the form breaks and what philosophical work that break is doing; and one thing a philosopher of logic would object to in the way the poem handles the argument.
