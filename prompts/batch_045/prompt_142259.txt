I work in clinical data management, and three of us from the office have a reading group that meets in a pub on tuesdays — we're slowly going through some metaphysics mostly for the argument, and we've hit the same shape of problem twice now and got stuck both times. Here's the shape. Someone asks whether some kind of thing really exists over and above the things everyone already agrees about. Say, whether once you've got all the tiny parts arranged a certain way there is also a further thing, the table; or, in the science case we did last week, whether a theory that talks about an extra quantity is committed to something extra when another version of the same theory, which predicts exactly the same result for every possible experiment, does without it. In both cases everybody concedes that no observation, no experiment and no amount of extra evidence could ever favour one answer over the other, and everybody agrees about which everyday sentences are true and which formulation is more convenient. Fine. But then the person on the 'yes, they really exist' side doesn't stop at 'we'll never know, it's just an open question'. They go on to use the claim: they say our everyday sentences are only loosely or derivatively true if the answer is no, or that one version of the theory is the more basic description of reality, or that a certain explanation only goes through if you help yourself to the extra things. And what I can't get a grip on is that this second move seems to take back what the first one gave. If the difference between the two answers is by construction beyond anything that could ever count as information about it, how can it also be what settles what we ought to say, which description is more fundamental, or which explanation works? It looks like the posit is introduced precisely as something we can't get at and then leaned on as though we had got at it.

It's not the same as the old worry about abstract objects being the sort of thing that can't push on anything, and it's not the same as 'you two just mean different things by exists', and it's not quite 'we only ever get at the structure and never at what fills it' — all three feel adjacent but each seems to miss the bit that's bothering me, which is the swing from humble to load-bearing within one argument. So: does this have an established name, or is there an established way of drawing the line between the harmless version of the move and the dodgy one — and if there isn't, I'd like you to coin something and define it properly, with a case where the move is legitimate and a case where it isn't. A bare label won't do me any good at the pub; I need to know what exactly the phenomenon is, what it isn't, and where its edges are.
