I keep running into something I can't find the word for, and its starting to annoy me.

Here's the pattern. When I try to look closely at my own experience — the red of a stoplight, the burn in my calves on a hill — I don't seem to find the experience at all. What I find is the stoplight, or my legs. Trying harder doesnt help; I just end up inspecting what the experience is of, more and more minutely. It isn't distraction, and it isn't that I run out of attention. It's more like the thing has no surface of its own to look at, and I can only ever look through it.

Now the part I never see anyone mention. Later, remembering the hill, I have no trouble at all: the remembered ache has a texture, I can hold it up and compare it against remembered soreness from some other day, and I even have opinions about whether my memory has it right. So: no inspectable properties while I'm having it, plenty once it's over. The people who compare consciousness to a computer desktop — a simplified interface over machinery we never see — seem to predict the first half and say nothing about the second.

Is there an established concept for this asymmetry, or for either half of it? I won't be satisfied with just a name: I want the nearest concepts, how my case differs from each of them, and whether the remembered half is really the same phenomenon with the looking-through removed or something else entirely. If nothing established fits, coin something for me and say what would and wouldn't count as a case of it.
