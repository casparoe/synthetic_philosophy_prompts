I help run a county wildlife sighting log — me and about forty volunteers, mostly retirees and a couple of high schoolers, who text me things or fill out a Google Form. In season I get maybe fifty records a week. Two grad students and the state natural heritage office pull from our log, and every few years the town leans on it when it rewrites its open space plan. So the stakes aren't nothing: a record of something rare can trigger a survey that costs real money and holds up a subdivision, and no record can mean a wet spot gets filled in. I've got about five minutes per record, no budget, and a retired state biologist named Dale who goes over whatever I flag, once a week.

I'm not academic at all, but I got interested in that old Indian epistemology stuff — Nyaya, I think — with its four ways of knowing: pratyaksha (you saw or heard it yourself), anumana (you worked it out from signs like tracks, scat, chewed bark), upamana (you knew it because it was like something you already knew — the example I keep seeing is knowing what a gavaya is from being told it's sort of cow-like), and shabda (somebody told you, or the field guide says so). I probly have some of this garbled. What grabbed me is that our unwritten rule right now is basically no photo, no record, which now looks to me like it worships one of the four and throws the rest in the trash.

So what I want is a procedure, not a discussion. Give me the questions in the order I should ask them, what counts as a good enough answer to each one, where the procedure just stops and says no, and where it hands the thing to Dale because the real issue is how much risk we're willing to take and not what's true. I need verdict labels I can put in a spreadsheet (confirmed, probable, in-but-unconfirmed, rejected, escalate), and I need it to be runnable by a volunteer in five minutes without me hovering.

Four things it has to handle, because these are the ones I keep getting stuck on:

Two reports of the same kind versus two of different kinds. My instinct is that two different kinds agreeing should count for more than two of the same kind — a photo plus a good inference beats two photos — but I don't know if "different kind" is even the right thing to be counting, or what to do when the different kinds disagree.

Stuff I've started calling laundered perception. A volunteer writes "my neighbor saw one in her yard," and the neighbor did see it, so it feels like perception. But it comes to me as testimony, and I think I've been counting it twice, once as the seeing and once as the telling. Where does the procedure draw that line?

Negative reports. A lot of what comes in is "walked the beaver marsh every week all summer, never heard a least bittern." Some lists of the ways of knowing seem to have a fifth one for knowing something from NOT seeing it, but the Nyaya four don't. If the four genuinely can't handle a negative, say so plainly instead of squeezing it in, because I'd rather have a hole I know about.

And there's something I read about checking your own knowing, a kind of second look at the looking. Sounds like a time sink for someone with five minutes, but maybe it's the step that actually matters. Tell me honestly if it earns its place or if I should drop it.

Then run it on three real-ish cases. First: a volunteer sends a sharp phone photo of a turtle with a yellow chin, first record for our county, with a note that her uncle has been trapping turtles since he was a kid and says it's the state-listed kind. Second: no photo of any animal, but photos of tracks in mud next to a ruler, described as bigger than coyote, round, no claw marks, from a volunteer who has tracked for thirty years — and the state says cougars aren't here, so this one could blow up. Third, and this is the one I care about unfairly: back in 2016 I saw a timber rattlesnake cross the trail on a ridge behind my own house, out walking the dog, no phone on me, and I've wanted the log to mark that ridge as occupied ever since. Run the procedure on my snake and tell me straight. I expect it says no, or in-but-unconfirmed at best, and I want to hear exactly why even though I definately won't like it.

Also, please don't send me to any reading or give me names of books and papers — I've got no library card and no patience for it, and I don't care who said something first. And if any step is really just my gut wearing a lab coat, label it as that. Finally, tell me what the procedure can't do for me at all.
