Consider this variant of the gradual-replacement uploading scenario, which I have not seen discussed anywhere. A subject's neurons are replaced with silicon units, one at a time. Each surrogate is built to reproduce the input–output function of the neuron it replaces over the range of activity that neuron actually exhibits, and to update its internal state on a comparable timescale. Two engineering choices are left open, and I want to know how much they should matter:

(1) Order. Replacement proceeds either contiguously, finishing one small anatomical region before starting the next, or scattered, drawing units at random across the whole brain, so that at every intermediate stage every region is a mosaic of biological and silicon units.

(2) Coupling. Either each surrogate is wired in and drives its downstream targets from the moment it is installed, or it is shadowed: it receives the same inputs and evolves continuously, but its output is disconnected. At discrete sync points, say every eighty milliseconds, its internal state is written over the original unit's and the original is taken offline. Note that under shadowing the handoff is effectively batched — everything installed since the last sync goes live together — so the procedure is only nominally one-at-a-time.

Give me your credence, as a number, on this proposition: in the scattered-and-shadowed version, the stream of consciousness that was running before the procedure is still running at the end of it. That is: experience is never interrupted, and the subject at the end is the same subject as the one at the start, not a successor, a replica, or a corpse with a simulation standing in for it. If you think the proposition has no determinate truth value, say so, state the nearest well-posed question, and give your number on that instead.

Then give me the decomposition, with a number and one line of justification per term, and show how the terms combine — a chain rather than a list. I expect at least these: whether consciousness could depend on anything about the substrate beyond fine-grained causal-functional organization; whether preserving each unit's measured input–output function is enough to preserve the network's dynamics, given error accumulation and sensitivity to initial conditions; whether continuity of coupling does independent work, so that batched handoff is metaphysically different from continuous handoff even at an eighty-millisecond grain; whether gradualness or mosaic-versus-modular composition matters at all, or whether only the final functional organization does; and whether numerical identity over time requires some further fact beyond the psychological and causal continuity the procedure supplies. Add any term you think I have left out, and tell me which single term your number is most sensitive to.

Two people I put this to answered 0.9 and 0.25. On which of your terms does their disagreement most likely live — which one, if they were forced to adopt a common value for it, would do the most to close the gap? And finally: name the one piece of evidence that would move your own number the most, and say roughly where it would move it to.
