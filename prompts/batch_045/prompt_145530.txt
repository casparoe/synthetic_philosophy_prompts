hey — i'm in a philosophy of AI seminar this term and we just spent two weeks on the tool-AI vs agent-AI thing, and for the final we're allowed to hand in a creative piece instead of an essay, so i want to do a satire. i'll obviously rewrite whatever you give me, i just need the bones.

Write a corporate press release, about 600-700 words, from a company (call it something smug and plausible, like Orridge Systems) announcing the launch and regulatory certification of the first AI product certified as a Tool and Not An Agent. Headline, dateline, a couple of quotes from invented executives, and then the bulk of it as a numbered spec sheet / FAQ laying out what the certification actually guarantees. Voice: real, calm, slightly proud product-launch copy — the kind where the legal language is doing all the work.

The jokes need to come from the actual philosophy, not from sci-fi:

- The safety case is that the system has no goals of its own, it only executes the user's. So push on where the user's goal comes from: the tool was trained to imitate text produced by creatures with goals, so "no goals of its own" and "not oriented toward anything" aren't the same claim, and the spec sheet can quietly treat that as settled.
- Give it a horizon cap — e.g. it may plan and act for at most fifteen minutes of wall-clock time per warrant before returning control — and let the FAQ defend that as the bright line, while the same paragraph brags about how it can chain dozens of these, book things, edit a codebase, follow up by email. The funny part is that "short leash" and "not an agent" are being treated as the same property.
- The best clause should be the one where the regulator's official definition of a goal excludes the objective that actually shaped the system: the developer's training objective counts as a "development-time procedure," not a goal, because it isn't active at call time. Let the FAQ be very precise and very self-serving about that.
- One more: the company markets the multi-step autonomy as "agentic" when it's a feature and "purely instrumental" when it's a safety question, and doesn't notice.
- Invent one outside skeptic (an academic at a made-up institute, fine) who gets a quote near the end and is allowed to sound reasonable rather than hysterical — no killbots, no "and then it became sentient" ending, nobody at the company ever admits anything outright.

At the end, outside the piece, add about 100 words: say how confident you are that this is a fair picture of the "tool AI is the safe kind" position, and single out the one joke you're least sure is fair — the one you'd cut if someone who actually holds that view read it.
