I'm putting together a lecture on Maxwell's demon, and I want a case that isolates one claim: that correlation — rather than measurement or erasure — is the thermodynamic resource a demon spends. Please build a thought experiment to the following specifications and then show that it works.

Setup: a working substance (a gas, or a single-molecule Szilard-type engine if you prefer) in thermal equilibrium at one temperature T, in a sealed box that also contains the demon, a device that sorts molecules so as to lower the gas's thermodynamic entropy by a definite amount.

Specifications:

1. The demon never performs an irreversible measurement, never erases a record, never resets itself, and never dumps heat into anything. Every step it takes is reversible in principle — frictionless mechanics or reversible computation, no quantum exotica required.
2. It nevertheless lowers the gas's entropy reliably and on demand, not as a rare fluctuation, and by an amount equal to the mutual information it consumes.
3. The entire compensation for that decrease is located in a correlation — mutual information between the gas and a physical system the demon already holds before it starts — not in measurement, not in erasure, and not in any heat flow.
4. The total entropy of the closed system never decreases, and the reason is not the standard one.

Constraints: the decrease may not come from a temperature gradient, from external work, or from a hidden low-entropy reservoir other than the correlation itself, which must be accounted for. Idealizations may be no stronger than those already standard in the Szilard-engine treatment. You may give the demon a memory initially in a known state, but you must then say what it costs to make the device run a second time. The verdicts must fall out of the physics rather than be stipulated.

Deliverable: the scenario in one short paragraph; then the entropy bookkeeping stage by stage — joint entropy, marginals, and mutual information, with which term moves by how much at each stage; then which feature of the case is doing the work and what the case collapses into if that feature is removed; then the strongest objection available to someone who holds that the cost is always paid in erasure. Close with a verdict: does the case refute that claim, or only reveal it as a special case of something more general — and if the latter, state the more general constraint.

Assume I already have the standard material in hand — the Szilard engine, Landauer's kT ln 2 per bit, and the point that measurement can in principle be done reversibly so that the bill comes due at erasure — so don't re-derive it; build the case and show how it bears on that claim.
