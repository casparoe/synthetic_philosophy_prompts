I'm a backend engineer with no philosophy training, and a colleague used the argument below to kill a uniform prior I'd put in a reliability model. I can't locate the flaw, but I can't accept the conclusion either, because it seems to take down priors I rely on elsewhere and that empirically work.

(1) When I have no reason to favor one member of a partition over another, rationality requires me to spread my credence evenly across them.
(2) Bertrand's chord case: pick a chord of a circle at random and ask how likely it is to be longer than the side of the inscribed equilateral triangle. Even spread over the midpoint's distance from the center along a random radius gives 1/2; even spread over the midpoint's position in the disk gives 1/4; even spread over the angle the chord makes at a fixed point on the circumference gives 1/3. One question, three applications of (1), three answers.
(3) Nothing in the question selects a parameterization, and any continuous quantity can be re-described by any smooth monotonic function of itself, so a flat distribution over a radius is a peaked one over the corresponding volume. For any answer you like, some parameterization as natural as the others delivers it. My colleague adds that this isn't special to continuity: dividing the space as {red, not red} or as {red, blue, green, yellow} changes the answer too, and (1) never says which description to use.
(4) So (1) constrains nothing: it licenses every answer and therefore supports none. Indifference is empty, and any prior resting on it is arbitrary.
(5) But the same move is what gives me an even spread over directions when a process is isotropic, a flat prior over an unknown probability (the rule of succession, which does fine on my real failure-rate data), and the uniform-over-microstates prior in statistical mechanics.
(6) So (4) proves too much. It would discredit (5), which I trust, and my model with it.

Where does this break? I would rather keep (5) than (4), but my colleague says that conceding (4) means admitting my choice of parameterization is unjustified, and I don't have a reply.
