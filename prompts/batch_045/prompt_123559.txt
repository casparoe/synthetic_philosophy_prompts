Hey — I help run a small demography reading group (about ten of us, half of them pronatalist-ish and worried about dependency ratios, half of them suspicious of the whole framing), and we keep going in circles on whether a larger population is actually better. I want to put a sorting tree in the discussion pack so people can see which view they're really committed to rather than thier favourite label.

Could you build me a decision tree / taxonomy for the question "other things equal, is a bigger population better?" Start everyone from the same commitment: we can affect how many people there will be, and the question is about the value of the outcome, not yet about what policy to run.

What I'd like:

1. First, the logical space rather than the names. Give me five or six branching questions, and for each one say what the possible answers are and which combinations of answers are even available. Every node has to be a concrete case or claim someone could answer without knowing which ism it leads to — so no node that amounts to "are you a total utilitarian?". Please use (or improve on) these cases somewhere: a choice between 8 billion people at welfare 70 and 12 billion at welfare 55; adding one extra life that is only barely worth living; a case where we can either save a 40-year-old or, for the same money, cause an additional happy person to exist; a choice that changes who exists without making anyone worse off than they would otherwise have been; and a case that asks what happens if you think the long-run future is more likely to be astronomically bad than astronomically good. Please also include at least one node that splits two views which otherwise give the same verdict on all the standard cases — that's the bit the group always glosses over.

2. Then locate the actual positions in that space as leaves: totalism, averagism, the critical-level family, person-affecting / no-difference views, the asymmetry-ish antinatalist option, a suffering-focused leaf, a leaf for people who only think population size matters instrumentally (more possible innovators, agglomeration, fiscal sustainability, recovery from catastrophe), a leaf for the "size is beside the point, only the worst-off or the threshold matters" crowd, and one for the view my mate calls scale-value — that a civilisation of ten billion people doing things together is worth something over and above the sum of ten billion individual lives. Say whether that last one is really just instrumental value in disguise.

3. Note any combinations of answers nobody occupies, and diagnose why — incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. And flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely cannot seperate rather than pretending the last node did the work.

4. Where the usual impossibility-style results bite (the repugnant conclusion and the other unpalatable ones), mark which leaves get squeezed.

5. Finally a short note on which leaves actually hand you a reason to encourage births, and which ones leave it all down to contingent empirical facts — the group conflates "more people is better" with "we should have more babies" constantly.

Aim for about eight to ten leaves. Please write it in British English. Nobody's going to check references, so no reading lists — just the tree and the reasoning.
