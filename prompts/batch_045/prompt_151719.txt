I'm a junior in my school's French program, and for the end-of-year language showcase I'm giving a short talk on translating verse — specifically on what happens when you insist on keeping the original's rhyme scheme and line length instead of settling for a prose rendering of the sense. I got started on this after reading about a book-length stunt in which one short Renaissance French lyric is redone dozens of times, each version obeying a different formal constraint, and I want to close my talk with a poem rather than a summary.

Please write a shortened sestina — three six-line stanzas plus a three-line envoi — in which the six recurring end-words are words from translation vocabulary, three belonging to sound (things like rhyme, meter, cadence) and three to sense (things like meaning, fidelity, drift). The rotation should do the arguing: because the form keeps pairing each sound-word with a different sense-word in turn, no pairing is privileged, and I want the poem to use that to make the point I keep failing to make in French class — that there may be no canonical word-for-word mapping to be faithful to in the first place, so faithful versus free may not be the axis the choice is really on. Each end-word should mean something slightly different as its neighbors change, so that a glossary underneath the poem would not capture it. The envoi should land on something I can say out loud to an audience of teachers and parents: whether the constraint is a loss or a discovery.

The test I have been using is the gloss test: write out a line's plain-English meaning, and if nothing is lost the form in that line was decoration, while if the gloss comes out poorer than the line then form was doing semantic work. I would like the poem to survive someone applying that test to it.

After the poem, add a note of a paragraph or two in prose separating what people who work on this would broadly agree about — that holding a tight form forces real changes, that something is always lost — from what is genuinely contested: whether form can carry content that no paraphrase recovers, and whether faithfulness to an original is a coherent target at all. Please flag the places where the poem takes a side or leans on a contested reading, rather than letting it pass as settled.
