Her argument as I understood it: the well-known impossibility results show that the standard fairness criteria are mathematically incompatible with each other whenever the groups have different base rates, so there's no fact of the matter about which one you should satisfy, so mandating an audit just amounts to arbitrarily picking one criterion and pretending it's *the* definition of fairness — and, she added, "fairness isn't free, the math says you lose accuracy," and our contract already locks the vendor into hitting a specific accuracy number.

I'm pretty sure that doesn't follow, but I can't say where it breaks and I don't want to walk into the next meeting just repeating "surely there's *some* sense in which a tool is fairer than another." Do the same for "fairness isn't free" if that's really a separate claim hiding behind the first one. A menu I can literally read out in the meeting would be perfect, ideally noting which readings I'm most likely actually facing given how she used it, and whether any of them survive the fact that our score feeds a human decision used for two different purposes.
