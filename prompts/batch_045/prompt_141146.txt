I've been reading about pain asymbolia — you still get the whole burning or stabbing sensation, you can point to exactly where it is and rate its intensity, you just don't mind it, some people laugh — and I can't get stable on what's supposed to be bad about pain in the first place. So here are my gut verdicts; tell me what principle, if any, they add up to.

1. Textbook asymbolic patient, full-intensity pain, zero unpleasantness, wouldn't lift a finger or pay a cent to stop it: nothing bad here for them.
2. Same sensory pain, still insists it doesn't feel bad, but it keeps hijacking their attention — can't finish a sentence, loses the thread of a page — and after ten minutes they'd take a pill just to get their concentration back: bad, and fairly bad. (1 and 2 are the pair I can't reconcile, since neither involves anything they'd call unpleasant.)
3. Chilihead: the burn really is painful and they'd call it unpleasant in the moment, but they seek it out and enjoy the whole thing: not bad for them, maybe good.
4. Marathon runner at mile 22 who hates the pain and wants it stopped, then says afterwards it was one of the best days of their life: the pain was bad, the race wasn't, and I don't think those are in tension.
5. Someone born with no pain sensitivity at all: I don't want to say their life is worse for missing out on a bad experience. It's worse because they burn and break themselves without noticing, which seems like a different complaint entirely.
6. Phantom limb pain in someone who calls it awful but refuses anything that would remove it, because it's the only remaining sense that the limb is still theirs: bad — and I dont think keeping it is irrational — but I'm shakier here than anywhere else.

Is there one account of what makes pain bad that gets all six? Which of my verdicts does it force me to give up? And what does it say about the one I keep flip-flopping on: long-session tattoo or wound-dressing pain that someone has learned to sit perfectly still inside of, and reports as neither pleasant nor unpleasant — just there. Bad for them or not? Commit to one account, defend it, tell me plainly which of my verdicts it cant accommodate, and name one further case it gives a verdict on that I haven't listed.
