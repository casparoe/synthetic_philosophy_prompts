Set aside what any historical Jain text or commentator may have meant, and do not settle the question by appeal to scripture or scholarly authority; treat the theses below as I define them. I want your credence in this reconstructed claim: if anekāntavāda is true—understood as the thesis that concrete reality is irreducibly many-sided, so that no unqualified assertion captures the whole truth about an object, though standpoint-qualified assertions can be partly or conditionally true—then in a dispute where each side seems to capture a real but partial aspect of the object, such as whether a restored ship is the same ship as the original, the rationally ideal response is to adopt a sevenfold pattern of qualification rather than to suspend judgment, split the difference, or accept a plain contradiction. By the sevenfold pattern I mean: in some respect the claim holds; in some respect it does not hold; in some respect it both holds and does not hold; in some respect it is indescribable; and in some respect it is indescribable together with each of the first three. My own credence is 0.3; a friend sympathetic to Jain philosophy puts it at 0.7.

Please decompose your number: give rough probabilities for (a) the many-sidedness thesis itself being true, (b) its truth entailing the sevenfold response for disputes of this kind, (c) the sevenfold pattern being logically coherent rather than collapsing into trivial relativism or mere hidden equivocation on unstated parameters, (d) the relevant notion of a rationally ideal response being doxastic rather than merely linguistic or pragmatic, and (e) any further term you think belongs. Treat (a) and (b) as claims about this reconstruction, not about what any tradition historically asserted. Explain how the probabilities combine into your final credence, state which single term your number is most sensitive to, and say what argument or conceptual distinction would move it most. Finally, tell me whether the gap between 0.3 and 0.7 is more likely a disagreement about (b), about (c), or about (d).
