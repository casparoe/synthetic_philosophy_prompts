hey — I'm writing a short position paper for a logic reading group and I want my package of views stress-tested before I hand it in. I'm mostly self-taught here so I may well be reinventing things badly. Roughly where I've landed:

1. Some contradictions are true. The Liar is both true and false, and I don't think the hierarchy response or the gap response is available — I think both end up saying something false about what truth is.

2. I definately don't want to say classical logic is wrong. Paraconsistent logic is the general case and classical logic is what you get where nothing in the domain is both true and false, and I want that to underwrite a real recapture: when I'm doing arithmetic or ordinary empirical reasoning I get all the classical rules back, explosion included. My own version of the safety condition — I've been calling it "sheltered recapture" — is that you have to establish that the region you're walling off is consistent using a logic strictly weaker than the one you're recovering inside it. I've not seen anyone put it quite that way but it feels right to me.

3. The T-schema is unrestricted and non-negotiable: it is true that A iff A, no levels, no exceptions. This is the main thing pushing me into (1).

4. Nothing in logic is a priori and nothing in logic is immune to revision. You pick a logic the way you pick any other theory — costs, benefits, fit with the rest of what you believe, how much you'd have to give up elsewhere.

5. Triviality is the one outcome I'll pay anything to avoid. So I've given up disjunctive syllogism, and contraction too, wich is what Curry's paradox seems to force once explosion is already gone.

6. Local dialetheism only. Empirically, contradictions are never true: if two well-supported empirical claims are contradictories, that's always a reason to drop one, never a reason to accept both. And I want to say the same sort of thing about law — where a code contains a provision that says of itself that it doesn't apply, or an unamendable clause that collides with the amendment procedure it sits inside, I think what we've got is a practical conflict for a court to settle, not a true contradiction in the Liar's sense.

My worry is that one of these is doing work I can't pay for. My own guess is that the soft spot is somewhere around (3) and (4); a friend who's read more than me says the real problem is (2), and that my sheltered-recapture condition can never actually be satisfied. I don't trust either of us.

What I want: go through the pairs where there's something real to say, tell me which tensions are genuine and which only look like tensions — I keep being told (1) and (2) can't both be true and I've never understood why — then rank the genuine ones by how much damage they do, and for each say which clause has to give and what it costs me to give it up. Please don't just flag everything as in tension; I need to know which ones are load-bearing and which I can wave off. And if there's one revision somewhere that clears up the most at the least cost, say which and what I'd be signing up for.
