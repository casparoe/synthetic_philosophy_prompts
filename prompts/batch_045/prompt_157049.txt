I keep arguing with a friend about whether Malthus was right but early, and every round just turns into rescue moves: he says the Green Revolution only postponed the reckoning, I say that's unfalsifiable, he says its falsifiable just on a longer clock. Before we go another round I want the spec, not the verdict.

Write me the desiderata an account of premature truth in long-run predictions has to meet. Concretely: what would a theory have to deliver for us to be able to say of a prediction — Malthus on population, the Club of Rome style resource ones, the peak oil ones — that it genuinely hasnt come due yet, as opposed to having been falsified and then quietly retro-fitted, or never having been testable in the first place. The purpose is fixed: good enough to adjudicate these retroactive claims in a way both of us would have had to accept before seeing how things turned out. For each requirement give the case or consideration behind it, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are really just one camp's prejudice.

Three things I want tested as candidate requirements rather than assumed — dont feel obliged to keep any of them:

- a trichotomy of rescues: date-shift (same mechanism, timeline slipped), domain-shift (it now applies to other regions, other species, or a horizon nobody alive will see), and mechanism-swap (population pressure quietly becomes resource pressure, or the positive check becomes the preventive one). Which are legitimate, and what does a theory have to specify in advance for a rescue to be legitimate rather than ad hoc?
- a mortgage rule: every rescue has to cost something — give up a class of cases it used to claim, drop a prediction it used to make, or commit to some new observable. Real desideratum, or just my taste for Popperian bookkeeping?
- a mirror test: if we would be embarassed to grant the same rescue to the opposite prediction, the rescue is degenerate. Is that a genuine test or does it just punish unpopular theories?

Then tell me which single requirement does the most work at knocking out candidates, and whether any pair on your list cant be jointly satisfied — if so, which one you'd drop and why. Skip the scorecard for Malthus himself, I'll do that arguing with my friend. British English please.
