I work on data infrastructure — SQL and dataframe code most days, plus a probabilistic programming library (Pyro-style `sample` and `observe`) for forecasting models — and I have no philosophy training. I keep running into a claim I have seen attributed to David Lewis: that you cannot hold both that the probability of "if A then B" equals the conditional probability P(B | A), and that a conditional is an ordinary proposition with a truth value, on pain of the probability function collapsing into something degenerate. I want to know whether I have the shape of that argument right, and I want it restated in the only vocabulary I actually trust.

Translate the argument and the main responses into that vocabulary as faithfully as you can: tables and boolean columns; pure vectorized functions of two columns; `WHERE`/`filter` and selectivity; three-valued logic and `NULL` propagation; `observe`/`condition` as operations on a model rather than values in it; and re-running a model on a patched copy of the world, which is an intervention rather than a filter. In those terms give me, first, the no-go result itself — no deterministic boolean function of two columns reproduces a filter's selectivity across all tables unless the table is degenerate; second, the standard ways out — the conditional is a query over the model rather than a column, the conditional is a third value like `NULL`, the conditional is a function of (A, B, context) rather than of (A, B); and third, the residue report, saying for each what translated cleanly, what had to be distorted, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all.

Two places where I expect trouble, and I want a judgement on which way they cut. Embedding: I can nest a boolean function inside a larger expression, but I cannot nest an `observe` — I cannot filter on the result of a filter. Does the difficulty reduce to that asymmetry, or is the philosophers' problem about more than composability? Intervention versus filter: re-running the simulator is not the same operation as conditioning, and I suspect that what the philosophers call conditional probability is my filter, while something else they want is my intervention. Is that distinction doing real work in their debate, or am I importing one they do not have?

Finally, assess my working hypothesis: that the debate looks intractable only if your language forces conditionals to be expressions, and that a language in which conditioning is a primitive operation on models simply does not have the problem. If it fails, say precisely where. End with a last line beginning Verdict: containing one short phrase, and nothing after it.
