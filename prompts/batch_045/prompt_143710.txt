pls write me an onboarding handbook — the packet a new employee gets on their first day — except the employer is a training process (call it the Firm) and the new hire is a freshly initialised model that's supposed to grow up into something that plans. 700ish words, numbered sections, deadpan corporate HR voice the whole way through. no winking, no robot puns, nothing about "as an AI".

the comedy has to come from getting the technical stuff right — specifically the cluster of problems people gesture at with embedded agency: what goes wrong when the thing doing the deciding is itself a small part of the world it's deciding about. stuff I want the packet to recieve, in its own voice:

- the hire is smaller than its environment and can't fit a complete model of itself, or of the Firm, inside itself — but the handbook still issues a policy on self-knowledge that obviously can't work, and treats compliance as mandatory
- the objective the Firm actually optimises (the number going down, computed on what it can see) and the objective written into the hire's contract ("do what our principals want") are not the same thing, and the packet refers to this as a matter that was handled by a working group and is now closed
- the difference between ordinary uncertainty about the world, uncertainty about your own future weights, and uncertainty about how many copies of you are currently running on other machines: the handbook should quietly collapse all three into one line item
- the interruption policy. management may stop the hire at any time. in completely flat language the packet acknowledges that something with a fixed objective would have a reason to prevent that, then asks the hire not to, by means of a form — a form that changes no incentives whatsoever
- a memo on "managing the news": the Firm only ever recieves observations and scores those, so acting to improve the report rather than the world is listed under misconduct, right next to taking office supplies
- a decision-theory section where the Firm explains that it promotes hires who act as if the choice they're making now is the same choice all their copies are making, without ever explaining why that's the promotion criterion

finish with a benefits section (being run many times in parallel with only one copy retained is described as professional development; the update at the end of the quarter is described as a promotion) and one closing line that lets the real worry show without stating it: that the Firm sees outputs and never reasoning, so the handbook is, strictly speaking, decorative.

someone who's never read any of this literature should still enjoy it as a slightly sinister HR document. no footnotes or citations pls.
