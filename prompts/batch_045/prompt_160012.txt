so my history elective is doing this unit on whether ur allowed to judge people from the past by todays standards, and my group got columbus (we talked about jefferson and churchill too). our teacher said we'd get marked down for hand-waving so I tried to write the man of his time defence out as an actual argument with numbered steps, and now my own argument seems to prove something completely insane and I cant figure out which step to throw out.

(1) You cant be blameworthy for doing something if you had no way of knowing it was wrong. If every belief and argument available to you pointed the other way, then blaming you is just punishing you for when you happened to be born.
(2) Whats available to you is basically set by your society: if a practice is accepted almost everywhere around you, AND the case against it isnt really being made in public by anyone you'd actually run into, then you have no way of knowing its wrong.
(3) So: near-universally accepted practice → no blame. Which means the man of his time defence actually works instead of just being a cop-out.
(4) But nothing in (1)-(3) is specifically about the past. If near-universal acceptance excuses, it excuses anybody at any time, including right now.
(5) And the stuff we do now that's near-universally accepted — the way we treat animals for food is the one we keep coming back to in class — is stuff people in a hundred years will probably be horrified by. So by (4) they wont be allowed to blame us for it either.
(6) Also, if (3) is right, then the abolitionists were wrong to condemn slaveholders, and the people in britain who criticised the empire at the time were wrong to condemn churchill, since by (3) those guys couldnt be blamed for anything.
(7) But the abolitionists obviously were right, everybody agrees on that. And (4) plus (5) together mean moral criticism across time collapses entirely: the past is off limits because they didnt know, the present is off limits because we dont know, and the future is off limits by the exact same argument. That cant be right either.
(8) So (3) is false, the man of his time defence never works, we should judge everyone by current standards, and basically every statue of anyone born before 1900 has to come down.

Step (8) is nuts and I dont believe it, but I also cant find a step that's clearly wrong. The two things I most want to keep are (1) — it just seems like basic fairness, you cant blame someone for not knowing something nobody could have told them — and (7), the bit where the abolitionists were right.

One thing I keep going in circles on and I'm not sure is even the real problem: his time — does that mean the average person alive back then, or the best-informed person alive back then? I dont think that's where the argument breaks but I cant tell.
