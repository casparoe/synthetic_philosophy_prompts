I'm the quant on a regional flood-defence board. Every quarter we get about a dozen proposals (raise this levee, buy pumping capacity, restore a floodplain), and our hydrology consultants have stopped giving us point probabilities — for each option they hand over a 90% interval for the annual failure probability, because they can't agree on a prior for extreme rainfall. The board is seven people, mostly civil engineers plus two councillors, none of who have heard of imprecise probabilities, and they sign off in a half-day session, so I need a procedure I can run in a afternoon per proposal and then defend to auditors.

Write me that procedure. It should cover what to do when two options' intervals overlap heavily, when (if ever) to decide off the bottom of an interval versus the top versus something in between, and how to judge whether it's worth paying to narrow an interval before deciding instead of just choosing now. Be blunt about which steps are judgment in a lab coat.

Then run it on two cases: a cheap levee option whose interval almost entirely overlaps the expensive one's, and a pumping station where most of the expert range looks fine but a couple of experts put the failure rate somewhere catastrophic. One wrinkle: the board has a standing rule that we may not recommend an option that any one of our experts ranks as the worst on the table, unless we flag it explicitly in writing — tell me whether that rule quietly breaks the procedure, and what to do instead if it does.

Give me exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, no intro and no conclusion: the screening step that can kill a proposal outright, the ranking rule for overlapping intervals, the rule about paying to narrow an interval, the two worked cases, and then the board's rule plus what the procedure can't settle. American English please.
