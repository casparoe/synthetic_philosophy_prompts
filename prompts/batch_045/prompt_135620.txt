ok so me and two friends from my old undergrad philosophy study group got into it again last night about the torture vs dust specks case — you know the one, you're asked whether you'd rather have one person endure 50 years of torture or have some astronomically huge number of people (we always say 3^^^3) each get a single dust speck in their eye for a second — and as usual nobody budged but nobody could say why their answer was anything more than a gut feeling. The running joke version we've developed is that the specks are administered by a very boring federal agency that has to fill out paperwork and hold comment periods for every harm it causes, and I've decided I actually want to write that up for a little zine I put together with some friends about thought experiments that break people's brains.

So here's the ask: write me a mock Notice of Proposed Rulemaking, the kind of thing that gets published in the Federal Register, from a fictional 'Office of De Minimis Harms' that is trying to codify a legally binding conversion rate between imperceptible harms (specks) and severe ones (torture-days) — I'm picturing a schedule of tradeable 'microharm credits,' a noticeability exemption saying that harms no subject ever consciously registers don't count unless they're aggregated across more than some absurd threshold of subjects, a per-subject cap, and an appendix of impact tables with numbers in them. The jokes have to come from getting the underlying moral mathematics right rather than from puns: transitivity and the way it forces anyone who picks the specks to admit there's *some* number at which they'd flip while being unable to name it, the gap between 'imperceptible' and 'nonexistent,' the difference between capping harm per person and capping it in total, and the weirdness of a harm that's real but that nobody ever has a bad day about. Also, one of my friends is a hard-nosed aggregator and I want the satire to be fair to her, so give the agency at least one genuinely strong deadpan line in its own defense — something like pointing out that whoever refuses the specks owes an explanation of why the single torture victim outweighs each of the 3^^^3 others. Keep the voice flat and bureaucratic throughout, don't ever come out and say the words 'utilitarian' or 'aggregation' as a wink to the reader, and include at least one footnote citing a fictional agency study whose own numbers quietly give away how arbitrary the chosen threshold is.

A couple more things: the house-rule version we argue about is one where you're offered the same trade every morning for the rest of your life, so what you're really picking is a standing policy rather than a one-time act — feel free to work that in if it's useful, e.g. as a 'recurring elections' section. Aim for 700-900 words, formatted with numbered sections, and please write the whole thing in American English. No 'dust settles' jokes, we've heard them all.
