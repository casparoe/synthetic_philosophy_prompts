ok so slightly weird situation. my younger sister just got hired to write marketing copy for a horoscope app startup and she told her new bosses, over dinner, that astrology is "basically Kepler-approved" — which is my fault, because I'm the one who told her that. Now they apparently want to build the whole launch around being the "scientific" astrology app, and she's panicking and asked me for help, and I'm aware that what she actually needs is for someone who knows the real history to write the thing.

The problem is that the real history is funnier than the marketing pitch and doesn't support it at all. As far as I understand it: Kepler genuinely made his living partly off casting charts for paying patrons (Wallenstein among them, I think), and genuinely thought astrology was worth salvaging — but what "salvaging" meant for him was tearing most of it out. He wanted to dump the twelve signs and the houses as arbitrary, keep the aspects (the angles planets make to each other) and even add new ones that had a geometric/musical justification, and explain the whole thing by the idea that the Earth has something like a sensitive soul that registers harmonies — geometric ratios — the way a soul registers consonance in music. So: real, but not physical forces; real, but he thought it bought you weather, temperaments, broad inclinations, and definitely not "on Tuesday you'll meet a tall stranger." He also seems to have been pretty frank that astrology had to keep working as a business because it was the daughter supporting her broke mother astronomy.

So: write her the launch blog post. ~600–700 words, founder-voice, pitch-deck enthusiastic and completely deadpan, for an app that has taken Kepler's reform program absolutely seriously and shipped it. Call it what you want; Aspect or Harmonice or whatever. The jokes have to come from getting his actual views right rather than from "lol astrology" — someone who's read about Kepler's astrology should laugh because it's fair, and someone who hasn't should still follow it. Please include: a "what we removed" changelog (houses, signs, most of the traditional kit), at least one straight-faced explanation of the Earth-soul mechanism and why it can't do specific-event predictions, a customer-support exchange with a beta user who is furious that the app won't tell them anything about their career, and a blurb from an invented traditional astrologer who thinks this is a betrayal. The reductio should land on its own: the more rigorously Keplerian the product gets, the less it can sell, until the only honest remaining feature is something like a push notification about a conjunction — while the funding pitch still depends on astrology paying for astronomy. Don't have anyone wink at the reader, and don't make Kepler look like a fool; he was a serious man doing this without gravity to lean on. And no fake quotes attributed to real people — paraphrase him, but the invented astrologer and the beta user can say whatever you want.
