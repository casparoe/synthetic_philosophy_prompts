I'm an undergraduate in a seminar on probability and anthropic reasoning, and I'm assembling a short diagnostic for my study group: a decision tree that leads someone from their verdicts on concrete cases to a named position on self-locating belief, instead of asking them which label they like.

Assume the reader has never heard of the self-sampling or self-indication assumptions. Every node should be a question they can answer from a described case alone, and every leaf should carry a position name plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Keep it to roughly eight to ten decision nodes.

The cases I want doing the work are: the standard incubator (one observer created if the coin lands heads, two if tails); Sleeping Beauty; the Doomsday argument; the Presumptuous Philosopher, where self-indication style reasoning appears to confirm a vastly larger universe on purely anthropic grounds; and two variants I invented to block the stock answers — the Sealed Incubator, where the tails branch adds a trillion observers in a causally sealed region that you are reliably told you are not in, and the Shuffled Incubator, where both branches create the same number of observers but assign them serial positions, and you learn only that you occupy some position or other.

Leaves that must appear: plain person-based self-sampling, the observer-moment version, self-indication, the approach on which indexical evidence is never genuinely new evidence and one conditions only on non-indexical facts, and the response that treats the Presumptuous Philosopher as a problem about action rather than belief. Include a scoring key, mark the answer combinations that are available in logical space but that nobody defends and say why, and flag any two leaves your tree cannot actually separate.
