hi — I need someone to audit an analogy my supervisor keeps using, because I can't tell whether it's doing real work or just laundering a conclusion.

Setup: I'm working on whether 'better than' is transitive, in the Parfit-style mere-addition / Temkin-ish spectrum territory. Concretely, the case I keep coming back to: start with a smallish population all at a very high level of well-being, then take a long series of steps, each of which adds a few people whose lives are just barely worth living and shaves a tiny amount off the best-off, so every step is both more numerous and slightly more equal. After a few hundred steps you've got a huge population sitting barely above zero. The verdicts most people want to give: every step is at least as good as the one before, and the first outcome is better than the last. Which can't both stand if 'at least as good as' is transitive.

The analogy: a ruler with only centimetre marks. He says: I can't discriminate 5.00 from 5.02, so 'same length as far as I can tell' chains along a series of barely-different rods running from clearly short to clearly long, and nobody concludes on that basis that lengths are intransitive. The order is determinate, transitive and complete; our *access* to it is coarse. So the spectrum arguments, he says, are exploiting the grain of our discriminative powers, and the right response is to keep transitivty and locate the problem in us rather than in the relation.

I want the full audit: what maps onto what (the magnitude, the unit, the instrument, the observer, the finer instrument, the end-to-end check, measurement error), where the mapping breaks, which features of the length case it quietly imports into the value case, and whether his conclusion survives once those are on the table.

Two things I can't settle myself:

(a) The ruler case has a diagnostic the value case might not: the end-to-end measurement. Put the shortest and longest rod side by side and the big difference tells you the local verdicts were unreliable. But comparing the top of the spectrum with the bottom is itself just another pairwise value judgement, and as far as I can tell it only counts against the chain if you've already assumed transitivity — otherwise 'first is better than last' plus the whole chain of 'not worse' steps is just what the intransitivity claim looks like from the inside. Is the end-to-end move circular here, or does it still constrain something (which verdict you give up, say) even if it can't tell you which?

(b) It may be aimed at the wrong target. The ruler is about *failures* to discriminate; the verdicts in the spectrum arguements I care about are positive — each step is not worse, and people are happy to say so — not 'I can't tell'. A finer instrument is no help with a verdict you're already confident in. Is there a version of the measuring picture that models that (a stick that's warped across part of its range? readings that are individually stable but jointly inconsistent?), or does this mismatch show the analogy was carrying the conclusion the whole time?
