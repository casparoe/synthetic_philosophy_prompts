I hold a strong form of path dependence about social and technical arrangements: not merely that early events leave traces, but that if history were re-run with a small perturbation to some early and apparently inconsequential episode — a procurement decision, a standards vote, a strike, a bad harvest — the eventual settlement would differ not just in which firm, label or standard won, but in what the arrangement enables people to do, judged at a functional level of description rather than a brand-level one. I have also, I admit, retreated upwards: as the textbook cases have been empirically contested, my claim has become less about any particular case and more about the shape of the space of possible outcomes, and I now demand of anyone who says the same outcome would have arrived anyway by another route that they name in advance the durable underlying variable doing the work, together with a proxy for it I could in principle measure — otherwise, I say, their inevitability story is unfalsifiable.

What would count against this? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want you to commit to which are live rather than merely logically available. What would I have to find — in the comparative record, or in formal results about coordination, increasing returns and凸 lock-in — to make me concede that re-runs converge on functionally equivalent outcomes under different labels? Is my demand that critics name their variable in advance a fair methodological constraint or the manoeuvre by which I have insulated the view, and if it is the latter, is that a defect in a thesis of this kind or the right posture for a claim about what would have happened? Please include what a defender of my position should concede rather than dismiss. I am most moved by arguments that grant me the distinction between mere relabelling and genuine divergence and then show that the distinction cannot bear the weight I put on it.
