I'm in an advanced seminar on moral responsibility and autonomous systems, and the final project is a 1,200-word adversarial-collaboration document. Please write it as the joint product of two positions I'll call the gap realist and the gap institutionalist. The realist holds that when a system's behavior is produced by learning under conditions no individual could have specified in advance, there can be outcomes for which nobody satisfies the control and foreseeability conditions that blame requires, and that the honest conclusion is to restrict deployment. The institutionalist holds that responsibility practices have never required that anyway: we already handle organizational and corporate harm through strict and vicarious liability, mandatory insurance, and enterprise liability, and where a new technology does defeat those devices, the right conclusion is that the device needs redesigning, not that responsibility has run out.

I want you to use, as the organizing tool, a distinction I've been working on: an attributability gap, where nobody satisfies the conditions for backward-looking blame, versus an accountability gap, where nobody is in a position to answer for the outcome, explain it, and change the practice that produced it. My suspicion is that much of the shouting here comes from the two sides defaulting to different members of that pair. Use the distinction where it genuinely separates something, and say plainly where it does no work.

The document should contain: (1) a numbered section of claims both sides can sign — I'd expect at least that the dispute is not about how often harm occurs, that the slogan that someone must be responsible is not itself an argument, and that the hard cases are ones where the performance gain and the opacity come from the same feature of the system; (2) a disagreements table giving each side's position in wording its own proponent would accept, flagging any row you think is verbal rather than substantive; (3) three cruxes, one of which should be whether a gap that would equally arise inside a large human organization is any less of a gap, and one of which should be a redesign test: whether the system could have been built with an intervention point legible to a named role without losing the gains that justified deploying it.

For each crux, give one case or piece of evidence both sides accept as bearing on it, and record in advance what each side would predict about it. Please use the case I've been working on rather than a trolley-style one: a hospital's patient-allocation system, trained on throughput data, learns a triage policy that improves survival overall but concentrates delays on one group of patients; no clinician or engineer can reconstruct why, and the pattern only appears under a rare combination of admissions. For that case I also want each side's answer under a should-have-known-at-a-cost standard: suppose the hospital could have detected the pattern by spending a specified amount on monitoring that it chose not to spend. Format the whole thing as a text both parties could put their names to, under about 1,200 words.
