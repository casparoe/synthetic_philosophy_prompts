hey — our meetup is doing a re-read of the decision theory stuff and everyone's supposed to write down their verdicts on the standard cases beforehand so we can see whos secretly a two-boxer. I've been lurking since the OB days and I thought I knew where I stood, but writing it out I've got this feeling that something doesnt fit and I cant put my finger on what. Here's me:

1. Newcomb's problem: I one-box. My reason is basically "I'd rather be the sort of agent that walks away with the million" — I've never bought the whole logical-counterfactual / algorithm-output machinery people use to argue for it, I just think the two-boxer's reasoning is a reliable way to end up with a thousand dollars.

2. Death in Damascus (Death knocks on your door and says he's coming for you tomorrow; you can pay to be in Aleppo instead): I stay put. Death is a perfect predictor, so paying to get to Aleppo doesnt help, it just costs me the trip. I'm fine with the "you cant buy your way out of a prediction" conclusion.

3. Smoking lesion (there's a lesion that causes both the urge to smoke and the cancer): I smoke. There's no path from not smoking to not getting cancer, so abstaining buys me nothing. I take this as the case that kills evidential decision theory — correlations with no causal path behind them shouldnt move you.

4. Parfit's hitchhiker (driver takes you to town on your promise to pay, then you decide whether to pay): I pay once I'm in town. The "but you're already in town, the ride already happened" objection strikes me as exactly the two-boxer's move and I've never found it convincing — I'd rather be the sort of person who gets rides out of the desert.

5. Counterfactual mugging (Omega flips a coin; heads and he asks you for $100, tails and he'd have handed you $10,000 if he'd predicted you'd pay): I dont pay. The coin's already landed, tails didnt come up, and handing over $100 doesnt put $10,000 into a box that's already empty.

6. The general principle I'd appeal to, which I'm most confident about and least able to state: I dont think an action can be justified by the fact that *this* action, right now, is good news — the justification always has to be something like "you'd rather be the sort of thing that does this." I know that's handwavy.

7. And one I dont think is inconsistent but I've gotten grief about: if Death werent a predictor at all, just some guy who is going to be in Damascus tomorrow and tells me so, then of course I go to Aleppo. Same surface situation, opposite verdict, and I think that's fine.

So: does this hang together? Go through the pairs and tell me which ones are actually in tension and which only look like it, rank them by severity, and for each real one say what would have to give and what that costs me — I'd rather give up a verdict than a reason, if that matters, but tell me if it shouldnt. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. My vague feeling is that 5 is the odd one out, but every time I try to say why, the reason I give seems to also sink 1 and 4, so maybe the problem is really 6, or maybe 3 and 1 cant both stay. I genuinely cant tell and I'd rather find out before Wednesday than in front of twelve people. If there's a way of stating 6 that saves my verdicts on 1, 2 and 4 while keeping 5, I want to hear it, and if there isnt, say so plainly.
