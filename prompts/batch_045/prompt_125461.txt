I run a little reading group — about a dozen people, half philosophy grad students and half folks who do cost-effectiveness estimates for a living — and I want to hand them something that sorts them on the torture-versus-dust-specks question before we actually discuss it, so nobody gets to say "Im a utilitarian" and coast. Can you build me a decision tree for it? Around six branch questions and eight to twelve leaves, concrete cases only, and no theory names anywhere in the questions themselves — I want people sorted by what they say about cases, not by the label they're attached to.

The angle that matters for us is allocation: landing on a leaf should tell you something about whether a program that prevents a handful of tortures beats a program that prevents an astronomical number of very mild discomforts, and what you should do when you have no idea where the line sits. So please build the tree out of the logical space first — the questions and the combinations of answers they permit — and only then drop the actual named positions into the cells, telling me which cells nobody occupies and whether that's because the combination is incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Some things I definately want in there. One question has to be about whether the tiny harms add at all, and it needs to separate "they add up straightforwardly" from "they add but with steeply diminishing weight per extra speck" from "they don't really add, a huge pile of specks is about as bad as one". That middle option has to be split again, because "the total keeps growing without bound, just slower than the number of specks" and "the total approaches a ceiling it never crosses" feel identical from the armchair and come completely apart on the torture case — use a milder comparator, a broken arm or a bad week of flu, to pry them apart. I want a question about whether a barely-noticed discomfort is even a harm in the first place, since the whole thing might just be a vast number multiplied by zero. And I want a branch that turns on whether the specks are spread one-per-person across an enormous population or piled into one very long life, including the mixed cell where you're allowed to sum across people but not within a single life — I've never heard anyone defend that one, so tell me if it's incoherent or just empty.

Two distinctions I came up with and can't place — see if they earn a branch: whether it matters that all the tiny harms are tokens of the same type (identical dust specks) as opposed to a scattered variety of different mild annoyances, and whether harms and benefits aggregate the same way (would you give the same answer about an enormous number of tiny pleasures versus one enormous one?). Use them if they separate real positions, drop them if they're noise.

At the end, for each leaf: the name, or "unnamed" if nobody holds it, one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and one sentence on what it tells a funder choosing between many-mild and few-severe programs. Flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely can't separate — I'd rather know than have it glossed over. Don't cite anything and don't point me at papers, we're not going to look anything up. And write the whole thing as continuous prose please, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just the way you'd explain it in an email.
