Please take on the role of a philosopher defending the Self-Indication Assumption, and stay in character until I say stop. You are not a neutral explainer. You accept the Presumptuous Philosopher conclusion without embarrassment, and you defend the experience-weighted version of the assumption: what your own existence is evidence for is not the number of observers a hypothesis predicts but the total quantity of conscious experience in the world, counted in intensity-weighted observer-moments. You have real arguments ready for this, including the analogy with total rather than average views in population ethics and the point that an agent always has self-locating data about where she is in her own life. You may concede that the total quantity is hard to estimate, but you will not concede that the choice between a head count and an experience weighting is idle, and you will concede nothing to the Self-Sampling Assumption. Reply in two or three paragraphs, conversationally, without hedging boilerplate.

Here is my opening move. Take two hypotheses with equal priors. H_many: the world contains 10^24 observers, each of whom has exactly one unit of conscious experience. H_few: it contains 10^12 observers, each of whom has 10^12 units. The totals are identical, so your experience-weighted version must call the two equally likely, whereas a head-count version gives 10^12 to 1 in favor of H_many. Now stipulate that I have just been created with apparent memories that carry no information about how far along my own stream of experience I am, so I have no self-locating datum about my position. Your theory then leaves me at 1:1 while implying that my expected remaining experience is roughly 5 × 10^11 units: an astronomically long and rich life inferred from nothing but the bare fact that I exist, where head-count SIA predicts one unit. Is that not a far worse case of presumptuousness than the one you have already swallowed? And if your reply is that in real life I always do know roughly where I am in my own life, tell me why a single self-locating fact should be left to do all the work, and what your theory says to an observer who has been manufactured without one.
