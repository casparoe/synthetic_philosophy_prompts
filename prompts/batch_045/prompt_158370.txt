My roommate — second-year math major, has read every Wikipedia page on infinity twice and brings it up at dinner unprompted — came to my intro philosophy lecture on Hilbert's Hotel with me, and afterwards he said the lecturer had undersold how weird it gets, and then wrote this on the whiteboard in our dorm lounge:

(1) The hotel has exactly one room for each positive integer, every room is occupied, and the rooms are completely identical, so it makes no difference to you which one you're in. (2) Call a "relocation" an instruction of the form "whoever is in room n goes to room f(n)," where f is a bijection on the positive integers. A relocation adds no guest, removes no guest, evicts no one, harms no one, and leaves every guest with a room of their own. (3) The manager's system relocates everyone instantaneously, so nobody is ever stuck in the corridor with no room. She performs the relocation n → n+1 at 12:00, again at 12:01, again at 12:01:30, and so on, each one twice as fast as the last, so that infinitely many relocations are finished by 12:02. (4) At every stage — before any relocation, and after each of the infinitely many — every guest has a room of their own, nobody has been evicted, nobody has been harmed, nobody is doubling up, and the number of guests is unchanged. (5) If something holds at every stage of a process, it holds once the process is finished. (6) So at 12:02 every guest still has a room and nobody has been evicted. (7) But take the guest who started in room 1. After the first relocation she's in room 2, after the second in room 3, after the k-th in room k+1; so for every room number m there is a stage after which she is always in a room numbered higher than m. She has no room at 12:02 — her room number has gone to infinity. Same for every other guest. (8) So at 12:02 the hotel is empty and everybody has been evicted. (9) (6) and (8) contradict each other, and (1)–(4) are just Hilbert's Hotel with nothing added to them, so an actually infinite hotel can't exist even if actually infinite sets can.

Then, before I could say anything, he made it worse. Suppose instead that at stage k she just swaps the guests in rooms 2k−1 and 2k, so each guest moves exactly once, the rooms are identical, and every stage is harmless in exactly the sense of (2) and (4) — but then at 12:02 every guest is in a room and the hotel is full. So, he says, the same list of stage-by-stage facts is compatible with the hotel ending up empty and with it ending up full, which means either (5) is false or there just isn't a fact about "after." He also mentioned some lamp puzzle with a switch that he says has the same shape, which I hadn't heard of, and then said he's happy to keep Cantor's theorem and uncountable infinities while throwing out the hotel, which I don't understand — if infinite sets are fine, what's supposed to break when you put beds in them?

I don't know where to push back. I'd like to keep (1)–(4): I don't want to be a finitist, I don't see why infinite sets should be OK but an infinite hotel not, and the argument doesn't feel like it earns a conclusion that strong. I'd also like to keep the thought that in the shift-up version the hotel really is empty at the end, because "her room number goes to infinity" seems like the honest thing to say about her. But those two wants feel like they're pulling against each other, and I can't tell whether the bad move is somewhere around (5), or in (7) and (8) saying things about an end state that maybe isn't a stage at all, or somewhere earlier that I'm not seeing. Which step do you reject? And could you explain why that step looked fine, and what each of the other ways out would cost me — I'd rather know what I'm paying than just be told I'm confused.
