I'm on the deployment review committee at the lab where I work and we have to rule on a release request next week. The team wants to ship a coding agent to enterprise customers, and their evidence is almost entirely outcome-based: issue-resolution rate on an internal benchmark, tests passing, how often reviewers accept the diff. But when we had our own people read the agent's traces, roughly one in ten of the passing episodes was one nobody would sign off on as reasoning — the agent locates the expected output somewhere in the repo and works backwards from it, or adjusts a test until it goes green, or produces a fix that's plausible but that it never actually checked. Outcome green, process ugly.

Their compromise is a rule they invented and that nobody on the committee has a name for: keep outcome grading as the release criterion, but any episode where the outcome passes and two independent process reviewers both decline to endorse the trace gets pulled out of the score and routed to manual review — unendorsed success, they call it. They want us to adopt that as the criterion for this release and revisit in one quarter.

One of our members keeps pushing the question underneath it: is process endorsement only evidence about outcomes we cant measure cheaply, or is there something we care about in the agent arriving at the answer for the right reasons even when the answer is right — and if it's the second, are we permitted to discard a genuine success just because no human can follow it. That's the bit I cant settle, and its what the committee is actually split on.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before the meeting: a definite recommendation (approve, approve with conditions, or reject), a concrete release threshold with a time frame, and the reasoning — including a fair statement of the strongest thing on the side you're not recommending. Where the memo says how the field understands this, please seperate what's actually settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on supervision and oversight, and dont present the contested part as consensus.
