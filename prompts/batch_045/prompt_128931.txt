I've read the usual popular treatments of Bell's inequality — the socks, the twins, the "no local hidden variables" slogan — and what keeps nagging at me is that everyone stares at the word *local* while *realism* and *free will* do all the work. So I'd like a story rather than an explanation.

Set it in a world where the no-conspiracy assumption is false in a way that is public, boring, and measurable. Their entangled pairs come from a natural source they call the cascade, which also emits a slow classical signal — the herald — that reaches each station a few minutes before the experimenter there picks a detector setting. The herald carries partial information about the hidden state of the pairs. And this is the reproducible fact in their logs, year after year: the settings the experimenters end up choosing are correlated with the herald. Nobody is being controlled; there is no puppet master; the correlation is just there, the way a confound is there in a badly designed trial. It is strong enough that their raw Bell violation is worthless as evidence.

I want the drama to come from what they do next, not from a twist. Give me characters who actually know the theorem. Have them work out whether the correlation they can measure could even in principle account for the size of the violation they see; whether choosing a setting on the basis of a herald differs in kind from choosing it on the basis of a bribe; and whether a correlation with an ordinary local common-cause explanation in the past deserves the word "conspiracy" at all. Have at least one character press the point that nothing in the data lets either station signal to the other, and ask what is left of nonlocality once that is granted. Nobody gets to settle anything by slogan — not "superdeterminism is unfalsifiable", not "the wavefunction is just real", not "many worlds dissolves this".

After the story, return a JSON object with exactly these fields: "premise" (one sentence: this world's departure from ours); "device" (the cascade and herald: what information the herald carries and how the experimenters get it); "best_case_for_locality" (2–3 sentences, the strongest argument a character gives that no local model has been ruled out); "best_case_against" (2–3 sentences, the strongest argument that something nonlocal or otherwise costly is still forced); "load_bearing_assumption" (the one assumption the disagreement actually turns on — measurement independence, parameter independence, outcome independence, counterfactual definiteness, or single-outcome — plus one sentence saying why); "open_question" (one sentence on what the reader should still be unsure about); and "our_world_parallel" (one sentence mapping the setup onto the actual Bell debate).
