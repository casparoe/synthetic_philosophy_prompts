Draft the joint statement that a Mises–Hayek critic of socialist calculation and a contemporary Langean could both sign at the end of a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

State the Langean side in its strongest available form, not in the original trial-and-error version: nobody proposes that a board adjusts prices by rule of thumb until shortages disappear. Suppose instead that the planning board maintains a continuously re-estimated simulator of the whole economy — a learned model that, given a price vector and a set of proposed production plans, predicts demands and availabilities — and uses it to iterate prices until predicted excess demand vanishes for every good, capital goods included, with claims on whole productive projects allocated through an internal futures market whose contracts pay off only in consumption goods, so that no private property in the means of production comes into existence. Separate the two arguments on the other side and give each its own standing: the monetary-calculation argument, that without genuine market prices for factors of production there is no common scale on which to compare heterogeneous multi-period production plans, and the knowledge argument, that much of the relevant knowledge is local, temporary, and generated only inside competitive rivalry, so that it is not the sort of thing that could be collected and fed to a simulator at all.

Deliver: a numbered section of claims both would sign — about what general-equilibrium theory does and does not establish, about what the simulator would have to contain and where its inputs come from, about what any historical episode could and could not show here; then a table of the residual disagreements, each stated in wording its own side would accept; then, for each disagreement, the crux beneath it and a concrete case or thought experiment both sides agree would bear on that crux, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Say explicitly which parts of the dispute, if any, are merely verbal — in particular whether "calculation" is doing two different jobs — and say whether the futures-market variant is offered as an answer to the monetary-calculation argument, to the knowledge argument, or to something else neither of them has quite named.
