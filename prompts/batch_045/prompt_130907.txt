I'm marking a short-answer question for a graduate seminar on anthropic reasoning and I'd like a second reader. Below is the question, including the conventions the students were instructed to use, followed by one student's response. Please assign a mark out of 20 and, more importantly, write out the specific comments you would put in the margin: what is correct, what is incorrect and why, which errors are merely imprecise versus load-bearing, and what the answer would have had to say to earn full marks. Keep the comments keyed to parts (a) through (e) rather than rewriting the answer as a whole.

Question

State the self-sampling assumption (SSA) and the self-indication assumption (SIA) and put them to work. Use the following conventions throughout: for SSA, take the reference class to be all observers who ever exist, anywhere (in part (a), follow the Doomsday argument's usual practice of taking it to be all humans and ignoring other observers); for SIA, take a hypothesis to be weighted in proportion to the number of observers it says exist; and treat the two together as a package, with SIA supplying the update from the fact that you exist at all and SSA the update from which observer you are.

(a) Explain why SSA is held to support the Doomsday argument and why SIA is held to cancel it.
(b) State the Presumptuous Philosopher case and explain why it is thought to tell against SIA.
(c) The Sealed Annex. Two cosmological theories, Sml and Big, are equally well supported by every piece of evidence you could in principle obtain. Both say that our region of the cosmos contains 10^9 observers in subjectively identical situations, each with total evidence E. They differ only about a region sealed off from ours forever: Big says it contains a further 10^18 observers, each with total evidence F; Sml says it is empty. You know that your own total evidence is E rather than F. Give the likelihood ratio Big : Sml delivered by SSA alone, by SIA alone, and by the package, and show how you get each.
(d) The same, except that the sealed region's 10^18 observers also have total evidence E: they cannot tell whether they are in our region or the sealed one, and neither can you. Which of your three answers change, and what are the new values?
(e) What does the contrast between (c) and (d) show about what is really doing the work in the Presumptuous Philosopher case?

Student's answer

SSA says you should reason as if you were a random sample drawn from the observers in your reference class: all actually existing observers, past, present and future. SIA says you should reason as if you were a random sample from all possible observers, so that a hypothesis on which more observers exist gets proportionally more weight before you ask which of them you are.

(a) The Doomsday argument works like this. Let N be the total number of humans who ever live. My evidence includes my birth rank, and on SSA the chance of finding myself at the rank I occupy is 1/N, which is larger when N is small. So SSA shifts the odds towards early doom by roughly the ratio of the two candidate totals; if doom soon gives N = 100 billion and doom late gives N = 100 trillion, that is a factor of about a thousand. SIA cancels this exactly: it multiplies each hypothesis by the number of observers it posits, which is the same N, so the two factors of N cancel and no Doomsday shift survives in the package.

(b) In the Presumptuous Philosopher case, scientists have narrowed cosmology down to two theories that fit all the available evidence equally well, but one of them says the universe contains enormously more observers than the other. SIA then tells you to favour that theory, by the ratio of the two headcounts, on the basis of no new evidence whatever: the philosopher derives a cosmological conclusion from an armchair argument about how many observers there are. The presumptuousness objection is really an objection to any principle that lets the total number of observers move your credences independently of the evidence, which is why it is not specific to SIA.

(c) SSA alone: under Big only 10^9 out of the total 10^18 + 10^9 observers have evidence E, so the probability of finding myself with E is about 10^-9, while under Sml it is 1. SSA therefore favours Sml by about 10^9. SIA alone points the other way, since Big posits roughly 10^9 times as many observers in total, so SIA favours Big by about 10^9. In the package, SSA contributes nothing further: once I conditionalize on having E, I am a random draw from among the E-observers, and there are equally many of those under the two theories, so SSA's likelihoods match and only SIA's factor is left standing. The package therefore favours Big by about 10^9, reproducing the Presumptuous Philosopher verdict.

(d) Now SSA does some work. There are 10^18 additional observers with my evidence under Big, so SSA favours Big by about 10^9 on top of the 10^9 that SIA's headcount already supplies. The package therefore favours Big by about 10^18.

(e) The contrast shows that the presumptuous verdict is robust: what drives it is the sheer number of observers a theory posits, and the sealed annex makes SIA look worse rather than better, because Big gets its boost even though the annex observers are ones I could never have been and could never have observed. What matters for SIA is how many observers exist, not what evidence they enjoy.
