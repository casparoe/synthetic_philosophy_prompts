Write me a deadpan government white paper -- five-year plan, numbered clauses, around 650 words -- from the Office for Ladder Position of a made-up country (give it a bland northern name) that has put itself on a crash program to top the World Happiness Report.

The jokes have to come out of the real methodology rather than out of cartoon dystopia, so get this stuff right and build on it: the ranking is the three-year rolling average of a single survey item, the Cantril ladder, where people rate their current life 0 to 10 against the best possible life for them; the samples are nationally representative but only about a thousand respondents a year; the reports own six-variable breakdown (income, having someone to count on, healthy life expectancy, freedom, generosity, corruption) is a regression across country-years and not a causal recipe; the ladder is a judgement about your life, not how you feel moment to moment, so a country can climb while nothing that anyone would call happiness improves; and the whole thing is a ranking, so you can also rise by standing still.

Given all that, the ministrys flagship program should exploit a genuine feature of the measure -- I'm thinking an "aspiration hygiene" curriculum that trains citizens to picture a more modest best-possible life, which should lift the national score without lifting anything else -- and you should invent one more scheme of your own, ideally one that works off the sample size or the rolling average rather than off peoples actual lives.

Near the end let the memo accidentally say out loud that what's being maximized is the number and not anyone's wellbeing, without the memo ever noticing it did that. Include one short quote from a skeptical outside statistician in the national press. Keep the prose flat and civil-service throughout, no winking, no dystopian cliches, and keep it readable for someone who only half-remembers the rankings.
