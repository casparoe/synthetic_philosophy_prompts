My supervisor is a hardline real-patterns Dennettian: she thinks the question "is the intentional pattern here real, or is the intentional stance just a useful façon de parler" has a right answer case by case, and over the years she's ruled on a pile of them. Her verdicts, as near as I can reconstruct them from supervision notes:

- brute-force chess engine: real — thin and shallow, but real, because you can't predict its moves without saying what it wants and what it thinks the position threatens
- honeybee waggle dance: real
- prediction-market prices: real, even though there's no agent there at all
- an ant colony's foraging trails: real, and she always stresses that when you move the food the trails re-route, and that's what makes it a pattern rather than a summary
- a retrieval chatbot that just replays the most similar conversation from a big corpus: not real
- a 2023-era text-only language model: not real — the stance is seductive, but the "beliefs" are only a compression of the corpus

I need her verdict on the system my chapter is about and I'd rather guess it before I ask. It's a big agent built by iterated self-distillation: a model acts in a simulated office, you train a new model to predict the first one's actions, repeat for a dozen generations, then do a little RL on top. Findings so far: within the training distribution the intentional stance is by far the cheapest predictor ("it thinks the report is due Friday and it doesn't want to look incompetant"), nobody has found a design-stance description that compresses as well, and it generalises fine to held-out offices from the same generator. Two other results though. When the reseachers break a correlation the generator always carried — say, the one between the coffee machine being full and the printer working — the behaviour comes apart from the beliefs you'd attributed, in exactly the way a shortcut learner's would. And two lineages trained the same way with different seeds come out behaviourally indistinguishable, while the same interpretability method reads incompatible contents off their internals.

Does she say the pattern is real? I can see at least three rules that fit her six verdicts: real iff the stance buys compression you can't get from the design stance; real iff the pattern is projectible, i.e. survives interventions and off-distribution scrambles; real iff the attribution isn't underdetermined by twin interpretations. Which way does each go on my case, which one is she most likely actually running given how she talks about the ant colony, and what single further case would pry apart whichever two disagree here?
