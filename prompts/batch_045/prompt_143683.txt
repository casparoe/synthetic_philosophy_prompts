I teach jurisprudence in a philosophy department, and I have agreed to write a short commentary on the separate opinions of a judge who retired last year from our highest court. Her record on the four cases everyone cites is as follows:

1. A denunciation prosecution left over by the previous regime, where the decree relied on had never been promulgated and was applied retroactively: she held it was not law at all, so the conviction had to rest on the ordinary criminal law and no retrospective statute was needed.
2. A murdering heir under a wills act containing no exception for wrongdoers: she disinherited him, while conceding that the enacted words did not compel the result and describing the court as completing a scheme rather than applying a detached sentence.
3. A prosecution under a nineteenth-century prohibition whose terms plainly covered the defendant but which had gone unenforced for forty years: she stayed it, on the ground that a norm officials have systematically declined to enforce has, through that very practice, ceased to be law, although nothing was ever repealed.
4. The code of another jurisdiction, which was general, promulgated, clear, prospective, internally consistent, possible to obey, stable, and administered exactly as written, but substantively brutal: she held it was law without qualification, adding that if that verdict disappoints us, the disappointment belongs to our politics rather than to our theory of law.

The case now before her court is this. Two years ago the jurisdiction, as part of a reform that abolished preambles, purpose clauses, and any resort to legislative history, enacted a code whose provisions were generated by a commissioned drafting system that expands a short list of policy parameters approved by the assembly into full statutory text; the assembly votes on each block of text as produced and cannot amend it. The provision in issue imposes strict, no-fault liability on a class of defendants, unambiguously on its face; the provision it replaced in the old code required fault; the parameters the assembly approved refer only to compensation and deterrence for culpable conduct; the new wording appears to be an artifact of a drafting pattern common in the corpus the system was trained on rather than the record of any decision; nobody noticed the change until the first defendant was sued; and the assembly has been dissolved for an election and cannot correct the text before judgment.

I want to know whether she will give the provision its literal effect. Please set out the two or three rules about the identification and interpretation of law that fit all four of her earlier verdicts, say what each predicts here, tell me which one you think she is actually running and which is the closest rival, and give me the further case, real or hypothetical, that would separate them. I am especially interested in the wrinkle that the enacted words have no author behind them: does that favour the positivist reading, on which statutory meaning is fixed by linguistic convention and the jurisdiction's acknowledged canons rather than by anyone's intentions, or does it favour the purposive reading, on which there is here no enterprise whose point could guide a faithful reading at all? And what status should the approved parameter list have on either view? If you think her opinions genuinely underdetermine the outcome, say what she would be committing herself to by answering either way.
