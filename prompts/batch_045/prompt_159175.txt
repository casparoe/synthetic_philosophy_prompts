I'm in one of those group chats where about a dozen of us size real positions on a prediction market, and a few of the guys are all-in on Kelly, geometric rationality, growth-rate talk, whatever you want to call it — and we've now had essentially the same argument for two years without anybody moving a milimeter. The concrete case we keep circling: roughly every day there's a bet at somewhere near even money where our own forecasts say we're 55–60% to win, we'll get maybe 200–300 of these over a couple of years, we always stake a fixed fraction of current bankroll, and the only question is what fraction. Camp one says: stake the fraction that maximizes the growth rate of the bankroll, i.e. the log-optimal fraction; if that feels too violent, bet half of it, but be honest that half-Kelly is a haircut for nerves or for uncertainty about your own edge, not a rival theory. Camp two says: that's just expected-utility maximization with one particular utility function, and nothing about having many bets ahead of you forces that function on you — the number of repetitions and your attitude to risk are separate inputs, and "you'll get hundreds of these" is not an argument that the log is special. Camp one comes back with: no, you're changing the subject, growth rate is a property of the single trajectory you actually live on, whereas taking an expectation of utility averages over parallel copies of you whose winnings nobody ever collects. Everyone has heard all the standard moves and nobody bites on any of them: Kelly maximizes the median and not the mean; Kelly's dominance is only asymptotic and says nothing on a finite run; on the very last bet the growth argument literally has no content; any concave utility gives you some fraction, so "Kelly" just names the log one; log utility will make you refuse genuinely positive-EV gambles; and fractional Kelly is either an admission or a different utility, depending on who's talking.

My working hypothesis, which I want you to shoot at rather than flatter, is that this isn't a fight about rationality at all but a fight about the horizon that nobody states: the Kelly people are implicitly modelling us as having effectively unboundedly many more bets, so the asymptotic and time-average results carry all the weight, and the utility people are modelling a known finite run with an actual last bet, where those results are silent — and the whole "log versus some other concave function" dispute is downstream of that. But I can also see it being: a verbal dispute where "optimal" is left unattached to a quantity; two different questions sharing a name, since we do pool our bankroll and one member cares about the group total while another cares about his own drawdown, and those give different answers even on identical beliefs; or just bedrock temperament about variance that no argument touches.
