I am writing a novel and need a short story to drop into it as a self-contained set piece: a piece of fiction that carries a philosophical argument in its bones rather than in its dialogue. The material I want dramatized is the problem of individual contributions to collective harms — the thought that any one person's contribution is too small to matter, whatever exactly "matter" is supposed to mean there.

**The setting (mine; please keep it as given).** A valley city heats itself with a cheap, dirty fuel whose winter smoke is known, with reasonable confidence, to cause a certain number of early deaths per season — call it one statistical death per N households' worth of burning. The city runs an Office of Marginal Effects, which issues every household an annual "difference number": the expected fraction of a statistical death attributable to that household's own burning. Two rules of the Office are invented for the novel and do a lot of work, so please respect them exactly:

1. *Rounding.* Difference numbers are published to three decimal places. Anything smaller is printed as 0.000, and the household is officially classed as "without marginal effect."
2. *Carry and tipping.* The unpublished remainders are not discarded. They accrue to a common pool, and the pool is settled in whole units: whenever the city's cumulative total crosses into a new statistical death, that death is charged in full to whichever household's burning happened to be the last to push the total over the line. That household gets a printed notice naming it the tipping household.

**The story I want.** Nine hundred to fourteen hundred words, third person, past tense, in American English. Its protagonist is a school custodian in her sixties who has burned the cheap fuel for years and has always justified it by her difference number: it is 0.000, and she takes the rounding seriously as a moral fact, not as an excuse. In the story she receives a tipping notice. Give me one concrete scene — the notice arriving, and a conversation with someone who presses her about it (a grandson, a neighbor, a colleague at the Office, your choice) — and let the drama come out of the gap between two claims she can now make: that her own burning was the thing that completed a death, and that had she refrained, some other household's burning would have completed it instead, so nothing she could have done would have prevented it. Do not resolve this into a verdict. I want the reader left holding both thoughts, and I want the ending to be a decision or an image rather than an argument. Keep the prose free of philosophers' jargon; the analysis goes in the table below, not in the characters' mouths. Please do not build the story on any empirical claim about real-world pollution, climate, or mortality figures — the valley city is invented, and the numbers in it are my fiction.

**After the story, a table of margin notes.** Add a row for each place where the story leans on a substantive thesis, in this order of columns:

- *Move in the story* — the line, scene, or plot turn that carries the weight.
- *The thesis it leans on* — one sentence, in your own words. If you are confident the thesis has a standard name in the literature, you may name it, but do not cite authors, papers, or dates; I have no use for references here.
- *Status* — exactly one of: settled among people who work on this; genuinely contested among them; invented for this story.
- *Strongest objection* — one sentence on the best thing someone could say against it.

I care about the *Status* column more than the others: I would like you to separate what people who actually know this material agree on from what is genuinely up for grabs among them, and not to dress up a contested position as the consensus — my narrator will be unreliable enough on his own. Finally, a short closing paragraph under the heading *Liberties*, noting where the story takes liberties with the ideas or rests on a contested reading of them, and flagging any point at which my invented rounding and carry-and-tipping rules quietly do philosophical work that the real debates would not license.
