So I've somehow ended up owning "culture" at a ~180-person remote-first company, and I've got about a year, no extra headcount, and basically three levers: what gets said out loud in incident reviews and retros, how onboarding frames our norms, and which behaviors senior people visibly model. I can't fire my way to a norm and I can't run anything with more than maybe n=40 per arm.

I keep coming back to the old anthropological guilt-culture / shame-culture distinction. I know that anthropology is way too coarse (and probably shouldn't be trusted much at all at that scale), but the pracitcal version feels real to me: do people comply because they'd feel bad about the act, or because they'd feel bad about being seen — and can an organization deliberately pick one, or does it just drift?

Two things I've been going back and forth on, which I want slotted into your answer rather than ignored:

- leakage: my sense is shame is alot cheaper to install but generalizes to stuff the target never chose (accent, body, family, neurotype), whereas guilt stays closer to the act and to a magnitude. If that's right, is the whole question just "how bad are the tails," and is that even measurable at my n?
- reintegration/forgetting: the non-stigmatizing kind of shame needs some public ritual of re-acceptance, but we're a Slack company with searchable history and screenshots, so the audience never dissolves. That makes me think the real crux isn't guilt vs. shame at all — it's whether I can build any working forgetting mechanism, and if I can't, the medium has already picked for me and the rest is decoration.

What I actually want is an agenda, not a literature review and definately not reading recs. Which open questions here are worth my year, and in what order? Give me a dependency-aware ranking: which questions are upstream of which (is the medium-determinism thing upstream of everything, or downstream of leakage?), which are tractable with the three levers I have, and which I should drop because the answer wouldn't change what I do — I suspect "is shame ever fitting/appropriate in the abstract" is one of those. And what would I observe over the next few quarters that should move something up or down the list?

Also please flag anything the guilt/shame framing makes me think I have to choose between that I actually don't — enforcement via in-group pride, or plain "this rule is annoying but I've internalized it anyway" compliance, or whatever else I'm not seeing. I'd rather hear about what's not on my list than get my existing two ideas steelmanned.
