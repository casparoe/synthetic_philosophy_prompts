I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, reading von Neumann's short posthumous book The Computer and the Brain on my own, and one strand of it has me stuck: his contrast between a computer's fast, high-precision arithmetic and the brain's slow components, enormous parallelism and very low precision per operation, together with his claim that what the nervous system runs on is better described as a statistically defined code than as anything like logic or mathematics.

Please write a dialogue of roughly 700–900 words between two equally competent engineers who disagree about what follows from this. One reads von Neumann as having more or less predicted modern machine learning: intelligence is what falls out of slow, low-precision, massively parallel, statistical machinery, and the fact that trained networks now do what they do is confirmation of that picture. The other reads him as issuing a warning instead: the brain's signals are only statistically defined because nothing outside the system fixes what they mean, whereas a trained network is built on exact arithmetic, a precisely specified objective and a precisely specified training signal, so the resemblance, if there is one, lives at the hardware level and nowhere else.

Have them genuinely engage rather than trade set-piece speeches: each should restate the other's position in the form they would themselves accept, concede what really should be conceded, and press on the point where the disagreement actually bites. As a shared test case halfway through, give them this: suppose we could reimplement someone's neural signalling using exact, deterministic, high-precision arithmetic — same connectivity, same timings, every noisy quantity replaced by its expected value — and the resulting system's behaviour came badly apart. Have each say what that outcome would and would not establish about von Neumann's claim, and whether it should change their mind.

Keep the language plain and avoid terminology I wouldn't have; don't let either character win outright; and don't have either one cite passages, page numbers or secondary literature I would have to chase down.
