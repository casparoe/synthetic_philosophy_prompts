I'm the TA for a grad seminar on probabilistic reasoning in philosophy, and the last couple of sessions have been on heuristic arguments — you know the shape: you want one headline number, so you name the half dozen things that all have to go right, slap a rough probability on each, and multiply. The running example we made up is about the odds that a biosphere on a given kind of planet gets all the way to something we could already have picked up: a world that stays habitable, chemistry that starts, cells, complexity, signals we'd recognize, and a window long enough that anyone's listening — six links, multiply them out, get a number small enough to make everyone in the room uncomfortable. The fight has been about whether there's any principled default of presuming independence when you've got no positive evidence of correlation. My office mate says the whole move is just maximum entropy in a trenchcoat; I half agree, except maxent isn't invariant under how you carve up the space, so it seems to just push the problem back one step rather than solve it. I want to close the term with a poem instead of another summary slide, and I could use some help with it.

What I have in mind is a sestina — six stanzas of six, plus the three-line envoi — where the six end-words are the six links of the chain. The rotation should be doing the arguing: as the words cycle through their slots, the reader should quietly notice that permuting the factors changes nothing, since multiplication commutes, so all that shuffling was never where the risk was. Then the envoi, where all six words get crammed into three lines instead of spread across six stanzas, should be the collapse — the moment you see that the six "independent" links were really six measurements of one shared parameter, one rate you don't know, and that the product of the means and the mean of the product are not the same number, and the gap between them gets wider the more links you bolt on, not narrower. I'd also like it to land the point that the presumption isn't neutral: if the links are positively associated, the product formula is a lower bound, so "presuming independence" is quietly taking a side rather than withholding judgment.

Two more things I'd want it to get right, because a couple of people in that room will notice if it's mushy. First, independence is partition-relative: factorization can hold inside every cell of a partition and fail marginally, and it can also fail conditional on a common effect even when the causes are independent — so "presume independence" has to name the partition, and when you buy conditional independence by conditioning on the shared parameter, you haven't killed the correlation, you've relocated it into the prior over that parameter, which is now doing all the work. Second, I'd like the poem to end on that relocation rather than resolve it — my own hunch is that the presumption is a bookkeeping convention about where you're allowed to park your ignorance, and the parking spot has a shape, and that shape is what the number is really about. Don't define any of the technical stuff inside the verse; it should work as a poem for someone who's never seen a factorized prior, and be recognizably correct for someone who has. Start with the poem itself, no preface or "here's a sestina" line before it; put any notes about which end-words you picked and what the shared parameter is after, and if a sestina definately can't carry all this, give it an honest try first and tell me in the note what you'd use instead.
