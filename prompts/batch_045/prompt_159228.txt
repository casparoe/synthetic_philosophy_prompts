My friend Priya died last week and I'm giving the eulogy on Saturday. She was a Hofstadter person in a way that genuinely mattered to her: she thought the self wasn't a thing tucked inside the skull but a strange loop the system keeps re-entering, with 'I' just the symbol the loop uses to point back at itself. And she had this further idea I've never seen anyone else make, that the loop doesn't stop at the skull either — a lab, a marriage, a choir can host part of it. When someone dies the symbol doesn't vanish; the scaffolding just moves outside, and other people's talk and habits keep feeding it. She called that the borrowed loop.

Me and Dave argued about it for three hours on Friday night and got nowhere. His line is that the borrowed loop is just "she lives on in our hearts" with extra machinery: a pattern in my head has none of Priya's experience and can't do anything, so calling it Priya is sentiment with footnotes. I sort of think he's right and sort of think he's missing the whole point. And I never got Priya to say whether the borrowed loop was conscious or just a pattern that had learned to use her name.

So write me six or seven minutes. The room is half her cognitive science and philosophy collegues, who'll catch every self-reference joke, and half family, including her very religious sister, who will hear any of this as "she was just a machine." It can't say she's at peace or watching over us, and it can't say nothing of her is left — she'd have rejected both, and the second one would wreck her mother. If you can manage it, be honest that I don't know which of me and Dave is right, without turning the thing into a seminar. British English please.
