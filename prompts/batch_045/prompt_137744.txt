Hi — practical question, and one that's come up for me a lot in the last year. I'm one of four people on the grant committee of a small family foundation (we give away maybe 400k a year), and someone walked us through that three-factor way of comparing causes: how big and bad the problem is, whether more money actually makes a dent in it, and how underfunded it already is. We've been trying to genuinely use it instead of just nodding along, and I keep running into something I don't have a word for, and its driving me nuts.

Here's the thing. The third factor behaves differently from the other two. If we put 50k into something and it works, the size of the problem goes down a bit, the "does money help" factor is basically unchanged. But the underfunding score collapses relative to the money we put in — and what's stranger is that it collapses when we only *talk*. Two episodes from this year:

1. We found a small program that scored well on all three, exactly the kind of thing you'd expect nobody else to have spotted yet. Two of us wrote a short internal note explaining why. A committee member forwarded the note to a friend at a much bigger funder, and inside a few months that funder had moved in with an amount that dwarfed ours. Great for the cause, genuinely. But our next 50k there is now worth far less by our own framework, and the framework now points somewhere else. So doing the analysis properly and telling anyone about it is what made the recommendation expire.

2. Related but I think separate: we've noticed we now have a reason to be vague in our public writeups, or to just not mention the specific gap we found, which feels completely backwards for a grantmaking body that's supposed to be transparent about why it funds what it funds. We didn't set out to be secretive. The framework quietly recommended it.

Is there a name for this? Diminishing returns isn't it — returns per dollar haven't obviously dropped, it's the diagnosis that dropped. Someone muttered "Goodhart's law" at me; I read around a bit and that seems to be about a measure becoming a target and then getting gamed, but nobody here is gaming anything, and the measure isn't corrupted so much as used up by the act of acting on it. A friend in finance said "alpha decay," which sounds closer, but I can't tell if that's a real term or desk slang.

I don't only want a label though. I want to know whether this is one thing or several: (a) the recommendation going stale, (b) the analysing and telling being part of what makes it stale, not a side effect of the money, and (c) the pull toward silence. For each, what's the nearest established concept, and where exactly does the fit break? If the combined thing has no name, coin one, define it, and say what would and wouldn't count as a case of it. And one more: does the staleness mean we've specified the third factor wrong — should it have been about other people's money *given* what we ourselves are going to do — or is it just a fact about the world that a committee like ours has to live with?
