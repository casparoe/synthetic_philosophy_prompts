what i want is a procedure, not an explainer. i already know the slogan version of heritability and it hasnt been enough — last month i nearly ran a line implying that because something is heritable an intervention cant work. give me a checklist i can actually run in 20 minutes on an abstract + press release: the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each given that i cant read the methods section, where the procedure just stops and says "dont print that sentence", and where it hands the decision to a person because the remaining question is about values (whether to run the story at all, whether naming the trait in a headline is worth the misreading risk) rather than facts. give me a one-line safe-rewrite template for each stopping point, because my editor will not read three sentences of caveat.

the stuff i keep tripping on, which the procedure has to handle: heritability is a ratio of variances in one population at one time, not a fact about the trait and definitely not about a person; within-group variance tells you nothing about the cause of a between-group gap; broad vs narrow heritability and the gap between a twin estimate and what a polygenic score actually explains, including what happens to a score when you move it to a different cohort or ancestry; gene-environment correlation (the passive/evocative/active thing) and the equal-environments assumption; and traits whose measurement partly encodes the environment — years of schooling, screen time, number of books at home, or a test that got renormed halfway through the study. also please tell me what to do when the abstract just doesnt report the number a step needs: do i email the author, do i downgrade to a vaguer sentence, or do i drop the line.
