Longtime lurker, finally posting my own profile so people can poke at it — I've had this written up for a while and it feels like it should hang together but I can't tell anymore. Where I've landed on decision theory + alignment:

(1) I'm a payer in the tails branch: even once I know the coin landed heads and I'm in the branch where nothing is at stake, I hand over the money, on the grounds that what's actually being traded on is my decision procedure rather than my action in this branch.
(2) I think that generalizes to acausal threats, and that being a known payer is worth having, in the way being a known cooperator is worth having. The version I actually hold is narrower than the usual one though: I only think the threat is worth paying when the threatener had to simulate me to make it, so the correlation runs through my own source code and not through any fact about the world.
(3) On goals: I think we're stuck with surrogate goals for the foreseeable future. The real target isn't writable down, so we point at the best proxy we can actually point at — approval, a learned reward model, whatever — and accept the gap.
(4) So my answer to the proxy breaking down is to turn the optimization pressure down rather than to specify better: satisfice or quantilize near the top instead of squeezing the proxy for everything it's worth, so we stay in the regime where it tracks the target.
(5) I think corrigibility is the real safety valve and roughly instrumentally convergent — a system that lets us shut it off or rewrite it is the thing to aim at, and this is the part of the plan I'd actually bet on.
(6) I think the "we'll acausally trade with other civilizations" stuff is a mirage and I don't want anything of mine depending on it.

My guess is that the trouble is (1)+(2) versus (6) — that if I really think my source code is the thing being correlated with, I can't then refuse to count on acausal trade with aliens, and I should either pay up on (6) or stop believing (2). But I'm not confident, and the (3)/(4)/(5) block feels fine to me in a way that's starting to look suspicious.
