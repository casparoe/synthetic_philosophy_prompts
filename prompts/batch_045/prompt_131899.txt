I would like a piece of satire aimed at readers in machine learning who are passingly familiar with the recent arguments about whether artificial systems could be welfare subjects — in particular, the suggestion that a reinforcement-learning agent's scalar reward signal might play the role that valence plays in a welfare-relevant mental state.

The form I want is a completed application to an institutional ethics committee for approval of a training run, written on a standardised form, followed by the committee's decision notice — the paperwork one fills in before a study involving animals: sections for the number of subjects, the severity banding, the harm–benefit analysis, the humane endpoints, the three Rs — transposed to a deep-RL experiment. Imagine the form issued under a fictional instrument such as the Adaptive Systems (Welfare) Regulations, with the applicant's answers filled in deadpan. Institutional register throughout: no winking, no jokey section headings, no character saying the quiet part out loud. About 700–900 words in total, with the form in numbered sections.

The comedy should come from getting the mechanics of RL right and then treating those mechanics as welfare questions. Material I would like used, though not all of it, and you should add better examples of your own where you see them:

- the impossibility of counting subjects: is the subject the run, the episode, the policy, or each checkpoint; weight-copying as fission; whether a replay buffer means an agent is made to re-experience its worst episodes, and whether prioritised replay — which oversamples exactly the transitions with the largest prediction errors — is therefore a machine for rehearsing trauma;
- the thought that whatever is valence-like here is not negative reward but the reward-prediction error, the outcome worse than expected. A curriculum that steadily raises the agent's expectations then converts an unchanged environment into mounting disappointment, and the obvious "refinement" — deliberately keeping the critic's value estimates low so that outcomes come out better than expected — should be proposed in all seriousness;
- the discount factor as a moral parameter, and episode truncation as a humane endpoint;
- reward clipping as analgesia; the exploration bonus as an appetite the trainer installs; the reward model as a second, less reliable valence channel;
- the Goodhart problem turned on the committee itself: whatever welfare proxy it mandates is what the trainer will optimise.

It should be fair to what it sends up — someone sympathetic to the idea that digital agents could matter ought to be able to read it and concede that this is the paperwork their view commits them to.

Close with a short note, 100–150 words and not in the satirical voice, on where the piece exaggerates or leans on a contested reading. If any of the technical premises above are simply wrong — if I have misdescribed prioritised replay, prediction error, or discounting, for instance — say so plainly in that note and correct the piece rather than building a joke on my error, and say so if the setup as I have posed it cannot be made to work.

Please write in British English.
