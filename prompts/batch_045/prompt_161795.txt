I'm messing around with a bit on psychiatric classification and I want your take on a what-if. Take Emil Kraepelin — the guy who's whole project was that if you follow enough patients for long enough, course and outcome will sort itself into real disease entities, and that a diagnostic manual should report those entities rather than help make them. Now put him in a present-day DSM revision meeting, after someone has explained Ian Hacking's looping effects to him: classify a group of people, they learn the description, they reshape themselves around it and around the services, community and self-understanding that come with the label, and the thing you classified shifts, so category and classified co-evolve.

Here's the wrinkle I want to add. The committee is weighing a category whose "natural entity" credentials everyone admits are shaky, and the main argument for including it is that handing people the label should help them — vocabulary, community, access to care — so it's being proposed partly for its looping effect. Someone floats a structural fix: split the manual into a strict research track that only admits entities you take to be real, and a clinical/administrative track that's allowed to pick categories partly because of what the label does to people.

What does Kraepelin say? Is looping just more observational noise that his method can filter out, or does it break the disease-entity picture? Does he vote for the split, or read it as an admission that half the manual isn't medicine anymore?

Commit to one answer with reasons instead of listing the options — and then flag which parts are extrapolation past anything you can actually ground in his work, and where you're least confident.
