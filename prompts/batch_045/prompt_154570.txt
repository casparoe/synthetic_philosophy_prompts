I have been writing on the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents and have talked myself into an argument whose conclusion I cannot accept. Recall Löb's theorem: for any theory T at least as strong as Peano arithmetic and any sentence p, if T proves that (if p is provable in T then p), then T proves p. Hence T cannot adopt the reflection schema "provable-in-T(p) implies p" for arbitrary p without proving every sentence and collapsing.

(1) An agent's deliberation can be idealised as a search, in some such T, for derivations of action-consequence sentences C(a,u) — "if I perform a, my expected utility is u" — followed by performance of the a with the best derived u.
(2) A derivation of C(a,u) can guide action only if the agent is prepared to move from "T proves C(a,u)" to "C(a,u)". That transition just is reliance on one's own reasoning, and formalised it is the reflection instance for the sentences that figure in deliberation. Call this requirement Trust.
(3) The action-consequence sentences are not a special, well-behaved class: they range over the agent's own computations and can express arbitrarily strong claims. So Trust is in effect reflection for arbitrary p, and by Löb's theorem it is unavailable — any T enjoying it would prove every C(a,u) and be worthless as a guide. This is the Löbian obstacle.
(4) Nothing in (1)–(3) turns on p being about the agent's own actions. Löb's theorem is fully general in p, and what makes the collapse bite is only that the agent is relying on a derivation that is its own. So the obstacle generalises from self-trust proper to reliance on anything the agent itself produces: its memory, its perceptual system, a proof-checker or compiler it runs, the successor agent it builds.
(5) Therefore no arithmetically capable agent can rationally rely on its own memory, its own senses, its own proof-checker, or its own successors.
(6) But verified proof-checkers, self-hosting compilers and memory-checked systems exist and are routinely relied upon, and we rely on our own reasoning without taking ourselves to be committed to contradiction. (5) is preposterous.
(7) So the Löbian obstacle is not really an obstacle: something in (1)–(4) is false.
