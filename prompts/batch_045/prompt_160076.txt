hey — I'm the student chair of our school's Community Standards Board and I need something we can actually run in a meeting.

the setup: 6 students, one teacher sponsor who can't vote but can veto anything we do, meetings are 45 minutes on wednesday mornings, and we get 2 or 3 petitions a month. The only two things we can do are (1) not give someone a platform — pull a graduation speaking slot, a club officer title, a spot on a funded trip, a role in the fall play — and (2) publish a short statement in the school news with the person's name in it. We can't suspend, fine, or put anything on a transcript, and we can't require anyone to do anything. We can also only act once per incident: whatever we decide is permanent, and we're the only body that can do anything, so when we say no, nothing at all happens to the person.

Whenever anyone says we're punishing people, the board's answer is that we aren't — we're just declining to associate with someone and declining to amplify them, and anyone is free to do that. I think that line is doing alot of work and sometimes it's bs, especially since for stuff like the fall play or graduation we're kind of the only venue there is.

what I want: turn whatever the philosophy of punishment actually says about social sanction into a checklist we can run in 45 minutes. In order: the questions to ask, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure just stops with a don't-do-it, and where the question is really a values call that has to go to a human — and tell us which human, us or the sponsor. Please mark the steps that are honestly just vibes with a checklist around them. Don't give me a reading list or cite anything, we're not writing a paper, I just need the questions, askable by someone with zero training in a Wednesday meeting.

The distinctions I think have to survive, but fix me if I've got them wrong: whether withdrawing something counts as punishment when the thing withdrawn isn't a right but also isn't something the person can get anywhere else; proportionality, and how you even tell what's a big sanction versus a small one; whether the point of what we're doing is to censure, to protect people, or to hurt; whether there's any way back for the person afterward; whether it matters that what they did was private and got leaked; whether it matters that most of the signatures are from people who weren't affected; and whether two hundred people each doing something fine can add up to something that isn't fine. Also: we're 16 and our evidence is screenshots, so tell us what to do when we genuinely can't work out what happened.

then run it on three, including at least one where the answer is one we won't like:

1. a senior whose speaking slot at graduation people want pulled. Two years ago, when he was 14, he used a slur in a private group chat; a screenshot just surfaced. He says it was a joke between friends and hasn't really addressed it.
2. a club president who keeps making comments to members of her own club, over months. She won't apologize but says she'll stop. The petition asks for both: strip the title and publish the statement.
3. a junior who made one dumb joke in a tiktok last year. The petition specifically asks only for the statement, and says we're not asking you to take anything away from him — so we were going to agree, because it felt like the soft option.

and at the end tell us what the procedure can't do: where it'll misfire, and where it just doesn't apply. thanks
