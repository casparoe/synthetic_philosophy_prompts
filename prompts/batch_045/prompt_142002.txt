The Machian reply is that it's rotation relative to the distant stars: take the stars away and there's no fact of the matter about whether its rotating. But then Newton apparently has this second example, two balls joined by a cord floating in an otherwise completely empty universe, where the tension in the cord is supposed to tell you they're revolving — and that one seems to me to knock out the Machian reply entirely, because there aren't any other bodies around to be relative to. Is there an answer to that, or is that example just accepted as decisive?

That makes spacetime more of a substance than Newton ever claimed, so substantivalism wins. I can't work out which reading is the right one, or whether they're even talking about the same question.
