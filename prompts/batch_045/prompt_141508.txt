I'm a novelist, stuck halfway through a book, and what I need is a short story I can maybe steal a scene from. It has to do a specific job. My character — a retired insurance underwriter who still settles bets for people at the dog track for beer money — gets Dutch-booked: a run of wagers, each at odds he'd call fair or better if you offered it to him on its own, that together lock in a loss no matter how things turn out.

Two wrinkles I want built into the plot, not narrated around. First: nobody plans the book. The bets come from four or five different people over a week who've never met, and the guaranteed loss only appears when you put them side by side — an accident of aggregation, not a con. Does that accidental book damn him the way a deliberate one does, or does the argument secretly need a bookie who's out to get you? Second: the last bet in the chain is one he can only almost take. Say the sure loss requires him accepting wagers numbered 1, 2, 3, out to infinity, and he dies at bet nine hundred. Is a loss that only shows up in the limit still a reason to call him irrational now?

Let him argue this out with the people he's betting with, in his own stubborn vocabulary — no narrator voice explaining the philosophy, and please dont tie it off with a tidy moral about how we're all irrational deep down. Under 1200 words, and I'd like the last beat to be him deciding which single bet he'd take back, if he could take back exactly one.
