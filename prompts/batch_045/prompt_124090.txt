I'm in a second-year metaphysics reading group, and last week's session was on the question of why there is anything at all. One member — very fast, very confident, and fond of ending discussions — kept deploying the old tag "nothing comes from nothing" (by the end he had put the Latin version, ex nihilo nihil fit, on the whiteboard) as though it settled the topic. He used it to do four things in a row: rule out any story on which something first began to exist with nothing prior; infer from that that something has always existed; infer from that that there was never a time at which nothing obtained, and hence that the *rather than nothing* half of our question never describes a genuine alternative; and finally rule out the brute-fact answer — that the universe simply is, and there is no explanation of its being — on the grounds that a brute fact is something-from-nothing in explanatory clothing. I want to hand him a one-page response before next session, but every time I try to write it I get stuck, because I can't tell what the slogan is even claiming, and each of his four steps seems to need a different version of it.

So I'd like a menu of the distinct precise theses it could be expressing, and I have some axes in mind that I want kept apart rather than merged. First, subject matter: whether *nothing* is functioning as a quantifier — so that the tag is just a general claim about events, causes, and materials, and does not commit anyone to there being such a thing as the null state — or as a name for a condition, so that the tag is the substantive claim that the null state is infertile and that nothing can get a transition out of it started. Second, kind of dependence: is it causal, or explanatory and grounding, and does the difference matter, given that a universe with no first moment might still have an ontological ground, or lack one? Third, strength: is it a claim about what in fact happens, or about what could happen; and if the latter, does it give us a necessary being or merely a sempiternal one? Fourth, whether it is a thesis about origins at all, or a covert simplicity or probability thesis — I suspect he is quietly sliding in something like *the empty world is the default, so the existence of anything is what stands in need of explanation*, which looks logically independent of any causal claim. Fifth, the wrinkle raised by the one theist in the group, who says her tradition affirms the world's coming to be out of nothing and that the tag governs only making (producing one thing out of another) and not creating; I can't tell whether that is a dodge or a distinction the slogan has to respect.

For each reading on the menu I'd like, ideally in a compact table: a one- or two-sentence statement precise enough that I could deny it while accepting the others; a verdict — trivial, true but too weak to do any work, substantive and defensible, or substantive but question-begging; and a note on which of his four steps that reading would actually license, with the weakest link in the chain flagged even under the strongest reading you can construct. Please also say which reading you think people who repeat the tag are normally expressing. Two further suggestions came up that I'd like placed somewhere: one member said the real content is that the empty world is a fixed point, since there is nothing there for any law or causal process to operate on, and that this is a further thesis rather than a restatement; another said that if numbers or other abstract objects exist of necessity, then the empty world is not even possible and the question collapses for free long before anyone reaches the proverb. I'd like to know whether either of those is genuinely the same claim as the slogan or a separate one wearing its clothes, and if a coherent reading occurs to you that nobody in the room defended, include it as an unoccupied slot. This is going to be a handout rather than an essay, so I don't need sources, quotation, or further reading — please spend the space on the distinctions themselves.
