I sit on the admissions committee of a small intentional community that farms a fixed plot of land. There are 40 of us now, and by any reasonable standard each of us leads a very good life. We have to decide how many applicants to admit this year. The land's yield barely rises with extra hands, and everything is shared equally, so per-person consumption falls as we grow: at roughly 60 members everyone's life is still clearly good, by 200 it is merely comfortable, and somewhere near 400 it becomes, by our own judgment, worse than never having joined at all. Almost everyone who applies would otherwise remain in the nearby town, where life is decent but noticeably harder.

A majority of the committee defends admitting as many as we can, in two steps. First: an extra member gets a life worth living and is better off than they would have been outside, so admitting them makes no one worse off and cannot make the outcome worse. Second: since we share equally, we should then level everyone down to the same middling standard rather than keep a small group flourishing. Together the steps seem to show that we ought to grow to just short of 400 barely-comfortable people instead of staying at 60 flourishing ones. I think that conclusion is monstrous, but I cannot locate the mistake, because each step sounds right in isolation, and I can't tell which one to abandon or what rule to put in its place.

Two complications I would like taken seriously. Our estimates of where "worth living" sits come from asking current members how satisfied they are, and people in the harshest conditions report almost as much satisfaction as anyone else, which makes me distrust the numbers. And we cannot be sure where the tipping point is: there is some chance that admitting near-400 people tips a few of those lives just barely below the point where existing is a benefit to the person living it.

Which step in the committee's argument is the weak one, what exactly does it assume, and — given the sharing rule, the applicants' outside options, and the uncertainty — what should we actually do? I want the structure of the problem identified and applied, not general advice about community decision-making.
