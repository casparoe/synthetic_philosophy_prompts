hey — 4th year polisci here, IR / formal theory side. I've got a twenty minute slot at our rational choice reading group next thursday on commitment races, and instead of opening with the usual slide with a 2x2 matrix on it I'd like to open with a poem. I'm useless at verse, so: can you write me one?

Quick stipulation on what I mean, because I use the term more narrowly than most people do. A commitment race isn't an arms race. In an arms race both sides want more capacity. In a commitment race both sides want fewer options: the prize goes to whoever is first to be visibly, irreversibly unable to back down — the old bridge-burning idea. The bit my talk is actually about is a case I've started calling a phantom race: a commitment race that runs on beliefs alone. Each side's reason to commit now isn't the other side's commitment but the bare possibility of it, and because an irreversible commitment and a bluff look exactly alike until something tests them, uncertainty about the other side converts straight into urgency — so the race can get run, and won, and lost, even when neither side has moved yet and both would rather nobody did. My claim is that what kills the value of waiting isn't the other side's commitment, it's your uncertainty about it; and whoever is still free when the other is bound ends up paying the whole cost of the flexibility they kept. (I've been calling that the last free party's curse. Naming is not my strength.)

So the ask: a villanelle, 19 lines, readable aloud in forty seconds or so. I want the two refrains to be the two commitments — one in the speaker's own voice, saying they have already bound themselves, and one about the other side, saying they've done it too, or so the speaker has been told. I'd been toying with "I bound myself already" and "you bound yourself, or so I'm told" — which obviously don't rhyme, and villanelle refrains should, so rewrite freely. What has to be right is this: every time the refrains come back they should be doing a different job — first a boast, then a bet, then a rumour, then a plea or an epitaph — and someone who works on this stuff should be able to tell which is which without being told. The interlocking should carry the idea: two commitments chasing each other through the same five tercets and never once confirming each other, then the closing quatrain puts them side by side when it's too late.

Constraints, sorry, there are a few: no clocks, no mushroom clouds, no cold war nostalgia generally, and the words "commitment race" and "phantom" shouldn't appear in the poem at all. Two people in the group think commitment races are just arms races with extra steps, so if the poem can make the fewer-options-not-more-capacity distinction felt rather than stated, that'd do my work for me. And please don't resolve it — the thing I want the room left with is that neither refrain is ever verified and the race was real anyway. It should be accurate enough that nobody winces at the mechanism or thinks I've confused a credible commitment with a cheap one.
