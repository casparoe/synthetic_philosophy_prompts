my friend dev has this whole theory about creativity that he got out of some essay, and he wont shut up about it. basicly: every artist or genre is a point in a big space, and you can describe that point by a list of knobs — how fast, how loud, how many chords, how weird the lyrics are, whether theres ever silence, whatever. making something new is just moving around in that space. but most knobs are boring low level ones, and the moves that matter are (a) turning a knob that everybody in that style always leaves alone, and (b) every once in a while leaving the whole space and building a new one. he also says spinning knobs at random doesnt count at all, because you have to be able to tell which of the variations are the good ones, otherwise its just noise.

so far his calls on music:

- cover band playing a song exactly like the original: not creative
- a sped up, high pitched version of a pop song: not creative, just one low knob
- same melody, completely new funny lyrics: creative
- a guy who writes like 50 songs off one chord progression, all different melodies and words, and you can tell hes going somewhere with it: creative
- a script that randomly mutates notes in a melody: not creative
- a kid who cranks every knob to a random weird value all at once and it comes out as noise: not creative
- a singer from a scene where every song is under three minutes and never stops, who puts out a 14 minute track with two minutes of total silence in the middle and it works: most creative thing he can think of
- a band that invents a sound that sounds like nothing before it: creative, but he ranks it below the silence track

me and him have a bet now because AI stuff came up and he has literally never ruled on any of it. the three cases:

1. a model trained on one bands entire discography spits out a new song that sounds exactly like them, nothing they wouldnt have done themselves.
2. a producer tells the same kind of model to write in that bands style but in a genre the band never went near, and it comes out coherent and actually good.
3. the producer instead has it generate 500 versions with knobs all over the place and picks the one that gives him chills; the one he picks happens to be a version where a deep knob nobody in that style ever touches gets moved, but he didnt set that knob on purpose, he just picked the output.

what is dev gonna say about each one, and whats the rule hes actually running underneath? and what other rules fit all his old verdicts just as well, and where do those rules disagree with each other on case 2 and case 3? most importantly, what single extra case should i put to him to find out which rule hes really using, because ive got five bucks on this and i want to pick the case, not the answer.
