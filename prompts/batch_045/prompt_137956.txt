Behavior genetics reports a trait's heritability as the share of the variation in that trait, within a defined population at a defined time, that is statistically attributable to genetic differences — classically estimated by comparing identical with fraternal twins. The same quantity then gets used in three ways that seem to pull in different directions. Plant and animal breeders use it as the input to predicting how fast a population will move under selection (the expected response equals narrow-sense heritability times the selection differential), so for them a high value means the trait is easy to change. Policy commentary treats a high value as a reason to think the trait is fixed and that interventions aimed at it are wasted effort. And popular reasoning moves from "T is highly heritable within group G" to "the gap in T between groups is genetic."

I want the implicit theory made explicit. Does the practice of estimating and reporting heritability presuppose a specific view of causation — that causes come in degrees, that a cause's importance is measured by how much variance it accounts for rather than by how large a difference it can make to an individual, or something weaker? Does it presuppose that "genetic" contrasts with "changeable," or is that an optional extra? Test your answer on two cases. (i) In population P, T has heritability 0.9, yet 2% of children there carry a cheaply treatable parasite that pushes T three standard deviations below the mean and whose removal restores T entirely. (ii) Within each of two populations, T has heritability 0 because nobody in either population varies genetically in the relevant way, but the populations differ genetically from each other and their mean T differs by a standard deviation.

Does (i) license "fixed, so don't bother intervening"? Does (ii) license "the gap is environmental"? Finally: which of the three uses does heritability estimation strictly commit us to, which does it merely sit well with, which does it actually rule out — and what would have to change in how the quantity is defined, estimated, or reported for it to support the second and third uses?
