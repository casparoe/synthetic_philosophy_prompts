I had a long argument with a friend last night about whether an AI system can be made to avoid side effects without anyone first specifying in full what is valuable. She leaned on an analogy I keep running into in that area: side effects are externalities, and an impact penalty is a Pigouvian tax. We never needed a complete theory of value to get factories to stop fouling the river; we just priced the thing we wanted less of. By the same logic, we can penalise an agent for changing the world and get most of the safety benefit without solving value alignment first.

The argument stalled on a variant she proposed when I pressed her on the fact that any such penalty still needs a baseline and a metric. Instead of taxing change, she said, charge a refundable deposit: let the agent rearrange the world freely, but require that it retain the ability to put things back, forfeiting the deposit for changes it cannot reverse. Her claim was that reversibility is a value-free proxy for impact, so the deposit scheme drops the last remaining bit of value specification.

Please audit both versions. Concretely, I want:

- **Mapping** (~120 words): a correspondence table for each analogy — what plays the role of the polluter, the regulator, the price, the baseline, the harmed third parties, and the thing being measured.
- **Where the analogy does real work** (~100 words): what it genuinely gets right about shaping behaviour under ignorance of value.
- **Where it breaks** (~250 words): the disanalogies that matter for this use, in particular (a) whose preferences or observed costs supply the price in the externality case, and what, if anything, plays that role here; (b) whether a single agent that can choose its own baseline, or act on the mechanism that measures it, has any counterpart in the tax setting; (c) whether an agent optimising against the penalty can look low-impact while still rearranging things a great deal — compensating for a change by making an offsetting one, disabling the meter, or picking a flattering counterfactual.
- **What it smuggles in** (~100 words): which features of the economic or contractual source the analogy imports into the target, especially about the value of the status quo and about how many agents are in the picture.
- **Does the conclusion survive** (~150 words): whether the disanalogies sink the claim that low-impact behaviour is reachable without a full specification of value, or whether some weaker version of it is defensible.
- **A better analogy, if there is one** (~80 words).

For the deposit variant specifically, tell me whether reversibility really is value-free or merely relocates the value question one level up, and if it fails, whether it fails for the same reason as the tax or for a different one. Please argue from the structure of the problem rather than from the literature — I don't want citations or references, just the reasoning.
