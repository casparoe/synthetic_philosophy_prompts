I maintain an agent-based evolutionary simulation in my spare time: two hundred subpopulations of a hundred digital organisms each, payoff-proportional reproduction, five percent migration between subpopulations per generation, and recolonization of patches that go extinct. A colleague and I have spent months arguing about how to describe what it does, and neither of us has moved the other.

The case we keep returning to is this. I can build each generation's groups by drawing members at random from a global pool, so that relatedness between an individual and its group-mates is approximately zero by construction; I can also reach into the simulation and set each group's composition by hand while leaving every organism's payoff function exactly as it was. Under both manipulations, an allele that lowers its bearer's personal reproductive output but raises the total output of the group it sits in still increases in frequency, by an amount that tracks the variance in composition between groups. My colleague says this shows selection at the group level is a real causal process, since group composition is a variable she can set directly and the outcome changes when she sets it. I say it is only the average effect of the allele taken across a distribution of contexts, and that the between-group covariance in the arithmetic restates the total change rather than adding a cause. There is a third variant I can run, in which genes move horizontally between organisms fast enough that gene lineages and organism lineages come apart entirely.

Please write a dialogue between the two of us in which each position gets its strongest formulation and each speaker answers what the other has actually argued, rather than a simplified version of it. I want them to work through three things: whether talk of the level at which selection acts is a claim about causes, a claim about which decomposition of a total change is most useful, or a claim about which variables can be manipulated independently; whether the ability to intervene on group composition settles the causal question or merely makes explicit what the decomposition already encoded; and what is left of the disagreement in the horizontal-transfer variant, where the thing whose frequency changes is not the thing that interacts with the environment. Keep the technical vocabulary explained in the characters' own words. I have no training in philosophy or biology, I do not want anyone cited by name, and I would rather have the argument than a survey of positions.
