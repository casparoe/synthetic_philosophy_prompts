I want a dialogue, not an essay. Two characters, roughly equally competent, no strawmen.

Setup: some historians and ML people build a "responsibility audit" for the July Crisis — a big causal model of who decided what, when, and what they expected. For each actor it spits out a Shapley-style share: put the actors in some order, set each one's decision back to a no-escalation baseline one at a time, and average how much the probability of a general European war drops when each actor is added. Assume it's reliable about counterfactual causation. It says nothing about blame. Output: the single largest share goes to the civilian leadership in Berlin, but a big chunk goes to "the alliance system and the arms race" treated as a node, and Berlin's share shrinks a lot if you hold that node fixed.

Miriam thinks "Germany was responsible for the First World War" is true and matters — the Fischer reading: the blank cheque to Vienna, the war-aims program drafted that September, a bid for continental hegemony they knowingly risked a general war for. Jonas thinks blame can't attach to a nation: only agents can be blamed, the shares are artifacts of how you carve up actors and what you hold fixed, you'd get the same share for someone who acted well, and the people now asked to "own" it were born after the next war ended.

Have them actually argue. I want Miriam pressed on what the numbers buy her — does a counterfactual share convert into blame, or is she really reading off the reasons and foresight of individual men and keeping only the national label? And Jonas pressed on the recognition point: if nobody is to blame, what is owed to the dead, and why? Include Miriam conceding guilt is individual but insisting the state is a continuing agent that can owe answerability and repair, and Jonas's best reply.

Somewhere in the middle have Miriam raise that the shares swing wildly depending on whether "the German people" count as an actor at all — they decided nothing, but they cheered in August, and the Reichstag voted the credits. Make it a problem for both of them, not a gotcha for one.

Under ~1200 words. Then a short note seperating what specialists actually treat as settled here from what's live dispute — I don't want a contested reading dressed up as consensus, especially not on how well Fischer's thesis has held up.
