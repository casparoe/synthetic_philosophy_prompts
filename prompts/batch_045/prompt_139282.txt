Our reading group does a "worst referee report" night at the end of term, and I volenteered to write this year's entry: I got my first ever referee report back last week, on a paper about the Sleeping Beauty problem, and reading it aloud got a bigger laugh than the paper did.

Here's the setup. An author has submitted a manuscript to a respectable general analytic journal arguing that Beauty's credence that the coin landed heads stays at 1/2 when she wakes (she already knew the protocol, so she's learned nothing new) and that her credence that it's Monday should be 3/4. The referee is a committed thirder who never once says so.

Ground rule: every objection has to be one a real referee could actually make, and has to be technically right — the joke is in which objections get made, how much weight they get, and what they give away, not in nonsense. Please work in: the betting/fair-odds argument (what Beauty should pay per awakening for a $1 stake on heads); a version of the case with a thousand tails-awakenings, where the author's number doesn't budge; and the bit where the referee demands the author say what Beauty should think if she's then told "today is Monday" — this is the one place the two sides swap places, since the halfer's credence in heads goes up to 2/3 there while the thirder's stays at 1/2. Also one objection that's plainly just a disguised demand that the author cite the referee, and at least one gratuitous complaint about length.

Don't name or quote any real philosopher, and don't cite anything real — invent the titles. Keep it deadpan: the referee never uses the words "halfer," "thirder," or "Sleeping Beauty"; a reader should be able to reconstruct the case and work out which side the referee is on from the objections alone. It should be funny to someone who knows the literature and still legible to a first-year who doesn't.

Sections in order: Recommendation (one line, icy), Summary of the argument (about 100 words), Major concerns (about 400 words, numbered, four or so), Minor concerns (about 100 words), Confidential comments to the editor (about 80 words, where the referee stops pretending). Around 700 words total.
