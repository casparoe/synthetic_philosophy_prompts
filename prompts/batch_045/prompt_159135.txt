The difference is that the predictor is both fallible and lopsided in a particular direction: of the people who ended up taking only the opaque box, it correctly predicted 90%, while of those who ended up taking both boxes it correctly predicted only 55%. Money is the only thing at stake, and you know these figures when you choose.

Rank the following five rules in tiers by the average payout an agent who followed each would collect over a long run of such cases, with one sentence on each about why it lands where it does: take both boxes, on the ground that the million is either already there or already not and the extra thousand is free either way; take the opaque box only, on the ground that your present choice is strong evidence about what was predicted; take the opaque box only, on the ground that you should do what you would have bound yourself to do before the prediction was made; take the boxes that leave you with the smallest possible regret once they are opened; and take the boxes that secure the largest guaranteed minimum.

Then tell me which rule you think someone in this situation ought actually to follow, and, if that is not the rule that tops your payout ranking, what the mismatch shows about what the case is really testing. Finally, say roughly how weak or how lopsided the predictor's record would have to be for the evidence-based rule to switch sides. Please write all of this as continuous prose, the way you would in an email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
