I am writing a seminar paper on Schopenhauer's philosophy of music — roughly four thousand words, arguing that his account is more interesting than the standard story about it — and I want to audit the summary I have been working from before I build anything on it. The summary comes from a short companion to nineteenth-century aesthetics that our seminar uses as a crib, not from Schopenhauer himself; my professor independently said something very similar in a lecture, which is part of why I am unsure whether to distrust it. It runs as follows:

"Schopenhauer thought music was the highest of the arts because it is the only one that is not about anything. Where painting, poetry and sculpture copy the Platonic Ideas — the eternal patterns that particular things imperfectly instantiate — music copies the Will itself, the blind striving that is the inner nature of everything and the true reality behind the world of appearances. Because the Will is the thing-in-itself, music gives us direct acquaintance with ultimate reality, unmediated by space, time and causality, which is why he described it as a kind of metaphysics done without knowing it. He also mapped the parts of a piece onto the levels of nature: the bass line is inorganic matter, gravity and stone; the harmonies in between are plants and animals; the melody is the human will's self-conscious desire. A piece of music is thus something like a diagram of the whole chain of being, and this is why music moves us with a significance that words can only gesture at. It is also why he despised programme music: making music imitate a storm or tell a story degrades it to the level of painting. The obvious objection is an empirical one: people raised outside the Western tradition do not reliably hear our minor-key laments as sad, so the language of the Will turns out to be a local dialect, and the metaphysics collapses."

Please go through this more or less sentence by sentence and tell me what a careful reader of Schopenhauer's own text would accept, what is a tolerable simplification, and what is subtly wrong — and for each thing that is wrong, what a defender of the view would say the crib has conflated, overstated, or taken in the wrong sense. Two specific suspicions of mine: the opening sounds to me like Hanslick-style formalism, which I had thought Schopenhauer was the opposite of, and the diagram-of-nature bit sounds too neat for a philosopher who says music does not depict the world at all. I also do not know what to do with the empirical objection at the end: it sounds devastating to me, but I suspect it only lands against a version of the position nobody holds.

The reason the details matter is that I want to use the corrected account on two cases of my own, and I get different answers depending on which version I use. First: elsewhere in his aesthetics, aesthetic experience seems to require that the subject stop willing and become a purely knowing, painless subject; but music is supposed to be a copy of the very thing we ourselves are, and whose inner nature we know from our own striving. So consider a being that never wills at all — permanently a pure subject of knowing. Is that being the ideal listener for music, or is music meaningless to it? And is the tension I think I see a real one in Schopenhauer, or have I confused two things he keeps apart? Second: take a piece consisting of a single unresolved dissonance held for twenty minutes, with no melody, no bass line, no metre — only a slowly changing timbre. Under the crib's diagram version this is degenerate music, since there is no layered structure to map onto the grades of nature; under the direct-copy version it looks like the purest possible music, all striving and no satisfaction. Does Schopenhauer's actual position give a determinate answer here, and does the answer differ from the one the crib's version would give?

One further thing I would like flagged: how much of this crib is Schopenhauer, and how much is the later Romantic reception of him — the music-as-cosmic-revelation line that people read into him afterwards?

Then single out the one claim in your own reply that you are least confident in, and say what would settle it.
