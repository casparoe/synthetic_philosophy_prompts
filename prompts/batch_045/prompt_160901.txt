I'm in a small reading group that has been working through the cosmological self-undermining literature — Boltzmann brains, Boltzmann bubbles, simulation scenarios, and the general question of when a cosmological model leaves the person who accepts it without any route to the evidence that supports it. One member, a senior philosopher of physics, keeps something like a league table: for each toy model we discuss she gives a verdict on whether the model is epistemically admissible, meaning that someone who accepted it could still go on treating their evidence as a reason for accepting it. Her verdicts so far: model A, an eternal de Sitter space with an infinite future thermal phase in which fluctuation observers with fabricated memories vastly outnumber ordinary observers both globally and among observers whose evidence is qualitatively like ours, she rules inadmissible. Model B, a closed universe that expands, recollapses and spends a long but finite stretch in thermal equilibrium, so that ordinary observers in the early low-entropy phase vastly outnumber fluctuation observers, she accepts. Model C, an eternal de Sitter space whose only fluctuation products are momentary observers — sub-second, unstructured single experiences with no memory-like or self-locating states — she accepts, even though these outnumber ordinary observers by an enormous factor overall, since none of them has evidence remotely like ours. Model D, an eternal de Sitter space in which the fluctuations are Boltzmann bubbles, that is, an observer together with a local environment (an office, a night sky, a set of instrument readings) whose apparent memories of a long scientific career are false, and where such observers vastly outnumber ordinary observers among those with evidence qualitatively like ours, she rules inadmissible. Model E, which contains no thermal fluctuations at all but holds that almost all observers are implemented by a short-lived ancestor simulation run by a civilization in the base reality, with fabricated records of a deep past, she also rules inadmissible — so her objection is clearly not specifically about thermal fluctuations.

Next week I have to present model G, and I would like to know where she will put it. Model G is an eternal de Sitter background whose late thermal phase produces fluctuation observers, but with an extra stipulation I have been calling the no-spurious-records condition: a fluctuation counts as an observer only if the memory records it instantiates are exact copies of records actually produced by some real observer earlier in the multiverse's history, and the fluctuation carries with it a local environment — a real room, real instruments, real interlocutors — that matches those records. These copy observers vastly outnumber ordinary observers, and they do so among observers whose evidence is qualitatively like ours as well. But nothing they believe about the past, or about their surroundings at the moment, is false. What is false is only that they came to be through the continuous embodied history they seem to remember and that they have the future they take themselves to have: each is a fraction of a second old and will dissolve. A friend in the group thinks her whole table is just the reference class problem wearing a lab coat, and I am not sure he is wrong, but I also cannot tell whether what she is tracking is the numerical dominance of abnormal observers within some evidence class, the truth of the content of our records and observations, or the reliability of the process that produced them in the first place.

So: what is your prediction for model G, and why? Please spell out the distinct rules that fit her five verdicts equally well — I can think of a dominance-in-the-evidence-class rule, a narrow rule about whether the content of our evidence comes out false, and a wider rule about whether the provenance of our evidence and our future-directed and self-locating beliefs survive — say what each of them predicts about model G, tell me which you think she is actually running and what the strongest case for the runner-up is, and note explicitly which of the rules already come apart on G and which do not. Finally, if the table is genuinely underdetermined, tell me which further toy model I should put to her in order to separate the survivors, and what principle she would be committing herself to under each of the two verdicts on G.
