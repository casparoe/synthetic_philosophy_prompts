Im putting together a short story for an intro ethics reader on the why be moral question, built around Glaucon's challenge in book 2 of the Republic: the ring of Gyges, the we're-all-too-weak-to-do-injustice-for-free genealogy of law, and most of all the part where he says reputation is where the real payoff is, that the perfectly just man everyone thinks is a monster gets tortured while the perfectly unjust man everyone thinks is a saint gets everything.

Except I want a variant ring so it isnt a retelling. This one doesnt make you invisible, it makes you unremembered. Wear it and nobody keeps any memory of you once you leave the room; photos, signatures and text threads go smudgy; you cant be caught, but you also cant be trusted, credited, promoted, or loved, and you lose your own past too, since the people who shared it forget it along with you.

Protagonist: someone who has actually read the Republic, thinks the answer is obvious, and is sure the ring is a pure upgrade. Set it somewhere concrete and contemporary, not ancient Greece. Keep Glaucon straight, hes saying people are just for the wages and the reputation, not that justice is nothing, i want a philosophy TA to be able to teach from it. Around 1200 words, and no sermon ending, no and then he learned kindness: the last line should leave it genuinely unclear whether putting it on ruined him, and whether that has anything to do with justice or just with what being unrememberable does to a person.
