I'm a third-year undergraduate in a seminar on power and social theory. We've spent the last few weeks on Foucault's account of disciplinary power: panopticism and the internalization of the gaze, the exam as a technique that joins hierarchical observation to normalizing judgment and thereby makes individuals comparable, confession as an incitement to discourse, the claim that power is productive and capillary rather than mainly repressive, subjection as the very process through which one becomes a subject, and the insistence that resistance is never outside power. For my final creative assignment I have to produce a parody of a real administrative genre, and I'd like you to write it.

The form: a memo of about 700 words, with numbered sections and a short FAQ at the end, from something like an Office of Institutional Visibility at a university, announcing a new initiative — a name of your own devising — under which every moment of student learning is to be made continuously visible, recorded, and fed back to the student. The voice is cheerful, competent, sincere: the office genuinely believes it is emancipating students, and nothing in the memo should read as a lie.

The constraints that make this more than an office-satire: the jokes have to come from getting the machinery right, so that someone who knows the material laughs rather than winces. Power must be shown producing things — subjects, pleasures, vocabularies of self-description, a profession of analysts — not merely forbidding them. Visibility should do the work the gaze does. There should be a section in which objection and resistance are not just permitted but actively solicited, and are thereby converted into more of the same material; include one brief testimonial from a student who has discovered that being known is a form of freedom. The memo should never name Foucault and should avoid his vocabulary — no panopticon, no dispositif, no subjectification — while remaining legible as a reductio: by the end a reader should feel that a machinery for producing docile, self-policing, endlessly self-reporting individuals can present itself as empowerment without ever having to dissemble.
