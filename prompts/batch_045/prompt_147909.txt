I'm running a graduate reading group on deflationary theories of truth, and for the last session I want to hand out a decision tree that takes a student from a single starting commitment to a named position, the way one can take a student from accepting the experimental violation of the Bell inequalities to an interpretation of quantum mechanics. The starting commitment I want is this: when the cat is on the mat, calling 'the cat is on the mat' true seems to add nothing to just saying that the cat is on the mat, and any account of truth has to respect that. From there I want nine or ten branch questions, each phrased so that a student who has done the reading but doesn't yet know which label it leads to can answer it — concrete cases and claims, never 'do you think truth is a real property?'. Every leaf should carry the name of a position or, where nobody defends it, 'unoccupied'. The leaves I definitely want represented: the redundancy view, disquotationalism, the minimalism associated with Horwich, prosententialism, primitivism, a substantive correspondence or truthmaker view, verificationist anti-realism of the Dummett stripe, alethic pluralism, and one invented position of my own that I describe below. Where two leaves can't be separated by any question on the tree, say so explicitly and explain what would have to be added to separate them; I'd rather have a flagged collapse than a fake branch. Also build the tree from the logical space first — the questions and the answer combinations they permit — and only then drop the actual views into it, noting any combination that stays empty and giving one sentence on whether it is empty because incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

The cases I want doing the work are these: an inference of the mixed form (everything in the archive is true; the archive says the bridge is unsafe; so the bridge is unsafe); an explanation of someone's practical success that seems to traffic in the truth of her beliefs rather than in their specific contents; a sentence you cannot disquote because it is written in a script you don't read, or on a card someone else is holding; the question whether a theory of truth should be conservative over the theory it's added to, given that adding a truth predicate lets you derive new claims about the original subject matter; and a domain-shift case where the candidate truths are mathematical, comic, or normative rather than empirical, to split monists from pluralists. Two further cases are mine and shouldn't appear in any textbook. First, the Saphi: an imagined community whose truth-word is used only to endorse an utterance made just before by another speaker, is never applied to the speaker's own current utterance, carries an overtone of deferring to that speaker, and has no use in generalizations at all — I want the tree to force a verdict on whether the Saphi word is a truth predicate, because my sense is that this case separates views that take blind endorsement to be the core function from views that take generalization to be the core function. Second, calibration expressivism: a friend in machine learning argues that for a classifier the only real norm is calibration, that calling its outputs 'true' is just bookkeeping over labels, and that nothing is gained by asking whether truth is a property — she thinks this is obviously deflationary, I think it might collapse into expressivism about truth attributions, and I want the tree either to give her its own leaf or to show which existing leaf she already occupies. Please don't include a bibliography or reading list; the handout is the tree, the scoring notes, and the diagnoses of the empty cells and collapsed branches, and it should be usable by someone who has read the core papers but has no library to hand.
