I write about science and ideas for a living, but I let a friend talk me onto the city's Parkway Structures Review Commission, and somehow I'm the one who has to draft the memo we vote on in three weeks. Could you give me a first pass?

Here's the case. Three stone-arch overpasses on the old riverside parkway have to be rebuilt no matter what — spalling, salt damage, the usual. Their clearance is 9 foot 6, which is fine for cars and impossible for buses, which need about 11. The housing-and-transit coalition wants the rebuilt arches raised: they say the low clearance was built to keep bus riders away from the beach, that a structure designed to exclude keeps excluding every day we leave it standing, and that fixing the concrete without fixing the height is just maintaining somebody else's 1930s housing policy. The city engineer says the height was standard for that arch form and that era, that theres no document anywhere establishing intent, and — this is the bit I keep chewing on — that the people excluded now aren't the people excluded then: the neighborhoods that lost out never got their bus service back, so a raised bridge would mostly serve suburban commuters and a couple of hotel shuttles. Historic preservation wants the arches rebuilt at the current height and interpreted instead.

So I need a memo that lands somewhere — raise all three, raise one and leave two, keep the height and reroute the money into bus service, whatever you think is actually defensible — with a rough timeline, since the council want's shovels in the ground within two years, and with a genuinely fair statement of the strongest argument against whatever you recommend, because the losing faction will be sitting in the room.

Two more things it has to handle. First, the plaque. Draft language going around says the arches were built deliberately too low for buses, and I don't think we can establish that; I'd rather put up something honest than something that wins an argument by asserting the disputed part. Second, the conceptual bit: one side talks as if the politics is in the object itself, and the engineers say the politics is entirely in the route maps, the maintenance budget and the parking fees — and in fact, if we change nothing physical but the transit authority reroutes buses, who gets excluded flips anyway. Does that mean the politics was never in the concrete? I don't need the memo to settle that. I need it to make a decision we can defend whether or not it was.
