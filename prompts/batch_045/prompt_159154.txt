I clerk for the Reconsideration Panel of our borough's work-and-progression scheme (I'll just call it the Scheme). When the Scheme proposes to cut someone's allowance for four or thirteen weeks, the Panel decides whether the cut stands. Here are six rulings from the last year, with the facts that seemed to matter to them.

1. Latoya missed a 9am work-search review. Her bus broke down; she phoned the office at 8:15, was told to come at 2pm, and did. No sanction — the office had moved the appointment, so on their view there was no failure.

2. Yusuf didn't attend a mandatory group session held on the first floor of a building with no lift. He's a wheelchair user. He'd emailed the provider's adjustments contact two weeks earlier asking for an accessible venue and got no reply; he didn't attend and didn't phone on the day. No sanction, and the Panel told the provider to cover his travel costs and move future sessions.

3. Dean refused a four-week unpaid placement in a warehouse run by a big online retailer, where the same firm was advertising a paid vacancy of the same description at the same site. He wrote in asking to be reassigned and proposed three non-profit placements; all were refused without reasons. No sanction: a placement that stands in for paid work the same employer is recruiting for isn't something they'll enforce.

4. Amara missed a mandatory CV clinic. Her childminder cancelled that morning; she texted her coach at 9:40 asking to move to Thursday and got no reply; she didn't attend. She'd had three weeks' notice, there was a recorded earlier warning for a previous missed clinic, the letter told her to phone the office rather than text her coach if she needed to reschedule, and she hadn't used the Scheme's emergency childcare line. Her rep argued the cut would put her in rent arrears. Sanction stands, reduced from four weeks to two: hardship is what the discretionary payment route is for, not a reason to withhold.

5. Tunde logged no applications on the portal for three weeks and said it kept timing out on his phone. The Panel found a documented outage on one of those days only, that he'd been shown how to use the library terminals, that he never reported the problem or asked for help at the time, and that he'd told his coach he'd given up on the website. Sanction stands at four weeks.

6. Mariam was terminated from a placement on day one because her trainers weren't safety footwear and she couldn't afford boots. She came back the next day in borrowed boots two sizes too big and was sent home again; the provider logged it as attitude. No sanction, provider told to supply the footwear, and the attitude entry can't be used against her. Their reason: a condition that costs the claimant money she hasn't got, applied through a provider's own rulebook, isn't enforceable, and there'd been no written warning and no chance to put it right.

Now the case I have to draft for next week. The Scheme rolled out a jobsearch app three months ago: claimants in the top two bands must install it, it logs location 9 to 5 on weekdays, and it's now the only way to get search activity verified. Chidi is 54, a former forklift driver, and has a basic phone, no smartphone. He won't install the app: he says he's not going to be tracked and can't afford the data. He keeps a paper diary and makes around twelve employer visits a week, and his coach has put in writing that this is above what his claim requires. The Scheme offered him a free refurbished phone plus a data top-up for as long as he's on the Scheme, gave him written notice and two warnings, and wants thirteen weeks. Against that: the notice never says what the app collects, who can see it, or how long it's kept; he twice wrote in asking for a non-app way to verify his search and got no answer; and the sanction was triggered by an automated non-compliance flag from the risk-banding model they bought last year rather than by his coach, who had signed off his jobsearch. Nobody can tell us which input pushed him into the top band — his coach guesses it was the gap in his record from when he was in hospital, plus his age.
