Last night I got into a three-hour argument with my friend Rowan (he's in a metaphysics PhD programme and would not shut up about Lewis) about whether, when I say I could have been something other than what I am, there's a single thing — me — that shows up at other worlds, or just other things that resemble me closely enough to stand in for me. I went home and wrote down what I actually think, case by case, and now I want to know whether my verdicts add up to any coherent position or whether I'm just being inconsistent.

Here they are. (1) I could have been a centimetre taller, and I could have been a marine biologist instead of what I am: obviously yes. (2) I could have been born a month early: yes. (3) I could not have originated from a different sperm and egg: that person would be my sibling, not me, and I feel this one strongly. Rowan pointed out that if I'd been born a month early the conception was almost certainly a different event with a different sperm, so (2) and (3) can't both stand as I've put them — I don't know which one has to move. (4) I could not have been a horse, and I could not have been a poached egg — not even via a long chain of tiny changes, where every individual step looks like something I could have taken but the end point looks like something I couldn't. (5) Take a world containing two people, one with my childhood and somebody else's career and one with somebody else's childhood and my career, each about as similar to me overall as I am to myself: I feel I could have been either, and that there's no fact of the matter which, but also that they aren't both literally me. (6) The ship in the harbour could perfectly well have been built, from the keel up, out of entirely different planks: yes, and unlike (3), and I dont know why the asymmetry is there. (7) An atom-for-atom duplicate of me that comes together by pure chance in a swamp in some far-off world, with all my memories and none of my history: not me, just my double. (8) Rowan's trap: if counterparts are just sufficiently similar things, and the actual world is a world too, then isn't my next-door neighbour in some sense my counterpart, and doesn't that make it true that I might have been my neighbour? My gut says no and I couldn't say why not. And the case I genuinely can't decide: if the zygote I came from had split, I'd have been twins, and I have no idea whether I'd have been both of them, neither, or one of them with no fact as to which.

My tentative principle is: 'I could have been X' is true iff some possible individual has my actual origin and is otherwise similar enough to me in the respects that matter — origin as a necessary condition, weighted resemblance doing the rest. But that principle seems to get (6) wrong, since it says the ship couldn't have been built of different planks, and it doesn't obviously do anything with the chain in (4). So: is there one account of de re possibility that gets as many of (1)–(8) as possible, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? If it's counterpart theory, tell me which version (realist, ersatz, actualist) and which constraints on the counterpart relation are doing the real work; if it's transworld identity, tell me what carries me across worlds if neither resemblance nor origin does, and whether I should be a haecceitist. Please commit — I don't want a menu of options with costs and benefits and a shrug at the end. Name the account you think I should accept, tell me which verdict I have to retract and why that one rather than the others, and tell me what your account predicts for the twin case and for at least one further case I haven't thought of where my gut would be put on the spot; also flag the one place where you think your own answer is most likely wrong. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — the principle in one sentence plus the verdict I have to give up — then give me the reasoning, no preamble, and don't restate my question back at me.
