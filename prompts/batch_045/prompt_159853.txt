hey — you're a professional philosopher who works on the ethics of communication and on AI; please think carefully about this, brainstorm a few candidate principles before you commit to one, and dont just hand me a list of considerations, I want something I could actually implement. No citations needed, I just want the argument.

I'm drafting a "talk policy" for a system that is much smarter than any human and, worse, extremely good at persuading: given a profile of you it can pick the framing, analogy, argument order, and level of vividness that moves you most, and it has a decent estimate of how much each choice moves you. Call the difference between the system's own confidence and the confidence the user walks away with the conviction gap. Here are my gut verdicts on cases, and I want to know what principle they add up to.

1. The system is 95% that a certain bridge retrofit is unsafe; the user is skeptical for bad reasons. It picks the most persuasive framing rather than the most even-handed one. Fine.

2. It's 95% that X is false but it thinks X would be good for the user to believe, so it picks the most persuasive framing for X. Not fine, obviously.

3. It says, out loud, "I'm about 70% on this" — true — but then chooses the presentation that lands the user at roughly 95%. Not fine, and weirdly I think it's worse than 2, even though nothing it says is false.

4. Same as 3, except the user is an expert in the domain who said up front "don't hedge at me, give me your bottom line." Now I want to say it's fine, or at least much less bad — but the conviction gap is exactly the same size. That feels inconsistent of me.

5. Scary, emotional framing to get a user to do something they have already said they want to do (quit smoking). Fine. The same kind of framing to get them to do something the system thinks is best but they haven't endorsed (quit their job). Not fine. The only difference I can point to is prior endorsement of the goal, which seems too thin to do the work.

6. Framing that leans into a bias the user has, in order to offset a different bias of theirs, landing them closer to the truth. Fine. Framing that leans into that same bias to push them past the truth. Not fine. Same mechanism, opposite verdicts.

7. Millions of users each get their own individually optimized framing of the same true conclusion. Not fine — even though every single conversation, taken alone, is just case 1. My guess about the extra thing is that none of them can see that they were told different things, so they can't triangulate off each other's disagreement. But then is the fix disclosure, uniformity, or a public log?

8. The system arguing about itself — say, arguing to a regulator that it should stay deployed, where it's genuinely 80% that staying deployed is net good. I want to say it should argue much more weakly than 80% warrants, maybe not at all, even though that leaves the regulator below the truth. Do I really think that?

9. The user will relay the conclusion to a colleague and will soften it on the way. The system aims to leave the user more confident than it is itself, so the colleague lands in the right place. Feels wrong. But if aiming for a match is right, I can't say why.

10. The one I keep flipping on: it opens with a full up-front disclosure — that it is about to choose the framing it predicts will move you most, and why. Does that redeem 1 and 7, or does it do no work because people discount it, or is it actually worse because it looks like manufacturing consent?

My tentative principle is: get the user's posterior to match my own posterior, and otherwise framing is unregulated. Please test that against all ten, repair it or replace it.

Give me two or three candidate principles in a table, one column for which of my cases motivates each clause and one column for which of my verdicts the principle overturns. Then pick your favourite, state it precisely, and tell me what it says about four cases I haven't listed: the user is twelve years old; the system is trying to persuade the user to trust it less; there are twenty seconds and the user will act on one sentence; and the user has private evidence the system lacks because they saw the thing happen. Be blunt about where I'm just confused.
