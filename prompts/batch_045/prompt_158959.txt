I'm writing a little arbitrator module: you hand it a convex polygon of feasible deals (x = payoff to A, y = payoff to B) plus the walk-away point d, and it returns one point. I don't care about the economics literature, I just want a rule I can actually implement that won't embarrass me. Below are my gut calls. The feasible set is the convex hull of the listed points, d = (0,0) unless I say otherwise, and obviously never return a point that leaves value on the table.

1. Hull (0,0),(12,0),(0,6) → (6,3). And if I re-express A's payoffs in units ten times smaller — hull (0,0),(120,0),(0,6) → still (6,3) in the old units. The answer shouldn't depend on what units one side's numbers happen to be in. (This kills "maximise the sum" and "equalise the gains", right?)

2. Hull (0,0),(10,0),(10,2),(4,8),(0,10) → (6,6).

3. Same situation, except we then discover (10,5) is reachable too: hull (0,0),(10,0),(10,5),(0,10). I'd guess roughly (7,7). What I'm confident about is that neither side should end up worse off than in case 2 just because more options turned up — (10,5) feels wrong to me even though I can't say exactly why.

4. Hull (0,0),(100,0),(60,5),(0,10) → (60,5). And if the (100,0) corner turns out to be unimplementable, so the hull is just (0,0),(60,5),(0,10) → still (60,5). An option nobody was ever going to pick shouldn't be part of the calculation at all.

5. Case 1, but B's walk-away improves to 3: d = (0,3), hull still (0,0),(12,0),(0,6) → something like (4,4). B should come out better than before and A worse.

The rule I keep coming back to is: both sides should end up with the same fraction of the most they could possibly get, counting gains from their walk-away, and we push that fraction as high as the frontier allows. A mate says that rule will give me answers I hate on case 4, and that no rule at all can satisfy both 3 and 4. Is he right? If I have to give up one of my verdicts, which one, and what do I lose by giving it up?
