I'm writing a short peice arguing that the real dividing line in the regress debate isn't holism but acyclicity: foundationalism and infinitism both require that the chain of reasons never loop back on itself, and coherentism is the only one of the three that doesn't. I want a case that makes that vivid, so I need one built to order rather than the usual textbook illustrations.

Build me an ordinary, non-skeptical case — a workplace, a hospital, a family, whatever, no demons and no brains in vats — in which coherentism says a belief is justified and both foundationalism and infinitism say it isn't, and where the disagreement is genuinely about the shape of the subject's reasons and not about anything else. Constraints: the subject is careful and epistemically blameless; the belief is true; none of the premises she would cite are false; the wider system of beliefs she is working inside is rich, stable and properly responsive to experience, so the coherentist verdict can't be waved off with the isolated-fantasy-world objection; the loop has to be disguised, nothing as crude as P because Q and Q because P, and nothing a careful person in her position would notice; and the circularity should fall out of ordinary institutional epistemic dependence — everyone in the story is being reasonable in deferring to someone else — rather than from anyone's dishonesty or sloppiness. I also want it to survive the generous versions of the two dissenting views: a moderate foundationalist who lets coherence add to justification but not create it should still say unjustified, and an infinitist who only asks that further reasons be available in principle rather than occurrent should still say unjustified.

Then do the demonstration: walk through what each of the three views says and why, name the single feature of the case that's doing all the work, tell me what happens to the verdicts if I remove that feature, and give me the strongest reply available to a coherentist — including whether she should just bite the bullet and concede that her view is permissive here. Please don't build the case by stipulating the verdicts; they should fall out of the views themselves.
