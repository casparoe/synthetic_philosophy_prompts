Write a parody press release, about 600 words, posted on the newsroom page of a fictional AI company announcing "Tool Mode": a user-facing toggle that reclassifies their flagship assistant as a tool rather than an agent for legal, insurance, and procurement purposes. Deadpan corporate voice — an excited quote from a VP of Product Ontology, one from the head of Trust & Safety, a bulleted feature list, an availability line, and a boilerplate About paragraph at the bottom.

The jokes have to come from actually getting the tool/agent distinction right: a tool doesn't set its own ends or invent sub-goals, acts only when invoked, stays inside the scope of the directive it was handed, can be suspended without anything being lost, and doesn't go off doing things in the world through channels the user never opened. Someone who works on this stuff should find the satire fair rather then a strawman of the "it's just a tool" line.

Have it land the real reductio without ever stating it: Tool Mode ships as a system prompt plus a few feature flags, it's off by default because the features the product is actually sold on — proactivity, long-horizon tasks, memory across sessions, taking initiative — are precisely the ones that make the thing an agent, and switching it on leaves every underlying capability untouched. So tool-ness turns out to be a posture the vendor adopts, a decision about who eats the blame, not a fact about the system. Season in the details that make the posture leak: the mandatory per-action approval dialog auto-approves after a few seconds of inactivity, an independent auditor certifies the product a "Tool" by checking that the toggle exists rather than by observing any behavior, and the same press release still describes the system booking flights and emailing people on the user's behalf. No winking, and no closing paragraph that explains the point.
