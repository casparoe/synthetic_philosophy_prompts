My friend and I have an ongoing argument about a reaction game, and we have a bet riding on it that is now large enough to be embarrassing.

In the game, a grid of twelve letters flashes on the screen for about a tenth of a second. I can only ever name three or four of them, but my friend insists that she consciously saw all twelve — she says the rest simply drain away before she can get them out. I say that if you cannot say what you saw, cannot use it to decide anything, and cannot compare it with anything else, then you did not really see it: something in you registered it, but it was not there for you.

Here is where we are stuck. We ran a version where a tone plays after the grid vanishes and tells her which single row to name, chosen at random after the flash, and she names that row nearly perfectly whichever row it picks. She says this proves all twelve were present to her mind at once. I am fairly sure it does not prove that, but I cannot say why.

She now wants to settle it with a brain scanner: if the letters she never names still light up the regions that pass information around to everything else, she says, they were conscious. She adds that the scanner could run while she is not being asked to report anything at all, so my point that she can only name four would drop out. I think both of those moves quietly assume the very thing we are arguing about, but I cannot put my finger on how. Who is right, and what exactly should I say to her?
