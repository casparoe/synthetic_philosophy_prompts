Me and my three brothers have inherited our mum's house. Either we sell it now and split the money, about £90k each, or we keep it, let it out, and split whatever it's worth in ten years. The second option is worth more on average — call it £110k each — but it's a gamble: if the local market tanks we'd all end up with less than £90k. Our youngest brother would be hit hardest, he's saving for a deposit and needs the money to actually be there. Two of us are comfortable either way and have said they'd lend him the difference if things went badly, but somebody pointed out that if the market does tank we'd probably all be skint at the same time, so that promise isn't worth much.

He reckons "on average everyones better off" isn't good enough, and that what matters is whether he could end up worse off. I sort of see both sides. Is he right? And would it fix things if we promised him a flat £95k no matter how the market does, with the three of us splitting whatever's left over?
