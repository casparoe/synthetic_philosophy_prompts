I run a small reading group — mostly people I know from the old LessWrong / ACX comment sections — and we're doing a few weeks on transformative experience. Everyone keeps leaning on the standard epistemically-transformative vs personally-transformative split as if it were obvious which big life decisions fall on which side, and I think half the group is just pattern-matching to the canonical examples (kids = transformative, new job = not) rather than having a view about what actually makes a choice hard in this particular way. So I want you to build me a sorting instrument, not an essay: a decision tree I can wire up as a branching form, where you feed it a concrete life decision and it spits out (a) what kind of case it is and (b) which of the live recipes for deciding is actually forced on you for that kind of case.

What I need:

- About 6-8 branch questions, numbered so I can do the go-to logic, ending in 7-9 leaves. Every question has to be phrased about a concrete case, in plain language, with all the jargon banned: no transformative, no expected utility, no revealed preference, no what-it-is-like, no discovery, no authentic self. If someone who has never heard of this debate can't answer it, it needs rewriting. And no question should be answerable by noticing that the case is one of the famous ones — 'would you describe this as life-changing' is exactly the kind of thing I don't want.

- The axes I think matter, though tell me if one of them is fake: (1) whether you can get a genuine partial sample without fully undergoing the thing — a weekend with a borrowed kid, a low dose, a retreat, a high-fidelity simulation — or whether sampling is impossible or useless; (2) whether the change afterwards bottoms out in what you like, or deeper, in how much you care and how you weigh considerations, i.e. in who you are; (3) my own hobbyhorse, which I don't think anyone names: afterwards, can you still retrieve from the inside what the old you wanted, well enough to judge whether the new you is better or worse off by the old you's lights? Name the ends of that axis whatever you like. I think it's doing a huge amount of hidden work in the literature's favorite cases: in some there's genuinely no common currency left afterwards, and in others you remember the old self perfectly well and just don't want what it wanted anymore — and to me those call for completely different decision procedures even though the standard split lumps them in the same bin.

- Leaves I want occupied: nothing special is going on, this is ordinary decision-making under uncertainty, go get evidence and compute; the specialness is real but cheaply fixable, so run the experiment; the case where the only thing left to go on is whether you want to find out who you'd turn into; the case where the choice isn't really a choice so the decision theory is idle; and a nasty one where even asking whether it was worth it for me has no determinate answer. Each leaf gets one line on the most awkward thing it commits you to. I also want at least two unoccupied cells from the logical space your questions define, each with a one-line diagnosis: incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

- Test it. Run these through and tell me where they land: becoming a parent, starting HRT, converting to a religion, starting a PhD, moving to a country whose language you don't speak, taking a psychedelic, your first serious relationship, getting a cochlear implant as a deaf adult, a decade-long prison sentence, a chronic illness diagnosis, adopting a dog. If two cases that intuitively feel different come out on the same leaf, flag that rather than quietly flattening them.

- Be honest about which pairs of leaves your tree can't actually separate. I recon the 'epistemically odd' leaf and the 'merely very uncertain' leaf are going to collapse into each other at the margins, and I want to know whether that's a real continuum or a verbal dispute wearing a substantive one's clothes.

No citations, no reading list, no survey of who said what — I can read the literature myself. I want the instrument, and I want it to sort people by what they say about cases rather than by which label they like.
