I build little agent-based simulations of the prisoner's dilemma in my spare time (software engineer, no philosophy background) and I'm trying to write down what I actually believe about where cooperation comes from. Here's the set, and I want to know if it hangs together or if I'm quietly helping myself to something:

1. In a real one-shot PD against someone I'll never meet again and who reasons roughly the way I do, defecting is just correct. I've never been able to take seriously the "you're both the same kind of agent, so reason as if your moves were linked" move.
2. I do think the old tournament result where tit-for-tat did so well shows something real about how cooperation gets started, not just a quirk of what happened to get submitted.
3. I think reciprocity is enough to explain cooperation in the wild — you don't need kin selection and you don't need group selection.
4. And I think that's *all* cooperation is: long-run self interest. Norms, guilt, reputation, wanting to be the sort of person who cooperates — downstream descriptions of the same thing, not extra machinery.
5. None of this needs the agents to actually think. Mine don't deliberate at all, they just get copied in proportion to their score, and I've been assuming the selection story just replaces the rational-choice story rather than sitting next to it.
6. What I keep running into in the sims: with no fixed endpoint (I draw the number of rounds from a distribution) tit-for-tat does fine, but as soon as I add even 2-3% noise — moves get misread — it collapses into permanent mutual defection, and the only two things that rescue it are letting agents interact mostly with their own kind, or giving every agent a visible arbitrary tag and letting them pick partners by tag. With tags on, cooperation goes way up even when I strip out all memory of past moves.

Someone in the reading group said 1 and 2 contradict each other, which I don't think is right, and someone else said 6 is the interesting one, which I also can't quite see. Go through the pairs, tell me which are genuinely in tension and how bad, which only look like they are, and for each real one what the cheapest fix is and what it costs me elsewhere. If one revision fixes more than one thing, say so.
