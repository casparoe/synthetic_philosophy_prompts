I spent about thirty years as a field ecologist, mostly counting things I couldn't actually see — birds by their calls, ground cover from a grid of pins — so proxies aren't new to me. Now I'm retired and poking around in philosophy, and I keep running into Goodhart's law and this specification-gaming business. The slogan version, that a measure which becomes a target stops being a good measure, strikes me as too blunt, and I want a case to show my reading group why. Half of them think all of this is AI-alignment scaremongering, so the case has to be plainly real world — a hospital, a department, a field station, something like that — and short enough to say out loud, one paragraph.

What I want is a scenario where all three of these are true at once, and true because of how the case works, not because you stipulate them:

1. The measure has clearly been gamed: the people being measured are redirecting effort toward the number rather than at the thing we actually care about, and anyone in the story can see it.
2. The thing we actually care about genuinely got better, and by more than the number moved — so the number now understates the improvement.
3. And still, for the one decision the number is actually used for — which of two or three units gets funded, rewarded, or shut down — the number is worse than useless: it points the wrong way, and acting on it does more harm than flipping a coin.

After the scenario, show me that each of the three verdicts holds and why, tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work, what happens if you remove that feature, and what someone who thinks the measure is basically fine would say to wriggle out of it. No omniscient inspectors, and nobody being paid to outright lie — the gaming has to be something a conscientious person could do without feeling like a fraud. My own hunch is that a number can stay a perfectly good guide to the overall level while being poisoned as a way of picking between units, and I'd like the case to make that vivid, but if you think the real distinction lies in definately a different place, say so and build that case instead.
