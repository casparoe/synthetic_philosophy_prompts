I work on a proof assistant that admits cyclic, non-well-founded proofs. A derivation is a directed graph rather than a tree and may cite itself; soundness is not secured by requiring derivations to be finite, but by a global trace condition — every infinite path through the graph must pass infinitely often through a node marked as making progress. There is also a small trusted kernel whose axioms are accepted without derivation, and any derivation can be unrolled into a possibly infinite tree.

I have been using this machinery as a crutch for the epistemic regress problem — foundationalism as kernel plus finite tree, coherentism as cyclic graph, infinitism as the unrolled infinite tree — and I would like the translation done properly rather than gesturally. I am fluent in the proof-theoretic vocabulary and know little epistemology beyond the standard labels, so please build the mapping from my side.

Three things I want after the translation. First, whether coherentism and infinitism are distinguishable at all in this vocabulary, given that unrolling a cycle yields an infinite chain and a finitely generated infinite chain looks like a cycle — or whether their difference lies entirely in what each demands of the believer: finite surveyability versus merely possessing a procedure that generates reasons on demand. Second, whether anything in my framework can play the role the trace condition plays, that is, supply a formal criterion separating a virtuous circle from a vicious one; and if nothing can, whether that is a resource coherentism lacks or a sign that the analogy between soundness and justification is doing no real work. Third, what in the original debate has no counterpart here at all — the regress argument itself, the requirement that a reason be available to the reasoner, the step from "a structure of reasons exists" to "the belief is justified" — and, for each item of residue, whether it indicts my vocabulary or the epistemologists' claim. Please flag any place where you would be reading a formalist assumption into their position and calling it translation.
