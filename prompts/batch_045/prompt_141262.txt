I am about to start writing a paper on moral responsibility and would like the package of commitments I plan to build it on audited for joint tenability before I commit to them in print. The set is roughly Strawsonian in spirit, but two items in it are not part of the canonical package and I am not confident they fit the rest.

(1) Facts about who is responsible are not attitude-independent metaphysical facts waiting to be discovered and then matched by our responses. They are at least partly constituted by our practices of holding one another responsible, and the reactive attitudes are where those facts become available to us. I also accept the usual corollary: there is no standpoint outside the practice from which the practice as a whole could be assessed and found wanting, so the global question of whether holding people responsible is ever justified is, as posed, idle.

(2) The reactive attitudes are not under direct voluntary control, and they are not the sort of thing that can be required, forbidden, or owed at a time: an "ought" attached to resentment or guilt is at best elliptical.

(3) They are nonetheless fully assessable by reasons. Resentment can be disproportionate; it can track racial or class bias in a way that makes it a genuine fault rather than merely a psychological fact about the resenter; and I think we can and should try to reshape our patterns of response over time.

(4) The manipulation argument succeeds. An agent who satisfies the standard compatibilist conditions — reasons-responsive, values-integrated, acting without compulsion — but whose relevant values were covertly installed in her is not responsible. So the conditions on responsibility include something historical, about how the agent came to be the way she is, and not only about how she is now.

(5) Determinism as such is no threat: the thought that her action was ultimately up to factors beyond her control over-generalizes, and responsibility requires control without requiring ultimate origination. I also accept the familiar datum that resentment tends to lapse when one learns a causal-psychological story about why someone acted badly — childhood, a tumour, exhaustion — and I do not think that datum is mere sentimentality.

(6) What distinguishes blame from mere negative appraisal is that blame is a demand addressed to the wrongdoer for acknowledgement: a form of moral address that can in principle be answered.

(7) It can be apt to feel resentment toward wrongdoers who are long dead, and toward institutions — a corporation, a university — that profited and persist. I want to hold this even though no address in (6)'s sense can be received.

(8) The proper unit of assessment is not the individual episode of resentment but the pattern of a relationship over time. An episode that is apt considered on its own can still be criticisable in virtue of what it contributes to the pattern it belongs to.

I do not know where the trouble lies, which is the point of asking. Please tell me which of these pairs are in genuine tension and which only look like it, order the genuine ones by severity, and for each say precisely what would have to be given up and what that would cost me: I would rather revise one commitment badly than touch up all eight. Where two items are in tension only given a reading of one that I do not need to accept, say so, and where an item is doing less work in the set than I seem to think, say that too.
