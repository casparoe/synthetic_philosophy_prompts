ok so I had a three hour argument about this with my friend Dev last night and came away with the feeling that my package is probably inconsistent somewhere but I genuinely can't find the seam, and he was pointing at the wrong thing I think. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. The fundamental laws are time-symmetric. Nothing in the microphysics picks out a direction of time. (Dev kept banging on about kaons and T violation in the weak interaction and I don't know enough particle physics to answer him — it sounded like he was saying the laws already contain an arrow, which if true would wreck 2.)

2. Everything else — the thermodynamic arrow, the causal arrow, the radiative arrow, the fact that we have records of the past and not of the future — is one arrow, and it's all explained by the Past Hypothesis (the early universe was in an extremely low entropy macrostate) plus ordinary statistical reasoning over the microstates compatible with it. No extra primitive ingredients, no additional law. Entropy gradient all the way down.

3. Causal direction just is the entropic direction. For A to cause B is in part for B to lie in the higher-entropy direction from A. There is nothing more to causation than that.

4. So nothing deep privileges our direction. In a region where entropy happened to run the other way relative to us, there could be creatures whose memories and records point opposite to ours, and by their own lights they'd be correct. "Past" is close to being indexical.

5. I also think temporal passage is objectively real — there's a genuine flow and an objective present, and it isn't merely something our psychology does to us.

6. The asymmetry of deliberation is nothing over and above 4: we deliberate about the future and not the past simply because we're ignorant of the future, and that ignorance is just the epistemic arrow. No open-future metaphysics, no primitive fixity of the past.

7. Cosmology: I want to say that even granting a positive cosmological constant and a de Sitter future that goes on forever, we can still trust our records and our inferences to the past — we're ordinary observers, not Boltzmann brains that fluctuated out of thermal noise with fake memories — and I want to get that out of 1 and 2 without adding anything to them.

8. An actual confusion rather than a commitment: if the early universe was a hot, smooth, nearly uniform gas, that looks to me like thermal equilibrium, i.e. maximum entropy. So I don't understand in what sense the Past Hypothesis is claiming it was low entropy. Maybe I'm just missing something basic.

Dev's verdict was that 5 is obviously the one that has to go, that you can't have real flow sitting on top of 1 through 4.
