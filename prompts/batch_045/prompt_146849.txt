A small foundation where I work is about to reallocate its research budget. We fund in two areas, global public health and institutional reform, and a quiet background assumption in much of our strategy is that violence, in something close to the ordinary sense, has been falling over the centuries and can be expected to keep falling. If that is right, then money spent directly on reducing violence is money spent on a problem that is already solving itself, and our marginal dollar does more elsewhere. I have been asked to spend a modest sum on the prior question of whether that assumption is sound, and on what it would even mean for it to be sound. The sum is roughly six funded projects over three years, plus some in-house analyst time. It is conceptual and measurement work I can buy, not fieldwork: I can hire philosophers, historically minded social scientists, and people who think about index construction, but I cannot commission large-scale new data collection.

I do not want a survey of the debate, and I do not want a reading list or a set of names to consult; I want a defended agenda for this money. Here are the open questions I currently have on my list, offered as candidates rather than as a checklist I expect you to work through:

Whether there is a single quantity being tracked across epochs at all, or whether violence is a cluster concept whose extension has shifted, so that any long-run trend claim is really a claim about a weighted index over heterogeneous items such as homicide, war deaths, assault, state punishment, colonial conquest, domestic abuse, and the treatment of animals, with weights that are themselves normative rather than discovered.

What the right denominator is. Declines are standardly stated per capita per year, but one could instead measure per lifetime, per person-year actually exposed to risk, per unit of territory, or per unit of social contact; and since the number of people any one person can harm or be harmed by in a year has grown enormously with population density, mobility, and communication, a falling rate per capita per year may coexist with a rising rate per unit of exposure.

Whether realized violence is the right object of measurement, or whether a decline in events should be discounted by the growth in standing capacity for violence, the stockpiles, delivery systems, and targeting infrastructure that make harm available on short notice but need not be used, so that the ledger should include something like violence held in reserve at some discount.

Whether apparent reductions in one part of the ledger are offset by entries in another: geographic displacement toward poorer countries, conversion of physical harm into bureaucratic or economic coercion, deferral of harm onto future generations, or displacement onto nonhuman animals; and what accounting rule would tell us whether these are genuine offsets, mere redistributions, or category mistakes.

Whether the processes usually credited with the decline, state formation, commercial interdependence, literacy, changing norms, and demographic structure, are self-reinforcing or self-limiting, and whether the trend is a trend in a quantity we have structural reason to expect to keep moving in one direction.

And finally whether any of this is decision-relevant for us: how sensitive our actual allocation is to the sign, size, and fragility of the trend, given the other things we already believe about the badness of violence and the effectiveness of interventions aimed at it.

Please rank these, add the ones I have missed that you think matter more, and tell me which belong off the list entirely as pseudo-questions or as questions that collapse into technical choices a competent analyst can settle without philosophy. For each item I want the reasoning rather than the verdict: what other questions its answer is upstream of, how tractable it is in three years with the kinds of people I can actually hire, how neglected it is relative to its importance, and how much our grantmaking would change depending on how it came out. Be explicit where you are trading importance against tractability, and tell me what specific findings over the next two years would move an item up or down your ordering.
