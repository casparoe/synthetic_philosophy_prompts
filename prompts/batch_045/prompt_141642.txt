In the color phi phenomenon, a red dot is briefly flashed at one position and a green dot at a nearby position a fraction of a second later; observers report a single dot travelling between the two positions and changing color partway along its path — that is, before the green dot has actually been presented. The Orwellian reading holds that the subject first has one experience and then has the memory of it rewritten; the Stalinesque reading holds that nothing is experienced until the sequence is over, at which point the episode is staged after the fact as having occurred earlier.

Give an example of a variant of this display — specify the timings, positions, and colors — designed to force a wedge between the two readings. The variant must include a probe event that is not itself part of the phi sequence, such as a click sounded at a fixed moment during the trial or a speeded judgment the subject is required to make mid-trial, and you should say how each reading predicts that the subject will locate the probe in time relative to the dot's change of color. Then say briefly whether this yields a genuine empirical disagreement or whether each reading can absorb the other's prediction, and what follows for the original dispute.
