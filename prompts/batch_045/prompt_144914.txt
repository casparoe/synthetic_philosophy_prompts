You are a moral philosopher who also works on intergenerational ethics and the machinery of social discounting. Please think it through in stages: begin by brainstorming candidate readings freely and without judging them, then organize them, and only then start rejecting any. I want the work shown, not a conclusion delivered.

I work in the cost–benefit unit of a transport and water ministry. We are appraising a flood-defence scheme whose costs are front-loaded over the next decade and whose avoided damages run out roughly two hundred years. At a scoping meeting, a senior colleague killed a proposal to run a sensitivity case at a zero rate of pure time preference by laughing and saying, "Why should I care about posterity? What has posterity ever done for me?" The room treated this as decisive. I have since seen the same line on conference slides, attributed to all sorts of people; I do not care who said it first. I care about the work it was made to do. It was being used to keep a positive pure time preference term in the social discount rate — the part that is not explained by expected growth in per-capita consumption, by the risk that the project or its beneficiaries will not exist, or by the opportunity cost of capital — and at two hundred years that term is doing nearly all the damage to the benefit stream.

So: give me a menu of the precise theses that quip could be expressing, ordered roughly from the most deflationary to the most radical. For each reading I want four things.

1. The claim stated precisely enough to have a truth value, including what kind of claim it is: about morality, about individual prudence, about political legitimacy or constituency, or about psychology and the limits of imagination.
2. Your verdict: trivial, substantive and defensible, substantive but probably false, or not a normative claim at all — and if the last, tell me what work it is really doing in the argument.
3. Whether the claim, if true, actually entitles my colleague to a positive pure rate in this appraisal, or whether it supports at most something weaker: a shorter effective horizon, a different treatment of uncertainty, a constraint on whose interests count as stakeholder interests, a procedural requirement to consult present voters.
4. Its results on three tests I have been using to sort these apart. (a) The stipulation filter: stipulate away extinction risk, project failure, uncertainty about future preferences, changes in population size, growth in consumption, and any effect of our choices on who comes to exist. Which readings still generate any discounting once all of that is gone? (b) The block-shift test: compare two structurally identical packages — one in which the present generation bears the cost and a generation two centuries out receives the benefit, and another in which that entire sacrifice-then-benefit package is relocated two centuries further into the future, so that it is later generations bearing and later generations gaining, with us standing where we actually stand in both cases. A reading that ranks the nearer package higher merely because it is nearer is weighting temporal position as such; a reading that ranks them equally, or ranks them only because of growth, risk or identity, is licensing its discounting from something else, and I want that something else named. (c) The reverse-direction check: apply the reading to the generation immediately before mine, choosing about me. Is the resulting principle one the speaker would have wanted applied to himself, and is his own good fortune partly the product of its not having been applied?

Please also flag any item on the menu that is really two readings wearing one sentence, and say which single reading you think my colleague actually meant — the one that makes his intervention an argument rather than a joke.

Present the answer as a numbered list, one entry per reading, carrying the four fields above. Do not give me a bottom line that collapses the menu into a verdict; the disambiguation is the deliverable. Do not cite papers or authors — give me the claims themselves, and where a reading leans on a piece of technical machinery, define it in a clause rather than pointing at it.

Finally, in three or four sentences each, give the same compressed treatment to two replies I keep meeting, and say which items on your menu each one actually touches: first, that discounting a good merely because it falls later is a failure of imagination rather than a reason; second, that a rate we would not have accepted being applied to ourselves by our predecessors is one we should not apply to our successors.
