"Heritability is a number between 0 and 1 that tells you how much of a trait is due to genes rather than environment. It's estimated mainly from twin and adoption studies: identical twins reared apart turn out strikingly similar in things like IQ, personality, and even political attitudes, and since they share all their genes but none of their family environment, the similarity that remains has to be genetic. For IQ in adults the number comes out somewhere around 0.6 to 0.8, which is high, and it rises with age — by adulthood the shared family environment contributes almost nothing, which is why parenting matters less for how children turn out than most people assume. Importantly, heritability is a population statistic: it describes variation within a group at a particular time, so it never licenses claims about any particular individual, and it says nothing about whether a trait could be changed. A high heritability does not mean immutable — adult height is around 0.8 heritable, and yet average heights have climbed steadily with better nutrition."
