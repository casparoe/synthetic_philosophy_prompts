Draft the joint statement that a defender of a counterfactual analysis of causation — causation as counterfactual dependence along a chain of dependence, with closeness of worlds doing the work — and a defender of an interventionist analysis — causation as invariance of a generalization under interventions on a variable in a causal model — could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Begin with the claims both can sign, including claims about the standard hard cases: late preemption, symmetric overdetermination, causation by omission and double prevention, and the case where the two frameworks demonstrably converge rather than conflict — a closed two-variable system in which X and Y are perfectly correlated and no further variable is available, so that neither dependence under a fixed background nor any intervention performable within the system settles which variable depends on which.

Then state what remains in dispute, each disagreement phrased so that its own side would accept the formulation as its view: at minimum, whether intervention can be characterized without circularity; whether the counterfactuals that matter are those of surgical intervention or those of overall similarity of worlds, with the consequence that backtracking to a different past is permitted; and whether either account yields causal claims about a system into which no intervention is possible. On the last, have both sides address a deterministic, physically sealed universe of finitely many variables arranged in a closed cycle of dependence, with no external handles and no agent anywhere in the story — the counterfactual relations among the variables are perfectly determinate, while interventionism appears committed to silence about them — and record whether each side treats that commitment as a cost to its theory or as the correct verdict.

Then give the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each crux, name the case or argument both sides accept as bearing on it, write down each side's prediction in advance, and say whether the dispute at that point is genuine or merely verbal. Include at least chancy late preemption, where a first mechanism fires and an indeterministic backup never does, and the status of omissions.

Format: a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table, then a crux table.
