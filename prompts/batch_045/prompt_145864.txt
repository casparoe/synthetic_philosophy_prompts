You get a big payout at midnight for having formed, by then, the intention to drink a nasty but harmless toxin the next day at noon; you never have to actually drink it. Everyone argues about whether you can, and the fight is usually about whether intending to A requires believing you will A. But the set-up rigs that question, because it makes your post-payout prediction certainty: you know for sure you wont drink. What I actually want to work on is a graded version. Suppose the agents own evidence about what she will do tomorrow puts her at something like 0.4 that she will drink it, maybe because she has a long habit of honoring her resolutions, or because the money arrives through a channel that sometimes fails so she wont know whether it landed. Then the question stops being binary: is there a confidence threshold below which you cant intend, and is any such threshold a fact about what an intention is, or about when its rational to adopt one, or about when you count as having settled the matter for purposes of further planning?
