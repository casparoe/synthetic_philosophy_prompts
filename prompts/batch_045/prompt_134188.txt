So I've somehow ended up owning the risk register at the small housing association I work for — I'm not a risk person, I do comms — and the board wants, every quarter, a single likelihood × impact score for each risk so they can rank them and decide what to spend money on. I've been reading a bit about imprecise probabilities (upper and lower probabilities, intervals instead of point estimates, that old risk-versus-uncertainty distinction) and its obvious to me that half our entries just aren't single numbers, but I can't turn that into something I can actually run in the half-day I get per quarter, for a lay board who will only ever ask "so is it a 3 or a 4?".

What I want is a procedure I can reuse. Concretely: how do I decide whether a risk gets a point estimate or a range; how wide is too wide to be useful any more; when is collapsing a range to one number legitimate and when is it just fake precision; how do I rank two ranges that overlap — and I assume that sometimes the honest answer is "these dont rank, and here's what the board should do instead"; and how do I say anything about the register as a whole when most of our risks share the same underlying cause, so adding them up feels like double counting. One more thing I keep hitting: sometimes new evidence makes my range wider rather than narrower, and I want the procedure to have something to say about that rather than treating it as me messing up.

It has to be honest about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as method, and it has to be defensible by a non-specialist both to the finance director who signs it off and to an external auditor a year later.

Please run it on three entries and show the working: (a) our main repairs contractor going insolvent in the next 12 months, where we do have some sector base rates; (b) a fine under a new building-safety regime that hasn't actually started enforcing yet, so there's no base rate at all; (c) reputational damage from a social media storm, where our own response changes the probability. And include at least one case where the procedure gives an answer the board is going to hate. British English please, plain language, and as little notation as you can get away with.
