so I write sad books for a living — three of them now, all with endings people send me angry letters about — and I'm trying to get my own story straight about why anybody wants that. here's roughly where I've landed, and I want to know if it holds together:

(1) I don't think readers are enjoying real suffering; the schadenfreude-style explanation is a nonstarter for me.
(2) what a reader feels at the end of my third book is the same grief they'd feel at a funeral, not some pretend or watered-down version of it.
(3) fictionality is doing real work: swap in footage of an actual person's worst day and the enjoyment collapses. the fact that it didn't happen matters.
(4) the sadness is a cost, and what makes the whole experience worth having is everything else the book does — sentence-level pleasure, shape, the sense of an ending being earned — riding on top of it.
(5) part of what people are after is coming to see something true about loss, which is why a manipulative weepie is worse art even when it makes readers cry harder.
(6) methodologically, I don't want a theory that tells readers they're confused about their own experience. what they report is data.

two things from my own mail that I can't fit into (4): the readers who love the book most are the ones who say it wrecked them worst, and they definitely aren't treating the sadness as a price of admission; and if you could press a button and lift the sad part out while leaving the language, the characters and the structure identical, I think the book gets worse and they enjoy it less. I also keep coming back to what I call the reread problem — people on their fifth time through, who know exactly what's coming, report the grief as stronger, not weaker.

go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and how badly, and which only look like they are. for each real tension say what would have to give, rank them by severity, and then say which single revision cleans up the most. don't lead by telling me which one to drop — I don't know where the problem is, that's the whole reason I'm asking.
