In a reading group I help run on Dennett's crane/skyhook distinction, one member has started using the image as a general test: any explanation that accounts for something by appeal to something above it — a meaning, a norm, a reason, an agent's decision — is a skyhook, and so not an explanation at all; the only honest explanations are crane explanations, assembled from below out of the very stuff they lift. Another member has replied with two claims I can't adjudicate: first, that a real crane lifts at the level of girders, cables and counterweights rather than at the level of the molecules in its steel, so the image seems to license exactly the level-relative explanations that greedy reductionism (the demand that a genuine explanation always go all the way down) forbids, and to leave it unclear why the design-level story is the greedy one rather than the molecular one; and second, that some apparent skyhooks are themselves crane-built — the content of a norm constrains behaviour from above, but was shaped by a long, mindless, cumulative process — so the test as stated condemns explanations the crane picture ought to permit.

Audit the image as an argument. Give the correspondence table: what plays the load, the ground, the hook, the cable, the operator, the chain of cranes building cranes, and whatever would count as the sky. Then say which features of real cranes get smuggled into the target that the target does not have — that cranes are designed and installed by agents, that they stand on something outside the system they lift, that their lifting is fully accounted for by physics at every level, that a crane's load does not also do the lifting — and which of those imports actually matter for the two uses being made of it here. Finally, say what survives: is the no-skyhooks test a usable filter, or does it exclude what it should not; does the crane-built-skyhook reply rescue level-relative explanation or merely relabel it; and is there a better machine image for this work, or something the crane image is doing that no image can do.
