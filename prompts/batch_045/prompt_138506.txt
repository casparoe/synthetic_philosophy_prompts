I am assembling the first session of a graduate seminar on material objects, and before we look at any of the proposed answers I want the class to have the standard of assessment in hand. Please write the specification rather than the survey: what an adequate answer to the special composition question — the question of under what conditions some things compose a further thing — would have to deliver, with the case or consideration that motivates each requirement, and with each requirement marked as non-negotiable or as the preference of one camp.

Adequacy is relative to a purpose, so let the purpose be mine. I need an answer that could actually be used by a court asked to say whether some artifact is the same one over time: a cathedral rebuilt in different stone after a fire; a ship named in a will whose planks have all since been replaced; a digital object whose every token has been migrated to new hardware. And it should be usable by a field biologist counting organisms in a clonal colony. An answer that is silent or arbitrary in those cases is no use to me, however elegant it is in the armchair.

The requirements I keep returning to, which you should keep, discard, restate, or expose as confused, are these: it must return a verdict for every plurality, including for pairs of particles in a collider and for the totality of galaxies, and not merely for middle-sized things; it must not double-count causal powers, so that a composite's causal work at a time is never a plain duplicate of its parts' work at that time; it must be temporally local, in that whether the xs compose at t cannot depend on what happens at times remote from t; its verdicts must not shift with the interests of whoever is asking, since a court needs an answer that does not move with the litigants' purposes; it must be fully determinate, returning yes or no for any plurality with no in-principle indeterminacy; and it must be non-vacuous, not a principle that never fires or that merely redescribes the notion of composition it was meant to explain.

Add whatever I have omitted. Then tell me which of these is a genuine desideratum and which is only a prejudice of a camp — I suspect the determinacy requirement is smuggled in from a taste for tidy semantics. I also suspect that interest-invariance, determinacy, and any graceful treatment of the ship and the clonal colony cannot all be had together; if some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, identify it and show why, as that is the part I most want. And name the single requirement that excludes the most candidate answers. You may spend one sentence each on how the view that any things whatsoever compose something, the view that nothing has proper parts, and the view that composition occurs only where the result is alive fare against the two hardest requirements, and no more — I will do the scorecard in seminar.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence: either the requirement that is doing the real work, or the claim that the list cannot be jointly satisfied and where it breaks. Then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of my question.
