I'm a PhD student in public policy, and the third chapter of my dissertation is about how a large philanthropic funder with a fixed annual budget should allocate money across cause areas. I began from the importance/tractability/neglectedness framework that my funders actually use, and I have become convinced it is the wrong shape. Before I build on the replacement in chapter four, I would like someone to audit the set of commitments I have ended up with, because I cannot tell whether it is a framework or a pile of rationalisations. Stated as flatly as I can:

(1) Neglectedness, measured the usual way as how little money or how few people are already working on a cause, is a bad proxy and should be dropped as a separate factor. What it was ever trying to track is what I will call counterfactual marginal yield: expected welfare difference per additional dollar, where the counterfactual is what the other actors in that space would have done anyway. So there is really only one quantity I want, and importance and tractability enter only as its inputs, not as independent axes to be scored and multiplied.

(2) I am a cause-neutral expected-value maximiser in a strong sense: I accept that a sufficiently small probability of a sufficiently large payoff can legitimately dominate a ranking, and I reject the usual devices for blocking that conclusion (probability floors below which we refuse to look, discarding models with heavy tails, refusing to act on tail implications).

(3) In practice I nonetheless think a large funder should hold a diversified portfolio of, say, five to eight cause areas rather than concentrate in the top-ranked one, on the grounds that our cost-effectiveness estimates are noisy and the noise is correlated across causes.

(4) Some spending is yield-shifting rather than yield-taking: it changes the yield curve for later dollars instead of buying good directly on the current one, by building a delivery channel, training a cohort, or creating the option to spend well on a problem currently out of reach. I want this scored as a distinct consideration, added on top of counterfactual marginal yield.

(5) The whole thing has to stay usable by an allocation committee that decides quarterly from a spreadsheet; it cannot be a full dynamic decision tree over future funding rounds.

(6) I am also drawn to a thought that sounds like it contradicts (1): I would rather put money where there is already a concentration of competent people and working infrastructure, since my dollar rides on theirs, and the marginal yield of my dollar in a well-populated field can be higher than in an empty one.

Here is the case that makes me suspicious of myself. Cause A: scale of ten million welfare units if fully solved, forty per cent of it addressable with current methods, two billion a year already spent, and a counterfactual marginal yield for my extra million of about forty units, estimated robustly across many independent replications. Cause B: scale of one hundred thousand, ten per cent addressable, essentially no other funders, counterfactual marginal yield of about twenty-five units for my million, from a single study whose ninety per cent interval includes zero, and where the real argument is that the spending is yield-shifting: if it works it unlocks the remaining ninety per cent. On the only quantity I say I want, A wins. I keep finding myself wanting to fund B anyway, and I cannot tell whether that is (2), (3), (4) or mere contrarianism.

Please audit the set. I am attached to all six, which is presumably the trouble. Do not spend time telling me that all of this collapses into cost-effectiveness analysis; I know, and the question is which of the six has to give.

Structure the reply in this order, with roughly these lengths: Verdict (~100 words) — is the package jointly tenable; Tensions, most to least severe (~450 words) — each with what exactly has to be revised and what that revision costs me; Tensions that dissolve (~200 words); Cheapest repair (~200 words) — the single revision that cleans up the most and what it commits me to elsewhere; What I should have specified (~150 words).
