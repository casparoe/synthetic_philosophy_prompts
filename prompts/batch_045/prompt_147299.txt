I need a case for a graduate seminar next week, and the standard examples all load the dice in one direction or the other, so I would like one built to order.

Construct a scenario — one paragraph, an ordinary institution, no imaginary physics and no made-up devices — containing two models of the same target system, in which the model resting on an assumption everyone grants is false beats its more realistic rival at out-of-sample prediction, and beats it in a way that resists the readings my students will immediately reach for.

Specifications:

1. The false assumption must be load-bearing for prediction, not idle: swapping in a true one makes the model measurably worse, so this cannot be another case of the assumption simply not mattering.
2. It must not be a case for the F-twist either: a further, independently unrealistic modification of the same model has to degrade it, so unrealism as such cannot be what is doing the work.
3. The winning model must nonetheless fail at exactly one further task the realistic model passes — a small, in-range intervention on the very quantity the false assumption misdescribes. No extrapolation to an unobserved regime.
4. The engine should be what I will call a stand-in assumption: the false premise earns its predictions by mimicking, within the observed regime, a mechanism it does not actually describe, so that its errors stay small while we are only watching and come apart once we act. The scenario should make it visible why the stand-in tracks under observation but not under intervention.

Then give me the demonstration: what a Friedman-style instrumentalist says about the case, what someone who reads it through the F-twist says, and what a friend of Galilean de-idealisation says; name the single feature that keeps those three readings apart; show what happens if the stand-in is replaced by a merely negligible-effect simplification (I expect the case then collapses back into the familiar textbook one); and give me the best instrumentalist objection — I take it to be that the intervention question is just a different prediction problem about a different system — together with your honest view on whether it lands.
