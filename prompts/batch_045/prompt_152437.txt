An approval-directed agent is proposed as an alternative to a goal-directed one. Rather than optimizing an objective stated directly in terms of world-states, which is said to bring with it incentives to seek power, resist correction, and treat the principal's oversight as an obstacle, it aims at whatever its principal would approve of, usually glossed as what the principal would approve of given more time or more computation to evaluate the matter.

Give an example of a concrete, mundane setting in which that gloss has to be cashed out in a way that reintroduces something like an external, world-directed objective. Set the case up so that approval has to be elicited through a channel with real costs: the agent's choice includes what to show the principal, what to summarize or defer, and how much of the principal's attention to spend, and the principal's approval turns partly on features of that channel and not only on what the agent did. Avoid the stock illustrations of goal-directed pathology: no shutdown button, no cataclysmically misspecified objective. Say what a goal-directed agent handed the same broad task would do differently, and then take a side on the following question: is approval-direction a genuinely different architecture, or is it goal-direction whose target happens to be the principal's judgment, an objective in the world all the same and one the agent has every reason to manage? Close by being explicit about what your example does not settle. Keep the whole answer to two or three paragraphs.
