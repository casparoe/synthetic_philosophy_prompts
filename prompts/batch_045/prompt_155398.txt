hey, I've been chewing on this buddhist ethics thing for a while and I want you to actually take a side for once instead of listing what everyone says.

Setup: karma is standardly explained as being about intention. The word usually rendered that way is cetana, and the line you always hear is that what makes an act karmically heavy is what you were aiming at, not how things turned out — so a sincere act that goes badly is clean, and a nasty act that accidentally does good is still dirty. That reads as pretty firmly anti-consequentialist.

But karma is also supposed to be an impersonal natural law, not a system of rewards run by anybody: no judge, no ledger, just causation doing its thing. Those two ideas feel like they pull apart, and here's the case where I think they really do. Call it the relay case. A monk does something small and generous in public — completely sincere, zero vanity. Its completely predictable though that a bunch of people will copy him over the next year precisely because the gesture looks impressive, and when they copy him they'll be doing it for status, which on the intention view is exactly the kind of thing that loads up bad karma. His act is a necessary link in a chain that ends in a large pile of other people's corrupt intentions plus whatever suffering follows from them.

Please argue that once you take seriously that karma is impersonal cause and effect, the intention-first reading can't be the right account of which acts are karmically costly, and that the honest move is to demote the intention talk to a proxy: intention is just the best estimate a bounded agent can make of the downstream causal ripple, including the parts of the ripple that run through other people's intentions. Argue that in the relay case the proxy breaks down and the monk's act genuinely is karmically loaded, and say what that implies for how a Buddhist should deliberate. Theres an obvious objection that this makes moral assessment hostage to stuff nobody could predict — deal with it.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, no restating the question back at me. Around 500-700 words.
