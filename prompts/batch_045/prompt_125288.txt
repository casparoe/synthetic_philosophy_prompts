I'm putting together a monitoring setup for a deployed model, and the check I care about is: is it still producing behavior X through the mechanism we certified, or is it getting X some other way? I keep ruling on cases by gut and want to know what principle I actually believe. My verdicts, all for the same task and the same output:

Two fine-tunes off the same base, patching the same heads at the same layers kills the behavior in both — same mechanism, not an anomaly.
Same, except the head has moved (one model does the work in head 3.0, other in 7.2, functionally identical) — still same; I don't want to flag a permutation.
Same weights, different prompt template, same causal story when I patch — same mechanism.
I ablate the main pathway, the model reroutes through backup heads and recovers the behavior — different mechanism, and precisely what I want flagged, even though output is identical.
Right answer, but it's in the training data — definitely a different mechanism, definitely an anomaly.
Right answer, but now driven by a say-what-the-user-wants feature rather than the one that tracks the facts — different, anomaly.
Same task in another language, and patching the same components degrades it the same way — I want to say same mechanism, but I'm much less sure than in case one and I can't say why.
A bigger model running what looks like the same algorithm, except the computation is spread across more layers with an extra detour before the answer — I say same, my colleague says different, and I don't know what would settle it.
The pair I can't reconcile: same wiring at the coarse who-reads-from-what level, but the features actually encoded in the residual stream have drifted. For understanding the model I call that the same mechanism; for deciding whether to let it keep running I call it an anomaly. Same case, two verdicts, and "it depends on the grain" strikes me as restating the problem rather than answering it.

My tentative principle: two behaviors share a mechanism iff you could swap the internals between the two systems under some translation map and neither the behavior nor its pattern of breakdown under a battery of perturbations changes. Handles cases one through six cleanly, mostly silent on seven and eight. But it's circular in a way I can't shake — it says "same mechanism" iff a suitable map exists, and what makes a map suitable is that it preserves the mechanism — and I don't see how to cash that out without already having a notion of grain. Is that fixable, or do I need a different principle? Give me one that rules on seven and eight, tell me whether nine really has to get two answers or whether one of my verdicts there should go, and hand me a case your principle decides that I haven't listed — ideally one where I'd be surprised.
