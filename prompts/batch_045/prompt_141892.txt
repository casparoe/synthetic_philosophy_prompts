The bit I keep going in circles on is how much a heuristic argument is worth once you've said precisely what its independence assumption is. Call a heuristic m-robust if every model that reproduces the prime counts in each residue class mod q for all q up to m, and is otherwise maximally random, gives the same asymptotic *shape* for the quantity being predicted — same power of x, same power of log x — with the added local constraints only shifting the constant, and that constant converging to the Hardy–Littlewood singular series as m grows. The twins heuristic is m-robust for every m in exactly that way, which feels like it should be worth a lot: it means no local congruence obstruction is hiding a refutation.

But the two failure cases I know about both came from somewhere robustness can't see. The mod-4 race: every admissible local model says fair coin, and it isn't — the bias toward 3 mod 4 has logarithmic density around 0.9959 on the usual strong hypotheses, and the mechanism is the low zeros of a Dirichlet L-function. And li(x) > π(x) had every scrap of numerics on its side until Littlewood killed it in 1914, and again the explanation lives in the zeros. Both times the heuristic got the main term right and the next-order structure wrong. So either robustness is fine for a claim like infinitude, which only needs the shape, or the fact that both failures came from the one source local modeling is blind to should worry me alot more than it does. I genuinely can't tell which.
