Novelist here, mid-draft on a book set mostly inside a reinsurance office, and I've written myself into a corner. Two characters — an underwriter and a quant she keeps arguing with — were meant to have a two-page spat about probability, and it's turned into the structural spine of chapter six. The problem is I can't make it end, because I dont actually understand why it doesn't end.

Here's where they're stuck. The underwriter: the probability that this particular building floods next year isn't the sort of thing there's a fact of the matter about. What's real is the frequency across the class of buildings relevantly like it; if you quote me 0.04 for the single building you're just borrowing from the class and hoping. The quant: probability is just how confident I should be given what I know, class frequency is one input among many, and making probability be a frequency would disqualify it from exactly the cases we get paid to think about — one-off risks, new perils, a book of buisness with no history. Then they say it again louder, and nothing moves.

My working theory, and I want it checked not flattered: they aren't disagreeing about what probability is. They're disagreeing about what a probability statement is for — one wants it to be the thing that keeps a portfolio solvent across many cases, the other wants it to be the thing you'd bet at. Under that reading the fight is fake, or is really a fight about which job to optimise for. Two things make me doubt it. One: the underwriter sometimes presses an objection that sounds genuinely factual — that a number is unsupported, not merely unhelpful. Two: my quant has a line I invented, that frequency is just what confidence looks like once you've seen a lot of data, so the underwriter is doing the same thing with worse inputs. Is that a real position with a name, or am I making my character talk nonsense?

So: is this verbal, substantive, or two projects sharing a word? Is a hidden empirical question doing the work — whether the world contains irreducible single-case chances, say — or is it bedrock intuitions about objectivity versus usefulness neither of them could argue for? And what would progress even look like, so I can write an ending that isn't just somebody getting tired?

Also, since some of this is going in a character's mouth and readers who know the literature will notice: please keep clear what's actually settled among people who work on this versus what's genuinely contested, and flag anything you hand me that's a minority view wearing consensus clothes.
