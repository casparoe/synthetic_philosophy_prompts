I live in a limited-equity housing cooperative and was recently elected to the small committee rewriting our occupancy agreement: about a dozen enforceable clauses, one winter of meetings, and a volunteer lawyer. The drafting has turned on which conception of freedom the document should be built around — a non-interference clause (the co-op and fellow members stay out of your unit), a capability clause (the co-op secures your effective ability to live well there: repairs, accessibility adaptations, and now broadband), or a non-domination clause (no member stands to the arbitrary will of another, board and manager included, backed by some machinery for contesting decisions).

I need a defended ranking, not a survey. Which of these three actually changes what we would write, and which of the distinctions philosophers treat as load-bearing would collapse into drafting noise or into another clause? Concretely: does the non-domination clause buy anything over a non-interference clause plus appeal rights, once "arbitrary" has to be cashed out as something like "not required to track the interests of affected members as they could openly avow them"? And does the capability clause reach anything the other two cannot, or does it mainly license paternalism under a friendlier name?

Rank two further questions alongside these rather than treating them as separate. First: the board is piloting an automated system that triages maintenance requests and flags arrears for review, and I cannot tell whether domination does real work there or is just a vivid restatement of ordinary accountability problems. Second: what I have started calling agenda power — the unchecked capacity to settle which grievances are intelligible as grievances at all, so that a complaint nobody has a name for never reaches a vote. Is that a distinct wrong deserving its own clause, or a redescription of something the three frameworks already cover?

Tell me which of these deserve our scarce clauses and meeting time, and why: what criteria you are using (how much downstream drafting each choice settles, how contestable it is by members who are not philosophers, how much of it is merely symbolic), how you would trade those criteria off, and what would move an item up or down the list.
