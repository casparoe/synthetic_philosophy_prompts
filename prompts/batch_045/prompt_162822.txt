I am upgrading from MPhil to PhD next spring and need a 1,500-word proposal for the thesis I want to write. The topic is whether the Solomonoff-style vindication of Occam's razor survives the move from an ideal agent to one with bounded computation. I have a half-formed angle on it that I would like sharpened rather than replaced, and I would rather you engaged with that angle than wrote me a safe version of the standard project.

Here is where I have got to. The standard story: Solomonoff's prior weights hypotheses by 2 to the minus their Kolmogorov complexity, converges on any computable data-generating process, and has a bounded total expected error, so a simplicity-weighted prior dominates its rivals and that is what justifies preferring simpler hypotheses. Three things bother me. First, the dominance is asymptotic, and it is dominance only up to a multiplicative constant fixed by the true environment and not bounded in any useful way; at the horizons an agent actually faces that constant can swamp the advantage the razor is supposed to confer. Second, the invariance theorem says only that complexity is machine-independent up to an additive constant, so for the short hypotheses a bounded agent can actually entertain the simplicity ordering may be largely an artefact of the reference machine. Call the extent to which a hypothesis's rank in the simplicity ordering shifts when the reference machine is changed its fragility, and say that a razor is stable to degree d over a hypothesis class when at most a fraction d of pairwise rankings in that class flip under the change. My suspicion is that the razor earns its keep only for hypotheses whose descriptions are long relative to the invariance constant, and that the claim that simplicity pays may be close to vacuous in exactly the regime where real agents invoke it. Third, and this may be the real question: at bounded compute nobody applies the prior by weighting an enumeration; one searches hypotheses in order of increasing complexity and tests them. Does the justification transfer from weighting to ordering, and if so does it need a separate argument?

Please turn this into a proposal with: the question or questions stated tightly enough to have determinate answers; why the existing positions leave it open — the convergence and dominance results, the minimum-description-length tradition, the no-free-lunch results, and the resource-bounded or speed-prior approaches, but argued rather than catalogued; the method, year by year, including which parts genuinely fit a thesis and which I should cut; why that method rather than the obvious alternatives, such as proving another dominance theorem, running a simulation across programming languages, or doing an empirical model-selection study; what the possible conclusions are and what would decide between them, including whether a robustness-corrected razor could still have the convergence property or whether robustness and convergence pull apart; and an honest account of what would count as the project failing — for instance if fragility turns out not to be measurable in any non-arbitrary way, or if the whole problem collapses back into the standard asymptotic result.

Do not include a bibliography or any citations; I will add those myself. Please write the proposal in British English.
