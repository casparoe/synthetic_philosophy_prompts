I've been reading way to much about conventions lately — the David Lewis-ish idea that a convention is just one of several equally fine solutions to a coordination problem, which everybody ends up on not because it's the best one but because everyone expects everyone else to end up there — and I want a short story that makes that machinery visible without any character ever spelling it out.

Some constraints on the setting, so it isn't the usual fare: no roads, no sidewalks, no "which side of the corridor" business. Pick somewhere people who don't really know each other have to dovetail their actions over and over, and where getting it wrong costs something small but real — a bit of money, a bit of sleep, a bit of dignity.

Beats I want hit, roughly in this order:

1. Two or more ways of handling the recurring situation, and genuinely nothing to choose between them. It's arbitrary which one wins.
2. Something completely irrelevant to the problem but noticeable — a stain on a wall, something somebody once said offhand, a quirk of how the room is arranged — that's what actually breaks the tie. Nobody decides anything.
3. Then the hardening: soon people aren't just doing it, they think it's the correct way, they're faintly disgusted by people who don't, and they've got a story about why it's correct (hygiene, safety, respect, whatever). The story is invented; they don't know that. Bonus if there's a wierd second layer where pointing out that it's arbitrary lands as an insult rather than an observation.
4. Someone with actual authority notices that a different option would be better for literally everyone, and mandates it — notice, speech, new rule. It flops. I want the story to show why it flops in terms of what the mandate did and didn't change about what each person believes about what everyone else is going to do. Not stubbornness, not stupidity.
5. The switch to the better option happens anyway, later, and not because anyone got argued into it: a new cohort arrives, there's a scare, the furniture gets moved, something shifts what people notice.

The last beat should land on the part I find genuinely strange — that what everyone was treating as a moral or practical fact never had a reason under it, and the thing that replaces it won't either. Don't let a character explain that and don't have the narrator wink at me. Real scene, real people, something at stake, somewhere around 1200-1500 words.
