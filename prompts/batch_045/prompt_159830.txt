you are a professional philosopher of science — someone who has spent years on inter-theory reduction, the special sciences, and the metaphysics of emergence, but who also reads press releases and knows how the word gets abused. please think carefully and take your time. before you write the final answer, do a private brainstorm: dump twelve to fifteen candidate requirements that somebody or other has demanded of an account of emergence, then prune hard — keep only the ones you can back with a concrete case — and at the very end tell me which candidates you threw out and why. step by step, no rush, and don't hedge everything into mush.

here's the situation. i'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and i get maybe two embargoed studies a month where the key word is emergent. ant colony foraging trails. a convection cell. superconductivity. some protein folding thing. a new large language model capability. occasionally consciousness. half the time i think emergent is doing real work and half the time it's just a fancy way of saying complicated and surprising, and my editor wants an actual house rule for when we're allowed to print it with a straight face. last week we nearly ran a line about a swarm of robots having more-than-the-sum-of-its-parts behavior and i couldn't articulate what would have to be true for that to mean anything.

so what i want from you is the spec, not a survey. write me the desiderata: what any adequate account of emergence has to deliver to count as saying something, with the case or consideration behind each requirement, and mark each one non-negotiable, negotiable, or a prejudice of one camp. i'll say up front what it has to be adequate *for*, since i gather that matters: i need criteria that let a non-specialist decide whether a scientific claim of emergence is substantive, or whether it's a shrug in a lab coat. not criteria for settling a metaphysics seminar — criteria for deciding what's worth printing and what questions to ask the researcher on the phone.

the cases i want handled, at minimum (add better ones if you have them):

- temperature and pressure, where we all feel like we understand exactly how the macro quantity comes from the micro one, versus something like the wetness of water, where the macro property seems to have no home at the micro level at all
- a glider in the game of life: perfectly determined by the micro rules, completely invisible in them, and causally active at its own scale
- a hurricane, or a convection cell: persists while its constituent matter turns over completely, and only exists while energy is flowing through it
- a traffic jam, or an ant foraging trail: a macro pattern that individual members respond to and that none of them represent, and that survives the total replacement of its parts
- protein folding, where you can in principle simulate every atom and still not have explained why *this* fold, and where the same fold shows up in chemically quite different sequences
- the maximal case people actually care about: consciousness, where a good chunk of the audience is hoping emergence does metaphysical work
- a gerrymandered whole: my hurricane plus one specific nitrogen molecule over the sahara. nothing interesting is true of that conjunction, but i want to know which plausible criteria accidentally let it in, and what has to be added to keep it out
- the reverse direction: spacetime, or the micro laws themselves, being emergent from something deeper. i don't want a definition that is quietly scale-chauvinist and builds in that small things are the base and big things are the emergent ones

distinctions i want the account to respect rather than collapse: emergent-for-us versus emergent-in-itself; emergence versus mere complexity or surprise; emergence versus outright inexplicability; the property being emergent versus the object or system; and the big one — irreducible-because-unpredictable versus irreducible-because-not-derivable-even-with-bridge-principles versus irreducible-because-having-novel-causal-power-of-its-own. those three get run together constantly in the copy i read and i want them prised apart.

there are also three tests my newsroom has been kicking around that i suspect are partly garbage, and i want your verdict on each, separately: (1) the laplace test — nothing counts as emergent unless a demon with complete micro facts and unlimited computing power still couldn't have predicted it; (2) the swap test — if you can swap out the substrate and keep the macro variable and the pattern still holds, that's the mark of the emergent; (3) the funding test — a genuine emergence claim is one that licenses a separate science with its own laws and its own budget, rather than more application of the base science. for each: genuine desideratum, defensible-but-optional, or prejudice dressed up as a requirement? and are any two of them secretly the same requirement wearing hats?

then the part i most want. flag any pair of desiderata you think can't be jointly satisfied. i'm especially suspicious of the tension between a macro phenomenon having genuinely novel causal power of its own and the micro-level story being causally complete — if the micro facts already fix everything and already do the causing, it's unclear what causal work is left, and i want you to say whether that means the list is jointly unsatisfiable and something has to give, or whether there's a formulation that survives. i also want the tension between emergence requiring genuine surprise and emergence being something a theory can explain and predict. if the list as a whole can't be satisfied, say so plainly — that's a result i can use.

after that, tell me which *single* requirement does the most work in excluding candidate accounts, i.e. which one is doing the real filtering, and which requirements are the ones people only demand because they already like a certain theory.
