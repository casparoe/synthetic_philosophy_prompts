Write an in-universe annual report of the Bureau of Comparative Violence Statistics — a fictional UN-affiliated body — announcing that global violence has fallen to its lowest level in recorded history, the twelfth consecutive record low. Format it as a press release: headline, standfirst, about 700–800 words of body broken into numbered methodology notes, plus a short sidebar.

The comedy has to come from getting the measurement problems right, so that a reader who knows the debate over whether violence has declined laughs rather than winces:

- Per capita vs. absolute. The Bureau treats per-capita rates as canonical and dismisses absolute body counts as denominator artifacts. Its Denominator Management Unit has kept the rate falling partly by broadening who belongs in the denominator: everyone alive at any point in the year, then everyone who has ever lived, and, briefly, all mammals, before the Oversight Committee called that denominator fraud.
- Definition creep. When the Definitions Committee recognizes a new kind of violence (structural, symbolic, slow, epistemic), the new category is at once quarantined in a supplementary index so the headline series is never disturbed. A chart footnote quietly shows what the trend looks like if the indices are consolidated.
- The unit of account. Violence is denominated in intent-weighted death-equivalents, with a published conversion table: a deliberate battlefield killing 1.0; a foreseeable famine death 0.3; a death foreseen but not aimed at 0.15; a threat never carried out 0.02. The factors, a note explains, are revised annually for consistency with the target.
- Sample size. Everything before the modern census era is extrapolated by the Arrowhead Extrapolation Unit from a small number of excavation sites, with a confidence interval wide enough to contain both a golden age of peace and a charnel house.
- The meta-question, played straight. Let a passage concede in deadpan that the series is robust within the measurement framework, and let the reader feel the circularity without anyone naming it.

Close with the Bureau's pilot initiative, described with full bureaucratic enthusiasm: since the rate is already indistinguishable from zero once the denominator includes everyone who will ever live, the Bureau proposes retiring the concept of violence altogether in favor of unauthorized force incidents, a category for which it has no historical data whatever — and therefore a clean baseline. Include one quotation from an external archaeologist whose objection is inconveniently well-founded, and one from a Bureau spokesperson who answers it by changing the subject to the direction of the trend.
