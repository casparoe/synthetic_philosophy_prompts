Two definitions so we don't talk past each other. Call a target performative if the system's own published output can shift the value of the thing being predicted — election results, market prices, whether a user clicks, how fast a rumour spreads. Call a system epistemically contained if its training signal is a strictly proper scoring rule applied to one query at a time, so that reporting its honest belief is its best move, and it never optimizes over its own future inputs.

My opening: epistemically contained systems are exactly the ones nobody will pay much for. A proper scoring rule only pins down honest reporting when the prediction doesn't feed back into the target. Once the target is performative, "report your honest belief" stops picking out a unique thing to say — there's a family of self-fulfilling fixed points, and a system that picks among them is steering the world through its output, which is agency coming in the back door. And the performative targets are the commercially valuable ones, so deployment pressure will always be toward them. Your program either builds systems nobody deploys, or it builds agents and calls them tools. Which is it?
