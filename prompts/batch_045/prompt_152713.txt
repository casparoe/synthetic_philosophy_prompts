After a career in experimental condensed-matter physics I have, in retirement, been working my way into philosophy of law, and a small circle of us has taken up Nomic — the game in which the rules include rules for changing the rules, so that any rule, including the amendment rule itself, may in principle be amended. I am used to formal systems in which the object language and the metalanguage are kept apart; here they are not, and the resulting loops are the part that interests me.

We want to adopt one amendment procedure as our standing one for the coming year, and I would rather reason about it than ballot on it. Set aside strategic voting: assume six players acting in good faith, and judge the procedures only on how they handle paradoxes of legal self-reference — a validly adopted change that undermines the procedure which authorised it, or two rules each of which validly governs the amendment of the other.

The candidates I have drafted:

(a) Flat majoritarianism — any proposal, including one rewriting the amendment rule, passes on a simple majority, and the new amendment rule takes effect at once and is understood to authorise its own adoption.

(b) Grandfathered self-amendment — a proposal altering the amendment procedure is voted on under the old procedure; the new procedure governs only proposals introduced in later rounds.

(c) Entrenchment by class — rules are ordinary or entrenched; a rule's class can itself be changed, but only by a two-thirds vote, and any change to the amendment rule counts as entrenched.

(d) Precedence numbering with cycle-detection — every rule carries a precedence number; where a proposal's validity depends on a rule of lower precedence the conflict is escalated, and where escalation returns to its starting point the proposal fails and the ruleset is untouched.

(e) Temporal quarantine — changes to the amendment rule take effect only after a delay of one round; during that round no further proposal touching the amendment rule is admissible, and if two such proposals are in conflict both lapse.

(f) Snapshot composition — every proposal carried in a round is evaluated against the ruleset as it stood at the opening of that round, their effects are applied in a fixed order, and any proposal whose effect cannot be computed under that order is void.

Please sort these into tiers, most robust first, with a score from 0 to 10 and one sentence of justification each; then name the single failure mode you would expect to bite first in actual play, and say whether any pair of the six would be worth combining.
