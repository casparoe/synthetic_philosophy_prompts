I have just come away from a long and rather unsatisfying argument with an old friend who works as a research hydrologist, and I want to turn what went wrong in it into a final-exam question. Her position, pressed with considerable impatience, was that any conviction for which you cannot specify in advance what would count as evidence against it is not a belief but a prejudice, and that good scientists retire exactly those convictions by finding data that contradict them. I found myself defending the Wittgensteinian thought that some commitments are not held on evidence at all but are rather what makes evidence possible — and I defended it badly. Two things undid me. She asked how, on my account, you tell the difference between such a commitment and a paranoid delusion, given that both are held in the face of what the rest of us call counter-evidence. And she pointed out that her own field maintains formal procedures for retiring the assumptions its instruments presuppose, which on my account ought to be impossible. I want my students put under precisely that pressure, in a form in which reciting the vocabulary of the topic gets them nothing.

The setting: a third-year undergraduate epistemology seminar, closed-book final, ninety minutes for a single question worth 25 marks, with students expected to write about 900 words. They have read the primary text — Wittgenstein's late notes published as On Certainty — and a general survey of epistemology. Assume no knowledge of the secondary literature, and do not let the question or the answer depend on it: I want no reading list, no citations, no "as so-and-so has argued", and no move that is licensed merely by attributing a view to a commentator. Every move in the model answer should be one a strong student could have produced from the primary text plus argument.

Here is the case I want built into the question. The Kestrel Hydrographic Office has charted the Kestrel Bank for a century. Its survey method treats the Bank as geologically stable: soundings taken years apart are pooled into a single depth model, and an individual anomalous sounding is discarded as instrument error. That the Bank does not move is not something the Office has evidence for; it is what makes the pooling of soundings into one chart possible at all. Over thirty years the proportion of discarded anomalies has risen steadily, and eventually the director invokes the Office's standing revision protocol: when more than one sounding in five must be discarded, the stability assumption is to be retired and replaced by a model in which the Bank migrates. The retirement is authorised by counting discarded anomalies — that is, by a procedure that itself presupposes the very assumption being retired. Meanwhile a separate team, the Tidalists, has for some years produced independent estimates of the Bank's position by satellite altimetry, a method that never presupposed stability; they argue the assumption was testable all along. The Kestrel Bank is invented, so please do not try to look it up, and do not import facts about real hydrography or real satellite methods into the case.

Please produce:

1. The question itself, in a form I can paste into the paper, with sub-parts and marks allocated (I have in mind something like: a part demanding an account of what it is for a proposition to play this framework role, and how that differs both from being a well-confirmed empirical hypothesis and from being merely something no one in the community thinks to question; a part asking whether the director's retirement of the assumption is a rational revision, given that the only evidential standards available are ones the assumption itself makes possible; and a part in which a critic says the framework account cannot distinguish the Office's assumption from a patient's conviction that his food is being poisoned, and so either collapses into relativism or must smuggle in an external standard — with the student required to say what work, if anything, the Tidalists' independent method does).

2. A model answer of roughly 700 words, written as a strong but not miraculous student's script rather than as a polished paper. It should, among other things, distinguish revision-as-conversion from revision-as-evidence-response and take a stand on which the protocol enacts; say what functional difference between a framework commitment and a delusion could do the work without appealing to "it is one we accept"; and say whether the relativity of framework-hood to a practice is a relativism about anything that matters.

3. A marking scheme organised around the specific moves a top script must make, with the marks each is worth out of 25, and, for each move, the one characteristic way candidates get it wrong. I especially want named the error of treating framework propositions as basic beliefs in the foundationalist sense, the error of answering the delusion objection by saying the patient's belief is false, and the error of resting on "cannot be doubted" understood as psychological impossibility. Finish the scheme by stating the single move that most reliably separates an A script from a B script.

4. After the model answer and the marking scheme, a JSON object — valid JSON, nothing after it — with this schema:

must_hit_points: an array of objects, each with keys id (short string), part (which sub-part it belongs to), claim (one sentence: what the candidate must establish), marks (integer), top_move (one sentence: the specific move that earns full marks), common_failure (one sentence: the specific way a weaker script falls short).

misconceptions: an array of objects, each with keys misconception (string), diagnostic (the kind of sentence in a script that reveals it), marks_lost (integer).

boundaries: an object with keys distinction, merit, pass (integers: the mark thresholds).

discrimination_note: a string of one or two sentences on which single move separates the top band from the one below.

case_revisions: an array of objects, each with keys issue (anything in the stem that is ambiguous enough to let a candidate wiggle out of the question or that makes an intended answer unavailable) and fix (a concrete rewording).
