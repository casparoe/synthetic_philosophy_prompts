Since retiring from a career in chemical kinetics I have joined a philosophy reading group, and there one meets a comparison treated as near self-evident: a polity is like an organism. It is born, it matures, it grows rigid, it senesces; its death is a datum to be explained by pathologies rather than by accidents; and so the outside view on any present-day state should be read off a survivorship curve with the organismic shape, juvenile fragility followed by a plateau of robustness followed by a hazard of death that climbs with age, the Gompertz pattern I spent years fitting to failure-time data. The conclusion drawn from it is that a great power two centuries into its run must be late in its life, and that its own inside view of exceptionalism is exactly the bias the outside view exists to correct. I am not convinced. What I have seen of quantitative work on how long states last suggests a hazard that is roughly flat or even falling with age, closer to the memoryless decay of a radionuclide than to the senescent mortality of a mammal, and if that is right the analogy is not merely loose but is carrying the conclusion in the wrong direction. Before I raise this with the group I would like your audit. Set out the correspondence as you see it, what plays the role of birth, of maturity, of senescence, of death, and of a cause of death; say which features of organisms empires lack that matter for this use and which missing features are harmless; tell me where you take the hazard-rate question to stand and what a record-breaking lifetime ought to do to our confidence; and say whether the political conclusion survives if the mapping is redrawn along memoryless lines. Add, if you will, a case of my own devising: a federation whose provinces may secede without the whole thereby ceasing to exist, so that it can shed most of its territory and population and still count, in its own eyes and everyone else's, as the same polity. Does the organism comparison say anything useful about that, does the decay picture do better, and is there some third source domain you would substitute for either? One request of form: answer in continuous prose, as to a correspondent, with no headings, no bullet points, no bold type, no tables, and no enumerated lists.
