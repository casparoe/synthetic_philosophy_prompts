Hey — I'm in a little cog-sci reading group and last week we went through Dennett's four kinds of minds, the tower of generate-and-test thing: Darwinian creatures that get all their competence the slow way, by selection over generations; Skinnerian ones that can be shaped by reinforcement inside a single lifetime; Popperian ones that can pre-test candidate actions in an inner environment, so that bad hypotheses die instead of them; and Gregorian ones whose brains get re-tooled from the outside by culturally inherited thinking-tools, words above all. We spent most of the evening on the obvious question of what each upgrade actually costs, and someone joked that no risk committee would ever have approved any of them. That joke is what I want you to write.

So: a satirical bureaucratic document, deadpan throughout, in which a regulatory body reviews the three upgrades and, of course, rejects each one. My suggestion for the frame (change it if you have better) is a Regulatory Impact Statement issued by the Directorate of Fixed Action Patterns, whose Standing Committee on Adaptive Risk — imagine members with names like Commissioner Mugwump, Dr. Skeuomorph, and the Secretary for Innate Release Mechanisms — works through three items in ascending order: an Application for Operant Enhancement, an Application for Internal Preselection, and an Application for Word-Tool Installation. Each item is then kicked downstairs to a subcommittee made of creatures at the level below the one being proposed, so the Skinnerian application is judged by hardwired Darwinians, the Popperian one by the Subcommittee on Covert Behavior, and the Gregorian one by the Popperian Office of Pre-Testing. Format it as numbered agenda items with Resolved clauses, at least one minority dissent, a couple of footnotes from the Secretary, and an Appendix of Definitions written in totally sincere jargon; 800 to 900 words.

The humour has to come from getting the biology and the theory right, so please work in the real costs and the real objections rather than inventing silly ones: the Darwinian committee insisting that fixed action patterns are already the product of aeons of free R&D, that learning only pays off if the world is stable within a lifetime but not across generations, that a learner has to pay for its lessons with its own body, that nothing learned may be written back into the genome (someone should raise the Baldwin-effect loophole, i.e. that behaviour can drag the selective environment around and so let soft inheritance in by the back door, and the lobbyists for the applicant will absolutely exploit it), and that a conditioned habit cannot be audited the way an innate routine can. The Skinnerian board should object to Popperian pre-testing on the grounds that covert behaviour is unobservable and therefore unregulable, and that an inner arena gets reinforced by accidents, which — per their own classic finding about pigeons who develop pointless rituals because a reward happened to land once — will manufacture superstition; they can also grumble that calling rigid hardwired behaviour sphexish is Gregorian propaganda. The Popperian office should block the word-tools on the grounds that they reorganise a brain from the outside, that most candidate thoughts are junk, that catchy items out-replicate true ones, that a creature equipped with words can be terrified by a danger it has only been told about, can be marched off to die for a slogan, will confabulate free-floating rationales for what it did, will end up believing in belief, will install a little Cartesian theatre as a user illusion, and will report its own workings so unreliably that the office would need something like heterophenomenology just to process the paperwork. Somewhere in there I want one member to state, out loud and sincerely, the winning argument for the upgrade he is voting against — the point that each level is valuable because it shrinks the set of candidates that have to be tested on the actual neck — and the Chair should rule it out of order. End with a resolution that bans all three upgrades while quietly grandfathering in a licensed pilot programme, so a reader who knows nothing about Dennett can still see the tower being built by people who hate it. Please don't quote or imitate Dennett's actual prose, and don't wink at the reader: no asides, no modern slang in the committee's voice, no punchlines that step outside the document.
