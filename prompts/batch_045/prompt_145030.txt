Suppose you are advising the director of a small philanthropic fund that can support about five researchers full-time for four years, all on conceptual work: philosophy and conceptual analysis, plus enough formal and empirical bridge-work to make the concepts precise. The fund's mandate is to determine whether and how a lexical threshold negative utilitarian view should redirect giving away from present-day human welfare causes. On the version of the view the director has in mind, suffering and happiness are commensurable in principle, but suffering above some threshold of intensity or duration is lexically prior: no amount of mild pleasure, however widely distributed, outweighs a single episode of suffering above that threshold.

Two complications she wants you to take as given. Call the first the threshold placement problem: because the disvalue of intense suffering plausibly outruns any finite rate of compensation, the threshold cannot be located by eliciting an exchange rate between pleasures and pains; it has to be located by asking how bad the worst possible experiences are, which turns a question in value theory into one about the conceptual and empirical upper bounds on suffering. Call the second the uptake asymmetry: suffering-focused arguments, even when granted, seem to move actual allocations far less than arguments about creating enormous amounts of happiness do, and it is unclear whether the remedy is more philosophy, better framing, or nothing.

Which four or five questions should the fund prioritize, and in what order? I care much more about the reasoning than the list. State the criteria you are using, including at least importance to the allocation decision, tractability for philosophers rather than neuroscientists, how much other work the answer is upstream of, and how much it matters whether anyone would act on the answer; explain where these criteria conflict; and say what evidence over the next two years would move a question up or down. In particular, argue for or against treating the uptake asymmetry as a reason to fund persuasion and framing work instead of threshold placement.
