I'm retired from a career in isotope geochemistry (mostly lake sediments, nothing glamorous) and have been sitting in on a philosophy of science reading group, and the Anthropocene argument has got under my skin. I want to know what principle my gut verdicts add up to, if there is one. Here they are.

- The 1610 candidate, the CO2 dip and American pollen showing up in European lake mud after the die-off and reforestation in the Americas: yes, that's a real boundary.
- The mid-20th century one, bomb plutonium and the Great Acceleration, the one the working group actually put forward for a golden spike at Crawford Lake and which got voted down in 2024: I dislike it and I think it's wrong, but I can't deny it's the better marker by any standard I can actually state.
- The early farming / rice methane one, thousands of years back: no. That's a ramp, not a boundary, even though it's closer to the true cause then either of the above.
- A diachronous version, where it starts around 1800 in England, 1960 in the Amazon, and never really in Antarctica: I hate it and can't say why.
- Procedure: I think the stratigraphers' vote was the right way to settle it even though I think it settled it wrong. But I also think the date is something you discover, not something you decide. Those can't both be right.

My own stab at a rule: a marker only counts if (a) a geologist with no written history and no idea we ever existed would pick out the same layer, call it the blind stratigrapher rule, and (b) the signal would still be readable after the rock has been buried, folded and half cooked, call it the cooked-rock rule. Test those against my five verdicts, repair or replace them, tell me which verdicts I have to loose, and say what your principle implies about two cases I haven't listed: the level at which domesticated animal biomass first exceeds wild mammal biomass, and the suggestion I heard someone make that the Anthropocene isn't a slice of time at all but an event, more like the Great Oxidation than like the Holocene.

Please answer in American English and go easy on the jargon, I'm still learning it.
