In every safety discussion I end up in, somebody eventually says AI should be built the way planes are: aircraft are certified so that a catastrophic failure is supposed to be vanishingly unlikely (I've seen one in a billion flight hours quoted, no idea whether that's a real requirement or just folklore), nobody would accept "on average this aircraft lands", and we got there by treating every crash as a design failure rather than a cost of doing business. It's always wheeled out to license the same conclusion: average-case eval numbers are basically worthless and we should refuse to deploy frontier systems until we have worst-case guarantees, the kind only formal verification could really give.

Two things came up last week that I think wreck it, but I can't tell whether they do. First, what we'd actually be certifying isn't one airframe flying a million hours and slowly building up a base rate. It's closer to a million copies of the same airframe each flying one hour, in environments nobody has inspected, with passengers who are actively trying to make it crash. Second, aviation learns from crashes; here the first one might be the last one, so there's no accident record to converge on. Someone else in the room said the better model is drug approval: accept known rates of harm, ship, monitor, iterate.

I want an audit rather than a verdict. Give me the correspondence table for the aviation analogy — what plays the flight hour, the crash, the regulator, the black box, the maintenance schedule, the test pilots, the airworthiness certificate — then which of the disanalogies genuinely bear on the conclusion and which are decoration. Then the actual question: does the no-deployment-without-worst-case-guarantees claim survive once the million-copies-and-hostile-passengers part is on the table, does the drug-approval analogy support the average-case side any better, or is there a third thing that fits more closely? And tell me honestly at the end whether either analogy is doing real argumentative work or whether they're just two vivid ways of restating a prior about how much risk is acceptable.
