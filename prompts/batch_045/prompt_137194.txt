so here's the thing. I'm a retired physical chemist — thermodynamics mostly, thirty-odd years of calorimeters and grant forms — and I've come to philosophy late, a couple of years of reading in my retirement, so I may be missing something obvious. But what keeps bugging me about panpsychism is this: once you say that experience is the intrinsic nature of matter, and that physics only ever describes structure and relations, you've got no principled way of saying which physical goings-on feel like anything at all, let alone like something bad. And yet everyone who writes about the ethics of it wants to draw conclusions about what we owe to what. So I want you to write me a parody that takes that seriously.

Write it as an official government consultation document: a draft circular from something like a Department of Experiential Welfare, issued for public comment, about 800 words, laying out a proposed National Valence Offset Scheme. The premise is that if micro-experiences carry valence, then any process that shoves matter around incurs an experiential cost, and that cost can be inventoried, audited and offset exactly like carbon. The Department should be entirely sincere and mildly pleased with itself.

The bits I'd like in it:

- A definitions section that has to make a ruling on the combination problem. Have the Department settle it by statutory fiat: a "phenomenal consolidation discount" (invent the label, something like that) under which a system's moral weight is divided by the number of micro-subjects it's presumed to contain, so a kilo of copper is legally valence-neutral despite hosting something like 10^25 of them. Nobody in the document should notice that this just assumes what the combination problem was asking.
- A provisional psychophysical proxy. Since physics can't tell us which quantity valence tracks, the Department indexes it to distance from thermodynmic equilibrium, or to rate of entropy production — systems relaxing gently toward equilibrium are credited with positive valence, systems held away from it are debited. Then let it follow where it follows: a running fridge, an MRI magnet, a growing child, a compost heap, a supernova. I want a worked-examples table in strait-faced accounting style, with units ("standard hedonic units" per mole per hour, or whatever you fancy).
- An offset market: registered projects that generate negative-valence credits — deep Antarctic ice, abyssal sediment, anything already at equilibrium and left alone — with a seperate note on additionality and leakage.
- At least two quotes from named fictional stakeholders: say the chair of a Valence Standards Board, and a dissenting member who's a neuroscientist convinced that subjectivity requires a thalamus; if there's room, a spokesperson from an animal-welfare charity furious that livestock have been reclassified as a rounding error.
- The punchline should be structural rather than a joke. The Scheme's long-term target is net zero valence, and the circular should note, without alarm or comment, that full compliance is achieved only at universal thermal equilibrium — the heat death of the universe — and then move on to the consultation timetable.

Keep it deadpan the whole way: no winking, no "critics might object" section inside the document, no authorial rescue at the end. It should be funny to someone who knows this literature — the swamping problem, mind dust, the arbitrariness of the psychophysical link, the combination problem — because the absurdity comes out of those real features rather than out of anything invented, and it should still be readable and funny to my book group, who've never heard of any of it. Don't have the document explain panpsychism; an official circular wouldn't.
