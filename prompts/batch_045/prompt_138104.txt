write me a parody of one of those blameless internal postmortems we write after an outage, for a company that runs a typogenetic compute platform: customers submit strands, a pool of enzymes (the "runtime") walks along them copying, cutting, switching and deleting bases, and every enzyme is itself decoded from some strand, so the program, the data and the interpreter are all the same substance. about 500 words, sections like Summary / Impact / Timeline / Root cause / Action items, deadpan all the way through — no winking at the reader, no explainer footnotes, the jokes have to come from getting the mechanics right so an actual nerd laughs instead of winces. feel free to invent enzyme names, team names, ticket numbers.

the incident: our flagship product is a self-replicating strand — one whose decoded enzymes rebuild that exact strand, the quine-ish thing the DNA/Gödel isomorphism crowd goes on about. it stoped replicating the week we rotated the genetic code table for security (new triplet-to-amino-acid mapping, identical chemistry). the engineers' proposed fixes should each quietly be a real philosophical problem in disguise: you can't unit-test a strand because what a strand does depends on which enzymes happen to be floating in the soup, you can't cold-start the cluster without shipping a hand-written primordial-soup image, "code" and "machine" keep dissolving into each other, and nobody can say where the formal system ends and the implementation begins. end with action items that are obviously cosmetic.
