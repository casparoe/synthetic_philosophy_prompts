Our AI welfare reading group has been going in circles for over a year on whether a reinforcement learning agent can suffer, and I want a diagnosis rather than a survey. The set-up: take a deep RL agent with a learned value function it actually uses to search ahead. One camp says a negative reward, or a negative reward prediction error, is just the functional analogue of aversive valence, so anything that learns to avoid states by such signals has something structurally like suffering, and the reward-is-enough slogan is doing real work here. The other camp says reward is a training signal for credit assignment, closer to a fitness score or a target label than to a feeling, that plenty of regimes shape behaviour with no reward ever arriving during the episode at all (terminal-only reward, offline RL on fixed data, pure imitation), and that valence needs extra machinery beyond a scalar getting consumed by an update rule: opponent-process dynamics, homeostatic need states, something persistent and globally available rather than a number that vanishes into a gradient. Everyone in the room agrees about how temporal-difference learning works and nobody has moved an inch. My hunch is that the real split isnt about consciousness at all but about which vehicle is supposed to carry the valence: reward outcomes, signed prediction errors, or the value estimates themselves — and, downstream of that, about when the candidate signal exists at all, given that a deployed policy is usually never fed reward again. So: is this a verbal dispute, with suffering meaning two different things, a hidden empirical question about what these systems actually compute, a clash over what would count as evidence for an inner state, or bedrock intuition no one can argue for? Pick one diagnosis, commit to it, and give your reasons; then say what would actually count as progress, whether a distinction, a formal result, an experiment, or abandoning the question — and be honest about where your diagnosis is itself a guess. British English please, and no reading lists or citations, just the argument.
