So don't explain the engine side to me, and don't assume I know the philosophy jargon.

What I want is a translation, done as honestly as you can, of four bits of Berkeley's idealism into my vocabulary — and then, which is the part I actually care about, a report on what didn't survive.

The four bits:

1. The so-called master argument: he dares you to conceive of the trees in the park existing unconceived, and says the task destroys itself, because in trying to think of them as unconceived you have to think of them, so all you've managed is to conceive of them as conceived.
2. The likeness principle: an idea can only resemble another idea, so the supposed material thing behind our ideas can't resemble them — and worse, we can't even say what it would be for it to resemble them. Comparison starts to look like a type error.
3. The test for real versus imagined: real perceptions are forced on us, steady, vivid, orderly, independent of what I will; what I can summon and dismiss at will is imagination. That's what separates the tree in the park from the tree in my head.
4. The bit where the tree in the park keeps existing while nobody's in the park, because there's a perceiver that never stops perceiving — I think he brings God in for this.

Do each one into my vocabulary, then the residue: what mapped cleanly, what only went through once you fudged it, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. Specific things I want you to get at:

- (1) reads to me like it's just saying you can't point at a thing without pointing at it, i.e. an artefact of having one render and no way to step outside it. But in an engine I can have a dozen cameras, an authoritative scene graph none of them owns, and chunks that are loaded but not drawn. So is the master argument a discovery about the world, or a quirk of the interface of introspection? And does the fact that my vocabulary makes the view from nowhere trivial mean my vocabulary is smuggling in exactly what he's denying?
- (4) is where I suspect the translation breaks hardest. For me persistence just is storage: a thing survives unobserved because its state is on disk or on the server. He seems to want persistence guaranteed by continued observation instead, with no storage anywhere. Can that be said in my terms at all? Or is the nearest thing I have — an authoritative server that keeps simulating every chunk whether or not any player is nearby — actually a picture of the opposite of his view, since it is precisely a stored world state?
- Connected to that: if I built a stateless renderer that produces each frame procedurally from a seed with no stored scene at all, that's the version of Berkeley everyone parodies — nothing exists when nobody's looking. Is his real system closer to that or to the always-on server? And what should I conclude from the fact that the parody and the actual position sit at opposite ends of my vocabulary?
- Anything in (2) or (3) with no counterpart whatsoever. My guess is the forced-on-us, what-it-is-like part is where my vocabulary just runs out, because nothing in an engine is like anything. Say whether that's a limitation of my vocabulary or a sign that the original claim was doing less work than it looks like it was doing.

Don't quietly swap in my framework's assumptions and then call the result a translation — tell me where you had to cheat. British English please, and go long and specific rather than tidy. I'd definately rather have 1500 words that actually engage than a neat summary.
