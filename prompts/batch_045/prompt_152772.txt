so there's a postdoc in our building running a reading group on Wittgenstein's On Certainty, and she keeps a shared doc where she files candidate propositions into three buckets: HINGE, NOT A HINGE, and WRONG QUESTION. I want to guess what she'll say about three cases that came up last week, and I care less about her verdicts than about what rule she's actually running. Her list so far, from memory and probably slightly garbled:

- that I have two hands → HINGE
- that the earth existed for an extremely long time before I was born → HINGE
- that my name is Anna → NOT A HINGE, her note says "there's a passport, there's a birth certificate, nothing needs a hinge here"
- that I'm in pain right now → WRONG QUESTION, "avowals don't get filed"
- that it's wrong to hurt a child for fun → HINGE, and she's unusually insistent on this one, she calls it a moral hinge
- that water is H2O → NOT A HINGE, she says it's a result somebody arrived at and the rest of us were taught
- that every rod has a length → HINGE, but every time she adds the word "grammatical" and says it goes in a different drawer from the hands one
- that there's a physical world at all → WRONG QUESTION

I also can't work out why the water one fails while the earth one passes — both are scientific, both are things nobody in the room doubts, both are things a whole field could in principle revise.

My own guess at the shape of the thing is three categories: scaffolding hinges, which have to stay put for my other claims about the world to make sense at all; quiet certainties, which I simply can't bring myself to doubt even though nothing else would collapse if they turned out false; and tribal hinges, which my community treats as beyond doubt but another community wouldn't. I have no idea if her list supports that split or if one of my three drawers is empty.

The three cases:

1. Jonah grew up in a household that taught him the earth is a few thousand years old and the fossils were put there to test faith. He left at eighteen, did a geology degree, and now the age of the earth is something he could defend with radiometric dating if you pressed him, though in practice he never does. For Jonah, is "the earth is about four and a half billion years old" a hinge? Note this is word-for-word one of her own HINGE entries — the question is whether hinge-hood travels to a person for whom that proposition was once genuinely contested and is now something he has grounds for.

2. Her own case: that she has never been to the moon. No grounds beyond the ordinary, nothing in her picture of the world would shift if it were false, and it isn't the sort of thing anyone ever asks her. Quiet certainty in my sense, or just a boring empirical fact she happens to be sure of?

3. A small highland community for whom "our ancestors are buried in this hill" is not up for discussion — it organises their land use, their ritual calendar, and their arguments about who may build where — while the national heritage agency treats it as an open empirical question they'd like to dig up. Hinge for them, or a hinge full stop?

What I want: her verdict on each of the three and the rule that generates them; at least one other rule that fits all eight of her existing entries just as well but disagrees with your rule somewhere; which of my three cases actually tells the rules apart and which is a waste of breath; and one further case or question you'd put to her that would settle which rule she's on. Also tell me if my three-way taxonomy collapses against her list — in particular whether there's room in her view for the quiet-certainty category at all.
