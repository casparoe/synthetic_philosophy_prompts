I have been reading about the ways history gets stuck, and I keep running into two situations that feel importantly different, but I only have a name for one of them.

The one I have a name for is the familiar lock-in case. Our village theatre company has performed in a small, damp hall since 1963 because that was the hall that happened to be free when the first booking was made. Everyone agrees the other hall is better and that moving would cost almost nothing, but the question is never reopened, so the worse option simply stays. As far as I can tell this is roughly what is meant by path dependence: an early accident gets locked in, and the endpoint is one that would not have been picked from scratch.

The one I have no name for comes from a patch of old farmland near my house that I have watched go back to woodland for twenty years. People who know the area say it will end up as oak and hickory, and I believe them: that is what every patch around here becomes, however it starts. But this one spent fifteen years locked up in a dense stand of invasive shrub, while the field next door went straight to pioneer trees. Anyone walking the two woods in their fortieth year would have called them different places, and the difference would have been entirely a matter of which seeds arrived in the first couple of summers. The destination was never in doubt. The route and the date were, and the date is long enough that for anyone watching, the destination is invisible.

So my question is about the second situation. Is there an established concept for it, and how exactly does it differ from path dependence, where it is the endpoint itself rather than the timing and the route that is supposed to hang on accidents? I am also unsure the distinction survives scrutiny. It seems to me that if you describe outcomes coarsely enough, every result is inevitable, and if you describe them finely enough, none is; and there is a further case that worries me, where the temporary state alters the soil so that the expected endpoint is no longer what it would have been, which seems to blur the two together. Please do not just hand me a label: I want to know what the phenomenon is, what it is not, how the nearest existing concepts fall short of it, and where you would draw its boundary cases, including whether an endpoint that nobody alive will see should count as an endpoint at all. If nothing established fits, say so and give me a name and definition of your own.
