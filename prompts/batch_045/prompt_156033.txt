I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy background beyond stuff I read on the internet, and I keep losing an argument because I don't have a word for what's going on in it.

My colleague Dave is a pretty serious Christian, my dad is too, and it isn't that either of them denies evidence or claims to have some private proof. It's the shape of the thing. If I ask what would change their minds, both more or less say nothing would, and they aren't embarrassed about it — but they also don't treat it like a taste or a preference. They say it's true the same way it's true that the server is down, not true-for-them.

Two examples. Dave says he'd never try to argue a stranger into it: it isn't the sort of thing you reach by argument, you have to be inside the practice first — prayer, church, the calendar — and only then does it make sense. Ten minutes later he's telling me the resurrection either happened or it didn't, and if it didn't the whole thing is garbage. My dad says God is someone he trusts, like I trust my wife; when I point out that trust can be misplaced and I could be wrong about her, he says no, it isn't that kind of thing. Then he prays for a parking space and is genuinely delighted when one opens up, which sure looks like treating it as a prediction.

The metaphor I keep coming back to is a protocol mismatch: both sides emit well-formed sentences using the same words — knows, exists, true — but nothing is actually transmitted, because what's really in dispute is each side's rules about what counts as successful transmission, not any message sent under the rules. Which is fine as far as it goes, except that the mismatch shows up inside a single person. Dave slides between the two registers constantly, sometimes mid-sentence, and he doesn't seem confused or evasive; it works fine for him. So it isn't two systems failing to handshake.

Is there a name for this? I gather there's a Wittgenstein thing about religious belief not being a hypothesis and about groundless believing, and someone at a meetup said it's all language-games and forms of life and that religious language just isn't in the business of stating facts. But Dave plainly is stating facts some of the time. Is the Wittgensteinian picture committed to saying he can't be, or only that he doesn't have to be? A different friend suggested it's like beliefs you're allowed to hold without argument, the way perception or memory are just given — is that the same claim as the Wittgensteinian one or a rival to it? And is there anything on the register-switching itself, the way one speaker runs both modes at once, and on what happens to a claim when it gets moved from one mode to the other?

Please don't give me a reading list, I can't chase references anyway. What I want is to know whether my 'protocol mismatch' framing is cutting at the right joint, what the nearest established concepts are and how mine differs from each, and where the edges of the thing I'm describing are — i.e. what would be a case that looks like this but isn't.
