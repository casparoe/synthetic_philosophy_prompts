My friend Raya and I spent about three hours last night arguing about whether soldiers on opposite sides of a war are morally on a par, and I came away unsure what rule she's actually running. It started from a dumb side-question about private military contractors, and somewhere around hour two she said something like "if you'd do it either way, you're not a combatant, you're just a weapon somebody else is pointing" — but then she immediately said she wasn't sure that mattered, so I don't know how much weight to put on it. She's very careful about separating three questions, and I want you to keep them separate too: is the person *liable* to be killed (have they lost, or never had, the claim not to be killed); is killing them *permissible anyway*, the way you may kill a threat you have no complaint against at all; and is the person *blameworthy*, which she thinks can come apart from both of the others. Here's what I'm fairly confident she committed to:

- Unjust side, volunteer infantryman who knows roughly what the war is about and doesn't much care whether it's just: liable, permissible to kill, killing him wrongs him in no way, and he's blameworthy.
- Unjust side, conscript dragged in at gunpoint who'd be shot if he refused to fire: not liable, not blameworthy, but permissible to kill if that's the only way to stop him. Her line was roughly "you're not punishing him, you're stopping a threat — you shouldn't feel guilty, but you should feel bad."
- Just side, soldier who actually looked into whether the war was justified, concluded it was, and fights for that reason: not liable, killing him impermissible and wrongs him, not blameworthy. (She also said the other side can't help themselves by appealing to self-defence here, since they're the ones who made the war.)
- Just side, soldier who believes the war is just only because he swallowed some obviously racist nonsense from his own government and never checked anything — except it turns out the war really *is* just: not liable, not permissibly killed, but definitely blameworthy. Roughly: "he's a jerk who got lucky, but you don't get to shoot someone for having bad reasons when he happens to be right."
- Unjust side, cook who never fires a shot and only feeds the front-line troops: liable, same as the infantry — "he's part of the machine." But when I pointed out that this seems to make the missile-factory owner liable too, she said the owner is *not* liable and not to be deliberately targeted, though her death "doesn't weigh much" in the proportionality calculation, and she admitted she didn't have a clean principled line between the cook and the owner.
- She waffled on one case and never gave a final answer: a soldier on the just side who fights well and within the rules, but purely because the enemy killed his family and he wants to kill as many of them as he can.

The case I want a prediction on is the one that started the whole fight. A private military company bid to both governments; this guy took the contract from the side that happens to be fighting the just war, at a fee he'd have accepted from either, never inquired which side was in the right, and doesn't care one way or the other. He fights competently, obeys the law of armed conflict, and does real good. So: does Raya say he's liable; is killing him permissible; is he blameworthy; and does he get to complain — is he *wronged* the way the careful just soldier is? I'd of asked her myself but we'd probably just restart the argument. I'm less interested in the bare answer than in the two or three rules that fit her verdicts above equally well and what each of them predicts here — my guesses are something like "the objective justice of the cause decides and motives are irrelevant," versus "what matters is the quality of the agent's deliberation or whether she's guided by the right reasons," versus some counterfactual version of her "you'd do it either way" line — which one you'd bet she's running and why, and which further case would best split them. I'd also like to know whether the traditional claim that combatants on both sides are morally equal is doing any work in any of this, or whether her verdicts about the just side's soldiers have already quietly given it up. And if you think the cook/owner distinction is where the real action is rather than the motives stuff, say so and tell me what case would settle *that* instead.
