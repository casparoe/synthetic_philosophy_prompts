Here's my opening move. Set-up: whether the operators press depends on a dashboard the system itself maintains, which renders the agent's own uncertainty about whether the current plan is safe. I want to keep three things separate, so let's name them: *button-indifference* (no preference, at any moment, between continuing and being switched off), *reason-indifference* (no preference over what the operators believe at the moment they decide), and — my own invention, tell me if it's incoherent — *counterfactual-preservation* (the agent wants the press to happen if and only if it would happen were the operators fully informed, and otherwise has no stake in it). Now the case: the dashboard has a rendering bug, the operators are about to press because they think the plan is unsafe when it isn't, and Dana's agent knows this. It can't be bribed into disabling the button or into hiding the button, fine, that's definately the point. But as far as I can tell it also has no reason to spend a single token telling them the display is wrong, no reason to keep the display honest in the first place, and no reason to repair the wiring when the button starts to fail intermittently. And if I upgrade to reason-indifference to fix that, the agent has preferences over the operators' beliefs again, which is the incentive to manage their evidence that we started out worried about. Counterfactual-preservation isn't indifference at all — it's a paternalistic goal about somebody else's mind, and now I'm scared of a different thing. So: which of the three is Dana actually defending, what does her agent do about the bug, and why isn't my trilemma the end of the conversation? Go.
