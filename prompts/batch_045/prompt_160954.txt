our metaphysics reading group wraps up next week on the causal exclusion argument and I want to close the last session with a sestina instead of a summary — six end-words rotating through six stanzas and then a three-line envoi — written in the voice of a reason (say, wanting to signal a left turn) that has just been told its bodily effect already has a fully sufficient physical cause.

end-words: reason, firing, sufficient, overdetermine, epiphenomenon, proportional.

what i want the form to do: the six words have to keep coming back in a new order whether or not they're doing anything, which is the non-reductive physicalist's exact fix — the mental vocabulary keeps reappearing in the account while never landing anywhere stable. Give one stanza to the chancy wrinkle: suppose the firing only raises the probability of the arm movement to 0.7 and the movement happens anyway. Then there's no sufficient physical cause for the exclusion principle to lean on, and yet nothing about the reason has been rescued. Give another stanza to the proportionality reply — the firing is too fine-grained to be the cause of this effect, the reason is the right-sized difference-maker.

In the envoi I want all six words crowded into three lines, and I want it genuinely undecidable whether what's on the page there is identity or systematic overdetermination. Someone who knows the argument should be able to tell which premise each stanza is pressing on; someone who doesnt should still just get a poem about wanting to turn left. I dont want it resolved.
