hey — I'm writing a short paper for a grad seminar (4k words or so) arguing that blameworthiness doesn't require being able to do otherwise. I don't want to use the usual Frankfurt-style setup, because the reaction I got last time was that the device is doing all the work: you bolt a counterfactual intervener onto the story, and of course the responsibility intuition survives, because the agent's actual sequence is untouched and he acts for his own reasons. So I want a case in the same family where nobody engineers anything — no neuroscientist, no failsafe, no mysterous stranger with a chip — and the agent still has no alternative possibilities.

My idea is to get the "couldn't have done otherwise" from the *kind of thing* she's blamed for rather than from a device: blame her for something nobody can do at will — a reaction, an attitude, a feeling that leaks out — not a decision and not an omission she chose. Build me this:

- One paragraph, realistic setting: a hospital ward or a care home, with real job details (handover, charting, the specific thing she's meant to be doing at that hour) so it doesn't read like a vignette. Nothing science-fictional. No counterfactual intervener of any kind, and no device that *would* have kicked in either — I want a setup where nothing had to go differently for her to have no options.
- What she's blamed for is a mental state or an involuntary reaction: contempt, disgust, relief at someone's bad news, whatever fits the setting. Not an action she decided on, not an omission she chose. The lack of alternatives should be constitutive here, not engineered.
- No tracing route: she didn't cultivate the reaction, she didn't decide at some earlier point to stop caring, and she's in fact tried to get rid of it and is distressed by it. If you find you need a bit of tracing after all, say so out loud instead of burying it.
- She's fully aware of the reaction as she's having it and knows it's the wrong one to be having — I don't want any of the intuition coming from ignorance, self-deception, or her not noticing what she's like.
- Don't smuggle in a consequence. Either nothing results from the reaction, or, if it leaks into how she says something, the downstream harm is also something she couldn't have prevented. If there's a bad outcome for the reader to latch onto, the case stops testing what I want it to test.
- And dont just tell me she's blameworthy — the case has to generate that, not stipulate it. If, honestly, the intuition comes out weaker here than in a standard Frankfurt-style case, tell me that and say roughly how much weaker.

After the scenario I need:

1. What three views say and why. (a) The principle of alternative possibilities: blameworthiness requires being able to do otherwise, so no alternatives, no blame. (b) The actual-sequence view: what matters is how her capacities actually operated — whether she was reasons-responsive or reasons-insensitive in the situation as it really unfolded — so the absence of alternatives is beside the point. (c) The answerability view about attitudes: blame for what you feel or believe tracks whether it's rationally assessable and reflects your take on the world, not whether you could have switched it off.
2. Which single feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens to the intuition if you delete just that feature and leave everything else standing.
3. A matched control: rewrite it so the only change is that the thing she's blamed for is voluntary — she says the contemptuous thing on purpose, or deliberately does the small thing the reaction was nudging her toward — and tell me whether the blame verdict moves. That's the variable I actually want isolated, so keep the rest of the story identical.
4. The strongest reply available to a defender of the principle of alternative possibilities in *this* case — specifically one they don't get against the standard device version. That's the bit I need for the paper, so don't give me the easy one.
5. If my constraints can't all be met at once, which one would you drop, and what does dropping it cost me?

I'd rather have a case that's a bit less punchy and actually works than a tidy one with a hidden move in it, so flag where yours is soft.
