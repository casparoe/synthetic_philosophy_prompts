I am a PhD student in moral philosophy, finishing a dissertation chapter on the procreation asymmetry, and I want to pressure-test my own credence rather than rehearse the dialectic once more.

Fix the target proposition as follows: in a choice between creating a person whose life would be clearly worth living and not creating them — everything else held fixed, no one else affected — there is no moral reason to create; in the parallel choice concerning a life that would be clearly not worth living, there is a strong moral reason not to create; and this difference in the reasons is a real feature of the normative facts, not merely an artifact of how the two cases get described. My own credence in that proposition is around 0.55. My supervisor's is around 0.2, and she thinks the intuitive pull is explained by our reading the misery case as a case of harming someone we can already identify.

Give me your own number, but I principally want the decomposition: a probability and one line of justification for each sub-question the number rides on, and then the combination. At minimum I want terms for the following, plus anything you think I have left out:

1. Whether any formulation of the person-affecting restriction, or of the counterfactual comparison involved in claims about benefiting and harming, yields the asymmetry without collapsing either into totalism, which has no asymmetry, or into a symmetric view on which creating a miserable person is not wrong either.
2. Whether the standard symmetry pressure can be met: whether one can state the pair of claims without relying on a shifting comparison class, or on a notion of harm that quietly presupposes the person exists to be harmed.
3. Whether the asymmetry is a difference in kind or in degree. Suppose we represent it by a weight lambda on the welfare of merely possible people, so the asymmetry as standardly stated is the case lambda = 0 while a weak procreative reason corresponds to small positive lambda. What is your credence that the intuitive data force lambda = 0 rather than merely lambda much less than 1, given that the standard two-option case looks the same to us either way?
4. Whether the asymmetry can be stated so as to avoid the consequence that no number of created good lives could outweigh the smallest benefit to an extant person — and, conditional on its not being avoidable, whether that consequence is acceptable or fatal.
5. How the asymmetry extends to choice under risk. There are two natural extensions that do not commute: apply the one-sided weighting to expected lifetime welfare, or take the expectation of the one-sidedly weighted welfare. The first is permissive about creating under risk; the second forbids creating under any risk of a bad life and never credits any upside. Give me your credence that exactly one of these is the correct extension, and your credence that the need to choose between them is itself evidence of ad hocness.
6. Whether the asymmetry intuition is best explained by a debunking story — identifiable-victim effects, framing, heuristics keyed to the parental role — rather than by sensitivity to the normative fact.

Then tell me which single term your final number is most sensitive to, which argument or kind of case would move it most, and where you think the gap between my 0.55 and my supervisor's 0.2 actually lives. I suspect the debunking term; I would like you to check whether it is really concentrated in the aggregation term instead, since that is where I think I may be refusing to follow my own view through to its consequences.

Please be explicit where a number is close to a placeholder, and do not hedge into non-numerical talk. Conclude with a single machine-readable line, and nothing after it, in the form \answer{0.xx} giving your all-things-considered credence in the proposition as stated, to two decimal places.
