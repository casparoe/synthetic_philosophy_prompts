Our formal epistemology reading group (six of us, mostly grad students plus one philosopher of physics who keeps asking what any of this is for) spent two hours last week on the Judy Benjamin case and got nowhere. Quick recap of the setup so we're on the same page: Judy parachutes into a square territory, bisected by a river into North and South, and divided the other way into Blue-held and Red-held halves — four cells. She lands somewhere in it with a prior over the four cells (mine is 0.3 north-and-Blue, 0.2 south-and-Blue, 0.1 north-and-Red, 0.4 south-and-Red; use your own if you prefer). Then a staticky radio message: if you are in the Blue half, the odds are three to one that you're north of the river. She has to decide which way to hike and the stakes are real, so this isn't only a bookkeeping puzzle.

What I want, and what we never got round to writing down, is the spec rather than yet another proposed solution. Fix the purpose for me as this: a rule good enough to be the update step inside a decision procedure for an agent like Judy — it has to hand back a full distribution over the four cells, it has to be runnable in something like real time, and it has to not make her exploitable or manipulable by whoever is choosing what to say over the radio. Under that purpose, write the desiderata: the cases the rule must get right, the distinctions it must respect, the limiting cases it must reduce to, with the case or consideration behind each. Here's the pile of things I think are in the running, which I want sorted into genuine desiderata versus the prejudices of one camp: that it collapse to plain Bayesian conditionalization when she learns a cell or a union of cells outright, and to Jeffrey conditioning when the message fixes the unconditional probabilities of a partition; that it leave P(Blue) untouched, since the message seems to say nothing about whether she is in Blue territory at all; that it leave the internal odds inside Red untouched, for the mirror-image reason; that it be order-independent when two messages arrive at once (my test case: the Blue-odds message plus a second one saying the odds of north given Red have doubled — do those commute?), and more generally that the result not depend on how the space is carved up or on which of two equivalent phrasings of the message she hears; that it handle messages which constrain the *new* credence in terms of the *old* one — this is the wrinkle I most want adjudicated, because 'the odds have doubled' is not a linear constraint on her posterior, and I genuinely dont know whether demanding this is reasonable or whether it should be split off as a separate and harder problem; that it do something sensible with vague messages (at least three to one), with jointly unsatisfiable ones, with messages she already expected, and with the case where she can partly determine which cell she ends up in by acting on the update; and that it be computable at all. For each: why it's a requirement or why it's just taste, and mark non-negotiable versus negotiable.

Then the part I care about most: tell me which pair or subset of these you think cannot be jointly satisfied, with the case that shows it, and which single desideratum excludes the most candidate rules. Please also flag any desideratum that quietly smuggles in a view about what a message even is — a proposition, a constraint on a partition, a report about her own credence function, something else. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, the one thing you'd insist on and the one thing you'd drop, then give the reasoning and the list, no preamble and no restating my question back at me. A short scorecard at the end is fine — three or four lines — for relative-entropy minimization (closest posterior to the prior among those meeting the constraint), the rigidity rule (hold P(Blue) and the within-Red odds fixed, rescale inside Blue), and treating the message as a report to be conditioned on using a model of how messages get sent. Skip citations and reading suggestions entirely; I have those, and it's the criteria I'm after.
