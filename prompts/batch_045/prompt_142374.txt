write me a paroddy product page for a desktop appliance called the Solmonoff Universal Prior (Home Edition) — the pitch being that you feed it any data stream at all and it predicts what comes next better than any computable rival, the perfect inductive reasoner in a box. about 600 words, and i want the whole shape of a crowdfunding listing: a spec table, a short FAQ, and fine print that gets more alarming the smaller it gets.

Every joke has to come from the actual machinery, so that someone who knows this stuff laughs instead of cringing. The 2^-length weighting over programs, and the punchline that "simpler" just means "shorter in whichever language you picked" — put a reference-machine dropdown in the spec table, and let the fine print admit the language-dependence is only bounded by a multiplicative constant, while quietly declining to say how big that constant is. There should be something about probability mass lost to programs that never halt, held in escrow, unrecoverable, amount unknown and in principle not computably estimable. The performance guarantee should be stated correctly: dominance over computable predictors only, up to a constant that depends on the complexity of the true data source, with the honest caveat that this tells you nothing about any finite run. And delivery: convergence with probability one, in the infinite limit, no refunds before then, warranty void in uncountable environments.

Land it as a reductio without ever announcing it. What actually ships has to be a computable approximation, so it isn't universal and doesn't dominate anything, and the razor stops looking like a principle about how nature is and starts looking like an artifact of how you're doing the books. Keep the copy deadpan and enthusiastic throughout — the reader should feel the letdown, not be told about it.
