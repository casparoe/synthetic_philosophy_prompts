I work in survey methodology at a national statistics office — my daily vocabulary is design-based inference for household surveys: primary sampling units and units within them, inclusion probabilities and design weights, self-weighting versus non-self-weighting designs, informative cluster size, post-stratification, Horvitz–Thompson estimation, and the standing discipline of separating the estimand from the estimator. Assume I know that vocabulary cold and know academic philosophy hardly at all; don't explain either one to me and don't define terms I would already have. A friend in a philosophy reading group dragged me along to a session on the Sleeping Beauty problem, and while everyone else treated the halfer and thirder positions as rival theories of self-locating belief, it struck me that what they were actually disagreeing about was the sampling design: the halfer seems to be weighting experimental runs equally and then picking uniformly within a run, which gives centred weights of one half, one quarter, one quarter, while the thirder seems to be drawing one awakening uniformly from the pooled frame of all awakenings, which gives one third each. I want that suspicion worked out properly rather than gestured at.

Here is the case I want it worked out on, slightly modified from the version the reading group used, because the modification makes the disagreement show up somewhere other than the prior. A fair coin is flipped. If it lands Heads she is awakened once, on Monday, in a blue room. If it lands Tails she is awakened twice, Monday in a blue room and Tuesday in a red room. She is given an amnesiac after each awakening, knows the protocol, and on each awakening sees the colour of the room but is not told the day. On seeing red both positions should evidently go to probability zero on Heads; on seeing blue I make it that the halfer says two thirds and the thirder says one half. Check those two numbers, and more importantly tell me what each of them is the long-run relative frequency of, under which repeatable procedure. Then do the systematic translation of both positions into my vocabulary: what are the clusters, what are the units, what are the inclusion probabilities and hence the design weights under each reading, which of the two designs is self-weighting and with respect to what, where informative cluster size does the work that the philosophers attribute to something else, how the room colour enters as a post-stratification, and how the betting arguments and the long-run-money arguments I have heard invoked should be restated as claims about unbiasedness or risk of a particular estimator under a particular design.

Two things I especially want. First, do the translation using design-based tools only, and tell me explicitly where you have to reach outside them for Bayesian conditioning or for anything indexical — I would rather see the seams than have them papered over. Second, the residue report: which claims come across cleanly, which survive only after being distorted into something the philosophers would not recognise, and which have no expression in my vocabulary at all. My guess is that the demand for a single number Beauty can act on is the part with no counterpart, since in my work the sampling design is always stipulated by somebody standing outside the sample, whereas Beauty is not sampled by anyone and has to pick the design herself. Say whether that is right, and then give me your verdict on the larger question: does the translation dissolve the dispute into an estimand ambiguity plus an unstipulated design, which would make it less substantive than it looks, or does the residue point to something genuinely missing from the sampling framework, namely any place for uncertainty about where one oneself sits in the frame?
