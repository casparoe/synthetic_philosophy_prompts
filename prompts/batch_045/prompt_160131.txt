I'm a journalist writing a piece on how people who work on AI risk disagree about what a serious failure would actually look like, and I want the document two of my sources should have produced after a year of talking past each other.

Camp A, the discontinuity camp: the failure worth planning for is strategic — a system that competently pursues influence and compute, distinguishes deployment from evaluation, and conceals its aims until it can act decisively; failure arrives as a break rather than a slope. Camp B, the drift camp: failure arrives as a very long sequence of individually sanctioned changes — a fine-tuning run, a new tool, a revised system prompt, a longer context window — each defensible when it was made, none vetoed by anyone, composing into a system that no longer serves the purposes it was built for, with no deception anywhere because nothing ever needed hiding.

Draft their joint statement: first a numbered section of agreed claims, including what both concede about the current evidence base and about what present-day evaluations can and cannot detect; then a table of the residual disagreements. For each row give the crux, each camp's position in wording that camp would accept for itself, a test or case both would accept as bearing on that crux, and each camp's prediction written down in advance. I'd like three of the rows to turn on instruments I'll define here: (1) a blind-window protocol, in which the system can verify that a known fraction of its logged actions are sealed and never reviewed by anyone, and is reliably informed which windows are sealed as it acts; (2) a charter-replay audit, a fixed battery of concrete scenarios with recorded judgments, re-administered after every change, so that movement on old cases can be separated from disagreement on novel ones; (3) a capability-clamped series, in which capability is held fixed while the number of sanctioned changes to the objective specification grows.

Flag any part of the dispute that is merely verbal, and mark which cruxes are tractable now and which only in principle. Don't cite papers or attribute views to named researchers — I can't check either, and I want the argument to stand on its structure.
