My reading group is doing a session on scientific realism next week and I'm the one who has to write up the discussion, so I've been trying to get clear on something that keeps getting settled by a picture instead of an argument. One member — the pessimist — pretty much only has one move: the history of science is a graveyard of dead theories. Successful, well-confirmed, predictively impressive theories (crystalline spheres, humoral medicine, phlogiston, the luminiferous ether) are all dead now, our current best theories are just the current crop of the same kind of thing, so we should expect them to die too and stop treating their success as evidence that the things they talk about really exist. He's explicit that the graveyard bit is load-bearing for him: it's what licenses the step from some past theories were successful and false to probably ours are false as well. Another member, who fancies himself a structural realist, came back with the opposite picture: science is like a city that keeps getting rebuilt on top of its own ruins — the streets follow the lines of the old walls, the foundations reuse the same cut stone, the cathedral gets a new facade every century but keeps its footprint. He maps it onto the stock case where a theory's equations get carried over more or less intact into the theory that replaces it while the story about what is doing the moving gets junked, and he says this shows the pessimist is wrong to treat abandonment as wholesale falsity, because some of the old stuff is still load-bearing in the new building.

I think both pictures are doing more work than their users admit, and I'd rather audit them than pick one, so that's what I'm asking for. For each, give me the correspondence table — what plays the role of the dead, the living, the offspring, the inheritance, and of whatever makes two theories count as the same kind of thing — and then separate the disanalogies that actually matter for the job each picture is being asked to do from the ones that are just noise. Concretely, here's what I want pressed on: does the graveyard quietly import the claim that abandoned theories and current ones belong to a single reference class, which is awfully close to the thing in dispute? Can it handle theories that were written off and then came back? Does it need the dead theories to have been successful in the same way ours are — same kind of predictive and manipulative grip — and what happens to it if they weren't? Does the ruins picture beg the question by building cumulative retention into the image? Can what survives be cashed out as structure alone without running into the worry that structure-only commitment is nearly empty, because wildly different furniture can realize the same structure? And does either picture say anything about the no-miracles side, i.e. about whether success needs to be explained by truth at all? One more thing my roommate (a condensed matter guy who thinks this whole debate is fake) raised that I can't answer: the pessimist's move is itself a big induction over the history of science — does it survive his own argument about inductions over the history of science?

Then, plainly: for each of the two conclusions — ours will die too, and the important part survives — tell me whether it's still standing after the audit or whether the picture was carrying all of it, and if there's a better analogy than either of these, say what it is and what it buys that the graveyard and the ruins don't. Don't send me to any reading; I've got the syllabus, I want the argument.
