I work in ML — mostly RLHF and policy distilation — and I've been reading a bit about the idealised-adviser / indirect-normativity family of views in metaethics: the rough idea that what you ought to do is what a fully informed, perfectly rational and perfectly calm version of you would advise (or would want you to do), rather than whatever directly maximises the good.

I'd like that family of views restated in the vocabulary I actually use: policies and value functions, reward models fit to human labels, distilling a big oracle policy into a small deployable one, train/deployment distribution shift, off-policy evaluation, and a bandwidth-limited channel between the oracle and the agent who has to act in real time.

Here's the wrinkle I want you to take seriously, which as far as I can tell the philosophers never discuss explicitly: suppose the idealised adviser is never directly available to the bounded agent. All the agent ever gets is a lossy compression — a small decision rule keyed to features it can actually observe at decision time, obtained by distilling the adviser's policy on a finite training distribution, and then run off-distribution. So the thing that actually guides action is a surrogate, not the ideal. What should the indirect theorist say here — is the surrogate what makes the action right, or only an approximation of whatever does?

So: translate the view as faithfully as you can into my terms, then give me the residue report. What maps cleanly? What did you have to distort to fit? What has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? I'm especially curious whether the gap between "what the adviser would do" and "what the adviser would tell someone like me to do" survives the translation, or whether it just collapses into something I already model (off-distribution correction, advice conditioned on the advisee's limitations). And where there is residue: is my framework missing something, or is the philosophical distinction thinner than it looks?

Please write in British English, and keep it concrete — no literature survey, no citations, just the translation and the residue.
