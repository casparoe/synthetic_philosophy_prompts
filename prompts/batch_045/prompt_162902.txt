Setup as best I remember it (I'm a novelist; I read one pop psych book and half a wikipedia page, so fix me if I've got it wrong): the claim is that liberal moral judgement runs mostly on care and fairness, while conservative judgement also weights loyalty, authority and purity/sanctity — the "binding" foundations — and that in surveys where people answer as if they were the other side, one side's simulation of the other is notably worse than the reverse. Juna and Peter then argued about whether any of that tells you anything about who is *right*.

1. Juna: breadth is a kind of adequacy. If one outlook registers every consideration the other one registers and then some, that's like a theory that explains all of a rival's data plus more — it's evidence of comprehensiveness, not just of difference.
2. Peter: more triggers isn't more insight. Someone with more phobias isn't better at detecting danger. Extra moral inputs can be noise, and adding noise to a model makes it worse, not more complete.
3. Juna: calling the extra foundations noise is exactly what you'd say from inside the narrow set, so it's circular — you're using your own moral outlook to rule out the parts it can't see.
4. Peter: it needn't be. Take a hypothetical: suppose future work shows purity judgements track pathogen-disgust sensitivity and largely dissolve when you experimentally damp disgust, while judgements about straightforward harm don't budge under comparable manipulations. That's an independent reason to treat one foundation as off-target.
5. Juna: two problems. Care has off-target causes too — kinship, cuteness, the identifiable victim — so if "has a non-moral causal story" debunks, it debunks everyone and settles nothing. And even a misfiring mechanism can lock onto something real that impartialist moralities have stopped being able to name: the value of some things simply not being up for renegotiation.
6. Peter: I'll accept care has causes, but there's a difference between a distortion in how a judgement is delivered and a mismatch between its input and anything morally relevant — which is the point. And on your second move: you'd have to describe what is actually lost in a world with no purity intuitions, in terms I already accept, instead of asserting a value only your extra foundation can detect.
