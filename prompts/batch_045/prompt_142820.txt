I work in the algorithmic accountability unit of a national audit office. About twenty times a year we review a public body's risk-scoring system — a model that ranks cases (taxpayers, tenancies, benefit claims) by predicted risk and sends the top ones to human investigators — and we publish a short opinion which the body must answer publicly. We have fifteen working days, a validation sample with scores, observed outcomes and protected-characteristic labels, and, for proprietary systems, no training data and no model internals. We cannot tell a body which fairness criterion to adopt; that is for it and its ministers. What we can do is check whether the commitments it has made are coherent, and whether they hold for the deployment it actually plans.

Before looking at any fairness numbers, we now record what we call the deployment envelope: the decision the score feeds (a fixed threshold, or selection of a fixed-capacity top slice), the outcome being measured, the group definition in force, whether that outcome is observed for everyone or only for cases that were selected, and the time window. Please turn the familiar incompatibility results — the ones showing that, away from degenerate cases, calibration within groups cannot be combined with equal error rates across groups whose base rates differ — into a decision procedure our reviewers can run inside a deployment envelope: the ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the points at which it returns 'internally inconsistent claim — send back to the vendor', the points at which it returns 'holds under this envelope only', and the points at which it must stop and hand the decision back because it is a choice of values rather than a matter of coherence. It has to cope with outcomes observed only for investigated cases, with group labels that are themselves proxies, and with the same scores being used at two different thresholds by two units of one body.

Then run it on two submissions: a tax authority's model for selecting returns to audit, where the vendor reports calibration within each group together with equal false-positive and false-negative rates across groups; and a council's model for allocating housing-inspection visits, where the council is under a statutory duty to equalise the rate at which each group is wrongly flagged, the base rates differ, and the model already outperforms the process it replaces — this is the one where I expect an answer the council will not want. Close by saying plainly what the procedure cannot tell us. Please write in British English and keep the whole response, procedure and applications included, under 1,200 words.
