I am putting together a coding manual for a small collaborative project: six of us are coding roughly sixty episodes from twentieth-century science so that we can compare how scientific effort actually got allocated with how a Lakatosian rational reconstruction says it ought to have been. To get inter-coder reliability above the level of "we all know a degenerating programme when we see one", I need a real instrument rather than a list of labels, and I want you to design it.

The deliverable is a decision tree that takes a coder from a short written description of an episode to one of a fixed set of verdicts. Everything should follow from the distinctions I set out below or from ones you add and flag; please do not quote, cite, or reconstruct the secondary literature, and do not treat identifying the real historical episodes as part of the task. Work offline, from the framework as given.

**The distinctions I take to be load-bearing.** A programme as a structured object: a set of claims its practitioners are barred from calling into question (call it the core), surrounded by a belt of auxiliaries that gets adjusted; a positive heuristic that tells practitioners which models to build next; a negative heuristic that redirects refutations away from the core. Then: whether the belt changes generate predictions of previously unlooked-for facts; whether those predictions are corroborated; whether the changes are independently testable or exist only to save the core; whether a rival accounts for the programme's earlier successes and then does more; and how long a programme has been going without deliverables.

I also want four distinctions that, as far as I can tell, are not in the standard kit, and I want the tree to give them somewhere to bite:

- *Zombie survival*: the hard core is retained by convention while essentially nobody in the field believes it, yet the apparatus built around it keeps turning up corroborated novel predictions. Is this progress or a kind of institutional afterlife?
- *Borrowed belt*: a belt adjustment is made using a technique or assumption imported from the programme's main rival. Whose achievement is the resulting novel fact?
- *Predictive without content growth*: arbitrarily good fit and reliable prediction, obtained by multiplying free parameters domain by domain, with no increase in what the programme says is going on.
- *Retrospective ratification*: a programme that looked finished at one date and was vindicated by techniques developed later for unrelated reasons. This forces the question of whether the verdicts are time-indexed, and I want the manual to have an explicit "as of" convention plus a rule for when to re-code.

**Constraints on the questions.** Eight to twelve branch nodes, at least seven leaves. Every branch node must be a question a coder could answer from a two-paragraph episode description, phrased in terms of what happened rather than in terms of the theory's vocabulary: no branch question may contain the words "progressive", "degenerating", "ad hoc", "hard core", "protective belt", "heuristic", "research programme", or "superseded". Where the only evidence available is practitioners' testimony about what they take themselves to be doing, say so at that node. Nodes may be binary or ternary, but each ternary node has to earn its keep.

**Order of construction.** Build the branch questions from the distinctions first, then enumerate the answer-combinations the tree permits, then place the verdicts in that space. Mark at least two combinations that no position occupies and diagnose each: is nobody there because the combination is incoherent, because it is merely unattractive, or because it is unexplored? And flag any pair of verdicts your tree cannot actually separate, with a note on what further evidence would break the tie; do not hide a collision by adding a question that begs the answer.

**Leaves that must appear**, under whatever names you prefer plus a plain-English gloss: fully progressive; empirically progressive but theoretically stagnant; theoretically progressive but not yet empirically; degenerating but rationally tolerable; degenerating and no longer rationally tolerable; superseded by a rival that explained its successes and did more; zombie survival; predictive without content growth; not a programme at all because no core commitment can be identified; and unclassifiable pending resolution of a contested core. Each leaf needs its awkward commitment stated in one sentence, and one sentence on what it implies for whether continued support was rational.

**Test cases.** The tree has to be run on these, so that I can see where it strains. They are stylised composites; please do not try to name the originals.

- *A.* For eleven years almost nobody in the field has taken its central entity to be literally real — practitioners describe it as the thing you put into the equation — yet over the same period the framework has yielded four quantitatively correct predictions of effects nobody had looked for, using apparatus purpose-built to detect them. Its leading figures say in print that they expect the entity to be replaced within a decade.
- *B.* Over about thirty years, each recalcitrant result has been absorbed by adding one more auxiliary at the periphery; none of these additions led anyone to look for or find anything new. The core has never been revised. A rival explains the same body of results with fewer moving parts and has now matched its remaining predictive successes.
- *C.* Three years old, about twenty researchers. It accounts for one striking observation the established framework had been treating as anomalous. It has not yet predicted anything new, proposed new mathematics, or prompted new apparatus. Its proponents say the predictions are coming.
- *D.* After a much-advertised experiment returned a result the framework said was impossible, its defenders re-analysed the experiment's calibration using a method borrowed from their main rival, argued the apparatus had under-reported a background, and then used the corrected treatment to predict an unobserved effect, which was found within two years.
- *E.* Its models fit to very high precision and are widely used to make accurate predictions, but each new domain requires refitting a large number of free parameters, a fit in one domain tells you nothing about the next, and practitioners have stopped expecting the models to say what is going on. Nobody has declared the core false; nobody has revised it either.
- *F.* In the 1960s it was generally taken to be finished: the central mechanism had been measured, the measurement was negative, and the graduate students went elsewhere. A decade later a technique developed for unrelated reasons let someone look again; the mechanism was there, and over the next five years the revived framework predicted three effects nobody had predicted.
- *G.* Around ten laboratories share a method and a set of exemplary results, but there is no statement any of them would defend as the commitment they would abandon under pressure; when asked what would have to fail for them to stop, they answer with a list of techniques rather than claims.

**Format.** Return a single JSON object, and before it at most 120 words noting any distinction you had to invent beyond the ones I supplied. Use these fields:

- `framing`: {`unit_of_analysis`, `as_of_convention`, `recode_rule`, `confidence_scale`}
- `distinctions`: array of {`id`, `plain_question`, `role_in_tree`, `source`: "given" | "added"}
- `logical_space`: {`distinctions_used_at_branches`, `n_combinations`, `occupied`, `unoccupied`: array of {`combination`, `diagnosis`: "incoherent" | "unattractive" | "unexplored", `why`}}
- `nodes`: array of {`id`, `question`, `branches`: array of {`answer`, `target`}, `evidence_type`: "events" | "testimony" | "mixed"}
- `leaves`: array of {`id`, `label`, `plain_gloss`, `minimal_example`, `awkward_commitment`, `funding_implication`}
- `collisions`: array of {`leaves`, `why_inseparable`, `tie_breaker_evidence`}
- `codings`: array of {`vignette`, `verdict`, `deciding_node`, `confidence`: "high" | "medium" | "low", `runner_up`, `information_that_would_flip_it`}
- `coder_warnings`: array of short strings — the ways you expect coders to go wrong with this instrument, especially where an episode description invites reading the core off practitioners' self-descriptions.
