After a long argument with a friend last night, I want to know how exposed my reading of Nāgārjuna actually is: what would have to be true for it to be false, and what it risks.

The position I defended is that Madhyamaka is committed to no ontology of its own. Emptiness is not a property that things have, nor a description of how reality is in itself, but the reminder that nothing whatsoever has svabhāva — an intrinsic nature that is non-relational, independent of causes and conditions and parts, and not merely imputed by concept and convention. The four lemmas of the tetralemma each fail for that one reason, and emptiness applies to itself, so there is no fifth position and no ultimate standpoint left to state. My own refinement, which my friend called cheating and which I think rescues the view, is a distinction between asserting no proposition at all and asserting no proposition that attributes svabhāva; I claim Nāgārjuna does only the second, which is why his denials are not self-undermining in the way a global scepticism about assertion would be.

Three lines of attack from last night that I could not settle, which you should feel free to rank, replace, or improve on. (a) Every test we could run for whether something has svabhāva is itself relational, so nothing could in principle distinguish a svabhāva-bearing thing from an empty one, and a negation with no possible contrast has no content. (b) The arguments against svabhāva work from causal and mereological dependence, but my conclusion is also meant to cover dependence on conceptual imputation; if that third kind is doing all the work, my view stops being about things and becomes a thesis about our classificatory practices. (c) If everything is empty, then the conventional/ultimate distinction is itself only conventionally true, and the two-truths framework my reading leans on cannot say what it is telling me.

I would be most moved by an argument showing that my view either collapses into a rival — a quietist or semantic reading, a realism about absences, mereological nihilism — or cannot state its own point.

Reply as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. The first four are defeaters in descending order of how much they should move me, each noting whether a defender of my reading would concede it as counting against the view or reject it as resting on a misunderstanding; somewhere among them, assess whether the emptiness-of-emptiness clause has insulated the view from any possible counter-instance and whether that insulation is a problem for a view of this kind. The fifth item should give your overall confidence in what you have claimed and name the single point you are least sure of.
