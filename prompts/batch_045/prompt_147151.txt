ok so im on my school's track team and we have to do random drug tests and tell them where we are every day basically, and i got thinking about what the rules actually assume, because they seem to point in different directions at once

stuff i think is true, correct me if im wrong: anabolic steroids and EPO are banned, creatine and caffeine are fine, sleeping in a low oxygen tent is fine (i think??), and pumping your own blood back in before a race is not. Also a guy on my team is allowed to use some banned asthma thing because he actually needs it medically, so the same drug is medicine for him and cheating for me. And if i tested positive, i'd get disqualified but my school would keep the team trophy, and nobody would punish my coach even though he is the one who sets our training loads

my econ teacher called doping a race to the bottom, like a multipolar trap: everyone would rather nobody did it, but each person has to or they fall behind. fine. but what does the actual ban presuppose? is it that athletes are being pushed into it and need protecting, or that its unfair because not everyone has access, or that the sport stops meaning anything if everyone is chemically jacked, or just that the stuff is bad for you? because those give different banned lists, and it feels like the rules are running more than one of them at the same time

two things my friend and i came up with and we couldnt tell if they're dumb:

(1) instead of a banned list, give every school the same season "enhancement budget" — say 100 points, spend it on whatever you want, steroids or an altitude tent or a better coach or more sleep or better food, your call
(2) let people take whatever they want, but every athlete's protocol and blood values go on a public ledger printed in the meet program, and lying about it is a lifetime ban

does the current ban's own logic say either of these would work, and which one are the current rules secretly betting on?

like concretely: if the ban is really about people getting forced into it, why punish the athlete and not the coach or the federation, and why ban stuff that isnt harmful? if its really about fairness, why is every advantage you can buy with money — shoes, private coaching, warm weather training all year, the tent — completely fine? if its about the meaning of the sport or the records, why doesnt the medical exemption wreck the meaning too? and what would actually have to change in the rules if you dropped one of those theories and kept another
