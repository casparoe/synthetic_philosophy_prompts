Suppose a lab wants to deploy a system to do economically useful work, and suppose it is not willing to assume that system is aligned. The argument it produces must go through even if the deployed system is adversarially misaligned, knows it is being evaluated, and is actively trying to look safe. Call that argument a control safety case: control in the sense that every load-bearing premise is licensed to treat the deployed system as an adversary rather than as a partner or a faulty tool.

I do not want to evaluate anybody's actual proposal yet. I want the specification first. Please write the desiderata: what must a control safety case deliver to count as adequate for that purpose? For each requirement, give the concrete failure case or motivating consideration behind it, and mark whether you take it to be non-negotiable (definitional of control, or forced by the stated purpose) or a preference of one camp that a reasonable opponent could drop.

At minimum I want you to settle the following, though your list need not match mine grain for grain:

The grain at which the risk bound is stated. A per-task or per-output guarantee does not aggregate into a deployment guarantee, and a bound that ignores how many copies run in parallel and for how many steps is not a bound on anything. Say what quantity the case must actually put a number on, and whether that number is a rate over useful work, an upper bound on the harm of a single worst-case success, or something else.

The validation problem. Any measure can only be tested against attempts we can generate, using systems safe enough to experiment on, while the conclusion is about attempts we cannot generate from a system too dangerous to run. Address directly whether red-teaming and sabotage-simulation yield anything better than a lower bound on detectability, and what that implies.

The trusted-component bottleneck. If a trusted monitor or human must check the untrusted system's work, then either the checker can do the work without the untrusted system, or the case rests on a component that cannot verify what it is signing off on. Is *the checker need not be capable of the task* a requirement, or merely the aspirement of one camp?

Correlated failure. Identical weights, shared training, and permitted output channels mean copies can adopt the same strategy at once and can signal to one another. State explicitly whether the case assumes independence across copies and what could license that assumption.

Capability drift and loud failure. The case must say how its bound moves as the untrusted system gets more capable, and it should include some mechanism that fails visibly rather than silently. Then confront the tension: a tripwire or decoy affordance that the adversary can inspect and rehearse against becomes a target, yet a case whose security rests on the adversary's ignorance of it is a case that dies on disclosure. Say whether randomized per-deployment tripwires dissolve this, or only relocate it.

Non-vacuity. The case must be able to fail: it should be possible for a deployment to be too capable, too broad, or too fast to be safe by the case's own standard, and *safe enough* must be defined against something. Say whether the comparison class is the no-deployment counterfactual, the risk from a competitor deploying first, or a fixed threshold, and whether importing that comparison is admissible given that the case cannot verify facts about other actors.

Scope discipline. Distinguish intentional subversion by the deployed system from misuse by operators and from ordinary accident, and say which the case owes an account of and which would be scope creep.

Then do three more things. Flag any pair or triple of your own desiderata that you believe cannot be jointly satisfied, and if the conflict is real, say which requirement you would weaken and what that costs — that is the part I most want to see. Identify the single requirement that excludes the largest share of the obvious candidate approaches, and justify the pick. Finally, in a brief table or a paragraph each, score four generic families against your list: trusted review of every action; restriction of affordances via sandboxing and least privilege; tripwires plus rapid shutdown; and cross-examination in which untrusted copies audit each other under randomized spot-checks.

If you conclude that the purpose is incoherent — that any case meeting all the non-negotiable desiderata is impossible, or is an alignment claim wearing a control costume — say so plainly and name the premise you would abandon. Keep the weight on the criteria and their cases; skip the literature, since I want the specification and not who proposed what.
