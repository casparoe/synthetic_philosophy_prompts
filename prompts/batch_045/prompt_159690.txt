I've got to lead next month's meeting of our little reading group — eight of us, all retired lab scientists (I was a crystallographer), working through Wittgenstein's On Certainty, and we're stuck. Half the room thinks it just says you can't doubt everything so get on with it; the other half thinks hinges are Kuhn's paradigms with a German accent. I've read a bit of the secondary literature and it hasn't helped me much, so don't explain hinge epistemology to me: tell me how to explain it to them in about an hour.

Two things I want built into the plan. First, the whole thing should hang off one example from lab life, not off Moore's hands or the earth being very old, which just makes my chemists roll their eyes. My candidates are the calibration chain — you check the meter against buffers, the buffers against a standard, and somewhere it stops — or the fact that the samples in the -80 are the ones I labelled last year. Pick one, and say why it carries the idea and what it has to lack. Second, the plan has to do three jobs: seperate a hinge from a Duhem-style auxiliary hypothesis you could actually go and test (they'll insist "the instrument was calibrated" is just an ordinary empirical assumption, and I want to know where to concede and where the difference really bites — my own candidate is fabrication: nobody treats faked numbers as a surprising result to be explained, they treat the person as outside the game, so use that rather than dropping it in as a aside); seperate it from a Kuhnian paradigm commitment and from a Bayesian prior, which is what people will map it onto; and handle the riverbed image so nobody leaves thinking hinges never shift. Give me the order the pieces have to come in and why, the misconceptions in the order you'd expect them to surface with the point in the sequence where I head each one off, what I should flatly refuse to get into at this level (I'm guessing the fight about whether hinges are propositions at all), and one question I can put at the end that tells me whether the idea landed rather than whether they enjoyed themselves.
