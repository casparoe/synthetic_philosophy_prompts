I've got about $40,000 set aside to give away this year — a bonus plus some money my grandmother left, and I dont need it — and there are two things pulling on me that I honestly can't rank against each other. One is a malaria-nets-and-medicine charity that a friend who works in global health swears by; he's shown me the numbers and says that per dollar it does something like a hundred times more measurable good than anything I could do locally, and that the fact that I happen to have been born in this particular country is not a reason. The other is the community music school in the old steel town where I grew up. I was a scholarship kid there, my old teacher still runs it, and it shuts its doors in June unless it raises roughly the full $40,000. My sister says I owe that place something specific in a way I don't owe anyone in particular anything, and that music, and a teacher who stays, and a town not losing another piece of itself, are worth things that aren't the kind of thing you count.

The problem isn't that I've never thought about it; its that I can't settle which way of thinking is even the right kind, and I don't think I'm going to. Some weeks the arithmetic feels overwhelming. Then I go home for a weekend and the arithmetic feels like a parody of moral thought, and I swing back. My flatmate's advice was to split it, twenty and twenty, which sounds grown-up and moderate — except here's the part that's been bugging me: the school needs the whole amount or it closes anyway, and twenty thousand wouldn't even delay the closing, it'd just close with a slightly smaller hole in the budget. Meanwhile the health charity takes any amount and does proportionally more with more. So the split doesn't look like a compromise to me at all; it looks like I'd be burning the school and doing half of the other thing. Am I right that splitting isn't the moderate option here, or am I missing something about what splitting buys?

And if it isn't, what do I actually do with the fact that I can't tell which picture is right? Is there any non-arbitrary way to weigh "a hundred times more of the good you can measure" against "the thing you specifically owe," when the two sides don't seem to be measuring the same stuff — I mean, what would it even mean to say I'm sixty-fourty between them, or to say one matters "twice as much" to me? My brother says just ask which one I'd be least sick about if I found out in five years that the other way of thinking had been the true one, which sounds sensible but I'm not sure "least sick about" tracks anything real, and part of me thinks that even weighing the school in these terms is already a small betrayal of it. Please don't point me at more philosophy blogs or books, I've read plenty and it hasn't moved. What I want is your actual verdict, plus how confident you are in it, where it rests on machinery that smart people genuinely disagree about, and — I mean this — if I've set the question up wrong, or the two pictures aren't really the only ones, or there's some option I haven't thought of, tell me that plainly instead of answering the question I asked.
