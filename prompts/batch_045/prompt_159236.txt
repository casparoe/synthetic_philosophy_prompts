hey, kind of a weird ask but here goes. I'm a junior and I run this little bio-and-philosophy club at my school — we meet in the chem lab on fridays and mostly just argue — and this week we totally stalled out on the idea of function. Like, when a textbook says the function of the heart is to pump blood, or the function of a moth's eyespot is to scare off birds, what does "function" even mean there? Our teacher gave us two competing answers: on one, a trait's function is whatever it was selected for in the history of the lineage, so the history is what separates a genuine function from a mere side effect; on the other, a trait's function is whatever it currently does that contributes to some larger capacity of the system it belongs to, in which case history doesn't matter and a thing can have a function the very day it gets assembled. We burned an hour on whether a heart that pumps blood in an animal with no ancestors at all — say somebody fabricates one from a scan — is really a heart with the function of pumping blood or just a pump, and then someone said "ok but who cares", and somebody else said "field medics care, because calling something broken is different from saying it's doing something else, and you treat those differently", and it kind of blew up from there. I'm writing this up for the club newsletter and I'd like something better than my notes.

I think we're stuck because every example we reach for is already loaded — eyes, hearts, wings, and we all know what we want to say about them before we start — so what I'd love is a short story, in American English, set somewhere else entirely, with creatures nobody has opinions about yet. Here's the setting I sketched and you can use it or improve it: a small survey team on a terraformed moon, cataloguing local life, with a medic who has to make calls about what to fix and a fabricator that can build a living animal molecule by molecule from a scanned template, no ancestry involved. I want three invented cases to run through the story. First: a local grazer with a big membrane flap on its back that, in most populations, clearly evolved for display to mates, but that in this particular population also happens to deflect a parasitic spore that only showed up a few generations ago — and in one sister population the display use is gone but the deflection is still there. Second: an animal the team printed from a template of a wild one, with no lineage and no selection behind it at all, whose organs all work exactly like the wild version's, plus one chamber the fabricator's own spec sheet says was designed to cool the blood but which in fact mostly filters something else. Third, the case I made up and can't stop thinking about: a creature whose crest isn't held in place by selection at all but by a loop inside each individual's own lifetime — the animal keeps the crest functional by grooming it constantly, and individuals that stop grooming lose it within a season, so the trait is stable across generations without ever having been selected for. Each of these should, somewhere in the story, push someone to say out loud whether the part in question is supposed to do what it does, whether it's malfunctioning when it doesn't, and whether "broken" is even the right word — and I want the two accounts to visibly come apart rather than agree. Please write it in third person, around 1000 to 1400 words, and keep it a story: no character should sit down and explain the two theories by name, and I'd rather it not wrap up neatly with somebody announcing which account wins. It should end with the disagreement still live but with the reader able to feel what's riding on it — ideally with the medic having to actually decide whether to intervene on one of these animals, and the choice looking different depending on which answer you'd give. Tho if you think one of my three cases is boring or doesn't actually pry the two views apart, swap it for something better.
