I run a small reading group (like 5 or 6 of us, none of us studied philosophy) and I got into this topic after a nerve thing in my arm a couple years ago — for a few weeks I could feel the pain perfectly well, could tell exactly how strong it was and where it was, but I sort of wasn't bothered by it. Then I found out there's supposedly a name for that, and that people argue about what it proves. I've been trying to draw a flowchart for the group on what exactly is supposed to be the bad part of pain, and I keep going in circles.

Can you build one for me? Roughly 8 branch questions, 5 to 7 endpoints. Every branch should be a concrete case or a concrete thing you could be offered, never a question like "do you think pain is intrinsically bad" or "is unpleasantness a separable dimension" — I want people sorted by what they say about cases, not by which label they like.

Cases it has to be able to handle:

- someone in the asymbolia state: full sensation, correctly reports intensity and location, shrugs, says it doesn't bother them, maybe even finds it funny
- the reverse: a hypothetical person with no distinguishable burning/aching/stabbing sensation at all who is nonetheless miserable and wants it to stop — is that even pain?
- sensation and bother coming apart over time: the intensity goes up but the bother goes down (people who get into very hot chili, or long distance runners). Is that more evidence for separating the two, or less, since it might just be that they like it?
- someone who says the state is awful while it's happening but wouldn't lift a finger to avoid it tomorrow, and someone who says it doesn't bother them but works hard to avoid it. Those feel different to me and I want the tree to keep them apart.
- pain that wrecks your day (can't read, can't concentrate) vs. an equally intense pain in someone who had nothing going on anyway. Does the badness depend on what it costs you?
- the offer: a pill that leaves the sensation completely intact and removes only the bother, given before surgery. Would you take it? Would you say a doctor who gave it to you without asking had harmed you? Would it be fine to inflict that state on someone for an experiment, or for fun?

My half formed idea, which I want the tree to test rather than just assume: that "bothersome" is doing at least three different jobs at once — how much the feeling grabs your attention, how much you want it to stop, and how much you mind that you're in it — and that those can come apart. If that split is real, I think some of the standard positions deflate into each other and I want to know which.

For each endpoint give a name if it has one in brackets (I'll look up names later, don't build the tree around them, and don't give me any reading list), one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and one sentence on what it predicts about the pill. Also flag any two endpoints your tree can't really separate, and any combinations of answers nobody seems to hold — with one line on whether that combination is incoherent, just unattractive, or only unexplored. And tell me where a person is likely to stall, i.e. which pairs of answers are hard to hold at once.
