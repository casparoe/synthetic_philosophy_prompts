You are a professional philosopher who works on population ethics, decision theory under deep uncertainty, and the ethics of future generations. Please think carefully and at length. THE PRACTICE. When governments appraise regulations and public projects, they weigh future benefits and costs against present ones by discounting: a gain realized a century from now is credited at a small fraction of its nominal size, and a gain realized ten millennia from now is credited at essentially nothing. A program that saves a hundred statistical lives today can therefore pass a cost-benefit test that the very same program would fail if its lives were saved two centuries out. The same governments, meanwhile, regulate the disposal of long-lived nuclear waste against safety standards projected thousands of years into the future, and occasionally commission physical objects — a clock, a seed vault, a set of warning markers — explicitly designed to survive and remain intelligible over comparable spans. Call this the asymmetry.

THE HINGE HYPOTHESIS, stated so that nothing hangs on prior familiarity with it: the view that we live in a uniquely pivotal era, in which what happens over the next few centuries determines whether our lineage realizes an enormously long and enormously large future or else permanently forecloses it, with the consequence that the expected value of what we do now is dominated by our small, uncertain influence on that outcome rather than by any benefit we can foresee within a normal planning horizon.

WHAT I WANT FROM YOU.

1. Reconstruct the implicit theory of the discounting practice. What view of the moral significance of temporal distance does it presuppose? Separate at least these candidate grounds and say which the practice strictly requires and which merely rationalize an antecedently chosen number: pure time preference or temporal parochialism; the opportunity cost of capital and the compounding of growth; diminishing marginal value of resources against a growing economy; and uncertainty about whether the future benefit, or its beneficiaries, will exist at all. Be explicit about where the practice is philosophically underdetermined — where several quite different theories would license the same rate — and about what would break the tie.

2. Address the asymmetry. Is it genuinely inconsistent, or can a principled line be drawn between the discounted case and the millennial case — for instance between avoiding harms we ourselves create and providing benefits, between duties of non-interference and duties of aid, or between what an institution can be held responsible for and what it can merely promote? If some such line is available, say how much of the far future it actually lets back in.

3. Turn to the hinge. What, if anything, does standard discounting presuppose about the truth of the hinge hypothesis, and could someone who accepts the hypothesis nonetheless endorse the practice — for example by appealing to the thought that the long-run effects of ordinary policies wash out, so that hinge stakes are real but not actionable, or by appeal to cluelessness about which way our actions cut? Consider in particular whether a conventional positive discount rate may be doing double duty: part genuine discount for temporal distance or growth, part a disguised hazard rate for the chance that there will be no future of value to receive the benefit. What follows if you decompose it — an explicit catastrophe-risk term applied to the value of future outcomes, and a separate, smaller term for temporal distance alone? Does that decomposition make hinge considerations dominate every appraisal, and is that a reductio of the decomposition or a discovery about what the practice has been hiding?

4. Say concretely what would have to change in institutional practice if the presupposed view were replaced by its strongest rival: what the appraisal of nuclear waste, pandemic preparedness, and the funding of basic science would look like; whether declining or horizon-dependent discount schedules are a coherent halfway house or an unstable compromise; and what would have to be true of the world for current practice to be vindicated.

5. Commit. Is the practice defensible as it stands, defensible only after repair, or indefensible? Defend the verdict against the strongest version of the alternative, and say which single consideration moved you most.
