I make a tiny internal podcast for the engineers at my job, and last month I got into an argument I now can't stop thinking about. We shipped a support assistant that keeps a store of facts about each account, and when a user asks something that isn't in the store but follows from three things that are, it just shrugs. Half my team says it knew the answer and merely failed to work it out; the other half says it never knew it. So I booked a philosopher who works on the problem of logical omniscience. Her line is that the whole thing is a artefact of modelling belief as a set of possible worlds: on her view there's no implicit or derived belief, there's just an explicit representational base plus a resource-bounded inference procedure, and the frightening closure result only looks like it follows because "believes" is doing two different jobs. She also likes to insist the problem is misnamed, because what breaks isn't closure under specifically logical consequence.

Draft me eight to ten questions I can actually ask her, and none that just let her restate the setup. I want at least one on whether dropping implicit belief dissolves the problem or only relocates it into the inference procedure, with a follow-up ready for when she says implicit belief was never anything more than shorthand for what follows from the base; one pressing the misnaming point — if resource limits also stop agents from drawing abductive or statistical inferences, why is logic supposed to be special — with the follow-up for her likely reply that logical norms are structural rather than substantive; and one that makes her apply the account to our actual case, where the system is a pipeline of modules that don't share memory and no single module has the answer, so is that thing a believer in any sense or does the question collapse on her view? Add one on whether the original complaint was ever really descriptive or was always about what agents ought to believe, and finish with something that pins her down on what could count as evidence against her account as opposed to against the possible-worlds one — I suspect she'll say it's only ever a modelling choice, so have the follow-up built for that answer.
