Write me a short story (say 1,200 words, one night shift, mostly one room) about Iman, who works for a firm in Derby that sells 'causal twins' to factories — models that predict when a machine is about to fail. Iman hasn't prayed in fifteen years but shes still, at bottom, an occasionalist of al-Ghazali's sort: fire doesnt burn the cotton, God does, every single time, and what everyone else calls a cause is only the occasion for what God has got into the habit of doing next. She still checks the pressure gauges like everyone else, obviously.

The thing I want the story built around: the teams just found out that one of the twins has been calling failures perfectly for eleven months while running on scrambled input — the machine IDs got shuffled in an update, so its been tracking nothing anyone can name, and it still hasn't put a foot wrong. Her colleague on the shift, Priya, is a hard-nosed realist about causes and thinks this is gorgeous: it means there's some deeper mechanism out there nobody's mapped yet. For Iman its not evidence for anything.

Give Priya a real argument, not a straw one — the strong version being that 'God's habit' doesn't explain the regularity, it just re-describes it and then stops asking questions. And turn the story on what Iman actually does at four in the morning: does she file the ticket, sit on it, tell Priya what she thinks? Does a metaphysics with no causes in it make any difference to the next hour of her life, or is that the sort of thing you can believe and then go on exactly as before? Keep it dry and a bit funny, no mysticism, no miracle at the end, and no lecture aimed at the reader.
