Basically: look at all the theories from the past that were really successful at predicting and explaining stuff but that we now think are just false (the caloric theory of heat, phlogiston, the luminiferous ether, humoral medicine, that kind of thing). By induction, our current best theories are probably false too, so we shouldnt believe that electrons or black holes or whatever are really real, we should just think our theories are useful instruments.

Ive been trying to work out the best comeback for the realist and ive got two ideas, but I cant tell if either of them actually works or if im just talking myself into something. Reply 1 — "shrink the target". The induction counts whole theories as its data points, but a theory is a big bundle of claims stuck together, and the bits dont all stand or fall as a unit. If you re-run the induction over the smaller pieces instead — the equations, the specific claims about what causes what — the historical record looks much friendlier: Fresnels equations for reflection and refraction are still used today, the maths people worked out for heat flow while they believed in caloric mostly got carried over into thermodynamics, and so on. So the pessimist is counting at the wrong grain size, and once you count at the right one you get an optimistic induction instead: the pieces that did the real predictive work tend to survive even when the big story they were told as part of gets thrown out.

Reply 2 — "the graveyard is curated". A theory only gets onto that famous list of dead theories if it was once famous enough to end up in the history books and later got rejected. But nothing that we still believe can ever be on a list of abandoned theories, no matter how similar it is to the dead ones. So the list is kind of guaranteed to be 100% false in the way a list of people who have died is 100% dead — youve only got the numerator. To get the scary number the pessimist actually needs (out of all the theories that were once counted as successful, what fraction turned out false?) youd have to go back and count the theories nobody remembers, and we have no idea what that number is.
