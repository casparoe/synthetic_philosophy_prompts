Hi — I'm writing the final for my upper-level philosophy of mind seminar (14 juniors and seniors, one 90-minute in-class essay worth 20 marks) and I need a single question on whether qualia are an incoherent posit. We did the back half of the semester on Dennett's Quining Qualia chapter, the Chase and Sanborn case especially, plus the Moore-style reply that of course I know what my experience is like even if I can't say it, plus Mary, zombies, and the inverted spectrum. Every version of the question I draft gets answered the same way: a paragraph listing the four properties qualia are meant to have (ineffable, intrinsic, private, directly apprehensible), then either the announcement that nothing could have all four or the announcement that Dennett is confusing a property with our defective concept of it. I want that answer to be worth about half credit, and I want the question to force them into territory they haven't read about.

Here's the case I've been playing with; build the question around it. Suppose we build a scanner, call it the Calibrator, that for any stimulus a subject tastes prints a stable alphanumeric signature. Within a subject the signatures are perfect: the same signature recurs exactly when the subject judges two stimuli taste the same, a different one exactly when they judge they taste different, and the signatures also track their preferences, their re-identifications from memory, and their surprise reactions, with no exceptions found. Two further stipulations. First, the signatures are subject-relative: there is no way to determine whether Ada's signature for espresso is the same as Bo's signature for espresso, since every bijective mapping between their two code-sets fits all the evidence equally well. Second, there is a transfer procedure: swap the signature-producing mechanisms between two subjects and both report that everything tastes different afterward, with their preferences flipping to match. Call the view that these signatures just are the qualia signature realism.

I want the question to ask whether signature realism answers the quining argument or quietly concedes it, and to make them earn the answer. Specifically: which of the four properties do signatures actually have and which do they fail, and is the combination that survives enough to still be called qualia; does the subject-relativity of the signatures mean the interpersonal inverted spectrum is now unstatable, and is that a cost or a result the qualia theorist should welcome; what is left of Mary, given that she could in principle have learned every signature and its causal role in advance and yet something still seems to happen to her on first seeing red; and where, if anywhere, does the residual gap go — I have in mind the move that signature realism relocates rather than removes the mystery, since you can still ask why a signature's being tokened is like something for the subject rather than nothing, and I want a top answer to state that move and then say whether relocating counts as vindicating the posit or as eliminating it.
