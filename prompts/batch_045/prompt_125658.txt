Write a dialogue between two people who've both gone pretty deep on animal suffering and already accept the whole framing — they agree suffering matters, they agree vertebrates count, neither of them opens with "cows are big so beef is worse" or the smug "chickens are killed by the thousand per steer, so eat beef" reversal. They've both heard those a million times. One of them currently eats only pasture-raised beef from land that supposedly can't support crops; the other eats chicken plus low-tillage legumes. They're honestly trying to work out which of them is causing more suffering, and both should be sharp enough that neither gets to win on a slogan.

The thing they keep circling around is an argument someone in their group chat sketched: once you count soil invertebrates and insects, the death tolls per hectare absolutely swamp every vertebrate number they've been arguing about — but a hectare of annual cropland also supports far fewer wild animals than the habitat it displaced, so those crop deaths may be partly cancelled out by all the short, brutal wild lives (mouse pups, beetle larvae, fledglings) that never happen on that land at all. Call it the prevention offset. Have one of them lean on it hard — if wild lives are net negative, tilled monoculture might look like a suffering *reduction* — and have the other one push back on it in a way that actually bites rather than just going "ew, that's repugnant."

They should also get into: what the right counterfactual baseline for grazing land even is (cropland, wild habitat, or abandoned scrub, and who gets to pick); the fact that her extra pack of chicken breasts probably doesn't change a single flock decision that was made months earlier, so both of them are arguing about marginal effects that mostly route through thresholds and supermarket waste; and what to do with insects at all, given the evidence on their sentience is thin — is that an expected-value question or a reason to quietly leave them out.

Keep it to roughly 700–900 words, no made-up citations or precise-sounding statistics, real back-and-forth rather than alternating monologues, and let at least one of them concede something concrete and shift a bit. Don't let it collapse into "we actually agreed all along" — they should end still stuck on something.
