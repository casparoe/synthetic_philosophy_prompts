Hey — I've been messing around with the Lucas/Penrose style argument that we can't be machines, and I've sketched out some notes for a short essay. I can't tell whether the middle bit is actually new or whether I've just redressed something everybody already knows. Would love comments; questions at the end (2 and 3 matter most to me). Please don't give me a bibliography or reading list — I know the literature roughly, I just want the argument poked at.

The argument as I'm running it: suppose I am some formal system F. If I can tell that F is sound, I can produce its Gödel sentence, see that it's true, and thereby do something F cannot. So I'm not F, and by the same move not any machine. Lucas's version leans on my being able to run this against any machine anyone puts forward; Penrose leans on it for the stronger conclusion that mathematical intuition itself isn't computational.

The usual objections: (i) I may not know which F I am, so I don't know which sentence to produce; (ii) I can't verify F's consistency from inside, and if F is inconsistent then it proves everything anyway and there's no gap; (iii) humans are fallible, so the "see that it's true" step is shaky.

What I want to add is that all three treat the ignorance as contingent — you don't happen to know which machine you are, or you can't happen to verify it. I want something structural instead. Grant the agent unlimited time and ideal logical ability. Now suppose its self-knowledge isn't handed to it from outside but inferred from its own behaviour, which is what self-knowledge would have to be if mechanism were true. Call the resulting obstacle the identification gap. The gap isn't that I fail to identify myself; it's that the system I am isn't the kind of object the Gödel step needs it to be. Finitely much introspective evidence underdetermines it: for any candidate consistent with my evidence there are infinitely many stronger and weaker candidates equally consistent with it. And the Gödel sentence is exquisitely sensitive to which one you pick — aim slightly too strong and you get a sentence you can't see to be true, slightly too weak and you get one that isn't beyond you.

Second thing. The mechanist fallback is that the agent just keeps climbing: take F, add the statement that F is consistent, add that system's consistency statement, and so on, even along a transfinite path, and you'll eventually swallow whatever sentence the anti-mechanist produces. I think that reply assumes the climb is monotone and that each step is known to be sound — that you only ever go up, and that you know you went up. But an agent with an empirical self-model doesn't climb, it wanders. Its next self-model may be weaker than the last one, because new introspective data made it distrust the old one, and it has no independent check that a given self-model is sound rather than merely current. So you don't get an ascending progression, you get a non-monotone sequence of guesses, and the completeness argument behind the climbing reply has nothing to bite on. The obstacle isn't that the agent is too weak; it's that the thing it's supposed to diagonalize against isn't fixed.

Third, the upshot I want to defend: the Gödelian argument secretly depends on a transparency thesis about self-knowledge — that an agent has non-empirical, self-verifying access both to what it is and to the fact that it's sound. That thesis is doing all the work; the incompleteness machinery is decoration. And worse for the anti-mechanist: if mechanism is true, self-knowledge is empirical, so the transparency thesis is false, so the mechanist picture predicts the very appearance of a gap that Lucas and Penrose treat as evidence against it. The argument only looks convincing to someone for whom it's already false.

3. Is the conclusion too strong? Does it undercut Penrose too, or only the Lucas version? I suspect Penrose has resourses I'm ignoring but I can't put my finger on them.
4. Which section is least worth developing? My gut says the identification gap section is the weakest and should get cut, but it's also the one I like most, so I don't trust my gut here.
