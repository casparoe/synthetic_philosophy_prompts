Hi — hoping you can help with a teaching thing. I teach one "what is art?" seminar a term to first-year fine art students at an art school, and every year it dies the same death: someone mentions the urinal, someone else mentions the soup cans, we spend five minutes on "but is it art though?", and nobody's actual view ever gets pinned down. So this year I want to run it as a sorting instrument instead — a decision tree they work through on paper in pairs, which spits them out at a named position at the end. Please act as an experienced aesthetics tutor who's taught this session loads of times and work through it carefully before writing anything: I'd suggest brainstorming the logical space first (what combinations of answers are actually available) and only then dropping the real positions into whichever cells turn out to be occupied, rather than starting from the list of names. Institutional, intentional-historical, aesthetic/functionalist, expression-theory, cluster-or-disjunctive, Weitz-style family-resemblance anti-essentialism and eliminativism-about-the-concept all need to appear as leaves — six to eight of them in total.

The bit I care most about is the cases, and this is where I want you to invent rather than reuse. My students have heard Duchamp and Warhol till they're sick, and half of them are doing software, social practice or craft-based work, so I'd rather the branch questions turn on stuff like this — please make up your own concrete examples in this vein and give each a name: (i) a piece that runs entirely unobserved — say a device in a disused water tower translating barometric pressure into a hum for twenty years, demolished without ever once being heard; (ii) an ordinary domestic object made with no artistic ambition whatsoever, installed in a biennale a century later, plus the question of why that one and not its identical siblings still in the cupboard; (iii) a set of written instructions published as an artist's book, first performed forty years later, and performed badly in a way the artist would have hated; (iv) a work the artist later disowns and destroys, which a museum then reconstructs from photographs against her stated wishes; (v) a revered late canvas largely executed by a studio assistant from the painter's own detailed drawings, signed by the painter, only discovered decades afterwards; (vi) a machine an artist builds to make and immediately destroy one drawing a day, which keeps running after she dies; (vii) a practical or ritual object from a community with no concept corresponding to 'art' — boundary markers, a devotional carving, whatever — now sitting in a vitrine; and (viii) something that forces them to say whether 'art' is doing classifying work or evaluative work for them (a wasp nest, a motorway interchange at dusk). Every branch question should be answerable by someone who has no idea which position it leads to, and I want a note on each about how a sharp student is likely to dodge it.

Give me back a single JSON object please, no prose essay. Top-level keys: "nodes" (one per branch question: id, question_text, case_name, options as label + answer text + the id of the node or leaf it leads to, what_positions_it_separates, dodge_risk); "leaves" (id, position_name, path_of_answers_that_reaches_it, one_sentence_summary, most_awkward_commitment); "empty_cells" (answer_pattern, and whether it's empty because it's incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored, with a sentence on which); "indistinguishable_pairs" (pairs of leaves your tree can't actually seperate, why not, and — the bit I'd find most useful — a new tiebreaker case you'd add that would pull them apart); "cut_list" (the two node ids I should drop if we're running ten minutes short); and "teaching_notes" (three to five short strings: likely sticking points, one thing I should say before handing it out, one question that will definately come up that I should refuse to settle). Eight branch questions, six to eight leaves, British spelling throughout, and no bibliography or reading list at the end — I've got plenty of those already.
