I teach a graduate seminar on measurement and optimisation, and I should like to open the first session with a handout rather than a slide: a sestina — six stanzas of six lines, unrhymed, plus a three-line envoy — that enacts the failure it describes. Use these six end-words: measure, target, world, drift, trust, audit. The point of the form is that the words stay fixed in their rotation while everything else moves around them, which is rather the situation I want to capture: the proxy holds its place while the thing it was standing for shifts underneath it.

The distinction I want carried is one I shall be pressing all term: a proxy can fail under sustained optimisation in at least three ways, and they are not the same way. It can erode — the correlation with the target simply weakens, and the measure becomes noisy. It can invert — the correlation stays strong but changes sign, so the measure is as reliable as it ever was and now points the wrong way, and anyone auditing it gets a clean signal and the wrong answer. Or it can be substituted — the number is maximised, and the thing it named survives only inside the definition of the number, so that there is now nothing left for the measure to correlate with. Give each roughly two stanzas, in that order, without ever presenting them as a list; and let the third feel different in kind from the first two, closer to a disappearance than a decline.

In the envoy, three lines, each pairing two of the six end-words, and let the pairings be legible as the three failure-modes to someone who has just heard the distinction. The closing line should leave us with the case where auditing has become perfect and pointless at once.

Some constraints: under two minutes read aloud; no use of the familiar dictum about measures becoming targets, and no naming of whoever formulated it, since I want the structure rather than the slogan; and where the poem needs a concrete case, take it from machine learning — a reward signal, a benchmark, an evaluation harness — rather than from hospitals or school league tables. Someone who works on specification gaming should find it accurate; someone who does not should still find it a poem. Please write it in British English.
