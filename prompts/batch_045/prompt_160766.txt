Write a satirical internal engineering standard: a deadpan conformance specification, 800 to 900 words, in which a platform organization rolls out a framework for classifying employees by teleological role assignment — the doctrine that certain people are, by their function, the kind of thing whose end is to be directed by someone else's deliberation, and that the arrangement is therefore not a management decision but a discovery about their nature.

I have no philosophy background; I am a backend engineer who recently read a summary of Book I of Aristotle's Politics and recognized our org chart in it. The humor has to come from getting that argument structurally right, so please let each of the following do real work in the document rather than appearing as decoration:

1. The classification must be derived from a person's function or end, not from any contractual or legal status, and the standard must insist on a hard distinction between subordination that holds by nature and subordination that holds only by convention — the latter being conceded to be force plus paperwork, and treated as out of scope.

2. The operational test for a natural subordinate is the capacity to follow a specification without being able to author one: such a person takes in a reason supplied by someone else but does not generate reasons of their own. The standard should specify exactly how this is measured, and the measurement should only ever be administered to people who are already taking direction from someone.

3. Include a normative precondition, phrased as a gate that must pass before any classification is valid, requiring the organization to certify that no self-acting instrument could perform the work — the thought being that if shuttles wove and quills played harps by themselves, no one would need subordinates. Apply the gate scrupulously, without the document noticing that it makes the whole classification contingent on the present state of the tooling.

4. Include a benefit clause with a consent waiver: because being directed is held to be for the directed person's own good in the way that a body is benefited by being governed, consent, appeal, and compensation review are waived, and the benefit is asserted by the directing party rather than measured.

5. Add a short appendix on relations of friendship between a manager and a natural subordinate: none is defined in the subordinate's capacity as a role-holder, though one may exist in their capacity as a human being. Schedule that capacity for outside working hours.

6. Include a deprecated legacy heuristic based on origin or ancestry — formally prohibited by the standard, still widely used in the field — and a Known Limitations section recording, without comment, that the only admissible field evidence for natural subordination is observed subordination, with the result that the standard certifies the existing reporting structure wholesale.

Format and voice: write it as a genuine standards document — numbered clauses in RFC 2119 conformance language (MUST, SHOULD, MAY), an abstract, a short conformance table, and an Appendix A worked example applying the standard to someone concrete, such as a junior engineer on an on-call rotation or a QA contractor, which is where the recoil should land. The register should be flat, competent, and procedural throughout. Do not use the words slave, slavery, master, or Aristotle anywhere in the document: it should speak only in corporate-technical vocabulary. No winking asides, no moral commentary, and no footnote explaining the joke — the document should never appear to realize what it has concluded, and the recoil should belong entirely to the reader. The authors of the standard are fictional; the target is the shape of the argument, not any person.
