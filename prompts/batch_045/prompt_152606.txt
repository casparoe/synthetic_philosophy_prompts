Act as a philosopher of mind who's actually worked on higher-order theories — I need an audit of my own view, not a literature survey.

I'm finishing up a chapter chapter on higher-order theories and I've written down my profile so the reading group can attack it. Tell me whether it hangs together. Where I'm at:

1. Actualist, Rosenthal-style: a mental state is conscious iff it's suitably targeted by a distinct higher-order representation; the relation is contingent and empirically implemented, and it does nothing to make the first-order state a mental state in the first place.
2. No self-representationalist or same-order moves — the higher-order state has to be a genuinely separate state, not an aspect or part of the first-order one.
3. But the higher-order state can be nonconceptual and perception-like (inner-sense-ish) rather than thought-like, and I lean on coarse-grained demonstrative HOTs to answer the richness/overflow objection. Otherwise infants and most non-human animals come out non-conscious, which I really don't want.
4. A given state's being conscious is all or nothing, not a matter of degree.
5. Transparency is the theory's best asset: HOTs are transparent, introspection reveals the first-order state and through it the world rather than the higher-order state, and that's what dissolves the explanatory gap. And introspection is reliable even though HOTs are themselves typically unconscious.
6. Phenomenal consciousness is what grounds a creature's moral status, full stop.
7. Two cases I think the view handles: the "dimmer" one, where the higher-order mechanism degrades gradually and consciousness, I want to say, fades out rather than switching off; and the "graft" case someone floated at a conference — an intervention that makes one creature's higher-order mechanism target first-order states sitting in another creature's brain. I want to say the targeted states are conscious there, and conscious for the creature doing the targeting.

Which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, which only look like they are, what the cheapest fix is for each, and which single revision would clean up the most? Also mark which bits of this are settled among people who work on it and which are contested, so I don't present a contested reading as consensus in the chapter. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, no preamble and no restating my question, and think it through properly — brainstorm a bit before you commit. British English please.
