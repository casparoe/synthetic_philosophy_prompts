I have been reading about consciousness on my own for a while, with no formal background, and I keep noticing the same thing happen in these arguments. I do not have a word for it, and I would like to know whether the thing I have noticed is something that has already been carved out.

One case. A friend and I were arguing about whether a machine built out of the right parts, wired up the right way, could have experiences. He said that no account given in terms of parts and signals could ever capture what he has, and that he can see this immediately, just by thinking about it. I wanted to reply that his inability to see how such an account could satisfy him is itself just a fact about him — about what his imagination will and will not accept. But once I said that, we were no longer arguing about the machine. We were arguing about why people go around saying that no account could capture it. And that second argument felt exactly as stuck as the first one: he was just as unwilling to accept a psychological story about why he insists as he had been to accept a physical story about what he insists on.

A second case. People who argue that experience is not really there at all seem to spend nearly all their effort on a different question — why the rest of us are so certain that it is. But that project looks structurally identical to the one it replaces.

So here is what I am after a name for. The difficulty does not stay where it was put. Whatever is in dispute, the argument slides to the question of why anyone would say, think, or deny the thing in question, and the slide appears to buy nothing: the new question inherits the whole difficulty of the old one. I also have a vague sense that this slide is sometimes presented as a way out, on the grounds that explaining why people talk as they do should be easier than explaining the thing they are talking about. My cases do not feel easier. And in the first one there is something extra: the explanation I offered would have applied to my friend's own conviction, so that his insisting on it started to look like the very thing needing an explanation rather than a reason for me to accept it.

Does any of this have an established name — the slide itself, the fact that it does not seem to become easier, or the way an explanation of someone's conviction can appear to pull the ground out from under that conviction? If there are only near misses, I want to know how each one differs from what I have described and which comes closest. And if nothing fits, please coin something, define it, and give me the boundary cases, because a bare label would not help me: what I want is to know precisely what I have noticed and where it stops.
