Me and a few friends run a monthly argument night, usually 8 to 12 of us and none of us academics: a programmer, a nurse, a retired teacher, a guy who builds robots in his garage. Last time we burned two hours on whether modern AI systems are actually intelligent and got nowhere, because everyone was using the word to mean something different and nobody noticed. So I want to make a sorting thing for next month: a decision tree that each of us walks through before the arguing starts, so we can see who is really disagreeing with whom and who is just using the word differently.

What I want: about 8 branch questions, each one a short concrete vignette about a made-up system (System A, the Question-Box, whatever you want to call them), with two or three answers to pick from, ending in 6 to 9 leaves. Every question has to be answerable by someone who has no idea where it leads, so I dont want any question that just asks does it really understand or is it conscious, because unpacking those is the whole point of the exercise. Please also make the cases fictional and not about any real product or company, or the night will turn into a brand argument.

Start it from something everyone in the room already grants (we all accept that a crow is more intelligent than a houseplant, say), and build the branches out of the questions first rather than starting from a list of famous positions and then finding questions to fit them. Then show which combinations of answers are actually occupied, and for the empty ones say whether they are empty because the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Distinctions I definately want in there, at least some of them as their own branch:

- doing well on the test you were prepared for versus doing well on the kind of thing nobody prepared you for, and separately: coming up with a new way to do a familiar job versus coming up with a new idea of what job to even do
- getting the answer right only with a hint, or by producing a hundred attempts and keeping the best one, or with way more time than a person would need
- whether the raw system on its own has to be the intelligent thing, or the system plus its prompt and tools and the person operating it, or whether the intelligence can be spread out and not in any one object at all
- two systems that behave identically, one learned what it knows from data and one was hand-built rule by rule by engineers: does where it came from matter
- a thing that will answer any question perfectly if you ask it, but never wants anything and never does anything on its own
- a thing that crushes hard problems but falls apart if you reword the question slightly
- two systems where the first is better at one kind of task and the second is better at another, with no way to trade them off: is there a fact of the matter about which is more intelligent, or is it a profile and not a ranking
- a thing that never learns anything but was made already able to do everything

Leaves I want present, under whatever names you think are right (say unnamed if nobody really holds it): intelligence is just doing well on the tasks we test, the no-single-ranking view, the you-need-a-model-of-how-the-world-works-so-you-can-ask-what-if view, the view that the intelligence is not located in one object, and the view that you have to be the kind of thing that means something by what it does.

Also: flag any two leaves your tree cant actually seperate, tell me what to do if someone's answers dont land on any leaf, and keep the whole thing doable in 20 minutes at a kitchen table.
