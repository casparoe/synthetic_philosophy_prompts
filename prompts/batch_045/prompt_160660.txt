I'm an undergraduate in a philosophy seminar on the social construction of kinds, and I'm writing a paper in which I have to reconstruct the implicit theory of race, and of the relation between race and human population structure, that an existing institutional practice presupposes. The practice I've chosen is the package of policies at the teaching hospital where I am a patient, which I'll describe fully below so that you don't need any outside information about real hospitals or clinical guidelines.

For years, the hospital estimated patients' kidney function with a formula that, holding age, sex, and measured blood creatinine constant, produced a higher number when the patient's electronic health record listed race as Black and the same lower number for every other patient. The hospital's old explanatory sheet said this was a race correction reflecting average differences in muscle mass. Last year the hospital eliminated that race term. In its place, it introduced a new ancestry-adjusted estimate. Before the test, patients are given a consent form saying that the hospital now measures genetic ancestry, not race: a saliva test estimates the proportions of a patient's genome attributable to continental ancestry groups labeled African, European, Indigenous American, East Asian, and South Asian, and the kidney formula uses those proportions instead of self-identified race. The consent form also states that genetic ancestry is a biological variable, whereas self-identified race is a social category with no biological meaning.

Yet the hospital has not retired self-identified race. Its transplant committee continues to award equity priority points to candidates on the basis of the fixed racial categories from the old record—Black, white, Asian, Native American, Pacific Islander, and other—and its public quality dashboard still reports gaps in outcomes between exactly those categories. The committee's stated justification is that self-identified social race, not genetic ancestry, is what tracks a patient's exposure to racism and disadvantage, so social race is the correct basis for remedial priority. Patients may choose multiracial or decline to answer in the diversity questionnaire, but for anyone who does not update it, the system auto-populates the race field from birth-certificate data.

I'm puzzled by the apparent inconsistency: in one algorithm the hospital treats race as biologically meaningless and replaces it with genetic ancestry, while in another it treats self-identified race as the socially real and causally efficacious thing that matters for justice. What theory of race, and of the relation between race and genetic ancestry, does this package of policies presuppose? Does its use of a genomic continental-ancestry adjustment in the kidney formula strictly presuppose that there are biologically real human population kinds, or at least real biological population structure, or could the same policy be licensed by a view on which continental ancestry is itself a partly conventional or constructed grouping? Does its use of fixed self-identified racial categories for transplant priority strictly presuppose that race is a real social kind, or could it be justified by a more deflationary view on which the categories are merely instrumental proxies for disadvantage and not a kind at all? Are the two halves of the package consistent with each other, or do they secretly presuppose incompatible views of what race is?

Please reconstruct the implicit theory that would make the whole package coherent, and be explicit about what the practice strictly presupposes as opposed to what it merely fits well with. Tell me which rival views about race the practice quietly rules out—for example, crude biological racial realism, the eliminativist view that talk of race should be abandoned altogether, or the view that self-identified race is just a rough proxy for genetic ancestry—and where the practice is philosophically underdetermined, so that several different theories of race could license it. Finally, say what would have to change in the hospital's policies if it replaced its implicit view with, first, a thoroughgoing view that both social race and continental genetic ancestry are socially constructed categories with no biological reality, and, second, a biological realism on which race itself is a biological kind and self-identified race is a decent proxy for it. I don't need any bibliographies or citations, just the conceptual reconstruction.
