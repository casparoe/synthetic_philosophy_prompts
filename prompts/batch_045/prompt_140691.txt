hi — I chair the restitution working group at the Fennimore University Museum (small teaching museum, about 400 objects with colonial-era provenance gaps). We have to bring a recommendation to the trustees in March and I'd really like a draft memo to circulate, because right now the group is going in circles.

The case: a carved wood and pigment memorial effigy, roughly a metre tall, accessioned in 1947 but collected in 1899 by a colonial district officer during a punitive expedition against the Okwei. It commemorates a war leader, and — this is the bit I keep coming back to — it was made to be impermanent. Figures of this kind were left at a grave site and allowed to weather away as part of the commemoration; they weren't meant to last. Our conservation file says it was consolidated with a synthetic resin and put on a steel armature in 1972, and the base was cut down to fit a display case. So the thing we'd be handing over is preserved in a way its makers apparently didn't intend, and it will never decay now.

Three claims, and they conflict:
- The Okwei Heritage Council, speaking through the current Keeper of the Grove, wants it returned to be 'retired' — put back in the grove, allowed to weather, no display, no photography. Complication: our 1899 records describe that office as male-only and the current Keeper is a woman, and one trustee has started using this to argue her claim is a modern invention. I think that's a bad argument but I can't quite say why.
- A community museum committee, partly diaspora-funded, wants it back and on display locally. They've been explicit that the tourism income matters and that young people should be able to see it.
- The national museum in the capital claims it under Ndara's heritage law and would show it there. There's a decent chance it's the only legally recognised recipient.

Legal snag: under our state's rules we can basically only deaccession to another public institution, and we can't transfer anything we have reason to think will be destroyed. Our lawyer says a transfer to the Council is probably a non-starter and has floated two workarounds — transfer title to the Council but keep the object here on indefinite loan, or a rotating three-year cycle between us, the community museum and the capital.

The group is split roughly down the middle on three things I want the memo to take a position on:

(1) Is returning it while insisting on our own conditions (climate control, no ritual handling, full documentation) actually restitution, or just guardianship with better PR? My instinct is that the conditions quietly reassert the museum's authority — but I also can't shake the feeling that letting a 120-year-old carving rot is something future Okwei kids might resent us for, and that irreversibility is a real cost rather than just a curator's squeamishness.

(2) How do we handle the disagreement inside the Okwei claim without us effectively picking which Okwei count as authentic? Is there a way to structure this so we're deferring rather than arbitrating, or is that just a way of hiding that we're arbitrating?

(3) Does it matter that the object's being here is part of why there's a tourist economy and a diaspora committee with views about display at all? One of our curators says yes — so the display faction's preferences are partly our own creation and shouldn't work as a neutral tiebreaker. Someone else says that's condescending, and I'm not sure they're wrong.

Also: we hold three more objects from the same 1899 expedition. The Council has said it does not want two of them back, since they belong to a cult the community abandoned. I don't want a memo that quietly commits us to returning all of them or none of them, and I don't want a principle that lets us keep things because a community declined them.

What I need is a memo with a bottom line the trustees could actually vote on — who gets what, on what conditions, on what timeline, and what happens to the other three — plus the reasoning. And please give the strongest version of whichever position you're not recommending; I definitely know which side I want to win and I need to be able to argue with myself about it in the meeting. Say so if you think the frame of the question is wrong.
