Hey — I run a very informal philosophy reading group (six of us, mixed levels, we meet in a pub) and last week we did Strawson's *Freedom and Resentment* paper and it went completely sideways. Half the group kept arguing about whether blame is ever *fitting* and the other half about whether it's ever *fair to actually voice or act on*, and I'm now definately convinced we were answering different questions and not noticing. So I want to build a handout that stops that happening.

What I want from you: a sorting quiz — "which account of blame are you actually committed to?" — that works purely off reactions to concrete cases, never off labels. Ten questions, each a short concrete case with three answer options phrased the way a person would actually react ("you'd feel resentful and think that's the right response", "you'd feel it but think you shouldn't", "you'd feel nothing much, just concern about what to do next"), plus a scoring key that maps answer *patterns* onto positions.

Two things I care about enough to insist on:

1. The quiz has to separate the fittingness of blame from the appropriateness of expressing/acting on it. Use that as one of the branching questions, not as an afterthought.
2. Some of the cases have to involve odd blame-targets, because that's where our group actually came apart: a corporation, a friend who died last year, a chatbot, a four-year-old, yourself last Tuesday. Also required: the distracted driver who glances at a text and hits a cyclist with no ill will whatsoever; two people who do the identically careless thing where one victim walks away and one dies; someone raised in a household where a cruel practice was taught as kindness, who sincerely thinks he's being decent; someone who does the right thing for the worst possible reason; a friend who breaks a promise that only mattered because of a private understanding between the two of you; and someone who is blamed purely for an attitude they never acted on and don't endorse.

Leaves I want to be able to land on: a Strawsonian/participant-stance view where it's the quality of will that does the work; the Scanlon-ish view where blameworthiness is a matter of what the action *meant* and blame is a modification of a relationship; a reasons-responsiveness / fair-opportunity view; a deep-self or identification view; a forward-looking, purely consequentialist treatment of blame; the hard-incompatibilist/revisionist "nobody is ever blameworthy in the desert-entailing sense" leaf; and a fittingness-only leaf where blame is often fitting but almost never all-things-considered appropriate.

Please build the tree from the logical space first — the questions and the answer-combinations they allow — and then locate the actual positions in it, and tell me which combinations nobody occupies and whether that's because they're incoherent, just unattractive, or merely untried. Also flag any two leaves your ten questions can't actually tell apart, and either give the extra question that would separate them or say why nothing would.

No question is allowed to use the words "free will", "determinism", "reactive attitudes", "moral responsibility", or any position's name — if someone can tell from the question which box they're being sorted into, it's a bad question. And please don't add a reading list, bibliography, or citations: I can't look anything up from the pub and I don't want to.
