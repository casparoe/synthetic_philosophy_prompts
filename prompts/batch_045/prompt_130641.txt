I work on safe RL — constrained MDPs, Lagrangian penalty methods, runtime shields that overwrite the agents action when it would violate a hard constraint, and a lot of argueing about whether safety belongs in the objective or in the action filter. A philosopher friend described something called the paradox of deontology to me roughly like this: a deontologist holds that you must not kill an innocent person even to prevent five other people from being killed, which seems odd because the constraint then makes you preside over more of exactly what it forbids — five deaths instead of one — so the constraint can't really be about the deaths at all. My team hit something that feels similar: we bolted a hard per-step constraint onto a policy, and in sim the total number of violations per episode went up, so someone said the guardrail is causing the thing it guards against, and we have started calling it the guardrail paradox.
