hey — i want a spec before I read any more of the knowing-how literature, becuase I keep reading a paper, nodding along, and then realizing I never decided what I was asking the theory to do.

where I'm coming from: I don't care about Ryle vs. the intellectualists purely for its own sake. I want a theory good enough for two jobs. First, to tell me whether I'm being loose or literally correct when I say a system trained by gradient descent "knows how" to translate a language, or write passable code, or play a decent endgame — and relatedly whether it knows how in the same sense a person does. Second, to say what a teacher is actually trying to produce in a student. If knowing-how is just knowing-that under a practical mode of presentation, then teaching starts to look like telling, and my gut says that's wrong, but I can't say what's lost. So: write me the desiderata for an account of knowing-how that would be fit for those jobs, with the case behind each requirement.

The cases I want it to earn, not just accommodate: the expert who can't say how she does it (the chicken-sexer sort of case, or someone who recognizes a face she can't describe); the beginner with the complete set of instructions who gets it right slowly and falls apart the moment something deviates; the pianist who temporarily can't move her hands but hasn't forgotten; the person who currently succeeds but only by luck; the aging coach who can no longer perform but can still see what you're doing wrong, next to the athlete who performs beautifully and can't teach a thing — I don't want these collapsed into one phenomenon if they aren't one. Also: knowing-how decays with disuse and comes back faster on relearning than it was acquired, which knowing-that doesn't do, and I want to know whether an account has to explain that profile or is allowed to shrug at it. Also whether the skill is one thing or many: cycling versus cycling on ice, operating versus operating with unfamiliar instruments on unusual anatomy. And whether the account has anything to say about the complements that aren't about producing a success — knowing how not to, knowing how to lose gracefully. And how know-how gets transmitted by demonstration and correction when nothing propositional seems to change hands.

For each requirement say what it's a requirement on the strength of, and mark which ones are non-negotiable and which are just prejudices of one camp — I especially want you to flag any pair you think can't be jointly satisfied (my hunch is that "don't reduce knowing-how to an ability" and "explain the decay-and-relearning data, which looks like it's about dispositions" are on a collision course, but you tell me). Then tell me which single requirement does the most damage to the most popular views, and close with a short go at how a Ryle-style anti-intellectualism and the intellectualist reply each fare against the list. Weight the thinking on the criteria and their cases, not the scoring.

Oh and: write it as one continuous blob of prose, like an email — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, and please don't number the desiderata, carry them with phrases like "the next thing I'd ask" instead, even though I know that's harder.
