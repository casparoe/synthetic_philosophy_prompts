I'm on the five-person panel that decides who gets beam time on our observatory's dish — we're privately funded and give away a few hours a month to outside proposals. A donor-backed group has put in for ten hours to transmit a message at a G-type star roughly 50 light years out with a candidate temperate planet: active SETI/METI, the deliberate kind, not listening. I have to draft the recommendation the panel votes on — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with a time frame and reasons the applicants could actually reply to, because they're sharp and they'll push back hard.

Their case: (i) Earth is already on the map, a century of radio leakage plus a biosphere anyone with a decent instrument could pick out spectroscopically, so staying quiet buys us nothing; (ii) if you take the dark forest idea seriously, the cheapest moment to be known is now, while we're one small planet with no interstellar reach, since the cost of being found only grows as we spread; (iii) the message is written to be harmless, self-description, no military detail, an explicit statement that we aren't building toward interstellar travel.

Where I'm starting, so you don't just hand me my own view back: (i) feels like it slides between "detectable in principle by someone already pointing a big instrument at us" and "detectable by a survey at 50 light years," and I think the irreversability point is what settles it — silence is reversible, a transmission isn't, and we'll pick targets better in twenty years. But their strongest move is the one I want treated fairly, not waved off: "wait till we know more" is what a species says forever, and five people have no standing to veto a message that's only information. Also tell me whether I'm right that the harmless-content bit is actually the worst part of the message under their own dark-forest logic. I dont want the memo resting on aliens probably being friendly or probably being hostile — we have no clue, and it has to work without knowing.

Bottom line first, please, and under 700 words.
