I write a column on science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about why cooperation is easy to model and hard to explain to a general reader. I want to use Arrival (2016) as my worked example, so I'd be grateful for a careful reading of the film through the distinction between the one-shot and the iterated prisoner's dilemma, and through the standard accounts of how cooperation gets off the ground without iteration.

Here is the angle I want tested rather than flattered. The heptapods hand over their language as a costly gift, and the return on it — humanity's assistance — lies three thousand years in the future, to be supplied by parties who can never reciprocate to the givers. A chain of pay-it-forward transfers across generations has no obvious slot in the usual menu of mechanisms for cooperation, since there is no repetition, no shared future, and no reputation. I have been calling what the film seems to invoke diachronic reciprocity, and I want to know whether that is a genuine addition to the menu or merely one of the existing mechanisms with the discounting concealed. There is a second wrinkle I care about more: because the aliens experience all of time at once, an exchange that is a delayed one-shot from Louise's frame may be simultaneous from theirs. Whether a game is one-shot at all would then be relative to the players' temporal frame, which is not something the formalism is built to say.

I also want the reading resisted where it deserves to be. If the aliens already know what they will do, they do not deliberate, and an agent with no alternative strategies is arguably not in a game in the first place; on that reading the film is not a solution to the problem of cooperation but a portrait of a world in which the problem never arises. Which scenes carry which reading, where does the lens distort the film or the film exceed the lens, and is there anything the film shows about cooperation that the formal arguments cannot? Please keep explicit which game-theoretic claims are settled and which are live disputes among people who work on this, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. I know the film well, so I don't need plot summary or quotation. Close with a final line of the form \answer{...} giving, in five words or fewer, your verdict on whether Arrival supplies a real mechanism for one-shot cooperation.
