hey! so im on the sustainability club at my school and somehow we ended up with an actual job: every month we hand the food services director a one page recommendation about what to change in the cafeteria. She has final say, and she kills anything that costs more or that kids won't eat. Our meetings are 40 minutes, we're 17, nobody on the club knows philosophy or ag science, and we can only look stuff up on the regular internet. So please write this in plain American English, and probly don't send us to papers or studies we can't check anyway — just give us the reasoning and rough numbers we can argue about in the room.

Here's what we keep getting stuck on. We all agree factory farming is bad, and we all assumed that means "less meat = better." Then someone pointed out that growing crops kills animals too — field mice, bugs, whatever gets hit by plowing and harvesting and pesticides — and that beef takes way more land and way more feed per serving than chicken does. So swapping beef for chicken might help wild animals while hurting the chickens themselves, because you need a lot more chicken lives for the same amount of meat. And then someone else pointed out that the animals only exist at all because people are gonna eat them, and a broiler chicken lives like a month and a half while a beef cow lives more than a year, so depending on whether their lives are good or bad on balance, eating the shorter lived animal is either the kinder or the crueler thing. And then there's the question of whether our school even changes anything, or whether the beef we don't buy just gets sold to somebody else and the total stays the same. We go in circles every single time.

What we actually want is a procedure we can run in one meeting on one proposed swap. Something like:

- what to write down first (what's the baseline, are we comparing per serving or per pound or per meal actually served, how many meals is this)
- how to count the harm to the farmed animals, including the weird part where number of animals and length of life point in opposite directions
- how to count harm to wild animals from the crops, using only numbers we could realistically find — acres or kilos of feed per serving, whether it's local, that kind of thing
- whether our order is even big enough to move anything, or whether it just shifts the meat somewhere else
- what to do when we can't get a number for a box
- a rule for what happens when one option looks better for farmed animals and worse for wild ones, versus worse on both
- where the procedure has to stop and hand it to us as a straight up values call, because it isn't a fact question and we shouldn't pretend it is

Then please run it on the three things we're actually fighting about:

1. switching the ground beef on taco day to ground chicken
2. swapping the beef chili for black bean chili
3. a farm near us wants to sell us pasture raised beef for the burgers, and everyone is treating that as the obvious ethical upgrade

We're expecting 3 to come out looking good, so if it doesn't, say so and explain why. Also tell us where this procedure is most likely to give us the wrong answer, and what we'd have to find out to fix that part.
