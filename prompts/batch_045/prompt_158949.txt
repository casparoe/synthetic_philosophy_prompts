Hey — I'm on the four-person grants committee of a small animal-welfare funder (~$2M a year, majority vote, and we publish a short methodology note with every round). I've been reading rationalist and EA blogs since the old SSC days, so I've absorbed all the standard moves — sentience not neuron count, shut up and multiply, the sheer numbers of small animals probably dominate everything — but now I actually have to sign a number and the standard moves don't cash out into one. Our consultants gave us a weighting table and I half trust it: relative to shrimp = 1, roughly 10^2 for octopus (they flagged this one as basically made up), 10^3 for chicken, 10^4 for pig. The three live applications are (A) ~$400k for electrical stunners in one shrimp supply chain — applicant claims tens to a few hundred million animals a year affected, tiny change per animal; (B) ~$600k for a corporate broiler campaign (slower-growing breeds, lower stocking density) — far fewer animals, much bigger change per animal, and some chance of industry flow-through; (C) ~$400k for welfare standards at what would be one of the first commercial octopus farms — trivial animal numbers, enormous per-animal change, plus a real shot at setting a template for the whole sector if it scales.

A few things I keep going in circles on and want the memo to actually resolve rather than gesture at. First, I think the table is comparing the wrong units: what our grants change is a slice of a life, so maybe the right denominator is animal-years affected rather than animals — and since shrimp and broilers live months or weeks, that does something counterintuitive to the ranking, and I can't tell if it's a correction or a bug. Second, one board member wants to scrap neurons entirely and weight by what she calls "affective repertoire" — her definition: how many distinguishable valenced states the animal can be in, judged from behavioral flexibility and learning — which weirdly *promotes* the octopus and demotes the chicken, and I don't know whether that's a better proxy or just a different intuition wearing a number. Third, and this is the one that smells like fanaticism to me: the weights are uncertain over orders of magnitude, and if you just multiply point estimates the shrimp win by such a huge margin that the calculation feels like Pascal's mugging with crustaceans; I want a decision rule that survives that without collapsing into "ignore the numbers and fund what feels proportionate." And someone has to deal with the reductio head-on — taken literally the table ranks a human infant below an adult human and puts some large-brained nonhuman animals above some humans — and say honestly whether that's a real objection to the methodology, or just trustee squeamishness, since our biggest donor will absolutely bail if the published note looks like it prices human moral value.

Can you draft the memo I circulate before Thursday? Address it to the committee, ~800 words, plain prose, with a real bottom line: adopt the consultants' table, adopt it only within bounds as a tiebreaker, or refuse cross-species weighting and allocate on other grounds — and pick one, with reasons our resident anti-neuron person could accept, plus an honest statement of the strongest thing on the losing side. Please don't just tell me sentience is what matters and brain size is a bad proxy; we've all read that post, and we still have to send the money somewhere.
