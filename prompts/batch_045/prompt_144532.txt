so our last seminar session is on explanation in history and i want to close it with a poem instead of another handout. topic is hindsight bias running into what people call the narrative fallacy — the "i knew it all along" thing, plus the way knowing the ending makes a messy sequence get glued into a clean causal story that then feels like it *had* to go that way. i'd like a sestina: six six-line stanzas plus the three-line envoy, end words permuted by the actual rule, and I want to pick the words — BECAUSE, KNEW, LUCK, THREAD, AFTER, INEVITABLE. don't swap any out.

the case is invented, its not a real episode, don't go looking: the Halloway Valley levee, built 1896 by a drainage syndicate, breaches on 14 March 1902, eleven people drown, the town is abandoned. here's the archive the poem has to work with, five items:

- 1896: the engineer's letter says the bank was built four feet above "the highest freshet on record," and notes in passing that the survey was taken in a dry year
- 1897: the board votes 5–4 to spend the reserve on a stone gatehouse rather than raising the bank; the dissent says floods, not visitors, are what come up the valley
- 1899–1901: three wet winters pass with no breach, and the local paper starts calling the levee "the valley's spine"
- Feb 1902: a clerk's memo about short bolts on the gates gets misfiled and is found in March
- 14 March 1902: two days of warm rain on frozen ground, and it goes at the old mill race

contemporaries had at least two other futures in view, and the poem should let one of them in somewhere: the breach happens in 1899 instead, the bank is rebuilt properly and the town survives; or nothing happens until 1910, by which point the rail line has taken the traffic and nobody much lives in the flood plain. in both the 1896 letter and the 1897 vote look like different animals.

how the form should carry the idea: each stanza is another pass over the same archive — same facts, re-linked, so the chain of causes changes while the ending doesn't — and by the last stanza it should read as though it could not have gone otherwise. then the envoy undoes that: it should make the ordering feel like the writer's achievement, not the valley's. dont resolve it, our discussion didn't, and i really don't want a final couplet going "beware the stories we tell ourselves."

one more thing, the part i care about most: somewhere in stanza 3 or 4 put a single line that a reader arriving cold hears as a hedge ("this might matter later") and a reader who already knows the levee broke hears as a portent. same words both readings, no wink, no italics. call it the janus line.

voice is the syndicate's own historian, writing the official county account in 1906, so it can be self-possessed and a little pleased with itself, but not a villain.
