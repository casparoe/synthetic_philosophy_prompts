After forty years in experimental physics I have started sitting in on a philosophy reading group, and I would like to predict how its convenor will rule on a case that came up at our last meeting. She is a philosopher of science with a book manuscript on explanation, and she has been using Dennett's vocabulary of cranes, skyhooks, and greedy reductionism to grade candidate explanations. Her verdicts so far, from the manuscript and from her blog, are these:

(a) The fit between a woodpecker's skull and its drilling habits, explained by cumulative selection acting on heritable variation over many generations: crane.
(b) The ventilation architecture of a termite mound, explained by the evolved and socially coordinated behaviour of the colony: crane, on the grounds that the insects' own capacities are themselves products of selection, so that no design enters the story unexplained; she has said more than once that a mind is not automatically a skyhook, since a mind can itself be the product of a crane.
(c) The same mound, explained by a designer who understood ventilation: skyhook.
(d) The origin of the first information-bearing polymer, explained by a general law to the effect that matter held far from equilibrium tends of itself to organise into information-bearing structures: skyhook, on the grounds that this is simply the phenomenon to be explained promoted to the status of a law.
(e) The apparent fine-tuning of the constants, explained by a physically generated ensemble of universes together with an observer-selection effect: crane, though she adds that she would not wager anything on it, on the grounds that it obtains its result the way a sieve obtains a shape rather than the way a sculptor does.
(f) The wetness of water, explained by intermolecular forces and statistical mechanics: crane; she insists that reduction as such is not greedy.
(g) Phenomenal consciousness, explained by the claim that there is no such thing and that introspection misreports a purely functional state: greedy reductionism.
(h) The meaning of a gene, explained as nothing but a codon triplet: greedy; the same meaning explained by the selection history that installed the gene: crane.

Nothing in her published cases involves a causal loop, or designers who owe their existence to the thing they design. The new case does. A paper argues that both the fine-tuning of the constants and the adapted fit of organisms are explained by the hypothesis that our universe is a simulation run by a civilization descended from creatures very like ourselves. Every posit of design in the story is, the authors insist, derived: the simulators' intelligence arose by ordinary evolution, their machines arose by ordinary engineering, and there is no unexplained Designer and no primitive organising principle, only cranes arranged in a circle. On their own account the simulators exist only because creatures like us existed first, and we exist only because they are running the simulation. The circle is the part I cannot get past, though I cannot say whether it is the circle that matters.

Would she call this a crane, a skyhook, an instance of greedy reductionism, or something else again? I care less about the label than about the rule she is running, about the other rules that fit her record equally well, and about which version of the case would separate them. Please give me exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no preamble and no concluding paragraph: the first two setting out the two or three rules that fit her verdicts and what each predicts here; the third identifying which of her precedents is the closest analogue and why; the fourth giving your best guess with the reason for preferring it; the fifth naming the variant I should put to her next in order to decide between the rules.
