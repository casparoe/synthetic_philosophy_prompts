A wealthy acquaintance of mine — a retired options trader who now amuses herself by running small experiments on her friends' ability to act against their own future incentives — made me an offer yesterday. She will wire $40,000 to my account at noon tomorrow if, at the moment the wire clears, I genuinely intend to spend the following Saturday alone in a rented room hand-copying, in longhand, every page of a 1,100-page municipal zoning code, and then shredding the copies. She will never check whether I did it, she has no use for the copies, and she does not care what I do with the rest of the weekend. The transfer is unconditional once it is sent and does not depend on my performance. Nor may I rig things in advance: no contracts, no forfeitable deposits, no public announcements, no commitment apps, no hypnotists, no pills that manufacture resolve. She wants the intending itself, unaided, and she is a disturbingly good judge of whether someone is serious — she says she will talk with me for twenty minutes first, and that no one has ever fooled her.

The trouble is that I cannot see how to do it. Every time I try to form the intention, I catch myself thinking that once the money has arrived there will be no reason whatsoever to spend a Saturday copying zoning codes, that I know this now, and that I therefore will not do it — and whatever I was building up collapses into something closer to a wish, or to a fantasy about the sort of person I would like to be. My brother says this simply shows the offer is worthless to me and that I should forget about it. My roommate says I am overthinking it: people form resolutions they expect to break all the time, so I should just say yes and take the money.

Two further complications. First, she mentioned that if I convince her tomorrow she will repeat the offer monthly for two years with a fresh task each time, and that among her previous participants the ones who actually performed the first task found every later one easy, while those who skipped it never convinced her again. Second, my roommate's actual suggestion is that I resolve not to think about the payment until after Saturday; I genuinely cannot tell whether that is a route to forming the intention or a way of hiding from myself that I have not formed it.

Should I accept? Is the money in some strong sense unobtainable for someone in my position, or is there a real reading of "intend" on which I can intend to do something I know I will then have no reason at all to do? Is the case of the half-expected-to-break resolution really the same as mine, or does it differ in a way that matters? And if performing the first task is the only path to the later payments, can that future-directed reason support the intention now — or, by supplying a reason to perform, does it quietly turn this into a different intention than the one she is paying for?
