I've been going back and forth on the grounding literature for a while and I can't tell anymore whether the fight between the people who think grounding is a real, distinctive, hyperintensional explanatory relation we can build a theory around — with its own structural profile, transitivity-ish, asymmetry-ish, and so on — and the people who think "grounding" is just bookkeeping, a label that re-describes what we already get from essence, modality, constitution and realization, is a substantive disagreement at all. Both sides seem to have said everything they have to say and nobody moves.

My hypothesis is that its actually three disagreements stacked under one name: (1) whether the word should pick out one relation or a whole family of explanatory relations, (2) a fight about what "work" would even have to look like — unification and systematicity versus new derivations or new entailments we couldn't get before — and (3), the one I suspect is load-bearing, a straight-up bet about the expected returns on a research programme, i.e. about whether the framework will ever earn its keep, which nobody can currently refute either way. There's also a parallel brawl about whether "fundamental" is a single status or a cluster (uncaused, independent, building-blockish, not-grounded-in-anything) and I want to know if that's the same disagreement in a different costume or a genuinely separate one.

Diagnose it, using what each side actually argues — no survey of who said what, please — and take a position: which of my three is really doing the work, or what I'm missing, how confident you are in that diagnosis, what its weakest point is, and what would count as progress given it (a formal result about the structural principles, a distinction, changing the question) or why we should expect none.
