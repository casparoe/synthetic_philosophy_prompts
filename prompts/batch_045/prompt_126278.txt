I'm putting together a prospectus for a one-semester MA thesis (around 8000 words) on finks and masks, and my half-formed idea is that the whole debate has been run on dispositions whose manifestation is basically all-or-nothing — the glass breaks or it doesn't — and that this hides something. My own made-up term for the case I want to build the thesis around is a 'dimmer': an interfering factor that neither destroys the disposition nor fully blocks the manifestation, but raises the threshhold at which it manifests (the vase in thin bubble wrap; the sedated patient who does get angry, just at much greater provocation). Once you allow dimmers, masks start to look like the limit case of a continuum with finks as the limit case beyond that, which would mean the fink/mask distinction is a difference of degree — and that any repair of the simple conditional analysis has to be built for degreed dispositions from the start rather than retrofitted.

Turn that into a proposal: state the question so it has a determinate answer, say why the standard repairs (sure-fire style analyses, conditionals that relativize to the stimulus or to background conditions) don't already settle it, lay out what I'd actually do month by month — I was thinking of running three families of cases (threshhold dispositions like fragility, rate dispositions like solubility, probabilistic ones) through the dimmer test — say which findings would be interesting and which would be a dud, and give an honest verdict on whether this is too big for one semester and what to cut.
