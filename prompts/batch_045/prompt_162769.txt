I sit on the technical review panel for a programme that funds field trials of genetically engineered organisms for vector control and agricultural pest suppression. Nearly every proposal we read runs the same risk: the case for release is argued at the level of the construct or the gene — a transmission advantage, a super-Mendelian inheritance bias — while the risk assessment is argued at the level of the local population or the host–symbiont system: persistence, collapse, spread beyond the release site. When the two lines of argument point to different outcomes, we have no principled way to decide which to act on, or what we would have to measure in a two-year monitoring window to find out. What we need, then, is not another position in the units-of-selection debate but a specification: what a theory of the units of selection would have to be able to do in order to be adequate for this job — planning and auditing deliberate releases into evolving systems we cannot fully control.

Please write that specification. Keep adequacy tied to that purpose throughout, and build it around the cases a theory would have to get right, including at least these:

- A construct with a large within-population transmission advantage whose spread lowers the productivity or viability of the groups it invades, up to local extinction. Gene-level and group-level descriptions give different expectations here, and the theory has to tell us what to bet on, not merely how to redescribe what happened.
- An engineered symbiont in a host–microbiome system, where heritable material moves horizontally between lineages and parent–offspring relations are not tree-like. There is no obvious answer to what the replicating unit is, and the theory has to say something actionable rather than treating this as a degenerate case.
- A release design in which groups happen to be composed of relatives, so that the same predicted outcome can be laid out either as kin selection or as selection between groups.
- A organism that modifies the environment in which it is selected, so that what a decomposition treats as the fixed environmental background is partly a product of the population's own heritable traits.
- A system in which the lower-level units progressively lose the capacity to reproduce independently over the timescale of interest, so that the status of a candidate unit is itself something that changes rather than something given in advance.
- The general worry that any observed outcome can be retrofitted to some level or other. We have to commit to measurements before we know the outcome, so the specification must say what makes a level claim testable in advance and what would count as it failing.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, and mark it as non-negotiable or as the preference of a particular camp. Say which distinctions a candidate theory must respect and which are spurious — for instance between a replicator, an interactor, and a beneficiary; between selection of a unit and selection for a trait; between selection acting at a level and selection at that level being the strongest; between being a unit of selection and being the bearer of an adaptation; between a causal claim and a bookkeeping decomposition that is mathematically equivalent to another. If some subset of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied — maximal generality across substrates versus testability with noisy field data, say, or refusing to presuppose a hierarchy of levels versus being able to say which partition is the real one — flag that explicitly, since a list that turns out to be internally unsatisfiable is itself the result I most want. Then name the single requirement that does the most work in excluding otherwise popular theories, and explain why.

End, if it is cheap to do so, with a short table scoring gene-level selectionism, multi-level selection, and inclusive-fitness approaches against the list; the criteria and their motivating cases are the deliverable, not the scoring. Please do not give me a literature review, a reading list, or citations — I want the specification itself, written so that a regulator who is not a population geneticist could follow which requirements are doing real work.
