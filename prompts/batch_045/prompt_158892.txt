I want a number on a claim about sortition, but I want the number together with the argument that produces it, not the number on its own.

The claim is not about lottery-selected government in general. It is about one design, which I will call a rotating mirror chamber, so that the question stays sharp:

- A second chamber of 200 members drawn by stratified lot from the adult citizenry, stratified on age, region, sex, and educational attainment; service is compulsory unless a member qualifies for a narrow exemption.
- Members serve four years, with half the chamber replaced every two years, staggered; no one may serve twice.
- No member may stand for any elected office for eight years after their term ends, and members may not join a party caucus or accept party direction while serving.
- The chamber cannot originate legislation. It receives bills from the elected chamber and may approve them, return them with amendments, or delay them for up to eighteen months, a delay the elected chamber can override by explicit re-passage.
- A randomly designated fifth of each cohort is told, at the start of its term, that its sole function is to construct the strongest case against whatever proposal is currently gaining support in the plenary; these members deliberate but do not vote on the final question.

The comparator is the country's existing elected upper chamber continuing unchanged. The country is a large, wealthy, institutionally stable democracy with an independent civil service and a free press. The window is the first twenty years after adoption, including the transitional period in which procedures are being worked out. The proposition I want a credence for is that this country would, over that window, produce better legislative outcomes than it would have produced had it kept the elected chamber. I am aware that "better legislative outcomes" may be doing most of the work here, so treat the definability of the outcome as one of the things the credence depends on rather than as something I have settled; if you want a provisional operationalization to argue against, take a pre-registered index combining the frequency with which major statutes are substantially reversed within five years of enactment, the variance in independently assessed quality of enacted legislation, and measured public trust in the legislature.

Please give me:

1. A headline credence, a single number between 0 and 1, for the proposition.
2. The decomposition that yields it. I expect at least four channels through which the design could help or hurt: the composition channel (does a descriptively representative body bring different knowledge, priorities, or veto points into lawmaking, or does mirroring the population merely mirror its ignorance?); the incentive channel (does removing re-election, campaign finance, and party discipline change what members are willing to vote for, and is the eight-year cooling-off rule load-bearing or merely symbolic?); the deliberation channel (do balanced briefing, structured small-group deliberation, and the designated-objector mechanism improve judgment under uncertainty, or do they create an adversarial faction and a dependency on whoever drafts the materials?); and the capture channel (given that members have no independent expertise, no institutional memory because of rotation, and no electoral base to appeal to, how much of the chamber's behaviour is set by the secretariat, the expert panels, and the elected chamber's control of the agenda?). Add a term for whether the proposition is well-posed at all, and any other term you think belongs. Give a number and one line of justification for each, state whether you are treating the terms as independent, and say explicitly how you are combining them.
3. Sensitivity. Report roughly what the headline becomes if each term in turn is moved to 0 and to 1, and identify the one that moves it most.
4. Two conditional numbers besides the headline: the credence if the chamber is given delay powers only, and the credence if it is given full amendment powers. I want to know how much of the effect you are attributing to formal authority rather than to selection and deliberation.
5. A diagnosis of a disagreement. My credence is about 0.45; a colleague whose work is on democratic theory puts it at about 0.8. Tell me which of your terms most likely separates us, and what each of us would have to be shown in order to converge.
6. Evidence. Say what evidence would move your number most — including what a set of, say, a dozen adoptions with matched non-adopting controls, scored on a pre-registered index, would have to show to move it, and by roughly how much — and say honestly whether any real-world evidence you have about citizens' assemblies convened on contested constitutional questions already bears on this proposition at all, and if so in which direction and with what weight.

Two constraints. First, do not give me bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I want your reasoning, and I will not be able to check references. Second, mark the places where you are reasoning from a mechanism you can actually specify — how staggered rotation with no repeat service changes the incentive structure, for instance — as opposed to places where you are relying on a claim you remember being made about sortition; if a term in your decomposition is doing nothing more than echoing something in the literature, say so and discount it accordingly.
