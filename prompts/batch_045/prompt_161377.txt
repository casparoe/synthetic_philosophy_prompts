So me and my co-founder run a smallish online shop (homebrewing supplies, maybe 40k customers) and last year we replaced our support inbox with a model we fine-tuned on about nine years of emails written by one former employee, Mira, who everyone loved and who left in 2019. It signs its replies – Mira, Customer Support, it has her habits (she always offered a replacement before a refund, she never wrote "unfortunately"), and customers constantly write back stuff like "thanks Mira, you said you'd send a new hydrometer by Friday".

Three things we keep arguing about:

1. When it writes "I'll get a replacement out to you today" and then our warehouse drops the ball, customers say we broke a promise. My co-founder says nothing was promised, because an LLM can't promise anything, it just emits text shaped like promising. I say: from the customer's side a promise happened, and whether the sentence came out of Mira's fingers or out of a fine-tuned model isn't the part that matters. Which of us is talking nonsense?

2. He wants a banner on every reply saying it was generated by an AI. I hate the idea, but his actual argument is more interesting than the banner: he says these models aren't one agent at all, they're more like a machine that can step into any role, so "Mira" is just a mask it happens to be wearing this week, and letting people think otherwise is the real deception. His evidence: last month a customer threatened to sue us and the bot answered in this stiff formal legal-ish tone that sounded nothing like Mira, like it slipped into a lawyer character for a paragraph. I said that's just sloppy fine-tuning, not proof of anything. A friend suggested a tie-breaker: try to talk it out of character — if it slips easily it was only ever playing Mira, if it holds the voice it genuinely is Mira-ish now. But I pointed out that a well-trained actor would also refuse to break character, so the test doesn't seem to separate the two stories. Is there any outside-view test that does?

3. We're about to give it real tools, so it can issue refunds up to 50 euros and cancel orders on its own. He says: if you don't think it's an agent, you shouldn't let it act. I sort of think the opposite — that hooking it up to actions is exactly what makes the agent question urgent instead of settling it.

I think I'm conflating two different things and can't untangle them: whether the thing has Mira's voice, and whether it's the kind of thing that can be on the hook for what comes out of it. Are those actually separate questions? And concretely, what should we do about the disclaimer and the refund button?
