I am assembling teaching materials on statutory interpretation and I want a case study rather than a survey of doctrine. Please analyze *Yates v. United States*, 574 U.S. 528 (2015), as a test of textualism against purposivism.

Work from the facts as I state them, since you cannot look anything up, and do not let anything below depend on your recollection of the record or of the opinions. If you think some detail here is wrong, flag it in one sentence and then work from the case as I have described it.

The case: In 2007 a federal agent inspecting a commercial fishing boat in the Gulf found 72 undersized red grouper in the catch, in violation of federal conservation rules, and told the captain to keep those fish segregated so they could be measured on shore. When the fish were remeasured, only 69 were undersized. Yates was charged under 18 U.S.C. § 1519, a provision enacted as part of the Sarbanes–Oxley Act of 2002, which applies to anyone who knowingly alters, destroys, mutilates, conceals, falsifies, or makes a false entry in "any record, document, or tangible object" with intent to impede or obstruct a federal investigation; the maximum penalty is 20 years. He was convicted and sentenced to 30 days, and the Eleventh Circuit affirmed on the ground that fish have physical form and so are tangible objects. The Supreme Court reversed 5–4. A four-justice plurality (Ginsburg, joined by Roberts, Breyer, and Sotomayor) held that within § 1519 a "tangible object" is one used to record or preserve information, relying on the canons *noscitur a sociis* and *ejusdem generis*, on the provision's enactment as part of a financial-fraud statute, and on its placement in the criminal code; the fifth vote came from Alito, who wrote separately. Kagan dissented, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas, arguing that a tangible object is anything capable of being touched, that the phrase covers physical objects of every kind throughout the U.S. Code, and that Congress meant the provision to have a wide range — bad drafting, on her view, is Congress's problem and not a reason for the Court to narrow it.

Note the feature that makes the case worth a seminar: the justices usually described as textualists produced the broader, more literal reading, and the justices usually associated with purposive or contextual reading produced the narrower one. I want you to take that inversion seriously rather than explain it away.

Please do the following.

   (ii) the company the phrase keeps — the other words of the same sentence, and the associated-word canons;

   (iv) unenacted legislative history and what the enacting Congress expected the provision to be used for;

   Then name, for each method, which input or combination of inputs is doing the real work, and say explicitly where the two methods' admissible-evidence sets differ and where they do not.

3. **Four controlled rewrites.** Each variant changes one variable in the same statute.
