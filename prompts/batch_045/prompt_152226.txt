I work in machine learning, mostly on lossy compression and learned world models — I'm fluent in minimum description length, rate–distortion trade-offs, Kolmogorov-style complexity bounds, latent-variable models, amortised versus iterative inference, inverse reinforcement learning and theory-of-mind modules — and I've been reading Dennett on real patterns and on the three stances (physical, design, intentional) for a small reading group. Much of it reads to me like work I already do under other names, and I can't tell whether that's genuine recognition or just me finding my own tools everywhere. A colleague at the group said, offhand, that the intentional stance is "just amortised inference about a rational agent", which I suspect loses something but can't say what. So I'd like a systematic translation into my vocabulary, and — more importantly — an honest account of what the translation fails to carry.

Please restate, one at a time and as faithfully as you can: (i) his criterion for when a pattern counts as real — roughly, that there is some description of the data, shorter than the data itself or at least enabling predictions better than brute-force simulation, with no bound on how much computation the predictor is allowed, but with the coding scheme not permitted to smuggle the content in behind the scenes; (ii) the physical stance; (iii) the design stance; (iv) the intentional stance, together with the associated claim that there can be a fact of the matter about what a system believes and wants even though nothing in the underlying physics seems to single out beliefs and desires as such. Do the translation into the vocabulary I named above — code lengths, model classes and their capacity, encoder–decoder pairs, rollouts versus analytic prediction, planning-as-inference, IRL, predictive baselines — and flag explicitly whenever my vocabulary forces you to choose one formalism where the original talk leaves several options open, since I want to know where the choice is yours rather than Dennett's.

Then the part I actually care about. Suppose we build a system that is post-trained, via an auxiliary "legibility" objective, so that predictors using belief-desire vocabulary — human observers, or a learned theory-of-mind module — do unusually well at predicting it; imagine an agent shaped so that ascribing it goals and beliefs is the cheapest available compression of its behaviour. On the real-patterns criterion, does the resulting belief-desire pattern count as real? Does the framework have the resources to distinguish "the intentional stance succeeds because the system is approximately rational" from "it succeeds because the system was optimised to be legible to predictors like us", and if it has no room for that distinction, is that a defect in the account or is my distinction empty? More generally, give me the residue report: what translated cleanly, what translated only with distortion, and what cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all — I'm thinking of aboutness, the normativity of rationality, the difference between predicting and explaining, the teleology baked into the design stance, and whether a stance is something we adopt or something that is there to be found. For each piece of residue, say whether you think it indicts my framework or shows the original claim to be less determinate than it looked.
