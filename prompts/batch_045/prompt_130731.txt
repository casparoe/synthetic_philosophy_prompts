hey, me and my debate partner got into this in the car after a meet and i think i argued myself into a corner and cant find the exit

our topic this semester is inheritance taxes. our case was basically: nobody picks their parents, nobody deserves what their parents hand them, so the state should take most of it back so that kids start from the same place. standard stuff. then I tried to make the argument airtight and it blew up. here it is

(1) The real reason to tax inheritance is that a huge chunk of how your life goes is decided by pure luck — specifically, which family you got born into — and justice says luck shouldn't decide that.

(2) But there's no principled place to stop at money. Everything a parent gives a kid is unearned advantage of exactly the same kind: the cash, sure, but also the house, the neighborhood, the accent, the fact that somebody sat and read to them every night, the internships they can call in a favor for, the genes.

(3) So if the luck thing is really the reason, an inheritance tax is a bizarrely weak tool. It grabs one channel and leaves every other channel wide open.

(4) My partner and I came up with a cleaner version, we call it the Swap Test: the amount of your advantage that's "unfair" is the difference between how well you'd do with your actual parents and how well you'd do if you'd been handed to a randomly picked pair of parents from the whole population. Justice says the state should claw back that whole difference, not just the cash slice of it.

(5) Under the Swap Test, though, a dad reading his kid bedtime stories is doing literally the same thing a rich dad does when he wires his kid 200k: handing over an unearned boost. So if we're fine taxing the wire transfer, we should be fine taxing — or just banning — the bedtime story, and if we follow that all the way down we should raise kids in state institutions where nobody can sneak them anything extra.

(6) Nobody believes that. Banning bedtime stories is monstrous, and seperating kids from their parents is worse.

(7) So the luck reason for the inheritance tax is either bankrupt or it was never doing the work: it either collapses into something much weaker than (1), or it forces (5).

So: which step do i kill? And what does killing it cost me in the actual debate round — like, if i drop (2) I have to explain why money is special, and i dont have a good answer for that, and if i drop (1) I dont have a case at all. I'd really like to keep (1) and (6).

One more thing in case it matters: my partner says the exit is that the Swap Test tells us to *level up* instead of level down — like, if the problem is that some kid got less than a random kid would, then the fix is to top up the kids who got less (books, preschool, whatever), not to take away the bedtime story. But that feels like a rhetorical swerve to me and not a real answer, because then the same logic seems to say the state should force every parent to do the maximally enriching thing, and it stops looking like a tax argument at all. Is that swerve the actual answer or is it a dodge? And if it IS the answer, does (4) survive it or is the Swap Test itself the broken part?
