Retired physical chemist here, fourty years in catalysis, and I've lately started going to a philosophy of physics reading group down at the library. They've asked me to open next month's session on the arrow of time with something light — two minutes, one joke — before we get into the past hypothesis and why the microscopic laws don't seem to care which way things run.

I dont want the usual material: nothing about time flying when you're having fun, nothing about my late wife tidying the house in defiance of the second law (someone in the group already emailed me that one), and no physicist-walks-into-a-bar template. I want the punchline to turn on the actual physics, the way a decent joke for chemists turns on the chemistry. Here's a thought, use it or bin it: a handheld gadget you point at a room that reads out the coarse-grained entropy of whatever's in front of you, with a second dial for the fine-grained entropy, which of course sits there unchanged while the first one climbs, because the dynamics isn't doing the work — the coarse-graining is. Something about pointing it round the house, or at a scrambled egg, or at myself in the mirror.

Give me one or two jokes — setup and punchline — that would land with a room of retired physicists and chemists, that don't depend on anyone remembering who Boltzmann or Loschmidt were (half the room would switch off), and that aren't built on entropy-as-disorder, since one of the chemists will stand up and say that's misleading. Add a line on what each joke turns on, so I can check I'm not about to say something false out loud.
