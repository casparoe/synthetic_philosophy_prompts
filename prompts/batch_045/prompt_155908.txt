You are a philosopher of mind who works on the temporal structure of conscious experience and on the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction Dennett drew for cases where the content a subject attributes to a moment seems to depend on input that arrives after that moment — Orwellian revision meaning that an experience occurred and the record of it was afterwards rewritten, Stalinesque revision meaning that the draft was edited before it was ever presented, so nothing was experienced at that moment and the episode is merely dated there. Before committing yourself, generate more than one rule that could be generating the verdicts below.

I am a graduate student working with a senior philosopher of perception — call her R — who is writing a book on the timing of experience, and I have to predict what she will say about a new case before I write the section that turns on it. Her verdicts so far, on cases where the content attributed to a time t may depend on input arriving later than t:

1. Standard colour phi: a red spot at A, then about 100 ms later a green spot at B a few degrees away; subjects report a single spot travelling from A to B that changes colour partway along the path, at a point reached before the green spot is even presented. She says Stalinesque.
2. Metacontrast masking: a target disc followed roughly 80 ms later by a ring mask; subjects deny seeing the target at all. She says neither — no revision is needed, since nothing was ever presented at the target time.
3. The cutaneous rabbit: rapid taps at the wrist, then further up the forearm, then near the elbow; subjects report taps hopping up the arm, including at places never stimulated, each dated to its own moment. She says Stalinesque.
4. The natural blind spot: subjects report an unbroken textured field across the region where they have no photoreceptors. She says neither — no revision, because the filled-in content is built from surrounding input available at the same time, not from later input.

The new case, from a pilot study of ours. A steady tone is replaced by a 500 ms burst of noise, after which the tone resumes; listeners report hearing the tone continue throughout the noise. On probe trials a light flashes 250 ms into the noise and listeners must make a speeded two-button judgement about whether the tone is present at that moment; responses land about 200 ms after the flash, hence still before the tone has resumed, and are overwhelmingly tone absent. So at a moment when the input that settles the question has not yet arrived, listeners indicate that there is no tone; then, at the end of the trial, they report having heard the tone continuously through the noise.

Predict R's verdict on this case — Stalinesque, Orwellian, neither, or no fact of the matter — and say which rule she is implicitly running. As far as I can see, her four verdicts fit all of these equally well: a postdictive default, on which later-dependence always means Stalinesque, because an unconsummated draft can drive a button press without ever having been presented; an evidence-driven rule, on which later-dependence plus a probe that catches an experience at t whose content conflicts with the final report means Orwellian; and an integration-window rule, on which Stalinesque is only available while the later input falls inside a short window, so that a 500 ms gap forces some other verdict, such as a confabulated report or no fact of the matter. She has never once returned the no-fact-of-the-matter verdict, which makes me think she is not running the sceptical rule about the distinction having no empirical content. Tell me which of the surviving rules you think she is running and why, what each of the others predicts about the new case, and which further case — a variant of our pilot, or one of 1 to 4 rerun with a probe — would best separate them.
