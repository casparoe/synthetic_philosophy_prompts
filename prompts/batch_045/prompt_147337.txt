I build infrastructure on top of large models for a living and follow the forecasting debates on the side, with no formal background in philosophy. I would like help drafting the joint statement for an adversarial collaboration between someone who expects a sharp, discontinuous intelligence explosion and someone who expects a long, continuous takeoff diffused through the economy.

Before they begin, I want them to adopt one distinction I rarely see made cleanly, because I think it removes some of the shouting: separate capability takeoff — the growth of a system's latent capability per unit of effective compute, measured under the best elicitation anyone can find — from realized takeoff — the growth of its measured effects in the world per unit of calendar time. Realized takeoff is capability takeoff minus deployment friction, and I suspect both camps are often describing the same trajectories while reading off different sides of that equation. If the distinction is less useful than I think, say so rather than building the document on it.

Please produce, naming positions rather than papers and without citations:

1. "Agreed claims" — a numbered list of about ten claims both parties could sign (~300 words). Keep them substantive rather than platitudes: include what both accept about the decomposition above, about the gap between demonstrated and latent capability, and about how much of the research-and-engineering loop a system would have to be performing itself for its improvement to count as recursive in the relevant sense.

2. "Disagreements" — a table (~300 words) with columns for the issue, the sharp-takeoff formulation, the continuous-takeoff formulation, and whether the disagreement is substantive or merely verbal. Phrase each formulation so that the side in question would accept it as a statement of its own view.

3. "Cruxes and pre-registered predictions" — a table (~300 words) giving, for each crux, the argument or measurement both sides accept as bearing on it, and each side's prediction written down in advance. Candidates worth using: whether the gap between best-known scaffolding and default deployment widens with scale; whether the share of the improvement loop performed by systems themselves rises smoothly or jumps; and whether capability growth looks continuous when plotted against effective compute but discontinuous when plotted against calendar time.

4. "What would move us" — (~150 words) the consideration each party would have to encounter to revise its position, and, separately, where this document's own framing is most likely to be wrong or to make the residual disagreement look tidier than it really is.
