So here's my situation and I'm kind of losing sleep over it. My brother-in-law Marcus is 34 and for the last eight months or so he has not been himself — he quit his job, he barely sleeps, and he's convinced his downstairs neighbors are running some kind of signal through the walls to mess with him. He's not violent, he's never threatened anyone, he's just... inhabiting this story that nobody else can see the evidence for. My sister finally got him to see a psychiatrist and they're talking about schizophrenia and medication. Meanwhile my other brother has been on this kick that mental illness is a myth: that a disease has to be a real lesion in an organ, that "mental illness" is just a fancy medical word we slapped onto behavior that annoys or frightens the rest of us, and that psychiatry is basically the state's polite way of dealing with people it doesn't know what else to do with. When he first started in on this I thought it was crank stuff. And then a friend of a friend — woman, about Marcus's age — had what everyone around her treated as a full psychotic break, and it turned out her own antibodies were attacking receptors in her brain, and they put her on steroids and she was basically herself again in a few weeks. That genuinely rattled me, because it makes the "there's no lesion, so it's not a disease" line sound like it might just be an argument from the current state of our scanners. Maybe there are lesions and we cant see them yet.

But here's the thing I cant shake, and it's the reason I'm writing this instead of just going along with the plan. Marcus doesn't want treatment. And my sister has started looking into whether she can get him committed, or at least dragged into an evaluation he has to attend. The whole apparatus suddenly looks to me like a machine for making a person swallow pills he doesn't want because what he believes upsets other people. And weirdly, that's when my brother's myth-of-mental-illness rant stopped sounding like crank stuff and started sounding like it was pointing at something real. Yet I also notice: if they did find a lesion in Marcus's brain tomorrow, none of my unease about the commitment paperwork would go away. A tumor wouldn't make forcing him any less forcing. And on the other hand, if Marcus's trouble is "just" that his marriage and his career came apart and he's built this story about the neighbors because it makes his life make sense to him, I'm not sure that makes his suffering fake or unimportant either. So the lesion question and the coercion question feel like they're sliding past each other somehow, and I cant tell which one is the actual disagreement.

I guess what I want from you is this: is the "mental illness is a myth" argument really about whether there is a physical lesion, or is the lesion stuff doing some other job in the argument? Does the autoimmune case — where the crazy-sounding behavior turned out to have a plain physical cause and cleared up when they treated the cause — actually count against the myth thesis, or is it beside the point? And more usefully: I have two intuitions that seem to be at war, one that Marcus is genuinely unwell and needs help and one that hauling him in against his will because of the content of his beliefs is a morally different animal from hauling him in for a brain tumor. Is there a way to hold both, or does taking the second one seriously basically commit me to saying he isn't ill? My sister thinks I'm being uselessly philosophical while her husband falls apart, which — fair. But I'd rather have this sorted out before the paperwork gets filed than after.
