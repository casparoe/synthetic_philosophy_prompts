I work on the procurement side of a public agency, and we're trying to write fairness requirements into the contracts we sign with vendors who sell us risk scores (benefits eligibility, that sort of thing). Vendors bid with different models, we audit them after they've been running for a while, and everyone building a model knows the rule in advance. I need a spec before I write anything down.

What I want from you: the list of requirements a fairness criterion has to meet to be usable as a procurement rule, with the motivating case behind each. The ones I keep circling: an auditor has to be able to check it from decisions and realised outcomes alone (we don't get the features, and outcomes are only observed for people who were selected); it has to be comparable across vendors whose scores aren't on the same scale; it can't be something a vendor can hit by tuning against the audit while making things worse on the ground; it should compose, or at least not fall apart, when the same person passes through three of these systems in a row; and it should still mean something when the score allocates an intervention that changes the outcome it was predicting. Add any I'm missing, say which are non-negotiable and which are just preferences, and flag anywhere I'm confusing the definition of a criterion with an estimator we could actually compute.

The bit I really care about: given the familiar no-go results — you can't have calibration and equal error rates across groups at once when base rates differ, and the rest of that family — which subset of my requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, and what would we have to drop. One line each on the existing criteria at the end, no scorecard.
