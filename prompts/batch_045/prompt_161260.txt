ok so im on the staff of my school paper and I've been reading Kierkegaard on the press and the public for an elective, and honestly it lines up way too well with what's happening at our school right now. Our principal just rolled out this thing called the Pulse: an anonymous comment board where anyone can say anything about anyone, and every morning the app spits out one number, the Student Voice Index, which the guy who built it describes as what the school actually thinks, averaged. The paper is being told to run aggregates instead of articles and nobody signs their stuff anymore. I want to satire this for the next issue but I definately can't make it land on my own.

Can you write it? What I want: a parody letter to the student body in the school paper, around 600 to 700 words, in the voice of an editorial board announcing that the paper is going fully anonymous and will no longer print opinions, only what the Pulse reports that students are saying. Deadpan, completely earnest, no winking, no sneer quotes, no moment where the writer lets on what's actually happening — a reader should figure it out from the content alone. Keep it signed in a way that makes clear nobody wrote it, and end with something like an invitation for readers to anonymously edit it so it becomes a document no one is the author of.

The jokes have to come from getting Kierkegaard right, not from vibes: the public as this phantom that no one in particular belongs to and that no one can be held to account for, everyone being leveled down so that nobody gets to be someone, all the talking that stands in for doing, and why he thought it matters that an actual single person stands behind a sentence and owns it — that stuff he went after the anonymous Copenhagen weekly Corsaren over, and the fact that he taunted it into mocking him first. One thing though: he hid behind a pile of invented names himself, and I want that used against him in a way that's fair and not a cheap gotcha, like the board is genuinely puzzled by it and decides it proves their point somehow. Don't attack any real person, living or dead; invent the faculty advisor, the app guy, whoever.

And please, no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — I have to paste it into the paper's template as one block of continuous prose, paragraphs only, the way you'd write an email.
