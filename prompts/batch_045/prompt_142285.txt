My friend Nadia is writing a thesis on Du Bois view that all art is propaganda — her line is that on his account every work of art is propaganda, so the only real questions left are whose side it works for and whether it actually works. In our reading group she's been handing down verdicts on cases (some real, some invented), and I've been writing them down:

- a blunt NAACP anti-lynching poster, made explicitly to change minds, which did help move a legislature: propaganda, and the good kind
- a 1920s romance novel by a Black author with no politics in it at all, written for money and for fun: propaganda, and the good kind — making Black people ordinary to their readers does more than a speech does
- a beautiful novel about Black life that ends up reenacting the tragic mulatto plot: propaganda, but for the other side, so it fails
- a two hour protest play that lectures the audience and changes nobody's mind: not what he's after at all
- an unsigned abstract quilt with no representational content: propaganda, and the good kind — beauty made by us is itself an argument

New case, and I definately can't call it: a young artist builds an installation around what she presents as her great-grandmother's diaries from a Georgia prison farm. White legislators who saw it credited it with changing their minds, and a memorial got funded. Then it came out that the real diaries were three pages of grocery lists and she wrote the rest herself, filling in from other people's histories. Everyone agrees the thing worked. Nobody agrees whether that settles it, or whether the fact that its power came from a fabrication disqualifies it.

Give me exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, no intro and no wrap-up at the end:

1. your best guess at Nadia's verdict on the installation, with your reason in the same breath
2. the single rule that best fits all five of her earlier verdicts taken together
3. the strongest rival rule that fits those verdicts just as well, and what it predicts about this case
4. the specific thing in her record that can't tell those two rules apart
5. the one further case you'd put to her to settle it, and why that one
