Heres the thesis I'm defending in chapter 3. Vague gradable adjectives dont have a stable extension even *within* a single conversation, because the comparison class is a short recency-weighted sliding window over the items under discussion: at step n, tall (or heap, or bald) means something like tall relative to the three or four things we've most recently been considering, with the most recent ones weighted more. So the tolerance premise in the sorites isn't nearly-true-but-unusable; it's an equivocation. Each step silently re-sets the standard, so the series never chains the same predicate, and the conclusion never gets off the ground. Alleged upsides: you keep classical logic and bivalence, you keep the intuition that tolerance is *almost* right, and you get order and recency effects for free. Assume the window length N is a fixed small number (say 3) unless you think that's where the whole thing breaks.
