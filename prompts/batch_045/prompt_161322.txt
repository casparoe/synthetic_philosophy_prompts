hi — i run the philosophy club at my school. about 14 people, ages 15 to 18, and its split pretty evenly between art kids and math kids who get annoyed with each other. we get one hour, no homework between meetings, no budget, and a room with chairs and a whiteboard and thats basically it.

for the next meeting i want to do something on otto neurath and his idea of a picture language. the thing he actually built — the picture-statistics system from vienna in the 20s and 30s, rows of little identical human figures where one figure stands for so many people (isotype? i think thats what its called, i might be making that up). and the bigger claim behind it: that you can say real, checkable stuff about the world in pictures alone, without words and without people needing to be literate, and that this does something sentences dont do.

i want people to run into two things on their own rather than me telling them:

1) that pictures really can carry a lot — quantities, comparisons, change over time — and that this lands differently from a written report. more immediate, or harder to argue with, or it feels like its just showing you the fact instead of claiming it.

2) that a picture language gets into real trouble once you need it to do certain jobs: say "not", say "all" or "some", say "if this then that", mark something as an estimate or as uncertain, and especially say stuff about its own symbols — like "this figure here doesn't mean what you think it means" or "this picture is misleading" or "these two pictures disagree". they should find that fixing any of that means adding conventions, and that conventions have to be taught, which is its own kind of literacy. so the "no words needed" claim kind of eats itself.

and then a third thing i have no idea how to stage: his point that you can't step outside your pictures or statements and compare them against the world directly — you can only compare them with other statements you've already accepted. the boat thing people associate with him, sailors rebuilding their ship plank by plank while its still in the water with no dry dock. my idea was to give someone the role of an inspector who has to check whether a picture is a faithful report of what's actually there, but who is only ever allowed to recieve more pictures and is never allowed to look at the setup. does that work or is it just confusing?

can you design the whole thing? what i need:

- a mostly-silent game with real rules, roles and rounds, where the tasks escalate in exactly the way that makes trouble number 2 show up by itself. no lecture up front, nobody hears his name until the end, and the quantities in the early rounds should be about actual stuff in the room (how many chairs, how many windows, how many people in hoodies) so there's a real fact of the matter.
- materials: paper, markers, index cards, envelopes, a barrier, whatever — nothing that costs money or needs color printing.
- scoring that doesn't involve real money. i was thinking each team also has to predict in advance how well another team will decode their picture and gets scored on the prediction too, so overconfidence costs them — tell me if that's a good idea or if it makes the whole thing too fiddly.
- a minute-by-minute timeline for the hour, including setup and cleanup, because we always run over.
- a debrief that maps specific moments from the game onto specific parts of the idea. include what i should say when someone goes "ok but we could just write a caption under it" or "well obviously you have to learn the symbols, so what" — i want answers to those that dont sound like a cop-out.
- an honest list of what will go wrong the first time we run it (art kids dominating? people drawing way too much detail? decoders guessing from context instead of reading the picture? someone getting frustrated and just talking? groups finishing at totally different speeds?) and what to do about each.
- and be straight about what the game can't do: which part of the view i'll just have to explain myself at the end because no activity can produce it.

also, half the club sometimes doesn't show up, so give me a version of the roles that still works with 7 or 8 people.

nobody is reading anything beforehand and i don't want a reading list or quotes for a handout or anything like that — just the activity.
