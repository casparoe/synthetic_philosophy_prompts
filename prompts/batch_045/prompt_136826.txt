I'm doing a piece on a guy who thinks the doomsday argument is one special case of something much more general, and I've been chewing on his version for a week and can't find the bad step. Here it is more or less as he wrote it to me:

1. Don't treat yourself as special. If you're reasoning about where you sit in some population, treat your position in it as a random draw from the members of that population.
2. I'm roughly the 100-billionth human. Call that n. (Demographers' back-of-envelope, and I'm happy to be off by a factor of two.)
3. If the total number of humans who will ever live is N, the chance of finding myself at rank n is 1/N for any N at least n. So a world with N = 10^20 is disfavored against one with N = 2x10^11 by a factor of about 5x10^8: my position is wildly uncharacteristic of a huge world.
4. Feed that into any prior that doesn't already build in an enormous future and out comes: roughly 95% confidence that N is under 20n. At eight billion of us and counting, that's centuries, maybe a couple of millennia. Doom soon. Its just the doomsday argument at this point and I can live with it.
5. But nothing in 1-4 mentions humans. The only inputs are a class with a fixed order, a member of it (me), and my position in it. So run it on anything I belong to.
6. Years of my own life: I'm 43, so ~95% confidence I'm dead within about 1700 years. Silly but harmless.
7. Humans ordered by when they first grasp this very argument: I'm maybe the 100,000th, so with 95% confidence fewer than 2 million people ever understand it. The argument is true and will never be more than a cult curiosity.
8. Run it at rank 10^6, an early forager who somehow had the arithmetic: 95% confidence the species ends before 20 million humans have lived. We're at 100 billion. She'd have been wrong, and so would nearly every generation since.

His warm-up case, which he thinks settles it, he calls the Lighthouse: a species seeds 100 colonies, 99 fizzle out after about a thousand members each, one goes on to produce 10^18 people over a billion years. You wake up as the 500th member of some colony, told all this. He says 1-4 tell you you're in a fizzler, since almost nobody in the big colony is as early as 500th. I said almost everybody who ever exists is in the big one, so surely you should think you're there. He said that's a different principle with its own crazy consequences, and then mentioned a variant where the big-colony people never invent arithmetic and said that changes the answer, which felt like a dodge but I couldn't say why.

Which step do you reject, and why did it look airtight? I'd like to keep 1, I really don't think I'm special, and I've checked the arithmetic in 3 and 4 twice and its fine. So price the exits for me: what does each one commit me to elsewhere? And is the Lighthouse the same argument in miniature, or is something else going on in it?
