Last night at a town meeting about whether to let a warehouse go up next to the wetland, a guy kept repeating "everyone affected by a decision deserves a say in making it", and used it to argue that commuters from the next town over — who'd drive past the thing every day — should get a vote in our election. Nobody could quite say why that felt wrong, but it did.

Can you lay out the precise claims that slogan might be making? Stuff like: does it pick out people whose interests get set back, people who'd be forced to obey whatever rule comes out, or people merely governed by it; is it saying the affected get a say or that only the affected do (so a resident who doesn't care about the warehouse loses theirs); is "a say" a vote, a veto, a right to be consulted, or just not being ignored; is there a threshold for how much you have to be affected, or does any effect at all count; and are interests objective or just what people take them to be?

For each reading, tell me whether it's a platitude, whether it's actually plausible, and whether it really does hand the commuters a vote. And flag whether any version dodges the obvious blowup — that on the wide readings basically everyone on earth, including future people and foreigners downstream of our emissions, is affected by everything, so the principle stops picking out a demos at all — without getting so narrow that it can't do the boundary work it's supposed to do.
