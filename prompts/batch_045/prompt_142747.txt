My friend and I spent three hours on this last night and went in circles. The question was whether sphexishness — the mechanical rigidity the digger wasp is the textbook case of: a fixed action sequence released by a specific stimulus, running to completion once initiated, and having to be restarted from the beginning if it is interrupted — is a threat to free will that is independent of the threat from determinism. My friend says no: what should worry us is not determinism but rigidity, because what agency requires is flexibility, and a deterministic system can be flexible. He cashes flexibility out as two things he runs together: sensitivity of output to variation in input across nearby situations, and the capacity to revise a course of action mid-stream on the basis of information gathered while acting. I say flexibility is just determinism at a finer grain: the wasp's routine is insensitive to one variable and the human's deliberation is insensitive to none only relative to a flattering description; a deterministic mechanism that is sensitive to everything still yields exactly one output for its inputs, and the wasp's routine was shaped by selection to be sensitive to exactly the variables that mattered for it. We agree on every empirical fact about the wasp, about human neurobiology, and about what flexibility consists in operationally. We still disagree.

My hypothesis, which I want you to test rather than confirm: "sphexish" is doing at least three separate jobs here — a structural one (fixed action pattern, ballistic, not interruptible and revisable), a bypassing one (the agent's own take on the reasons was not what produced the behavior), and a counterfactual one (low sensitivity across nearby situations). And these come apart: overlearned skilled action — braking at a light, typing a familiar sentence — looks ballistic and hard to interrupt yet seems fully reason-mediated, while some post-hoc confabulation looks flexible and revisable while the agent's stated reasons do no causal work. If so, part of our argument is verbal and part is not, and we have been trading on the ambiguity.

Is that right, or is the real disagreement elsewhere — a hidden empirical question about how much ordinary human action is ballistic-then-rationalized, a difference about which cases count as the data, or bedrock disagreement about whether "could have done otherwise" means anything when the alternative-producing mechanism is deterministic? Price the options: which diagnosis you endorse, why the others look tempting, and what would settle it — a distinction I am missing, an experiment, a formal result about what sensitivity can and cannot buy you — or tell me honestly that nothing will, and why.
