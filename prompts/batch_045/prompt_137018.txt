One room, whiteboard, no projector, and I can bring a bag of candy as stakes but that's the entire budget. What I want them to feel: the pull of "there are way more observers like me in the big world, so I'm probably in it," and the equally strong pull of "the coin was fair and nothing I've learned touches how it landed" — both, before anybody says self-sampling or self-indication out loud. My half-formed idea, which I can't get to work: run a bunch of real trials, 10 or 12 of them. A pre-sealed envelope says H or T; if H, exactly one person in the room is in the trial and gets to bet on how the coin came up; if T, everyone is in it and everyone bets. Score it for candy, repeat. The thing I keep tripping over is that they can just look around the room and count how many people are betting, at which point the self-locating uncertainty evaporates and it turns into a boring inference from an observation instead of a clash of priors. Blindfolding everybody feels like a gimmick and I think it'll make them suspicious. Is there a version where the ignorance is structural rather than staged, or a way to use the leak itself — an early round where they can see, then rounds where they can't — as the teaching move instead of a bug?

The wrinkle I actually care about, and which I haven't seen anyone do, is scoring the very same trials in two currencies at once. Per-observer currency: one point to each bettor who gets it right, so a T-round with sixteen bettors is worth sixteen points to the room. Per-world currency: the round carries a fixed pot, say ten points, split among whichever bettors were right, so a T-round is worth ten no matter how many people were in it. My hunch is that in this kind of case the two principles aren't really disagreeing about the world at all, only about what the bet is denominated in — that self-indication-ish reasoning maximizes the fraction of observers who are right and self-sampling-ish reasoning maximizes something closer to per-world accuracy — and that if that's true the cosmological argument owes us an answer to a prior question about what it's even trying to be right about. I might be totally wrong and I'd rather the game be built so that it could show me I'm wrong.
