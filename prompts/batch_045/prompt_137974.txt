I work in quantitative risk — mostly Monte Carlo models of correlated losses — and most of my arguements with colleagues are really arguments about dependence: how much of our tail risk is real and how much is an artifact of the copula somebody happened to pick, whether independence is ever a neutral default or just the assumption you fall back on when you don't want to defend a correlation matrix, that sort of thing. Marginals-versus-dependence-structure is the lens I reach for by reflex, and I want to see how much of number theory's "heuristic argument" business survives being said in it.

The move I mean is the one where you treat "n is prime" as an event with probability about 1/log n, treat those events as independent — the so-called presumption of independence — count expected occurrences, and end up with a formula for how many twin primes there ought to be below x, plus the correction factors you have to bolt on for dumb local reasons like one member of a tuple being forced to be divisible by 3. Can you restate that whole practice in my vocabulary: marginals, dependence structure, copulas, tail dependence, non-stationarity, model risk? And be precise about what I suspect is the actual crux: these aren't random events at all, they're facts about the integers, and there's no uniform probability distribution on the naturals, so whatever "probability" means here it's earning its keep via density or symmetry or equidistribution rather than via anything like sampling.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about: what translates cleanly, what only translates if you fudge it, and what has no expression at all in the vocabulary I use all day. My own guess is that the correction factors look exactly like a dependence correction and that "independence" is standing in for a copula nobody can estimate — but I also half-remember that this style of model gives the wrong answer for primes in short intervals, and I'd like to know what went wrong there in my terms. And if there's something in what the number theorists are actually doing that dependence modeling just can't reach, tell me whether that's a blind spot in how I think about dependence, or a sign that the heuristic was never the clean object its name suggests.
