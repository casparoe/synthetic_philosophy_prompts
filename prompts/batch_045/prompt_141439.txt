Ok so I fell down a rabbit hole about Hacking's looping effects and now I'm not sure I get the point at all. Say some researchers propose a new diagnosis — call it "compulsive context-switching disorder", where someone can't stay on one task because they keep flipping to notifications — and suppose that a few years later someone finds a really solid neural signature for it, the sort of thing you could pick out on a scan with decent accuracy. My instinct is that this basically ends the constructivist worry: if there's a genuine biological mechanism underneath, then the diagnosis is just tracking something that was already there in nature, and all this talk about classifications creating the people they describe doesn't get off the ground. But other people keep insisting that no, handing out the label still changes how people narrate their own lives, what accomodations they can ask for, how their manager treats them, and the symptoms themselves drift as a result.

So which is it — is a psychiatric category either a real biological kind or a looping, self-fulfilling one, and can't be both? And if it somehow can be both, does finding the biomarker weaken the looping, or does it actually make it stronger?
