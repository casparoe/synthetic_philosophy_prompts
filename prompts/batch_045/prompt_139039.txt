You are a professional philosopher who specialises in axiology and population ethics, and who also writes very dry comic prose. Work in two stages, and show me both.

Stage one: a short list — bullet points I can skim in under a minute — of the structural commitments of lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism that a straight-faced application of the view would expose. I have in mind at least these four, and I would like you to add any I have missed: (i) the lexical veto itself — above some fixed threshold of intensity, no quantity of milder enjoyment can compensate a single episode of suffering; (ii) the awkward consequence that below the threshold pleasures and pains still have to be traded off against each other, which quietly presupposes a common hedonic currency that the view has given itself no way to justify; (iii) the fact that the threshold's exact location is arbitrary while doing all of the moral work; and (iv) what I have started calling the no-tunnelling rule — that the view must forbid a great many small discomforts from accumulating into one threshold-crossing discomfort, even though in every other respect it treats suffering as additive.

Stage two: the piece itself. Write a parody of a municipal Hedonic Impact Statement — the sort of numbered, footnote-heavy environmental assessment document my own field used to produce — filed by a mid-sized city that has formally adopted lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism as its decision rule for public works. The proposal under review should be small and ordinary: replacing a playground's cracked asphalt with a splash pad, say. Include a findings table, a conversion schedule between something like a Standard Pleasure Unit and a unit of pain (supplied by a consultant, with a footnote conceding the exchange rate was calibrated to the outcome the council wanted), and appendices. The document must treat its own conclusions as good news and never once name the reductio it is enacting: that the rule blocks a project producing an enormous amount of happiness plus one genuinely bad afternoon, while waving through an alternative that produces far more total misery distributed entirely in sub-threshold instalments.

Length about 700 to 900 words, deadpan throughout, no winking at the reader. I came to philosophy late from a research career in environmental chemistry, where we insisted on stating our units, so the part I most want to land is the commensurability problem rather than the more familiar worry about how grim threshold views are. My reading group contains one member who genuinely holds a version of the view, and I would like her to laugh rather than wince: the jokes should come from getting the doctrine exactly right, including the no-tunnelling rule, not from caricaturing it.
