Want a parody startup launch page, ~500 words: headline, a short press-release intro, then a five- or six-item FAQ, in the flat sincere voice of Series-B landing-page copy.

The company sells costly signals. For a flat fee it arranges for clients to pay a genuinely wasteful, verifiable cost — a pointless multi-year credential, an absurdly expensive office nobody wants, a public failure staged on their behalf — so the client ends up carrying the handicap without having the underlying quality the handicap was supposed to certify.

The joke is that they are not cheating, and they insist on this. Every cost is real and actually paid. A Fair Access clause forbids charging different clients different prices or checking whether a client has the trait the signal advertizes — which is precisely why the signal carries no information. Somewhere in the FAQ have them explain, in their own marketing logic and without breaking voice, why a signal that is merely expensive isn't the same as one whose cost is easier for high-quality signalers to bear, and why the only fix for their product would be to price by quality, which their terms forbid. Let them also note, regretfully, that they can't help with signals that are indices rather than conventional ones. Add one testimonial from a mid-tier firm that bought its way up, and one line of dynamic-pricing copy that quietly admits the whole thing is a treadmill.

Nobody should wink or explain the biology to the reader. Someone who knows signaling theory should wince in recognition; someone who doesn't should still see why the product is hollow. No real companies, no citations, and nothing in quotation marks from an actual text.
