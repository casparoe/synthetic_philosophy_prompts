I edit the satire page of a British magazine of ideas, and our readership is roughly half people who have actually read the multiple-realizability literature and half generalists who have not. I want a piece that lands for both.

Write it as a numbered set of clauses, plus one short informative annex, extracted from a fictitious Draft International Standard — give it a plausible-looking invented number and title, something like ISO/DIS 77777-2, "Realisation of mental states — Requirements for substrate-independent role conformance" — issued by an invented joint technical committee whose individual members are never named.

The document should behave exactly as a real draft standard behaves: it defines its terms, states requirements using "shall", adds deadpan NOTEs, records minority objections and closes them by procedural means, and defers whatever it cannot settle to an annex. Its constitutive move is that a mental state is specified entirely by its causal role — the conditions that typically bring it about, the relations it bears to the other states it is wired into (the belief that something has gone wrong, the desire that it stop, the memory of worse), and the behaviour it tends to produce (withdrawal, wincing, the word "ouch", a change of plan). Nothing in the specification concerns what any of this is like for the unit in question. Silicon, hydraulic, cephalopod and Martian realisers are all explicitly eligible; the standard's selling point is that the role is supposed to survive wholesale substitution of the stuff that plays it.

The comedy has to come from getting the philosophy right, not from jokes bolted on around it, and the register must stay deadpan from the first clause to the last — no winking, no explained punchlines, no character breaking cover. Without ever naming or lecturing about them, the piece should land these three worries: that a causal-role specification is either so permissive that something as crude as a control loop satisfies the pain role, or so tightly drawn that only one kind of wetware can satisfy it and the entire substrate-independence programme quietly collapses; that a unit could pass every test in the conformance suite while there is nothing it is like to be that unit; and that two units could differ in how things feel to them and remain indistinguishable by the standard's own testing regime. Give the standard two grades of certification — Grade R for role conformance and Grade Q for the felt character of the state — and let the Grade Q clause be a reserved placeholder containing no requirements, which no applicant has ever been refused.

The annex should be a worked conformance example: a nine-line self-diagnostic routine in a server, or similar, walked through the pain role element by element and found compliant, with the Committee recording that it makes no finding as to whether the routine is in pain.

Constraints: no more than 700 words in total, counting clause headings and the annex; no footnotes, references, or bibliography; no real named philosophers, works, or institutions anywhere; British English throughout, with -ise spellings and "behaviour".
