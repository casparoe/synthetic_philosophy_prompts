I'm putting together a special issue on historical objectivity and I've got two contributors who've been sniping at each other in footnotes for years, so I want to make them do an adversarial collaboration instead. Draft the joint statement they could both sign. One is a reconstructionist in the Rankean line: the historian's job is to recover the past as it actually was, and the archive is what disciplines you into doing that. The other is a narrativist in the Carr/White line: facts only become historical facts by being selected and emplotted, and the plot structure — tragedy, comedy, farce, whatever — is brought to the evidence rather than read off it.

They've agreed to argue over one fixed hypothetical, the sealed archive case: for a single event there is a closed body of documents, both sides grant every document is genuine and agree about what each one says, and no further evidence will ever arrive — yet they still produce accounts with incompatible causal structure and emphasis. I want them to say what is still in dispute in that case, and whether whatever remains is a factual dispute at all.

Then give me (a) a numbered section of claims both can sign — about what the archive does and doesn't constrain, about whether a narrative carries counterfactual commitments that evidence could in principle check, and about what "objectivity" would have to mean for either side to care about having it; and (b) a table of the residual disagreements, each row worded the way its own side would accept for its own view, not the way the other side would caricature it.

For the cruxes I'd guess at least these two: whether emplotment carries counterfactual content the archive can adjudicate, and whether slow convergence among historians with very different politics is evidence of a mind-independent past or only of a shared professional genre. For each crux, name one test, case, or argument both would accept as bearing on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts it will show. If you think part of the dispute is purely verbal — especially around "objectivity," and around whether the narrativist is making a claim about truth or about something else — say which part and why. Don't give me footnotes, bibliographies or reading lists; just the document.
