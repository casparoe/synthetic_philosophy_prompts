Hey — I'm putting together something for a dumb internal newsletter my team does (we're all backend/infra people, I have basically no philosophy background, so pls don't assume I know any of the literature).

Context as I understand it: there's this whole movement about welfare in aquaculture, shrimp especially, and the thing I can't stop thinking about is that nobody can actually measure the thing they say they care about. Like: we don't know whether a shrimp is in pain, we can't ask them, the nervous systems are tiny and weird, and there's a genuine live scientific disagreement about whether they feel anything at all. But farms still have to get certified humane, so the certifications end up using proxies — stocking density, water chemistry, time out of water, whether the animals got electrically stunned before chilling, whether the hatchery clips the eyestalks off the breeding females. (I might be mangling some of this — iirc some country legally decided decapods count as sentient, and I think eyestalk ablation is exactly the sort of thing the standards are starting to care about, but the point stands either way.)

What I want: an internal engineering document — an SLA, or a production runbook, or an incident-response doc — written by the platform team at a startup that has taken over a shrimp farm. These people genuinely and earnestly want to do the right thing. Totally deadpan, zero winking, no shrimp puns. Maybe 700–900 words, with headings and numbered sections.

The jokes have to come from the real structure of the problem, not from gags:

- They're SRE people, so they reach for the only tools they have: dashboards, SLOs, error budgets, percentiles, postmortems. Give it actual numbers — an error budget denominated in shrimp-hours, a p95 attached to something that has no business having a p95, a target like "99.4% of the cohort has an acceptable day."
- Somewhere they have to define "welfare," and the definition quietly ends up being whatever their sensors can read (dissolved oxygen, ammonia, throughput), even though everyone writing the doc agrees that the thing that matters is what the animal feels. Let that happen in the Definitions section and never comment on it.
- At least one spot where hitting the metric obviously makes things worse for the animal, and the doc logs it as a win. Don't name or explain the principle involved.
- An on-call / runbook section on what to do when the animals themselves look like they're suffering: file a ticket, page someone, note that the sensor that would settle it doesn't exist.
- Somewhere, honestly and without cynicism, the fact that the whole enterprise rests on a scientific question nobody has settled. The doc should never resolve it.

What I want at the end is a reader who has actually felt the "we are measuring the wrong thing, we know we're measuring the wrong thing, and we have to keep shipping" problem, rather than one who has been told about it. Keep it fair, too — someone who actually works on shrimp welfare should read it and laugh at us, not feel like we were mocking the idea. No real company or certification body names; make up the farm, the auditor, and the standard.
