ok so context first: about three months ago I joined a tiny team that's writing the values/rubric doc for a model that ships in like fourteen countries, most of which none of us grew up in. we're not doing anything fancy, we basically have a list of scenario types and annotators in each country rate them, and we keep discovering that our "neutral" examples are dripping with the specific moral reflexes of the countries we did grow up in. it got personal for me a couple weeks ago: my partner's family thinks putting my mother-in-law in a care home would be a moral failure full stop, and my family thinks that refusing to do it *because of what the neighbours would say* is the actual moral failure. and I genuinely can't tell whether that gap is two different values, or a factual disagreement about what old people want, or one side being run by a bias about reputation. and I notice the same three options show up over and over in our annotation arguments.

I need a theory of how to *read* cross-cultural variation in folk morality — one that tells us, for any given disagreement, whether it's (a) a difference in values, (b) a difference in non-moral factual belief, (c) a distortion (in-group loyalty, disgust, social-desirability pressure, that sort of thing), or (d) a case where one side is just wrong in the way that makes moral progress possible. you're a professional philosopher who's spent real time on moral psychology and cross-cultural stuff — please think carefully and do some unstructured brainstorming about the space of possible readings before you commit to the list, then give me the finished thing.

a few things I specifically want you to deal with:
