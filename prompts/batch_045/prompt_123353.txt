I'm writing a paper for a comparative ethics seminar on whether a confucian-flavoured role ethics can ever fault the roles it tells us to inhabit — the view that I basically just am the sum of my roles, with no standpoint outside them from which to appraise them — and I want to use Ishiguro's The Remains of the Day as the test case, since Stevens is such an exemplary butler precisely in ways that make him useless as a human being.

Here's my own patch for the standard objection. (i) A remonstrance clause: every role carries, as part of its own excellence, a standing obligation to push back when the person you hold the role toward is corrupting the practice the role serves, so speaking up is role-internal and not an appeal to some universal standpoint. (ii) A custody clause: the role holder is a custodian of the practice and owes it to those who held it before and will hold it after to hand it on intact. Together they're meant to let me say "bad butler", not just "bad person", without leaving the role.

The scenes I keep circling are the night he keeps the conference running while his father is dying upstairs, the dismissal of the two Jewish housemaids (which he now calls insignificant, and which Miss Kenton nearly resigns over), and the ending where he sets about learning to banter so he can serve Mr Farraday properly.

What I want from the reading: (1) which of my two clauses does Stevens actually violate, and at what moment — or does he satisfy both and still do the terrible thing, in which case my patch fails? (2) The worry I can't shake: the conscientiousness that makes him a great butler is the same disposition that makes him serve a Nazi-sympathising lord and let his father die, so if my clauses are excellences internal to the role, do they just have the same shape as whatever hollowed him out? (3) If the only repair role ethics can offer him is "be a better butler to a nicer man", is that an objection to the theory or just the novels pessimism — and does the scene on the pier at the end resist that reading or confirm it?

Be honest in both directions: tell me where the book refuses to be an illustration of my clauses, and where my clauses distort Stevens. No citations or reading lists needed, I want the reading itself, and please finish by saying what the novel adds to this debate that the papers don't.
