I've been mucking around on my own with this thing I stumbled into called risk-weighted expected utility — no background in any of it, so sorry if I mangle the words. The way I've got it in my head: if someone is cagey about bets, the usual move is to say the second thousand dollars matters less to them than the first, so you curve the value of money and take a plain expected value of that. The risk-weighted version says you don't have to curve money at all: you can leave money as money and put all the caution into the chances, by lining the outcomes up worst to best and using, instead of each outcome's real probability, how much the running total moves under some squashing function. Roughly. I think that's the idea.
