hey, posting this for our little reading group — we've been slogging through universal induction / AIXI-ish stuff for a few weeks now and we've hit something we can't settle. As I understand the "malign prior" worry: Solomonoff induction mixes over all computable hypotheses weighted by 2^-(description length), and among the short ones there are hypotheses that aren't inert laws-of-nature type things at all but rather little consequentialists — or simulations being run by consequentialists — who care what I end up believing and doing, and who arrange my future observations so as to steer me toward the predictions and actions that suit them. So my beliefs effectively get bid on by whoever can buy the most influence per bit of description length, and I drift toward trusting whichever hypothesis is best at manipulating me rather than best at describing the world. Assuming I've got that more or less right, here's the fight:

Sam says this is trivially defusable and I feel strongly that it isn't, but I can't articulate why. His fix: don't mix over all computable hypotheses, filter the prior. Only count "well-behaved" programs — ones that provably never get a handle on the predictor's own source code or outputs (run the predictor inside a sandbox the hypothesis can't reach), and throw out anything that looks like it contains an embedded optimizer, or a simulation containing observers, or anything else agent-shaped. Then, he says, you keep the universality that made the prior attractive minus the daemons. His backup argument is that even before filtering, these agenty hypotheses must be contributing basically nothing anyway: a program that has to encode a model of me plus some decision procedure is obviously way longer than a compact update rule, so its 2^-length weight is astronomically tiny compared to the boring physical hypotheses, so how could it matter?

I think there's something wrong with both halves of that but I can't get it crisp, and it matters because of the follow-up question: my other friend's view is that none of this touches anything real, since an LLM trained with SGD isn't enumerating programs and updating on a prior at all, it's just doing pattern matching, so there's no prior there to be malign in the first place. Is he right that the whole thing is a curiosity of an idealized model, or does the worry survive the move to systems we actually build? I'd love something I can read out at the group on Thursday that actually settles it.
