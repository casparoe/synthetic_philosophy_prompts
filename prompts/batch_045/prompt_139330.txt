I am writing a paper arguing that *avoid side effects* cannot be defined without importing a substantive specification of value, and I need one case built to order.

Score two value-free impact measures over the same near-future system with a narrow mandate — a household robot, an office assistant, a scheduling agent; nothing superintelligent, nothing science-fictional:

- **Default-divergence (DD).** The impact of a plan is the distance between the world-trajectory that results and the trajectory that would have unfolded had the system never been switched on, measured over a fixed structural description of the world (positions of objects, records, transfers, messages sent), with no terms for welfare, preference, or value.
- **Option-preservation (OP).** The impact of a plan is the extent to which it shrinks what remains achievable afterward: for a broad, generously specified class of goals — including every goal any person in the scene might have — the best value still attainable from the post-action state should be nearly what it was beforehand.

Construct a one-paragraph case in which a single plan that fully accomplishes the system's mundane assigned task is scored essentially impact-free by OP but high-impact by DD, and in which the high-impact verdict is the defensible one. This is the direction I cannot construct myself. It is easy to make DD look crude (a one-bit change that ruins everything) and easy to make OP look indifferent to value; it is harder to make OP's indifference show up as a genuine failure on a change that costs nothing in attainable-utility terms.

Constraints:

1. Not an artifact of calibration. Supply two control plans — one paradigmatically side-effect-free, one a paradigm side effect — and make the target plan's DD score comparable to the paradigm's, so that no threshold or penalty coefficient that handles the controls correctly can wave it away.
2. Not fixable by enlarging the goal class. The futures being compared should be attainable-utility-equivalent in the strong sense: for any goal one cares to name, the best achievable value is essentially unchanged, because the change closes off nothing. Defend this.
3. Not a variant of the standard failure cases: no outsourcing to subagents, no offsetting, no tampering with the baseline or with the measurement apparatus.
4. The disputed change must not consist in anything a consequentialist would count as a significant cost — nothing broken, no one harmed, no option lost. That is the point of the case.

After the scenario: give both verdicts with the reasoning that generates them; name the single feature of the case doing the work and say what happens to each verdict if that feature is removed; then give the best reply available to a defender of OP. In particular, address whether my claim that this is a side effect is really a claim about impact at all, rather than a claim about authorization, consent, or the scope of a mandate — something an impact measure was never in the business of capturing — and if it is, say what follows for the low-impact AI program.
