Suppose I need a theory of well-being whose output could be fed into a cost-effectiveness calculation: one interpersonally comparable quantity per person-year, aggregable across a population by someone allocating a fixed public budget. Before I look at candidate theories I want the specification.

Write the desiderata such a theory has to meet. For each, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable for this purpose or merely a preference of one camp. Cover at least: what the theory must say about the familiar experience-machine case, and about a variant in which the subject's actual projects are being carried out successfully in the world by an agent she authorized, while she is truthfully informed of this inside the machine — does its verdict shift, and should it; how it must treat a subject whose hedonic set-point has permanently shifted, upward or downward, given that self-report is the only instrument available at population scale; whether duration and intensity trade off on one scale, and whether the theory is permitted to return incomparability rather than equality; and what it owes us when one person's gain is another's loss.

Then flag any subset of the requirements you take to be jointly unsatisfiable — I suspect deference to a subject's own considered judgment, correction for adaptation and for misinformation, and measurability by self-report cannot all be held at once, and I want to know whether the conflict is real or an artifact of how I phrased them. Say which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidates. Keep the scorecard for hedonism, desire-satisfaction, and objective-list views to one line apiece; the criteria are the part I care about.
