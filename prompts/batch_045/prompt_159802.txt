I'm a PhD student in distributed systems, and a philosopher friend I argue with on our group's reading-group nights finally got me to read up on the block universe and the Rietdijk–Putnam argument. I have the physics of a first course in special relativity — light cones, spacelike and timelike separation, no frame-independent simultaneity, the Andromeda paradox she keeps throwing at me — but very little metaphysics, and what I do have I instinctively re-describe in my own vocabulary. I'd like you to do that re-describing properly, and I'd like the failures of the translation to be the main deliverable.

Concretely: take the Rietdijk–Putnam style argument — roughly, that relativity of simultaneity plus the premise that whatever is simultaneous with a real event is itself real forces all times to be equally real — and restate it, step by step, in the vocabulary I actually use: events and runs, Lamport's happens-before partial order and its linear extensions, concurrency as the counterpart of spacelike separation, consistent cuts and global-snapshot algorithms, vector clocks, and the way a system with total-order broadcast or a leader gets a canonical serialization that a leaderless, fully symmetric asynchronous system does not. I am fluent in all of that; you don't need to explain it to me. Give me presentism, the growing-block view, the moving-spotlight view and eternalism in those terms too (my guesses: a distinguished current cut; an append-only log; a pointer moving over a log that is already complete; and the log itself, with no distinguished position). Then the residue report. Two things in particular I want stress-tested. First: in my framework the relation "can appear together in some single consistent cut" is *not* transitive — I can have a concurrent with b, b concurrent with c, but a happens-before c — so the chain construction in the argument has no single cut it terminates in, and I suspect the argument is quietly relying on a transitivity of "co-reality" that my analogue refuses to give it. Is that a real objection or am I attacking a strawman, and does the argument actually only need the weaker claim that no cut is privileged by the physics? Second: I've been toying with a position I call cut-relational presentism — reality-for-an-observer-process = membership in that process's current consistent cut, so that "the present" is a family of cuts rather than one — and my friend says it collapses into eternalism, since every event is in some cut relative to somebody. Also: she says a presentist can simply help herself to a dynamically distinguished foliation (a comoving/cosmological one) rather than doing without a privileged slicing, which looks to me exactly like breaking symmetry with an out-of-band clock — TrueTime or GPS — instead of electing a leader. Is that move available inside the argument's own setting, special relativity with no gravity, and is it a legitimate resource or a cheat? And what about temporal *becoming*, the sense that the future is not yet and the past is over: my vocabulary has speculative execution, uncommitted writes, and committed prefixes, but I don't think it has anything that is just "not yet real" rather than "not yet decided" — tell me whether that gap is my framework's blind spot or the metaphysicians'.

Two constraints on how you answer. Keep the translation honest: don't smuggle the conclusion in by picking a data structure in which every event is stored forever and then observing that of course all events are "there" — that is the block universe in costume, not an argument for it, and I'll notice. Flag explicitly, at each step, whether you're translating or arguing, and mark which parts of your reconstruction are inventions of the analogy rather than features of the original debate. And separate three things clearly: what is settled physics (the causal structure, the relativity of simultaneity, the non-existence of a frame-independent simultaneity relation); what is genuinely contested among people who work on the philosophy of time — including whether the argument is sound, whether presentism can be made compatible with special relativity and at what cost, and whether the dispute ever touches anything empirical; and what you yourself are inclined to think, with your confidence and your reasons. I would rather be told "experts are split and here is the crux" than be handed a confident verdict that half the field would reject. Aim for something in the 1,200-word range; prose with headers is fine, bullet-pointed residue lists are fine, and don't pad.
