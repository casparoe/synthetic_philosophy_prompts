Hi — hoping you can help me put something together that my committee can actually use.

I'm the governance officer (clerk, minute-taker, and de facto rules nerd) for a professional institute of about 9,000 members. Our rulebook is amended at the AGM and at emergency general meetings — 30 or 40 substantive motions a year, plus the occasional one shoehorned in between. My job is to rule, sometimes in advance of the vote and sometimes the morning after, on whether a motion that carried actually changed the rules, or whether it's void / void in part / did something different from what the proposer thought. I then give the Rules Committee three or four paragraphs of written reasons they sign off on. Two of my rulings have ended up in front of a judge, so they can't be hand-wavy; but the committee is five lay trustees who will not sit through a lecture on self-reference, and I usually have about two days from motion to ruling.

The bit we keep crashing into is that the rules govern their own amendment, and the drafting is a mess. Roughly:

- Rule A1: rules may be amended by a two-thirds majority at a general meeting; A1 itself may be amended only by a three-quarters majority.
- Rule A2: the rules in force are those printed in the current Handbook, as updated by the Secretary. The Handbook is reprinted every other year, so what members vote against and what's on the shelf are often two different texts.
- Rule A3: if part of an amendment is void, the rest stands.
- Rule A4: no amendment may remove an accrued right of a member. Nobody knows what accrued means here; we've been reading it as a right someone has already started to exercise.
- Rule A5: defines who counts as a voting member.

So: give me a decision procedure. Ordered questions, what counts as an answer to each one, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to the committee because it's a values call dressed up as a rule question. It has to be runnable in an afternoon with the rulebook and the minutes, and it has to handle self-referential amendments specifically — an amendment that changes the threshold it has to clear, a rule that tries to lock itself against later amendment, a motion that repeals the amending power and hands it somewhere else. Please don't just tell me these are paradoxical; I still have to issue a ruling on Monday.

Run it on these:

1. A motion to lower the general amendment threshold in A1 from two-thirds to a simple majority. It got 68%. Did it carry, and if so what threshold applies to next year's motions?
2. In 2022 a motion added to Rule 14 (the subscription cap) the sentence "Rule 14 may not be amended or repealed." Passed 71%. In 2024 a motion to repeal Rule 14 outright passed 69%. What's in force now — and was the 2022 lock itself valid, given A1?
3. A single motion, passed 78%, repealing A1 and replacing it with "the Board may amend these Rules by resolution." Do we still have a rulebook, and can a later board resolution be challenged?

And two hard cases the procedure has to say something about: (a) the 2019 Members' Charter — a block of protections including a right to appeal expulsion, inserted by a 71% vote, with a preamble saying these are fundamental and shall not be amended; this year a 70% motion deleted the appeal right. My instinct, and the members', is that the preamble protects it. I suspect the procedure will tell me otherwise. (b) A motion to exclude retired members from voting, which passed 67% counting the retired members who voted and 61% excluding them — the amending majority depends on the content of the amendment.

Please flag where the procedure can't settle things: rules we've all been relying on for years that were probably never validly adopted, and the printed-Handbook lag under A2. And tell me what it can't do at all. No need for citations or further reading — I want the tests and the reasons, not a reading list.
