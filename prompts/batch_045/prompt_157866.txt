I'm in the final year of a doctorate on the Synoptic tradition, and I keep encountering the same methodological shape in my own work without having a word for it. I'd be grateful for help identifying it — or, if it has no established name, for a label and a definition tight enough to be usable in a footnote.

The situation. The familiar toolkit for getting behind the tradition — double dissimilarity, embarrassment, multiple attestation, coherence, Semitic linguistic features, Palestinian environmental detail — functions as a filter: it is applied unit by unit to individual sayings or pericopae, and whatever passes is treated as material one may build on. My worry is not the usual one about the criteria being individually weak or jointly circular, and it is not the base-rate worry either (that with a low prior on authenticity most 'authenticated' units will be false positives however good the criteria are). Those I have seen coming. My worry concerns the character of the set that survives.

Most of the criteria work by detecting the fingerprints of the transmitting tradition and discarding whatever bears them. But those fingerprints are just the features that make a unit typical of early Christian transmission: continuity with Jewish practice, usefulness to the community, consonance with what the movement came to believe. The filter is therefore not neutral with respect to what it selects. It selects, more efficiently the more tightly it is applied, for the units the tradition had no use for — and the assemblage that results is skewed towards the eccentric end of whatever the historical figure was actually like. Multiple attestation is a partial exception, since it certifies on the basis of source independence rather than content, though even there the independence of the attesting sources is itself contested. Two consequences I keep circling back to:

(i) Adding authenticated units to the pile does not converge on a representative picture. It converges on a distillation of the atypical — much as reconstructing an author's prose style from only those passages that stylometry flags as unmistakably theirs yields a style more idiosyncratic than anything the author wrote.

(ii) The better the filter works, the harder it becomes to place what remains. Contextualising a unit requires continuity with something we independently know about, and continuity is precisely what the filter strips away. The ideal output of the procedure — a figure continuous with nothing — is also the figure about whom least can be said.

The obvious candidates do not seem to fit exactly. Selection bias and survivorship bias are close, but in the standard cases the sample is unrepresentative because of an accident of sampling, whereas here the unrepresentativeness is generated by the content of the selection rule and worsens as the rule improves. Conditioning on a common effect is closer in structure, but seems to predict spurious correlations among the surviving features rather than a drift towards the eccentric end. Goodhart-style worries about a measure becoming a target do not apply, since nobody is optimising against the criteria. And the hermeneutical circle picks out the circularity, not the skew.

So: does this failure mode have an established name — in historical method, in the philosophy of the historical sciences, or elsewhere, since I gather something structurally similar turns up in archaeological surface survey and in observational cosmology? If it does, I want more than the word: what exactly the concept covers, and where the case I have described sits relative to it. If it does not, I should like a name and a definition: what the phenomenon is, what the nearest named thing is and how this differs from it, and what would count as a look-alike that is not a case of it — in particular, whether the phenomenon requires the selection rule to be content-directed in the way I have described, or whether it generalises to any filter whose output is a residue. Please keep to no more than 700 words, and no bibliography: I am after the carving, not the label.
