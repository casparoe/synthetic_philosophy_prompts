I'm a backend engineer with no philosophy background, and Ive half-convinced myself of a systems-flavoured spin on Hofstadter's idea that an "I" isn't stuck inside one skull: you're a replicated, eventually-consistent structure spread over other people's heads, and you survive death to the degree those copies keep doing work. My own addition: a copy counts as you if it causally shapes what its host does, not if it's accurate — so my mate's hazy, partly wrong memory of me that still changes how he acts is a genuine bit of me, and the more such copies there are, and the more behavioural difference they make, the more of me there is.

What would count against that? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and seperate them from objections I can just wave off. The ones nagging at me: if "counts as you" just means "makes a behavioural difference", haven't I collapsed into something boring where every influence on anyone is a bit of them, including influences from people who never met them? Replica drift — my sister's version of our dad is now confidently wrong about loads of things; is that still him, and what would have to be true for drift to kill a copy? Forks, where two people carry sharply incompatible versions of me and both organise their lives around them: is that just the fission problem in a distributed-systems costume, teeth and all? And is the "more of me there is" bit so vague that nothing could ever contradict it — and if so, is that a bug or just what a metaphysical view looks like?

Please be clear which of these someone who already holds the view would concede as counting against it rather than dismiss, and tell me which single one you'd find most persuasive and why.
