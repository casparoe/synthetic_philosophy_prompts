I'm a journalist working on a piece about the split between Europe and the US over eggs and wombs, and I could use help with the philosophy underneath, because the bans keep getting defended with slogans that don't seem to match what they actually ban.

The pattern, as I understand it: across most of Europe you can donate eggs or carry a pregnancy for someone else for free, and you can be reimbursed for travel, lost wages, childcare, pain and risk — but you can't be paid for the eggs or for the gestational work itself, even though the clinic, the doctor, the agency and the lawyer all get paid. In the US both are paid, and egg compensation scales with donor attributes: education, height, looks, test scores.

So what does that "expenses yes, the thing itself no" line presuppose about what's wrong with the American market? Commodification — some things shouldn't have a price at all? Exploitation of poorer women? The thought that an offer can itself compromise consent? Something about the child, or about gestation being labor of a kind that can't be alienated? Reconstruct the best version of each, tell me which one the bans strictly presuppose and which merely fit, and where the practice is underdetermined — where several quite different theories would license the same ban.

Two things I keep getting stuck on. First, is the burden/product distinction stable, or is paying generously for time, discomfort and risk just paying for the egg and the pregnancy under another name? Second — the bit nobody I interview wants to touch — paying more for a donor's traits looks less like buying tissue than like a market shaping which children get made, a selection worry rather than a commodification one; and a ban on payment dosen't obviously answer it, since unpaid donation selects on traits too. Does that reframe what the bans are even for?

Last thing: what would have to change on the ground if the operative worry were one of the others — say if it's really price and bargaining power rather than commodification? I'm guessing you'd end up with regulated fees, caps or floors and actual labor protections instead of criminal bans, but I want to know whether that follows or whether I'm smuggling in my own politics.
