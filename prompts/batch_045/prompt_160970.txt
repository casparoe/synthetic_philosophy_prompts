I'm on the editorial board of my school's civics magazine, and I'd like a satirical piece — about 550 words — on subsidiarity. Please write it as an official notice from a school district's Office of Jurisdictional Review, in deadpan bureaucratic register, headed something like "Re: Delegation of Dress-Code Authority," announcing that under the district's new subsidiarity policy every decision must be handled by the lowest level capable of handling it, and tracing what happens as the hoodie question travels from the state department of education down through the district, the principal, the homeroom teacher, and finally to the individual student's closet.

Two things I care about. First, get the actual structure of the doctrine right so the comedy comes from inside it: authority flows downward, the one power that never flows downward is the power to certify which level is competent, and the higher level's remaining role is to "assist" — which is how the district ends up auditing a fourteen-year-old's wardrobe twice a semester. Please invent one mechanism to make this bite, for instance a Certificate of Demonstrated Competence that any body must earn before it is permitted to decide anything, graded by the body immediately above it, and follow it until the rule eats itself: the student can only demonstrate that she is competent to choose her own hoodie by choosing a hoodie, which she may not do until she is certified. The notice is issued jointly by the state and the district, and each insists it has no authority in the matter and is merely helping, so that by the end nobody is answerable for the policy.

Second, keep it fair and legible: no named theorists, no citations, no character stating the moral aloud (nobody says "so the centre keeps everything"), and a reader who has never met the word "subsidiarity" should still follow the farce. The goal is a reductio someone who knows the federalism literature would recognise as fair rather than a straw man.
