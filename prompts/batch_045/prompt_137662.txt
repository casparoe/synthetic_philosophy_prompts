I am a novelist. In the book I am finishing, two characters are asked by a fictional oversight body to produce, and both sign, a single one-page statement of where they stand after a year of argument with each other. One of them holds that unilateral action in the conditions the unilateralist's curse describes calls for an additional norm of deference to the group; the other holds that the curse is simply a fact about the case to be priced into an agent's own expected-value calculation and supports no special norm at all, only better calibration. I would like you to write that page for them.

What I need is the document itself, not commentary on it. First, a numbered list of claims both of them would sign: about the shape of the case the argument reaches in the first place (an act that is beneficial if few perform it and harmful if many do, agents whose judgments are imperfectly correlated, and no corrective feedback to the one who acts alone); about which actions the argument does and does not touch; and about what any norm proposed here would have to cost in order to be worth adopting. Then a table of what they still disagree about, each row giving the first character's position in wording she would accept for herself and the second's in wording he would accept for himself, with a column marking whether that disagreement is substantive or only about words.

Then the cruxes, and for each the study, historical episode, or simulation both would accept in advance as genuinely bearing on it, together with what each character predicts it will show. One crux I want included is my own: suppose the number of potential actors is not fixed but grows as the risk itself comes to be discussed, so that a norm of open deliberation and a norm of deference pull against one another. Please do not give my characters a consensus they would not actually reach; I would rather have the residual disagreement stated exactly than smoothed over.
