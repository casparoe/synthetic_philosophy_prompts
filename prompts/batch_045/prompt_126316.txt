I do a science-and-ideas column, and I've been asked to walk a room of editors through Scanlon-style contractualism in the only vocabulary that survives a news meeting — so translate it, don't explain it to me as philosophy. Target vocabulary: news values (impact, reach, novelty, proximity), the anecdotal lede and the nut graf, sourcing and clearance, "who's the story about", the identifiable victim effect, proportionality of column inches, the correction standard. Assume I'm fluent in all of that; my grasp of the philosphy is a couple explainers deep, so don't assume I've read the book and don't hedge for my sake.

The bit I actually need carried over is the aggregation problem: why a contractualist won't let an enormous number of very small complaints outweigh one person's serious complaint, and why that refusal isn't just an arithmetic mistake. Include the part about there being no impersonal standpoint in which the small complaints pool into one big one — each is a separate complaint that has to be answerable to the person who has it.

Here's the bridge I've half-invented, and I want you to stress-test it rather than flatter it. Call it the clearance rule: before any claim goes in the piece you have to be able to answer, in one sentence, the objection of whoever ends up worst off under that claim, and you are never allowed to add complaints together across people who can't be named in the story. Run the translation through that rule as your pairwise test, and tell me where the rule quietly smuggles in something the contractualist didn't say, or where it breaks in a way that shows contractualism isn't really doing what I think it's doing.

Then the residue report: what carries over cleanly, what only survives with distortion, and what has no expression at all in newsroom terms. Two things I want you to be blunt about. First, our impact heuristic is basically reach times severity, which is aggregative all the way down — so does any of this survive contact with how space actually gets assigned, or is the translation only possible for a desk that doesn't exist? Second, is "representative anecdote" doing the same work as the contractualist's individual standpoint, or is that a pun on the word individual? And whether each gap is newsroom vocabulary being too blunt or the philosophy being less clear than it sounds.

After the prose, give me a table, one row per claim you translated plus one row for my clearance rule, with these columns: claim_stated_straight (the claim in plain non-technical words), newsroom_rendering (the same claim in my vocabulary), verdict (exactly one of: clean, distorted, unsayable), and diagnosis (whether the gap is my vocabulary's fault or the original claim's).
