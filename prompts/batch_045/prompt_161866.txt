I spent my career in experimental geophysics and have, since retiring, been sitting in on a graduate seminar in philosophy. This term the topic is the norm of assertion, and I have a question about a pattern I keep noticing that none of the views we have read seems to capture.

Three instances from my old department. First, a postdoc who had read only the abstract of a paper announced its central finding at a group meeting; a senior colleague said afterwards that she was not in a position to say that. The finding was true, the paper was sound, and she believed it. Second, a technician who had run an assay correctly reported its result and was criticised for asserting it, because he could not explain what the assay measured; the result was true and his evidence for it was excellent. Third, a colleague repeated a well-established result from a neighbouring field and was criticised until he named his source, at which point the criticism stopped, although nothing about his evidence had changed.

What interests me is that the criticism survives every standard fix. The proposition is true; the speaker believes it; the speaker's evidence looks good enough for knowledge, at least where the source is a reliable one; the audience is not misled; nothing of practical importance hangs on it. The complaint is not that anything went wrong but that the speaker cannot answer for what was said — cannot defend it, cannot say what would count against it, cannot draw out its consequences — and so is not the sort of person who may say it here.

Does this have a name? I am not after a label as such. I want to know whether it is a genuine norm of assertion, distinct from the knowledge, truth and belief norms we have been discussing, or whether it is better understood as something else: a norm of inquiry, a matter of intellectual character, or a fact about who counts as an authority for whom. I would also like its edges. Why does naming a source discharge it? Does it admit of degrees? Why does it seem to vary with the audience — a room of specialists does not seem to demand it in the way a journalist or a student does? And what should one say about a case in which the speaker is a device that emits true sentences it cannot defend at all?
