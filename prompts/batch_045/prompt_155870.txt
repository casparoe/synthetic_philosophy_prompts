My partner and I are stuck on whether to have a kid, and I've gotten myself into an argument I can't finish.

I'm the hesitant one, and it's not about us — I think we'd be fine parents. It's that every child is a gamble: real odds of serious illness, or a hard depressed life, and even a good life has a lot of pain in it. It feels strange that we'd be the ones rolling those dice for someone who never asked to be here. She says: fine, but ask any actual person whether they're glad they exist and nearly everyone says yes.

Then I ran into this idea online that finally felt like it captured my worry: the two directions aren't symmetric. A kid who'd suffer is a reason not to have them; a kid who'd be happy isn't a reason to have them. Suffering is the thing that counts, and creating happiness doesn't fix or balance anything.

But here's where I lose it. Say we do have a kid and we get to pick which kid — IVF, a couple of embryos, one would clearly have an easier life than the other. Or even just: wait a year and it's a different kid with a slightly better shot. Choosing the better-off one feels like something we'd owe that kid, not optional charity. But if a happy kid isn't a gain to anybody, who exactly do we owe it to? And isn't picking between possible kids basically the same kind of choice as whether to make one at all? The idea that made sense to me ten minutes ago seems to fall apart the moment it has to say anything about which kid, and I can't tell if that kills the idea or if I've stretched it wrong.

What would someone who actually holds the asymetry say here?
