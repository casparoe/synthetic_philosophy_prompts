So I got into ant keeping a few months ago — two colonys on my desk now — and my roomate and I have this running argument about whether a colony is *anybody*, or just a pile of insects doing their thing. I'm running one session of a very casual discussion group on Hofstadter's ant-colony dialogue (the Aunt Hillary bit: dumb individual ants, little clusters of ants acting as 'signals', signals making up teams, the colony writing with its trails), and I want something better than a list of -isms.

What I'd like is a dichotomous identification key, the sort of thing you use to identify a plant: 8 to 12 numbered couplets, one page, for people who've never done philosophy. You feed in a candidate system and the key spits out a verdict about where, if anywhere, there's a mind. Every couplet has to be a question someone can answer without knowing any of the labels — so not 'is the whole more than the sum of its parts?', but something like: no single ant carries the information about where the food is, but the trail network as a whole does — does the colony *know* where the food is, or is that just a pattern?

Cases it has to sort: an ordinary colony; one whose queen has just died but which carries on for months; a colony cut into two equal halves that never meet again; one where the ants are swapped out one at a time for tiny robots running the same local rules; a foraging team that behaves like a single agent; and, for contrast, a corporation and a human brain.

Leaves I want represented: mind only in the ants; only in the colony; nested in both, at different levels; the colony is a subject only in an as-if or instrumental sense; and the verdict-is-indeterminate option. Hard constraints: no couplet may turn on how many units there are, or on any threshold of behavioural sophistication — that just collapses the key into a behaviourism test, and I'd notice. Also flag any two leaves your key genuinely can't separate, and name a couple of answer-combinations nobody actually occupies, with one line on whether that's because they're incoherent, unattractive, or just unclaimed. Finish by telling me which single couplet is doing most of the work, and what happens to the key if you delete it.
