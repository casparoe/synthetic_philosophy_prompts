Facts as I have them, so nothing here depends on you looking anything up — take them as given unless you're confident I'm wrong, and don't spend your answer fact-checking exact figures:

- The cascade hypothesis as stated in the early 90s puts amyloid beta accumulation as the initiating cause, with tau tangles and inflammation downstream.
- Roughly three decades of amyloid-lowering drugs either failed outright or failed on cognition while clearing plaque.
- Aducanumab got accelerated approval in 2021 on the strength of amyloid reduction, over an advisory committee that said the evidence didn't support it; several advisers resigned; the maker dropped it in 2024.
- Lecanemab got accelerated approval in 2023 and full approval later that year off CLARITY-AD, around 1800 patients, primary endpoint CDR-SB at 18 months, a between-group gap of well under a point on an 18-point scale, with amyloid-related imaging abnormalities in a large fraction of treated patients. Donanemab approved 2024, similar shape.
- Recent meta-analyses and a Cochrane review put the clinical benefit at small or nil.
- And the genetics went the programme's way: rare early-onset mutations that push amyloid processing toward the aggregating form cause familial disease, and rarer amyloid-lowering variants look protective.

What I want you to actually do:

Map it. Is the hard core one claim or a nest of them, and what exactly is the negative heuristic protecting here? Then walk the belt: which adjustments are genuinely independently testable and which are textbook ad hoc moves — oligomers versus plaque, stage of disease, ApoE4 subgroups, epitope and dose, biomarker-confirmed enrolment, which endpoint counts as primary.

Then the bit I think is genuinely nasty and that I haven't seen handled: amyloid PET and CSF amyloid let the field redefine Alzheimer's in biomarker terms, so that patients in trials are by construction amyloid-positive and the claim that Alzheimer's involves amyloid drifts from an empirical finding toward a condition of membership in the category. Is that a triumph — new technology, corroborated novel fact, whole diagnostic apparatus rebuilt — or is it the programme quietly redrawing its domain so the core becomes hard to contradict? Does Lakatos's framework, which protects the core by methodological decision and polices only the belt, have any resources at all for criticising a redefinition of the kind rather than a tinker with an auxiliary?

Then run the novelty condition properly, and notice what's unusual here. Normally novelty is reconstructed after the fact by whoever writes the history, which is the standard complaint — retrospective novelty is whatever the historian says it is. In this case we have prospectively registered endpoints, so we can check without hindsight whether a fact was predicted in advance. Do that check for the recent trials. And then say whether the fact that the check is available makes MSRP look better here or worse: is this the rare case where the demanding reading of novelty bites, and the programme fails it?

Then argue about materiality, which I think is the real problem. A programme that stopped missing clinical endpoints entirely and started hitting them by a clinically disputed whisker counts, as far as I can tell, as a progressive problem shift: content grew, a novel fact was corroborated, there is no magnitude threshold in the account and Lakatos famously refuses to hand down verdicts, only appraisals, with no time limit on a degenerating programme recovering. So: is MSRP's silence on how much is enough a defect exposed by this case, or is it the whole point of a methodology that refuses to tell anyone when to stop? If you think it's a defect, what is the minimum thing you'd add to fix it — and what does adding it cost the framework?
