I want a case, designed to order, in which Gricean conversational implicature and Gricean speaker meaning come apart in the direction that is hard to construct.

The target: a single sincere speaker utters one short sentence. A competent hearer, reasoning exactly the way Grice's account says hearers reason, derives a conversational implicature that passes the usual diagnostics — calculable from the assumption that the speaker is observing the Cooperative Principle and the maxims, cancellable without contradiction, not detachable by rewording, not conventional. Yet the speaker does not mean that content, does not intend the hearer to reach it, and would be right to say so under pressure. She must nonetheless mean something in the full Gricean sense — intending to produce a belief in the hearer by way of the hearer's recognition of that very intention — and what she means must be incompatible with what the utterance implicates.

Constraints: one paragraph; an ordinary office, no courtrooms or classrooms; one speaker, one hearer, one utterance; no lying, irony, metaphor, hyperbole or other figure; no private code, no prior agreement, no document or script authored by a third party; the speaker's false background belief must be one a careful person in her position would hold, so the case cannot be defused by saying she was negligent or should simply have been more explicit; and the hearer must not take her to be evasive — he must hear the utterance as perfectly cooperative.

After the scenario, do three things. First, run both derivations explicitly: show that the clauses of Grice's analysis of speaker meaning are each satisfied for the content she means, and show the implicature's derivation, naming which maxim does the work. Second, test the implicature against the standard diagnostics and tell me which of them, if any, fail to separate genuine implicature here from speaker-meant content. Third, give me the one-variable variant: hold everything fixed except that the speaker's informational advantage becomes mutually known to both parties, and show what the case collapses into when that feature is removed.

Finally, I want the honest verdict on the three replies I expect: the Gricean who says the case only shows that implicates must be indexed to utterances rather than speakers; the relevance theorist who says the hearer's conclusion is a contextual implication and so not an implicature in Grice's sense at all; and the neo-Gricean who says a cooperative speaker with a false background belief is still flouting Quantity. What would I have to add to the case to block each? I am building an argument that conversational implicature should be located in hearer-side inference from common ground rather than in speaker meaning, so tell me whether a case of this shape can bear that weight or whether it only shows that Grice's definition needs a modest repair.
