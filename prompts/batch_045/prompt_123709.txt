Hi — I'm a third-year PhD student, working on reasons and moral psychology, and I've got to put in a chapter plan for my department's work-in-progress session in a couple of weeks. It's meant to be a one-term chapter, 8–10k words, and what I need from you is the plan document itself, not the chapter.

The half-formed idea I keep going round on: the internalism/externalism debate about reasons is almost entirely run on cases of action. The internalist story — a reason for you has to be able to get some grip on you, there has to be a route of some kind from what you already care about to the thing you supposedly have reason to do — works off the assumption that the output of the relevant bit of practical thought is something you *do*, so it makes sense to ask whether deliberation could have got you there. But a huge amount of what we criticise each other for isn't action at all: you have reason not to be quite so afraid, you had reason to be angrier than you were, you have no reason to still be grieving about that. Emotions aren't produced by deliberation in the way actions are, so the 'route' has no obvious home. My suspicion is that the emotional cases split the internalist's options in a way the action cases don't: either she says something fairly wild (you only have reason to grieve if you could somehow reason your way into grief), or she quietly becomes an externalist and reads reasons-for-emotion as fittingness straight off, and either way both sides get forced to say what the grip requirement was ever *for*. I also want to know whether the thing that saves internalism in the action cases — idealising the route until it's nearly empty — is what kills it here, because an idealised route into an emotion doesn't look like anything at all.

Can you turn that into a proper proposal? Specifically: the question stated tightly enough that it has a determinate answer; why the existing positions, as they're standardly set up, leave it open rather than settle it by implication; the approach, i.e. what I'd actually do week by week over a term and why that approach rather than the obvious alternativse (I assume someone will say just run it on reasons for belief instead, or just do fittingness and be done with it — tell me why not, or tell me if they're right); what the possible conclusions are and what sort of consideration would decide between them; and an honest bit on what would make this too big for 8–10k, and what would count as the chapter failing even if it goes fine.

One objection I want handled head-on rather than footnoted: a supervisor-type will say that reason-claims about emotions are really just claims about aptness or fittingness, so my test case smuggles in externalism from the start and proves nothing. Say how the plan deals with that.
