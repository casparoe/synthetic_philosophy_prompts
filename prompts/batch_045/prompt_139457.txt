I've been reading a lot of amateur history arguments lately — who wrote a set of letters, why a company's records have a two-year gap in them, that sort of thing — and the same shape keeps turning up, and I don't have a word for it.

First version: everyone agrees the candidates are A, B and C. Nobody ends up believing C, nobody thinks C is even especially plausible, but once C is put on the table the group switches from B to A. C doesn't win, it just changes who wins, the way a spoiler candidate does in an election. The reason is always that C is close to B in exactly the respect where B was beating A, so A's advantage becomes the one that decides it.

Second version: three candidates and it honestly looks like A is better than B, B better than C, and C better than A — because each pairwise comparison gets fought on a different virtue. A fits the surviving documents better; B needs fewer assumptions; C sits better with what's known about the period. Ask people to rank all three at once and they stall, and it isn't idleness: they seem to think that a bit more simplicity against a bit worse fit just isn't something anyone can price.

I've read a bit about underdetermination and about evidence being theory-laden, and neither is what I mean — nobody here doubts there's a fact of the matter or that the evidence is shared. The trouble is with the ranking itself. So: does this have a name? The way which explanation counts as best depends on what else is on the menu, and maybe doesn't settle down into an ordering at all?

I keep thinking of social choice — spoiler effects, majority cycles — so if it's near that, tell me how near: is picking the best explanation really the same kind of operation as aggregating preferences, or does the analogy break, and where? I don't just want a label; I want to know exactly what the thing is, where its edges are, what it isn't, and whether it's a problem for inference to the best explanation in particular or for anything that picks a winner by balancing several virtues at once. If there genuinely isn't a name, coin one and define it carefully enough that I can tell it apart from its neighbours. Also: if the fix is to cash out best explanation as a single number like posterior probability, the menu-dependence seems to disappear — is that a solution or a change of subject? Please answer in British English.
