I work mainly in enactive and ecological psychology, with a second area in developmental systems theory: I am fluent in the vocabulary of affordances, ecological information, skilled coping, developmental resources and scaffolding, niche construction and ecological inheritance, and I read the cognitive science of religion only from the outside. I would like you to restate, as faithfully as you can, the main naturalistic accounts of religion as a human phenomenon — the by-product and cultural-epidemiological family (agency detection, minimally counterintuitive representations, content and transmission biases), the costly-signalling and credibility-enhancing-display family, and the moralizing-gods-and-group-benefits family — in my vocabulary rather than in representational and computational terms.

Two stipulations, to keep the translation honest. First, sort each account according to which of three explananda it actually targets: a population-level recurrency, an individual-level cognitive profile, or a historically extended tradition with institutions and practices; and say where my vocabulary has no resources for one of the three. Second, test each translation against the case of the fully practicing agent who sincerely disavows the beliefs of the tradition she is expert in — a case my vocabulary handles easily and which, I suspect, the representational accounts handle badly.

Then the residue report: which claims survive the translation unchanged, which survive only if I quietly reintroduce representations, content, or proposition-shaped belief, and which cannot be stated in my terms at all. And give me your view on what the residue shows: whether my framework simply lacks the resources to state a genuine explanatory problem, or whether the debate is partly held together by a notion of belief that does less work than its users suppose. Please do not present my framework's assumptions as though they were neutral translation.
