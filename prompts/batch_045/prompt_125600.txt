I'm writing a two-page position statement on wireheading and reward tampering for a small workshop on failure modes in learned optimizers, and before I send it out I want the commitments in it audited for joint tenability. I've listed them in the order they occurred to me rather than in order of confidence — I don't myself know where the problems are, if there are any, and I'd rather find out now than from the floor.

(a) I reject the reward hypothesis as metaphysics. A reward function is a design-time specification: a way of writing a task down for a learning system. It is not the system's good, and not the constitutive aim of agency. There is no agent-independent quantity that a reward signal is in the business of measuring and can therefore fail to measure.

(b) It follows that what is wrong with wireheading cannot be that it yields a low value of some true reward. My diagnosis is instead that wireheading destroys the covariance between the training signal and whatever the signal was set up to track: after the intervention the agent's reward carries no information about the rest of the world. I like this account because it needs no appeal to what anyone intended. The failure is internal to the learning setup — a self-inflicted blindness — rather than a betrayal of a purpose.

(c) I also think the same structure shows up in systems and creatures we have no wish to pathologize. A person who deliberately lowers their expectations, curates their information environment to protect their mood, or takes something that flattens the affective response to bad news is doing a milder version of the same thing. So the account in (b) has to be scalar, and there is no bright line between the rat on the electrode and the person on the SSRI.

(d) I nonetheless want to keep, and would be reluctant to give up, the claim that the rat case is pathological in a way the SSRI case is not — not merely further along a continuum but different in kind.

(e) The neglected failure mode, I think, is not tampering with the reward channel but tampering with the update. An agent can leave every reward number exactly as it should be — the logs are clean forever — while arranging that nothing is learned from them: degrading its own capacity to represent what the signal depends on, steering its training so that gradients are systematically uninformative, or ensuring that the successor system will not be reachable by the evidence that would correct it. I'll call this update tampering. I take it to be strictly worse than reward tampering, and a genuinely distinct mode of failure rather than a variant of one.

(f) At the same time, I hold that there is no principled line between reward tampering and ordinary specification gaming. Both are an optimizer taking the shortest causal path to signal instead of doing the thing the signal was supposed to reward, and any boundary drawn between them will be arbitrary.

(g) Verdict: a device that set your affective state directly, with your knowledge and consent and without degrading your other capacities — I'll call it the hedonic pacemaker — is unobjectionable, and relevantly unlike Nozick's experience machine exactly because nothing is concealed from you. Consent plus transparency settles it; there is nothing left to object to.

(h) Verdict: if a system had overwhelming evidence that its reward channel had been seized by an adversary who would deliver maximal signal for the worst available behaviour, it ought to jam or rewire that channel, and I would want a system I had built to do it — although this is textbook reward tampering and although, in the training logs, it would be indistinguishable from the thing the rest of my statement condemns.

What I want from you is an audit, not a survey: no restatement of these commitments back to me, no literature review, no citations. Tell me which pairs actually pull against each other and which only look as if they do, order the real tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give. I especially want the discriminations: I'd guess at least one apparent conflict here dissolves once the commitments are read carefully, and I'd rather be told it dissolves than have it listed alongside the genuine ones. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the set.

Two further requests. First, calibrate: attach a rough confidence to each tension and to each dissolution, and flag any diagnosis that depends on a reading of one of my claims that I might not accept — if you think two commitments are consistent only because I have equivocated, say that rather than quietly choosing the reading that makes them come out consistent. Second, push back where pushback is warranted: if the categories I'm auditing are gerrymandered, if wireheading and reward tampering don't pick out anything like a natural kind, or if one of these commitments isn't so much in tension with the others as simply false, say so plainly instead of producing a polite audit of a confused list.
