What I want next week's session to make land is evidential cooperation in a large world: why, and under precisely what conditions, an evidential agent's own act can be a reason to cooperate with thousands of strangers over whom it has no causal influence whatsoever, and how the strength of that reason depends on the size of the population, on what the agent gets to observe, and on whether the population's composition is known. By a large world I mean only this: the agent is one of many similar agents, and the joint distribution over all of their acts is something that has to be specified rather than something the decision problem hands you.

The candidate case I have been working with, which you should feel free to trim or replace, is what I call the Nine Thousand Silos. Nine thousand agents, each sealed alone, one-shot, no communication, no commitment devices, no repetition, no reputation, no shared signal anyone observes. Each may press or not press. Pressing costs the presser one unit and adds three units to the payoff of every other agent, so the benefit is non-rival and undiluted: my payoff is three times the number of other pressers, minus one if I press. Two dials. The first is visibility: how many other agents' choices are revealed to me before I decide, which I want to be able to set at zero, at ten, and at eight thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine. The second is provenance: either the Registrar has truly announced that exactly three thousand of the nine thousand will press, or nothing about the composition is announced and the agents are exchangeable, their common tendency being one of two values, 0.2 or 0.8, with prior probability one half on each.

Define the evidential leverage of pressing as the difference between the expected number of other pressers conditional on my pressing and the expected number of other pressers conditional on my not pressing; the agent presses exactly when three times the leverage exceeds one. My own numbers: under the unannounced, exchangeable version with no observations, leverage is about 0.36 times eight thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine, which is absurdly large; under the Registrar's announcement it is minus one, so the evidential reason does not merely vanish but reverses; and as the visibility dial is turned up, leverage collapses towards one regardless of how large the population is, because with a uniform-ish prior it comes out at roughly the population size divided by the number of observed acts plus a small constant. Correct me if any of that is wrong.
