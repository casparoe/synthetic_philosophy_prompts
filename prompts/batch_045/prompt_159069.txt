I'm a science-and-ideas columnist, and I have a draft sitting with my editor in which I basically take the line that weakness of will isn't a real, separate phenomenon — that what people have meant by akrasia is just hyperbolic discounting plus a bad story about there being one continuous self whose judgment got overruled. The version I'm defending is eliminativist in a fairly specific way: at any moment you do what you most prefer at that moment, there's no moment at which you act against your own all-things-considered judgment, and the felt experience of resisting-and-losing is just what it's like to have a steep curve between now and later. So the explanatory work is all supposed to be done by the shape of the discount function (or whatever generates that shape), and none of it by a distinct faculty, a divided mind, or a failure of practical reason. I'm reasonably happy with how the piece handles the standard machinery — the old result that only exponential discounting yields plans you won't abandon midway, the sophisticated-versus-naive distinction, the fact that a naive discounter will be systematically surprised by her own reversals while a sophisticated one should be reaching for pre-commitment devices — but I'd like someone to stress-test it properly before I file.

What I want is the defeater map, ranked, and honest about which are live rather than merely logically possible. A few things I'm already chewing on, and you should feel free to tell me which of them actually bite. One: the fitted curves come mostly from hypothetical choices about money, over weeks and months, at modest stakes, and I'm extrapolating to second-by-second temptation about things like whether to check the phone — is that extrapolation licensed, and what exactly would it take to test it? Two: an RL researcher friend of mine argues that apparent hyperbolicity falls out of assuming a fixed hazard rate on reward delivery, and that once you let hazard rates evolve you get the curve without anyone having time-inconsistent preferences at all; if that's right my mechanism is an epistemic artifact rather than a preference, and I'm not sure how much of my column survives it. Three: the delay-dependence worry — hyperbolic discounting has a slot for temporal distance but none for salience or attention, and people report losing battles where both options are available right now, or where the tempting option wins because it's in front of them rather than because it's sooner. Four, and the one I find most annoying: if all conflict is really conflict between earlier and later selves, my view owes an account of which temporal slice speaks for the person, and it can't answer that in discounting terms without circularity. There's also the thing where people who know they're hyperbolic and know the cheap pre-commitment device is available still decline to use it, which looks like a second-order version of the very thing I'm trying to explain away.

So: what would have to be true for the eliminativist line to be wrong, and what should I actually look for? I especially want the predictions that are mine and not my rivals' — does my view commit me to akrasia reports tracking delay and magnitude in ways they measurably don't, or to delay-shifting interventions beating attention-shifting ones? Which of these would a committed defender of the view concede as counting against it, as opposed to dismissing as confused, and where does the dismissal look like a genuine rebuttal versus a retro-fit? And be straight with me about whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability: if any pattern of behaviour can be absorbed by adjusting a discount function or adding an uncertainty story, is that a fatal problem for a view of this kind, or is it fine because the view was only ever meant to be a redescription? I'd rewrite the lede for anything in the top two tiers, so dont soften the ranking.
