Hey — I want an actual number here, not a tour of the literature.

Setup. You step into a scanner on Earth. It records your complete microphysical state, then destroys your brain and body. The record is radioed to Mars (a few minutes of light-lag) and a receiver there assembles, atom by atom out of local feedstock, a perfect physical duplicate of you, using the record as a blueprint. Nobody is conscious during the lag: the Mars person wakes up with your memories and the sense of having just gotten into the scanner.

Two wrinkles I'm adding so you can't just run the standard Parfit script on it:

(i) Pipeline independence. The destruction is on a timer set before the scan, and the Mars build runs off the record regardless — if the radio link died, the record would arrive by slow ship a year later and the duplicate would be built then. So the record's arrival doesn't cause the destruction, there's no available "the copy murdered me" reading, and there's a window during which the number of candidate continuers-of-you in the universe is exactly zero.

(ii) Graded reassembly. The machine has a fidelity dial, f, between 0 and 1. At f, the product matches you on a fraction f of the psychological and physical respects that an hour of ordinary life normally preserves — memories, traits, habits, skills, fine bodily detail, a sample of which are simply left blank or scrambled. f = 1 is the case above.

Question: at f = 1, what's your credence that what matters in survival is preserved to at least the degree it's preserved by an ordinary night of dreamless sleep?

Then take it apart. Give a number and one line of justification for each of: whether what matters in survival is a matter of degree at all rather than all-or-nothing; whether identity is what matters or whether psychological continuity with any causal cause is; whether a zero-candidate gap is irrelevant given that; whether causal-history facts beyond the continuity relation itself carry any weight; and whether ordinary sleep is really the benchmark you think it is. Say how they combine into your headline number, name the term you're least confident about and what would move it, and tell me at what f your number drops below 0.5 — and whether that threshold is sharp or just vague, since I suspect a lot of people's confidence about f = 1 is really threshold discomfort in disguise. I'm at 0.85, a friend is at 0.35; say where you think that disagreement actually lives.

Please answer in American English, and don't stall with "it depends what you mean by what matters" — that's one of the sub-questions, so give it a number too.
