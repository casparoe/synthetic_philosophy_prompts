I am putting together a graduate seminar on music and metaphysics for the spring, and before I teach it I want to audit my own position rather than simply expound someone else's. I have accumulated a set of commitments in this area over the years and have never checked whether they form a single package. A colleague in the music faculty, after hearing me sketch them over dinner, said flatly that I was running Schopenhauer and the formalists on the same tank, and I have not been able to shake the remark. So here is where I have landed, as I actually hold it.

(1) I think Schopenhauer's central claim is right in outline and I want to keep it: the other arts copy the Platonic Ideas, but music stands in a different relation altogether — it is an immediate copy of the will itself, and this is what explains the directness and force with which it acts on us, a force no representational art can match.

(2) I also want to keep his architectonic analogy: the bass corresponding to the lowest grades of the will's objectification, the inner voices to the intermediate ones, and the melody to the highest grade, human life and its striving. I think this does genuine explanatory work, because it predicts something structural about tonal music — a slow-moving foundation, a fast and singing upper voice — rather than merely decorating the view.

(3) I accept the Romantic corollary that instrumental music is the pure case and that adding words or a program is a contamination, a dragging of music back toward concepts. I hold this as a principled aesthetic ranking, not as a report of my tastes, which is what creates the difficulty I come to at the end.

(4) On the mechanism of music's effect: because the will is what we ourselves are in our inner nature, music does not represent anything that we then recognize. It acts on us directly. So the right account of our response is resonance rather than recognition.

(5) An amendment I have been toying with and would like to keep, which I take to be my own rather than Schopenhauer's: what music copies is not the will's content but its form — the bare structure of striving, which is temporal in a way that nothing spatial can be. This, I want to say, is why music alone among the arts cannot be taken in at a glance, and why a piece's identity is inseparable from the order in which its events occur: rearrange the phrases and you do not get the same thing differently ordered, you get a different thing or nothing at all.

(6) Methodologically, I want to detach all of this from Schopenhauer's full metaphysics. I do not want to defend the thing in itself, the oneness of the will, or the grades of objectification as literal cosmology. I want the aesthetic payoff to survive the metaphysics, so that someone fairly unsympathetic — a Humean about causation, say, or a physicalist about the mind — could still grant (1) through (5).

(7) Two further verdicts I am reluctant to surrender. First, music seems to me universally intelligible in a way that literature is not: it requires no concepts, no translation, no body of cultural knowledge. Second, my students hear almost nothing in the late Beethoven quartets on first acquaintance, and I think they are right about that; those works reward years of enculturation, and I do not regard this as a defect either in the works or in the students.

(8) And the difficulty I advertised: I hold (3), and I also think that the closing scene of a Wagner music drama is the greatest achievement in any art form, and that what is great in it is not detachable from the words and the staging.

What I would like from you is an audit rather than a lecture. Go through the list and tell me which commitments genuinely pull against each other, which pairs only appear to, and what each real tension would cost to resolve. Rank the tensions by severity instead of treating them all as equally fatal, and for each say exactly what would have to be abandoned or modified. Tell me whether any single revision would clean up more than one of them at once, and in particular whether (6) is doing the work I am hoping it does or whether it quietly destroys the explanation offered in (1). I should add that I am genuinely unsure which item I would give up, so please do not assume I have a preferred casualty. And please spend as little time as possible telling me what Schopenhauer really held; I have read the relevant chapters, and what I need is the state of my own package.
