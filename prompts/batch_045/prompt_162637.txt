i'm a TA for an historical methods seminar (honors section, ~20 papers a week) and i'm the one who has to decide whether a paper is being presentist — right now i basically go on vibes and then can't defend the comment when a student pushes back in office hours. can you turn this into a real procedure i can run while grading, something more than the old whig-history complaint?

the stuff i keep getting stuck on, and want the procedure to actually handle instead of hand-wave:

- vocabulary drag. when a paper calls a 12th-century queen a survivor of domestic violence, or a 19th-century senator a white supremacist, the present-day term drags a whole theory in with it. is there a usable test — something like restating the claim in words available to people at the time and seeing what gets lost — for when the drag is doing real work and when it's just a label?
- explaining vs. evaluating. the line i keep hearing is that present standards are illegitimate for explaining why people acted but fine for judging whether they acted well. that asymmetry feels too convenient. where does it actually hold up?
- silence laundering. if the category didn't exist yet, the archive is silent about it, and it is really easy to turn that silence into evidence about the thing rather than evidence about the category. how do i catch a student doing that?
- reciprocity. if you judge past actors by standards they didn't accept, you should be fine with some future historian judging you by standards you don't accept yet. does anything real follow from that, or is it just a rhetorical move?

what i want: the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure stops with a clear yes/no, and where it has to hand the call back to me because it's a values question rather than a factual one. it has to run in about five minutes a paper and be explainable to a sophmore without assigning them theory, and please be honest about which steps are really just my judgment wearing a checklist as a costume.

then run it on three papers from this week: (a) one arguing that a colonial governor's campaign in the 1780s amounts to genocide; (b) one describing an 18th-century food rioter as engaging in redistributive justice — i liked this one, so tell me if the procedure kills it anyway; (c) one that refuses to use any moral language about the past at all on the grounds that we weren't there. for each, give me the comment i'd actually write in the margin. american english, and please don't soften the result on (b) if the procedure says it's presentist.
