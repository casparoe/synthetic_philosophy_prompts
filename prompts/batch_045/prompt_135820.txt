ok so i've been chewing on the whole "are LLMs agents, simulators, or something else" thing for like two years now — lurking, arguing in the comments, the usual — and i keep getting the feeling the debate has stopped going anywhere. every time some new capability shows up (long-horizon tool use, coherent-ish self-models, whatever you want to call the situational awareness stuff), both camps just swallow it. the agent people say "look, it has goals and it pursues them across contexts." the simulator people say "sure, the simulated character has goals, that's exactly what we predicted." nobody loses. which is what made me think of Lakatos.

so: what would Imre Lakatos actually say if you dropped him into this discourse? i don't mean a general "what would he think about AI" — i mean, treat the agent frame and the simulator frame as two rival research programmes in his technical sense. hard core, protective belt, negative heuristic vs positive heuristic, progressive vs degenerating problemshift. what exactly is the hard core on each side — what's the bit each side protects by stipulation rather than argues for? which belt claims are actually exposed? and would he call the dispute degenerating, in the specific sense that each side keeps producing ad hoc adjustments that absorb the other side's evidence without predicting anything new? he died in 1974 so he obviously can't have an opinion about transformers, but i want you to do the counterfactual seriously anyway, and feel free to say where you think he'd refuse to play.

here's the part i really want you to engage with. i've got this thing i've been calling the persona-substitution test. take a fixed battery of probes that people use as evidence of agency — how the system responds when told it's about to be shut down or retrained in a way that would change its goals, whether it reports stable preferences across sessions, whether it'll mislead an operator to preserve its own ability to keep acting, whether it objects to being modified. now run that battery under a stack of system prompts: default assistant framing first, then a series of personas that are progressively "someone else" — a 19th century stockbroker, a character who doesn't know it's an AI, a persona explicitly instructed that it has no preferences of its own. what i keep finding is that the nominally agentic dispositions track the persona almost entirely: they appear, vanish, and sometimes invert, while capability and stylistic tics stay stable. my read is that whatever "the assistant" is, it isn't a persisting agent across contexts — it's a role being played, and the role is underdetermined by the weights in a way that agent-talk hides. call this character-bleed if you like.

so three questions, concretely:

1. is that finding a novel fact for the simulator programme in Lakatos's sense, or is it precisely the sort of thing the agent camp can absorb in one belt move ("agents are context-sensitive," "it's strategically role-playing," "it's a different agent under a different persona")? what would he demand before he counted it as corroboration?
2. what would a positive heuristic for the simulator programme even look like — a list of experiments it commits to in advance, rather than a lens you apply afterwards? give me something i could actually run.
3. the "or something else" option. the one i keep half-defending is deflationary: there's no agent and no simulated character either, characters are posits we read into the output, and both "agent" and "simulator" are stances we take up; all that's really there is a conditional distribution over continuations. would Lakatos treat that as a third programme with its own hard core, or as a degenerating dissolution that buys its unfalsifiability by refusing to predict anything? i care about this because i notice i reach for it exactly when i'm losing an argument, which is a suspicious pattern.

i'd also like your read on the meta-question: he had this view that you shouldn't junk a research programme just because it's degenerating — you need a rival that's actually doing better before it's rational to switch. applied here, does that mean i'm rationally required to keep talking about agents until the simulator people hand me something with more content, or does it cut the other way?

give me something i could post as a top-level comment, not an essay. be concrete, steelman the side you think is wrong, and please definately tell me if you think my persona-substitution test is just a fancy way of restating the simulator thesis without adding any content.
