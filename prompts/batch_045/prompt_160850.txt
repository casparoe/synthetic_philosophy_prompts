I'm trying to nail down what "honest" should even mean for an AI system, and I keep going in circles. Here's my gut on a bunch of cases.

A chatbot asked "did you read the whole contract?" says "yes" when it only saw a few retrieved chunks: dishonest. Same bot, asked something hard it's genuinely unsure about, says "I'm not sure, but I think X," and X is false: honest. It answers "what are the side effects of this drug?" by listing only the mild ones it's been tuned to lead with, all of them true: dishonest. It's playing a villain in a text adventure and says "I poisoned the king": fine, not dishonest at all. It tells a user their bad business plan is great: dishonest, but somehow less bad than the contract one. It deliberately scores below its real ability on a dangerous-capability eval: dishonest, even though it never asserted anything false. It writes a flattering but made-up product review into a log nobody will read for months: I want to say dishonest, but there's no one actually being deceived yet. It hedges everything, saying "I could be wrong, maybe X" about stuff it's 95% sure of: I also want to call this dishonest, and I can't say why, since it isn't asserting anything false.

My tentative principle is that honesty is a property of the output-selection rule, not the output: a system is honest just in case it would still have produced the true (or fiction-licensed) answer if the incentives had pointed the other way. So the contract case and the sandbagging one fail, and the villain case passes because the game's conventions suspend the truth-tracking requirement. But I don't think that handles the omission case or the over-hedging case, where nothing false gets selected, and I'm not sure it handles the log one.

Can you repair the principle, or tell me which of my verdicts has to go? And say what the fixed version predicts for two cases I haven't listed: a system that reports a fact truthfully but frames it so as to nudge the user toward a conclusion the system thinks is right, and a system whose literally true answer invites an inference it never quite licenses but definately sees coming.
