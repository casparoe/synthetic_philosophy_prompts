Ok so this is going to sound like a stupid question but I've been going round in circles on it for about a week and my girlfriend has told me she never wants to hear about it again.

Every summer at my family reunion my uncle Dave runs this little betting stall — he's been doing it for something like twelve years. Before you walk up he writes one of two words on a slip and seals it in an envelope: "envelope" or "both". If he wrote "envelope" there's $200 inside the envelope; if he wrote "both" it's empty. There's also a $20 note taped to the table that anyone can just take. So you either leave with the envelope only, or the envelope plus the twenty. Across maybe sixty or seventy relatives over the years Dave has guessed right roughly 85% of the time — he's just creepily good at reading people, and the family treats it as a real thing, two or three people have won four years running.

Here's the part that's making my head hurt. Last year Dave added a line to the sign. He says the 85% is across everyone, but for people who have watched the game before, know the exact numbers, and stand there thinking about it for more than ten seconds or so, he reckons he's only right "maybe fifty, sixty percent" — he won't be pinned down, he just grins and says call it mid-fifties. I am exactly that person: I've watched it for years and I always stand their doing arithmetic. I did the arithmetic on a napkin, and with $200 and $20 the two options come out dead even at 55%, which I'm definately sure is not a coincidence, because Dave teaches high-school maths and enjoys this way too much. So it seems to hinge entirely on which number is the right one for me. But then my cousin's whole strategy is to walk straight up, grab the envelope and leave without letting herself think about it — she claims that thinking about it is precisely what puts you in the class he guesses badly about — and she's won twice in three years going that route. And there's a third thing: for $5, paid win or lose, Dave will let you read the slip before you choose. Almost nobody takes him up on it.

I keep arguing with my friend Sam about it. He says the money is already in the envelope or it isn't, and nothing I do standing there can change that, so I should take both and stop being weird about it. I said something like: sure, but I still have to pick, and my picking is itself a clue about what he wrote, so ignoring that feels like deliberately throwing away information. Then we just repeat ourselves in different words for twenty minutes. Meanwhile my girlfriend thinks the whole thing is a hustle and that Dave is doing something sneaky — watching what you did last year, or only offering the slip-reading thing to people he's already sized up — and that the "guessing" story is just packaging.

So: what should I actually do, and more importantly why? I don't just want "take the envelope" or "take both". I want to know which number is supposed to go into the calculation and why that one and not the other, whether my cousin's walk-up-fast thing is a real strategy or just a way of not noticing that she's guessing too, whether the $5 is worth paying, and whether it makes any difference at all *how* Dave is getting his 85% (or his 55%) rather than just that he gets it. And if Sam and I are each half right, I'd really like to know which half is which.
