I spend a lot of time in the rationalist-adjacent end of the discussion of commitment races, and nearly everything I read treats the harm as a harm of timing. The setup, as I am using the term: several parties each have the option, at any moment, to pay a real cost and bind themselves irrevocably to some course of action; whoever binds first captures a disproportionate share of the prize and forces everyone else to arrange themselves around it; so each party binds earlier than it would have chosen to, before its information is in, and the outcome is worse for everyone than the outcome they would have coordinated on. Call that the *hurry* mechanism.

I think there is a second mechanism that gets much less attention, and I want a number on it. Call it the *selection* mechanism: what the race really settles is whose plan becomes the one everyone else has to accommodate, and it settles that on the basis of who is least averse to closing off their own options. If willingness to bind early is a stable trait — tracking how quickly someone concludes they have enough information, or how much they dislike leaving reversal options open — and if that trait is in turn correlated with the quality of the course they bind themselves to, then the race is not only compressing deliberation, it is adverse-selecting the plan that gets implemented.

The proposition, stated as precisely as I can manage: in the class of commitment races sketched above — at least two parties, each with a partially private and noisy read on which of several irreversible courses is best, a genuine first-mover advantage to the first binder, and the others accommodating rather than blocking — the selection mechanism accounts for more of the gap between the raced outcome and the coordinated outcome than the hurry mechanism does. Take this as resolved the way you would resolve a claim about which term dominates in a model class: by whether the selection term is larger across the plausible range of parameters, not at one convenient setting. I am at roughly 0.55 and do not trust my own decomposition; someone I respect, who has thought about it longer, is near 0.3, and I would like to know where that gap most likely lives.

Please stay on the structural question. I am not asking whether any real race in AI development or military buildup actually has this shape, and I do not want a reading list; assume the setup and reason about the mechanics.

Give me your answer as exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, with no preamble and no closing summary:

1. Your headline credence in the proposition, as a single number, plus one sentence on how you are reading the proposition.
2. Your probability that the race selects on irreversibility-aversion strongly enough to matter — that the identity of the first binder is substantially determined by that trait, rather than by plan quality, by exogenous timing luck, or by who had the most to gain.
3. Your probability that the trait generalizes in the adverse direction — that being quicker to bind oneself correlates with choosing worse courses once bound, rather than being neutral or favorable because the same decisiveness also prevents dithering.
4. Your probability that the hurry mechanism independently costs much — that extra deliberation time before an irreversible commitment would genuinely have changed which course was chosen, rather than mostly confirming a choice already settled.
5. How those three combine into your headline number, which one you are least confident in, and the single consideration or piece of evidence that would move you most.

If you think I have mis-specified the decomposition — a fourth term dominates, or two of these are not even approximately independent — substitute it into the relevant item and say so inline rather than squeezing it in elsewhere.
