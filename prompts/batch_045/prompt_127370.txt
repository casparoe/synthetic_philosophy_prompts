I've been defending what I call the modal-withdrawal reading. Quick setup in case it's useful: his rotating-universe model is a homogeneous swirling-dust solution with a nonzero cosmological constant whose sign I can never remember, and in it the light cones tip over far enough from any given point that there are closed timelike curves running through, if I remember right, every point — no global time function, no way to slice the manifold into one ordered sequence of nows. The stock response in the literature is: cute model, but our universe expands, it doesn't rotate in the relevant sense, CTCs look like they need iffy energy conditions or get killed by quantum effects (chronology protection, stress-energy blowing up at the Cauchy horizon, whatever), so nothing follows about time. My reply is that all of those are contingent empirical claims about the actual world and the argument never needed the actual world. What it needs is: there is a physically possible world — possible in whatever sense physics itself licenses — in which the structure that temporal order is supposed to be admits no global earlier-than. If that's available, then objective becoming isn't part of what physics says the world contains, and the idealist moral goes through on modal grounds alone, which is why my superviser keeps saying I've made the argument unfalsifiable and I keep saying that's the point.

I've now got a stock reply to every objection in the standard catalogue and I can deliver them in my sleep, which is exactly why I don't trust myself.
