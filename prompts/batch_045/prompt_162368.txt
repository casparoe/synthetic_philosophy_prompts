Not sure this is the right place to ask, but I keep running into the same shape of thing and I'd like to know if it has a name.

I do data work for a public agency, so I see these from the inside. Case one: a school district basically stops excluding kids. Genuinely less brutal, everyone delighted. But exclusion gets replaced by a "support pathway": the kid gets a check-in plan, somebody logs how they're doing every week, and the intake form asks about home situation, mood, friendships, sleep. Three years on, way more kids are inside the system than ever got sent home, they stay in it far longer, and there's a file that travels with them to the next school. Nobody decided to widen surveillance. They decided to be kinder.

Case two: a mental health unit scraps seclusion. Nobody gets locked in a room now. Instead every patient has an "observation level" that gets reviewed regularly, which means constant documentation, which means more eyes on more people for more hours.

The parts I keep coming back to: what happens to any single person gets milder; the number of people caught and the time they stay caught goes up; and the headline numbers look amazing — exclusions down to nearly nothing — because the thing being counted got renamed rather than reduced. And there's a fourth bit that I think is the real signature: object to the forms and the database and you sound like you want kids back in exclusion, so the whole apparatus gets defended by the word "support." A colleague calls it the caring ratchet, which I'm fairly sure she invented.

Is there an established concept for this? I've had Foucault thrown at me, but only ever secondhand, and I don't want to stick his name on it if what he's describing is actually a different animal. It isn't just mission creep either — the people doing it think they're doing less, and in a sense they are.

I don't want a bare label and I don't need references. I want the carving: what exactly the pattern is, what would not count as an instance of it (a reform that shrank both the harshness and the reach? an expansion that was openly about control and never used the caring vocabulary?), which nearby concepts it gets confused with, and whether the caring/risk vocabulary is doing essential work or is just an accident of how these things happen to get sold. And if there really is no name for it, name it and define it properly, with the boundary cases spelled out.
