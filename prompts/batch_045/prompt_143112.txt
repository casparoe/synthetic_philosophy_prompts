I'm giving the opening remarks (about seven minutes) at our group's winter offsite on Friday — the whole lab, roughly thirty people. Context: we spent the year building a coding agent that is now definately better than most of us at a decent chunk of our own work, and nobody can point to the part of the system that knows why it does what it does. The room is: the capabilities people and the interpretability people (who've been quietly sniping at each other about exactly this all year), three philosophers of mind we flew in to be the conscience of the place, our ops and finance team, and two funders who are deciding about next year.

Two things I need the speech to do, and they pull in opposite directions. The funders will hear "the model understands what it's doing" if I say anything at all warm about its performance, and I don't want to hand them that. But the engineers have spent the year treating the model's written reasoning as evidence — around here we literally call the chain-of-thought logs "the receipts" — and the safety story for the next release leans on being able to read them. One of the philosophers already told me in the corridor that the receipts have no more authority than a confabulated explanation, and our eval lead is going to hear that as being told her year was worthless.

So build the remarks around the Darwin/Turing inversion: competence without comprehension, reasons that float free of anyone who has them, the idea that you don't need a understander standing behind a performance for the performance to be real. I want that idea doing structural work — deciding what I can and can't say — rather than being a nice framing I drop in and move on from. Concretely: I want to praise the work and the eval work honestly, without crediting the model with reasons, and I want the philosophers to feel I actually got the point rather than that I borrowed their vocabulary and went soft with it. If it helps, the thing I keep circling is that the receipts are themselves produced by the same competence that produced the answer — reasons-shaped output rather than reasons — but I can't say it that flatly in front of ops, they'll glaze over.

Things I don't want: "but does it really understand", the map/territory thing, anything about consciousness as such (ops checks out, philosophers roll their eyes), and no Chinese-room-bashing. And it can't be a downer — people are exhausted and it's the start of a two-day thing where I need them to actually talk to each other.
