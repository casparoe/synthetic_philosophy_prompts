I serve on my university's animal research oversight committee and volunteer at a farmed-animal sanctuary, and between the two I keep failing to give a straight answer when people ask what I actually believe about animals. My housemate's diagnosis is that I am rationalizing the habits I was raised with, and I do not have a reply ready. So here are my verdicts, gut-level, on cases I have actually had to think about.

(a) Eating a conventionally raised broiler chicken is seriously wrong. (b) The same chicken, had it been given a genuinely good life and a genuinely painless death, would still be wrong to eat, though much less bad — and I cannot say why, since it does not seem to be the suffering doing the work. (c) Oysters and mussels: no problem. Octopus: not fine. Shrimp: genuinely torn, and my uncertainty does not seem to push me either way, which I suspect is itself suspicious. (d) Mice in a biomedical protocol involving moderate suffering and a large expected human benefit, with oversight: permissible. The same protocol on a dog: not permissible, and the reason feels like it is about us and our history with dogs. The same protocol on a chimpanzee: clearly not permissible, and here the reason feels like it is about the chimpanzee. (e) Pest mice in my kitchen: fine to kill quickly, though I would rather not. A healthy dog or cat in an overcrowded shelter: not fine to euthanize. (f) A dog born severely cognitively impaired, whose actual capacities are below those of an ordinary pig: I still extend dog-level protection, not pig-level. (g) Pigs bred to have cognitive capacities reduced to roughly a mouse's, which on my own view should make research on them more acceptable: I still find it nearly as bad as using ordinary pigs. Mice bred with capacities pushed up toward a dog's: I do not find it as bad as using dogs, though I am more uneasy than with ordinary mice. (h) Wild animals: I do not think we owe them anything — no duty to feed or shelter the ones who freeze or starve or are eaten alive each winter. But I do think a cull of an overpopulated, starving deer herd can be justified, and the justification I would give is precisely that it prevents a worse death. (i) Dogs versus pigs in a burning barn, none of them mine: I would save ten pigs over one dog, but I could not tell you the exchange rate I am using, and I would feel bad about it afterward. (j) Killing seems worse the more the death takes away — the dog waiting for its owner to come home is a clearer loss than a creature living entirely in the present.

My tentative summary is something like: everyone who can suffer counts, how much they count tracks how much there is to lose, and on top of that we owe more to animals we have made dependent on us or pulled into our lives. I would like you to test that against the list and repair it rather than replace it wholesale: state a principle precisely enough that I can apply it myself, say which clause is motivated by which case, and be honest about which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate — I would rather give up one verdict than accept a principle with a separate clause for every case, so tell me which verdict you would overturn and why. Two things I especially want addressed: whether the fact that my hierarchy seems to track the kind of animal rather than the individual in front of me is speciesism under a thinner disguise or a distinction that can be defended, and if it can be defended, what exactly does the defending; and whether my pair of verdicts about wild animals can be held together at all, or whether I have to choose between treating wild suffering as my business and treating it as none of my business. Then apply whatever you land on to cases I have not listed — insects killed in harvesting the crops I eat, shooting a fox that is getting into the henhouse, eating farmed fish, a horse that works and is then retired, a wild predator killing its prey — and finally to the one I go back and forth on: adopting a few ex-commercial laying hens, keeping them well, and eating their eggs, which I feel fine about and cannot justify.
