Since my research group started debating whether DSM categories such as major depressive disorder could be natural kinds in Putnam’s sense, I’ve been trying to work through the background in semantic externalism, and a footnote sent me back to John Stuart Mill’s A System of Logic (1843), Book I, Chapter II. I’m stuck on the part where he discusses names. I’m paraphrasing rather than quoting, because I’m working from a scanned copy and don’t trust my transcription: as I understand him, Mill claims that a proper name such as Dartmouth hooks directly onto the particular object and keeps applying to it even if the object ceases to have the descriptive feature that originally motivated the name, so a town called Dartmouth could retain its name even after it is no longer at the mouth of the river Dart. But he also says that some individual names, apparently including the sun and God, are nevertheless connotative, and that general names like water both denote objects and connote a set of attributes, so that the name applies to whatever has those attributes. My friend says this means Mill anticipated Putnam: if a proper name sticks to its object regardless of superficial changes, then reference isn’t fixed by speakers’ descriptions, which is the negative half of the Twin Earth argument. I’m not convinced, because on Mill’s own account a general term like water seems to apply to the Twin Earth liquid precisely because it has the same superficial attributes, which is the internalist view Putnam rejected. Could you walk me through what Mill is actually claiming in this passage—what it means for a proper name to be tied directly to the object rather than to its attributes, why he treats certain individual names as connotative, and how his contrast between proper names and general names bears on Twin Earth? Does his view end up supporting externalism for proper names only, making him a partial precursor to Kripke rather than Putnam—or am I missing something about Mill’s notion of connotation that would let his account of general names survive Twin Earth after all?
