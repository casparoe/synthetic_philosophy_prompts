I've been trying to write down, mostly for my own benefit, the epistemological position I seem to have ended up in, and I'd like an audit of whether it actually hangs together. As best I can tell, I hold the following:

(1) Veritism: accuracy, understood as closeness to the truth, is the only fundamental epistemic value, and every other epistemic norm is supposed to be derived from it.

(2) Credence is stakes-invariant: how confident it is rational to be in a proposition is fixed by one's evidence about it alone, never by what happens to turn on being right.

(3) Full belief is reducible to credence: to believe that P just is to have credence in P above a threshold. I'm relaxed about whether the threshold is sharp or set by context, as long as belief stays a function of credence.

(4) Belief is nonetheless stakes-sensitive: whether one may rationally believe, act on, or flatly assert P does depend on the costs of being wrong, so that one and the same body of evidence can warrant belief when little is at stake and withhold it when a great deal is.

(5) Permissivism: evidence does not in general single out one rational credence; a range of credences can be permissible, and two people sharing all their evidence can rationally come apart.

(6) The accuracy framework vindicates probabilism and conditionalization, and I have been assuming those results are robust across choices of strictly proper scoring rule, so I have never felt pressure to settle on one.

(7) It is never rational to be fully certain of a contingent empirical proposition; credence 1, and likewise 0, is always a mistake about matters of fact.

I have an inchoate sense that (2) through (4) are unstable as a trio, and that (6) and (7) do not sit comfortably together, but I cannot locate precisely where the damage is in either case, and I am genuinely unsure whether (5) is even coherent alongside taking accuracy to be the whole normative story. Please tell me which of these commitments are in real tension and which only look as though they are, rank the real tensions by severity rather than listing them as equals, and for each say exactly what would have to be revised and what that revision costs the rest of the package. I'd also like to know whether any single revision would clean up more than one problem at once.
