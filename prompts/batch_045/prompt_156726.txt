I've been trying to write down my actual views on value for a small reading group I'm in — four of us, most of whom come out of an EA-adjacent thing, one meditates a lot, one actually does philosophy — and every time I write it up it reads more confident than it feels. Here's roughly where I think I've landed, and I'd like someone to tell me whether it hangs together:

(1) I'm a tranquilist, more or less: the only fundamental good is tranquility, which I understand as the absence of craving, and the only fundamental bad is craving and suffering. Pleasure has no intrinsic positive value on my view — the reason pleasure feels good is that it's the removal of an itch you already had, so it's relief, not a good in itself. I want to be carefull about one distinction here: by craving I mean the restless, aversive, unsatisfied-urge state, not just wanting something. Wanting a coffee with no sting attached isn't the target; wanting it *with* the sting is.
(2) I'm impersonally aggregative about all this: what matters is the total tranquility and the total suffering, whosever it is, no special discount for strangers, distance, or people who don't exist yet.
(3) I accept the asymmetry in population ethics: that someone would suffer is a strong reason not to create them, and that someone would have a calm, craving-free life is no reason to create them. I'm not a full antinatalist — or I'm trying not to be — but I do think the asymmetry is right.
(4) Concretely: if there were a safe one-off intervention, call it the dimmer, that permanently turned down how much unmet wants sting — you'd still want and pursue things, they'd just hurt much less when unsatisfied — I'd take it, I'd want it rolled out widely, and I think that's an easy call.
(5) But I'd refuse the pill: permanent untroubled contentment with no actual accomplishments and no actual relationships, even though it feels from the inside exactly as if there were. I'd refuse it for myself and a world where everyone is on it strikes me as bad. I'm not embarassed about my reason either: I think there's more to a good life than how it feels from the inside — actually doing things, real contact with reality, mattering to real people. Of the whole set, I take this verdict to be the safest one.
(6) I work on far-future stuff and I think human extinction would be a catastrophe, though my version is the suffering-focused one: I'm not moved at all by "astronomical amounts of future happiness," which given (1) I think is incoherent; I'm moved by the thought that things could go very badly — vast amounts of suffering, digital minds, runaway dynamics, that sort of thing.
(7) I also think, though, that a universe with nothing in it would be worse than a universe full of calm, contented minds. Not that this gives anyone a reason to bring them about — see (3) — just that it's the better world.
(8) And in my own life I go looking for discomfort on purpose: cold water, long hard walks, sad films, sitting with grief instead of getting distracted. I think my life is better with that in it and worse without it.

One friend says (3) and (7) obviously contradict each other, and I think that's a cheap gotcha — surely I can hold that creating someone is never in itself a reason while also holding that a world containing tranquility is better than an empty one. The thing I actually lose sleep over is (8), which feels like it might just flatly contradict (1). But I suspect I'm worried about the wrong one. So please audit the set: go through it and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it, order the real tensions by severity instead of listing them all as equally bad, and for each one say exactly what would have to give and what giving it up would cost me elsewhere in the set. Then tell me which single revision cleans up the most, and whether (6) survives whatever it is I end up doing.
