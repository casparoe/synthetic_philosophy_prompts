I put this argument together for a reading group and now Im fairly sure it is broken somewhere, because the conclusion is nuts, but every step looks fine to me and I cant find the hole.

(1) On the interventionist (aka manipulability) theory, X causes Y iff there is some possible intervention on X — an exogenous, surgical setting of X to a value — such that Y (or the probability of Y) changes, with the other variables that aren't on the path from X to Y held fixed.
(2) So a causal claim is always evaluated inside a model: a choice of variables, a choice of which of them to hold fixed, a contrast of values, and — I'd add — a choice of time-scale.
(3) My example. A greenhouse with a gas heater H fitted with a thermostat, a roof vent V, and air temperature T. Model A: hold the heater's actual output fixed, open the vent → T drops. Model B: leave the thermostat in the picture and let the heater compensate → T doesn't drop at all, the heater just burns more gas. Both models are accurate descriptions of the very same greenhouse on the very same afternoon, and both look like models an interventionist ought to accept.
(4) The time-scale flips it too, independently: measure T two minutes after opening the vent and it drops; measure it at equilibrium and it doesn't. Same system, same intervention, opposite verdicts.
(5) And it generalizes: take any causal claim you like, embed the system in a slightly bigger model containing a compensating mechanism (or zoom in on a shorter time window), and the verdict flips.
(6) Nothing about the greenhouse itself settles whether Model A or Model B is the right one. What settles it is what I'm contrasting — whether I care about what venting does to the air directly or about the equilibrium the thermostat restores.
(7) So whether opening the vent lowers the temperature is true depends on what somebody happens to be interested in.
(8) The truth-value of a claim can't depend on anybody's interests. So no causal claim is true or false simpliciter: X causes Y only ever means "relative to a model we happen to find useful, intervening on X moves Y," and causal structure is something we project onto the world rather than something in it.
(9) That has to be wrong. However the argument goes, whether venting that greenhouse cools it is not up to me and what I happen to be curious about.
