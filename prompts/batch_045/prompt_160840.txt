Our book club somehow turned into an ethics reading group a few months ago — four of us, pub, two of the group are auditing a university intro ethics course and dragging the rest of us through it — and we have been going in circles for three weeks on the "what if everybody did that?" test. I think our disagreement is narrower than the ones in the course notes, and I can't tell whether what I've spotted is a real thing with a name or just us being confused.

Here's the pattern I keep noticing. When you run the test on some rule of action, the verdict sometimes seems to hinge entirely on whether "everybody" means literally one hundred per cent or something looser — and not in the boring way where the badness just scales up smoothly. There look like two kinds, pointing in opposite directions. First kind: harmless, even invisible, as long as compliance is high but short of total, and only falls apart at the limit. My friend's rule of "when a lender is sloppy about the paperwork, I take the money and sort it out later" is fine if a handful of people do it, and stops being something you can even do — the lending itself dries up — only in the world where everyone does it. Second kind is the mirror image: pointless or worse unless nearly everyone is in on it, and perfectly fine at the limit. "I step to the left when I hear someone coming up behind me on the path" is a shambles if a third of people do it and seamless if all of them do. The test only ever samples the endpoint, so it hands down the same shape of verdict for both, and yet they feel like failures of completely different kinds. I don't think this is the same complaint as the one where you can rig the outcome by how specifically you write your rule down — our thing shows up even when both of us agree on the phrasing, and we've tried rewording it every way we can think of.

So: does this asymmetry have an established name, and if not, what would you call it? I don't want a bare label either way. I want to know what exactly the phenomenon is, which nearby things it isn't (including the usual Kantian pair of failure modes and the "law of nature" phrasing, which I suspect is doing something relevant here), whether the test is even meant to be read as a claim about total compliance or about something else, and where the edges are — cases that look like mine but aren't, and cases where the two kinds blur into each other. If nothing established fits, coin a term and define it tightly enough that I could tell a borderline case either way. Keep it under 500 words, and please don't send me off to papers or reading lists — I'm somewhere I can't get hold of any of that, so just argue it out from the examples.
