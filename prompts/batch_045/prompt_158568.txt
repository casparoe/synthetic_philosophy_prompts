I write about science and ideas for a broadsheet, and I am working on a long piece about what would actually happen if the standard axioms of mathematics turned out to be inconsistent. It grew out of a dinner argument I have been unable to settle: a set theorist friend said that a contradiction in ZFC would be the single most consequential event in the intellectual history of our species, while her partner, a number theorist, said his field would barely notice and that she was confusing the notation mathematics is written in with the mathematics itself. I want to get past the dinner-table version of this, and what I need for the page is a concrete artefact: a damage table.

Please set the scenario up as I am framing it. A group working with proof assistants announces a derivation of a contradiction from the standard axioms of set theory as they are normally stated and used. The derivation passes the kernel of a mature proof checker, but it is far too long to be surveyed by a human being and nobody has yet extracted a short, intelligible paradox from it. Assume the community eventually accepts that the derivation is sound and turns to repair rather than to building a new foundation from scratch, and assume the repair takes the form of restricting the axiom schemes rather than changing the logic or abandoning set theory as a coding language. Tell me which axiom or scheme you would expect to be sacrificed, and why that one.

Then the table. For each entry below, give me two things: a number between 0 and 1 for the share of the field's ordinary, published, working results that you would expect to survive the repair more or less as stated, and a disruption score from 1 to 5 for how much re-training, re-writing and re-justifying the field would need before it could simply carry on. Distinguish, in your reasoning, between a field's results surviving as stated and its surviving only after translation into a weaker theory, and between its theorems surviving and its characteristic methods and idioms surviving. Here are the entries: elementary number theory and finite combinatorics; computability and computational complexity theory; real analysis, partial differential equations and analysis as practised by applied mathematicians; functional analysis and operator algebras; probability theory, ergodic theory and stochastic processes; infinite abelian groups, homological algebra and the parts of algebra that deal with large or unrestricted structures; algebraic geometry and arithmetic geometry in their scheme-theoretic form; category theory and higher category theory as used in modern geometry and topology; general topology, set-theoretic topology and infinite combinatorics; mathematical logic, model theory and set theory itself; and the numerical analysis, statistics and control theory that feed directly into engineering. Once you have scored them, sort the whole set into three or four tiers from essentially unscathed down to has to be rebuilt or abandoned, and give a sentence on why each entry lands where it does. Two caveats on the scoring: a scattering of respectable algebraic and analytic questions is already known to be undecidable from the standard axioms, so I would rather have those counted as reclassified than as lost; and please do not pad the answer with citations or suggestions for further reading, since I have a fact-checker for that and what I want from you is the reasoning itself.

Finally, two questions about the frame. First, how much does your ordering shift if the contradiction turns up instead in Peano arithmetic, or in ZFC together with a large-cardinal axiom, rather than in ZFC alone — and does the unsurveyability of the derivation change your answer in any way, or is a verified derivation a verified derivation? Second, my number theorist friend's thesis is that the whole episode would be a meta-event with no first-order consequences for his field, while my set theorist friend's thesis is that every theorem in every field is a theorem of set theory and is therefore implicated. I suspect each of them is partly right and that the argument is over what implicated means, so please add one sentence per tier saying which of the two that tier vindicates.
