Hi — I restore old reel-to-reel tape for a living, so I spend most of my day thinking about records, and on holiday I've been reading popular books about time. There's one move I keep bumping into and I want to know if it has a name.

First place I saw it. I ask why entropy goes up towards what we call the future. I get told: there are vastly more ways for a system to be spread out than bunched up, so if you start bunched up you'll almost certainly end up spread out. Fine. But the same counting seems to run backwards too — pick any moment and it reckons the moment before was more spread out as well, which isn't what we see. The answer I get back is that you don't run it backwards, because we know the universe started out extremely bunched up, and that's an extra thing you just put in rather than something you derive. So I ask how we know that. And I get pointed at records: old light, rocks, photographs, the tape on my bench. But a record is itself a bunched-up little structure that only gets made by a process that spreads entropy around, and it only points one way because of that. So the thing being used to justify the extra posit looks like the very asymmetry the posit was brought in to explain.

Second place. Why do we remember yesterday and not tomorrow? Because remembering is carrying a record around in your head, records get made by entropy-increasing processes, so the direction memory faces is the direction entropy rises. Then I go back to the first question — why does entropy rise towards the future? — and I'm told: the future is the direction we have no records of. Same pair of claims, each one propped against the other, and I can't find the floor anywhere.

I don't think this is just begging the question, and that's the bit I want pinned down. In begging the question the conclusion is smuggled in as a premise of the same argument. Here each argument looks fine on its own; nothing sneaks in; the trouble only turns up when you set the two side by side. It's not quite underdetermination either, as I understand that word — that's about data failing to pick between theories, whereas this feels like it's about which way the explaining is allowed to run. Someone also said to me that this is how all justification works anyway, that you always patch one claim with another and never get to stand on dry land, and that it's fine — which sounds too quick to me, though I can't say why.

So: does this have a name? I've had "initial conditions" and "you need the extra posit" thrown at me, but that names the posit, not what I'm describing. If there's an established concept, I want the nearest two or three and how my thing differs from each, and which fits best. If there isn't, coin one, define it, and give me the edges: what counts as the same move and what doesn't, whether my two cases are really one phenomenon or two, whether it helps or hurts that one half comes from physics and the other from psychology, and — most useful to me — a test that separates a bad version from an honest one, since explaining why cream never un-mixes out of my coffee by pointing at how the cup started seems perfectly respectable. No library access out here, so please don't just send me off to a book; I want the carving itself. And British English throughout, if you wouldn't mind.
