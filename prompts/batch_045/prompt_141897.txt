I'm reconstructing the passage from memory, so it won't be verbatim, but it runs roughly like this: Nyāya is the commonsense-realist school, and its epistemology is basically Indian foundationalism — knowledge, pramā, is a veridical cognition produced by a trustworthy causal instrument, a pramāṇa, and the Naiyāyikas count four of them, perception, inference, comparison and testimony. Perception is the bare pre-conceptual contact between a sense organ and an object; anything you add by way of concepts or judgement is really inference rather than perception. Testimony is likewise a special case of inference, since you accept what someone tells you only because you've inferred from their track record that they're reliable. Comparison — the business of recognising a gavaya in the forest on the basis of being told it's cow-like — is just loose analogical reasoning of the same sort. And Nyāya is distinctive in holding that a cognition's validity is established extrinsically, from outside, rather than being self-certifying the way the Mīmāṃsakas are said to have it, which means each cognition needs another cognition to vouch for it, and that one needs another, so the Naiyāyikas are caught in a vicious regress and can't really claim to know anything.
