hey — for a reading group on consciousness im meant to hand round a one-page statement of my own position so everyone else can take shots at it before I present next week. i want a consistency check first, because last time I presented something like this somebody found a hole in about ninety seconds and it was not a fun ninety seconds. there's a Chalmers-ish person in the group and one illusionist and I'd rather not get shoved into either camp by my own commitments.

here's the set, roughly the order I'd argue for it:

(1) "qualia" in the thick sense — intrinsic, ineffable, essentially private, non-relational, and wholly revealed by having the experience — is an incoherent posit. I think the quining line is basically right about that, and that a decent chunk of the "hard problem" is generated by the posit rather than by the phenomenon.

(2) but I'm not an illusionist and not an eliminativist about consciousness. There is something it is like to see red, and that's a datum to be accommodated, not a confusion to be explained away.

(3) I lean representationalist: what it is like for me just is what my experience represents. No extra ingredient over and above the content, and "same content, different phenomenality" is not a possibility I can make sense of.

(4) On Mary: she gains a new fact. Not just an ability to imagine or recognise or remember, and not just a new concept or label — she comes to know something she couldn't have known in the room. The "she only acquires a recognitional concept" reply strikes me as a dodge.

(5) I also think the explanatory gap is explained by the special nature of phenomenal concepts — indexical or demonstrative, constituted by the very experience they pick out — so the gap is epistemic and conceptual rather than out there in the world.

(6) Morally: what makes my suffering bad is how it feels, not what it does. A system that matched my functional organization exactly but had nothing it was like to be it wouldn't matter in the same way. I want the feeling to be doing the normative work, not the function.

(7) Spectrum inversion. In the sudden swap I think there's a determinate fact about whether my red flipped and it's a real possibility. In the gradual case — rewired a bit at a time over months, functional organization and all my reports preserved throughout — I think there's no determinate answer; "did the quale change?" has no truth value. Same for simple systems: for a bee I want to say it's indeterminate, not merely unknown, whether there's anything it's like to be it.

(8) And I deny privileged self-access: introspection is fallible and theory-laden, I can be wrong about whether im in pain right now, I don't get an infallible verdict on my own experience.

What I want from you: which of these actually pull against each other, ranked by how bad the tension is, and for each one what I'd have to give up and what giving it up would cost me. Some of them I suspect only look like they clash — if so say so and say why it dissolves. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Please be concrete about which numbered item has to move rather than walking me through the dialectic in general; I know the dialectic in general, that's sort of the problem.
