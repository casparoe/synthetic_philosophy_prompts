Hi — I help run the discussion sections for a first-year seminar at my uni called Life in the Universe (astrobiology-ish, mostly non-majors, a decent number of them are only there because they like sci-fi), and for the last section I want to hand out a one-page "Which Fermi answer do you actually hold?" flowchart that they can also click through as a Google Form the night before. Everyone starts at the same node: "I accept that we have no convincing evidence of extraterrestrial technology anywhere we've looked, and I'm not counting UAP/UFO reporting as evidence." From there I want eight to ten branch questions that sort them into named positions — but I really dont want it to be a thinly disguised "do you believe in aliens?" quiz, since that collapses about eight different views into one yes/no, and I also dont want any question that names a hypothesis ("are you a zoo person?") or that only someone who's read the literature could answer. So phrase each node as a concrete scenario, a bet, or a claim about what we should expect to see — the kind of thing a nineteen-year-old who has never opened a paper on this can answer cold, and where two views that agree about everything else come apart.

Please build it from the logical space rather than from a list of names: give me the questions and the combinations of answers they actually allow, then locate the real positions in that space, and tell me which combinations are unoccupied and why (incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored). The leaves I want on the page somewhere: the rare-earth style answer where life is easy but some later step is the bottleneck; "we're early and expansion is fast, so nobody has arrived yet" (the grabby / first-mover style idea, whatever you want to call it); the filter being ahead of us rather than behind; us being deliberately hidden — and I'd like the zoo/planetarium version and the dark-forest version separated if your questions can genuinely pull them apart, and if they cant, say so and tell me what extra question would do it; civilizations that stop expanding or go inward / change substrate / wait for a cooler universe, so they leave the kind of signature we wouldnt recognise as a signature; "they're already here and we dont see them"; "we simply havent looked well enough for the silence to be surprising"; and one leaf for the view that the question is malformed because the colonisation-imperative premise is parochial. Add a simulation-style leaf only if you think it honestly earns a slot.

Two more things I care about. First, one branch has to deal with our own temporal location: we're a few billion years into a long habitable window, in a galaxy full of stars older than the Sun, and I want students forced to say whether they're treating our epoch as a roughly random sample or as something special — it seems to me that's what really separates "we're early" from "they're rare" in a way none of the other questions do, so I'd like each of those two leaves to say what we'd expect to find if we looked preferentially at very old stars versus young ones. Second, each leaf needs four things: a name (or "unnamed" if nobody really defends it), one sentence on its most awkward commitment, one observation that would seriously dent it (a falsifiability tag — I want students to notice how many of these are nearly unfalsifiable), and one line on what it implies for whether we should keep deliberately broadcasting powerful messages outward, i.e. which leaves say shout, which say shut up, and whether any of them is genuinely indifferent. Keep it terse, it has to fit (just barely) on one side of A4, and no bibliography, suggested reading, or links — the professor was explicit that the handout shouldnt send anyone off to the literature.
