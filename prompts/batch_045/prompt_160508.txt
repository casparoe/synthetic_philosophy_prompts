I work in operations for a mid-sized organisation and I have no formal training in philosophy, statistics or economics — I read about this material on my own. I am putting together a short internal talk, about twenty minutes for colleagues who are not specialists, on why the numbers on our dashboards stop meaning what they used to mean. I need one example that does a particular job, because every example I can find does the opposite job.

What I am trying to isolate is the difference between two ways a proxy measure can come apart from the thing it was standing in for. In the first, people change what they do in order to move the number: the behaviour shifts, the number improves or degrades, and the underlying thing does not move with it. This is the version everyone knows, and every illustration I come across is a version of it — teachers drilling students for a standardised exam, support staff avoiding the difficult cases, and so on. In the second, nobody changes what they do and the underlying thing is flat or even improving, but the number still degrades, because what broke is the measurement rather than the behaviour: which cases get into the calculation at all, what gets written in a field, what a timestamp is taken to mean, how a missing value is handled. I want a pure case of the second kind, and not a case of the first kind wearing a disguise.

Here is what the case has to do, all at once:

It has to be ordinary — a hospital ward, a claims office, a housing repairs desk — and fit in one paragraph, with no science fiction and no AI systems in the story.

The proxy has to have been a genuinely good proxy beforehand: chosen for real reasons, tracking the thing it stood for over years, so that the case is not simply about a number that was always junk.

Nobody in the story may optimise against the number. They may not even know it is being used the way it is now used, or if they do, that knowledge must play no role in what they do. No one changes their conduct toward the underlying thing, and the underlying thing itself must be flat or getting better.

Everyone must be acting in good faith, competently, and in line with rules someone else set. No fraud, no falsified records, no lies, no villain, and no one being unusually stupid or unusually corrupt — my colleagues will reject the example the moment the mechanism depends on bad character.

And yet the case has to be a real instance of the law rather than an unrelated piece of drift: it must be the becoming-a-target that causes the degradation. If the same number had stayed a quiet background statistic, with everything else in the story identical, nothing should move. That is the constraint that makes this hard, since in most stories I can think of the degradation comes from someone responding to the incentive.

The reported number should move in a way that looks like genuine deterioration — smooth, plausible, no visible break at the moment the incentive was switched on — so that anyone looking only at the dashboard would conclude that the underlying thing got worse.

After the scenario, please show your work. Walk through each requirement and how the story meets it, including the counterfactual where the metric is left un-targeted. Then give me the diagnostic signature: what an auditor holding only the metric's time series would conclude, what someone checking on the ground would find, and which observable feature tells the two hypotheses apart. Tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens to the example if I remove it — my guess is that letting anyone in the story adjust their behaviour collapses it straight back into the standard story, but I would like that argued rather than asserted. Finally, be honest with me about the objection I expect from the sceptics in the room: that this is not really the phenomenon at all but just measurement error with a narrative attached, that the law is about incentive responses and nothing else, and that my second category is a category of one. Is my distinction real, or am I cutting the pie in the wrong place?

Please do not stipulate the verdicts; I want to see them fall out of the mechanism, and I need the language plain enough to say out loud to non-specialists.
