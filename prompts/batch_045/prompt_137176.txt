I do backend and distributed systems work — event sourcing, append-only logs, log compaction, CDC, materialized views, deferred writes, that whole world — and I keep bumping into the argument about the color phi illusion: you flash a dot at one spot, then a differently coloured dot somewhere else a moment later, and people report a single dot travelling between them that changes colour partway along the path, apparently before the second dot is even presented. The two standard stories are that the original version got recorded and then retroactively rewritten afterwards (the Orwellian one), or that nothing got written down until the value was known, so the finished version is the only one that ever existed (the Stalinesque one) — plus the further claim that these aren't really two different stories at all.

Translate that whole dispute into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can: retroactive update versus deferred write, commit points, late materialization, in-band telemetry emitted from inside the pipeline doing the processing, no observer with a clock outside the system. Please also cover a third case I care about, where there was never any stored record of the middle of the trajectory at all and the answer just got computed at read time, because that's what a lazily evaluated view looks like to me.

Then give me the residue report: what maps cleanly onto my machinery, what you had to fudge to make it fit, and what my vocabulary simply has no way of saying. My pet theory is that if all the instrumentation lives inside the same pipeline that might be doing the editing, then which story is true stops being answerable — is that the same thing as their being no fact of the matter, or am I mistaking an epistemic limit for a metaphysical one? And is the bit that won't translate the interesting part, or is it just the ghost of a commit point I shouldn't have gone looking for?
