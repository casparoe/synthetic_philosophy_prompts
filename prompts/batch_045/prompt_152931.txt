I am working slowly through George Berkeley's *The Querist* — the later one-volume edition whose Advertisement notes that the three Parts are printed together and that most of the queries concerning the sketch of a national bank were dropped — and I have come to a halt on one short run of queries about what money is. The passage is this (I have skipped queries 6–20):

4 Whether the four elements, and man's labour therein, be not the true source of wealth?
5 Whether money be not only so far useful, as it stirreth up industry, enabling men mutually to participate the fruits of each other's labour?

[…]

21 Whether other things being given, as climate, soil, etc., the wealth be not proportioned to the industry, and this to the circulation of credit, be the credit circulated or transferred by what marks or tokens soever?
22 Whether, therefore, less money swiftly circulating, be not, in effect, equivalent to more money slowly circulating? Or, whether, if the circulation be reciprocally as the quantity of coin, the nation can be a loser?
23 Whether money is to be considered as having an intrinsic value, or as being a commodity, a standard, a measure, or a pledge, as is variously suggested by writers? And whether the true idea of money, as such, be not altogether that of a ticket or counter?
24 Whether the value or price of things be not a compounded proportion, directly as the demand, and reciprocally as the plenty?
25 Whether the terms crown, livre, pound sterling, etc., are not to be considered as exponents or denominations of such proportion? And whether gold, silver, and paper are not tickets or counters for reckoning, recording, and transferring thereof?
26 Whether the denominations being retained, although the bullion were gone, things might not nevertheless be rated, bought, and sold, industry promoted, and a circulation of commerce maintained?

Here is my half-formed reading, offered so that you can break it rather than confirm it. I take Berkeley to be running two claims that are hard to hold together: a notation claim (money is nothing but a counter recording a ratio that is fixed independently, by demand and plenty) and a stimulus claim (money and credit are what call industry into being, so that more circulating credit means more wealth). I suspect he reconciles them by locating money's causal work entirely in the will — appetites, fashion, the prevailing will of a nation, as the earlier queries have it — while its representational work is in the ratio. But I cannot tell from the text whether that reconciliation requires money to be *validated* by something (a sovereign's institution, a warehouse of real goods, general confidence) or only to be *accepted*, and the two options come apart on cases.

Please do the following, in around 900–1,100 words.

1. Say what "the true idea of money, as such" excludes. Does "altogether that of a ticket or counter" reject all four of the alternatives he lists — intrinsic value, commodity, standard-and-measure, pledge — or is he rejecting the *combination* and quietly keeping one? What work is "as such" doing?
2. Test the two-role problem: is the notation claim and the stimulus claim genuinely in tension, and if the will is the hinge, say precisely how a counter that is "nothing but" a counter can move a will. Quote nothing; reason from the passage.
3. In query 25, is a denomination a notation *of* the proportion or a term *in* it? And what must hold for query 26 to be true — is the persistence of confidence sufficient, or does he also need the denominations to be backed by some stock of real goods?
4. Does "credit" in query 21 mean trust, transferable claims, or both, and does your answer make query 22's velocity–quantity equivalence plausible or merely rhetorical?
5. Use these three cases to separate the readings, and tell me which reading each one discriminates and what Berkeley is committed to: (i) a legislature re-scales every price, wage, debt and tax by the same factor overnight, leaving every real holding untouched; (ii) an inland people with no metal use engraved shells, accepted only because of a shared religious belief, the stock of shells fixed; (iii) a public bank issues notes denominated in hours of skilled labour, redeemable at public warehouses that are empty half the time.
6. Finally, say whether his immaterialism and his treatment of the sensible world as a system of signs does any real work here, or whether the money queries would look the same if written by someone who accepted matter.

Do not give me citations, a reading list, or background on immaterialism or eighteenth-century monetary debate that I could find anywhere; work from the passage and from the concepts. Give me your bottom line in the first sentence and the reasoning after it: no preamble, no restatement of my question, and tell me plainly where you think the text is simply underdetermined.
