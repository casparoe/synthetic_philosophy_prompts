You are a metaphysician who works on mereology and material constitution, and you are advising a graduate seminar that has just finished working through the framing of the special composition question. Before the seminar turns to scoring views, I want the specification. Begin by brainstorming the full space of things one could reasonably demand of an answer, then compress ruthlessly: I want the criteria and their motivating cases, not a survey.

Write the desiderata that any adequate answer must meet, where an answer says both what it is for the xs to compose something and how often it happens. Mark each desideratum as non-negotiable or as a prejudice of one camp. Requirements I already have in view and want you to adjudicate rather than merely list: it must distinguish composition from constitution and coincidence (the statue and its clay) and from identity over time; it must handle the problem of the many and the thinking-parts worry; it must take a stand on whether composition can be vague and on whether it can be extrinsic, depending on facts outside the xs and their intrinsic relations; it must say whether composites can be causally efficacious without every apparent case being one of systematic overdetermination; and it must say who or what is the tribunal for candidate composites — metaphysics, or the special sciences — so that the verdicts for molecules, organelles, organisms, ecosystems, and gravitationally bound systems either come out the way chemistry, biology, and astronomy talk or are explained away.

Two further cases I would add myself, which I want placed in the list rather than appended to it. First, the borrowed-activity case: the unifying activity that a life-based restrictivism appeals to can be partly sustained by something that is not alive but is bonded to the organism and indispensable to it — a permanently integrated synthetic organ, an extracorporeal circuit. Either such a thing falls inside the composite, in which case the organism's boundary has moved for reasons the theory did not advertise, or it falls outside, in which case the composite's unity depends on something it does not include and composition has turned out to be extrinsic and partly vicarious. Say which horn is worse and what this shows about whether a unifying relation can be had by proxy. Second, the near-miss problem: for any proposed condition there is a continuous series approaching it — bonding strength declining, vital activity fading, integration diminishing — with no salient discontinuity between the cases that satisfy it and those that do not. The theory must say whether composition is sharp but arbitrary, vague, degree-theoretic, or none of these, and it must argue rather than assume any stopping point for our ordinary judgments.

Then flag any pair of desiderata you believe cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement does the most exclusionary work against otherwise viable theories. Give the views themselves — universalism, nihilism, life-based restrictivism, brutal composition — no more than a few lines each. Close with \answer{...} containing that one requirement, in five words or fewer.
