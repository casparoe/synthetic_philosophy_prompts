last session of my collapse seminar is thursday and i want to close with a poem instead of a summary. write me a full sestina — six sestets and a three-line envoi — in which all six end-words are concrete things from the material record (ash, terraces, a tally of goats, whatever you pick), not abstractions like empire or ruin or time.

here's the idea i want the form to carry: the same six words come back in a different order every stanza, and nothing in the poem settles whether this is one poem that has changed or six poems that happen to share a vocabulary. that's the argument we've been stuck on all term about the Maya lowlands and the Aegean palaces — ended, or rearranged. so let the later stanzas get at the thought that much of what gets called collapse is our evidence running out: a society dropping below the level where it leaves the kinds of stuff we can read (the fire that destroyed the palace at Pylos is why we can read its accounting tablets at all), which is a fact about us, not about them. then the last three lines have to stop that thought from being a way out — population, long-distance trade, literacy genuinely did fall in some of these cases, and "it merely changed" can be a euphemism for something people suffered.

no name-dropping to do the work (no Diamond, no Tainter), and dont invent artifacts you aren't reasonably sure about — keep to things you know were really there. it should hold both pressures at once rather than picking one.
