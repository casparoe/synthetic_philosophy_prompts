Draft the joint statement that a defender of restricted luminosity and a margin-for-error anti-luminositarian could both sign at the end of a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the KK principle.

Treat the following as shared background rather than as up for argument. KK is best understood as the luminosity of the condition knowing that p, i.e. the claim that whenever one knows p one is in a position to know that one knows it. The anti-luminositarian's case proceeds by way of slow transitions — a room warming imperceptibly through the night, an ache fading minute by minute — together with the requirement that knowledge be robust across nearby cases the subject cannot discriminate, and concludes that no non-trivial condition is such that, whenever it obtains, the subject is in a position to know that it obtains, from which the failure of KK follows. The luminosity defender concedes that perceptual and other world-involving conditions are not luminous, and restricts the claim to a narrow class of occurrent phenomenal and doxastic conditions. Both parties further stipulate, for the purposes of the collaboration, that introspective confidence is well calibrated but never reaches certainty; that for every candidate luminous condition lying on a graded dimension the subject has a measured just-noticeable difference along that dimension; and that metacognitive sensitivity — the reliability with which confidence tracks accuracy — remains imperfect even for maximally confident reports.

Produce three sections. First, a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, including claims about what the anti-luminositarian argument actually establishes, about what a luminosity claim is committed to, and about which candidate conditions are live. Second, a table of residual disagreements, one row per crux, with columns for the crux; the claim as the luminosity defender would phrase it; the same in wording the anti-luminositarian would accept; the case, argument, or empirical finding both agree bears on it; and each side's prediction, recorded in advance, about how that bearing goes. Your cruxes should include at least: whether a safety requirement on knowledge strong enough to defeat luminosity also defeats ordinary perceptual knowledge; whether the transition arguments presuppose that adjacent cases are evidentially identical for the subject, and if so whether that premise is question-begging; whether the defender may retreat to categorially individuated, non-graded conditions, and whether what survives that retreat is non-vacuous and still does the work that self-knowledge was invoked to do; whether abandoning KK while retaining restricted luminosity is dialectically stable, and at which iteration of higher-order knowledge, if any, the breakdown occurs; and what the stipulated metacognitive record shows. Third, a short closing section stating what each side would count as a sufficient reason to abandon its own view, and flagging any part of the dispute you judge to be about words rather than about the subject matter — say which part, and why.
