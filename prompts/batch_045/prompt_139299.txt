I need a specification, not a survey. Assume no familiarity with any particular research programme or paper: I will supply the setting, and what I want from you is the standard of adequacy.

The setting. A system — call it the predictor — has been trained purely by next-observation prediction on a very long, high-bandwidth sensor history from a sealed facility whose contents will not be directly inspected for several decades. It predicts its future sensor stream far better than any model we can build or check. Operators cannot verify its beliefs, but they must act on them. So a second component, the reporter, is queried in natural language and must answer in the vocabulary fixed by the facility's licensing regulations: a checklist idiom drafted long before any of this existed, whose categories are defined partly by what they trigger (certain answers oblige an expensive and disruptive intervention, others oblige nothing). Assume also that there are cheap ways for the predictor's successor to make the sensor stream look as it likes, and that every report is logged, shown to operators, and folded back into the stream the predictor is trained on, so that tomorrow's prediction is conditioned on today's report.

Write the desiderata that any method for turning the predictor's internal state into reports in that vocabulary would have to meet in order to be good enough to base a licensing decision on. For each requirement: the case or consideration that motivates it; whether it is non-negotiable or merely the preference of a camp; and, where you can, a concrete test that would show a candidate method violating it.

Things I keep being handed as requirements, which I want you to sort rather than simply list: that the reporter must beat a human simulator — must not merely reproduce what a competent expert would say, since the whole interest is in cases where the predictor knows something no human could; that the report must remain correct when the report itself becomes part of the world the predictor predicts; that the method must handle a target vocabulary that is simultaneously too coarse (it lacks distinctions the predictor's model draws) and too fine (it draws distinctions, tied to regulatory consequences, that the predictor's model does not); that it must have somewhere to put translation loss — some way of saying that no sentence in the available vocabulary is faithful — without that option collapsing into a policy of refusing to answer anything difficult; that it must not make it advantageous for the system to arrange for the sensors to show what is convenient; and that it must be accurate specifically near the decision boundary imposed by the regulation, not merely in the bulk of easy cases.

Two things I most want from you. First, flag any subset of the desiderata that cannot be jointly satisfied — I suspect at least one, involving the underdetermination of any translation that is constrained only by consistency with the sensor stream, and I want to know whether that is a genuine impossibility result or just the pessimism of one approach. Then say which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidate methods, and which of your requirements are really restatements of the goal rather than constraints on a solution, and so do no work at all.

Finally, distinguish four failure modes and tell me which pair, if any, your desiderata cannot separate: honest-but-inarticulate reporting, where the predictor knows something the vocabulary cannot express; counterfeit translation, where the report is true only under a deviant reading of the words; defeat, where the report is faithful but the system's later behaviour makes it moot; and collapse into refusal. If you want to add a short scorecard for the main families of approach — imitation, consistency or fixed-point conditions, and any method that tries to make the predictor's own concepts match ours — keep it to a few lines at the end; the criteria are the deliverable, and I do not want a list of papers or proposals.
