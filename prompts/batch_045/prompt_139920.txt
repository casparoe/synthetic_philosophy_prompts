I'm putting in for a small one-semester fellowship (four months, deliverable is one paper of about 7000 words) and I need a short proposal, two pages max.

The half-formed idea: teleosemantic accounts get their grip from selection history, but a huge amount of the content we actually have — recognising a friend's face, a new word, the smell of a particular coffee — gets fixed inside the organism's lifetime, and I'm not convinced a short within-lifetime history of success does the same content-fixing work as a selection history does. I want to seperate two kinds of case: mappings that stick around because they produced success (I've been calling them sticky mappings) and mappings where something outside the system supplies an error signal that says what the mapping should have been (taught mappings). My suspicion is that taught mappings only borrow their content from whatever set up the error signal, the way a thermometer's readings are usually said to get their content from us — and if that's right then a lot of what looks like learning isn't content-conferring at all. But I'm not sure sticky mappings are in better shape, because the move of fixing content by what the downstream consumer needs may break when the detector and the consumer are learned together and neither has an independent function.

I've been using the contrast between nets trained by reinforcement and nets trained by supervised learning as the test case, since it makes the distinction concrete, but I don't want the paper to turn into a paper about machine learning.

What I want from the proposal: the question stated so it has a determinate yes-or-no-ish answer; why the standard etiological, informational and consumer-based versions and the usual hybrid moves don't already settle it; what I'd actually do month by month in one semester with no empirical work; what a positive result would look like (ideally a criterion for when a learning history confers content rather than transmitting it) and what a negative one would; and an honest section on failure — including whether the whole thing just collapses back into the general distality and indeterminacy worries, in which case there's nothing special about learning and the project isn't worth doing. Also please tell me if my sticky/taught split is just a redescription of a distinction that's already in the literature, because that would be its own kind of failure.
