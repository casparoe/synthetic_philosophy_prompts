I'm a second-year PhD student in philosophy of language, and I'm drafting the position statement on slurs that will open my dissertation prospectus. Before my committee takes it apart, I'd like to know whether the following package is jointly tenable.

Here is where I think I stand:

(1) A slur and its neutral counterpart make the same contribution to the truth conditions of what is said; the difference between them is not a difference in at-issue content.
(2) The derogatory component is not a conversational implicature: it is not cancellable by a speaker who says, in effect, that nothing derogatory is meant, and it survives embedding and verbatim quotation.
(3) Offense is not a function of the speaker's attitudes: the slur used without ill will still offends, while the neutral counterpart used with contempt does not offend in the same way.
(4) Who is speaking matters: uses by members of the targeted group can be genuinely non-derogatory, and this is more than a licensed exception to a norm that is still being broken.
(5) The offense is not merely an effect on hearers or on a public conversational record: the word is objectionable even in a diary entry, a silent thought, or a linguistic example read aloud in an empty office.
(6) A journalist who quotes the epithet verbatim in reporting what someone said does the right thing, and the resulting report is offensive.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and how badly; for each, say what would have to give and what that revision costs. Two things I already worry about, and I can't tell whether they are real: it looks as if (2)'s non-cancellability should force the derogatory component back into the semantics, against (1); and (3) and (4) seem to leave the offense attached to something that is neither an attitude nor a hearer nor a truth condition. Please also tell me if any of the tensions I've flagged dissolve on inspection, rank what's left by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and say which single revision would clean up the most.
