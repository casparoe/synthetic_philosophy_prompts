I teach a short unit on confirmation theory, and I would like a companion case to Hempel's ravens in which the paradox's sting runs in reverse: a single observation of something that is neither a raven nor black should confirm the hypothesis that all ravens are black by a larger amount than a single observation of a black raven does. My aim is to convince students that the standard Bayesian deflation of the paradox — that the non-black non-raven does confirm, but negligibly, because non-black objects vastly outnumber ravens — rests on a contingent fact about relative abundance rather than on anything logical, so the case has to earn the reversal rather than stipulate it, and the confirming observation should be one a student would concede is genuinely informative.

Please construct such a case, subject to the following constraints:

- One paragraph, set in an ordinary real-world context: field biology, a warehouse audit, manufacturing quality control. You may keep ravens and blackness if you can make the numbers realistic; otherwise substitute a hypothesis of the same logical form and say what it is.
- The population counts that do the work must fall out of the story, not be announced: the reader should see why the class named in the hypothesis is large while the complementary class is small, and the investigator should have rough, honest knowledge of both counts.
- The confirming observation must be produced by a procedure that could have turned up a member of the named class — random quadrats, a blind draw from inventory, a camera trap. If the sample comes from a pool the investigator already knows contains no such members, the confirmation collapses into Hempel's own point about tacit background knowledge and the case is worthless, so say explicitly why your procedure escapes that.
- No stipulating the verdict. Show the work: what Nicod's criterion and Hempel's equivalence condition jointly entail about the observation, and then the likelihood-ratio computation for both observations under a model you state openly — what the investigator knows, the hypothesis family, the prior, the sampling scheme — with the numbers worked through far enough that a student could check them.

Then tell me which structural feature of the case is doing the work, what happens to the comparison when that feature is removed and the ordinary abundance relation restored, and how someone who denies that non-black non-ravens ever confirm — whether by appealing to background knowledge or to some relevance or projectibility condition — would attack the case, and whether the attack lands.
