Trying to get my own view on AI art straight before a panel thing next week, and I want to know whether the package hangs together. Roughly where I've landed:

1. Whether something is art and how good it is depends entirely on the experience it gives a competent viewer; how it was made is irrelevant to the verdict. So an undetectable forgery is as good as the original, and worth just as much attention.
2. Generated images aren't art — there's nobody behind them with anything to express, nothing anyone meant by it.
3. Photography, collage and sampling are obviously art, because selection and arrangement are themselves the creative act.
4. Training on working artists' work without consent or pay is a real injustice and a good reason to regulate, but it's a labour-and-markets issue and tells us nothing about whether the output is art.
5. My guess at what actually matters: who did the searching — who went through the near-misses and could say why the survivor is better. Machines do that part now, and that's the loss.

Two cases I can't settle. A: a painter who lost the use of her hands after a stroke trains a model only on her own forty years of sketchbooks, then spends months specifying and rejecting outputs; the results look like a late period and are better than her earlier work. B: a friend typed one sentence into a general-purpose model, took the fourth of four, and I found it genuinely moving — then found out how it was made, felt a bit cheated, and couldnt get the feeling back.

Also, probably relevant: I think human creativity is fully caused and every artist works inside an inherited tradition, but I still want to say the model isn't creative in a way I dont want to say about a person.

Which of these actually pull against each other and which only look like they do? Rank the real tensions, say what I'd have to drop for each, and be decisive about which single commitment is least defensible rather than laying out the menu. Mark how confident you are in each call and flag the one you'd most expect someone to push back on.
