I'm in an upper-level philosophy of mind / cog sci seminar, and next Thursday I'm giving my 20-minute talk with 10 minutes of Q&A after. What I need is a prep sheet for the question period, not another pass at the talk itself — I've been staring at the talk for to long and I've lost all ability to predict the room.

The thesis: Dennett's ladder of kinds of creature (Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian — the four from Kinds of Minds) is missing a rung, and the missing rung is where a lot of the interesting action actually is. I call the missing one an "Archival creature". An Archival creature is a distributed system made of (1) agents none of whom individually needs to be smarter than Skinnerian — some can be pure Darwinian — plus (2) persistent external artifacts, plus (3) a population-level filter, arranged so that candidate hypotheses get generated, kept around, and killed off without ever being represented by any individual member of the system. The Popperian trick — let the bad move die instead of dying from it — gets performed by the system, and there is nobody inside who has the thought. My slogan: the hypothesis dies in the archive instead of in the head.

The argument, in three steps:
- Dennett's rungs are distinguished by where the culling of candidate actions happens: in the gene pool, in the individual's own lifetime, inside the individual's head before acting, and in a head that has been redesigned by inherited cultural tools.
- There's a fifth place it can happen: in a population of artifacts whose survival is keyed to downstream consequences, with the agents merely servicing the artifacts.
- So "who does the thinking?" has an answer that isn't a who. You can get the functional payoff of Popperian cognition without any component being Popperian, which is bad news for any taxonomy built out of individual creatures.

Four conditions I give for something to count as one of these: more candidate variants generated than adopted; candidates with a stable identity across the timescale of the test; culling that is sensitive to a downstream signal but implemented in the artifact rather than in any head; and survivors biasing what gets generated next.

Cases I use: pheromone trails in an ant colony (no ant represents "the food is north"; the trail network is the candidate, evaporation and reinforcement do the culling); branches in a large open-source repo with continuous integration and code review (nobody holds "this refactor is safe" as a hypothesis — the population of branches plus the test suite settles it); and, if I have time, registered reports and replication in a scientific field.

The room, about twelve people:
