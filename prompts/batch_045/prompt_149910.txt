I cover science and ideas for a newspaper, and I would like help reconstructing the implicit philosophy behind a set of forecasting practices I have been writing about.

Operational weather centres run one and the same model dozens of times over with slightly different starting states and publish the spread of the results as a probability: a 30 per cent chance that the city exceeds 35 degrees on Thursday, say. Inside those same runs they inject random noise into the model physics, described as standing in for processes too small to resolve. They insist that a forecast of the weather more than about two weeks out is worthless because the atmosphere is chaotic, while publishing confident statements about average temperature in 2080 and, increasingly, about how much more likely climate change made one particular storm. One centre I visited has begun labelling its own probability products: some are marked as coming from uncertainty about the starting state, others from the model noise, and it refuses to combine the two into a single headline number.

What view of chance, determinism and prediction does this bundle presuppose? Does publishing the spread commit these centres to a deterministic atmosphere, with probability entering only through ignorance of the initial state, or is the practice neutral between that and genuine indeterminism in the world? Does model noise commit them to a different kind of probability from initial-condition spread, and is the refusal to add the two together coherent, or does any usable forecast already have to treat them as one currency? What has to be true for the claim that one unrepeatable storm was made 20 per cent more likely to be well defined at all, given that the same institution calls individual storms unpredictable past two weeks? And is the two-week wall genuinely in tension with decades-ahead confidence, or does the bundle only need a distinction between predicting a trajectory and predicting a distribution? Please separate what the practices strictly presuppose from what they merely fit well with, and mark where the same practices would be licensed by more than one theory. Finally: if the centres abandoned the initial-condition story and treated the spread as randomness in the atmosphere itself, what would have to change in what they publish and in how forecasts are graded?
