I'm a PhD student in biostatistics — my dissertation is on spillover in cluster-randomised trials, so I basically live inside potential outcomes Y_i(a), consistency, SUTVA violations, partial identifiability, and the Rubin-vs-Pearl thing about whether counterfactuals are primitives you stipulate or outputs you compute from structural equations. Someone dragooned me into a reading group on Molinism and middle knowledge, and I keep having the feeling that the Molinists are doing something structurally just like what I do — but whenever I say "so middle knowledge is just the full potential-outcome schedule for every possible creature under every possible circumstance" the philosophers wince, and I cant tell whether the wince is substantive or just protective of the furniture.

So I want an actual translation, not a vibe. Do it term by term and be honest about where you have to cheat. Five things I want covered:

1. The three-fold distinction — the necessary stuff, the counterfactual middle stuff, and what's fixed only after the divine decree — in my vocabulary: what plays the role of the unit set, the treatment space, the schedule, and the realised assignment. Also tell me if "counterfactual of creaturely freedom" is the term of art I should be using.
2. Whether a counterfactual of freedom really is a potential outcome. My worry: in Pearl's setup Y_i(a) is pinned down by the structural equations, in Rubin's it's stipulated, and libertarian freedom seems to forbid both — no equations, and stipulation alone doesn't make it that creature's free act. Say which framework is the better home and why.
3. The grounding objection recast in my terms — and please don't let "we only ever observe one potential outcome" do any work here, since God's problem isn't missing data. Is the complaint that the schedule is undefined, or that it's defined but unanchored?
4. The bit I actually care about: interference. Circumstances contain other creatures' free acts, so the schedule can't be indexed to a single treatment — it needs the whole assignment vector, and that nests: what A does depends on what B does depends on what A does. Is there a fixed point anywhere in the standard picture, or does the literature silently assume no interference between creatures? Relatedly: since God also decides which creatures to create, the unit set itself varies with the assignment — doesn't the framework need counterfactuals of existence on top of counterfactuals of freedom?

The residue report: what has no expression at all in my vocabulary (my guesses would be the freedom bit, and whatever is going on with God being outside time and knowing things non-observationally, but tell me what I'm missing), and then judge whether that residue shows my framework being parochial or the Molinist claim being thinner than it looks.

Answer as exactly five numbered points, one or two long dense sentences each, with nothing before the first number or after the last.
