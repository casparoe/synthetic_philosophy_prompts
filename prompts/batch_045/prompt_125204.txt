I've taught the endurance/perdurance stuff maybe a dozen times and I still couldnt tell you what I myself think, so I want to try it the other way round. Here are my gut verdicts on a pile of cases; tell me what single account of persistence they add up to, which one has to go, and whether the endurance/perdurance dispute is doing any real work in any of them — because right now "it has temporal parts" feels like a redescription of "it persists" with extra machinery bolted on.

1. Ship of Theseus where the discarded planks are reassembled into a second working ship: the repaired ship in the harbour is Theseus' ship, the reassembled one isn't. I know that's arbitrary and I don't care.

2. One plank replaced a day, nothing else going on: same ship. Now imagine the replacement happening continuously — no discrete steps, at every instant only an infinitesimal bit of new matter: I still say same ship, and I also want to say there's no fact of the matter which midway stage is 'the ship' if you press me.

3. Statue and the clay it's made of: I can't bring myself to say two things are sitting on the pedestal, so the lump constitutes the statue and only the statue gets counted.

4. A lump of clay cut clean in half: the original lump is gone — not identical to either half, and not identical to both either.

5. Someone whose neurons are swapped out one at a time over decades for functionally identical prostheses, stream of consciousness never interrupted: same person, easy.

6. The same person annihilated and rebuilt atom-for-atom somewhere else during a dreamless night, with somehow no gap in the stream of consciousness: I find myself wanting to say same person — which I suspect is inconsistent with 5, if what's doing the work in 5 is continuity of matter.

7. Fission, both branches equally good: neither branch is the original and the original doesn't survive; but not because of some hidden further fact we're ignorant of — there's just nothing to say that isn't a decision.

8. Caterpillar into butterfly: same animal, I think. But I don't see why I then don't have to say the butterfly is a temporal part of the caterpillar, at which point I seem to have become a perdurantist without noticing.

9. A department that's turned over its members twice and moved buildings: same department, sort of — and I'm happy with 'sort of' here in a way I'm not in 1.

10. A conversation paused for a year and then resumed: same conversation. No idea why this one feels different from 4.

So: one story or several, and be straight with me about the part I keep suspecting — whether any verdict on these cases settles endurance versus perdurance at all, or whether that dispute is only about what persistence is made of and is compatible with every verdict above. Say which verdict you'd overturn, and give me what your account predicts for a case I haven't listed: a person whose hemispheres are separated into two bodies for a year and then grafted back together into one skull.
