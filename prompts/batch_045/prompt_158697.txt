hey — quick sanity check on an analogy I keep using. In my chapter on self-locating belief I lean on this move: Beauty's uncertainty is just like being a ball drawn at random from an urn containing all the possible awakenings, so there's nothing specially centered going on, you just count the balls and 1/3 falls out, and no new epistemology of self-location is needed. People in my reading group use it the same way, and a halfer in the department grumbles that the urn sneaks in a sampling process that isn't actually there.

Can you audit it? Give me the correspondence table — what's the urn, what are the balls, what's the draw, whose hand is doing the drawing, and what's doing the work of "random" — then the places the mapping breaks. The bit that worries me most is that in a real urn the contents are fixed in advance and independent of which ball you pull, whereas here how many balls there are depends on exactly the hypothesis I'm trying to get a credence in.

Here's the variant I can't get the analogy to give a stable answer on: heads gets you one awakening, placed on Monday or on Tuesday by a second fair coin flip, tails gets you both days, and you never learn the day. Counting awakening-types flat gives P(H) = 1/2; counting them weighted by how many awakenings the world contains gives 1/3. Which is the urn picture actually committed to, and does it matter that only one of the two heads awakening-types ever gets actualized? Then tell me which disanalogies carry weight for my "nothing exotic here" claim and which are harmless, whether the 1/3 survives, and whether there's a better analogy for the step where the size of the reference class depends on the hypothesis.
