I run the experimentation platform at a large two-sided marketplace. Every product change ships behind a controlled experiment: teams pre-register a hypothesis, a primary metric, and a horizon, ship against a fixed power calculation, and inherit a centrally owned set of guardrail metrics that they are not allowed to regress. I know this vocabulary cold: interference and violations of the no-interference assumption, switchback and cluster-randomized designs, novelty and primacy effects, corrections for repeated looks, the gap between a metric move and a ship decision, and the politics of it, which is that whoever owns the guardrails decides which consequences count as consequences at all.

I have also been reading Dewey on experimentalism: his account of inquiry as the transformation of an indeterminate situation into a resolved one, with ends-in-view revised in the course of inquiry rather than fixed ahead of it; his notion of a public as constituted by the indirect consequences that transactions between other parties have on people who did not choose them; his claim that democracy is a mode of associated living and not merely a machinery of government; and his disagreement with Lippmann about what expertise can and cannot do for such a public. I have read enough to know I am reading a position rather than a mood, but not enough to hold it steady on my own.

Please restate that position, epistemology and politics together, in the vocabulary I actually use: experiments, primary and guardrail metrics, statistical power and horizon, interference between units, ramp-up, rollback and reversibility, and platform-level governance of who may ship what and on what evidence. Be faithful rather than accommodating: I do not want his view quietly replaced by a falsificationist or Bayesian picture of testing that he would have rejected, and I do not want the political half of it dropped.

Then give me the residue report, in four labelled parts.

One: what translates cleanly, and where the mapping is more than metaphor.

Two: what translates only with distortion, and what exactly gets bent. I expect the public-to-affected-cohort mapping to be in here: we can identify affected parties by measuring spillover, but they do not constitute themselves by recognizing a shared consequence, and I want to know how much rides on that difference.

Three: what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. I have suspicions — the reconstruction of ends rather than the selection of a target, democracy as a quality of associated living rather than a decision procedure, the historical and non-stationary character of a situation as opposed to a fixed-horizon test — but I want your candidates, not just confirmation of mine.

Four: a verdict on a live case. We are piloting what we internally call an open-ended experiment: no pre-registered primary metric; the team may change which outcome it tracks as it learns what its change actually does; the readout is a written account of what the situation turned out to be, plus a decision. Is this an instance of inquiry in Dewey's sense, or is it undisciplined post hoc metric-shopping with better manners? Use the translation you just built to answer, and tell me what would have to be true of our organization for it to be the former.

Close by taking a side on the residue: is what fails to translate a defect of the vocabulary my team and I think in, or a sign that Dewey's claims are less determinate than they sound? Say which parts of the residue fall on which side, and what you would look at to tell the difference. Do not give me citations, reading suggestions, or a literature survey; I cannot check them and they are not what I am asking for.
