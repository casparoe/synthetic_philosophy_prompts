I want a map of the exposure of a position I've ended up holding after watching several years of argument about machine minds. Stated carefully, it has two parts.

First: a behavioral criterion of the Turing-test shape — an interrogator, a text channel, an open-ended exchange with no restrictions on topic, tone, or length — is a genuine, and for us the best available, sufficient condition for attributing mind to a system. I do not claim it is necessary. I claim that if a system passes it under demanding conditions, withholding the attribution is not epistemic modesty but a failure to update on evidence. In the idiom I trust most: the test is a likelihood-ratio machine, and that ratio is not close to 1.

Second, my own addition, made because I think the naive version is already dead: the ratio is not a fixed property of a test. It is a property of a system together with a population of judges, a time budget, topic constraints, and — above all — the cost, at that moment in history, of producing the relevant behavior by non-minded means. Call it the cheapness constraint. As generating fluent text gets cheap, the denominator of the ratio inflates and any fixed behavioral bar loses evidential force; a behavioral criterion has an evidential half-life. So I hold the criterion in a red-queen form: index the bar to what is currently cheap to fake, not to a fixed inventory of behaviors.

What I want is what would count against this package.

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and do it separately for each part. My guess is that the two parts have different exposures and that one of them is doing all the real work; I want to know which, and what happens to the package if only one of them survives.

Tell me which defeaters have arguably already arrived. We now have systems that many untrained judges cannot reliably distinguish from humans in short typed exchanges, and the visible effect has been not a wave of mind-attribution but a wave of re-specification: longer horizons, adversarial judges, no-memorization clauses, extra channels. I read that as a test being goodharted rather than refuted. Give me the strongest case that it is refutation — that the goalposts moved because the original claim never had content, and that my red-queen refinement in particular turns the view into consciousness-of-the-gaps, where mind just names whatever we have not yet mechanized. Then say whether that reduction is fatal, or whether there is a principled difference between a moving bar and a residual.

Give me the version of the objection list that actually bites against my formulation, not the summary version. I can produce the standard items myself — lookup tables and giant state machines, the other-minds parallel, the Chinese room, behavior without understanding — so tell me which of them have to be enriched, and how, to count against a criterion I have already restricted to sufficiency and indexed to a moving frontier. And for each: is a defender entitled to dismiss it, or is dismissing it just insulation? Please say plainly which is which.

Here is my declared crux, so you can tell me whether it is coherent. If a system passed a genuinely adversarial version of the test — judges skilled at spotting synthetic text, sustained exchange over months, real consequences for the judges, no topic restrictions — and the considered verdict of people who followed it closely was still that there is no mind there, I would treat the criterion as dead. If the verdict flipped only after such a pass, I would treat that as the criterion working, not as evidence that it was empty all along. Is that pair of reactions a coherent updating policy, or have I arranged things so that no outcome can move me? Relatedly: if indistingushability is achieved partly by exploiting judge psychology — sycophancy, register-matching, confabulated biography — is that a pass, or the signature of the goodharting itself?

Close with an honest verdict on whether this position risks anything at all, and if it does not, whether that is a defect in a view of this kind or simply what a well-specified criterion looks like. Argue against me in the strongest form you can; I would rather be moved than have the dialectic summarized.
