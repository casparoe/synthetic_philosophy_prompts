So I've been lurking around the rationalist blogs since the era when everyone was still posting about akrasia and picoeconomics, and I've got two friends who have been going at each other for years about whether weakness of the will is anything over and above hyperbolic discounting. One of them (call him the discounter) thinks the whole phenomenology of fighting yourself is just what a hyperbolic discount curve plus sub-personal bargaining between your successive temporal selves looks like from the inside, and that the fix is commitment devices, bright lines, bundling, stakes on Beeminder, etc. The other (call her the agency theorist) thinks that story covers one family of cases and misses the core one: at the moment of action the agent does what she judges worse, right there, says so at the time, and no discount curve by itself predicts that report. They've finally agreed to an adversarial collaboration and I offered to draft the document. Please write it.

Here's the bit I want to force them to chew on, and please treat it as stipulated data from a study: a paradigm I'll call the Zero-Cost Guardrail. At t0 subjects choose between $20 in 30 days (LL) and $10 now (SS); most pick LL. Before leaving they're shown a hold button that, if pressed at the moment of choice, disables the SS option for 90 seconds, costs nothing, delays nothing, and does not reduce the LL payoff; one press per trial. Most say at t0 that they want it pressed, and a solid majority will pay $1 of their own earnings to have it pressed automatically. At t1 about half don't press it. The debrief splits those non-pressers into two groups that barely overlap. The Faders: at t1 the SS option does not register to them as something to be avoided at all, it just looks like the obvious pick, and when the button is pressed for them automatically they mostly report relief and take the LL. The Strainers: at t1, while reaching for the SS button, they say things like yeah I know I shouldn't, I'm doing it anyway, and when the guardrail is applied automatically they report annoyance and sometimes try to work around it. Assume both groups pass comprehension checks, assume the split is stable across a week of repeated trials, assume neither group is better fit by a smooth exponential curve than the other, and assume the Strainers' will-I-regret-this report doesn't collapse under prompts designed to make people confabulate.

Draft the joint statement they could both sign. Section 1: numbered agreed claims — about the data, about what stands in need of explanation, about hyperbolic versus exponential discounting, about what would even count as an explanation of akrasia. Anything vacuous gets cut: if no one has ever denied it, sharpen it or drop it. Section 2: a table of the residual disagreements, one row each, with the discounter's formulation in his own preferred words and the agency theorist's formulation in hers, each worded so the person holding it would accept it as theirs, plus a column saying whether the row is substantive or a verbal dispute that dissolves under translation — and if you think part of it is verbal, say exactly which part and why.

Then the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of them. I suspect one crux is whether the Strainers' t1 self-report is evidence about the agent's actual judgement at t1 or is some kind of memory/self-prediction artifact, and another is whether the Faders are really a case of preference change or of something more like attention. For each crux give a test, case, or argument both of them would accept as bearing on it — behavioural or first-personal, runnable in a week, not more neuroimaging — and record in advance what each side predicts, including where each predicts their own account will look bad. If you think the Faders/Strainers split doesn't cut where I think it cuts, say that in one paragraph up front instead of smoothing it over, because these two will definately notice.

Last section, keep it short: both of them have to advise the same person — mid-thirties, works from home, keeps blowing up her own goal at 11pm. Where the two accounts converge, let them converge; where they'd actually recommend different things, say which recommendation follows from which account.

Markdown, numbered agreed claims first, then the table, then cruxes, then the practical bit. Somewhere around 900 to 1300 words, dense rather than padded, and please seperate the genuinely load-bearing claims from the throat-clearing.
