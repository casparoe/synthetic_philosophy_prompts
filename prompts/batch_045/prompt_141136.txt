I've been chewing on the old careenium setup — a box of substrate units (call them simmballs) that clump into configurations which, under a coding scheme, encode sentences, with the box coupled to the outside world so that its behavior depends on whether the encoded sentence is true out there — and I want to know whether my position on it hangs together before I post it. Roughly where I've landed:

(1) Causal closure: nothing non-physical pushes anything around. For any physical event, the fundamental physical facts plus the laws settle what happens next, and nothing else adds oomph.

(2) The higher levels are still where the real causal stories live: this cluster shoved that cluster, the blizzard caused the jam. Those claims are literally true and are usually the only explanations worth having; fundamentality isn't the same as causal or explanatory relevance, and it's just physics underneath doesn't make the macro story false.

(3) The careenium generalizes, and that's the interesting part: what makes the substrate move there is the content — the arrangement reacts to whether the sentence it encodes is true of the world outside, not merely to its own shape. Same for me: I walk to the kitchen because of what my belief means, not just because of the shape of the neural configuration. So symbols do push the substrate around, and that isn't a façon de parler.

(4) Meaning isn't in the territory: no fundamental aboutness, no truth-property in the world's inventory, map and territory firmly distinct. I'd cash out a state's having content as its standing in the right informational or correlational relation to whatever it's about.

(5) A property earns causal relevance only by making a difference: no difference-making, no causal work.

The case I keep tripping over is one I made up: take two slabs inside the box in identical internal configurations, one coupled to a world where the encoded sentence is true and one to a world where it's false. If the slabs behave the same, content can't be what's pushing; if they behave differently, the difference looks like it lives in the coupling rather than in the semantics. I genuinely can't tell whether (1) plus (2) defuses this or whether (2) is exactly the thing I'm not entitled to extend from simmballs to symbols.

Please audit the set: which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like it, and what would have to give. Commit to a verdict — name the commitment that has to go and defend that choice rather than laying out the menu.
