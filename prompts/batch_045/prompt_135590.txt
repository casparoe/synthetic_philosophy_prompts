hey! i run the philosophy club at my school (were all 16 or 17) and weve been argueing about personal identity for about six weeks. next week were doing a fake end-of-term exam for each other, each of us sets one question, and i got given origins — the view that a person couldnt have come into existence from a different sperm and egg than the pair they actually came from, no matter how similar the resulting person would of been and no matter how much better their life went. i need you to write the question, a model answer, and a marking scheme.

couple of rules first. our club has one real rule, which is that answers have to stand on their own arguments, so please dont name any philosopher or paper or book anywhere in your reply, dont cite anything, dont give a reading list, and dont tell us to go look something up. also please no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables and no numbered lists in your reply — just continuous prose like an email, because im pasting it into a doc and formatting it myself. and dont let the model answer burn its first half explaining what the view is: i want the question built so that reciting the definition is worth almost nothing and all the marks are in what you do with it.

the question should be doable in 45 minutes and marked out of 30, with a model answer of roughly 700 to 800 words. heres the shape i want, but please phrase it better than i can. part one: give the strongest argument you can for the view that isnt just "obviously i wouldnt be me", and the argument should be the kind that would work just as well for an ordinary object like a wooden table made from a particular block of wood — then say whether the fact that the argument works for tables is a point in its favour or a reason to be suspicious of it. part two: three cases, and for each say what the view implies and whether you think that implication is ok. case one, exactly the same sperm and the same egg and the same parents, but conception happens a week later in a different city. case two, the same sperm and the same egg, but the zygote splits in the first few days and two babies are born. case three, a different sperm — one that differs from the actual one by a single base pair, in a bit of dna that makes no difference at all to the person who results. part three is where the real marks are: cases one and three seem to pull in opposite directions, so which features of someones origin does your account treat as essential, what principled line separates them, and is that line sharp? if it is sharp, take a chain of cases running from the actual sperm all the way to a sperm from a completely different man, one base pair at a time, with a slightly different person at every step, and name the step where you stop saying "still you" and say why that step and not the one before it. if there is no fact of the matter where the line falls, explain what that does to the argument you gave in part one. and finish by saying what kind of case would actually refute your line rather than just make it uncomfortable, and whether any of the three cases is one.

then the marking scheme, in continuous prose: how many of the 30 marks each part is worth, the specific move a top answer has to make for each bit, the one mistake at each bit that should cost marks, and a sentence on what separates an answer that gets about 27 from one that gets about 21 — i want that difference to be a move someone makes, not just more detail or bigger words. keep it readable for 16 year olds who are into this stuff but dont have any jargon yet, and please dont dumb the philosophy down to get there.
