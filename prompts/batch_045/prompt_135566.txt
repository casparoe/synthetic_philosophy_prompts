Ended up in a three hour fight with my friend about free will last night and I honestly can't tell if she won the argument or just changed the subject. Her version: Strawson basically closed the whole debate — the reactive attitudes (resentment, gratitude, guilt, that whole family) aren't something we can switch off even if we try, the objective stance isn't available to us as a general policy, so determinism gives us no reason to stop holding people responsible and the skeptic has nothing left to worry about. My worry is that this feels like a bait and switch: "you will never in fact stop reacting" is a claim about human psychology, not an answer to whether anyone actually deserves blame — and if it really were psychological all the way down, then someone who through years of training (she mentioned some "equanimity protocol" they're trialing on anxiety patients) could genuinely turn off resentment toward anyone at will would have discovered that nobody is responsible, which definetly can't be right. We also deadlocked on whether the reactive attitudes are just feelings that happen to us or the kind of thing that can be fitting or unfitting; I took the first, she took the second. Where does that leave Strawson's argument: does the "we can't help it" move actually answer the skeptic or just dissolve the question, and does my example about the trained person show that the argument is secretly an empirical one?
