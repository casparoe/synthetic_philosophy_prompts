Build me a commitment race to spec. (By that I mean: two parties, each with a reason to irrevocably lock themselves in before the other does, where the damage comes from the racing itself.) Constraints:

- Ordinary setting, one paragraph — a workplace, a family firm, a research group. No states, no armies, no sci-fi.
- Both agents fully rational, everything common knowledge. No hyperbolic discounting, no akrasia, no panic. If anyone has to be irrational to make the race happen, the case has failed.
- It must not be a Chicken structure. They want essentially the same outcome and their commitments are mutually compatible; the only thing they're really competing over is a small private stake in who gets to make the call. So the damage can't come from deadlock or from the two commitments colliding.
- The loss has to be concretely premature foreclosure: there's a piece of information arriving at a known later date, both sides agree it would be better to wait for it, and picking blind is a real, specifiable loss in the story — not just a number you assert.
- Required verdicts: (1) the unique equilibrium has both racing to commit at the first opportunity (and tell me what the loser of the race does after losing); (2) both are strictly worse off there than in a world where neither had any way to commit; (3) quietly loosing the ability to commit is strictly worse for the agent who does it than racing. To stop (2) from being rigged, each side's private stake in being the decider has to be genuinely smaller than their share of the loss from deciding uninformed — say so explicitly and show it holds in the numbers of your case.
