Hi there — could you help me knock a half-baked idea into a fellowship proposal? The setup: my college gives a one-semester course release plus a little travel money, no RA, and the deliverable is one long paper (8–10k words) that I'd present at a spring workshop, possibly co-authored with my PhD student. No bibliography needed, I'll drop in citations myself later. The area is integrated information theory, and I want to stay down in the machinery of the theory rather than re-run the usual shouting matches about whether it's panpsychist or whether it's falsifiable in principle — I really don't want to produce Yet Another Paper arguing that IIT implies something spooky about logic gates.

So here's the thing I can't get out of my head. Take IIT in the rough shape where you take a system of elements, evaluate Φ over subsets of them, and declare the conscious subject to be whichever subset maximises it, with supersets, subsets and overlapping sets all excluded — I know the details shift between the 3.0-ish and 4.0-ish formulations, but the argmax-plus-exclusion structure is what I care about, and I want the proposal to be robust to those shifts. Now picture two systems A and B that are each individually high-Φ, and add a coupling knob: a parameter c that continuously ramps the strength of the interactions between them, from 0 (two sealed boxes side by side) to 1 (one thoroughly fused system of twice the elements, behaving like a single network). Somewhere along that ramp the union must overtake the parts, and then IIT seems committed to two subjects discontinuously becoming one; worse, right around the crossover the argmax could flip back and forth under ordinary noise, so that *what* is a subject flickers from moment to moment, and it also looks like you could get a subject that pops into existence for 50 ms and vanishes. My half-formed move is to replace the hard argmax with a graded rule: score every candidate subset by something like an exponentially weighted exp(Φ/T) and normalise, so that near the crossover you get a genuine, continuous 'mostly-union / partly-parts' description instead of an all-or-nothing jump — call it soft exclusion, or smoothed Φ, the name doesn't matter. What I can't tell is whether that is a real advance or just a way of hiding the problem in a free temperature parameter, and whether 'degrees of being a subject' is a coherent thing for a theory like this to traffic in at all. I got into an argument about this with a friend in neuro who works on callosotomy patients and thinks the whole ramp case is empirically empty, and another in ML who pointed out that swapping argmax for soft-max is exactly what you do when a hard maximum makes a loss landscape nasty — suggestive, but I don't think it settles the conceptual question.

What I'd like from you is the proposal itself, in a form I'd be willing to send: a precise statement of the research question that has some determinate answer; why the existing ways of handling the boundary of a complex leave that question open (and, honestly, why my angle isn't just the hard problem of combination wearing a different hat); what I'd actually do with the semester, month by month, including how I'd argue about the case without running any simulations or collecting data, since this has to be a philosophical project that makes progress on its own; what the two or three possible conclusions are and what considerations would decide between them; and an honest paragraph on how the project could fail — e.g. it turns out the soft-max version collapses back into the argmax case, or the whole thing was never about Φ specifically but about any theory that picks out a maximally integrated structure. Wether you think the flickering worry is a reductio or a prediction I should embrace is up to you to argue in the proposal, but do take a stand rather than listing options.
