My thesis: the standard suffering-focused advice to swap beef for chicken may be backwards, or at best a coin flip. Sketch: (1) per calorie, chicken is definately worse on farmed animals — many more individuals per kilo of meat, and plausibly worse lives per day lived; the room already grants me this. (2) But beef needs far more land and far more feed crop per calorie, so the animals killed by cultivation and displaced by habitat conversion, per calorie, are an order of magnitude bigger for beef — and deaths per hectare per year is exactly the kind of small number that gets large fast. (3) Whether (2) actually cancels (1) turns on two things nobody has settled: whether those wild lives are net positive or net negative, and whether animals killed by a harvester or pushed out by ploughing are really additional deaths, or whether density-dependent breeding just replaces them within a season. So the honest upshot is that we don't know, not that everyone should eat chicken.
