here's the shape of the argument i want to make: even if your step-level grader is genuinely *good* — accurate, correctly flagging steps that really are bad by any local standard, nobody is fooling it, the raters aren't rushed or underpaid or working from a rubric that's obviously wrong — process supervision can still lose to outcome supervision, because the grader only ever sees the steps up to the current one (it has to be prefix-conditioned, that's what makes step-level credit assignment possible at all), so it can only reward steps that are endorsable *right there*, while the strategy you actually want the policy to learn has to pass through waypoints that look bad in isolation. i've started calling this the endorsability bottleneck: the set of trajectories every prefix of which is locally endorsable is a strict subset of the trajectories that are optimal at the horizon you actually care about. that's the crux i want to make vivid, so build me a case for it.
