I'm a 4th year PhD student in political theory and I'm TAing the undergrad toleration seminar for the third time — every year every student says they "accept the paradox of tolerance" and means something totally different by it, so I want to hand them a sorting quiz instead of another essay prompt.

Build me a nine-question "which position on tolerating the intolerant do you actually hold?" quiz. Rules I care about:

- No question may name a theorist, an -ism, or a work. Each question is a concrete case with two or three options, and every option is an action or a verdict (ban the rallies, let them speak but block the amendment, do nothing until someone gets hurt), never a label.
- Build the tree from the logical space, not from a list of names. My guess at the branching questions: whether tolerance is a constraint on coercion or a virtue of persons and associations (or both); whether the "paradox" is a self-defeating-concept problem or an empirical threshold problem about the intolerant gaining capacity; whether it's doctrine, conduct, or organisational capacity that forfeits a claim to be tolerated; preemptive vs reactive suppression; whether the power asymmetry between the group and the state matters; how much weight to give the risk that whoever decides will mislabel ordinary dissent as intolerance. Re-cut these if you think I've got the wrong distinctions.
- Leaves I need: the mid-century "unlimited tolerance destroys itself" view; the constitutional / militant-democracy view that tolerates the intolerant and steps in only when the free order itself is at risk; the Marcuse-ish view where existing tolerance just reproduces domination and so "tolerance" is the wrong target; the absolutist no-limits view; the modus-vivendi view; plus two of my own — "no jurisdiction" (the state may suppress harm and organising, but has no warrant to suppress a doctrine as such, however intolerant) and my threshold idea (toleration is conditional cooperation with a capacity threshold, so the duty to tolerate the intolerant is undefeated until they cross it, and it's the crossing rather than the doctrine that does the work). Tell me honestly if either of my two just collapses into one of the others.
- Use at least these cases: a party at 12% that openly pledges to end judicial review by legal amendment; a religious community that teaches its daughters not to vote and breaks no law; a group that is intolerant in doctrine and individually harmless, where the state could only identify members by a belief test; a student society that wants a platform and has zero capacity to implement anything; a case where the people doing the suppressing have a track record of calling dissent "intolerance."
- Scoring key: answer patterns to leaf, with ambiguous patterns flagged as ambiguous rather than forced. Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't seperate and say why.
- For answer-combinations nobody occupies, say whether that's incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unwritten.
- Finally: which question do you expect to mis-sort people most often, and what would change your mind about the tree — which argument would make you redraw a branch rather than defend it?
