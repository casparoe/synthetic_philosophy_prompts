I've been reading my way into the democratic-theory literature on the boundary problem for a few months now — partly general curiosity, partly because a group of us (the same crowd I do the annual calibration meetup with) have been drafting a governance charter for a small intentional community of the charter-city/seastead flavor, and we stalled at the first clause: who gets a vote. Everyone at the table had a different principle, each sounded obvious in isolation, and each broke within five minutes of stress-testing. So before we argue about which principle to adopt, I want the specification. What would a principle of demos-membership have to deliver to count as adequate for a purpose like ours — a franchise rule that has to be written down in advance, applied by whoever is staffing the registration table, and stable enough that the people it enfranchises can then legitimately amend it? Give me the requirement list with the case or consideration behind each: the cases it has to handle (young children, long-term non-citizen residents, citizens who live abroad, temporary migrant workers, prisoners, people downstream of the community's emissions or land-use decisions, people who don't exist yet), and the distinctions it isn't allowed to collapse — having a say versus how much say, being affected versus being coerced versus being legally subject, the demos for one decision versus the demos that authorizes the constitution, de jure membership versus de facto causal impact.

Some requirements I want treated explicitly, because they're where our draft kept failing. First, administrative legibility: a clerk with bounded information has to be able to determine membership on a Tuesday morning from documents, not from a counterfactual model of how every decision might have set back every person's projects; say how demanding this really is and where it bites. Second, non-explosiveness: I keep running into the argument that any all-affected principle quietly becomes a principle about everyone subject to coercion, and therefore about essentially every human being, which would make the clause useless for us — is that a genuine desideratum or just a prejudice of statists who already have a demos? Third, non-circularity: the membership rule can't be defined via decisions made by the very body whose membership is in question, or via which decision is on the agenda. Fourth, and this is the one I most want your view on, fixed-point stability for outcome-dependent membership: we toyed with a clause enfranchising anyone whose ongoing projects would be set back by the decision under consideration — call it the itinerant demos — and noticed two failure modes, namely that the winners of a vote can thereby determine who was eligible to vote in it, and that a faction can gerrymander the demos by proposing policies calibrated to affect its opponents. I'd like weaponized-affectedness as its own desideratum rather than a footnote.

Then the meta-questions, which matter more to me than the scoring. Which of these requirements are genuine desiderata and which are just the commitments of one camp dressed up as constraints — for instance, is it a requirement or a preference that the demos be a standing body rather than a per-decision one, and is everyone subject to coercion gets a say an axiom or a theorem? Flag any pair you think cannot be jointly satisfied; my suspicion is that full counterfactual affectedness-sensitivity and same-day administrative legibility are incompatible, and that non-circularity plus completeness may be too, in which case the list itself is the interesting result rather than any theory's score on it. And tell me which single requirement does the most work in knocking out otherwise-popular candidates. Only then, briefly — a table or a paragraph each — score the standard families: all-affected-interests, all-subjected-to-coercion, subjection-to-law or membership-based views, and, if it deserves to be in the race, something proceduralist or fixed-point-flavored. Keep the weight on the criteria and the cases behind them; the scorecard is for my own notes, and our next session is on the 14th, so assume I have some background but no library access this week.
