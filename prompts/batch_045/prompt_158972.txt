Philanthropic funders in the EA world score causes on importance × tractability × neglectedness and multiply the three. What I can't square is how they actually behave: they'll shovel money into a cause that already has billions and thousands of researchers on it (AI risk, pandemic prep) the moment they decide it's important, yet they'll pass on a grant on the grounds that somebody else would have funded it anyway. And when they do score neglectedness they count dollars in and researcher headcounts, not how sharply returns diminish or whether their own next million makes any difference.

So what does the three-factor framework actually presuppose? Is neglectedness about diminishing marginal returns, about counterfactual difference-making, or about something closer to credit — who gets to claim the win? Are those even the same quantity? Does the multiplication double-count something that's already inside tractability? And what would actually change if you dropped neglectedness as its own factor and just did cost-effectiveness with a diminishing-returns curve, apart from the framework being less catchy?
