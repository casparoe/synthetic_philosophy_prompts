I build probabilistic state estimators for autonomous systems, so my working vocabulary is priors, observation models and likelihoods, measurement updates, importance weights and particle filters, minimum-KL projections onto constraint sets, and the order in which updates are applied. I have been reading about Jeffrey conditionalization — the proposed rule for responding to evidence you do not become certain of — and I would like it translated into that vocabulary, together with an honest account of what the translation fails to carry.

Here is my own attempt, which I want you to correct or sharpen rather than ratify. As I read it, a Jeffrey update on a partition is just an information projection: keep the prior's within-cell ratios fixed and reweight the cells so the posterior cell marginals take the new values. The rival I actually use is the explicit-observation-model route, where an uncertain reading enters as a likelihood and ordinary Bayes' rule does all the work, so that no posterior marginal is ever imposed by hand. Please take it from there, and cover four things: what in the philosophical dispute translates cleanly onto these two operations; what translates only with distortion; what has no expression at all in my vocabulary — I suspect something about justification, and about what makes a constraint normative rather than merely imposed; and finally whether the residue is a blind spot of the filtering framework or a sign that the philosophers' question is thinner than it looks. Two pieces of the dispute I especially want included: the rigidity condition, and the objection that soft evidence can always be reconstrued as hard evidence about some higher-order proposition, such as the reliability of the reading, so that Bayes' rule was sufficient all along.

One case I would like handled concretely. When the uncertain evidence concerns overlapping rather than disjoint propositions, Jeffrey updates are order-dependent; in my world that is a defect I engineer around. Tell me whether it is the same fact in both frameworks, and whether my fix — refuse imposed marginals, keep explicit observation models — quietly answers a philosophical question or merely declines it.
