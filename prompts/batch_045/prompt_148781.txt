ok so I'm in a third-year seminar on social kinds (we've done race, gender and money so far) and for the last session each of us has to hand in a one-page "here is roughly where I stand" sheet that the rest of the group gets to attack for half an hour, then a revised version a week later. I've been staring at mine for two days and I have a feeling two or three of these can't all be true at once, but I can't work out which ones. So: does this hang together?

1. Race is socially constructed — there's no biological race — but race is real in the way money is real: a real kind, causally held in place by what we do, not an illusion or a mistake people are making.
2. We should keep collecting and using racial categories in medicine, public health and discrimination monitoring. It tracks exposure to racism better than any proxy we've actually got (class, postcode, parents' education), and dropping the boxes would just make the disparities invisible.
3. Gender is constructed in the same sense. And when someone says what their gender is, that settles it: self-identification is authoritative, not merely evidence.
4. Sex is constructed too, at least partly — which traits we sort people by and how many boxes there are is up to us.
5. Even so, when doctors surgically assign a sex to intersex infants, I want to say they often get it wrong — not just that they're being coercive or premature, but that they're mistaken about the child.
6. In the long run we should get rid of the race category entirely; a just society wouldn't need it. But right now scrapping it would hurt the people it picks out.
7. Money is my model for all of the above: something is money because we collectively treat it as money, and that's the whole story. There are facts about what is money, they're just not mind-independent facts.

Does race still exist there, on my account, or did it go out of existence? Which of my commitments is even supposed to answer that?
