I teach an upper-level seminar on the philosophy of physics, and I am drafting this year's final take-home. The last third of the course was devoted to the thermodynamics of computation: the one-molecule engine, the thesis that information is physical, and the standard exorcism in which the demon is charged a fixed thermodynamic price per bit for acquiring and then disposing of the result of each cycle, so that the second law survives intact. Every student can reproduce that narrative, and I want a question that makes reproducing it useless.

Please write for me: the question itself, as it would appear on the exam; a model answer of roughly 700 words; and a marking scheme out of 25.

Build the question around a case I will call the heirloom demon. Describe it in full in the question text, so that the question is self-contained and closed-book: a gas in a container at equilibrium at temperature T; a demon running the usual cycle of inserting a partition, exploiting knowledge of which side the molecule is on, and extracting work as it expands against a piston. Then the variant: there exists, as a brute and unexplained initial condition, a register of N bits, each of which is already perfectly correlated with which side the molecule will occupy on the corresponding cycle. The demon performs no measurement, prepares nothing, and erases nothing; it simply consults the next bit of a register whose correlations with the gas were given at the outset, and operates the piston accordingly.

The crux I want the question to force is this. Something is given for free in the heirloom case, and something is given for free in the standard case too: a supply of blank memory, an apparatus in a ready state, a demon with an uncorrupted memory, a correlation between record and gas. So the question is whether the difference between the two demons is one of kind or merely of staging. If it is merely of staging, then every exorcism works by locating an assumed low-entropy resource, and the student must say whether that vindicates the information-theoretic account or hollows it out: does the accounting in bits do any work that a plain thermodynamic audit of resources would not do, or is it an illuminating redescription of a constraint we could have stated without it? If it is a difference of kind, the student must say what the extra ingredient is and why measurement or erasure, as opposed to mere correlation, carries a thermodynamic price.

I also want the question to make the student take a stand on whether the price-per-bit principle is an independent physical premise or a corollary of the second law itself. Ask what would count as a non-question-begging derivation of it, and ask what follows for the exorcism if it turns out to be a corollary: is the argument then circular, and if so is the circularity vicious, or does the second law survive by some other route? Do not let the student settle this by naming positions or citing literature; the question should be answerable, and gradeable, by someone who has never read a word of the secondary literature, and no mark should depend on knowing who argued what. Elementary bookkeeping in terms of Shannon entropy and the constant kT ln 2 is fine; anything beyond that should not be required.

For the marking scheme, give five must-hit points with the marks each is worth, totalling 25, and for each point a one-line note on the characteristic way students get it wrong — for instance, debiting a cost to the demon without identifying which physical system's entropy is supposed to fall, treating information as a stuff that gets converted into work, or answering the heirloom case by stipulating that the register must have cost something to prepare when that is precisely what is in dispute. Include a note on what separates an A from a B: my intention is that the A answer uses the heirloom case to reach a verdict about the standard case, while the B answer treats the two side by side and never draws the moral. The scheme should also say how to credit a rigorously argued answer that reaches the opposite verdict to the model answer's, since I would rather reward the argument than the conclusion.

Finally, after the marking scheme, add about 100 words sketching the answer a well-prepared but uncomprehending student would give: the fluent, correct-sounding, entirely derivative response that this question is designed to make unprofitable.
