I help run a monthly philosophy discussion night at my local library — mostly retirees, a couple of undergrads, and one guy who always brings a whiteboard — and last month we did "why is there something rather than nothing?" and it kind of fell apart: within ten minutes everyone had just announced their favourite -ism and then talked past each other for an hour. So for the next one I want to hand people an actual instrument instead of a topic, and I'd rather it sort them by what they say about cases than by which label they like.

What I'd like is a decision tree that's built from the logical space first and only then has the positions dropped into it. Give me 8–10 branch nodes ending in 7–9 leaves. Every node should be a plain question about a concrete case that someone who has never read a word of metaphysics could answer, and none of them should mention the name of a view. Please use these cases: a universe of completely empty space; a world with physical laws and a quantum vacuum but no objects; a world where nothing concrete exists but mathematics still does; a universe that flickers into existence for a single instant and then stops; a world containing only God; the subtraction case (remove things one at a time until none remain — is the endpoint even coherent, and if not, what does that tell you?); and a world where every possible universe is realized. Invent two or three more of your own. The separating questions I most want doing the work are: how much does your "nothing" contain — does it include space, laws, modal facts, abstract objects, or just no things; whether the target is the thin question (why anything at all) or the thick one (why this, with these laws); whether an explanation of the whole has to explain its own existence too — I've been calling this the invoice problem, that the answer has to pay for itself — or whether the answer is allowed to be a free lunch; and whether being told "it's necessary that something exists" feels like an answer or like a redescription of the puzzle.

The leaves need to include a brute-fact leaf, a necessitarianism-about-the-whole leaf, a transcendent-necessary-explainer leaf, a self-explanation leaf, an infinite-regress leaf, a "nothing couldn't have been / nothing is unstable" leaf, and a leaf for people who think the question dissolves. Name a leaf where the view has a name and write "unnamed" where it doesn't, with one sentence on each leaf's most awkward commitment. Also: mark the combinations of answers nobody actually occupies, with a one-line diagnosis of why (incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored), and flag any two leaves your tree can't genuinely pull apart rather than pretending the branch works — I have a feeling brute-fact and question-dissolving are going to collapse into each other. Since people in the room won't necessarily walk my branches in my order, add a short scoring key at the end mapping answer patterns onto leaves. Keep the whole thing to a page or two, and no reading list or citations — half the room doesn't have internet and wouldn't follow up anyway.
