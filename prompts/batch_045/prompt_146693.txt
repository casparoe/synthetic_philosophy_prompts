Me and my mate got into a stupidly long argument last night (beers were involved) about whether his motorbike is literally the same motorbike he bought in 2004. Every single part of it has now been swapped out at least once, frame included. He's a hardcore perdurantist: the bike is a four-dimensional spacetime worm, its 2004 bit and its 2025 bit are different temporal parts of one thing, and when we say the bike is bent we are really saying that some temporal part of it is bent. I'm on the endurantist side: the bike is wholly present at each moment at which it exists, and persistence over time is just plain numerical identity, no parts-at-times involved. It went in circles for about three hours, mostly because we kept restating our own view louder. One thing we did clear up: he's an eternalist and so am I, so please dont let this turn into a presentism-vs-eternalism fight. Assume a B-theoretic block universe and argue inside it.

What I want is the document we should have written instead. Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a three-dimensionalist (eternalist endurantist) and a four-dimensionalist (perdurantist) about what persistence consists in.

Start with a numbered agreed-claims section: the claims both of them can sign without feeling tricked. I'd expect things like: that there really are persisting macroscopic objects and that persistence is not mere resemblance; that change is real and not an illusion either way; that saying the bike survived does not commit either of them to saying the 2004 bike and the 2025 bike are identical *simpliciter* without qualification; that the debate is not settled just by ordinary language; that a four-dimensionalist is not committed to saying the bike is "really" a bunch of unrelated momentary things. Add whatever else genuinely survives from both sides, but keep it non-vacuous — if a claim is one no one has ever denied, leave it out.

Then a disagreements table, where each row is one question and you give the endurantist's own preferred formulation and the perdurantist's own, each worded so that side would actually accept it as their view. Include at least the constitutive question (what makes it true that the 2025 bike is the same bike as the 2004 one, and whether asking for a truth-maker here is legitimate at all), the question of what the relata of temporary properties are, and what persistence has to do with identity.

Then the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of us. Here are the ones I want covered, though add any you think matter more:

- The problem of temporary intrinsics: can either side give an account on which the bike's being bent is an intrinsic matter, and does the four-dimensionalist's answer actually work or does it relocate the problem?
- The Twin: in 2012 he rebuilt all the old parts into a second complete bike and rode both. What is the relation between the two bikes, and does a four-dimensionalist have an easier time here than she actually admits?
- The crate year: the bike spent 2011 as a crate of labelled parts, and if he'd never reassembled it, it would never have been a bike again. How does each side handle an object that goes out of and comes back into existence, or denies that it did?
- Whether the relativity of simultaneity in special relativity is real evidence for the four-dimensionalist, or whether it's a red herring that only looks like an argument if you already accept her ontology.
- My own pet case, which I doubt either of us handled well: suppose physics delivers a minimum temporal interval — call it a tick — such that there are no shorter times, nothing has temporal parts shorter than a tick, and yet things change *within* a tick. Does four-dimensionalism survive with only "chunky" temporal parts, or does it collapse into something the endurantist already believes? And does the same chunkiness cause trouble for the endurantist's claim that the object is wholly present?

For each crux, give a case, argument or observation both of them would accept as bearing on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts it will show. Also: what would each of them count as a real argument against their *own* view, and is there anything at all that both agree would genuinely move this debate rather than just re-describe it?

Two more things. If you think part of our dispute is merely verbal, say exactly which part and why — I suspect the fight about whether persistence is "identity" is definately partly about words, and I want to know where the real residual disagreement is after you strip that away. And dont give me a reading list or point me at papers: argue it out from scratch, define any jargon you use, and make both parties equally competent, not one of them a cartoon the other one gets to knock over.
