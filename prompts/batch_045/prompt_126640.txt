I'm TAing a world history survey this term and I keep tripping over how the course is built. The syllabus is chopped into chunks with European-derived labels — "the medieval world", "early modern", "the age of revolutions", "industrialization" — and then each chunk covers China, the Islamic world, West Africa, the Americas, whatever, inside that frame. But the same lectures insist these places weren't just waiting around for Europe: there's a whole session on Mongol-era connections across Eurasia, another on Indian Ocean trade, and the prof is at pains to say that before 1800 or so Europe wasn't obviously the centre of anything.

So what does this way of carving up the past actually presuppose about what a historical period is? Does it quietly assume time is one shared container with Europe holding the clock, so that a Chinese dynasty gets filed under somebody else's century — or is it defensible as a pure filing convention that says nothing about what caused what? If it really is just a convention, what does it cost us, i.e. what kinds of questions become hard to even ask? And a friend suggested the opposite fix: give every region its own period names and its own dates and never line them up on one timeline at all — does that setup smuggle in something equally substantive in the other direction? I'd like to know what each arrangement is committed to, and where each is just underdetermined.
