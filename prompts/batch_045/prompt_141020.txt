I need a specification rather than a position paper. A hospital ethics committee is rewriting its policy on the determination of death, and the policy has to govern two decisions: when life-sustaining treatment may be withdrawn, and when organ procurement may begin. Before I look at the candidate criteria, I want to know what we should be asking of one.

Write the desiderata: what any adequate account of death has to deliver, with the case or consideration behind each. My starting list, which I would rather you correct and extend than adopt, is: (i) keep apart the definition of death, the criterion by which we recognize it, and the bedside tests, and say which of the three a committee is entitled to revise; (ii) say whether irreversible means cannot be reversed by any means or will not be reversed because we have decided not to try, and whether a declaration made today could be falsified by a later technology that restores what was lost; (iii) handle cases where the criteria conflict, such as a body with a destroyed brain but sustained circulation, or a patient whose circulation is deliberately arrested and later restored, by giving priority rules or by explaining why there is no fact of the matter; (iv) rule on patients in whom loss of the capacity for consciousness and loss of integration of the organism as a whole come apart, as in the permanently vegetative patient and the anencephalic infant; (v) say whether an organism whose integration is maintained by an external controller rather than by its own brain is alive or is maintained tissue; (vi) either respect the rule that procurement may not cause the donor's death or show why that rule should be given up; (vii) yield a determinate time of death, since the law needs one, even if the underlying biology is a process; (viii) be administrable at the bedside by clinicians using imperfect tests under time pressure, and defensible to families.

For each requirement, state the consideration behind it and whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one camp. Flag any pair you expect to be jointly unsatisfiable — that is the part I most want to see. Name the single requirement that does the most work in excluding candidate accounts. Then, briefly, score whole-brain, brainstem, higher-brain, and circulatory-respiratory criteria against the list.
