My sister and I have been going at it for about four months over what our mum actually said she wanted — burial or cremation. We were both in the room, we remember it differently, neither of us can prove anything, and we each think the other is smart, honest and not obviously more biased than we are, which I gather is exactly the setup the peer-disagreement people argue about. We've been reading up on conciliationism versus steadfastness mostly so we can send each other links, and honestly at this point I'd rather laugh about it than keep going round.

So write me a parody: the standard terms-of-engagement document for a commercial service that resolves disagreements between epistemic peers, the kind of thing my sister and I could joke about signing. Numbered clauses, roughly 600 words, deadpan corporate-legal register, no winking and nobody ever saying the word absurd.

The humour has to come from getting the actual dialectic right, so work these in as clauses rather than as explanations:

- The parties must first certify each other as epistemic peers, but the certification is itself one of the things they disagree about, so it gets routed back into the same procedure — a Level 2 peer review, then a Level 3, and so on, each with its own fee.
- The core equal-weight clause: both parties move to the midpoint of their reported credences. Add a self-application surcharge for when they disagree about whether the clause applies, and let it iterate so the settlement approaches the midpoint of the midpoint.
- An independence rider: no adjustment is owed where the parties' evidence was not independent, or where they simply haven't pooled their reasons yet — and the fine print quietly makes skipping that step the default, since it's where the service's revenue comes from.
- A premium Right Reasons package, three times the price, where you're allowed to respond to the other party's reasons rather than to the bare fact that they disagree; the service holds your statement of reasons unread.
- A steadfast option, also priced high, and a spinelessness disclaimer noting that clients who conciliate with everyone end up holding nothing, with a Residual Conviction Storage facility offered at a monthly rate.
- A prohibition on bootstrapping: you may not first conclude the other party isn't your peer and then keep your original view on the strength of that. The auditors who check for this are themselves required to sit at the midpoint of all client credences, which is why customer service can never confirm whether a parcel has shipped.

End with a fee schedule, then a final clause covering the case where the disputed question has no midpoint — my case — where the remedy should follow straight from the service's own logic to something I'd actually recoil at. If it helps, give the firm a name that sounds like it merged with a wedding arbitration company.
