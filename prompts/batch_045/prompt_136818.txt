I am putting together the closing session of a first-year epistemology module on self-knowledge, and I am considering screening Memento to end on. Before I commit a whole session to it I want the philosophical reading worked out properly, at a finer grain than the observation that the film is about unreliable memory.

The two framings I have in mind are often run together and should not be. There is the KK thesis: knowledge is luminous, so that anyone who knows that p is thereby in a position to know that they know it. And there is the broader luminosity claim about one's own current mental states, of the sort that margin-for-error arguments target: for a wide range of conditions, if the condition obtains, one is in a position to know that it does. A third, weaker thesis also seems to be at stake: that belief is self-intimating, so that if one believes p one is in a position to believe that one does.

What I want from you:

1. Which of these three does the film actually put pressure on? Leonard's deficit is not obviously a failure of first-order evidence; he has notes, photographs, and tattoos. What he lacks is access to the *provenance* of his own evidence — he cannot tell whether a given record is his own, planted, or reused. That is a variable the standard debate never varies, because it takes for granted a subject with working source memory.

2. So: is this a genuine counterexample to KK, or does the right response say that KK was always a claim about agents with a certain cognitive architecture and is therefore insulated? If the latter, I want to know whether the insulated principle has been reformulated into something with any content left, or whether it is a real, restricted claim worth teaching. Give the missing condition a name and take a view on whether adding it is a repair or a surrender.

3. The Sammy Jankis material: the film supplies its own internal case about whether external records can substitute for self-knowledge, and then undercuts it, since the story Leonard tells about Sammy may be his own. Is the drama here epistemic at all, or is it a story about identity and guilt wearing epistemic costume? Say which, and say what it costs my reading if it is the latter.

4. The backward-running colour sequences put the audience in something close to Leonard's position: we are given grounds in an order that systematically defeats them, so we hold evidence we cannot know that we hold. Does the form do conceptual work here, or am I mistaking a stylistic device for an argument?

5. The ending, where he manufactures a false clue for himself on purpose: what does preferring comfortable false grounds to knowledge say about the value placed on knowing that one knows, and does the film endorse the trade or just stage it?

Finally, be explicit about how much this reading is worth. I would rather have the honest version than an elegant one: tell me where the film resists being read epistemically at all, which single consideration would lead you to abandon the reading, and where your own interpretation is most likely to fail. And end by saying what, if anything, the film adds to the KK and luminosity debates that the arguments by themselves do not.
