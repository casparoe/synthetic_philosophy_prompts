hey — I'm a philosophy PhD student (my own thing is causation, mostly interventionist / difference-making stuff) and I've been sitting in on our department's quantum foundations reading group, which I think is slowly making me worse at my own topic.

Last week a postdoc presented a toy model and it's been bugging me since. His model is deterministic: the pair leaves the source carrying a shared seed, each wing's outcome is a function of the local detector setting plus that shared seed, and each particle only ever "looks at" variables in its own backward light cone. The trick is that the seed is correlated with how the detectors get set — he traces both back to a common cause way back in the remote past (he shrugged and said yeah, fine, it's the superdeterminism move, so what). His claim is that the model is local in every sense he cares about, that it reproduces the quantum correlations right up to the CHSH ceiling of about 2.83 (against an algebraic max of 4), and therefore that Bell's theorem is really a constraint on experimental freedom rather than a nonlocality result.

Someone in the back shut this down with the line I have now heard about four times this term: "Bell forces you to give up either locality or realism, take your pick." It felt decisive and it also felt like it might be doing no work at all.

So: please take that slogan apart and lay out, as a list, the different precise claims it could be making. I want to know what "locality" is even doing in it — no superluminal signalling? Bell's factorisability condition, where the hidden variable screens each outcome off from the far setting? parameter independence and outcome independence taken separately? separability, i.e. whether the state of the composite is fixed by the states of the parts? something about local beables and relativistic spacetime? my own interventionist "no causal influence from the far setting to the near outcome"? or this thing I keep half-thinking, that no pair of local computations sharing only a seed and exchanging no messages can generate these correlations. Same for "realism": hidden variables, determinism, counterfactual definiteness, single outcomes, mind-independence, no-conspiracy / measurement independence — and please flag which of those is actually in the theorem's assumption set, because I have a nagging sense that "realism" is basically a misnomer that got stapled on, and that giving up determinism in particular buys you nothing, since the stochastic version of the locality condition already does all the work. And then "give up": give up for any theory at all, for any theory reproducing the quantum predictions, or only for theories reproducing the observed statistics given that the settings might be conspiratorially correlated with the seed.

For each reading say whether it's a platitude, whether it's substantive and defensible, or whether it's false — and, the bit I actually need, whether it tells the postdoc anything he doesn't already grant. Because as far as I can tell, on one reading the slogan just says "your model is nonlocal" where "nonlocal" has been *defined* as "violates the factorisability condition", which he'll happily concede and which is consistent with there being no superluminal anything; on another reading it says something he explicitly denies. I want to know which reading the guy in the back was probably reaching for, and whether that's a reading he'd want to actually defend rather than just intone.

Also: is there a version of the slogan that's straightforwardly false? My gut says "you have to give up realism" is, on the mind-independence reading, and I'd like to be sure before I open my mouth in that room again.
