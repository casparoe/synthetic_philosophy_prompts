And here's the answer I have to mark:

"MFT is a descriptive theory. It says liberals' moral judgments are mostly driven by care and fairness, while conservatives also weight loyalty, authority, sanctity and liberty — and that is a claim about what people actually respond to, not about what there is reason to do. You cant get an 'ought' from an 'is', so the bare fact that a judgment has a psychological cause isn't yet a reason to think it's false; otherwise you'd have to say we don't know there are tables, since our beliefs about tables come from our visual system. What you need is the extra claim that the mechanism in question doesn't track the truth.

That's what the disgust argument tries to supply. Condemnation of these practices is predicted by disgust sensitivity, and disgust evolved to keep us away from rotting food and faeces, not to detect wrongness. So there's a mismatch between what the judgment is about — moral status — and what the mechanism is for — avoiding infection — and in a mismatch case like that we have a reason to doubt the judgment.

There's a further point in favour of the conservative, too. If conservatives respond to six foundations and liberals to two, then conservatives are taking more of the moral landscape into account. The book's comparison with taste receptors is exactly right here: someone with five working receptors is a better judge of a dish than someone with two, and in the same way the conservative is in a better position to judge a case where loyalty or sanctity is genuinely at stake. Liberals aren't wrong about care and fairness, they're just responding to less.

The obvious objection is the symmetry problem: can't we tell the same kind of story about liberal judgments, tracing care and fairness back to kin altruism and reciprocal altruism? I don't think this gets off the ground. Those mechanisms are at least aimed at the right subject matter — other people's welfare and fair treatment — whereas pathogen avoidance has nothing to do with morality at all. And you can see the difference in the data: liberals really do score lower on the binding foundations, which is why they find these judgments so hard to understand. So the debunking argument fires in one direction only, and the psychological evidence ends up supporting the liberal rather than the conservative."
