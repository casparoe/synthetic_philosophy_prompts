Me and my mate have been going round in circles on Fitch's paradox of knowability and I want an actual number out of you, not another round of "well, it depends what you mean by knowable".

The thing in outline: the knowability principle says every truth p is such that its possible that p is known. Fitchs argument takes a truth nobody in fact knows — q = "p and nobody knows that p" — feeds it into the principle, and since knowing a conjunction means knowing each bit, and knowing that nobody knows p makes it false that nobody knows p, you get a contradiction. So if there are unknown truths, unrestricted knowability is out.

The fix my mate keeps pushing: read the "nobody knows" bit as being about us, the actual unimproved community, and read the "possibly known" bit as being about some possible successor or extension of us — an ideally improved version of our own epistemic practice, reachable by a chain of revisions we'd ourselves accept from where we are. On that reading the argument just doesnt go through: the extended lot can know p while it stays true that we, as we actually are, never did. No contradiction, no collapse into "every truth is actually known".

My worry is the opposite one: that this only works by gutting the principle. If the knower is allowed to be arbitrarily unlike us, "every truth is knowable in principle" stops constraining meaning at all, and the anti-realist who wanted to use it to argue against determinate truth values for the usual disputed class — undecidable maths, unobserved past events, counterfactuals, other minds, that sort of thing — has quietly turned into a realist who thinks all of those do have determinate truth values. So is it a way out, or a capitulation dressed up as one?

My credence that the index-shift reading is a genuine way out (blocks the derivation and still has enough bite to do the anti-realist's work) is about 0.25. His is about 0.65. Give me yours as a number, but mainly decompose it:

- that the derivation really does fail on this reading — and specifically whether there's a diagonal re-derivation waiting, using something like "p and no possible extension of us ever knows that p", or whether that instance is blocked for the same reason the original one is
- that what's left is non-trivial, i.e. that "knowable by some possible extension" isn't so generous that it just comes to "true"
- how much turns on how generous the modality is anyway — metaphysical possibility versus nomological or physically bounded possibility gets you a very different principle
- whether the knower is allowed to vary with the proposition (for each truth, someone-or-other could know it) or has to be held fixed (one ideal community knows them all), and how much that changes things
- that some reading with real bite is available at all — whether restricting the principle to propositions that don't contain knowledge-talk, or to ones that could be known while being known, or whatever other carve-out you like, can both escape the argument and still underwrite an epistemic account of truth

Then tell me which of those terms your final number is most sensitive to, what kind of argument or result would actually move it, and where you think me and my mate most likely disagree.

And if my framing of the fix is muddled, or the whole move is a known dead end with better clothes on, say that plainly instead of politely engaging with it — I'd rather be told the question is bad than get a tidy answer to it.
