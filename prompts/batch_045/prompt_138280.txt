Recap, just so we're using the same version (if you know it, skim): take a solution of the Einstein field equations, pick a region H where the stress-energy vanishes, and consider an active diffeomorphism that is the identity everywhere outside H but nontrivial inside. The dragged-along fields give you a second solution that agrees with the first everywhere outside H and disagrees inside it. If manifold points have their identity independently of the fields, and if the two models can therefore represent distinct physical possibilities, then nothing outside H fixes what happens inside H — and you can run the same move around essentially any point, so the theory looks radically indeterministic, or the point-identity looks physically empty. I've been thinking of there being three premises you can give up: (i) that spacetime points have primitive, field-independent identity; (ii) that isomorphic-but-nonidentical models can still represent distinct possibilities; (iii) that this kind of determinism is something a good theory owes us.

Here are the responses I want ranked. One line each, and if you don't recognize one just work from my description:

1. Relationalism: no spacetime points over and above the fields and their relations, so the two models just are the same possibility.
2. Sophisticated substantivalism / anti-haecceitism about points: points are real, but permuted models don't ground distinct possibilities, because a point's identity isn't brute.
3. Bite the bullet: yes, substantivalism plus GR gives you radical indeterminism — so much the worse for substantivalism.
4. The gauge move: the dragged-along structure isn't really a second model at all, it's the same model described differently, so the argument equivocates between active and passive diffeomorphisms.
5. Supersubstantivalism: identify spacetime with the metric field (or whatever structure does the real work) rather than with a bare manifold — no leftover point identity to exploit.
6. Ontic structural realism: only the common structure of the diffeomorphic models is real; points are just positions in a structure.
7. The dynamical/Cauchy reply: take the initial-value formulation as primary — the 3+1 problem determines the geometry up to diffeomorphism and that is all the determinism anyone was ever entitled to; the 4D block talk is where the pseudo-problem came from.
