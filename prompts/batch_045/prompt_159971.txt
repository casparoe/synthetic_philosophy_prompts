I'm a retired geophysicist, reading a bit of philosophy of religion for fun, and I keep running into a move I don't have a word for. Ordinary version first: someone says "had she been offered the money, she'd have taken it" about a choice that was never actually made, everyone treats it as plainly true or false, and nobody thinks that saying it forces her hand. The version I keep meeting in the reading: before creating anything, God is supposed to know what every possible person would freely do in every situation they might be put in, and then to pick which situations to make real — so nothing is caused by the knowing, but the outcome is still fully known and, up to a point, arranged. Two things I want pinned down.

First, these "would" facts are doing all the work and they look like a strange kind of thing. In my old field a counterfactual is true because of laws plus initial conditions plus some rule about what to hold fixed; there's machinery that makes it true. Here the machinery seems to have to be absent — if what she'd do followed from her character, her reasons, or the state of the world, the freedom goes — so the fact is, as far as I can tell, just true, and yet it's supposed to be knowable in advance and perfectly reliable. Is there a name for a counterfactual of that shape, and for the objection that theres nothing to make it true? If the nearest term is something flat like "brute counterfactual", which neighbouring terms should I be keeping it seperate from?

Second, the bit I really can't place: the same facts get used at a different scale. Once you've got a whole population of possible free creatures, each with its own fixed "would" facts for each situation, the question becomes which combinations are jointly available — whether there's any arrangement that gives you, say, a hundred free creatures who in fact never do anything terrible. My instinct from physics is that this is a constraint-satisfaction problem, and that for interesting global targets you should often expect no solution at all, or a solution only at absurdly fine-tuned parameter values. And when that happens the reply seems to be "then that world wasn't available to be made", which sounds to me like a limitation relabelled as a fact about the menu. Does that move have a name, and is it a distinct commitment from the first one, or part of the same package? One more thing: does it matter how finely the situation in the "would" claim is specified? The finer you make it, the less use the facts seem to be for arranging anything, and I'd like to know whether that tension has been written about — and if not, what you'd call it.
