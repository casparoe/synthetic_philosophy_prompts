I teach ethics, which is apparently why my colleagues elected me to the at-large faculty seat on the university's Food Systems Advisory Board — and which is now why I'm the one writing the memo for our meeting on the 14th. Can you draft it?

The case: Dining Services wants to take beef off the line in the two main dining halls and put chicken where the beef was. Their pitch is climate. Beef runs several times chicken's emissions per gram of protein (they say six; the number moves depending on whose lifecycle analysis you like), and the campus climate commitment has dining down for a 30% cut by 2030, so this is the biggest lever they've got.

Our problem is the 2016 Animal Welfare Procurement Statement, which the Board is supposed to enforce and which says dining will prefer suppliers whose practices minimize harm to sentient animals. Nobody wrote it with this case in mind, so the memo has to say what it actually means here.

Three positions on the board, and I definately don't think any of us has sorted out which one wins:

One of the undergraduate members, who worked a summer for a poultry integrator, says the swap is a disaster. Her spreadsheet has roughly one steer per million calories of beef against something like three hundred birds per million calories of chicken, and her point is that whatever welfare certification we bolt on is rounding error next to three hundred deaths.

I pointed out that the crop-cultivation argument actually cuts against beef, since grain-fed beef takes several times more cropland per calorie — and then realized that doesn't apply to us, because the beef we actually buy comes from a regional grass-fed operation: almost no grain, but a lot more land per calorie.

Which set off our staff ecologist, and now I can't beleive I'm the one who has to referee it. If we drop the rancher's contract, it renews in March, that pasture probably goes to corn and soy or to a subdivision, and either way the number that matters isn't animals killed but wild animals whose lives we cause to exist. She wants to know whether bringing a field mouse into existence counts as a harm under the 2016 language, because if it does the whole accounting flips, and if it doesn't we owe her a reason why mice are different from chickens. Half the board thinks that once you start counting rodents you never stop, and wants us to just count vertebrate deaths per 1,000 kcal and get on with it.

I don't want a memo that says both sides have merit and it depends on your values. I need a bottom line: approve, reject, or approve with conditions. Dining has been quoted about 18% over current prices for slower-growing birds raised with more space, and the Director can absorb that for one year but not two. Whoever reads this should be able to tell from it what to order from whom and by when. And give the losing side its best day in court — the strongest version of the argument you're rejecting, stated so the person making it would recognize it.

Format: exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no intro and no sign-off. Roughly: (1) the recommendation, (2) how you're reading minimize harm to sentient animals — which animals count, and per plate, per 1,000 kcal, or per gram of protein, (3) the conditions, (4) the dates, (5) the strongest thing on the side you're not taking. I'm pasting these straight into the agenda packet.
