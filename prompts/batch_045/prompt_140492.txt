I have been asked to contribute a short essay on blame and forgiveness to a collection of pieces by novelists, and before I commit a position to print I would like someone with more philosophy than I have to check that the parts of it fit together. I write novels, mostly about families. Two of the commitments below are not philosophical convictions at all but tradesman's ones, since a good part of my job consists of manufacturing resentment in readers and getting its pitch exactly right. Here, roughly, is where I have landed.

(1) What I take from Strawson's British Academy lecture "Freedom and Resentment" is this: to be morally responsible is not to satisfy some metaphysical condition — alternative possibilities, an act of will that is uncaused — but to be an intelligible target of the attitudes we take up toward someone from inside a relationship with them, resentment and gratitude and guilt and forgiveness, as opposed to the detached, managerial stance we take toward something we propose to handle, treat or explain. I also accept two things that seem to follow. First, no discovery made from outside that practice could give us a reason to dismantle it wholesale; whether to hold a person responsible is always settled from within the practice and never from some external vantage point on it. Second, the route by which an agent came to have the will he has is, in general, beside the point: a man whose character was covertly engineered by others is, I think, as open to resentment as anyone else.

(2) Blame, as I understand it, is a way of taking a person seriously: a demand addressed to him which he can in principle recognise and answer. So I hold that extending the reactive attitudes is presumptively respectful and withdrawing them is presumptively an insult — that to treat an adult of sound mind as a thing to be managed rather than a person to be answered to is itself a kind of injury, even when the management is kindly.

(3) But I also hold that there are people from whom resentment and indignation ought to be withheld: small children, my own mother in the last two years of her dementia, perhaps the profoundly psychopathic. And I hold that withholding it here is a decency rather than a slight, and that someone who cannot stop resenting a three-year-old has something the matter with them.

(4) I think we ought to train our reactive attitudes — to learn to stop resenting what we once resented, and to begin resenting what we once excused — and I think this training is a rational, reason-governed activity and not merely reconditioning. I do not mean only learning more of the facts in individual cases. I mean that what we discover about how human action is actually produced, from addiction or exhaustion or the situations people are put in, can and should reshape the attitudes themselves.

(5) Finally, two claims from my trade. I do not think that the indignation I feel toward a character in a novel is a different, merely simulated attitude, somehow less real than the indignation I feel toward my agent; I think it is the same attitude, and that fiction is among the best instruments we have for refining it. The narrator of the book I am finishing resents her dead mother for a decade, and I take that to be a perfectly intelligible state for a person to be in rather than a failure of her rationality. And I do not think it counts against my readers' outrage that I have engineered it — the causal history is not the point — but I do distrust, and want to be able to call defective, the outrage that a newspaper campaign manufactures in exactly the same way.

What I want is an audit rather than a lecture. Which of these actually pull against each other, and how badly? Rank the tensions by severity instead of listing them as though they were equal, and for each say what exactly would have to be weakened or dropped and what that would cost me elsewhere in the set. If one or two of the worries you would expect to be raised are in fact no worry at all, say which and why, so that I do not spend the essay defending myself against a charge that does not stick. Then tell me which single revision would tidy up the most. And please do not assume that the two claims which come from my trade are the ones I would surrender most cheerfully: if one of them is the weak point, I would rather be told so plainly than have it treated as local colour.
