I'm giving a 30-minute talk next week in our department's joint logic/analysis seminar and I want to be ready for the question period rather than for the slides.

My thesis is that constructive provability has been oversold as a proxy for computational content. The test I want to put on the table instead is representation-independent extraction: a proof of a statement like "for every real x there's a y such that A(x,y)" earns its computational content only if the algorithm you extract returns the same behaviour no matter how the input real is handed to it — Cauchy sequence with a modulus, Dedekind cut, signed-digit stream, decimal expansion. My punchline is that Brouwerian intuitionism does surprisingly badly on this test: the principles that make it distinctive (choice sequences, continuity, bar induction) let you prove things whose extracted content is a functional that reads off intensional details of the name you fed it rather than computing from the real itself. And the genuinely computational fragment, I want to say, is the one where Church's thesis is admissible (every function computable), which is the fragment that can't also have Brouwer's fan theorem — so the usual slogan that intuitionistic logic is "the logic of computation" gets things backwards.

The room: about half classical analysts plus one set theorist who think of constructive math as classical math with a hand tied behind its back; two proof theorists who do realizability and extraction in Martin-Löf type theory and know that machinery much better than I do; and a philosopher of maths who'll want to know weather I'm making a claim about logic or about meaning.

I'm shakiest on the consistency facts — which of continuity, the fan theorem and bar induction actually clash with Church's thesis, and by what argument — and I dont want to get corrected on the whiteboard in front of everyone.
