I'm drafting a paper on a commitment-based account of Moore's paradox — roughly, the view that the badness of *It's raining, but I don't believe it* lies in what assertion commits a speaker to, not in any incoherence among her attitudes — and I need a case that pressures the doxastic alternative, on which the absurdity is just the irrationality of simultaneously holding the two attitudes. Please build one to the following specification.

One paragraph, ordinary life, no brain lesions, hypnosis, drugs, split brains, or science fiction. In the scenario a single speaker, with respect to one specific proposition p that you state:

1. knows that p — tell me which account or sense of knowledge you are relying on;
2. does not believe that p, where this is not weakness of will, self-deception, half-hearted or partial belief, or a merely verbal dispute about *believe*;
3. is epistemically blameless throughout: nothing she did or omitted is criticizable;
4. can be described, without oddity, by a third party as knowing that p without believing it;
5. nevertheless cannot felicitously utter, first-personally and in the present tense, the conjunction of p with *I don't believe that p* — an ordinary hearer still hears it as absurd.

So the third-personal/first-personal asymmetry has to be generated by the case, not stipulated. Then show the case meets each specification: name the mechanism that is supposed to yield knowledge without belief, and say why it isn't really a case of hesitant belief.

Next, walk through the verdicts and the reasons for them: the doxastic reading, the knowledge-norm-of-assertion account, and the commitment/scoreboard account. Then, honestly: which feature of the case is doing the argumentative work; what the case collapses into if that feature is removed — in particular what it becomes if your speaker does believe that p; and the strongest reply open to a doxastic theorist. I already know the reply *knowledge entails belief, so (1) and (2) are incompatible*; give me something stronger if you can, and if you cannot, explain why that reply is more damaging than it first looks.

Conclude with a JSON object, values one sentence each, no keys beyond these:

- scenario_and_p: the scenario compressed to two sentences, with p explicitly identified
- mechanism: the mechanism producing knowledge without belief
- constraint_check: object with keys knows_p, lacks_belief_p, blamelessness, third_personal_felicity, first_personal_absurdity
- verdicts: object with keys doxastic, knowledge_norm, commitment
- load_bearing_feature: the feature the argument rests on
- ablation: what remains if that feature is removed
- best_doxastic_reply: the strongest objection from the losing side
