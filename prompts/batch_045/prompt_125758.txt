Last night my friend (she works in interpretability) and I spent three hours on the symbol grounding problem and I'm not sure I won, so I want the view I ended up defending mapped properly. Here's what I defended: a system trained only on text can't get from syntax to semantics, and none of the usual fixes close the gap — not more data, not scale, not retrieval or tool use, not even letting a system self-report its own internal states (train it to predict its own error magnitude and some token is at least causally tied to something real). What's missing is the right kind of causal-historical connection between a symbol and what it's about, and that isn't the sort of thing you can bootstrap out of more symbol shuffling. I also claimed that grounding, if it happens at all, is a property of a model-in-a-loop rather than of the model on its own, and that it comes in degrees and can be gained or lost.

She said the second half of that hands me a loss: if grounding belongs to the loop, then strapping a camera and a robot arm onto a text-trained model grounds it, so the problem was never deep, and my reply that the loop has to include the training signal rather than just deployment was definately ad hoc. She also had a testimony point: causal connection to the world mediated by millions of people writing about the world is still causal connection to the world, and objecting to the mediation is just scepticism about testimony in a new hat.

So: what would count against my view? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and tell me which ones a defender of the view should concede count against it and which it can reasonably dismiss. Please work through three cases I invented mid-argument, since I think that's where it's most exposed. (1) Retroactive grounding: a text-only model put into a perception-action loop at deployment with no retraining — grounded, ungrounded, or some degenerate third thing? (2) The interoceptive anchor: a system with no external sensors whose only causally tethered symbols report its own internal states. If that counts as grounding, does it spread through inference to the rest of the vocabulary, and if it does, hasn't the problem collapsed? If it doesn't spread, how do words like electron or democracy get grounded for us? (3) The swapped sensor: a system whose wiring is inverted so that its red token is reliably caused by green things. It has a causal connection but not the right one, and if I demand correctness I seem to have smuggled in a normative theory of content — I want to know the price of that.

What would move me most isn't a benchmark score; it's a case where a text-only system's symbol tracks its object counterfactually in a way no pattern in the training corpus explains, or an argument that my view predicts something its rivals don't and that prediction fails. And be honest about whether the degrees-and-loops hedge has insulated the view into unfalsifiability, and whether that's fatal for a claim of this kind or just what claims of this kind look like.

Give me five sections in order: The view, hedges stripped (about 100 words); Ranked defeaters (about 300); Conceded vs dismissed (about 150); Is it insulated? (about 150); Bottom line (one short paragraph).
