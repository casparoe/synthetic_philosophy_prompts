Draft the joint statement that a transformationist and a compensationist about the so-called paradox of tragedy could both sign at the close of a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

The two positions, each to be stated in the strongest form its own side would accept:

- The transformationist holds that the distress we feel at depicted suffering is not merely outweighed or offset but converted: the artistic presentation acts on the painful passion itself, so that the pain is an ingredient of the resulting enjoyment, and the enjoyment would be diminished — not improved — if the distress were removed while everything else was held fixed.
- The compensationist holds that the distress is genuine, unpleasant and bad as such; that what we enjoy is a distinct cluster of positive responses (to the craft, to the lucidity with which the emotion is rendered, to the sense of one's own responsiveness, to what the work makes vivid about human life) which co-occur with, or are causally triggered by, the distress, and which would on the whole survive — arguably be greater — if the distress were removed while everything else was held fixed.

Produce the document in four parts.

1. A numbered section of agreed claims, i.e. what both sides can sign. Cover at least: the characterisation of the phenomenon to be explained; the constraints on any adequate explanation (it must also cover instrumental music, which depicts no events, and it must not proceed by denying that the distress is real); and the methodological commitments both accept, including how much weight first-person reports carry and how much any behavioural or physiological measure should be allowed to override them.
2. A disagreements table: one row per residual disagreement, with columns for the transformationist's own formulation, the compensationist's own formulation, and a verdict on whether that disagreement is substantive or merely verbal — and where it is verbal, state exactly which word is doing the work and how the dispute would look once that word was fixed.
3. A cruxes section. For each crux, give the argument or case both sides agree would bear on it, together with each side's prediction written down in advance. Use these cases, or better ones of your own:
 (a) The titration case: a safe, selective agent that removes roughly four-fifths of the felt unpleasantness of a sad film while leaving comprehension, memory, aesthetic discrimination and the recognition that the events are terrible fully intact — and a matching agent that amplifies the unpleasantness. What happens to how much people like the film and how much they want to see it again?
 (b) The dissociation case: two patients with focal lesions. One reports the full distress but no longer seeks out sad music; the other reports no distress but seeks it out as much as before.
 (c) The paraphrase case: pair each tragic work with a "thin" version — a bare prose summary carrying the same propositional content about what happened. Both sides agree the two differ in how much they are enjoyed; say what each predicts about which components of the response survive in the thin version, and which components the transformationist should therefore insist are not about content at all.
 (d) The generalisation case: the same structure of response outside fiction — memorial services, funerals people describe as beautiful, deliberately re-reading letters from a loss.
4. A short closing paragraph on what, if anything, both sides agree would actually settle the matter, and what each would count as evidence against their own view.

Keep the whole piece in the register of a text both parties could put their names to: no adjudication, no verdict on who is right, no claims either side would call a straw man. Reason from the concepts and the cases, not from the literature — no citations, no author names, no bibliographies anywhere, and nothing that rests on an empirical result you are recalling.

Write in British English. End with a final line of the form \answer{<the single crux, in at most twelve words, on which both camps agree the whole dispute turns>}.
