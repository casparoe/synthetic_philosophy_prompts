A year ago my daughter was diagnosed with a severe peanut allergy, and a piece of metaphysics I used to teach as textbook furniture has become load-bearing in my own life. Our household now runs on ascriptions of exactly the disputed kind: that she is disposed to go into anaphylaxis on ingestion of peanut protein above her threshold; that the auto-injector we carry blocks that outcome without removing the disposition; that the desensitization she has begun raises her threshold rather than eliminating what is there; and — this from our allergist — that strenuous exercise within a few hours of a small exposure is a cofactor that can make the difference between the disposition's being present and its not being at all. I have to say, in earnest and with consequences, that she is still allergic, and I want to know what I am committing to in saying it.

The position I have ended up holding is basis-first: an ascription of the form "x is disposed to M in C" is made true by x's presently having some property B which, together with C, nomically suffices for M absent interference; the counterfactual "were x in C, x would M" is at best defeasible evidence for the ascription and never its truth-maker. What moved me is that the standard interferer cases seem to sort along one axis that has nothing essentially to do with counterfactuals: some interferers remove or alter the basis (the fink; and, over a longer timescale, successful desensitization), some block the manifestation while leaving the basis intact (the antidote — the auto-injector), and some produce the manifestation where there is no basis to be found (the mask, the mimicker). If that recut is right, the fink/antidote/mask taxonomy is a surface phenomenon, and both the simple conditional analysis and its revised descendant, with the "retains its basis" clause folded into the antecedent, are trying to make a counterfactual do work that only a basis can do.

Please tell me what would count against this, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and for each item say which a committed basis-first theorist should concede as counting against them and which they are entitled to dismiss as resting on a rival's assumptions. I am especially interested in four things. First, whether my notion of a basis can be specified without circularity: if B can only be picked out as "the property in virtue of which x would M in C," my view is the revised conditional analysis in disguise, and I would like to know whether there is a principled — perhaps model-theoretic, perhaps an argument from how much freedom one has in the selection function or similarity ordering — reason to think the two accounts are provably equivalent, so that my disagreement with the conditionalist is merely notational. Second, whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability: if anything that blocks a manifestation counts as an antidote by definition, what observation would ever make me withdraw "she is still disposed to react," and is the answer acceptable in the way that "this body still carries charge" is acceptable, or is it a genuine defect for a claim I want to act on? Third, some cases I have not seen handled and that worry me more than the textbook ones: dispositions whose stimulus condition is conjunctive with a temporally distant and extrinsic cofactor (peanut plus exercise within two hours), which threatens the intrinsicality requirement doing the work against masks; dispositions whose manifestation is graded and threshold-relative and can be partly manifested (hives versus full anaphylaxis, along a dose–response curve); dispositions whose manifestation is partly what sustains the basis while it lasts, as in a self-amplifying cascade, so that my basis/manifestation distinction is not clean; and dispositions with no stimulus condition, or with merely probabilistic manifestations, where "suffices for M" cannot be read deterministically. Fourth, what my view predicts that its rivals do not — for instance that there can be dispositions which are never manifested because an antidote is always present, or which survive a permanent change in the relevant counterfactual — and whether anything in clinical, engineering, or legal practice looks like it is tracking the basis rather than the counterfactual, which would be evidence either way.

The two things I would personally find most persuasive are, in order: an otherwise unproblematic case in which the basis is present and stable, the manifestation occurs in every nearby world in which the stimulus obtains, and the ascription is nonetheless false; and an argument that the "absent interference" clause cannot be stated without quantifying over dispositions — over things disposed to prevent M — which would make the central notion of my analysis depend on the very notion I was trying to analyze. Please also give an honest assessment at the end of how exposed the view really is, and of whether whatever exposure remains is the kind a metaphysical view may reasonably live with.
