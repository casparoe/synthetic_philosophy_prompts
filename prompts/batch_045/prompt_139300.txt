hey — I'm on a city advisory panel and I need help with priorities, not background info.

Setup: we've got roughly two years, a modest budget, and about a dozen contested commemorations to make recommendations on. Two statues downtown, a high school named for a segregation-era school board president, four or five street names, a mural in a public library, and a lecture hall named after a donor whose fortune came from housing discrimination. We have to give our reasons in public, we can't redo everything, and whatever we recommend gets appealed to the council and then fought over for another year.

What I want: of the philosophical questions this kind of work runs into, which two or three should we actually spend our limited argumentative energy on — the ones where getting the answer right would change what we recommend — and which are a distraction, fun in a seminar but not changing any vote we take. Rank them for *our* situation and defend the ranking. I care more about the reasoning than the list: what makes a question worth the argument here, how many of the other questions it settles, how tractable it is for a bunch of lay people with two years, and what we'd have to find out to move something up or down.

Here are the ones I keep circling, with my own labels for them so we don't talk past each other:

- *donor's shadow*: some of these were never a public judgment to begin with — a naming right was sold to a private person. Does that change the ethics of removal, and who has standing to complain?
- *contextualization drift*: keep the object, add a plaque that reframes it. Is that a stable third option, or does it predictably decay into either removal or restoration once nobody reads the plaque anymore?
- *the replacement burden*: are we obliged to put something on the empty plinth, and is there a real asymmetry between what it takes to install an honor (a donor, a vote) and what it takes to remove one (a public reason, a ritual of dishonor)?
- *saturation*: apply "don't honor the seriously morally flawed" consistently and basically the entire nineteenth- and twentieth-century civic landscape qualifies. I don't mean this as a gotcha reductio — I mean, what selection principle do we actually use: how bad, how central to what they were honored for, whether the honor is still doing any active work or has gone inert.
- *cooling-off*: we're also being asked to name a new park after someone who died last year. Does a cooling-off period for new honors fall out of the same principles we use for removing old ones, or is that a separate thing?
- *laundering*: the school gets renamed and everyone declares the matter closed while the thing the old name stood for is still going on. How much does the expressive-vs-material distinction matter for how we weigh that risk?

Add whatever I'm missing. And tell me if some of these are dead ends — I suspect the "you're erasing history" line and the anachronism / judging-the-past-by-present-standards debate are both things we should have a stock answer to but shouldn't sink months into, and that the art-vs-artist framing mostly doesn't fit.

Also, please seperate what's actually settled among people who work on this from what's genuinely contested, and flag anywhere my own framing above is doing load-bearing work or is itself a contested view. Say what you're confident about and what you're definately guessing at.
