I'm in eleventh grade and run a student philosophy podcast. Next episode my guest is Dr. Nora Reinhardt, a philosopher defending what I'll call reconciliationism about absurdism: the universe has no purpose and human lives are cosmically insignificant, and yet a life can be genuinely, objectively meaningful, because meaning is a local, terrestrial property that never needed cosmic backing in the first place. Her stock move is to say that whatever worry she has just been handed is really a claim about cosmic purpose rather than about meaning, and I want questions that make that reply harder to lean on each time she reaches for it.

Please draft seven or eight questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. I want at least one question on each of the following:

- whether absurdism is a thesis about the value of our lives or about our capacity to step back and see our own ends as arbitrary, and, if it is the latter, why anyone ever took it to threaten meaning;
- why we do not describe the life of a mouse or a dog as absurd, given that it is equally cosmically insignificant, and what that implies about whether the absurd is a discovery about the world or a fact about us;
- how reconciliationism differs from the hybrid accounts on which meaning is subjective attraction meeting objective worth. If those accounts are right, what was cosmic purpose supposed to contribute, and if the answer is nothing, has the reconciliation emptied absurdism of anything the pessimists actually denied? This is the question I most want her to answer without her stock reply.
- a case I invented: a woman spends thirty years keeping meticulous tidal records at a remote station. The instruments were miscalibrated from the start, no one will ever read the logs, and she never finds out. She is fully absorbed and the work is genuinely demanding. Does her life come out meaningful on her view, and if not, is that because meaning tracks actual worth or reasonably believed worth? Follow up by asking whether finding out would retroactively drain the meaning of the preceding decades.
- whether a meaningful life requires consequences that outlast it, for instance whether our lives would be less meaningful now if humanity left no trace after one more century.

Under each question add a one-sentence note flagging whether the point it presses is something specialists actually disagree about or something both sides already grant, and tell me plainly where reconciliationism is a minority position and where my invented case has an easy answer I have not thought of. I do not want to put questions to her that present a contested reading as the consensus one.
