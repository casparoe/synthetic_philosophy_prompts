I'm a high school junior applying for an eight-week summer research placement, and I need a one-page proposal for the project I'd run. My half-formed idea is this: in the classic Ellsberg setup, people prefer betting on the urn whose composition is known over the one whose composition is unknown, and the standard explanation is that they dislike missing probabilities as such. I'm not sure that's what's doing the work. I suspect a good part of what gets called ambiguity aversion is really a response to suspected agency — an urn whose makeup is unknown feels like an urn whose makeup somebody chose, and chose with an eye to what I would pick. My plan is to pull those apart by varying how the ambiguous urn was filled: a randomizing device whose setting was fixed and hidden before anyone saw the question; a person who receives a bonus if I lose; a person with no stake who never learns which color I chose. I'd also vary whether the ambiguous urn is shown on its own or alongside the known one, because I've seen it reported that the gap between the two urns nearly closes when they are judged separately, and I want to know whether my agency story predicts that too. If the missing-probability account is right, the three fillers should look roughly alike; if I'm right, aversion should track how much the filler stood to gain from my losing and how much they knew about my choice.
