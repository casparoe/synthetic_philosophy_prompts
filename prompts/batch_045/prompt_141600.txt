Me and a mate had a three-hour argument in the pub last night about what counts as a science — he's a hardline falsify-or-shut-up type — and I came away realising I've got a pile of verdicts that I don't think fit together. So here they are, gut reactions only:

- Predictive astrology done properly: big mathematical apparatus, pre-registered predictions, real statistical tests, and after thirty years every effect size is indistinguishable from zero, and the field just carries on. Not a science, and I'd say junk.
- A research programme I'll call chronistics: it unifies a dozen previously unrelated results, it's mathematically gorgeous, it's spun off technology that definately works, nobody can extract a single testable prediction from it, and its leading people say openly that nothing could refute it, since any apparent conflict could always be accommodated. Science, but I say it through gritted teeth.
- A discipline that only builds better instruments and publishes catalogues of measurements — no theories, no predictions. Obviously science.
- Reconstructing a one-off event deep in the past, like what wiped out most of the species alive 66 million years ago. Science, even though you can't repeat it and can't predict anything from it.
- Homeopathy. Not a science.
- Mathematics. Not a science, but not junk either, and I don't want my criterion to end up insulting it.
- A lab whose methods are textbook standard but which only ever publishes results favouring the one company that funds it. Not a science — and I'm holding that while I let chronistics in, which feels wrong.
- A theory whose risky, precise predictions keep coming true, but whose practitioners say outright that its central entities are fictions that just make the calculations work. Science, obviously.

My own stab at a principle was: a field is a science to the extent that its community has working machinery for catching and discarding its own bad ideas, and actually uses it. But that seems to kick chronistics out and let maths in, and I'm not sure what it does with the company lab.

Can you find a principle — or a short list of candidates — that gets these, or nearly? I want each clause tied to the case that forces it, I want to know which of my verdicts it can't accommodate and which I'd have to eat, and I want it run on cases I haven't listed.
