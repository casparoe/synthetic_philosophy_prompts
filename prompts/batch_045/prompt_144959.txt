Been chewing on the procreation asymmetry for a while and I think my own verdicts dont add up, so here they are raw. (Setting aside all reasons about other people -- I only want to know what the kid's own welfare does.)

Kid who'd have a great life: nothing wrong with having them, but also no reason to. Purely optional. Kid whose life would clearly not be worth living: don't. Kid whose life would land just barely above the line, net positive by a hair, and who'd probably say at forty that they wish they'd never been born: I feel a real pull toward not creating them, much more than in the first case, which is weird since both are supposed to be "worth living." Conceiving in June instead of May, so that a different kid exists (different gametes and all) with an equally good life: no complaint from anyone, and I feel nothing either way. But if the June kid would be slightly worse off: still nobody wronged, and yet there's this faint "well if we're doing it anyway, do it in May" -- which by my own lights shouldn't be there. And if we're having a kid anyway and could pick between two embryos, one headed for a great life and one for a life that's worth living but with a painful chronic condition they'd fully adapt to: I feel a strong reason to take the healthy one. Much stronger than that faint May/June pull, even though the welfare gap is the same sort of thing.

My attempt at a principle: there's never a reason to create someone however good their life would be, and there is a reason not to when the life would be bad for the person living it. That sails through the first, second and fourth cases and falls over on the third and the embryo one. Can it be repaired into a single principle, or does something have to go? Which of my verdicts is the weakest link? What does the repaired version say about the case I actually lie awake about -- we want a second child, there's maybe a 20% chance of a condition that would put their life below the line and an 80% chance of a really good one, and my gut keeps insisting the 80% counts for something. And give me its verdict on a case I haven't listed, say a being that exists for one afternoon of joy and then painlessly stops.
