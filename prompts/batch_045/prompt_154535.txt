I am a retired experimental physicist who has lately been sitting in on philosophy, and in three weeks I am giving a forty-minute talk, with twenty minutes of questions, to a philosophy of mathematics reading group on Skolem's paradox. My thesis is that the relativity of uncountability exposed by the downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem is not a special pathology of set theory but the same species of relativity that special relativity exposes in simultaneity: in each case a predicate that appears to state an absolute matter of fact turns out to be well defined only relative to a background structure that the theory itself does not single out, and in each case the right response is neither skepticism nor a return to absolutism but an invariance criterion — say what is preserved across the admissible backgrounds, and count only that as objectively stated. Applied to sets, I propose reading cardinality attributions as indexed to a background universe: uncountable means that no bijection with the natural numbers exists in that universe; what survives the relativity is the internal theory rather than the cardinality attribution.

The room will be: one mathematical logician who works on forcing and inner models and regards the internal/external distinction as having settled all of this long ago; a philosopher of physics who is attending because of the analogy and will test it hard; a defender of full second-order semantics who holds that categoricity blocks my conclusion; and two generalist metaphysicians whose real question is whether I am committed to saying that the continuum hypothesis has no determinate truth value.

Please draft my Q&A prep sheet for that room. Seven or eight objections in the order they are likely to arrive, each phrased in the vocabulary of the person raising it, not in mine, with a two-minute reply, a note on what I should concede outright, and a note on where my reply is softest. Rank them by how much damage they would do rather than by how likely they are, and say which objection has real force even though I would rather it did not. Finally, tell me which question you think I am hoping nobody asks, and what I should say if it comes anyway.
