After a career in physics I have been reading philosophy in retirement, and I am looking for a name for something I did for decades without ever having a word for it.

The dynamical equations of the theories I worked with — Newtonian, Maxwellian, quantum-mechanical, apart from a small CP-violating corner I spent my working life ignoring — are indifferent to the direction of time. Reverse the film and they are satisfied just as well. Yet no application of them yields a prediction without one further posit, which appears in none of the equations and follows from none of them: that the gas in the box starts uncorrelated and well away from equilibrium; that the fields arriving at the apparatus are independent of the state of the system they act on; that the early universe was smooth. Grant the posit and a great deal follows, including almost everything we call the second law. Withhold it and nothing follows.

What I want a word for is the kind of thing this posit is. It is a single, unrepeatable, evidently contingent fact about one boundary rather than a pattern running through the whole history of the world; it must be posited afresh for every system and is never inherited from the previous application; and yet we reason from it exactly as we reason from a law, treat departures from what follows from it as not to be expected, and do not count each fresh posit as a cost against the theory.

Does a posit of this shape have an established name? A bare label would not help me much. I want to know how it is to be carved off from the three things it resembles — an ordinary law; a merely specific initial condition such as the exact positions and velocities of the molecules, which nobody is tempted to call lawlike; and the hedges attached to inexact generalisations — and whether the carving can be done at all on either of the two accounts of laws I have met so far. On one of them a law is said to be nothing above and beyond a regularity that earns its place in the most economical description of everything that happens; on that account I cannot see what rules out a one-off boundary stipulation, which makes me think I have not grasped it. On the other, laws are said to govern or bring about what falls under them, and I cannot see what a fact about a single boundary could govern, nor what governing adds beyond the fact that events do fall under the pattern. If only one of the two accounts has room for the posit, that looks to me like an argument for preferring it; if neither does, I should be grateful for a term coined for the purpose, with its boundary cases set out. One last thing puzzles me: the posit is always made at the same end of the time coordinate, and I cannot find anything in the equations that singles out an end.
