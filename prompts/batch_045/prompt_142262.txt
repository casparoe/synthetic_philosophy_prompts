Here is the passage, reconstructed as best I could from my notes:

"Here, in a nutshell, is why some mathematicians still lie awake at night. All of modern mathematics is supposed to be reducible to Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory with the Axiom of Choice, a short list of axioms from which nobody has derived a contradiction in a century of hard use. But Gödel's incompleteness theorems tell us that no axiomatic system strong enough to contain elementary arithmetic can prove its own consistency. So the consistency of the whole framework is not something we can ever establish; it can only be believed. And if the axioms are inconsistent, then by the principle of explosion every single statement of mathematics is provable — '2 + 2 = 5', 'the continuum hypothesis is true', 'the continuum hypothesis is false' — and mathematics collapses into triviality, taking down with it the ordinary arithmetic that every physicist and engineer depends on. The situation is stranger than that. Gödel showed how to write down a specific sentence of arithmetic, the Gödel sentence, which is true but cannot be proved from the axioms: it says, in effect, 'this system is consistent', which is precisely why no consistency proof can be given from inside the system. Gödel had proved the completeness theorem a few years earlier, showing that our rules of deduction are complete — anything that follows from the axioms can be proved from them — so the gap that incompleteness reveals must lie in the axioms themselves rather than in our methods of inference. What is left to us is only induction from experience: no contradiction has turned up yet, so we assume none is there. But a contradiction might exist whose shortest proof runs to millions of steps. In that case the axioms would be inconsistent in principle and yet perfectly serviceable in practice — a condition some writers call deep inconsistency — because every proof any human will ever actually check is short enough to be unaffected. Either way, the old dream that mathematics rests on foundations we can be certain of is dead."
