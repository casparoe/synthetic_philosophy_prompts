I work in programming-language theory, and my native vocabulary is the type/token one: nominal versus structural typing, parametric versus ad hoc polymorphism, singleton and refinement types, type classes and interfaces, generativity, erasure, intensional versus observational equivalence, universe levels and impredicativity, types declared in a signature versus types inferred from the values that inhabit them. My philosophy is undergraduate-level at best.

Take the problem of universals — what grounds the fact that numerically distinct particulars can all be red or triangular; whether redness is one item common to them, a class of resembling particulars, a family of particularized property-instances, or nothing over and above our predicates — and do three things.

1. Restate each main position in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can: ante rem realism, in re realism, trope theory, resemblance nominalism, and predicate (ostrich) nominalism, plus the sparse-versus-abundant distinction. Say which of my distinctions each one lands on, and where the landing is a convenience rather than a genuine match.

2. Give me the residue report: what translates cleanly, what translates only with distortion, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. I expect at least three sticking points and want your verdict on each: (a) the realist claim that the universal in different particulars is strictly numerically one, where structural typing gives graded similarity and never identity; (b) discovery versus stipulation — my vocabulary treats a type system as designed, and I suspect that quietly begs the question against realism; (c) the self-predication and third-man regresses, which look to me like universe-level or impredicativity problems in disguise — tell me whether that is a translation of the original worry or a collapse of it into something else.

3. Now the case I actually care about. Suppose I add graded membership to my language: a term inhabits type T just in case its similarity to T's prototype exceeds a threshold that is itself a parameter of the typing judgment. Does that discipline express resemblance nominalism, some form of trope theory, or neither? If neither, say what extra structure it would need — or whether the failure shows that resemblance nominalism was never a genuine alternative to realism.

Throughout, flag any point where the translation only goes through by smuggling in an assumption from one side of the philosophical dispute, and finish by saying whether the residue you found is a blind spot in my vocabulary or evidence that the original question is less sharp than it looks.
