Long-time lurker, first-time poster. I keep flip-flopping on advance directives / Ulysses contracts, so I want to do reflective equilibrium on myself: here are my gut verdicts, is there one principle that spits out all of them, or do I have to eat one?

- Bipolar, currently manic, says "I've never felt better, stop the lithium" — the euthymic self's instruction ("if I'm manic and refuse, treat me anyway") wins. Confident.
- At 25 she writes "if I'm ever severely demented, no antibiotics"; at 84 she's demented, plainly enjoys music and visits, and picks up a chest infection. My gut: treat, ignore the document. (I know the usual critical-interests move à la Dworkin; my gut is still with the person in the bed.)
- At 22 he writes "if I ever get sucked into one of those obsessive online communities and start refusing treatment, assume I've been captured and override me". At 30 he's exactly there, lucid-ish, refuses a life-saving transfusion. My gut: honour the 22-year-old.
- A 20-year-old signs a hardcore "everything to x-risk work, no heroic measures" directive, then re-ups it annually for fifteen years via a one-click "still stands" prompt, never reopening the scenario descriptions. My gut: those re-ups add almost nothing, it's still a 20-year-old's document.
- At 40, after a decade of slow stable revision and burnout, he quietly revokes it and wants the money to go to his kids. My gut: the revocation wins, even though the 20-year-old would have said "the 40-year-old has lost the plot".
- New one: a model fine-tuned on forty years of her journals and forum posts ("the continuation") says, more reliably than her family, that she'd want X. My gut: this gets some weight but nowhere near directive-level authority, and I can't say why.

My tentative principle is: the earlier self binds only when it correctly picked out the specific mechanism by which the later self would come to disagree. That handles the first three but feels shaky on the last three.

The case I actually lose sleep over: stable and euthymic for three years, she writes a considered note saying lithium flattens her and she'd rather accept relapse risk, with reasons her earlier self never weighed. Half the time I think the psychiatric directive still binds (known failure mode, known base rates), half the time I think that's just paternalism in a lab coat.

Tell me which of my verdicts your account can't save and what it predicts about the continuation case.
