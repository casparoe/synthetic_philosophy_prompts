I keep bumping into a thing and I dont have a word for it. Example: arguing with a friend who says, seriously, that any photo in the news might be AI-generated, so nothing visual is evidence for anything anymore. I notice I can't produce an argument that stops him — every reason I give already assumes what he's denying — but I also don't treat his position as a live option I might turn out to be wrong about. I just treat him as outside the conversation. Same shape, different content, a couple of hundred years back, or so it seems to me: which sources count as testimony you can build on has changed, and it changed without anyone winning an argument.

So the pattern. Some claims work as stopping points where reasons run out, but (a) they're not insulated from revision — whole communities have swapped them out over time and you can often say roughly when, and (b) while one is in force, denying it doesn't get you a hearing; you get corrected, trained, or laughed at rather than argued with. They behave like facts when you deny them and like contingent historical commitments when you look at them from far enough away.

I half-remember that Wittgenstein's On Certainty is somewhere in this neighborhood, but as far as I can tell that's about propositions that can't be doubted, and mine plainly can be — just not all at once. Is there an established concept for this exact configuration, and what are the nearest candidates and where does each fail to fit? Don't just hand me a label: if nothing established fits, give me a name, a definition, and the borderline cases where it'd be unclear whether something counts.
