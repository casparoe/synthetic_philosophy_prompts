I've been going around in circles on infinite ethics, so here are my gut verdicts on some cases — I want the principle that captures them, or to be told which ones have to go.

All the worlds below are infinite in space and in future duration, with people spread out evenly.

- One extra happy person added to an already infinite population of happy people: no improvement. Feels like nothing happened at all.
- Everyone at +2 instead of +1: clearly better.
- Everyone at +1, but finitely many people in agony somewhere: worse. Only slightly, but worse, and I won't drop this one.
- Everyone at +1 versus half at +3 and half at −1 (same average): the equal world is better. Less confident here.
- Everyone at +1 except that one person in every thousand, everywhere, is in agony: much worse — and weirdly much worse than case three, even though those agonies are just as bad as each other.
- All the suffering packed into a single infinitely long filament that occupies no volume at all, instead of spread through space: I want to say this is better than spreading it, but I also don't want to say it's exactly as good as the all-happy world, and I can't see how to have both.

My own attempt: rank worlds by well-being per unit volume of spacetime; if that ties, count the volume of world that's "ruined", i.e. contains suffering. That gets 1, 2 and 5, but it says case 3 changes nothing, and it can't separate 6 from the happy world at all.

And the one I keep flip-flopping on: pulling a kid out of a pond in a world that's already infinitely good. Its the same shape as case 1 — same density, no ruined volume — and I'm fine saying case 1 isn't an improvement, but I'd feel like a monster walking past the kid.

Is there one principle that gets 1, 3, 6 and the kid at once, or do I have to give one up? Give me the rule clause by clause with the case behind each clause, say what it predicts for a case I haven't listed — one happy person per cubic parsec versus two people per cubic parsec at half the well-being each — and be straight with me about which of my verdicts it can't save.
