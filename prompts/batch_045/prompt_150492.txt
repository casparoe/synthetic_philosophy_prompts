I've been trying to work out what I actually think art is, not by reading about it but by going case by case on my gut, and my guts don't add up. Help me find the principle behind them.

My cases and my verdicts:

- Duchamp's urinal, the shop-bought one he entered as a readymade: art, and I'm bored of pretending to be shocked by it.
- A mass-produced ceiling lamp from a flat-pack furniture shop, currently in my hallway: not art. I know that's more or less the same move Duchamp made and I don't care, it still isn't. This is the pair I can't reconcile, and every answer I try — it's in a gallery, it was chosen, it has a title, people wrote about it — just pushes the question back a step.
- A painting in the style of Rembrandt, made in the 1890s, hung in a museum as a Rembrandt for a hundred years, then exposed as a forgery: still art, but I definately feel it got worse the moment we found out, and I can't tell whether that feeling is about the object or about me.
- A canvas painted by a zoo chimpanzee, sold at a charity auction, looks just like a 1950s abstraction: not art.
- A recording of humpback whale song, slowed down eight hundred times, released as an album by someone whose only contribution was pressing record and turning a dial: art, just about. Which is pure snobbery next to the chimp verdict, and I can't give it up.
- A glacier in Iceland that an artist declared a work on her website: she never went there, never touched it, just posted the coordinates. People flew out and say it changed them. No idea, leaning no.
- A recruitment poster for a regime I loathe, beautifully designed: art, and its politics don't disqualify it. A really well-designed motorway sign: not art, even though it's also designed to move you to act.
- An AI-generated image from someone who spent weeks art-directing it: art. The same model's output from one lazy prompt: not art, even when the two look basically identical.
- My niece's drawing on the fridge: not art, and I feel like a git saying so.
- The one I keep flipping on: a man who spent thirty years secretly carving a staircase that goes nowhere in his basement, found after he died, no title, no statement, no audience, no intention of anyone ever seeing it.

My own stab: something is art if someone put it forward to be experienced for its own sake by some audience. That sorts the poster from the motorway sign, dies on the staircase and the glacier, and I don't think it separates the lamp from the urinal at all.

Please don't give me a survey of isms — I know the institutional, historical and aesthetic families exist, I don't need the labels reattached to them. Give me the working thing, clause by clause.

Sections, in this order:

- "My verdicts, restated" (~150 words): the pattern you think is actually in my list, sorted, not just repeated.
- "The principle" (~300 words): your candidate, stated precisely enough that I could apply it myself, each clause tied to the case that motivates it, plus a runner-up if there's a real choice between saving every one of my verdicts with something ugly and dropping one for something clean.
- "What it decides" (~200 words): verdicts on three or four cases I haven't listed, including the staircase, and at least one you think I'd hate.
- "What it overturns" (~150 words): which of my verdicts your principle can't keep, and which one you'd drop first.
- "Settled vs contested" (~150 words): which claims of yours are genuinely agreed among people who work on this and which are one side of a live argument — don't quietly present a contested view as the consensus. And tell me honestly whether the "stop asking for a definition, it's a family-resemblance word" line is actually the majority position or just a famous one.

British English throughout, please.
