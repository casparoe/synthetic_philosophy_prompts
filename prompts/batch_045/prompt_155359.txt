I am convening a short graduate seminar on formal epistemology, and we have just finished the material on logical uncertainty. I want to close that session with a poem rather than another handout, because the failure mode my students keep falling into is collapsing the whole apparatus back into ordinary Bayesian credences: they hear the word "probability" and immediately picture a space of epistemically possible worlds with the agent somewhere inside it, unsure which world she occupies. On the market/pricing picture that is exactly the wrong picture. Take some particular machine M that has now run for 10^12 steps without halting. There is no possible world in which M halts and another in which it does not: the sentence has a determinate truth value, and what we are unsure about is what can be derived — the future output of a process, not the world we are in. There is nothing to discover but the proof, and yet we must act, which means quoting a number today.

So: a villanelle, five tercets and a quatrain, loose iambic tetrameter or pentameter, spoken in the voice of the market that prices the sentence "M halts." Let the form carry the idea. Refrain A should be the sentence itself, returning unchanged each time, because its truth value is not what moves. Refrain B should be the demand that a price be quoted now — no deferral, no shrugging "we do not know yet" — since that obligation, and not any evidence, is what forces the number into existence. Between the returns, give the prices themselves: let them drift downward as the runtime accumulates, but never to zero and never settled, and let the last quatrain bring both refrains together with the machine still running and the price still above zero. Somewhere in the poem I want the no-arbitrage condition to be felt rather than named — a trader of bounded wealth circling the wobble in the prices and finding nothing to exploit, the wobble being ignorance and not a market error. And somewhere I want the inductive part to show: the market is not a slow proof search, it is generalizing from patterns among claims already settled, which is the only reason it can have a view before anyone has a derivation. A test I will apply: the poem should not work if you substitute a coin flip for the machine. An ordinary 0.5 is uncertainty about the world; this one is not, and the reader should sense the difference without being told it in a footnote.

Aim it at someone who works on this material and would wince at a wrong rendering, while still being a poem someone outside the field could read with pleasure. Then add a single paragraph of prose after it: say where the poem takes liberties with the framework, which reading of the prices it tacitly adopts (are they credences, or something more like a bookie's exposure?), where the form forced you to sacrifice accuracy, and what would make you rewrite it — for instance whether you would reconsider if you came to think the limiting prices are just credences over complete extensions of the theory after all, or if you concluded that the no-exploitability condition is doing more work in your poem than the villanelle can actually bear.
