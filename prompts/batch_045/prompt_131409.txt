ok so I need a hand turning a half-baked idea into a short research proposal, and sorry in advance if this is rambley — I'm in a philosophy of science reading group (mostly grad students plus one retired physicist who keeps derailing us) and we did a session on the problem of old evidence a few weeks back. Quick recap of where I'm starting, so you know what I mean: you already know some fact E with full certainty, then somebody proposes a new theory T that fits E beautifully, and it really seems like E is part of the reason to take T seriously — but if P(E) = 1 then conditioning on E can't budge P(T) at all, so on the standard Bayesian story the evidence does nothing. Everyone in the group then went off into the usual fixes (re-run the numbers as if you hadn't known E, or treat what you really learned as a new fact about what T entails, that kind of thing) and I came away convinced we were all arguing about the wrong case.

Here's the half-formed bit. As far as I can tell the debate is basically always run on the assumption that T *entails* E, or at least that the link between them is tight and deductive, and all the fixes seem to be built for that setup. But in actual science the link between a theory and an old datum almost always runs through auxiliary hypotheses, idealizations, measurement error, ceteris paribus clauses — so really P(E|T) < 1, sometimes quite a bit less. And then the arithmetic does something worse than nothing: if P(E) = 1 then P(E|not-T) = 1 too, so the likelihood ratio is just P(E|T) < 1, and conditioning on the old fact actively *lowers* T's probability. Approximate fit to old data gets punished, and the worse the fit the harsher the punishment. My friend who does climate modelling says this describes something people in her field actually argue about — models that reproduce a known trend only within error bars — and I can't decide whether the formalism is telling us something deep (there's a real epistemic cost to accomodating old data only approximately, and it's discharged only when you also say something about the gap) or whether it's just an artifact of writing P(E) = 1 for a claim nobody was ever really certain of, in which case my whole "new problem" collapses back into the old one.
