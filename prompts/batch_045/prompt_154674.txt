Putting together my views on distributive ethics for a reading group and want someone to stress-test the package before I present it. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(a) I'm a prioritarian — a benefit matters more the worse off the recipient is — and the reason I believe it is that the worse off have a stronger claim on us: greater need, more urgent.
(b) I'm not a telic egalitarian; I dont think inequality is intrinsically bad. People keep telling me (a) is just egalitarianism in disguise and I've never bought it, though maybe I should.
(c) My version of the weighting: there's a decreasing priority weight as a function of someone's welfare level, and the value of a benefit is the area under that curve across the interval the person moves through — so a benefit that carries someone a long way up earns less weight per unit as it goes, and a large gain to the second-worst-off can outweigh a small gain to the very worst-off.
(d) I think the levelling-down objection doesn't sink the view: I'm willing to say the levelled distribution is better in one respect even though it's better for nobody, and I don't think saying that forces me to give up (b).
(e) I also want strict priority to the very worst off: in a straight choice between a tiny gain to the worst-off person and a much bigger gain to someone who is badly off but not as badly off, I take the tiny gain — and I want that to hold however many of the better-off people there are and however large their gains are.
(f) I'd never actually level anyone down and I don't think anyone should; I don't see that as a reason to drop (d).

Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest way back to consistency? Rank the tensions by severity instead of flagging everything as equally bad, and for each one say exactly what would have to give and what giving it up costs me. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning — no preamble, don't restate my list back at me, and skip the background lecture on what prioritarianism is.
