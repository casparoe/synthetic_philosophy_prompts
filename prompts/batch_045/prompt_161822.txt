Please explain, for someone who already knows the basics of Madhyamaka, how the logical mechanics of Nāgārjuna's tetralemma are supposed to work. I mean the fourfold schema in which a question about some subject is answered by four candidate positions: the subject is thus; the subject is not thus; it is both; it is neither — all four of which are then rejected. I am not looking for the slogans about emptiness, but for the machinery, and there are three things I would like carefully separated, because I think they are usually run together.

First: what exactly distinguishes the third and fourth corners from the first two, given that in ordinary logic both and neither look like contradictions or tautologies? Does the schema treat negation as exclusion rather than as the assertion of an opposite, and does that matter for how the rejection is to be read?

Second: is the rejection of all four corners itself a fifth, higher-order claim (none of the four is correct), or is it a refusal to grant that the question has a determinate answer to begin with? On the first reading the position seems to be a substantive thesis that applies to itself and so seems to undermine itself; on the second it looks therapeutic or quietist. Please say what the difference amounts to formally, and which reading better handles the standard objection that the claim that all things lack intrinsic nature is itself a claim.

Third, my own half-formed worry. Call a procedure lemmatically iterable if, once all four corners have been rejected at one level, you can then pose the same fourfold question about the meta-question whether the schema captured the structure of the original question at all, and then again about that, indefinitely. Is this regress benign — each level only defeating positions actually advanced at the level below, with no commitment generated at any level — or is it instead the real content of emptiness, the fact that no level terminates in a resting place? What would tell us which it is, and does either answer collapse Madhyamaka into nihilism or into a skepticism that exempts itself?
