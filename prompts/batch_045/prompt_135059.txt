I am writing a novel, and one scene keeps failing. My character Halloran is a cost-benefit analyst for a fictional drainage authority, the Aldwick Levels Board, which holds a three-hundred-year charter and is required by it to rank projects by net benefit to the Levels as a continuing concern. In about two minutes of dialogue at a parish meeting she has to explain to Crewe, a fisherman who is intelligent, hostile, and whose family will be bought out and gone within a decade, why the Board counts a cost falling in 2150 as smaller than the very same cost falling next year. What Crewe cannot see, and what the scene has to make legible without ever naming it, is Halloran's own suspicion that only one ingredient in that arithmetic is really a moral judgement: most of it is accounting — expected growth, the chance that no one will be left to bear the cost — while the residue, the sheer discount for lateness, is a claim about what the present owes its successors.

Do not write the speech. Give me the plan behind it. In what order must those ingredients be separated for a listener who will hear 'discounting' as either inflation or as writing off people who are not yet alive? Which single case should carry the whole thing, and what must it have and lack — I suspect a seawall is the wrong choice, but say why — given that my character must also distinguish a community discounting its own continuing members from a community discounting strangers who will replace them, since her charter collapses that distinction deliberately? What will Crewe object to, in the order he will object, and at which point should Halloran concede and at which defuse? Give me the one question he asks at the end that would show the distinction had landed rather than that he had been out-argued. And tell me what the scene should openly refuse to explain.

Commit yourself: take a position on whether any pure time preference at all is defensible for a community weighing its own successors, let that verdict fix the ordering and the example, and tell me where your position is most exposed. I would rather have a defended choice than a survey of the options.
