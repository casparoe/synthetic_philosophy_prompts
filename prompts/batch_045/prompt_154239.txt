I'm taking a one-hour session on superrationality with a mixed group of first-year computer science and philosophy students. They know the payoff matrix of a one-shot prisoner's dilemma and nothing beyond it: no decision theory, no game theory past the phrase 'Nash equilibrium'. I want a plan for the explanation, not the explanation itself.

Give me the order in which the components have to be introduced and, at each step, what it depends on that has not yet been said. Tell me what the one carrying scenario must contain and must lack: I am inclined to have each student submit a sealed decision procedure to a referee who runs both and pays out, but I want to know what has to be excluded for the case to do its work — iteration, reputation, communication between the submissions, and, more delicately, whether each procedure is shown the other's source — and whether including that last feature turns it into a different lesson entirely.

Given a computer-science-heavy room, someone will propose early on 'cooperate if and only if I can verify the other procedure is identical to mine'. Is that the target or the trap, and where in the sequence should it be met?

Three further misconceptions, each of which I want placed at a particular point: that superrationality is only evidential decision theory in costume and so falls with it wherever an action is correlated with but does not cause its outcome; that it is reciprocal altruism with the iteration deleted; that it returns 'cooperate' for any prisoner's dilemma, including heavily asymmetric ones or ones where most of the other players are not superrational. For each, say what the plan must already have established for the correction to land rather than be memorised.

Close with one question that separates a student who has the idea from one who has merely learned to repeat that both players are running the same computation — I would like it to turn on a variant where the two procedures reason alike but face slightly different payoffs — and tell me what I should openly decline to settle in an hour: I assume whether superrationality is correct, and anything turning on the general contest between causal and evidential decision theory.

Please write the plan in British English.
