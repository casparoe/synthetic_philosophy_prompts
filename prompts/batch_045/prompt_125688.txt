I'm a novelist, mid-draft on a book whose subplot is a family-run animal sanctuary next door to a contract research lab, and my critique group says chapter twelve has turned into a lecture, so I'm trying to work out what I actually think rather than what my protagonist thinks, by going case by case on gut feeling. Here's where I land. A pig who's had three genuinely good years at the sanctuary, killed painlessly in her sleep and replaced by a piglet who gets an equally good life: nowhere near as bad as factory farming, but still not fine. The same pig killed painlessly and not replaced: clearly worse, and I can't say why, given she never knows either way. A lab beagle with a short but net-positive life, used in a test that removes a small chance of harm to people: I feel uneasy but not condemning, which feels like cowardice. A crow that holds a grudge against one specific person versus a mouse, same quantity of pain: worse for the crow, and I feel that even while thinking I shouldn't. A fire, save either a human infant or an adult chimp: I'd grab the infant and then feel bad about it. A lump of human brain tissue grown from skin cells, no body, no senses: destroying it means nothing to me at all. A dog grieving at its dead owner's grave versus a feral pigeon freezing to death: I think we owe the dog something, whereas the pigeon is sad but isn't owed anything. An octopus pacing a bare tank versus a chicken in a cage the same size: worse for the octopus, because boredom. And the engineered low-affect pigs in my book's near-future setting, who can't suffer much but still enjoy rooting around: eating those feels definately fine to me, and I'm suspicious of how fine it feels.

Is there one principle that gets all of these, or close to all of them? The two I can't reconcile are the crow/mouse one and the infant/chimp one: those seem to need some kind of hierarchy of beings, while almost everything else on my list looks like plain equal consideration of suffering with no hierarchy anywhere, and I can't see how to have both without the hierarchy swallowing the equality. Please don't give me a reading list or name names, my deadline is Friday and I need the shape of the thing, not sources. Give me exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, no preamble and no sign-off: the principle stated in a single sentence, then which clause of it is doing the work in which of my cases, then what it says about the case I keep going back and forth on (a herd of deer on a predator-free island, slowly starving, and does it matter whether we caused it) plus one case I haven't thought of, then which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate, and last the single verdict my protagonist should dig her heels in on when she argues with the vet in chapter nine, and why that one.
