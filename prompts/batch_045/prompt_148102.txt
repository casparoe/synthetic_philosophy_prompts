I am preparing a working paper for a municipal heritage advisory committee that has to triage a backlog of contested commemorations, and I want the centrepiece to be a taxonomy of the positions rather than another position paper. Please build it as a decision tree: every branch node should be one discriminating question, phrased so that a council member with no background in the literature could answer it, and every leaf should be a named position (label it UNNAMED where you think nobody really defends it), with one sentence on that position's most awkward commitment and one case it handles badly. Aim for ten to fourteen leaves.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of familiar slogans: start from the questions and the answer combinations they permit, then locate the actual positions inside that space, and mark the combinations nobody occupies, with a line on whether each is empty because it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Where two leaves cannot be separated by your questions, say so plainly instead of inventing a distinction to part them.

The distinctions the tree must turn on are these: whether the object is currently taken up as an honour or only used as a name and marker, and what follows when nobody alive any longer recognises the referent; whether the wrongdoing is what the person is honoured for, incidental to it, or discovered only after installation; whether the object carries non-commemorative value, whether aesthetic, documentary, or the mundane navigational value of a name people use when giving directions; who has standing to press the complaint, among descendants of those wronged, current residents, the honoree's family, the artist, or future residents; whether removal is itself a condemnatory speech act by the state or mere housekeeping; whether relocation to a museum preserves or alters the meaning; and whether deferred maintenance is genuinely a fourth option rather than a slow form of removal, and if so where it sits in the tree.

Two constraints. First, drive the tree with short concrete scenarios rather than abstractions: a hospital wing named for a surgeon whose technique was developed on patients who could not refuse; a school renamed for a living donor whose firm is later found responsible for the wrongdoing; a plaque honouring someone now wholly forgotten; a street name that is also two hundred households' address and a bus route; a monument that arrived as a diplomatic gift. Second, nothing in the deliverable may require looking anything up: no citations, no attributions, no dates, no proper names of theorists, since the committee reads these offline and I want the positions identified by their content alone.

Close with a scoring key: ten yes-or-no questions drawn from the branch nodes, with answer patterns mapped onto leaves, so that members can sort themselves by what they say about cases before they ever see the labels.
