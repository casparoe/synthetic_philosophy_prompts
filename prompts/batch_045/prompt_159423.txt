Everyone writing about cluelessness treats it as one thing: I can't know the long-run effects of what I do, so I can't tell which option is better, maybe not even in expectation. Then there are two familiar camps — the tough-luck camp, which says maximise expected value on your best evidence and that imprecise credences are no excuse for paralysis, and the "so much the worse for act-level evaluation" camp, which says cluelessness is a reason to move the unit of evaluation up to rules, character, or whole lives. I think both camps are quietly assuming a fixed grain at which acts and outcomes get individuated, and that cluelessness is actually a function of that grain. Compare "give £5k to this charity rather than that one" (maximally clueless), "be someone who gives a fixed share of income, reviewed yearly" (noise partly averages out), and "live this life rather than that one" (cluelessness returns with interest). So my question isn't whether cluelessness sinks consequentialism; it's whether there is a non-arbitrary grain of act-and-outcome individuation at which cluelessness is minimised, and — if there is — whether choosing that grain *for that reason* is a consequentialist move or a smuggled-in non-consequentialist one.

I also want to split cluelessness into three: effect-uncertainty (which effects will follow), valuational uncertainty (how to weigh them — how much a far-future person counts, how to handle tiny probabilities of enormous stakes), and calibration or meta-uncertainty (how far to trust my own probability estimates at this scale). The bit I actually want to argue is that the third behaves differently from the first two, because gathering evidence about long-run effects is itself an intervention in the system, and in a branching or chaotic stretch of the world knowing more of the mechanism can widen the spread of outcomes you can foresee rather than narrow it. Then there's a coarse-grain worry I can't yet place: averaging over many acts may hide that nearly all the value sits in a few rare ones, so going coarse buys you less cluelessness by throwing away the thing you cared about.
