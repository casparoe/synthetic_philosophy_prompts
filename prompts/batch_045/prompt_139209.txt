I'd like a decomposed credence on a precise claim about Hempel's raven paradox — an actual number with its derivation, not a survey of positions and not "it depends."

Set-up: a population of exactly 1,000 objects is fixed in advance; you know its size and otherwise nothing about its composition. Objects are revealed to you uniformly at random, without replacement. Let H be "every raven in the population is black." Your first draw is a green apple.

The claim I want a number on: that Hempel's own verdict is essentially correct and the appearance of paradox is an artifact — that the green apple really does confirm H, by an amount that is small but non-zero and not merely stipulated, and that nothing philosophically important is lost in accepting this.

Decompose it. I'd want at least: the probability that confirmation is even well-defined absent a fixed background corpus and sampling model, and how much of the paradox dissolves if it isn't; the probability that my finite, known-size population is doing substantive work rather than just making the arithmetic vivid — does a bounded domain give the apple a contribution that an unbounded or unknown-size domain withholds, and should that change the verdict about the original case; the probability that Hempel's equivalence condition is a genuine constraint on confirmation rather than the actual culprit; the probability that the load-bearing intuition is instead Nicod's criterion; and the probability that the stubborn sense that the apple is irrelevant counts as evidence against the Bayesian treatment rather than as a heuristic that misfires at extreme base rates.

Assign each a number with a one-line justification, combine them, and then tell me which sub-question your final credence is most sensitive to, what argument or variation would move it most, and where you'd guess the disagreement actually lives between someone at 0.75 and someone at 0.25.
