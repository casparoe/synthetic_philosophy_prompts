My opening: I can either stop one stranger from dying, or stop a headache in each of a million different strangers — nothing else differs, no ties to anyone, no knock-on effects, no one ever finds out. If the numbers really never count, you seem committed to saying I may save the one from death, which looks nuts. But if you say the million headaches win, you've granted that a heap of tiny harms to different people can add up and outweigh one person's death — and once you've granted that, the six-vs-one case starts to look like a mere difference in how much harm sits on each side, not a difference in principle. So: do small harms across different people add up for you or not? If they do, what was the no-numbers principle ever really claiming? And what do you do with one death versus ten thousand headaches, and why?
