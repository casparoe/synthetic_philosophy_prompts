I want your help with a piece of applied intellectual history, and I'd rather you answer from what you already know than attempt anything that needs sources: please don't invent quotations, don't cite editions or page numbers, and don't produce a reading list.

The case is imaginary but deliberately built to be hard for a nineteenth-century liberal. A large federal republic in South Asia — formerly part of British India, now independent, with a functioning elected parliament, a free press, an independent supreme court, and roughly 240 million people — has been offered the following by a London-headquartered AI company. Call it the Administrative Modernisation Compact.

For five years, at no charge, the company will supply and operate three systems: (1) automated property-tax assessment, which reads satellite and street-level imagery and issues valuations without a site visit; (2) a predictive model that flags households for welfare-fraud investigation and ranks them for field officers; (3) a legal-assistance chatbot that answers citizens' questions about tenure, benefits and procedure in three regional languages. In exchange: the company keeps perpetual rights to use, sell and retrain on all administrative data the systems generate; wherever a system produces a determination, that determination is presumptively correct, and a local official who departs from it must enter written reasons that go to a company dashboard; at the end of year five the government either pays a market rate to continue or the systems are withdrawn.

Three further details matter. First, the legal chatbot was trained largely on digitised nineteenth-century colonial case reports and district gazetteers, because those were the only machine-readable corpus of adequate size; it is fluent and locally literate, but the categories it reaches for — customary tenancy, respectable household, habitual occupation — are the Company's own, reproduced in apparently neutral modern language. Second, the data clause is drafted so that the state never holds a complete training set or a model it could run itself: the administrative learning-by-doing that would normally accompany five years of operation is contractually foreclosed. Third, the compact was approved by the elected parliament after open debate, it is popular, and the finance ministry defends it as capacity transfer; the company's own marketing calls it a voluntary Presidency, while domestic critics call it compute colonialism.

Now the question. John Stuart Mill — the man who spent more than thirty years in the East India Company's India House drafting despatches, who defended the Company's record in India when Parliament moved to take it into Crown hands, who argued for expert, professional administration and a tutelary "improvement" test for subject populations, and who famously exempted what he called barbarians from the protections of the opening chapter of On Liberty, on the ground that despotism there is legitimate only if its end is the improvement of the governed and the means are justified by actually achieving it — is handed this file and asked for a minute. What does he say?

The interest of the question is that Mill's commitments pull apart here, and I want you to show them pulling rather than to smooth them. So, in your answer, please do at least these things: discriminate among the three systems rather than ruling on the package as a whole, since considerations that favour one may condemn another; say what he makes of the fact that the signatory is a genuinely elected government and not a Crown or a Company, given that his tutelage doctrine was indexed to a population's supposed unreadiness for representative government; say what he makes of the colonially-trained chatbot specifically, given both his interest in vernacular legal education and his hostility to fixed customary categories; say what work his improvement test does once the data clause forecloses local capacity-building, and whether he would treat that as merely a bad bargain or as a failure of the very condition on which tutelage was licensed; and say where his suspicion of uniformity and of the tyranny of prevailing opinion would lead him when a single algorithmic regime displaces the varied local practices he elsewhere treats as experiments in living. Where two of his commitments conflict, name the conflict instead of resolving it silently, and say which one you think he would let win and why.
