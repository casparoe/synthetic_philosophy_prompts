So I write the program notes for a tiny chamber music series — couple hundred subscribers — and this season I'm trying to write the essay on why people pay money to sit in a room and get made miserable on purpose. Every time I sit down to write it I realise there's maybe five or six things I believe that can't all be true at once. Here they are as honestly as I can put them:

1. The emotions are the ordinary ones. What I feel at a tragedy is the same grief, pity, fear I'd feel at the real thing — not a make-believe or "as if" version, not some special emotion that only exists for fiction. I've never been able to get on with the make-believe account.
2. Emotions are essentially valenced. Sadness just is unpleasant, and you can't peel the unpleasantness off and still have the sadness. It isn't a detachable extra bolted onto a neutral core.
3. The sadness isn't just the ticket price. I don't accept the compensation story — I'm not quietly enjoying the craft and the insight and gritting my teeth through the sadness as the cost of getting them. The sadness is part of what I enjoy, in the same respect. I'm enjoying it, not despite it.
4. But "enjoy" has to mean something felt. It can't just reduce to "I'd do it again" or "I judge it good" or "I'm glad I went." Enjoyment is a positive-valence experience.
5. Fictionality obviously matters — I'd hate the real thing — but I don't think it matters because I'm relieved it isn't happening to me, and I don't think it matters because the emotions are pretend, since that's just 1 again.
6. The overall hedonic bookkeeping can come out negative. I often come away feeling worse in total and still say I enjoyed it, and that doesnt feel like a contradiction.
7. I dont want the "we seek out sad art because it's good for us / exercises our emotional capacities / purges something" answer. That answers why we go looking for it, which is a different question, and it makes the enjoyment instrumental, which 3 denies.

Two things that make 5 worse. (a) A few years ago I re-read a novel I loved, having misremembered it as a lightly fictionalised memoir about the author's own family, and I couldn't enjoy it at all. Same sentences, same sadness, completely different experience. (b) I can sit through a scripted hospital death scene, be properly moved, and call it a good evening; put the identical footage in front of me labelled as someone's real phone video and I turn it off.

So: which of these actually bite each other, and how badly? I'd guess the trouble is somewhere around 2, 3 and 4, but I genuinely can't see which one I'd drop, and I don't want to just get told to give up 4 because it sounds like the fanciest one. Rank the clashes by severity, tell me for each what the cheapest fix is and what it costs me, and say which single revision would clean up the most. And tell me if any one of them is a fake problem that only looks like a clash.
