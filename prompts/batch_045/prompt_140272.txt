hey — I'm a backend dev, no philosophy background whatsoever, and I got completely nerd sniped this week. I've been messing around with a proof assistant for fun, and I keep trying to do what feels like a totally normal move — split into cases on whether some proposition is true or false — and the thing refuses. The way I've had it explained (just write as if this is right): in that setting a proof of "A or B" is supposed to hand back *which* side you're on, and a proof of "there exists an x" is supposed to hand back an actual x you could print, so case splitting on a question nobody can answer isn't free. And apparently you *can* prove not-not-(P or not P) there, you just can't get from not-not-(P or not P) to (P or not P). That gap is the bit I want the poem to live in.

Concretely: I have a search running that's hunting for a counterexample to a conjecture, it hasn't halted, I have no idea if it ever will. Define N = 0 if it never halts, N = 2^(-n) if it halts at step n. Ordinary math says N is either zero or it isn't, no drama. Here I can't assert N = 0 (that would prove the search never finishes) and I can't assert N > 0 (that would prove it does), and I can't even call N rational, since that means handing over an actual p/q. I love that: a number that can't say whether it's zero.

So: a sonnet, 14 lines, in the voice of the type checker talking to me. Octave = the complaint: you keep giving me existence proofs with nothing inside them, disjunctions where nobody picked a side, I'm not saying you're wrong, I'm saying I can't run what you gave me. Volta = the moment it lands that this isn't skepticism or relativism — the checker isn't denying P or not P, it's declining to assert it, and it'll happily certify not-not-(P or not P) while still never handing over P or not P. Couplet = precisely what has to be given up: the step from not-not-A to A.

Accuracy matters more than prettiness — someone who's actually done a constructivity exercise shouldn't wince. But someone who knows none of it should still get a poem about a number that can't tell you if it's zero. Ideally zero jargon inside the poem itself (no "constructive", no "intuitionistic", no "excluded middle" — make me see it). Meter can be loose, don't mangle sense for it, and please don't rhyme "true" with "you".
