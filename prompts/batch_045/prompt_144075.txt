I write literary fiction, and I am stuck on a short scene that has to carry a philosophical load without ever naming it. What I need is a case built to specifications, not an illustration.

The setting must be ordinary working or domestic life: no science fiction, no artificial intelligence, no neuroscience, nothing a reader would file as a thought experiment. Some entity in it — an animal, a machine, an institution, the habit-structure of a person: your choice — is predictable across the stretch of the text in two different ways: by the design stance, which reads off what it was made or shaped to do, and by the intentional stance, which reads off what it believes and wants. Those two attributions must be genuine rivals, not one attribution in two vocabularies.

Specifications:

1. Up to a single crux, each stance must compress the behavioural data the reader has actually been shown about equally well — a comparable predictive record and a comparable brevity of description. No rigging toward either side.
2. The physical stance — the mechanism as such — must be genuinely unavailable to the reader, yet the crux must be the kind of thing the physical stance would settle: a plain question of what happens next.
3. At the crux the two stances must issue conflicting predictions, and whichever way it goes, the losing stance must have been tracking something real rather than merely projecting it. The scene's emotional weight depends on the reader feeling a way of seeing collapse instead of an error get corrected.
4. Because this is fiction, the only data is the prose, so treat compressibility as compressibility of what the reader has read.
5. If it can be done without strain, let the colder-sounding of the two stances be the one that grants the entity more.

Deliver, in this order: the scene itself, 250 to 350 words and free of theory; then the demonstration — the compression accounting for each stance, the divergence at the crux, which stance wins under each possible outcome and why, whether the defeated pattern still counts as real on the compressibility criterion and how that criterion handles rival real patterns, the one structural feature doing the work and what happens to the case if it is removed, and how a defender of the losing stance would try to repair it.

Finally: tell me what would change your mind — where this design is most likely to be cheating — and which revision you would attempt first.
