So our school does this Tolerance Week assembly thing every spring and somehow I got picked to give the 3 minute student speech at this years one. Context you need: last week the principal un-invited a student group from tabling at the assembly, and half the school thinks that is literally the opposite of tolerance while the other half thinks it was obvious. I'm kind of on the principal's side but I can't figure out how to say why without either sounding like a hypocrite at a tolerance assembly or sounding like I believe tolerance just means tolerate everything, which I don't.

Can you write it? The constraints I actually have to work inside: (1) I'm not allowed to name the group or describe what they believe in the speech — the principal's rule, apparently repeating it out loud is how it spreads — so the argument has to work without ever saying what the view is. (2) Half the room is half-asleep freshmen plus parents, the other half is the debate club, who'll notice instantly if the reasoning is lazy, so it needs to be plain and still hold up. (3) Please use the point that tolerance is only ever about stuff you genuinely dislike — if you're fine with something you're not tolerating it, you're just agreeing — and then deal with the weird consequence I keep getting stuck on, that the more okay a school gets with everything the less tolerance is actually happening at all, plus who even gets to decide what counts as intolerant in the first place. Don't make it a both-sidesy speech that says nothing, and don't make me sound like I'm forty five.
