hi — hoping someone can help with a thing thats been nagging at me for years. It started with birding but I've since noticed it at work too, so I don't think it's really about birds.

Background: I get a bad, brief look at a bird. Nothing becomes certain — no probability goes to 1 — my confidence in a hypothesis just moves: 30% peregrine before, 60% after. I'm told there's a standard generalization of ordinary conditioning for exactly this case (named after someone whose name I keep forgetting) and that it works by keeping fixed how likely the hypothesis is given each cell of some partition, and only shifting the spread across the cells. OK. My problem is what to do when one look does two things at once.

Say the glance leaves me more confident that the tail looked short (0.5 up to 0.8) and also more confident that the wing bar looked pale (0.5 up to 0.7). They arrive together; there's no first and second. But the only numbers I have are those two shifts on their own. To use the rule I seem to need new probabilities for all four combinations of short/not and pale/not, and two marginal shifts don't fix four numbers — I can fill them in a dozen ways and each gives a different answer for peregrine. And if I cheat by applying the shifts one after the other, the answer depends on which goes first, and neither order is more honest, because the look wasn't in two instalments. At work it's the same shape: a scan pushes my confidence in a bleed up, a blood test pushes my confidence in an infection up, both land in the same hour, and I can't tell how worried to be until I've decided how the two pieces go together.

Is there a name for this? People keep telling me it's just that marginals don't determine a joint, which is true but doesn't feel like it settles anything. What I want: whether there's an established category for an experience that underdetermines your update, what the nearest named things are and exactly how my case differs from each (I suspect it's not the same as the well-known puzzle about one vague constraint on a coarse classification, and not the same as the complaint that doing two updates in the other order gives a different result, though both feel definately adjacent), and if there's no name, what you'd call it and where you'd draw the edges. Does it matter whether the two shifts bear on the same hypothesis or different ones? Does it vanish if they're independent? Does it change if one of the shifts is about whether I can trust my own eyes? Also: is there any principled reason to prefer one way of filling the gap over another, or is the honest answer to hold a whole set of distributions and refuse to pick?

No reading suggestions please, I've got no library out here. And no headings, bullets, bold or tables — just continuous prose, like an email.
