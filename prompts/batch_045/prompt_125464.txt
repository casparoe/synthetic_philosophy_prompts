Hi — need a favour. I'm giving the department colloquium in a fortnight and I want to actually be ready for the question period this time.

The talk defends a view I've been calling commitment-without-occupancy, aimed at the problem of logical omniscience (on a Bayesian or possible-worlds picture of belief, an ideal agent comes out knowing every logical consequence of what it knows, which no real person does). My claim: stop treating an agent as having one belief box that's either closed or leaky. There are two tiers. What you *occupy* — the propositions actually available to you right now, the ones you can use in reasoning and action. And what you're merely *committed to* — the unnoticed consequences of what you occupy. Those constrain what you can consistently say, and they're what an interlocutor may legitimately charge you with, but they don't guide anything until you notice them. Then one norm, the Audit Rule: when a latent commitment is made salient to you, you must either avow it or give up something you do occupy. So inconsistency isn't a defect of a belief system at a time; it's a debt someone can call in, and being a good reasoner is partly a disposition to settle those debts in advance.
