In three weeks I am presenting a paper at a small metaphysics workshop, and what I want from you is a Q&A prep sheet rather than a defense of the view. Below is my thesis, an abbreviated version of the argument, and a description of the room. What I need is the question period.

**Thesis.** The familiar metaontological disputes — whether the quantifier is fully unrestricted, whether mereological sums exist, whether there are composite objects at all, whether there are propositions — are not factual disputes. But they are also not *merely verbal*, in the sense that verdict is normally meant to carry. They are cases of what I call load-bearing notational variance: the competing languages are intertranslatable, but they differ in which consequences they make easy for a competent but non-ideal inquirer to notice, check, and be tempted to draw. Metaontology should therefore become notational pluralism *with a ranking*: existence questions have no answer beyond the choice of notation, but the choice is not arbitrary, because notations can be ordered by their inferential affordances relative to a specified research program, and that ordering is a question about proof length, search complexity, and error rates rather than a question about taste.

**Argument sketch.** (1) Call two candidate notations strongly intertranslatable when there is a recursive translation mapping each sentence of the one onto a sentence of the other that preserves truth value across the relevant class of models. Strong intertranslatability entails that there is no factual disagreement left between the parties. (2) Some strongly intertranslatable pairs are inert: the choice between them makes no difference to anything anyone goes on to do — the analog, in a formal setting, of a proof assistant's syntactic sugar that is mechanically expanded before processing. (3) Other pairs are load-bearing: the notations differ in inferential affordance. One makes a certain class of consequence visible in a two-line derivation; the other buries it behind a page of paraphrase. (4) The metaontological cases are of the second kind, which is why the disputes feel substantive and why they refuse to dissolve when the parties agree on meaning. (5) Hence the ranking in the thesis: the residual disagreement is about which research program to serve plus a partly formal, partly empirical question about affordance, and that question has an answer.

**The room.** Twelve people. Three ontological realists of the joint-carving stripe, who think there is a privileged structure and that the job of metaphysics is to find it. Two quantifier-variantist deflationists in the Carnapian tradition, happy to say the disputes are verbal and tolerant about which language you like. One person who works on verbal disputes and conceptual ethics and will care about my criteria more than my conclusions. Two easy-ontology people, who think existence questions are settled trivially once the relevant terms are in place and that the interesting questions lie elsewhere. The remaining four are general metaphysicians who regard ontology as a substantive inquiry and are antecedently hostile to any deflationary diagnosis. Nobody in the room works on proof complexity, formal epistemology, or anything that would let them assess the empirical claim in step (5).

**What I want.** The seven or eight objections I will actually get, ordered by how likely they are multiplied by how much damage they do, each phrased the way the relevant subgroup would phrase it and not the way I would paraphrase it afterward. For each: a reply I can deliver in about ninety seconds, and a candid note on where that reply is soft. Separately: what I should concede in the first two minutes before anyone asks, the single reply that has to land if the paper is to survive at all, and the question I am hoping nobody asks.

Two standing constraints on your answer. First, assume I have already read the standard moves on all sides; don't give me the survey. Second, and most important: keep separate what is genuinely settled among people who work on these topics from what is contested, and never present a contested diagnosis as the received view. In particular, flag explicitly which steps of my argument rest on premises that a substantial number of specialists reject, which of my distinctions are doing work that no one has shown they can do, and where my step (5) is closer to a promissory note than to a result.
