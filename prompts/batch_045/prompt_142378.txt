hey — I'm in a seminer on contingency in history and we have to do a short critical reading of a piece of fiction through one of the distinctions we've been using, so I picked Bradbury's A Sound of Thunder (1952, the time-safari-to-shoot-a-T-rex one: the client steps off the floating path, crushes a butterfly under his boot, and comes back to a present where the wrong candidate won the election and the spelling on the sign has changed). The distinction is nail-type vs tape-type contingency: nail-type is where a tiny event just flips which of two outcomes that were already sitting there gets realised (for want of a nail the kingdom is lost — the horse, the rider, the battle, the kingdom), while tape-type is where you replay the whole sequence and the trajectory itself goes somewhere else entirely, so there isn't a stable pair of outcomes to flip between. And then there's the third thing we keep tripping over, that chaos in Lorenz's sense is sensitive dependence inside a deterministic system, which is a claim about the limits of prediction, not about history being metaphysically fragile.

What I can't settle is which of these the story is actually dramatising, honeslty I think it may be cheating. The election bit looks nail-type — the candidate set is fixed, one name swaps for another — but the story gets cited as the butterfly effect story and the ending, where the guide levels his rifle at the client, seems to need the fragility to be total. There's also a tension I want someone to press on: the safari company's whole business model assumes you can go back and shoot the dinosaurs that were going to die anyway, so most interventions wash out, and yet one insect rewrites everything and nothing can be done about it.
