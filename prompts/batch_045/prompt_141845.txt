I've been reading rationalist blogs since the Sequences days (map/territory, cached thoughts, all that) and I work as an ML engineer — mostly interpretability-flavoured stuff these days, linear probes, activation patching, staring at feature dashboards — and I keep having the same dead-end argument with a philosopher friend about whether what you believe can reach down into what you *see*. Her version is about whether perception is 'cognitively penetrable', and she says it matters twice over: penetrability is supposed to be a test of whether perception is a module in the Fodor-y sense, and if perception is penetrable then it can't do the job people keep wanting it to do, which is acting as a check on your beliefs that is independent of your beliefs. I can't fight her on her turf, so I want to translate her side (and the standard pushback against it — it's just attention, it's just a shift in judgment, it's response bias, it's just inference and everyone already agrees perception is inference) into the vocabulary I actually use, and see what survives.

Here's the vocabulary: a frozen pretrained backbone versus one you fine-tune end to end; gradients from the task head writing into early layers versus context tokens conditioning a frozen model at inference time; where an effect *lives* — in the activations (probe it out) or only in the logits; modularity as encapsulation, i.e. the head can read the backbone but not write to it; priors and likelihoods in a generative model; the epistemic worry restated as a contaminated validation set, or a Kalman filter whose measurement noise is set way too low so it mostly echoes its own prior, or a sensor that's been wired into the thing it was supposed to be independently checking. I've got a homebrew three-way distinction I lean on — 'weight-penetration' (the belief literally changed the parameters), 'context-penetration' (weights are frozen, but top-down context changes what the frozen model computes), and 'readout-penetration' (the representation is the same, only the decision layer moved) — and my suspicion is that a lot of the experimental results people cite only establish the third and get reported as the first. Run at least these through it for me, one by one: the McGurk effect; the duck-rabbit where someone tells you it's a rabbit first and it flips; wine experts tasting; a missing phoneme getting filled in so you hear a word that isn't there; and my favourite, a radiologist whose expectations seem to change what's visible in the scan. For each, say which of my three it maps to, or whether it doesn't fit any of them.

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. What translated cleanly, what you had to bend, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. I already have guesses about three bits of residue and I want you to tell me which are real: (1) nothing in my framework corresponds to what the image on the screen looks *like* for anyone, so the distinction between 'it looks different' and 'I judge it differently' has nowhere to live; (2) there's no principled line in a net between perceptual and cognitive — only lines I impose, like frozen-vs-trainable or early-vs-late layers or timescale — so 'is perception a module?' partly degenerates into 'where do you draw the line?'; (3) the normative half, whether you're *entitled* to the resulting belief, has no counterpart, since all I have is accuracy and calibration. For each bit of residue, tell me whether it's my framework's blind spot or a sign the philosophers' question was less sharp than it sounds. Also: please flag anywhere a translation quietly smuggles in one of my framework's assumptions and calls it a translation — I'd guess 'perception is inference' is the big one, since if you assume that then penetrability sounds trivially true. And finally, straight out: is 'penetration' carving at a joint, or should the whole debate be reframed as a question about timescales — fast learning inside a forward pass versus slow learning in the weights — in which case a good chunk of it collapses into something I already have tools for?
