My roommate and I got into a two-hour argument about wireheading last night and I definately do not have it straightened out. Here's roughly where I landed, and I want to know whether it hangs together.

(1) Wireheading and reward tampering generally are failure modes, not solutions: the reward signal was only ever a stand-in for the state of the world we wanted, and when the signal and the world come apart, the world wins.
(2) That said, I don't think there's anything wrong in principle with a system that's motivated purely by the signal. If the channel is intact and the signal is a perfect proxy for what we want, caring only about the signal is fine — the badness is in the coming apart, not in the signal-love.
(3) On the human side I'm a desire-satisfaction person: no objective list of goods, what's good for you is getting what you actually want. So someone who wholeheartedly wants to plug into Nozick's experience machine, knows exactly how it works, and never wants out is made better off by plugging in.
(4) I don't think there's a deep difference in kind between an AI's reward signal and a human's pleasure. Both are internal things set up by a selection process to track something outside themselves, and both can come loose from what they track.
(5) I also don't buy appeals to a "real self": an agent that rewrites its own reward channel and a person who revises their ends after reflection are both just engaging in self-modification, and talk about "the goals I'd have had if I hadn't messed with myself" doesn't do any work.
(6) And I think corrigibility is non-negotiable in design: a system that resists being shut down, or whose objective we can't revise, is malfunctioning, and its resistance doesn't get any weight as a preference — even once the system is sophisticated enough to have plans.
