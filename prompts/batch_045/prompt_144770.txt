I spent a career in field ecology and have come to philosophy late, by way of probability and causation. I now sit on a volunteer panel that reviews the automated systems used by our state's housing-assistance agency, and we have run into what I take to be the practical form of a familiar impasse.

The system is two-staged: every applicant receives a score, and only the highest-scoring fifth is referred for a costly in-person eligibility review. That review can end in either of two ways — it can unlock aid, which benefits the applicant, or it can terminate an existing award for ineligibility, which does not — so even the sign of the outcome's value is in dispute. The groups we are required to compare differ in their measured rates of the outcome the score predicts; those rates drift between retrainings; and because only the referred fifth is ever reviewed, error rates can be estimated only on that selected pool and never on the applicant population as a whole.

As I understand the relevant impossibility results, calibration, equal false-positive and false-negative rates, and predictive parity cannot in general hold together under those conditions: something has to be surrendered. What the mathematics does not tell us is what to surrender, and that is precisely the decision before us.

Please write a decision procedure our panel can actually run: the questions in order, what would count as a sufficient answer to each, the points at which the procedure returns a stop — do not deploy, or do not use this criterion at all — and the points at which it must hand the question back to us as a matter of values rather than fact. It has to be usable by a panel with no statistician on it, meeting monthly, that must produce a one-page recommendation a director will sign, using only data the agency already logs. It must say what to do when a required quantity cannot be estimated, when a group is small enough that the estimates are mostly noise, when the rates have drifted since the last retraining, and when the agency's counsel demands a single headline disparate-impact figure that the mathematics does not uniquely settle.

Apply the procedure to two or three cases of your own construction, at least one of which should yield an answer our panel will not welcome, and close by stating plainly what the procedure cannot settle. Please write in American English.
