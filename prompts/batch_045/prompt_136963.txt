I teach a first-year seminar on mind and language — 16 students, 80 minutes, very mixed levels. I want an activity where they run into whats weird about Moore's paradox (the "it's raining but I dont believe it" sentence) by doing something, and only hear the name at the end — no mini-lecture up front.

My rough idea was a game where they score points for asserting things, with payoffs set up so insincere assertion is sometimes the rational move, so they feel that the oddness isnt just about lying or saying something false. But I cant work out how to get them from there to the first-personal bit, and I definately want a second phase where the speaker is split: pairs or trios who have to produce one joint assertion through a single spokesperson while each member holds private information, so that some "p but we dont believe p" utterances come out fine and others dont — and they notice the difference before I say anything about it.

Give me rules, materials, a payoff scheme that needs no money, and a debrief that maps what happened onto (i) plain contradictoriness, (ii) the specific Moorean weirdness, (iii) what changes when the subject is a committee. Also: what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be honest about which part of the puzzle the activity simply cant stage — the bit where I have to tell them what the absurdity is supposed to be and whats at stake in explaining it. In British English please.
