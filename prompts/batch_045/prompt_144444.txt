I'm writing a novel about a Dutch merchant family in the 1650s, and I keep banging my head against Max Weber's Protestant ethic thesis. Here's how I've got it in my head: Weber basically showed that Calvinist anxiety about predestination caused modern capitalism. The idea is that people who couldn't know whether they were saved threw themselves into disciplined work and reinvestment to calm the terror, and over time that produced the spirit of capitalism. So I've built a silly novelist's gadget, the Protestant Ethic Pressure Gauge, or PEPG. Every time a character has a nasty bout of double-predestination dread, the gauge goes up; the gauge is wired to their ledger, so higher dread means less spent on luxuries and more plowed back into shipping insurance, workshops, and Atlantic ventures. If I crank the PEPG high enough, capitalism more or less has to show up in the plot.

I also gave each character a doctrinal half-life: the number of years their Calvinist dread keeps shaping their ledger after they privately stop believing. In my draft, the third-generation characters get rich, start skipping church, and laugh at predestination, but their bookkeeping habits stay just as ruthless. That makes me think the religious values were only a one-time trigger, or maybe a useful illusion that got internalized, not a real continuing cause.

But historically-minded friends say I'm committing the sin of treating culture as a cause, and that Weber wasn't doing that at all. I don't get it. If values can push people around like this, why isn't that a cause? And if the later capitalists stop believing yet keep behaving like capitalists, doesn't that prove culture was just the match that lit a fire that then burned on its own? So which is it: is my PEPG a straight causal mechanism, or just a metaphor for something subtler? What exactly is the trap I'm falling into, and how should a novelist think about values causing things without turning ideas into billiard balls? Please don't give me a bibliography or footnotes; I can't check sources online, I just want the conceptual plumbing, and I want to keep the plumbing seperate from the poetry.

I definately have a blind spot here, so I also want the epistemic stuff: tell me how confident you are in your verdict, and—more importantly—say what would change your mind. What argument or consideration would make you think my PEPG actually captures something Weberian after all, or that the doctrinal half-life idea gets at how values persist? And where is your own reasoning most likely to fail, especially since I'm using this gauge as a novelist's metaphor rather than a real sociological variable?
