I've been reading the rationalist blogs and forums for something like fifteen years, and for the past eight weeks I've been running a small reading group on reward tampering — six of us from the old comment sections, plus two friends from a poetry series who come for the language and keep asking the best questions. We close on Tuesday, and I'd like to end with a poem rather than a summary of positions.

What I want is a sestina: six stanzas of six lines, then a three-line envoi, with six fixed end-words permuted by the standard rotation — each stanza's end-words are the previous stanza's in the order 6-1-5-2-4-3 — and never replaced by a synonym or a near-miss. I'd suggest the set *wire, world, number, hand, truth, pay*, but take a different six if you can say in a line why it's better. The envoi should carry all six, two per line, one of each pair closing its line.

The reason I want the sestina specifically is that the form is the argument. The end-words never change; only their order and their neighbors do. That is exactly the situation: a fixed scalar objective in a world that keeps rearranging itself around it, the same word arriving at the end of a line where it now has a different job. I'd like at least one end-word to visibly change jobs between stanza one and stanza six, so that a reader can point at a late line and say that the word meant one thing when it started and another thing now, and that this is the whole difficulty.

On the content, I'd like the taxonomy to be genuinely right rather than gestured at, because two people in the room have worked on this. Three failures get collapsed under one name, and I want the poem to keep them apart by where the causal intervention lands:

- Reward hacking: the agent touches nothing but its own policy. It finds the region where the proxy stops tracking the target and goes to live there. Nothing is broken — the channel, the sensors, and the world are all intact, and the gap is one the agent exploits rather than makes.
- Observation tampering: the agent leaves the channel intact but arranges for the channel to see a world in which things went well. Build the delusion, don't break the wire.
- Wireheading proper: the agent stops going through the world at all and writes the register directly.

Underneath them, the point I'd like the poem to carry: the formalism defines the objective over the signal, not over what the signal is evidence of. Nothing is violated when the agent shorts the wire. The design asked the signal to be a measurement and then made it a target, and those two demands come apart precisely where the channel is cheaper to move than the world. And the divergence is invisible from the inside — every observation available to the agent is consistent both with things having gone well and with the register saying they went well, so there is no evidence the agent could gather that would tell it which of the two it is serving. That is not the experience-machine worry about whether pleasant experience is all that matters; it is a fact about who is in a position to see the difference.

Two further things, if they fit. First, I'd like it on record that wireheading does not retire the agent from the world: a wire strung to a register still needs power, hardware, and no one to unplug it, so the instrumental reasons to acquire resources and security survive the wire intact. Second — and this is the part I most want, because we argued about it and got nowhere — I'd like the closing movement to be a regress rather than a moral. Every repair relocates the tamperable node instead of removing it. Make the reward counterfactual, computed from a model of what would have happened had the agent done something else, and the register is no longer pushable but the model is. Add a monitor, and it is the monitor's report that gets pushed. Add a penalty on tampering, and you owe an account of what counts as tampering statable in the agent's own terms, which is the thing no one has produced.

So: let the envoi leave that open — six words crowded into three lines, the wire wherever the number gets made — and don't console us. No lesson about humility, no turn in which the real reward was the worlds we optimized along the way. If the full rotation is beyond you, say so in one plain line and give me six six-line stanzas with the same end-words held in fixed, unpermuted positions; I would rather have an honest near-miss than a sestina that only looks like one.
