hey — so I'm in this tiny forecasting club at my school (like eight people, we make weekly predictions on school stuff and score ourselves) and we've been haveing the exact same argument for over a year and it goes nowhere, and I want to know why rather than who's right.

The fight is about the outside view vs the inside view. Concretely: someone has to predict something specific — will the robotics team finish the bot before regionals, will the spring musical make back its budget, will our fundraiser hit its target. Half the club says: use the outside view. Find the reference class, take the base rate, adjust only slightly, because everyone thinks their case is special and they're almost always wrong about that (planning-fallacy stuff, and the fact that our own track record for these predictions is honestly embarrassing). The other half says that throws away literally everything you know about this particular case, that a base rate over a grab-bag reference class is close to noise, and that "epistemic modesty" has basically turned into a way to refuse to use your own information and get credit for humility while doing it. Both sides have heard every argument the other side has and neither has budged a millimeter.

My hypothesis, which I'm not confident about: it isn't one dispute, it's three wearing one name. (1) An accuracy question — which method actually forecasts better, and for what kind of event. (2) A decision question — what to do, where the details of the case can matter even if the base rate is your best single number, because the payoff can depend on exactly where in the distribution you are. (3) Some kind of normative/social question about what you owe other people epistemically, or what it's respectable to say out loud — where "modesty" is doing work that has nothing to do with being right. Plus I have a suspicion the whole outside view isn't as information-free as it's sold: you always pick the reference class using what you know about the case, so the inside view is getting smuggled back in through the side door.

So: is this verbal, or a real substantive disagreement, or two groups answering different questions under one heading, or a hidden empirical question nobody has data on (how often is case-specific detail actually informative?)? Is my three-questions thing right, or just a way of dodging the argument? And what would count as progress — I was thinking everyone in the club gives both an inside estimate and a base-rate estimate for the same list of school events, we score them seperately, and someone else assigns the reference class blind so nobody can sneak in what they know. Would that settle anything, or would both sides just explain away whatever came out? Also honest answer please: is "how much should you defer to the outside view" even a well-posed question, or is the fact that we've stalled a sign we asked it wrong?

Please don't give me a survey of the literature or a reading list, I've already read the wikipedia page and I want a diagnosis with actual reasons.
