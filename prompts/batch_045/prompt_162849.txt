Two philosophers of physics have spent years arguing about the hole argument and have agreed, in good faith, to an adversarial collaboration. One is a manifold substantivalist: she holds that the points of the spacetime manifold are individuals whose identities are not conferred by the fields that occupy them, that a diffeomorphism which is the identity outside some hole but non-trivial inside it therefore maps a model of a generally covariant theory onto a genuinely distinct physical possibility, and hence that such a theory is indeterministic — the fields everywhere outside the hole fail to fix the fields at the points inside it. The other is a relationist: he accepts Leibniz equivalence, takes the diffeomorphism-related models to be two descriptions of a single physical situation, and reads the argument as a reductio of its substantivalist premise rather than as a discovery about determinism.

Write the joint statement both of them could sign, at the level of a paper's summary section, in three parts.

1. Exactly five agreed claims, numbered 1–5, one or two sentences each. They should be substantial enough that neither side could have signed them before the collaboration, and neutral enough that neither side's conclusion is smuggled in. Cover at minimum: what the hole argument validly establishes if its substantivalist premise is granted; the sense in which the dispute carries no difference in any diffeomorphism-invariant quantity; whether the residue is best called indeterminism, underdetermination, or something else again; and one real concession from each side about the strength of the other's best case.

2. The residual disagreements, each stated twice — once in wording the substantivalist would accept for her own view, once in wording the relationist would accept for his. Include whether the substantivalist's indeterminism is a cost or a discovery, and whether sophisticated substantivalism — points as individuals, plus Leibniz equivalence — is a stable third position or relationism under another name. If some part of the dispute turns out to be merely verbal, say which part and why.

3. A table, one row per crux, with columns for the crux, each side's favoured formulation of it, what both accept would bear on its resolution, and each side's prediction written down in advance. Use these four cruxes.

(a) The reference fluid. Suppose the theory also contains fields that physically realize a coordinate system — a material reference fluid, a family of worldlines able to serve as names for points. Stipulate that in this theory dragging the metric across the hole while holding the fluid fixed does not in general yield another solution, whereas dragging all the fields together does. Does the argument still generate indeterminism, and for whom?

(b) The hole with no outside. In a closed universe — compact, without boundary — let the hole be nearly the whole of spacetime, so that there is no surrounding region in which the fields are held fixed. Does the argument still run, does it lapse, or does it return as a global indeterminism?

(c) The small symmetry group. Consider a theory with a fixed background structure whose symmetry group is small, say a rigid one-parameter group, and ask whether the hole argument can be reconstructed there. Does the answer show that the argument was never really about general covariance — which, after all, almost any spacetime theory can be made to have — but about the absence of background structure?

(d) The successor theory. Suppose the manifold is not part of the fundamental ontology of whatever supersedes our current theory, and that spatiotemporal talk is recovered only in some limit. What becomes of the dispute: does it transfer to the new ontology, dissolve, or lose its subject matter?

Give me the five numbered claims first, then the paired disagreement statements, then the table. No preamble, no closing summary, no bibliographic pointers.
