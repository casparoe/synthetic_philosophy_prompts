I want a case built to order on the self-sampling vs. self-indication assumptions, and I want it to run the opposite direction from the usual presumptuous-philosopher setup. Here are the working definitions I'm using, so please use mine rather than swapping in your own:

- Self-sampling (SSA): reason as if you were picked at random from some reference class of observers. So the posterior on a hypothesis H is proportional to its prior times the fraction of observers in that reference class, under H, whose evidence matches yours.
- Self-indication (SIA): first multiply each hypothesis's prior by the total number of observers it says exist, and then do the sampling step. Equivalently: posterior is proportional to prior times the number of observers under H whose evidence matches yours.

What I want from you: a case where SSA is the reckless one. SSA should end up at least 99% confident in some substantive contingent claim about how things actually are, inferred from a single first-personal datum, while SIA stays basically where the priors put it — it shouldn't move by more than a factor of 2. And to make sure this isn't just the standard SIA-likes-big-worlds story in disguise, I want both hypotheses to posit exactly the same number of observers. So the whole disagreement has to come from somewhere other than the observer-count boost.

Constraints, and I do mean all of them:

- Everyone involved is alive at the same time, in one mundane setting — a conference, a hospital, a stadium, an airport. No birth ranks, no doomsday, no cosmology, no multiverse, no deity tossing coins, no infinities, and no more than a few thousand people (number spaces, codes, ticket ranges etc. can of course be bigger than that).
- The datum is one thing a single person could notice about themselves: a badge number, a seat, a room, a place in line, something on a screen.
- Under both hypotheses, exactly one person ends up with your exact evidence. Same count either way. So SIA has literally nothing to grip onto and the shift has to come entirely from the denominator SSA divides by.
- The two hypotheses must be equally supported by everything non-anthropic. Say explicitly how you arrange that and why the priors come out equal (a private coin flip deciding which procedure gets used is fine).
- Keep the scenario itself to one paragraph.

Then show your work:

1. The SSA computation, with your reference class named out loud. Then redo it twice: once with a narrower class (only the people who share your exact evidence) and once with a much wider one (everyone alive). Tell me what happens to the shift in each case.
2. The SIA computation, showing that it really doesn't move.
3. Attach a decision: two options, stakes a normal person would care about, same utility function on both theories, and the expected utilities spelled out. I want them to come apart — if you need to make one payoff slightly bigger to break SIA's tie and get it picking the other option, do that and say you did.
4. Isolate the feature doing the work, and tell me what happens if you remove it — what if the datum were something you'd have gotten under either hypothesis anyway?
5. Give me the strongest thing an SIA defender says to kill the case, and your honest read on wether it works.

If some of those constraints turn out to be jointly unsatisfiable, say so and tell me which one has to give — but I'm fairly sure they can all be met at once, definately with a bit of care about how the numbers get assigned.
