I have no philosophy background whatsoever — I got into this by accident, trying to write automations for our flat (fan, thermostat, lights) and getting stuck on conditions like "run the fan if it's warm and the room feels stuffy", where each half is fuzzy in the ordinary everyday way. My partner writes code and told me to just give each half a number between 0 and 1, then take the smaller of the two numbers for "and" and the larger for "or". I actually tried that on paper and it kept spitting out answers that felt wrong to me, and I've been chewing on it for weeks now.

Here are the cases that bug me. One: a sentence can feel half-right while the sentence you get by sticking it together with its own opposite isn't half of anything. "The room is warm" feels half-right, "the room is not warm" feels half-right in exactly the same way, but "the room is warm and the room is not warm" is flatly false and "the room is warm or the room is not warm" is flatly true — no middling-ness survives the joining, which the smaller-number/larger-number rule doesn't predict at all. Two: sometimes the joining depends on whether the two half-rightnesses are hooked up to each other. "The wall is blue" is borderline and "the wall is green" is borderline, but "the wall is blue or green" strikes me as plainly true and "blue and green" as plainly false, whereas "the wall is blue" and "the soup is hot" are both equally borderline and yet "blue or hot" just sits there doing nothing, and "the wall is blue if and only if the soup is hot" seems obviously wrong even though both halves are equally middling. Three, and this is the one that started it: if the fan needs both warm and stuffy and each is about fifty-fifty on its own, my gut says the combined condition is worse than fifty-fifty, not the same as it.

So, does any of this have a name? I've bumped into the heap puzzle — take away one grain at a time and nobody can say where it stops being a heap — but as far as I can tell that's about walking along a series of cases, and what I keep hitting happens in one step, inside a single sentence built out of two others. I've also seen the phrase higher-order vagueness somewhere, though from what I can make out that's about not knowing whether something is borderline in the first place, which isn't quite my thing either. I'm not after a label for its own sake: I want to know how many distinct things I've stumbled onto here, what the nearest established concepts are and precisely where each of them stops matching my cases, and if some chunk of it really hasn't been carved out, how you'd define it and what the edge cases of that definition would look like. My partner says the smaller-number rule is only one option among several and the others behave differently, which makes me suspect at least part of this is a known objection to something rather than anything new — but I can't tell which part, and that's really what I want sorted out.
