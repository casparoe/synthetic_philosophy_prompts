Ok so context: I somehow ended up as secretary of our neighbourhood association (long story, nobody else wanted the job) and for the past year I've been watching a guy on our commitee who I can't get a handle on, and I want a word for what he does. You're a political theorist who works on democracy and populism — take your time with this, and before you settle on an answer, brainstorm out loud for a bit: list the two or three nearest concepts you can think of and say what's wrong with each of them. I don't want a bare label, I want the carving: what the thing is, what it isn't, and where its edges are. And please don't point me at papers or reading lists, I have no access to a university library, just explain it.

Two episodes. Last spring our own survey showed members wanted the reserve fund spent on the clubhouse roof, by a lot. He started giving these short speeches about how what we really needed was to spend the reserve on suing the county over the bus depot. Nobody was into it. He got a petition going, it got talked about at every meeting, and eight weeks later we ran essentially the same survey and 60% wanted the lawsuit. His line: I don't lead anybody, I just say out loud what people already feel. The part that got me is that several members told me separately that they hadn't changed their minds — they'd finally understood what they'd wanted all along. So the fact that he had moved them was, for him and for them, the evidence that he was only following them.

Second: in November the association voted on a motion he opposed and it lost badly. He didn't claim fraud. He said the people who voted it down aren't the real association — mostly newer members, people who show up to one meeting a year, people who don't know the history — and that the people who built this place would never have voted that way. And this isn't some ad hoc excuse: he's been saying who the real members are for years, consistently, since long before any of these votes.

Are these one phenomenon or two different ones that happen to share a man? Is there an established concept for either? I half remember a podcast about Rousseau distinguishing what people actually want from what's really good for them, but that isn't it — he's not saying the lawsuit is better for us, he's saying we already want it and have been talked out of it, and in the second case he isn't claiming anything about our deeper interests at all, he's claiming that some of us don't count. It's also not just that he's a liar or a hypocrite; plenty of politicians are, and I don't think that's the interesting part. Both moves strike me as genuinely principled from inside his way of seeing things, and that's what I want a name for. If nothing established fits, coin a term, define it tightly, and give me a case that falls just outside it so I can see the boundary.
