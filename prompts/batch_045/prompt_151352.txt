I sit on our school board's Committee on Pluralism and Secularism — seven of us, three years of monthly meetings, and we've never once written down a principle, only decisions. We're in one of those jurisdictions where both traditions are pulling at once: the province has a secularism statute that binds people who work for the state, but the human rights code still obliges us to accommodate religion, and the two grind against each other at nearly every meeting. I've typed these up from memory so the dates and the order are probably a bit off, but the verdicts are right:

- A student may wear a headscarf. A teacher may not wear one in front of her own class.
- A student may wear a cross necklace. The big wooden crucifix that had hung in the main hallway since the fifties had to come down.
- A Jewish student's exam that fell on a Saturday: we rescheduled it, no argument.
- Daily halal meat in the cafeteria: refused. We required a genuinely adequate vegetarian alternative instead, and spent two meetings defining "genuinely adequate."
- A Sikh boy's steel bangle: fine. A kirpan: refused, bladed object.
- Year 9 girl excused from the partner-dance unit on modesty grounds: granted. Year 7 girl excused from swimming on modesty grounds: refused, but she may wear a full-coverage suit and we paid for one the first year. That pair still bothers me and I don't think we ever explained it.
- An evangelical prayer group may use an empty classroom at lunch. It may not pray over the PA before exams.
- The cafeteria is run by a private contractor. We put a clause in the contract saying their staff won't discuss religion with pupils, and then voted 4–3 that "we do not regulate the dress of staff we do not employ," with no reasons minuted.
- A parent volunteer who wears a visible cross and comes in once a week to hear kids read: allowed, 4–3, "case by case," again no reasons.
- Hindu parents objected to the yoga unit. We kept the yoga, dropped the Sanskrit posture names and the namaste at the end.

Now the new one. There's a nonprofit homework club run out of a community centre — free, open to anybody, genuinely open, no proselytizing, parents sign a form saying so. It wants to run 3:15 to 5:30 in our library, and to keep using the school's sign-up sheets and the noticeboard, whose heading carries a short dedication to God. Three of its tutors: two women in headscarves, one man with a visible cross. And for the last fifteen minutes the tutors supervise kids in the schoolyard until parents show up, which is the part I keep getting stuck on — same people, same clothes, same property, but for a quarter of an hour they're doing something that looks a lot like what teachers do.

My worry is that "the parents choose it" isn't the clean consent story it sounds like: this is the only free homework help in the neighbourhood, and for a lot of these kids the alternative is going home to an empty flat. One of our members keeps proposing a test — would a nine-year-old reasonably take this person to be speaking for the school? — and I can't tell whether that fits what we've already done or quietly overturns half of it.

So: if we want to be consistent with ourselves, do we renew as is, renew with conditions, or decline? Which of our own precedents is the closest analogue, and what are the two or three rules that fit *everything* we've decided so far and then come apart on this one? What does each predict here, which one's your bet, and what would we be committing ourselves to either way? And what case should we put on next month's agenda, since we clearly won't say it out loud, to find out which rule we've actually been running?
