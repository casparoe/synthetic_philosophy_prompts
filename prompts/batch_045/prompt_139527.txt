I've been chewing on something for a few years now and I can't find the word for it, which makes it basically unsearchable, and I genuinely can't tell whether that means it has a name I've just never run into, or whether it doesn't have one and deserves one.

Context so you know where I'm coming from: I've been reading the value-extrapolation family of ideas since forever — the CEV stuff, and the older philosophy version where you ask what you'd want if you were fully informed and fully rational and more the person you wish you were. What keeps bugging me isn't the standard objections (that the procedure is underspecified, that the extrapolated version might not care about us anymore, that "more rational" is secretly doing all the work). It's something about the order of operations, and I can't tell whether it's one thing or three.

Case one. I used to hate black coffee and now I genuinely like it. When I try to run the extrapolation on myself I get stuck on whether the idealizing is allowed to change me and then start over from the changed me, or whether it has to hold the starting point still and only strip away distortions. The fixed-point reading — keep applying "more information, more reflection, less akrasia" until it stops moving — presumably lands on a coffee drinker, since acquiring the taste is exactly what more information and more experience does to you. But if the starting point has to be held fixed and only the noise removed, then the answer depends entirely on whether you think the liking was there all along and I was misreporting it, or whether it got built along the way. People talk as if the fixed-point reading is obviously correct, but nothing I can see guarantees there's a unique place to converge to, and in my own case I can construct at least two, each of which would say the other was extrapolating from a corrupted input.

Case two, which might be the same thing and might not. Every few months I do this exercise where I try to write down what I'd want if I had full knowledge and unlimited time to think about it. The answer swings hard depending on which of my current motivations I treat as signal and which as noise. Some weeks my ambition reads as the real me and my desire to go read novels in a cabin reads as plain exhaustion. Other weeks it's exactly reversed, and the full-information part doesn't adjudicate at all, because both readings are compatible with everything I actually know. So I end up supplying by hand the one input that does most of the work, and flipping it gives me two different idealized selves who don't converge and who each regard the other's starting point as contaminated.

Possible third case. Whether I even want to be extrapolated is itself one of my current preferences, and I have no idea whether it gets to vote, or whether it's the kind of preference that the procedure is definitionally supposed to overrule.

I don't just want a label — a label would be fine but what I'm after is the carving. So: are these one phenomenon or several? What exactly is the thing I keep bumping into, what nearby concepts could it be confused with, and how does it differ from each of them? Where are its edges — for instance, is there a version of this that shows up in belief revision or in theory choice, where applying the improvements in a different order lands you somewhere different, and is that the same problem in different clothes or a genuinely separate one? Is there an argument that one of my three cases is a real problem and the other two are artifacts of me doing the exercise wrong? And if there honestly is no established concept here, then coin one, give me a definition that doesn't just swallow every case of "procedure is underspecified," and hand me a couple of clear non-instances so I can tell when I'm over-applying it.

Things I've already considered and set aside, so you don't spend the answer on them: it's not the worry that the idealized agent stops caring about the original, it's not about which people in a population get extrapolated from, and "idealization is underdetermined" is true but way too big to be useful to me. What I'm pointing at is specifically about the structure of the procedure over time and what it does to its own starting point. Also, if there's a reason to think the whole framing is confused — like there's no such thing as "the" extrapolation and I should have been asking a different question entirely — say that too, and say what the different question is.
