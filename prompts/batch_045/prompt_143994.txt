Some candidate readings I can half-articulate myself, so tell me if I've got them wrong or if two are really the same one: (1) the boring one — undergoing an experience differs from merely reading a description of it, roughly like being able to ride a bike differs from reading the manual, which no functionalist ever denied; (2) the Mary-ish one — a complete functional or physical description leaves out a fact, i.e. there's some truth about the subject's state that the description doesn't entail, and the subject could come to learn it; (3) the concept one — our concept of what red-experience is like isn't a functional or relational concept, and no amount of functional description could hand you that concept, you have to have been acquainted with the state; (4) the property one — experiences have intrinsic, non-relational properties over and above all their dispositional and relational ones; (5) the ineffability-plus-incorrigibility one — what it's like can't be fully put into words, and nothing could ever show you that you're wrong about it.
