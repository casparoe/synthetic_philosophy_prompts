I am writing a set of lectures on collective action problems, and I want to use the international regulation of ozone-depleting chemicals as my worked case instead of the usual pasture or fishery. Let me stipulate the facts so that nothing turns on looking anything up. Chemists proposed in the mid-1970s that chlorofluorocarbons — cheap, inert, and used in enormous quantities as refrigerants, aerosol propellants, solvents and foam-blowing agents — persist long enough in the lower atmosphere to drift into the stratosphere, where ultraviolet light liberates chlorine, which then destroys ozone catalytically, a single chlorine atom cycling through many ozone molecules before it is deactivated. Thinner ozone admits more UV-B, which raises skin-cancer and cataract rates and damages crops and plankton. The Antarctic ozone hole was identified in the mid-1980s. The Montreal Protocol was agreed in 1987, entered into force in 1989, has been tightened repeatedly since, has been ratified by essentially every state, and ozone levels stabilised in the 1990s and have been recovering slowly ever since.

What I want is an analysis of this episode through the lens of commons theory, run in both directions: theory onto case, and case back onto theory.

First, say what structure this actually is. Is the ozone layer best modelled as a subtractive commons from which users withdraw a rival resource, as an unowned waste sink with a finite assimilative capacity into which users discharge, or as an unproduced public good whose protection is non-excludable and non-rival and therefore underprovided? Argue for one, and say whether the choice changes any prediction the model makes about who free-rides, who must be coerced, and what counts as the remedy.

Second, give me a disciplined mapping. Who were the relevant actors — states, the handful of firms that produced most of the world's CFCs, the consumers of refrigeration and air conditioning? What did their genuine option sets look like, given that substitutes existed but were dearer, and given that the treaty threatened trade restrictions against non-parties? What were the payoffs, and in particular what did compliance cost the parties who complied early and the firms that happened to hold patents on the substitutes?

Third, the feature I care most about. I have started calling cases of this shape a delayed-feedback commons: no user can observe the marginal damage done by their own contribution at the moment of contributing, the damage materialises decades later, and it appears somewhere other than where the benefit was enjoyed. Set out what the standard tragedy model implicitly assumes about the information available to users, and what Ostrom-style design principles — clear boundaries, monitoring by the users themselves, graduated sanctions — presuppose about feedback. Then say what happens to those mechanisms when the feedback is delayed by a generation and displaced onto other people, and identify what in the ozone regime did the work that visible degradation would otherwise have done. I have in mind the standing scientific assessment panels and the treaty's built-in procedure for tightening the phase-out schedule without renegotiating the treaty. Is that a general substitute for feedback, or an artefact of this episode?

Fourth, the awkward question. Production was concentrated in very few firms, and the incumbents owned the patents on the replacements. Does that make the case structurally closer to a small-number coordination problem in which compliance turned out to be privately profitable than to an N-person prisoner's dilemma — and if so, how much of a vindication of commons theory is the success really?

Finally, a verdict and a transfer test. Is the ozone case a confirmation of the model, an anomaly the model cannot digest, or a case of a different structure that merely resembles the model from a distance? And, feature by feature — the availability of cheap substitutes, the concentration of producers, the separability of the harmful substance from the useful service, the visibility and attribution of the harm once detected, the distribution of the victims — say which features did the work, and whether any of them carry over to carbon dioxide in the same atmospheric sink. Where the theory is silent, say that it is silent rather than making it speak.
