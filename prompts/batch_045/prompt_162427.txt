ok so I run a weekly discussion group for undergrads who've never taken an ethics class, and I want a diagnostic I can hand out in week one on the whole moral luck cluster — but not a quiz where question one is "does luck affect moral responsibility, yes or no," because everyone just answers based on which label sounds nicer to them. I want a decision tree that sorts people by what they say about concrete cases.

What I need:

- Seven or eight branch questions max, each one a concrete case with two or three answer options, phrased so someone who has never heard the phrases "moral luck," "blameworthiness," or "quality of will" can answer it cold. No branch should be "does luck matter?" in a costume.
- Six to eight leaves, named where there's a name people actually use and marked "unnamed" where nobody really holds it. I want these in there somewhere: the strict control-principle view (luck never touches blame); the view where outcomes change what we're permitted to do to someone — compensation, punishment, hard treatment, shunning — but not what they deserve; the view where outcomes matter only as evidence of what the agent was willing to do; the view where the circumstantial and constitutive stuff is on exactly the same footing as the resultant stuff; and something like the skeptical view where constitutive luck eats agency altogether. Add whatever else the space needs.

Cases that have to show up:

- two drunk drivers, same blood alcohol, same lousy driving, one hits a kid and one doesn't
- the would-be killer whose gun jams, plus a matched version where the gun fires fine but a bird flies into the bullet. I care whether people treat these the same — my hunch is that distinction separates more positions than the usual split between luck in results and luck in circumstances does, so put it early in the tree.
- someone who abandons a family to go make art in another country and either becomes a great painter or ruins their life and produces nothing
- someone who leaves a loaded gun where a child can reach it, and either nothing happens or the worst happens
- someone who does something awful and nobody ever finds out — does that change what they deserve, or only what we should do?
- someone raised in a way that left them basically incapable of guilt or remorse, who then does something bad on purpose

Distinctions I want the tree to actually use, not just mention: whether the thing that's luck-sensitive is desert versus what we're permitted to do to the person versus whether the action was wrong in the first place; how wide the counterfactual neighborhood is (same decision procedure run again versus same person with the same dispositions); and the remorse-capacity thing above.

Before you write the tree, give me the logical space: the questions, the combinations of answers they allow, then which cells are empty and why — incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored. Then place the real positions in the occupied cells.

Two more things: flag any pair of leaves the tree can't seperate, and tell me what case would separate them if you can think of one; and flag any leaf that's really just another leaf in different words. If a cell is empty because the combination is unstable, say which commitment would have to go.

Keep it to something I can print on one page, and don't cite or quote anything — I can't check sources and neither can the group.
