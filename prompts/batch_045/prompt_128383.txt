Some things I want you to actually use rather than gesture at, because the boring version of this document is the one I keep half-writing myself. First, disambiguate "objectivity": mind-independence of moral facts, cross-cultural universality of true moral claims, convergence under idealized reflection, and the practical authority/non-negotiability of moral demands are four different theses, and I suspect half the fight is about which one the variation data is even supposed to threaten — so say explicitly which flavor each side is arguing about, and flag whichever parts of the dispute you think are verbal. Second, use this three-way taxonomy of disagreements and sort the disputed findings into it: cases where the principles are the same but the factual or metaphysical beliefs differ (does the ritual actually cause harm, does the ancestor actually mind), cases where the principles are the same but the weights or lexical priority differ, and cases where the candidate moral properties or the boundary of the moral domain itself differ (whether purity or loyalty is a moral consideration at all, versus merely conventional or religious). Third, I made up three hypothetical study designs and I want both camps to commit to predictions about them, since a crux that neither side will let anything settle is useless: the vocabulary-switch probe (the same fact pattern described to matched samples in rights language, honor language and purity language — do judgments track the described facts or the framing?), the reflective-convergence probe (ask people what an ideally informed, impartial member of their own community would say, and whether they'd revise after seeing how their reasons generalize — do replies converge across societies, and does either side think convergence or non-convergence would even be evidence?), and the bicultural-salience probe (people fluent in two moral frameworks, with one or the other made salient — do they switch, blend, or report one as merely pragmatic?). Also engage with the tu quoque question of whether the variationist's own measurement tools and normative commitments are themselves among the varied things, and with what fraction of the variance actually lies between societies rather than within them.
