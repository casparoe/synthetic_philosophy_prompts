Me and my group chat (a handful of ex-LW, current-ACX-comment-section types) have been nerding out about the Nyāya theory of pramāṇas — the four "means of knowledge" they count: pratyaksha (perception), anumana (inference), upamana (comparison — the sort of case where you were told a gavaya is cow-like, then you see one in the forest and recognise it), and shabda (verbal testimony). We tried to turn it into a tier list and immediately deadlocked, so I want an outside verdict.

Part one: score each of the four, 0 to 10, on irreducible epistemic load — how much work that source does that you couldn't rebuild out of the other three if you really had to — with one line of justification each, and put them in order. My gut ranking is perception on top, inference just behind, testimony third, and upamana down in the gutter because it feels like it dissolves into testimony-plus-inference the moment you poke it. A friend says that is exactly backwards: testimony is the one that really dissolves (inference from the speaker's reliability plus perception of the sounds), and upamana is doing something genuinely distinct. Settle that, and say what would decide it.

Part two: we tried to bolt on four modern candidates and couldn't agree where any of them go. (a) Beliefs you get from an LLM's output. (b) Perceptual-seeming experience inside a high-fidelity simulation — you "see" a red cube in VR; is that pratyaksha with respect to the cube, or something else entirely. (c) A black-box oracle that never asserts anything with content, only bare yes/no flags, with a long track record of being right 80% of the time — you take its flag as a reason to believe. (d) Counterfactual knowledge read off a causal model: you know the sprinkler would have come on if the rain sensor had been disconnected, even though nobody has ever observed that. Give each a 0–10 on the same dimension, and for each say whether a Nyāya-style taxonomy would give it its own bucket, dissolve it into an existing pramana (which one), or refuse to count it as a pramana at all — I have a vague impression they declined to count memory (smriti) for roughly this kind of reason, so correct me if that is wrong. Then collapse all eight into one S/A/B/C/F tier list.

Ground rules: I don't want bibliography, commentary-history, or who-said-what-in-which-text, I want the conceptual argument, and I'd rather you reason it out than recite. Please also mark clearly which of your placements are the boring consensus among people who genuinely know this material and which are live disputes you're picking a side on — my guess is that whether testimony reduces to inference is one of the genuinely contested ones, and I have a half-memory of some Buddhist logicians wanting only two pramanas total, but I don't trust that memory, so don't treat it as a hint. Don't dress up a contested call as the settled view, and flag any place where you're extrapolating past what the framework was built for. Last thing: give me a number at the end — what fraction of an ordinary educated person's everyday justified beliefs do you think actually trace to shabda rather than to perception or inference?
