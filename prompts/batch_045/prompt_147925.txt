And here's the script, transcribed pretty much as written:

"PAP is the principle that a person acts freely only if he could have done otherwise. Frankfurt's example is meant to show that this is false. Black wants Jones to kill Smith and is ready to intervene, by manipulating Jones's brain, if Jones shows some prior sign that he is about to decide not to; in fact Jones decides to kill Smith for his own reasons and Black never has to do anything. But because Black would have stepped in, Jones could not have failed to kill Smith. Yet it seems clear that Jones is morally responsible for killing him. So moral responsibility does not require the ability to do otherwise, and PAP is false.

The first objection is the flicker of freedom. Critics like van Inwagen and Kane point out that Jones does retain some alternatives: he could have shown the prior sign, or begun to decide otherwise, before Black intervened. So Jones has alternatives after all, just very thin ones. The Frankfurtian reply, which is Frankfurt's own and was later developed by Fischer as the robustness requirement, is that these flickers are not the right sort of alternative to ground moral responsibility, since they are not the kind of thing we could hold someone responsible for. That seems right to me, so the objection fails.

The second is the prior-sign objection, associated with Widerker and Ginet. For the case to work, the sign has to be a reliable indicator that Jones is about to decide not to kill Smith. But under indeterminism there could be a case where Jones is about to decide otherwise without showing the sign, in which case Black would not intervene and Jones would do otherwise. So the example only shows that Jones could not have done otherwise if we assume determinism, which begs the question against the incompatibilist.

Now the Reset case. Here Black's device does not cause the decision; it puts Jones back ten seconds and lets him deliberate again. I think this version is a genuine improvement, and in particular it escapes the prior-sign objection. In the standard case Black has to bring about the killing himself, so the example presupposes that Jones's decision can be produced by something other than Jones's own deliberation. In the Reset case the sequence that actually produces the action is Jones's own indeterministic deliberation: after any reset nothing settles which way he will go, so the example no longer builds in causal determinism, and the objection loses its grip. It also does something about the flicker worry, because after a reset Jones really does face an open alternative, so nobody can say the case was rigged from the start. And it does not matter whether the device is reliable or not — in the actual sequence it never fires, so Jones's responsibility rests entirely on his own deliberations. The case therefore shows, better than the original does, that responsibility can come apart from the ability to do otherwise."
