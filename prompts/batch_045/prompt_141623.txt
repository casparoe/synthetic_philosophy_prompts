So I got nerd-sniped by Hofstadter's careenium thing (from I Am a Strange Loop, I think? I only read somebody's summary of it) and I've been going in circles on a question I can't even tell is well-formed. Here's the setup as I understand it: a careenium is a big box full of tiny magnetic balls, the simms, careening around and clumping into bigger structures that form and break up. At a zoomed-out level some of those clumps are stable, and they track stuff outside the box closely enough that they can be read as simmballs — symbols. The walls change their bounciness depending on what's going on outside, which is how the world gets in; in the version I saw, simms that collide also change each other's magnetism slightly, so the clumping slowly gets better at tracking the world. Then the question he poses: who is pushing whom around in there? Seen from the bottom it's just simms shoving simms. Seen from the top it's simmballs shoving simmballs — including one particular simmball that stands for the whole system, the self one.

My instinct is that this is a situation I deal with at work all the time. When a program crashes, I say "the sort crashed it"; the hardware person says "no, the transistors did." I'm completely untroubled by both of those being true, and I think the reason is that I have a concrete mechanism: there's a compiler or interpreter, it guarantees the low level tracks the high level, and the high-level story is the one that survives being ported to different silicon. It generalizes; the transistor-by-transistor story is a complete history but not something you can reuse. Compare: a cosmic ray flips a bit and the program crashes — I call that a hardware cause, not a software one, even though it changes exactly the same transistor states as the bug does. The difference isn't in the physics, it's in what counterfactuals the description supports.

Here's what I want from you. I'm a software engineer, I have no philosophy training, and I don't want references or a reading list — I can't chase them and it won't help me. Just argue it out.

1. Is "push" even being used in the same sense at the two levels, or is the question secretly two different questions wearing one sentence? What would concretely have to be true of the careenium for it to be correct that a simmball shoves simms around, given that every simm trajectory is already fixed by simm-level physics?

2. Lay out the obvious worry — that the bottom level does all the causal work and the top level is just a description — and then say what's wrong with it. Because in my software case that worry clearly proves too much: it would make "the sort crashed it" false, which is absurd. So what is the careenium missing that my program has? Candidate answers I've half-formed: a designer, a spec, a guarantee that the low level tracks the high one, an enforced abstraction boundary. Take those apart, and tell me what a level has to do to earn its keep in a system where nobody designed it and there's no correct reading of a simmball supplied from outside.

3. Grain stuff. If I zoom in one level from the simms, do I find another layer of clumps that look like simmballs relative to that? If so, is there any fact of the matter about which level pushes which, or is the whole question relative to where you're standing — and if it's relative, is there still one privileged floor, and why would that one be privileged?

4. Is the self-simbal case special or not? Hofstadter's punchline is about the simmball that represents the whole careenium, and the loop where it shoves around the things that make it. Part of me thinks that's just the general case with feelings: it's one more pattern, and whatever you said in (1) and (2) already covers it. Another part of me thinks a symbol whose referent is the system containing it is genuinely different, because it's a closed feedback loop in the control-theory sense, not just a description. Settle that, and say what the difference would even consist in.

5. Evaluate a criterion I made up, because I suspect it's either trivially right or quietly circular. Call it the recompile test: a level is doing real causal work if you can recompile its history onto a completely different set of simms and the level's causal story still holds. Does that actually distinguish anything? Where does it break — a careenium that only ever runs once, or the learning version where the magnetism changes mean you can't re-describe the simmballs without also copying the substrate's state?

6. Where does the software analogy actively mislead me here? I trust it a lot and that makes me suspicious of it. And give me a straight bottom line: is there a fact of the matter about whether symbols push the substrate around, or is it a choice of description — and if it's a choice, what are we choosing on the basis of, and does that basis care what the answer is?
