Putting together a short statement of where I stand for an epistemology reading group — the others get to attack it, so I'd like to find the weak joints before they do. Roughly where I've landed:

(1) The margin-for-error argument against luminosity (Williamson-style) is basically sound, and I want to read it as a general moral rather than a point about a few sensations: for any non-trivial condition of mine — being in pain, seeming to remember, finding an argument compelling — there are nearby cases where the condition obtains and I can't tell that it does.

(2) KK is true: if you know that p, you're in a position to know that you know that p. I know it's unfashionable, I keep comming back to it anyway.

(3) Knowledge is safe belief: in the nearby cases where you form the belief in the same way, it's true. I like this because it does the anti-luck work without dragging justification back in.

(4) Knowledge is the norm of assertion and the input to practical reasoning — assert only what you know, act on what you know.

(5) In the misleading higher-order evidence cases — good first-order evidence for p, then a trusted source tells you, falsely, that your faculties are shot — I want to say you still know p. Your first-order position hasn't moved.

(6) Closure holds, at least in the ordinary one-premise form.

Please audit this rather than explain it: which pairs genuinely pull against each other, which only look like they do, and for each real tension what has to give and what giving it up costs me. Rank them by severity instead of flagging everything equally, and say which single revision would clean up the most. I should add that I can't tell whether (2) is an internalist leftover I ought to drop or something the rest of the list already commits me to — so if there's a reading of KK that survives (1), tell me what shape it has to have.
