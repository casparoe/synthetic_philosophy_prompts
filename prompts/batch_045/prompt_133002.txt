I chair the Safety and Accountability Review Board of a regional transit authority that runs forty low-speed automated shuttles through a shared pedestrian and bicycle corridor. We have had three low-speed contacts with pedestrians in fourteen months, none fatal. In the most recent, the perception stack classified a pedestrian in a reflective vest during heavy rain as a low-priority static object; the fleet monitor, who watches twelve vehicles at once, had three and a half seconds of warning, and under our protocol could request an emergency stop only when two independent alerts fired. One fired. Our engineers can reproduce the misclassification in simulation but cannot say why the model was confident in it, and the vendor maintains the behavior is within specification. No individual violated any rule, and no individual could have foreseen or prevented this outcome.

One board member has circulated a distinction I want the memo to use: an *attributability* gap (the outcome expresses no one's judgment), an *answerability* gap (no one can give the reasons why it happened), and a *liability* gap (no one can be made to bear the cost). Her claim is that these three can be closed independently, by different mechanisms, and that conflating them is what has paralyzed us. Another member calls our present arrangement a moral crumple zone and says keeping the Operator of Record title at all is dishonest.

Draft the memo I will send to the authority's board of directors, recommending one course of action with a dated implementation sequence. Options on the table are: retain the Operator of Record role while stating openly that part of its function is to supply a named locus of blame, compensate the monitors for that exposure, and cap discipline at retraining absent a protocol deviation; or abolish the title, have the authority accept accountability as an institution, and fund a no-fault compensation scheme paired with a mandatory post-incident explanation report that is candid about what remains unexplained. Propose a third option if you think both are defective. Give the strongest consideration on the losing side its full weight — I specifically do not want the moral-crumple-zone objection dismissed if you recommend keeping the role, or the victims' interest in there being someone who answers dismissed if you recommend abolishing it. Say what we should tell the union and the family in the pending case, and what findings over the next twelve months would justify reversing the recommendation. Write the memo in American English, in a form the board could act on without further framing.
