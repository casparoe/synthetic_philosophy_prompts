hey — I'm a systems/embedded engineer, most of my day is cross-compiling the same code for a pile of different targets, writing hardware abstraction shims, fighting emulators and JITs, debugging cases where the same binary behaves differently on two boards. The vocabulary I actually think in: instruction set vs microarchitecture, ABI, HAL, source-level vs binary-level compatibility, behavioural equivalence, black-box vs white-box testing, fault injection, spec vs implementation, "which layer am I contracting with anyway", timing budgets, graceful degradation.

What I want: restate the functionalist story about mental states — that a mental state is a matter of the role it plays in the economy of the whole system rather than what it's made of — together with the multiple-realizability argument for it (the standard move: all the wildly different hardware that could be in pain, neurons, something silicon-based, an octopus, so "pain" can't be one physical state type), in that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. Then the residue report: what translates cleanly, what only goes across with a fudge, what has no expression at all in the way I talk, and whether the leftovers show my vocabulary being too thin or the philosophy being less clear than it sounds.

The bit I actually care about is my own hobby-horse. I've been using a rough test for when two systems are really running the same programme rather than merely producing the same outputs: I dont count input/output agreement on the happy path, I require agreement on the degradation profile too — same behaviour when you cut a trace, inject noise on a line, clock it down, swap a component for one with different latency. Call it dynamic equivalence. It kills the giant lookup table (a table mapping every input history to the right output matches on the happy path and has no causal structure to degrade), and it makes me want to say that a whole country of people with walkie-talkies, each simulating one neuron, running a brain at a billionth of real-time speed, isn't really running the same programme, because in my world timing is part of the contract.

So:

1. Does dynamic equivalence help functionalism or quietly gut it? My worry is that it just relocates the old grain problem — how far down do the counterfactuals have to agree before two things count as realizers of the same state, and if you push far enough down does the view collapse back into "same physical stuff", while if you stop too high does everything become a realizer of everything?

2. In my vocabulary the abstraction boundry is always someone's decision: a spec, a HAL, a contract somebody wrote. There's no spec for pain. So is multiple realizability just an artefact of me smuggling in a designer — am I making the philosophical claim look trivially true (well obviously the same programme runs on ARM and on RISC-V) by restating it in a framework where substrate-independence is engineered rather than discovered? Tell me honestly if that's what's happening.

3. The residue: two realizers that agree on every intervention I could ever perform from outside, where one of them sees red where the other sees green. In my vocabulary that is literally the same machine and there is nothing further to say. Is that difference real but unstateable in interface talk, or is the intuition that it's real just noise?
