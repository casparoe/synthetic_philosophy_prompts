I am helping a foundation allocate a modest pot of money — roughly four full-time researchers for two years — on conceptual work concerning the singleton hypothesis: the idea, in its original formulation, of a world order in which there is a single decision-making agency at the highest level, capable of permanently preventing the emergence of rival agencies and of settling the major coordination problems that a plurality of top-level agencies cannot. The funded work would be philosophy and social theory — decision theory, social choice, institutional design, some political economy — and explicitly not empirical machine learning. The deliverable I want from the group at the end is not a survey and not a literature review (I will supply the citations myself): it is a defended research agenda, and the agenda is meant to change how other funders and actors divide their effort between approaches.

Here are six half-formed distinctions I have been circling, and which I suspect the group should spend its time sorting out rather than re-arguing whether singletonhood is in the abstract desirable:

1. **Protocol versus agent.** The singleton property might attach to a coordination layer rather than to an agency — a shared regime of evaluation, verification, or compute attestation that every sufficiently capable system must interface with, such that no actor can defect without becoming irrelevant. Can a protocol singleton deliver the coordination benefits the hypothesis advertises while avoiding the founder-capture problem that an agent singleton seems to inherit?
2. **Graded rather than binary.** Treat singletonhood as a matter of degree: over what portion of the relevant state space can the agency enforce, against which kinds of defection, and for how long? If enforcement capacity erodes, does the interesting risk lie in the erosion itself or in value drift within a still-enforcing agency?
3. **Custodial versus sovereign.** A trustee-style singleton that deliberately preserves option value — defers settlement, maintains a diversity of experiments, refuses to lock in a final answer about value — as against one that implements a settlement and enforces it indefinitely. Which is more stable given what we know about institutional drift and succession, and is the custodial variant coherent or merely a slower sovereign one?
4. **Singleton by accretion.** Top-level coordination may be reached as a by-product of concentration in supply chains, standards, or liability regimes, without anyone aiming at it, so that the intentionality of the process is unclear. Does the normative and strategic picture differ depending on whether the singleton was designed, and if so how much of the existing debate silently assumes a designer?
5. **The corrigibility of the singleton itself.** A singleton that can be corrected appears to be a singleton that can be captured; one that cannot seems frozen against moral and empirical progress. Is this a genuine dilemma, or does it dissolve once one distinguishes corrigibility with respect to a broad, constitutionally defined constituency from corrigibility with respect to any actor who happens to hold power?
6. **The pre-singleton window.** The present period, in which multiple top-level agencies still exist but concentration is rising. What is the option value of actions taken now, and which present levers — procurement rules, liability, evaluation standards, norms about publishing capabilities — most alter the character of whatever singleton later emerges, as opposed to merely altering its timing?

Advise me on the ordering. For each item: how much other work sits downstream of it; whether it is tractable in two years with this toolkit or whether it stalls into speculation; how neglected it is relative to its importance; and what evidence over the next two years — concentration trends, governance developments, the fate of actual coordination attempts — would move it up or down the list. Tell me explicitly which of the six you regard as pseudo-questions or as overrated, and which you would decline to fund even if a strong candidate proposed it, and why. I care much more about the reasoning behind the ranking than about the list; if you think the most valuable question in this area is one I have not listed, say so and give it a rank alongside the others.
