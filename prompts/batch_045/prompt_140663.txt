Hey — I'm a lab animal vet, and I've been the attending vet on our institution's animal care committee for about eight years. We're currently rewriting our severity guidance, which has dragged me into an argument I can't referee. Almost all of my working vocabulary is the 3Rs (replacement, reduction, refinement), harm–benefit analysis, severity banding (mild / moderate / severe, non-recovery), cumulative severity, humane endpoints, the Five Domains, and 'cost to the animal' as the unit that goes in the ledger. I've got no philosophy training; when I say an animal has moral standing I basically mean 'has a welfare state that can go well or badly for it, and that state is a cost or a benefit in its own right.'

So: translate for me, as faithfully as you can, into that vocabulary. Two things to translate.

1) Kant's own position, as I've had it explained to me: animals aren't rational persons, so we have no direct duties *to* them, only duties *regarding* them that are really duties to humanity — including duties to oneself. Cruelty is wrong because it brutalizes the agent and blunts the sympathetic capacities we need in our dealings with people, not because anything is owed to the dog. I want that restated as if it were written into our protocol review form: what does 'refinement' come out as, what does a severity band come out as, what does the harm side of a harm–benefit analysis actually consist in?

2) The modern Kantian repair I keep hearing about — the move where, roughly, because valuing anything at all commits you to valuing the good of any creature for whom things can be good or bad, animals end up as ends in themselves after all. Translate that too. My suspicion is that it just collapses into 'sentience is where the ledger starts,' which is what I already believe, and that the collapse is the interesting part. Tell me if I'm flattening it.

Then the residue report, and please run it through these four cases rather than doing it abstractly:

(a) Full refinement, automated home-cage monitoring, but the actual suffering happens at 3am with nobody in the room and no one's character being visibly shaped. My ledger says the cost is entirely real and undiminished. What does the Kantian say, in my terms?
(b) A technician who does a hundred gavages, is unfailingly decent to her colleagues, and shows no measurable blunting of anything. Zero character damage by any measure I can construct. Is the procedure then costless on Kant's account? If not, what is the residual wrong and can my vocabulary name it?
(c) Cumulative severity — I spend real effort on low-grade lifetime distress that no human ever notices and that certainly corrupts nobody. Why should a Kantian care, and if he does, has he quietly borrowed my framework?
(d) Replacement: if the wrong is really a wrong to humanity, then swapping animals for an in silico model seems to erase it. I think replacement is partly about the animals. Where does that disagreement even show up once you translate?

For each bit of residue, say whether it's a hole in *my* vocabulary or a sign the Kantian claim was never as clear as it sounds. Concretely: I have no idea how to operationalize 'damage to humanity in one's own person,' and I want to know whether that's my problem or his. One rule — please don't quietly turn Kant into a consequentialist during the translation and then report that everything translated fine. And answer in American English, US spelling.
