I run a small reading group on free will with two friends (one hard incompatibilist, one who thinks the whole debate is verbal) and I'm finally writing up a two-page "where I stand" note to circulate before next week. Before I do, I want someone to check whether what I believe actually hangs together, because I have a nagging feeling at least two of these are doing incompatible work. Here's the package, roughly in the order I'd defend it:

1. The question whether free will is compatible with determinism and the question whether the free will we have is worth wanting come apart, and I only care about the second one — I think a being could have all the metaphysically loaded stuff (indeterministic agent-causation, ultimate origination, whatever) and still lack anything I'd want.
2. Frankfurt-style cases convince me that alternative possibilities aren't what responsibility hangs on; what matters is guidance control, reasons-responsiveness, that kind of thing, not regulative control. I'm also fine saying the flicker-of-freedom strategy fails and we don't need it.
3. But I also think responsibility is at bottom answerability — it's about what your action reveals about your quality of will, and the reactive attitudes are basically the whole point of the practice. I find myself holding people answerable even in cases where their control is pretty degraded.
4. I don't accept any historical condition on responsibility. Snapshot the agent, check that the structural conditions are met, done; where the agent came from is irrelevant.
5. And yet in the original-design cases — a designer builds someone from scratch, picking the starting conditions knowing exactly how things will turn out — I have a very firm intuition the resulting agent isn't responsible, even though at the moment of action they're perfectly reasons-responsive and nobody is threatening them. I want to keep that intuition rather than explain it away as an illusion to be debunked.
6. The positive thing I want to defend is something I've been calling diachronic self-authorship: what's worth wanting isn't a momentary power to do otherwise, it's that over a life your earlier exercises of the capacity partly shape which reasons you later have, so that you become the sort of person for whom certain considerations are salient. I think this is available to a compatibilist, and I think it's the real content of what people are after when they say they want free will.
7. Coercion and threats excuse, but not because they remove options — they mess with what the agent is responding to. I take this to be consistent with 2, and I'd like it to stay that way.

My incompatibilist friend says 4 and 5 are flatly inconsistent and that 6 is secretly a historical view smuggled in the back door; the other friend says 2 and 3 are the real problem (guidance control is a control notion, answerability isn't) and that 6 is either trivial or quietly libertarian. Please audit the set: tell me which of these are real tensions and which only look like ones, rank the real ones by how bad they are, and for each say what would have to give and what that costs — then name the single revision that would clean up the most. Don't just tell me everything is in tension with everything; I want to know which pairs are fine. Start with one sentence giving your verdict: does the package survive, and if not, which commitment goes. 600 words max.
