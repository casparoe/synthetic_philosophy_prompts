we're wrapping up our epistemology reading group on Williamson's margin-for-error argument against luminous mental states, and for the last session I'd rather close with a poem than with a summary. Can you write me a villanelle — one that breaks its own one rule — in the voice of someone standing outside at dawn deciding whether its cold, while the temperature creeps up by a tenth of a degree every minute through a long series of cases.

The constraint I have in mind: the two refrains are the first-order claim (roughly, it is cold) and the iterated one (roughly, I know that it is cold). In a normal villanelle the refrains come back verbatim; here I want the second refrain to come back with one more "I know that" stacked onto the front every single time it returns, while its final word stays put so the rhyme scheme survives — keep the real villanelle machinery otherwise (two rhymes, ABA through the tercets, ABAA in the closing quatrain) and keep it readable aloud. So the regress in the KK principle is literally happening inside the line, and by the final tercet it should be visibly too long, too demanding, for the situation to underwrite. The closing couplet is where it breaks: the plain claim and the iterated one meet side by side and something has to give.

Two things I want it to get right rather than just name. First, the failure is at the meta-level, not skepticism: she genuinely does know it's cold in the early cases, and the poem shouldn't wobble on that. Second, I'd like one beat somewhere for the fallback move — fine, coldness isn't luminous, but the felt seeming is, "it seems cold to me" is something I can't be wrong about. The poem should register why that retreat doesn't buy luminosity back (whether because it changes the subject from the weather to the appearance, or because you can be wrong about your own seemings — pick one and stay consistent with it).

19 lines if you can manage it, 22 at the outside. Then one titled section, "What the form is doing", 150–250 words, on why you grew the refrain instead of keeping it fixed, where exactly the break lands, and which line someone who knows the argument would point at as carrying it. Anyone who hasn't read the chapter should still just find a good poem.
