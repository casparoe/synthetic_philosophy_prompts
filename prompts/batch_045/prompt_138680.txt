I was a physical chemist for the better part of fourty years and only got into philosophy after I retired, so go easy on the jargon. Lately I've been reading about dialetheism and I want to try it on a case from my own old field: the old quantum theory, roughly 1913 to 1925.

Take these as given, I dont want them argued with: Bohr's atom has electrons sitting in stationary orbits that do not radiate, while classical electrodynamics -- which is still being used elsewhere in the same theory -- says an orbiting charge radiates continuously and would spiral into the nucleus. Everybody knew it. They used both bits anyway for more than a decade, got real numbers out of it (spectral lines, Sommerfeld's corrections, that sort of thing), and stopped only when a consistent replacement showed up.

Before you start, split the thing two ways. I'll call the first derivational inconsistency: a single theory, with its own mathematics and auxiliaries, actually derives both A and not-A. I'll call the second patchwork: two blocks that each work fine in their own domain and only clash when you bolt them together. Tell me which one the old quantum theory had, and whether only one of the two is even a candidate for being a true contradiction.

Then give me exactly five numbered points, no preamble at the top and no summing up at the bottom, one or two sentences each:

1. What, by the dialetheist's own lights, were the physicists of 1915 actually facing.
2. What the paraconsistent machinery would have let them do that they didn't already do anyway -- and be specific about which classical rule you have to give up to get it, and what that costs.
3. One thing the case explains, and one thing it badly misdescribes.
4. Where dialetheism just goes quiet on this episode instead of having an answer.
5. Whether the episode is evidence for the view, an anomaly for it, or the kind of case the view was never built to handle.

My own hunch, which you're welcome to knock down: nobody in 1915 believed a contradiction, they just didn't know which part to throw away -- but I'm not shore whether that's an objection to dialetheism or only a fact about how scientists behave.
