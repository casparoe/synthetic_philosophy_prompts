for my pragmatics module we're doing performatives next week and I said I'd post something on the seminar blog, so could you write me a parody government form — Form PA-1, "Application to Undertake an Obligation (Promise, Bet or Undertaking)" — from a made-up Office of Verbal Transactions, plus the "Notes for Applicants" underneath, 600-700 words, deadpan HMRC/Home Office voice, the sort that sounds entirely reasonable and gets steadily more unhinged as it goes on.

The target is the idea that illocutionary force is just a matter of the right formula said by an authorised person through the correct procedure, so the joke should come from the Office genuinely trying to administer that. It can actually check some things — is the speaker licenced, was the wording right, was the utterance said on stage, in a poem, or as a quotation, in which case its void — but it can't check whether she meant it or whether the hearer took it up, so it keeps bolting on workarounds: a counterfoil the hearer has to sign, pre-registering the intention 28 days in advance, a compliance audit that voids the promise retroactively if its broken. Stick in one footnote where the Office insists it licences illocutions only and what the hearer went on to feel afterwards is none of its business.

End it as a straight-faced reductio: taken together the rules mean nothing has ever counted as a promise, and the Office hasn't approved a single application since it opened. Don't pause to explain the theory and don't let anyone wink at the reader — everyone on the course has read Austin, they'll get it. And no footnotes to real sources, I'll add those myself.
