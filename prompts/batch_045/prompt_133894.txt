Next Thursday I'm giving our department colloquium, and the talk is the one I've been circling for two years: a defense of what I've been calling the intractability objection to coherence normativity. The thesis is that computational intractability does normative work in epistemology, not merely pragmatic work. The standard reply to worries about logical omniscience is that a coherence requirement such as closure under known entailment may be demanding but is still a norm we imperfectly satisfy, so that incoherence is a failing to be weighed against other goods. My argument, in three steps, is that this reply is unavailable for a certain class of requirements: first, for those requirements, compliance is not merely costly but resource-explosive, and the explosion is discontinuous — the resources needed to determine what the requirement asks for jump by orders of magnitude once the agent's total information passes a fairly modest size threshold, so there is no nearby case in which the norm is cheaply satisfiable and no smooth trade-off to be struck. Second, the natural fallback — that hardness is only worst-case, while the instances an agent actually meets are the easy ones — fails for my purposes, because the hardness results I lean on are average-case ones: for some of these problems there are natural distributions over inputs on which the problem stays hard on all but a vanishing fraction of instances, so the easy-in-practice escape route is closed. Third, and this is the step I care most about, a requirement whose satisfaction is intractable in that sense is not just expensive but unspecifiable: to say which total belief state the requirement asks you to be in, one would already have to have solved the hard problem, so the norm cannot guide deliberation even in principle, and a norm that cannot guide is, I argue, not a norm. Call that the specifiability condition on normative requirements. I then spend the last ten minutes sketching what survives: a requirement of being well-calibrated to the resources you have, rather than coherent with everything you know.
