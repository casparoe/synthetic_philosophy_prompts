I am trying to get clearer on a distinction in the theory of intelligence, and I would like an explanation rather than a survey.

The question that interests me: does *intelligence* name an intrinsic property of a system, or is it irreducibly relational — a property of a system together with a setting, the way solubility is a property of a substance together with a solvent, or being a good key is a property of a key together with a lock? I would like you to explain what hangs on this, and why the relational answer is more tempting than it first looks.

Two testing procedures I have been playing with pull in opposite directions, and I want to understand why.

The first is the *rewrite test*. Hold the abstract structure of a task fixed and vary everything about its presentation: notation, modality, surface domain, the order in which information arrives, whether the relevant structure is buried in distractors. Then look not at mean performance but at the *spread* of performance across rewrites. A system whose success barely varies seems to be tracking structure; one whose success swings wildly seems to be latching onto surface correlates. Call the resulting quantity structural grip.

The second is the *fading test*. Provide scaffolding — worked examples, intermediate feedback, decomposed subproblems — and then withdraw it gradually, recording how fast it can be removed while performance is held constant. Call the rate at which a system needs less and less help absorption.

The puzzle is that these look like different virtues, and they can dissociate in both directions. A system can have excellent structural grip but no absorption: it solves each new task immediately or not at all, and practice never makes the next task cheaper. Another can absorb rapidly within a narrow family of tasks yet collapse under rewriting. Neither profile obviously deserves the name intelligent, yet I cannot say which one is missing something, or whether the two are secretly one virtue measured under different assumptions.

So, three things I would like explained:

1. Whether structural grip really tracks one thing, or whether it quietly presupposes a prior over which transformations count as merely presentational — a prior which, if chosen differently, would reverse the verdict. If so, is structural grip relational, and does that relationality generalize?
2. Whether absorption is better understood as a property of a system or of a system plus a curriculum — and, relatedly, whether a system that improves its own future absorptiveness (that learns how to learn) forces us to treat intelligence as a property of trajectories rather than of time slices.
3. Where this leaves the intrinsic-versus-relational question. Specifically, I want resource-relativity (doing more with less computation, data, or time) separated from environment-relativity (fitting a distribution of tasks). The first sounds as though it could still be intrinsic; the second does not. Is that a genuine distinction, or does resource-relativity collapse into environment-relativity under pressure? And if the setting contains other agents who adapt to you, does intelligence stop being even environment-relative and become straightforwardly comparative, like strength?

Please argue for a considered answer to (3) rather than listing considerations on both sides, but show where the answer costs you something.
