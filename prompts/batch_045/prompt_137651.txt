My friends and I keep arguing about whether a chatbot is really an "agent" or just a simulator. By simulator I mean: the underlying model doesn't want anything, its just continuing text, so its sort of sampling from a blur of all the voices and characters in its training data, and the thing your talking to is more like a role its spinning up than a someone with goals. That feels right for the raw model but I'm not sure it survives all the stuff that gets bolted on top of it.

So: rate each setup below from 0 to 100 on how much of an agent it is, meaning how much it makes sense to treat it as having its own goals and as the thing responsible for what comes out, and give one sentence per item on why it lands there.

1. a base model just continuing a chunk of text
2. a base model prompted with a few examples to play a character, which then holds that character and wont break out of it
3. an RLHF'd chat assistant with a bit of personality that sometimes pushes back
4. that same assistant wrapped in a loop with tools and a notes file, working on a task over several days with nobody checking each step
5. same as 4, except a second model reads every proposed action first and can rewrite or block it before it reaches the outside world

Dont stress about hitting the exact number, what I really want to know is whether there's a sharp jump somewhere in the list or whether its just a smooth slide. And if 5 comes out below 4, does that mean the agent was never really the model in the first place?
