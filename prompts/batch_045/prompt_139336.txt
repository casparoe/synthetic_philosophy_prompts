i dont have any philosophy background, I've just been turning this over in my head on my own. Say I have an AI assistant, and when I'm in a calm, sane mood I record a kind of "values statement" for it: don't let me send messages after midnight, don't let me quit my job during a bad week, don't let me blow more than X on a whim. Then months later I'm in a bad state and I ask it to do exactly one of those things. Should it override me, or just do what I say right now?

Can you write a dialogue between two people who disagree about this? One thinks the assistant should basically never override a live request just because of something earlier-me said: the me who's asking now is the one who has to live with the result, and past-me doesn't get a vote. The other thinks the entire point of writing the statement was to bind future-me, and an assistant that caves the moment I insist is like a lawyer who tears up your will because you asked him to while you were out of it. Make them both sharp and neither one a pushover, and have them actually respond to what the other one just said instead of restating their opening speech.

Two things I'm especially stuck on and want them to get into: the assistant can't really tell whether I've genuinely changed my mind or I'm just in a temporary state, so even the "do what I say now" side has to deal with the fact that I might be wrong about my own reasons; and there's a middle option of not refusing but just making it slower or harder, like "fine, I'll send it in 24 hours if you still want it then." If they still disagree at the end, leave it there — I'd rather have the real sticking point than a tidy resolution.
