I write software for a living, so I think in terms of specs: I want a rule that takes a pest-control scenario and returns a verdict, and I want it to reproduce the verdicts I actually have.

My verdicts:

- A wasp nest by the back door that my allergic kid uses every day — destroy it at night. Fine; not even a qualm.
- Ant bait in the kitchen that kills the whole colony, tens of thousands of individuals. Fine, though I notice I would rather not think about it.
- Ant bait in the kitchen when a housemate objects that the ants were there first and we can just keep the counters clean. Still fine.
- A grub treatment on a lawn nobody walks on, purely so it looks uniform. Not fine. The death count is probably in the same ballpark as the ant colony and my reason is weaker, so far so consistent — but the thing that makes it not fine doesn't feel like the numbers.
- A species-specific larvicide that wipes out the local Aedes population where dengue is endemic. Better than fine; I'd fund it. This kills vastly more individuals than the lawn treatment and ends every descendant of all of them, and I feel better about it, which is the pair I can't reconcile.
- A broad-spectrum spray on my roses that also kills a neighbour's hives. Not fine, and worse than the lawn case even though fewer insects die.
- Locust control across a large area to avert famine, billions of individuals. Fine, and genuinely untroubling — which sits oddly next to the roses.
- Same target, same count: a slow neurotoxin that causes hours of convulsions feels clearly worse than a fast one. A method that sterilises instead of killing feels better, but I don't feel obliged to switch to it, and I can't say why not.

My tentative principle: what I am tracking is not how many insects die but whether the deaths are the ones I was aiming at and whether the aim was worth anything — killing is permitted when it is targeted at the organisms actually causing a real problem, and stops being permitted when it spills onto bystanders or the aim is merely cosmetic.

Please test that against the cases and repair it rather than confirm it: state the best candidates precisely, say which case motivates each clause, be explicit about which of my verdicts no clean rule can save (especially the eradication-versus-lawn pair and the slow-death intuition), and tell me what the repaired rule predicts for two cases I haven't settled: a species-recognising trap that cuts bycatch by ninety percent but costs twenty times as much, and a gene-drive release that suppresses a crop pest region-wide with no spraying at all. If some of my verdicts turn out to be territorial or ownership intuitions wearing a cost-benefit costume, say so plainly.
