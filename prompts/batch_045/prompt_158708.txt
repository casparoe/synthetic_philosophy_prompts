By way of context: I am a novelist, and I have agreed to give the ten-minute opening address at a one-day public event on fiction and truth, run jointly by a university philosophy department and a literary festival. Roughly half the room will be philosophers, many of them deflationists about truth — people who hold that calling a sentence true says nothing substantially more than the sentence itself says, and that truth is not a property with a hidden nature for anyone to discover. The other half will be novelists and general readers who have bought tickets expecting to be told that fiction reaches a deeper truth than nonfiction can.

I do not want to flatter that expectation with a metaphor, and I do not want to lecture a festival audience on the disquotational schema either. What I want is for the deflationary thought to do structural work in the speech: to find a vocabulary for what a novel achieves — a sentence that is exactly the sentence the situation calls for, a description a reader can act on — without implying that some further property has been secured.

One wrinkle I would like used rather than smoothed over. My last three books are written in what reviewers have called the secondhand manner: nothing in them is asserted outright, every claim reaches the reader as something overheard or reported by someone with an interest in slanting it, so that the books never once ask to be believed. Treat this as a genuine test case: if truth talk adds nothing, what exactly is lost, or gained, when a book withholds assertion altogether? Take a definite position on that, and give your reasons.

Write the address, about ten minutes long, plain enough for the festival half of the room and not question-begging to the philosophy half. Then add a brief note, a paragraph or two, on where the speech leans on a contested reading of deflationism and where it quietly changes the subject from truth to something else — trust, accuracy, the reader's attention. A committed speech with an honest note, please, rather than a survey of the options.
