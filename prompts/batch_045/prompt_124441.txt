Some context. I'm an SRE by trade and I got dragged into a tiny reading group by a philosopher friend, and last week we ended up on dispositions — fragility, solubility, conductivity — and the old idea that a disposition just is a conditional: the glass is fragile iff it would break if it were struck. Then came the counterexamples to that. Cases where the stimulus itself destroys the disposition, so the thing the conditional talks about apparently can't happen. Cases where something interferes so the manifestation never shows up even though, intuitively, everything is in place. And I think a third family where the manifestation shows up for the wrong reasons, arranged rather than produced. My friend called them finks and masks and I think mimics, though I may be muddling the taxonomy, and she muttered that there were more where those came from and that people argue about whether the fix is an extra clause about ideal or normal conditions. Half the room thought the extra clause is a small patch; the other half thought the patch is where all the actual work hides. I went home annoyed, because the whole thing sounded like problems I already get paid to think about, just stated worse.

So here's the ask. Translate this debate as faithfully as you can into the vocabulary I actually use, and then give me the residue report: what didn't survive the translation. My vocabulary is: latent fault versus active fault versus error versus failure; masking by redundancy, where a fault is really there but a voter or a retry hides it; coverage, meaning the fraction of faults my detection actually catches; probe effect and Heisenbugs, where instrumenting or stressing a system changes whether the thing you're hunting shows up; fault injection and chaos engineering, where I create the stimulus on purpose; test oracles, which decide whether what came out counts as a failure; and the fact that the harness, the load generator, and the environment are part of the system, so where I draw the boundary is a choice rather than a discovery. Two cases I want handled specially, because I made them up and I genuinely can't tell whether they're new or just finks wearing a hat.
