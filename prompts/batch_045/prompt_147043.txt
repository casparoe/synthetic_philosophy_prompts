hey — I'm writing up my view on blame for a reading group zine thing and I want someone to poke holes in it before Saturday. Here's the package, roughly in the order I'd defend it:

(1) Blame is essentially communicative: to blame you is to address a demand to you and call for some response — an apology, an excuse, repair — not just to quietly downgrade my opinion of you.

(2) Standing is a real thing and it's separate from whether the blame is correct. A hypocrite can be exactly right about what you did and still have no standing, and that's compatable with you being genuinely blameworthy and with you having no complaint to make about the content of what she said.

(3) What kills standing isn't having done the same thing, it's invoking a norm you don't accept as binding on you. So the reformed tax cheat who paid up and changed her ways can blame the current tax cheat, and the person still doing the thing can't — even if they'd freely admit they're doing it and think it's wrong.

(4) But complicity does it too, and it isn't just a version of hypocrisy: the frequent flyer who writes the column demanding the coal plant get shut down, the engineer at one company who blames a rival for a practice her own company also does. I want to say these people lack standing even when they perfectly well accept the norm, blame themselves too, and are trying to stop.

(5) Standing is scalar, and roughly, how much you have left tracks how much you currently benefit from the practice you're criticizing. (This is my weird one, nobody in the group likes it.)

(6) Blame of groups and institutions is blame proper: blaming a university or a department or a firm isn't just shorthand for blaming the individuals inside it.

(7) I don't think blame needs alternative possibilities and I don't think determinism threatens it. Relatedly, I think someone who was coerced into taking part in something doesn't thereby lose standing to blame others for it.

(8) Two verdicts I'm attached to: a meat-eater has no standing to blame someone for wearing fur, but does have standing to blame someone who tortures dogs for fun. And a manager who personally thinks a rule is stupid can still blame an employee for breaking it in her official capacity, and that's real blame rather than just enforcement.

Which of these can't all be true together? I keep going back and forth between (5) and (4) and I dont actually know which one is doing the offending work. Rank the tensions by how bad they are, tell me which pairs only look like they clash, and for each real one say what the cheapest fix is and which claim has to move.
