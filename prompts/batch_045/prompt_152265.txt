my friend and I spent way too long last night arguing about whether evidential cooperation actually helps in a *large* world — by which I mean: nobody's hypothesis space contains the truth, the space can't be enumerated and given a prior, so everyone is working inside a misspecified frame whether they know it or not. His view is basically that more data in the pot can only help whatever frame you're in; mine is that in a large world pooling everything into one shared pool is exactly how a community ends up confidently converged on the best wrong model, and that what you lose by pooling is the thing most likely to tell you the frame is bad — namely that agents with different hypothesis spaces, working on their own data, keep failing to agree, because in a large world that residual disagreement is signal of misspecification rather than just noise.

So give me a number: your credence that a group of agents who pool all their raw evidence (each still updating inside her own hypothesis space) ends up, in the long run, closer to the truth than the same agents would on average if each worked alone. I'm around 0.3, he's around 0.85.

I want the decomposition, not a shrug about how it depends on how you set the model up. The way I'd break it: how often the truth is even in the "span" of the frames anyone brings to the table (call it frame luck); whether updating inside a misspecified space on a growing shared data pool converges on something usefully close or converges confidently on the wrong thing; how much of the disagreement left over after pooling is detectable misspecification signal as opposed to noise or prior differences; whether "closer to the truth" is even well defined across incommensurable hypothesis spaces, or whether the only honest score is predictive accuracy on the incoming data stream; and whether merging hypothesis spaces first — so you get the extra data without destroying the frames — dominates both pooling and isolation. Give a number and one line of justification for each, combine them, then tell me which term your final number is most sensitive to, which one our 0.3/0.85 gap most likely rides on, and what single argument or empirical fact would move you most. No reading lists, I just want the reasoning out in the open.
