I'm making a self-study handout for a pragmatics reading group, and everyone keeps lumping every inference a hearer draws into 'implicature'. So I want a decision tree: start from a hearer who's got more out of an utterance than the sentence's plain truth-conditional content, and end at a named leaf.

Constraints:

- About 10-14 yes/no nodes, each phrased as a question someone could answer about a concrete example without already knowing the taxonomy. No node that's just a definition, and keep the labels out of the questions themselves.
- Build the tree from the logical space, not from a list of names: do the tests first (can the speaker take it back without contradicting herself, can you say the same thing differently and lose it, does it depend on this particular speaker and occasion, is it tied to a specific word in the sentence or to the whole act of saying it, does it survive being embedded in a longer sentence) and only then locate the traditional categories in the space. Mark any combination of answers nobody actually occupies, with a one-liner on why not.
- Leaves have to include: content that belongs to what is said once you've fixed indexicals / disambiguated / loosened a word; conventional implicature; the generalized-or-default conversational kind; the fully occassion-specific kind; and one leaf for 'the hearer's inference isn't speaker meaning at all' (a speaker who'd be puzzled or annoyed to learn you drew it). Flag any two leaves your tests can't actually pull apart.
- Work these cases through the tree as you go: 'Some of the students passed'; 'She's poor but honest'; 'I ate three of the cookies'; 'The ham sandwich is getting impatient'; 'The lecture hall was empty' said when two people were there; and a reference letter that says only that the candidate has perfect attendance and lovely handwriting.

Also: a couple of these leaves sit on live disputes rather than settled ground (whether the 'generalized' kind is a real category or should be folded into defaults or into what's said; where loose talk and metonymy land). Tell me, leaf by leaf or in a short note, which parts are common ground among people who work on this and which are contested — I don't want the handout presenting somebody's settlement as the consensus. And be honest about where the tree itself does violence to the material.
