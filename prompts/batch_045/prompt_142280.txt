so ive been reading rationalist-adjacent stuff about the fermi paradox since the old great filter posts, and something that keeps bugging me is that nobody ever says what a *good* solution would even have to do. people have favorite hypotheses and then argue past each other, because there's no agreed spec for what counts as having explained the silence. so: write me the requirements list.

things I think belong on it, cut or add as you see fit, but give the case behind each one:

- it has to say whether whatever is keeping the galaxy quiet is mostly behind us or mostly ahead of us, and if ahead, roughly when we should expect to hit it. a theory that just says "some step is hard" without saying which one has not told me whether to worry about the next century or stop worrying.
- it has to deal with the crossing-time thing: a settlement wave at even a pathetic fraction of lightspeed gets across the galaxy in a time that's nothing compared to the age of the galaxy, so "nobody has ever come" is a strong claim that needs its own support rather than being the default.
- it has to handle convergence: if the answer is that loud civilizations choose to be quiet, it needs to say why *every* independently evolved one does, given they don't share a culture.
- it has to be usable by someone who noticed we're really early in the total window during which stars will still be forming, which I think makes "we're just first" less cheap than it sounds but also makes naive typicality reasoning about our date very weird.
- I want each family of explanation to cash out in a rate or a timescale for what we should expect next, because "they're hiding" and "they don't exist" predict exactly the same thing about next tuesday and I don't think that should be free. call it a latency requirement or whatever.
- here's one of my own: I've never bought that the classic hard-steps style argument can just assume the difficult transitions were equally difficult all along, since earth was a wildly different planet early on than it was later. if that's a real desideratum, say what it rules out.

mark which requirements are actually non-negotiable and which are just what one camp insists on (I suspect "the explanation may not make humans special" is doing more work than people admit, and I suspect null results from searches for galaxy-scale industrial waste heat get cited as evidence when nobody agrees what those searches could have detected). flag any pair that can't both be met — that's the part I most want. tell me which single requirement kills the most candidate explanations, and skip scoring the hypotheses against the list, I'll do that myself.

no citations or reading lists, I can't check them and don't care who said what. separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who actually work on this (no confirmed technosignatures = settled; filter ahead of us = not), and don't write as if "the filter is probably ahead" is the consensus view. american english please.
