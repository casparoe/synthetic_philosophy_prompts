Quick design task for a session I'm preping on the wrong kind of reasons problem (setup, so we're on the same page: a consideration can genuinely count in favour of holding an attitude without bearing on what makes that attitude correct — an offer of cash to believe that p is the stock case, since belief aims at truth and cash isn't evidence, even if it's a fine reason to get yourself to believe p).

I want a case built to spec, not another variant on the chequebook. Spec: one paragraph, ordinary setting (family, workplace, clinic — your call), in which a single consideration C is a reason of the wrong kind for the agent's belief that p, and a reason of the right kind for a second attitude with exactly the same content p — hope, trust, fear, expectation, whatever you can make work — with both attitudes held by the same person at the same time. Constraints: C can't be a bribe, a threat, a payoff, a pill, or an instruction from someone in authority, and it can't be the sort of thing that only favours pretending; it has to be a consideration a real person would take to genuinely favour the attitude.

Then run the check: name the two attitudes, state C, and for each give the correctness (aim, fittingness) condition you're assuming, and show why C falls on the wrong side of the line for one and the right side for the other, so the verdicts fall out of the accounts rather than out of how you described the case. I also want: which one feature of the case is doing the work; what happens to the verdicts if you delete that feature, or if you swap the second attitude for a third one with the same content; and what someone who thinks there's only one kind of reason — or that the second attitude has no correctness condition at all — would say to wriggle out.

The case is for an argument I want to make in the session: that the right/wrong kind distinction lives in the attitudes' correctness conditions, not in the reasons themselves. So a case that just stipulates "this is a wrong-kind reason", or smuggles the verdict into the description, is useless to me — and say so if you think what I'm after is begging the question.

Last thing: keep seperate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this. Don't hand me a contested account of the second attitude's fittingness conditions as if it were the consensus, tell me where your case leans on a contested bit, and say where you're least confident.
