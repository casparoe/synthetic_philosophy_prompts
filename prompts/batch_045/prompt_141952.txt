I have to teach a single fifty-minute section on whether a theory can be right but early, and I want Malthus's population theory to carry the whole hour. The audience is first-years in a general-education course on prediction, most of whom will arrive having heard that Malthus was refuted — by the Green Revolution, or simply by the fact that we are not starving. Please do not explain Malthus to me; plan how I should explain him.

What I chiefly need is the dependency structure: what must be in place before right but early can even sound like an available verdict rather than a face-saving excuse. My own starting thought is that the class has to first strip the mechanism claim — more people against a fixed resource base presses per-capita output back down toward subsistence — away from any timing or trend claim, and that only then can the question of what would have counted as a deadline be raised at all. Tell me whether that ordering is right and what it forces next.

Two devices I am unsure about. First, I want to mark a contrast between a theory that has been *out-run*, where the mechanism still operates but a countervailing trend the theory did not represent has overwhelmed it, and one that has been *disarmed*, where the mechanism itself has been dismantled by a change in the world the theory did not anticipate; I would then use it to say which of these Malthus is. Does that contrast do real work, or is the distinction hollow exactly where it matters? Second, I would like some case in which right but early is plainly the wrong verdict, set beside Malthus early in the hour: tell me what such a case must have and must lack, and give me one.

Then the misconceptions, in the order the class will form them, each with the point in the sequence at which it should be headed off. The ones I expect: that a dated forecast's failing refutes the mechanism; that enriching a model to accommodate a trend it missed is ipso facto an ad hoc rescue; and, more interestingly, that a theory with no built-in deadline cannot be early but only not yet tested — which I suspect is where the hour ought to land. Add the one you think I have missed.

Close with two things: the question or exercise I should end on, to tell whether they have the distinction or only the slogan, and what I should openly decline to settle in fifty minutes while telling them I am declining it. Finally, mark where your recommended ordering is most likely to break down in the actual room, and say what would persuade you to reorder it.
