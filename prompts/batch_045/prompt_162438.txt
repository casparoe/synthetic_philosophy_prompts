I need a thought experiment built to order. Its job is to do work in a paper arguing that the doing/allowing distinction and the killing/letting-die distinction are independent axes rather than two vocabularies for a single distinction, and that in clinical contexts it is the second of the two, not the first, that carries the moral weight. For the case to carry that argument it has to generate the mismatch rather than stipulate it, so I want it constructed against the specifications below and then checked against them, constraint by constraint.

The design target: a single scenario containing two agents, in which the two distinctions order the agents in opposite directions. On the doing/allowing classification, agent A does and agent B allows. On the killing/letting-die classification, agent A lets die and agent B kills. Everything that could otherwise explain a difference in verdict must be held fixed across the two agents: the outcome (one death each), the intention and motive with which each agent acts, the certainty of the outcome, the effort and cost to each agent, what each agent had to forgo, the patients' consent status, prognosis and expected quality of remaining life, and each agent's institutional role and the duties attached to it. No differences in probability, in numbers of people affected, or in what the agents believe.

Setting constraints: a hospital ward, a hospice, or a care home; ordinary clinical equipment and ordinary clinical routines; the whole scenario in one paragraph. Excluded as too well-worn to be informative: trolleys, transplant surgeons, bathtubs, the disconnection of a ventilator, a rescuer physically blocked from reaching a victim, and any agent who first creates a threat and then declines to neutralise it. Nothing in the scenario may be a device or arrangement invented for the puzzle rather than found in real wards.

The anti-cheating constraints are the ones I care about most. First, both classifications must be derived from the accounts below, not asserted by stipulation or by the way the story is told. Second, the mismatch may not be produced by redescription of identical behaviour (as in switching between starving someone and not feeding them), nor by assigning the two agents different mental states, nor by importing a difference in prior conduct between them. Third, if some constraint here makes the spec unsatisfiable, say which constraint you would relax and what the relaxation costs.

The accounts to test the case against:

1. A causal or difference-making account, on which an agent does an outcome just when her behaviour, as opposed to her mere presence, figures in the causal route to it, or when the outcome is counterfactually dependent on what she did.
2. A threat-versus-aid account, on which doing and allowing turn on whether the agent's conduct introduces or sustains a threat to the victim or merely fails to supply aid the victim needs.
3. A baseline account of killing versus letting die, on which the classification turns on whether the death is due to the agent's interference with the victim's own functioning or due to processes the agent merely fails to counteract, and hence on where the baseline of what would be going on anyway is drawn.

What I want back:

- The scenario, in one paragraph.
- For each agent and each of the three accounts, the verdict and one sentence of reasoning; mark explicitly any account that comes out silent or indeterminate, and say why.
- An identification of the single structural feature doing the work, a demonstration that the classifications collapse back into agreement when that feature is removed or equalised across the agents, and a statement of what they collapse to.
- The strongest objection available to someone who denies the independence claim, whether that person argues that the two distinctions are one distinction described at two levels or that both are morally inert and only foresight, intention and outcome matter, together with an honest note on where the case is softest against that objection.
