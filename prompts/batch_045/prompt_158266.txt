Draft the joint statement that a Dennett-style compatibilist and a Caruso-style free will skeptic could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on legal punishment — the compatibilist holding that basic desert survives once ultimate authorship is given up, the skeptic holding that it does not and that a rights-constrained public health–quarantine model (incapacitation, deterrence, rehabilitation, restoration, with proportionality of burden to danger rather than to blameworthiness) should replace it.

Produce it in two parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims — including what both accept about reasons-responsiveness, about the role of luck in shaping an agent's capacities, about what the actual practice of criminal justice (plea bargaining, sentencing disparity, the treatment of the mentally disordered offender) shows, and about what any punishment theory must explain. Second, a disagreements table: for each remaining dispute, the neutral statement of the question, then the formulation the compatibilist would endorse and the formulation the skeptic would endorse, each phrased so that side could sign it.

Identify three cruxes — sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side. Suggested, though use your judgment: (i) whether desert can be grounded in an agent's reasons-responsive capacity at the time of action when the provenance of that capacity is itself a matter of luck, versus whether such provenance defeats desert only if it defeats the capacity; (ii) whether a quarantine model can avoid detaining the dangerous-but-not-culpable for periods desert-based sentencing would forbid, or whether the retributivist's ceiling on punishment is something the skeptic must import anyway; (iii) whether a system that retains desert-shaped sentence lengths purely for their deterrent, expressive, or legitimating effects — call this desert-mirroring — has given up retributivism or merely stopped admitting it.

For each crux, give one concrete case or piece of evidence both parties would accept as bearing on it, and record in advance what each would predict. Include, as one such case, a wrongdoer who is now permanently and verifiably harmless (advanced dementia, no prospect of reoffending), whose crime is unknown to the public and whose continued confinement serves no deterrent or expressive purpose: both sides should state what is to be done and what that reveals about their view. If any part of the dispute turns out to be verbal — for instance about what "desert," "holding responsible," or "punishment" amounts to once the metaphysics is set aside — say which part, and why.

Write the whole document in American English, in a register both parties could put their names to: firm about what is agreed, exact about what is not, and free of rhetoric either side would strike.
