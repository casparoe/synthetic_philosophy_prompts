hey so I'm in this after-school physics club thing (Ms. Okonjo runs it, she's cool but she marks our writing hard) and we just spent a week on Maxwell's demon and the connection between entropy and information — the bit where wiping a single bit of memory has to dump a tiny fixed amount of heat into whatever's around it, so the demon doesn't actually get free work out of a gas because sooner or later it has to clear its memory to keep sorting, and the books balance again. I get it I think, and my friend Dani says its the coolest thing we've done all year, but for the end of the unit Ms. Okonjo wants a piece of creative writing instead of a lab report and she said no infodumping — she literally said if one of your characters stands up and explains the second law for two paragraphs she's giving it a C.

So here's what I want: a short story, around 1000 to 1400 words, that gets across why the demon fails, but set in a world where the physics is slightly different from ours. In my world erasing information is totally free and cold — you can wipe a notebook, a memory, a whole archive and it costs nothing and gives off no heat — but copying information is the expensive part: every time a bit gets duplicated, that spits out heat, the same tiny fixed amount per bit that erasing costs in our universe. Dani and I argued about whether a demon could still cheat in a world like that and we couldnt settle it, so I'd rather the story be honest about it than be one of those stories where the clever gadget works right up until the plot needs it not to. Two other things about the world that I think follow, but you fix them if they dont: reading a memory without destroying it means copying it, so reading warms the room and the station's library is the coldest place on it, and anyone who makes backups is basically running a heater.

Setting: a sealed station under the ice (or a ship in the deep ocean, your pick) where dumping heat into the water is the single hardest thing they do and every job is metered against the heat budget, so crews talk about hot shifts and cold shifts and everyone knows roughly how many joules a task costs. My main character is a sixteen-year-old apprentice to the station's heat-keeper, and because her job is literally to log where every joule went, she's the one who notices that something is off: an engineer has built a tiny shutter-and-sensor gadget on a valve that only opens for the fast molecules, and it really does seem to be handing them free cooling or free power. I want the story to end with her working out where the bill is actually being paid under this version of the physics — my guess is that it's the instant the gadget writes down what it saw, so the cost moved from clearing its memory to filling it, and the accounting still comes out even — but if that's wrong, or if the price has to show up somewhere else too, go with what's actually right and let her figure that out on the page. Please dont make the engineer a villain, he's just a guy who really wants it to work, and let there be exactly one short moment where a character says the physics out loud in plain language, and no more than one.

Formatting: continuous prose only, please — a plain-text title at the top is fine, but no bold, no headings, no bullet points, no tables, no asterisk section breaks if you can help it, and definitely no author's note or bulleted here's-the-science bit after the story. Just the story, first sentence to last, like something you'd read in one go.
