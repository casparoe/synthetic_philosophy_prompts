I'm drafting a paper on whether Occam's razor is objective, and my way in has been an analogy that recurs in informal explanations of Kolmogorov complexity and Solomonoff induction: a theory is like a compressed archive, the data is like the uncompressed file, and the best theory is the one that compresses the data most. I want to use the analogy to license the claim that a scientist's preference for the simpler of two hypotheses is not merely pragmatic or aesthetic but tracks something language-independent, since the size of the smallest archive is a fact about the data rather than a reflection of anyone's favourite notation.

A colleague says the analogy assumes what I need to prove. An archive can be small only because the decompressor already sits on the receiving machine; the analogue of that decompressor is the reference machine, so simplicity is invariant only up to an additive constant, which is reassuring in the limit and useless for any finite comparison, where the constant is all there is. She offers a case: two labs study the same periodic signal, and one lab's machine ships with a built-in Fourier primitive while the other's does not, so the phenomenon has a one-line description on the first and a hundred-line one on the second, and no quantity of data tells you which machine is the right one to measure against. Call it the blessed-primitive case. She adds a second complaint: a compressor yields one winner-take-all file, whereas Solomonoff's predictor averages over all programs weighted by length, so the simplest theory is not strictly speaking what does the predicting.

Please audit the analogy for me. Give me an explicit table of what maps onto what — compressor, archive, decompressed file, the shared decoder, compression ratio, lossy versus lossless. Then separate the disanalogies that bear on the conclusion I want from those that are artefacts of the archive format, and tell me whether the blessed-primitive case should worry me more than the familiar point that complexity is uncomputable; whether the averaging complaint is a defect in the analogy or only in how I stated the conclusion; and what better analogy, if any, there is for a prior that is dominated by short programs but not reducible to the shortest one. If the conclusion survives only weakened, state the weakened form.
