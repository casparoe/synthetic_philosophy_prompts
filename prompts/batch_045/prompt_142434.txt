hi — I write about science and ideas for a magazine, and I'm working on a piece about whether a whole ant colony can be a thinking thing. I read Hofstadter's Ant Fugue as a teenager (Aunt Hillary, the colony you can hold a conversation with, the part where you realize that no individual ant has any idea what the colony as a whole is up to), and now I keep getting press releases that say things like the colony decides, the superorganism learns, the nest remembers. Before I write that paragraph I want the spec.

So: what would a theory of colony-level minds have to deliver to be worth printing? I don't want the survey of who said what, I want the list of requirements, with the case or consideration behind each one, and your view on which are non-negotiable and which are just things one camp happens to care about. Purpose: good enough that I could say in print, and defend, either that this nest is a minded thing or that it isn't, and good enough that a field biologist could in principle run something against it rather than just arguing about words.

Here are the situations I keep getting stuck on, and that I think the theory has to handle. Take them as stipulations if the biology isn't quite like this.

1. Turnover. Over a year or two every worker in the nest is dead and replaced, and in plenty of species the queen dies or is swapped out too, and the colony keeps foraging and relocating as before. What makes it the same mind over time, and is that question allowed to have the same answer as the question of what makes it the same colony?
2. Fusion and fission. Two colonies get merged into one functioning nest; one nest gets cut in half and both halves carry on fine. One mind, two, one and a half, or is the cardinality question a fake question that the theory should dissolve?
3. Dilution. You remove ants, or knock out a caste, or block a particular pheromone trail, and the colonies behavior degrades smoothly rather than switching off. Is the theory allowed to be gradualist about how much mind there is, or does having a mind have to be all-or-nothing?
4. Nesting. If the colony is a mind, and the ants are (let's stipulate) little agents that can each learn a simple association and that differ from each other temperamentally, then you have minds inside a mind. Fine — but the theory has to say what stops the same move one level up: the whole mound plus its fungus plus the beetles living in it plus the patch of forest. I want to know which part of the theory does the work of blocking the explosion, and whether that part also quietly blocks the colony case.
5. The ants have to matter. Whatever colony-level states the theory posits, they have to be realized in, and causally driven by, what the individual ants actually do; a view that floats free of the insects underneath it isn't telling me anything about ants.

Distinctions I think it can't collapse, tell me if I'm wrong: having a mind vs being something whose interests count morally; doing something that looks like problem-solving vs genuinely representing the world; one unified subject vs several partial perspectives that don't add up to a single one; and the colony thinking vs us finding it useful to talk as if it does.

And two things I most want: first, is any subset of these requirements jointly unsatisfiable — e.g. can you really have gradualism about how much mind, a determinate answer to how many minds are in the nest, and a principled stop to the nesting problem all at once? If the list is itself a trilemma, say so. Second, which single requirement knocks out the most candidates, and which requirement should I as a journalist be most suspicious of as something smuggled in because it makes a better story?

Then, briefly — a short paragraph each, no scorecard, I'll do the reporting myself — how three options do against your list: the deflationary one where it is stigmergy all the way down and the mind talk is a façon de parler, the inflationary one where any distributed system that processes information and adapts is minded to some degree, and the Hofstadter-ish one where there is a genuine thinker here even though nothing inside it has the thought. Its definately the middle one I'm most tempted by and most unsure of.
