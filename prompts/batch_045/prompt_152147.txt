I am putting together a handout on de re modality for a graduate seminar, and what I need is a case built to order rather than an illustration: something whose verdicts are generated by the machinery of the theories I name, so that a student can check each step. Two specifications make the case worth building, and I want both satisfied at once.

First, the reversal. The usual expectation is that the ontologically lavish theory is the permissive one: a plurality of concrete worlds, unrestricted recombination, every way a world could be is a way that some world is. I want a case in which the direction is reversed. The Lewisian package (concrete worlds, individuals confined to one world each, de re modal claims analysed in terms of a counterpart relation that is a matter of similarity and that need be neither symmetric nor transitive) must deliver the restrictive verdict: no, this very thing could not have been that. A leaner actualist package (ersatz or abstract worlds, individuals literally present in more than one world, modality at least as strong as S4) must deliver the permissive verdict: yes, this very thing could have been that.

Second, the isolation. Those two packages differ along several dimensions at once, and I want a case that pulls them apart: ontology of worlds, whether individuals are world-bound, and how de re claims are evaluated. The case must make both of the following swaps checkable: (a) keep the Lewisian ontology and its plenitude but replace counterpart theory with trans-world identity of individuals, and the restrictive verdict must collapse into the permissive one; (b) keep counterpart theory but replace the concrete plurality of worlds with an actualist surrogate, sets of propositions or states of affairs or whatever ersatz worlds you prefer, and the restrictive verdict must survive untouched. If both swaps behave that way, the case shows that what blocks the modal claim is the counterpart-theoretic treatment of de re modality and not the abundance of worlds. Say explicitly which component carries the load and what each swap demonstrates.

Constraints on the scenario itself. One paragraph, no more than about 120 words. Ordinary life: a person and her character and career, a boat, a house, an instrument, a garden, something I could point to. No science fiction, no magic, no fission or fusion of persons, no time travel, no omniscient narrator. The case must be a chain: a named individual or object, a long finite series of steps, and an endpoint, where each step is a change small enough that no one would deny that this very individual could have undergone it, and where the endpoint differs from the start in a way we would ordinarily describe as a different person or a different thing altogether. No step may be blocked by a standard essentialist restriction: no change of origins, of species, of kind, or of what sort of thing it is. The entire gap between start and endpoint must be accumulated out of individually innocuous steps. This matters because the permissive verdict has to be generated rather than imported: if any single step can simply be denied on essentialist grounds, the chain argument never gets going and the case is useless to me. Finally, state the target claim in its de re form, and separately state the corresponding de dicto claim, could there have been someone or something of that description. The Lewisian package should accept the second and reject the first; explain why, and why the distinction does real work here instead of papering over the problem.

What I want after the scenario.

1. The verdict for the actualist package, with the argument set out. Name the axiom you are relying on, the collapse of iterated possibility, and say why trans-world identity lets each step in the chain be a step taken by one and the same individual. Note which step an actualist who is also an origin or kind essentialist would refuse, and why that refusal is unavailable here given the constraints above.

2. The verdict for the Lewisian package. Write out the translation you are using for the de re sentence in the counterpart-theoretic primitives, world, in-world, counterpart, actual, and show exactly where the truth-condition fails, which existential claim has no witness. Then identify the two features doing the blocking: the demand that a counterpart be similar enough, and the refusal to make the counterpart relation transitive. Show that they are not independent options: if the similarity threshold is to be respected, transitivity has to go, and if transitivity is restored the threshold cannot hold. Fix the context of assessment explicitly, since similarity is interest-relative, and say how much of the verdict is hostage to that fixing. Note also that the plenitude of worlds guarantees a world where the endpoint life is lived or the endpoint object sits, and explain why that does not by itself make the de re claim true.

3. The isolation test. Walk through both swaps. Then add a third variation: compress the chain into a single large jump straight from start to endpoint. Say what each theory delivers under that variation and why, and say what this shows about which structural feature of the scenario is load-bearing.

4. The honest note. Give the strongest objection the permissive side has against the restrictive verdict: that interest-relativity plus the vagueness of similarity makes the verdict a fact about what we asked rather than about the individual, that the vagueness reintroduces precisely the pressure the case was meant to block, and that denying transitivity looks like a manoeuvre tailored to this one problem. Then give the strongest objection the restrictive side has against the permissive verdict: that it licenses a claim we do not want to license, and that bare haecceity does no work in explaining what makes the far-end individual the same individual. In one sentence each, tell me whether each objection actually lands, and if the case is to survive, what minimal change to the scenario would blunt it.

Do not let the scenario stipulate the verdicts: no remark to the effect that of course no one would count the endpoint thing as a counterpart, no step that is itself impossible, and no shifting sense of could between one theory and the other. If some part of this specification is not jointly satisfiable, or if getting both verdicts requires a move you think is illegitimate, say so and tell me which clause to drop. I would rather have a working case with one specification relaxed than a case that merely looks as though it works.
