I'm on the committee rewriting my high school's student government constitution, and I have to put a short opinion in front of the student council before it votes next week.

The clause in dispute: our student activities fee, which funds every club including the student newspaper, is currently reallocated each spring by a simple majority of the council. Last spring a majority moved to cut the newspaper's funding to zero after it ran a piece criticizing the administration; the motion failed after two weeks of protest, and both sides now want the outcome written into the document. The proposal on the table is that any change to the fee's allocation requires a two-thirds vote and does not take effect until the following academic year, while the rest of the constitution stays amendable by majority vote at any time.

I've been reading about Ulysses contracts and constitutional precommitment and think that's the right frame, but I want the argument done properly. Please draft the opinion — recommendation, reasons, and the strongest consideration on the losing side — addressing:

1. Which kind of precommitment this actually is. Distinguish binding an agent's own later self against a *predictably impaired* judgment; merely raising the cost of a decision that remains available; and entrenching the present preferences of one group against a future majority that is not the same group at all. Our student body is entirely replaced every four years and the council is elected annually, so I suspect the third, not the first — tell me if that is too quick, and whether the second is the honest description of what we would be doing.
2. What Ulysses-contract reasoning demands as a condition: the later preference must be predictably distorted, not merely different or better informed. We have exactly one episode. What would count as evidence of predictable distortion, and does a single near-miss justify entrenching anything?
3. The mast versus the unburned fleet. Ulysses could have avoided the Sirens altogether; the rope is what let him hear the song safely. So: should the clause remove the option or only make it costly to exercise — and which serves a council that still needs to defund a club that genuinely misuses money?
4. The escape-hatch problem. A two-thirds-plus-delay rule is itself changeable by amending the amendment rule (as I understand it, even the German Basic Law's eternity clause is reachable by replacing the whole document). Is that a defect to fix with double entrenchment, or the feature that keeps the precommitment from becoming a dead hand?

Recommend a concrete course — adopt, adopt with conditions such as a sunset or re-ratification by each incoming class, or reject — with actual threshold and trigger language, and say plainly where the theory has nothing to tell us.
