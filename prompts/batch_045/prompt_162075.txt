Hey — I'm putting together a one-page handout for the value-alignment reading group I run (about eight people, mix of ML folks and phil undergrads, nobody's a metaethicist), and the session is on idealized preferences: the family of views that say you shouldn't give people what they currently want, you should give them what they'd want under better conditions. I want the handout to be a branch-labeled taxonomy rather than prose, and I'd love it if you built it from the logical space first and then dropped the named views into it, rather than starting from a list of names.

The root should be something everyone in the room already accepts — roughly "what we should defer to isn't a person's occurrent desire but that desire under some改善 of the circumstances" — and then every branch below it is labeled with the question that does the splitting. Ten or twelve leaves, give or take.

Here are the distinctions I care about most, partly because they're the ones my group actually argued about, so please build around these (add better ones if you think of them):

- Is the idealized thing meant to be a theory of welfare (what's good for the person) or a theory of authority (what we're permitted to do to them, what they'd consent to)? I think this fork changes a lot downstream and people keep conflating it.
- What exactly does the idealization subtract: factual ignorance, instrumental irrationality, incoherence between desires, weakness of will, impoverished experience, false metaphysical beliefs? Crucially, is it allowed to subtract all of them or only some, and does it ever add anything?
- Order-sensitivity. Does "inform them fully, then make their desires coherent" land in the same place as "make them coherent, then inform them fully"? I've been calling this the commutativity node and I want it on the tree whether or not there's a standard name for it.
- The aggregation node: do you extrapolate each person separately and then merge the results, or merge first and extrapolate the group as one agent? And separately: does the whole procedure assume convergence, or does it have something to say when the extrapolation diverges / when the iteration doesn't have a fixed point?
- Meta-preferences: what happens when someone's actual, current, fully-informed preference is "do not extrapolate me" or "stop after one step"? Does that get veto power over the procedure, and if not, why not?
- The "is it still you at the end" node: if the idealized endpoint no longer cares about anything the current person cares about (say their whole value system was built on a false belief about what they are, and it dissolves under the idealization), is deferring to it still deferring to them?
- And a case I want handled explicitly: the person whose idealized self would rather be replaced by something else entirely, or would rather not exist.

Leaves that have to show up somewhere, described by the answer-pattern that reaches them rather than just by name (and say "unnamed" where nobody I could name defends it): the coherence-and-convergence view in the CEV style; the Brandt-ish "full information plus cognitive psychotherapy" reformed-desire view (I might have the name wrong, label it loosely); the Williams-style view where idealization is just a sound deliberative route from someone's existing motivational set; a Hare-style universalization-across-persons view; the ex ante choice behind a veil of ignorance style view; the view where idealization is purely epistemic because there are independent facts about the good and the procedure is only a way of finding them; and an anti-paternalist leaf where actual preferences keep their authority no matter how idealized the alternative looks. Also a "the question is malformed / go procedural" leaf if the space allows it.

Two more formatting asks. Each leaf gets one sentence on its most awkward commitment and one sentence on what it would actually make you write down if you had to hand something to a powerful optimizer — the group is full of people who'll ask "ok but what's the loss function," so I want the taxonomy to cash out. And please flag honestly: any two leaves your tree can't really separate, and any combination of answers that's empty (say whether it's empty because incoherent, because unattractive, or just because nobody's bothered). Don't smooth those over — the point of the handout is that people sort themselves by what they say about cases, not by which label they like.
