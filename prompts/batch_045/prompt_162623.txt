I write a column on science and ideas, and over the past year I have watched one analogy do an enormous amount of unexamined work. The phrase comes out of interface design, got its book-length treatment from Tor Nørretranders, and has been taken up by Daniel Dennett; my own version of it arrived from a cognitive scientist I interviewed last month, who put it like this: consciousness stands to the brain as the desktop of a computer stands to the machine's circuitry. Folders, icons, a pointer, a trash can, drag-and-drop: nothing in the machine is literally inside a folder, nothing is literally thrown into a bin, and yet everything on the screen is usable and, at the level of the interface, perfectly true. He then used the picture to close two questions that are live for my readers: whether there is any further fact about what it is like to see red once the interface story has been told, and whether it even makes sense to ask whether a large language model might be conscious. He answered no to both, and my suspicion is that the desktop was carrying conclusions it cannot bear.

Before I print it, I would like the analogy audited rather than endorsed.

First, the correspondence. Set out explicitly what is supposed to play the role of the screen, the icons, the pointer, the drag-and-drop gesture, the operating system, the user, and whoever designed the interface. I especially want to know where the mapping is multiply realizable or simply unset: what, on the analogy, corresponds to the user, given that the most tempting candidate, the self, looks like one more item rendered on the screen rather than the person looking at it.

Second, the places it breaks, and which breaks matter. Three worry me and I would like them treated as the load-bearing ones. A desktop has an audience standing outside the machine; a user illusion of the brain's own making seems to have no one watching, and if the watcher is itself an icon the analogy appears to consume its own subject. An interface is designed for the convenience of that audience, which imports a designer and a purpose into a story that otherwise has only selection pressure to appeal to. And an interface is causally inert with respect to the computations it summarizes while remaining causally potent with respect to the hands on the keyboard; there are no external hands in the skull, so either conscious experience is causally idle altogether, which would make the interface a decoration the organism pays for and gains nothing from, or it acts on downstream systems inside the same head, which makes it less a display than a control layer and quietly gives back much of what the analogy was meant to take away. Tell me which horn the deflationary conclusions actually require and whether either horn is defensible.

Third, what the analogy smuggles in. Interfaces both conceal and report: a battery icon is not a battery but it tracks one. So distinguish the readings of illusion on offer, from mere compression through benign misrepresentation to outright falsity, and say whether the step from less than the whole truth to nothing it is like survives once the distinction is on the table.

Then the verdict I actually need: for each of the two conclusions he drew, state whether the analogy supports it, merely permits it, or was doing all the work, and separate the disanalogies that bite from the ones a defender can concede as cosmetic. I would also find it useful to know whether the picture has any teeth at all, by which I mean anything it predicts independently: that introspection should fail in patterned ways tracking interface convenience rather than underlying structure, say, or that there should be interface-level objects with no machine-level correlate at all. If it predicts nothing and can redescribe whatever we find, say so.

Finally, if the desktop is the wrong picture, propose a better one and say what it would license instead. I have heard the instrument panel of an aircraft on autopilot, a cache, a summary statistic, a log the system keeps for itself, and an application programming interface, all floated in this space, and I would like to know which of these does real work and which merely relocates the mystery.
