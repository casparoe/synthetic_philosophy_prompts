so I've been reading about this thing called evidential cooperation in large worlds and I want to make fun of it in the form of a parody advice column. The way I've got it in my head (fix it in the writing if I've got it wrong): in a really big world your own causal effect on anything is basically zero, but there are heaps of other agents out there similar enough to you that whatever you decide is decent evidence about what they decide — so cooperating means you should expect the other people-like-you to cooperate as well, and your reason to be decent comes from the news your action gives you rather than from anything it causes. And the bigger the world is, the better the numbers work out.

What I want is a "Dear Brenda" style agony-aunt column in a small-town paper, 500-600 words: one letter from a reader who has stopped voting, donating and recycling because one person obviously makes no difference, and then Brenda's reply.

Brenda has to actually run the argument right — the vanishing causal impact, why there being enough similar agents rescues it, the gap between what your action does and what it tells you, and the bit where the whole reason gets stronger as the world gets bigger. But play her totally straight and kind, and let her end up somewhere quietly awful: she never once says the reader's cooperation helps anybody, only that it makes her excellent evidence; she has to get past the obvious "that's just managing the news, not doing anything" worry without dropping character or admitting it's weird; and she should let slip that the reason dries up completely if you happen to be the only one of your kind around. No winking at the reader, no endnotes explaining the joke, and don't name any papers or people.
