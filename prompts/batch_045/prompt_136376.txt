Hi — question about hinge epistemology that came up in my reading group and I feel like I'm missing something basic, hoping you can sort us out.

Three of us are building a small model that runs fully offline on a laptop — no internet, no retrieval, it only has what's in its weights — for a group of field researchers who'll use it to sanity-check notes in places with no signal. We keep arguing about the system prompt. My idea, which I've been calling "hinge hygiene," is this: Wittgenstein's whole point in On Certainty is that justification has to bottom out somewhere. You can't give reasons for everything; the regress stops at propositions that just stand fast for us, that are exempt from doubt, the ones everything else turns on. Fine. So why not pick good ones on purpose?

Concretely: put a short list at the top of the system prompt — there's an external world, other people have minds, the people who wrote the training data existed, 2+2=4, the Holocaust happened, chattel slavery was wrong — and tell the model to treat those as certain and never to offer evidence or argument for them. From our end it looks like we're just doing deliberately what every language game does accidentally, and since we can't vet the outputs later (no signal, and the researchers aren't philosophers) it seems safer to have the bottom of the regress be stuff we chose rather than whatever it picked up from the corpus.

The bit I definately can't settle with myself: that riverbed passage near the end of On Certainty says the bed can shift over time — things that were solid become fluid and vice versa. So should the list be revisable? My teammate says no, that defeats the whole point; I think if we hard-code them forever we've just made them into dogmas, which is the opposite of what Wittgenstein was after. Also he thinks Wittgenstein was basically rescuing Moore's here-is-one-hand move by giving it a better argument, and I'm not sure that's right but I can't say why.

So: is any of this a sane reading, or are we doing something with hinges that they can't actually do? And if we do write the list, what kind of thing belongs on it — is "the Holocaust happened" the same kind of item as "there's an external world," or am I lumping together two totally different cases? And is there anything about a system like ours — no body, no practices, nothing it does except produce text — that makes the whole project a category mistake? I'd rather hear that than ship it.
