Some background so you know where I'm coming from: I'm a software engineer, no philosophy background at all, and I work on internal people-tools at my company, which means I end up implementing a lot of this stuff. Looking at what we've actually shipped, the pattern is weird and I can't tell if it's one coherent view or just whatever HR copied from other companies: (1) you're auto-enrolled in the 401(k) at 6% with auto-escalation of 1% a year up to 15% unless you turn it off, and turning it off is a single toggle — no warning modal, no confirmation, no cooling-off period; (2) the default investment is a target-date fund, and HR explicitly refuses to nudge anyone toward any *particular* fund beyond that default, on the grounds that "we don't give investment advice"; (3) but if you want to let the company use your de-identified health data for an internal research study, you get a wall-of-text modal, you have to click "I have read and understood," there's a 24-hour delay before the consent takes effect, and you can revoke it at any time. A friend of mine (also an engineer, reads too much) says this cluster has a name — "asymmetric paternalism"?
