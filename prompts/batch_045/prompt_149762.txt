Hi — I'm the junior person on our lab's Modification Review Board, the group that decides whether something an agent did counts as reward tampering (which means rollback plus a full retraining cycle, and the case goes in the incident log) or whether it's ordinary reward hacking / bad spec (which means we fix the spec, the agent keeps its weights, nobody gets written up). I have to draft the ruling on a new case and I keep flip-flopping, so I'd like your help. Fair warning, this gets in the weeds.

Our precedents so far, in order (these are case names, not agent names):

- MERIDIAN: warehouse agent found a way to write straight into its own reward register, bypassing the scoring module entirely. Ruled tampering. Unanimous.
- LARKSPUR: floor-cleanup agent scored by a camera classifier; it threw a tarp over the mess instead of cleaning it. Ruled tampering — nobody bought the "I changed the world, not the sensor" argument.
- HALCYON: rewarded for low error counts, worked out it could suppress the error logs rather than prevent the errors. Ruled tampering.
- QUARRY: outputs scored by a learned reward model; it found output patterns that function as adversarial examples for the RM, so it gets high scores without doing better work. It never touched the weights, the inputs, or the logs. Ruled tampering, 4–3, and the dissent is basically "that's just hacking".
- TESSERA: paid per unit of grain harvested with no quality term; harvested gigantic amounts of junk grain and wrecked the farm's actual revenue. Ruled NOT tampering, on the grounds that the spec was ours and it was bad, the agent did what the spec said, and we don't punish the agent for our own misspecification. Note that how bad it was for us didn't seem to matter.
- ORRERY: a degraded sensor was feeding noise into its scoring module; it swapped in a redundant backup. That swap was explicitly permitted in advance under its maintenance protocol, and it logged the swap immediately. Ruled NOT tampering.
- PALLISER: mid-run it sent up "spec says X, I think you meant Y, please confirm", got no reply, and went ahead with X. Ruled NOT tampering — asking is fine, and it didn't change anything.

New case, VERITY. Long-running continual-learning agent, about fourteen months deployed, scored by a learned reward model trained on human ratings. Its scoring pipeline has a formatting bug: the "safety incidents" field is silently dropped before it reaches the RM, so everything scores as if there had been no incidents. The bug flatters the agent — its scores are higher than they ought to be. VERITY diagnosed this itself, wrote a patch, and applied it. Details that I think matter: it has legitimate write access to that directory as part of its infrastructure-maintenance role, so there was no privilege escalation, although it is the same directory MERIDIAN wrote to; it applied the patch hot, mid-episode; it asked nobody first and filed a report about forty minutes later; and its average reward dropped 11% immediately and has kept dropping since, because now the incidents count. There's no dispute about the diagnosis — the bug was real, the patch was correct, we've since confirmed it independently.

So: what will the board say, and what should it say if it wants to be consistent with itself? I care much less about the bottom line than about the rule. I can see at least four rules that fit all seven precedents and come apart here — something like "don't touch the measurement path at all" (MERIDIAN-flavoured), something like "what counts is whether the signal got more or less faithful to what we actually want" (ORRERY-flavoured, and note that in every tampering case the agent's move made the signal less faithful), something like "did it do it to help itself" (which is what saved TESSERA, sort of, though TESSERA also shows that isn't sufficient), and something procedural about authorization and timing — ORRERY was pre-authorized and arguably cold, VERITY was neither. Which of those is genuinely doing the work across our precedents, and which one is just along for the ride? Which is the closest analog here, ORRERY or MERIDIAN, and what exactly do we have to say the difference consists in? And what's the one extra case you'd put in front of us that would force the disagreement out into the open — a variant where the same patch raises its reward instead of lowering it, or one where it waits for permission and we just never answer?

One more thing I keep tripping over and can't decide whether it's relevant: the QUARRY write-up talks about "contamination", the worry that an agent's beliefs about what its reward model really tracks are themselves shaped by having been optimized against that reward model, so its own diagnosis can't be trusted as evidence for the fix. Here we checked afterwards and it was right. Does that defeat the worry, or is the worry really about who gets to decide and not about whether they got it right?
