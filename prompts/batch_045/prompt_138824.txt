For years a reading group I sit in has been stuck on the same case, and it has not moved an inch: one person is severely tortured for fifty years, versus 3^^^3 — some unimaginably large number — people each getting a single speck of dust flicked into the eye, a momentary irritation that the subject forgets within seconds. The aggregators say the number settles it. The non-aggregators say that no quantity of trivial discomforts can ever add up to torture, and some of them invoke a lexical threshold below which a harm simply does not sum.

I want a diagnosis of the impasse, not a verdict on the case and not a survey of population ethics.

My hypothesis is that the dispute is not really about aggregation at all, because two variants split our group along lines that neither side's stated argument predicts. In the first variant, every one of the eye-flicks happens to a single person, strung out across an indefinitely long life, so that no other person is involved: several of the non-aggregators soften here, which suggests that what was doing the work for them was the separateness of persons rather than the smallness of the harms. In the second variant, the flick is replaced by a sub-perceptual irritation that no subject ever consciously notices and that leaves no trace in memory, mood, or behaviour, though we stipulate that it registers sub-personally: here some of the aggregators harden in the opposite direction, which suggests a hidden commitment about whether a state has to be consciously felt to count in a welfare comparison at all.

So: what kind of disagreement is this? Substantive, about whether disvalue aggregates across persons and whether thresholds can be lexical? Verbal, about what 'harm', 'worse', or 'adds up' are being used to mean? A difference about which question the case is meant to answer — the ordering of outcomes, the rationality of a choice, or whether intuition is any evidence when the numbers outrun anything we can represent? Or several questions being run together under one heading? Argue for one of these, or for a better framing, using what each side actually commits itself to rather than a catalogue of positions. Then, given your diagnosis, say what would count as progress: a distinction, a formal result, a redesigned case, an abandonment of the question — or argue that none should be expected, and say why.
