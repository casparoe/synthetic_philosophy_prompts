Had a massive argument with my mate Steve last night and I can't let it go, and it turns out I've got to make an actual decision about it next week, so I could use some help.

Steve's line was this: the welfare state is basically a big insurance pool that everyone pays into out of their wages, so someone who could work and just doesn't and draws the whole time is a free rider, like sneaking into a car insurance scheme the day after the crash. That's why he thinks the workfare and job-search-requirements stuff is fine — not cruel, just the terms of the club you're claiming from. I said the placements don't seem to lead to real jobs and that the councils own figures show they recover less from fraud investigations than the investigations cost, and he just shrugged and said that's not the point, the point is that taking out without ever putting in is wrong and the conditions are how we say so. That bit I couldn't answer, and it's still annoying me.

Here's the decision. I'm one of five trustees of a tiny local charity, a couple of thousand a year from an old bequest, and the deed says the money is for the relief of the deserving poor in our parish. We're rewriting the rules. Do we attach conditions? Steve thinks obviously yes — proof of work search, or turning up for a few volunteer shifts, and we stop people who won't. Another trustee says we should just give the same amount to anyone in the parish who asks and forget desert entirely, because who are we to judge. I'm stuck in the middle and I dont know what I actually think.

Things that make it harder: we can't check any of it without going through people's bank statements and medical letters, which feels grim, and the people who most need the money are the worst at forms and most likely to miss an appointment. Also the money isn't our contributions, it was left to us — but it was left by someone who clearly did want the distinction made.

So: is Steve's free rider argument actually a good one, or is there something wrong with it that I can't put my finger on? And concretely, what should we write into the rules? Is there a way of cashing out "deserving" that isn't just punishing people for being chaotic, and what do we do about the fact that policing the conditions would eat a chunk of the money we could simply be giving away?
