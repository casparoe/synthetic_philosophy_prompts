I keep having this argument with a friend of mine who's an economist and I always end up losing it because my entire contribution is basically a list of examples followed by "but that's obviously gross". He says my reactions are just noise dressed up as morality. I want to see whether there's actually a principle buried in them. So: going by gut on cases, no peeking at the literature (I can't look anything up right now, and please don't give me names of papers or a reading list, I've read enough of the debate and I want your own take on the pattern).

Here's where I land. A poor person selling a kidney to a rich stranger: ban it, no question. Donating a kidney to a stranger for free: not just fine, genuinely admirable — and I know those two sit badly together but I don't want to give either up. Getting paid for blood plasma: fine. Getting paid for whole blood: makes me uneasy, and I can't explain the difference. Paying a broke student to take real medical risks in an early-stage drug trial: fine, and I don't think exploitation is the right word for it. Selling eggs for IVF: uneasy, feels much closer to the kidney than to the plasma — but selling sperm is completely fine, and noticing that makes me suspect I'm just squeamish about women's bodies. Getting paid to queue up for someone else: a bit off, can't work myself up about it. Surge pricing for rides on New Year's Eve: fine. A shop trebling the price of bottled water during a flood: not fine, ought to be illegal — that pair is the one I go back and foward on most, because I can't say what the difference is supposed to be. Tickets sold on above face value: more annoying than immoral. Selling your vote: strongly not fine, ban it, and unlike the kidney case I don't feel any pull at all. Paying a grandson to go and visit his own lonely grandmother: grubby, not fine; paying a stranger to do companionship visits to a lonely old man: fine, that's just a job. The charity that pays women struggling with addiction to be sterilised: not fine, it actually makes me angry in a way the others don't. Commercial surrogacy: round in circles, no stable verdict. And I genuinely don't know what to think about selling a company access to your old photos and messages so it can train a model on them. One more thing: I'm told the one country that actually legalised a paid kidney system more or less cleared its waiting list, and learning that moved me not at all — which worries me, because it suggests I'm not really weighing consequences here.

My own attempt at a principle: it's fine to sell something if both parties are better off and nobody else is made worse off, unless the thing being sold is internal to a practice that's supposed to run on a different logic — votes, care, medicine, justice. But plasma and kidneys are both medical, and care shows up in both the grandson case and the paid companion case, so I already know it's broken as stated. Test it, repair it, or bin it and give me two or three candidates, stated precisely enough that I could apply them myself: which of my cases forces each clause, what each candidate says about cases I haven't listed (say: cash payments to get people vaccinated, selling residency rights to rich foreigners, and letting someone sell a place further up the transplant waiting list), and which of my verdicts I'd have to drop to get a clean principle — and if the choice is between something ugly that saves all my verdicts and something simple that costs me two of them, tell me which you'd take and why. Also say honestly whether the waiting-list consequence should move me. British English please, lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence and then give me the reasoning — no preamble and no restating my question back at me.
