I'm writing a chapter on computational creativity, and there's a move I keep hitting in our reading group: someone says creativity is just blind variation plus selective retention — i.e. evolution — and concludes that the only thing standing between us and machine creativity is search budget. I've been working through Hofstadter's picture instead, where a creator works inside a high-dimensional space of stylistic knobs (letterforms, chord choices, whatever) and creativity is twiddling them, with the deep kind being when you discover a knob nobody knew was there, or stretch one past where the style said it could go. I think the evolution analogy is carrying more than it can bare. Can you audit it for me? Give the mapping explicitly — what plays the gene, the mutation, the fitness function, the selection pressure, the generation, crossover — then the places it breaks. Two I especially care about: (a) evolution hands you a fixed genotype space and an external fitness function, and I suspect those are doing the heavy lifting; (b) here the thing judging the variants is the same system that generated them, so the landscape shifts while you climb it. Does the "just add search" conclusion survive once those are on the table, or was the analogy carrying it the whole time? And is there a source domain that fits knob-twiddling better than evolution does?
