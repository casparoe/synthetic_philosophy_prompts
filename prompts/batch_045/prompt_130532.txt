I am seventeen, in my final year of secondary school, and for an independent project I have been reading a short Danish book from 1843, published under the name Johannes de silentio, about the binding of Isaac. I have a question my teacher could only half answer, and I am hoping you can tell me whether the thing I keep noticing has a name.

The book opens with several retellings of the story in each of which Abraham is willing but something goes wrong — he loses his faith, or he is overcome by guilt — and the author says that none of these Abrahams really has faith. Later he contrasts Abraham with figures such as Agamemnon and Jephthah, who also had to kill a child; those are called tragic heroes, because what they did could be explained to their city and mourned by it. Abraham cannot explain himself to anyone. The usual label people reach for here is that he suspends the ethical for the sake of some end, and I do not think that is quite the thing I keep noticing, or at least not all of it.

What I keep noticing is a reversal of the signs. Ordinarily the marks of an action's being justified are these: you can give your reasons, other people can see their force, you would apply them to anyone in your position, you do the hard thing reluctantly, and you would stop if good evidence arrived that you were wrong. In the three cases I have been worrying at, every one of those marks seems to count against the action, and its opposite seems to count for it.

The first is Abraham. The second is a case I made up: a girl who has won a place to study music gives it up to look after her younger brother after their mother becomes ill. She does not claim to be bound by any duty, she denies that anyone else in her position would have to do the same, she gives no account of the consequences, and when pressed she says only that if she left she would no longer be the person she is. The people around her treat her imperviousness to the fact that nobody else is bound as depth rather than as stubbornness. The third is also made up: a man sells his family's house and takes the money abroad because a voice instructed him to. Structurally it is identical — private, unsupported by reasons anyone else could share, not applicable to others, unmoved by contradiction — and we call it a breakdown.

So what I want is not a word for Abraham's case. I want to know whether the reversal itself is a thing with a name, and four things about it. First: whether it has to be religious, or whether the same structure shows up in secular ethics under some other description, because in my second case nothing issues the requirement at all — there is no commander — and the structure still seems to me the same, which suggests the externality of the command is not what is doing the work. Second: how one would tell my three cases apart in a way that does not simply say that God exists in one and not in the others. Third: whether it is part of the phenomenon that being wrong does not settle anything — if it turned out that there was no God, or that the brother recovered and said he would have been fine, or that the voice was a tumour, then in none of the cases does that seem to me to settle whether the person did the right thing at the time, and the failure, if it is one, gets labelled as a failure of a different kind altogether. Fourth: which of two descriptions I have arrived at is better — either there is a second standard here, and its marks happen to be the negation of the ordinary ones, or there is no standard at all in this region and the ordinary marks simply fail to apply, so that what I am calling a reversal is only the absence of a verdict. I lean towards the second, but it seems to make my first case and my third case the same thing, and I do not want that.

If there is an established concept, tell me which it is, how my description fits it, and where it departs from it; I have already been told that it is the suspension of the ethical, and I want to know why that is more than, or less than, what I have described. If the nearest concepts are several, tell me which fits best and what each of the others leaves out. And if there is nothing established, then give me a name and a definition sharp enough that I could take a fourth case and say whether it counts. Please do not answer with a label alone — I can look up words myself. I want the carving: what this thing is, what it is not, and where its edges lie.
