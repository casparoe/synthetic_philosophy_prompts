After a long argument with a friend last night I need help working out what, if anything, actually separates us. The case we kept circling was this: a planet of sentient but wholly non-agential creatures, no one who can act, no one who could ever have acted. My friend insists that the creatures' suffering is nonetheless a reason for things to have been otherwise — a reason with no one whose reason it is. I insist that if nothing could ever have responded to it, we are not talking about a reason at all, only about a bad state of affairs.

I would like a systematic comparison of the two ways of spelling out her claim. Call the first unowned reasons: favouring relations whose agent slot is simply empty, but which otherwise behave like reasons — they have weight, they can be outweighed, they answer why-questions, they are what regret and wished-for-difference seem to track. Call the second bare betterness: facts about what would be better or worse simpliciter, with no relational structure and no agent slot to fill.

Run the two against three tests we could not settle. First, weight: can anything be outweighed where there is no deliberator whose options are being ranked, or does the language of weight quietly smuggle an agent back in? Second, explanation: can either account make sense of the pull towards saying the suffering ought not to be, given that ought-to-be with no agent attached seems to be a different mood from ought-to-do, and it is unclear which view can host both moods. Third, scope: my friend wanted to distinguish ownerless reasons, where no bearer of any kind exists; unanswerable reasons, where a bearer exists but no possible action could satisfy them, as with a conclusive reason to undo what has already been done; and counterfactually occupied reasons, where there would have been a bearer had circumstances been only slightly different. Do these stand or fall together, or do they come apart? If they come apart, that is the answer I most want, because then our disagreement was never about one thing.

Is there any work the unowned-reasons view does that bare betterness cannot, or is my friend's reason just a monadic value fact in relational clothing? Say which of the two you think survives, where exactly the analogy between them is carrying the argument, and what each view would have to give up to absorb the other's cases.
