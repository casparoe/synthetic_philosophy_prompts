I'm a journalist — I write a weekly column on science and ideas — and I could use help with an argument I'm having with a friend, partly because I want to steal it for the column. The angle I have in mind is something like "when should you stop playing by the rules," but I don't yet have the machinery to say anything interesting about it.

We're both hooked on this dumb free puzzle app called Sigil. Every level hands you a starting string of letters, a target string, and exactly four legal moves, and you have to get from one to the other. The level we're stuck on: you start with ME, and the target is MU. The moves are:

- if your string ends in E, you can tack a U onto the end
- you can double everything after the first letter (so MEU becomes MEUEU)
- you can replace any run of three E's with a single U, anywhere in the string
- you can delete any pair of adjacent U's

He's put maybe fourty hours into it. I put in three, gave up, and instead sat down with a notepad and convinced myself it literally can't be done — something about the number of E's never landing on a multiple of three, so you can never get down to zero E's and thus never to MU. I'm not totally sure my argument is airtight, but I'm fairly confident.

He's annoyed about it. His position is that the game asks you to *produce* MU, and a proof that MU can't be produced isn't a solution to the puzzle at all — it's a different activity, and a spoilery one, basically the same as reading a walkthrough. He also says the devs definately wouldn't ship an unsolvable level, so if it looks impossible there must be some trick move or glitch we haven't stumbled on yet, which means grinding is still the rational thing to do.

Two more wrinkles. (1) There's a data-mined copy of the level files floating around, and I could just look up whether MU is reachable. I haven't, and it feels genuinely different from my notepad thing, but I can't articulate why. (2) The new update adds a so-called reflex token: once per level you can permanently rewrite one of the four rules (e.g. change "three E's" to "two E's"). He says this is the game finally letting you get outside the system instead of just shuffling symbols around inside it. I think it's the opposite — it's one more move, inside one more system, and if you use it you're still just playing.

So: is my notepad thing actually a solution to the level, or is he right that it's a category mistake? Is looking at the level files different in a way that matters, and if so how? Who's right about the reflex token? And what do I actually tell him on Friday when he asks whether I want to keep grinding with him?
