I'm a journalist covering AI and the science of mind, and next month I'm giving a 25-minute talk at an interpretability and alignment workshop. What I need from you is Q&A prep, not a defense of my position.

The room: mostly ML researchers who think in training objectives, loss curves, and eval results, and who will demand an experiment that discriminates my view from its rivals; two philosophers of mind, one a functionalist about cognition and one who holds that any question about machine agency is premature until we settle what agency is in general; and three or four other science writers. No phenomenologists, no literary theorists, nobody interested in personae as a cultural form.

My thesis has two parts. The negative claim, which I label "no bare model": the agents-versus-simulators debate shares a false presupposition, namely that there is a model which, on its own, either has or lacks agency and the other psychological predicates; I argue that being goal-directed, deceptive, helpful, or evasive is never a property of the weights alone but of a model-in-a-frame — weights plus system prompt, fine-tuning, memory, interface, and an accountable deployer. The positive claim: what a frame supplies is not a character for the system to simulate, as the simulator picture has it, but a partial constitution of the standpoint from which text is produced, so that the "assistant" one addresses is not a role the model plays but a role the exchange calls into being; simulation is a misleading metaphor precisely because it covertly credits the system with a standpoint of its own from which to model someone else's.

Include the question I am hoping nobody asks.
