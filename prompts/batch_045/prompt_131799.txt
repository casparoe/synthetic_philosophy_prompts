I sit on the board of a 380-unit housing cooperative. Our monthly carrying charges fund one indivisible pool: building maintenance, a shared heating plant, insurance, and, since a contested 4–3 board vote last spring, a $14,000 contract with a local housing-advocacy group that lobbies the city council. One member has withheld the portion of his charge he attributes to that contract — roughly three dollars a month — while paying the rest. He does not claim the contract is unlawful, or that he is owed a refund, or that he was misled when he bought in. He claims that being compelled to fund advocacy he voted against and derives no benefit from is theft; that a taking does not stop being theft because a majority authorized it; and that our bylaw permitting charges only for the common benefit of all members supports him. He has offered instead to pay the disputed amount into an escrow directed to a charity of his own choosing, and asks that the board either accept that arrangement or put a standing conscientious-objection rule to a membership vote at the annual meeting in eleven weeks.

Draft the decision memo I will circulate to the membership. It should state a concrete recommendation with a time frame and give the reasoning for it. I want it to engage his framing on its merits rather than rest on the board's legal authority: say which feature of a taking is doing the work in the charge of theft — absence of consent, absence of benefit, absence of any prior entitlement to the money, the use the money is put to — whether that feature is actually present here, and whether the bylaw bears the reading he gives it. Note explicitly where a co-op differs from a state; he grants that the difference is that he can exit by selling his unit, and argues that this makes our position worse, not better. Give the strongest consideration on whichever side you reject its full weight, including what granting or denying an exemption does to the co-op as a voluntary association over the next several years. American English, 650 words maximum, under the headings Recommendation, Reasons, and Objections We Are Overruling.
