Next month I am defending a thesis chapter on epiphenomenalism and the paradox of phenomenal judgment at a departmental colloquium, and I need a Q&A prep sheet rather than a defense of the view.

My thesis is that epiphenomenalism survives the paradox. The reply I am defending is what I call the co-realization reply: a dedicated introspective mechanism produces my judgments about my current experience, and although the experience does not cause that judgment, the mechanism is partly realized by the very neural event that realizes the experience. So the judgment nomologically co-varies with, and is counterfactually dependent on, the experience, without being causally produced by it. I want to add two further claims: first, that this preserves first-personal authority, so that judgments about how things presently seem to one keep their distinctive epistemic status with no causal contact at all; second, that the residual cost of epiphenomenalism is not epistemic but explanatory, because on this picture phenomenal properties can figure neither in causal explanations of behavior nor in rationalizing explanations of action.

The room: two intentionalists about phenomenal character who think the debate should have been closed by representationalism; a philosopher of action who works on reasons and deviant causal chains; an epistemologist who works on safety conditions and on knowledge from mere correlation; and a physicalist who holds that any property doing no causal work should be eliminated. Nobody present is sympathetic to dualism, and nobody works on consciousness specifically.

Give me the seven or eight objections I will actually get from this room, in the order they will arrive, phrased the way each of these people would phrase them, ordered by how much damage they do, each with a reply I can deliver in about ninety seconds, a note on what I should concede rather than fight, and a note on where the reply is soft.

Two worries of my own I want placed in that ordering, with advice on whether to raise them myself or wait: the generalization worry, that nomological co-variation without causation looks like it would also ground knowledge of any nomologically co-varying epiphenomenal property I have no wish to be a realist about, so the reply seems to prove too much; and the self-defeat worry, that on my account phenomenal concepts no longer reveal anything non-physical, which removes the main reason anyone holds epiphenomenalism in the first place. Finally, include the question I am hoping nobody asks.
