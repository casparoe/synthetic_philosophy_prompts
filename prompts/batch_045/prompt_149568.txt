My reading group did consciousness last term and I've ended up somewhere between "consciousness is a user illusion" and "no, there really is something it's like", and I can't tell whether the spot I've landed in is stable or whether I'm just helping myself to two metaphors that pull apart. Roughly where I am: (1) conscious experience is a display — a heavily lossy, compressed summary the system shows itself, more like a dashboard than like the engine room; (2) but that doesn't make me a full illusionist: the icons are real, and what's false is only the assumption that they resemble the processing they summarize; (3) the display does work — deliberation operates on it, and a creature without it would behave differently, so it isn't epiphenomenal; (4) it was tuned for usefulness rather than accuracy, and I want to apply that to introspection too, not just to outer perception; (5) still, introspection is authoritative about which icons are currently on screen, even though it tells you nothing about the machinery; (6) there's an epistemic asymmetry — no complete third-person description entails what it's like — but that's a limit on description, not a metaphysical claim; (7) and so the hard problem more or less evaporates, because once you stop expecting the display to be derivable from the processing, the only question left is why this process gets this icon, and that's a design question.

Two case verdicts I've also committed to. I think phantom-limb pain is a real pain: "orphan icons", icons with no underlying state they're tracking, are perfectly genuine experiences — so I'm fine with the display fabricating. But I also think a creature that moved and spoke exactly as we do while having no display at all is conceivable, and if it's conceivable it's doing everything we do without one, which sits badly with (3). And I want to say there's a fact of the matter about whether the icon I now get from ripe tomatos is the same one I got at twenty — call it icon drift — while having no idea what could make it true or false. My flatmate, who's an illusionist in the Dennett vein, says (1) and (4) together collapse into his view and that (2) is a verbal dodge; he also says my whole package has a regress in it, because a user illusion needs a user, though I think that one's a fake problem.

So: audit the set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it, ranked by severity rather than all flagged as equally bad. For each real tension say what would have to give and what the cheapest fix costs me — I'd rather know which single revision cleans up the most than be told to rebuild. Please don't just hand me a label for the package and stop there. And tell me what the surviving view predicts for a case I haven't made up my mind about: an artificial system with a rich self-model and a dashboard-like summary of its own processing, no biology anywhere — does it get a display, and would we ever be in a position to tell?
