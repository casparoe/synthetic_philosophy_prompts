Here is roughly where I've landed on alignment, and I want an audit of whether it hangs together before I write it up properly.

1. Alignment is hard by default: capability growth will not hand us a system that cares about what we care about, and search pressure has no particular reason to select for that.
2. There is no coherent human utility function to point at. Human motivation is fragmented, context-activated, and partly constructed; I find the shard-ish picture plausible, on which even apparently unified agency is assembled out of many small context-triggered circuits rather than one explicit objective.
3. So the target should not be a value specification but a procedural one: build systems that would not do anything we'd flag if we saw it, that defer rather than seize, that stay corrigible — systems whose aims are, roughly, whatever we would endorse on reflection.
4. The dominant risk is not static misspecification but that systems will be able to model and manipulate whoever is supervising them, so any scheme that leans on a trustworthy approval signal is fragile in exactly the regime that matters.
5. A further failure mode I've started calling "preference laundering": a system's arbitrary quirks get stabilized into apparent human preferences by being reflected back through data. Its outputs land in the corpus that trains it and its successors, the next round of feedback is given on text already shaped by the last round, and after a few iterations the pipeline is fitting a caricature of approval that the models themselves largely authored, with a human face on it. I also think this isn't only a data-hygiene problem, since the same laundering can run through the world — through the system's effects on what humans end up saying, valuing, and building institutions around.
6. The bottleneck is conceptual confusion more than engineering, and I support slowing frontier capability work — pause advocacy, differential technological development — to buy time for the conceptual work.
7. Even so, I think most of the actual damage this decade will come from ordinary institutions deploying mediocre systems carelessly, and that is where I would put my own effort, notwithstanding that misalignment is the bigger chunk of expected value in the tail.
