I work on the family of puzzles that includes the lottery paradox and the preface paradox, and I want a number rather than a tour of the literature. Take the unity thesis: the two paradoxes are instances of a single underlying puzzle, such that any adequate treatment of one transfers to the other without invoking anything special to that case — the preface being, say, a lottery whose tickets are claims and whose odds are set by the author's own fallibility. My credence that the unity thesis is false is around 0.6; a colleague who has published on both puts it near 0.15, and I want to know where that gap actually sits.

Give me your own credence in the unity thesis, decomposed. The terms I would want separated, at a minimum: (i) whether the author's grounds for each individual claim differ in kind from the ticket-holder's — first-order evidence about a subject matter versus merely statistical knowledge of the odds — and whether that difference can bear any normative weight when what is at issue is binary justified belief and closure under conjunction, rather than degrees of confidence; (ii) whether the author's expectation of error is distinctive in being higher-order evidence about her own reliability, and whether its self-directed character does any work that third-person distrust would not; (iii) whether the two cases come apart under a variant I find useful: a referee's report from a reader whose error-detection record I have excellent reason to trust, who tells me the manuscript of two hundred claims contains at least one falsehood but not which, while my own evidence supports each claim individually. Call the original the author variant and this the referee variant.

So: a rough probability for each term, a probability that the referee variant is straightforwardly a lottery, a probability that the author variant is not one, and the combination into a single number. Then tell me which term your final number is most sensitive to, what argument or evidence would move it most, and which of these sub-questions my colleague and I are most likely answering differently.
