Me and two friends have been arguing for months about wether you actually experience anything while you're asleep and dreaming — is there something it's like for you at 3am, or does the whole "I had this weird dream" thing only get assembled when you wake up and start reaching for it? We keep talking past each other, so I want to turn it into one of those flowcharts where you answer a few questions and land on a named position, so we can see where we actually disagree instead of arguing about labels.

Can you build me that decision tree? Start from the questions that split the logical space, not from a list of theories, and phrase every branch so a friend can answer it without knowing which leaf it leads to. Aim for six to eight branch questions and five to seven leaves.

I need these leaves at minimum: the plain view that a dream is an experience going on while you sleep, something like a hallucination; the view that the dream "happens" at the moment of waking — the report is composed on the spot, not read off a stored sequence; the view that there's no fact of the matter about what went on in there, so our question is empty; the Wittgenstein-ish one (I think that's Malcolm?) where what breaks is the very idea of an experience nobody could ever check, so "I dreamt" isn't really a report of anything; and the one where dreaming is a different kind of mental act than perceiving, closer to imagining.

Use these cases for the branch questions, they're the ones that actually moved us: a trained lucid dreamer who can move their eyes in a code from inside the dream, so we get a report from inside the state instead of after it; a dream cut short, where what you say about the part before you were woken changes depending on how you're asked; a recording of your brain activity during one night of REM that years later gets played back into you and arrives with the feeling of remembering; a dreamer with all the usual sleep-lab signs who is never woken to report anything; and a report that mentions a crowd of people where nothing in the report ever looked at or thought about their faces.

Each leaf gets one line on the most awkward thing it commits you to. Also flag any two leaves the tree can't really seperate, and any combination of answers nobody seems to hold — with a note on whether that's because it's incoherent, just unattractive, or only unexplored.
