I am trying to decide what to demand of a theory of the *timing* of conscious experience, using the color phi phenomenon as the test case: a red dot flashes at one location, then a green dot at a nearby location a fraction of a second later, and subjects report a single dot moving between the two, changing color around the midpoint — that is, a change experienced before the stimulus normally taken to cause it. The live question is whether this is Orwellian (an experience occurs and is then revised or erased) or Stalinesque (nothing is experienced until after the second flash, and the history is written late, with the gap never consciously occupied).

Before I commit to either, I want the specification. Write the desiderata any theory of when an experience occurs must meet to count as adequate for settling this question: the cases it must get right and the distinctions it must not collapse, each with the case or consideration behind it. Include at least the following, and correct or add to them as you see fit:

- A non-circular account of what it is for an experience to be *at* a time — one that does not simply read the time off the subject's own sense of when it occurred, since the subject can be wrong about that.
- A verdict on what occupies the interval between the two flashes, and a principled rather than ad hoc criterion for it.
- Separation of the time *of* the experience from the time the experience *represents*, and from the time at which the subject can report or judge it.
- Handling of the cutaneous rabbit (taps at wrist, elbow, and shoulder producing an experience of taps marching up the arm, with the elbow tap experienced before it is delivered), so that the account is not a special-purpose story about vision.
- Handling of Libet-style backward referral, where a stimulus that takes a long time to reach consciousness is nevertheless experienced as earlier than it should be.
- Room for, or an argument against, a third option I have not seen named: that the sequence is registered twice, in a fast coarse path and a slow detailed path, with no further fact about which registration is *the* experience. Call it double-entry if you like. A theory should either locate it in the logical space or show it collapses into one of the other two.

Then the meta-questions, which are where I expect the real work:

1. Mark which desiderata are non-negotiable for this purpose and which are merely preferences of a camp — for instance, that the Orwellian/Stalinesque difference be empirically decidable, or that introspective reports of temporal order be treated as authoritative.
2. Tell me whether some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable; if so, that result matters more to me than the list itself.
3. Name the single requirement that excludes the most candidate theories, including the two classic ones.
4. Say whether a theory may legitimately answer that there is no fact of the matter about which option holds, and if it may, what it owes us in place of a verdict.

Add one predictive requirement. Suppose we run the two-flash color phi sequence and then, at a delay jittered between zero and 250 milliseconds after the second flash, present a random-dot mask over the whole motion path, asking on separate trials whether the dot was seen completing the path and what color it was at the midpoint. State, in advance, which pattern of results would support Orwellian revision, which would support Stalinesque delay, and which would support double-entry — and flag it plainly if no pattern of results could discriminate them, saying what would follow for the desiderata list if that is so.

Do not write a survey of the literature and do not give me a scorecard of named views; a sentence on where each of the two classic options fails is enough. Please answer in American English and keep it under 900 words.
