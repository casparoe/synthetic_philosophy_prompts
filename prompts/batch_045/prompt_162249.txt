I'm on the program committe of a small foundation that funds social-science infrastructure, and we've got an application nobody can agree on. A three-person team wants five years of money for a "macrohistory desk" that would track Turchin-style secular cycle indicators — elite overproduction, stagnant real wages, state fiscal strain — across about a dozen countries and publish an annual public instability-risk index with decade-ahead numbers, on the theory that a public risk estimate nudges elites to widen mobility and head the cycle off. Half of us thinks this is fortune-telling with a regression attached: a dozen countries at two or three cycles each gives you maybe thirty observations, they aren't independent trials in any case since these countries borrow institutions and grievances off each other, the target variable ("instability") is partly defined by the same theory that's supposed to predict it, and naming a country as instability-prone can help bring about what it names or give incumbents cover for repression. The applicants have pre-empted some of this: they've offered to pre-register the target variable and a scoring rule and be graded by a panel in year ten, they've floated publishing the forward-looking numbers only to a sealed archive at first, they cite one earlier forecast they say was borne out (we can't agree how much that shows), and they also argue the index is worth funding even if it forecasts badly, since a shared quantitative vocabulary is a coordination good for reformers. There's also a separable piece nobody objects to: coding the same indicators for historical cycles whose outcomes we already know. Draft our decision memo — fund, reject, or fund with conditions — with reasons the applicants could actually act on, and give the option you don't pick a fair hearing too.
