Hey — I'm in a tiny reading group on AI safety (three engineers and one econ PhD student) and we got stuck on the side-effects / low-impact material. The econ student keeps insisting that an impact penalty is basically just a Pigouvian tax: the agent's side effects are an externality it imposes on the rest of us, we don't need to write down anybody's full utility function to price it, we just put a price on the damage and let the optimizer internalize it — the same way we can put a price on carbon without first settling what a coastline is worth. He uses it to conclude that "avoid side effects" is definable without a full specification of value, and that the rest of the low-impact program is mostly the engineering problem of getting a decent price signal. I half beleive him but the analogy seems to be doing an awful lot of work.

Things that worry me: in the tax case the externality is marginal and the background stays in place, whereas what we're actually scared of is a capable optimizer reshaping the background itself; the tax setter stands outside the firm doing the polluting, while here the "price" has to be computed inside the agent's own objective, so it can be Goodharted or routed around (someone in the group muttered about a waterbed effect, where pressing impact down in one place makes it bulge somewhere else); and with a real tax there is at least in principle a government that can hand the revenue back to whoever was harmed, while a low-impact agent compensates nobody and there's no signal telling us whether the outcome was actually okay for anyone. My own half-baked fix, which I floated and got nowhere with: don't price the change, price the undoing. Score the agent on how costly it would be, in its own effort and resources, to restore the world to roughly the state it would have been in had the agent never acted — smash a vase and the score is enormous because restoration is impossible, move a chair and it's small because you can move it back. Call it a reversibility score. The econ student says that's just a tax with a weird numeraire and changes nothing; I think it changes something, because it never needs to know what's valuable, only what's repairable.
