And here is the answer I want graded, more or less verbatim, about 320 words:

Confirmation on the Bayesian picture is a matter of positive relevance: E confirms H just when P(H|E) > P(H), or equivalently when the Bayes factor P(E|H)/P(E|¬H) exceeds 1. Learning is modelled by conditionalization, so after you learn E your new credence in H is your old P(H|E). The problem is that if E is old evidence — already known, already built into the agent's credence function — then P(E) = 1, hence P(E|H) = P(E|¬H) = 1, so the Bayes factor collapses to 1 and conditionalizing on E is a no-op: P(H|E) = P(H). So the machinery has no room for the fact that the perihelion anomaly supported relativity, since nothing changes when you "learn" something you already knew. This is a genuine problem and not just a quibble about idealisation: whether the evidence arrives before or after somebody thinks up the theory is an accident of history, and it shouldn't decide whether confirmation occurred. The cleanest solution, I think, is to stop treating the evidence as certain. Reports of the perihelion anomaly came from fallible observation, so the agent should really have P(E) = 0.99 or so rather than 1; then the factor is something like 1/0.98, well above 1, and updating on E does push the probability of the theory up. So the old evidence problem is really a special case of the problem of uncertain evidence, which is exactly what Jeffrey conditionalization was designed for: once you update by Jeffrey's rule on a partition instead of by straight conditionalization, the difficulty disappears. The other well-known response — go back to the earlier prior you had before you learned E, sometimes called the ur-prior approach, and ask whether P(E|H) beat P(E|¬H) back then — reaches the same result by a longer route and is worse off, because it's ad hoc: it requires you to have kept a record of a credence function you have since abandoned, and it isn't part of the standard updating machinery. A third response, that what you really learn later is that the theory entails the evidence, is a variant of the same idea and does no extra work. Since all three fixes agree, choosing between them is merely a matter of convenience, which shows the problem was never very deep.
