Our campus free-speech committee settles basically every dispute with that old Popper line: a tolerant society has to be intolerant of intolerance, unlimited tolerance ends with the disappearance of tolerance. Last week it got used twice — once to justify disinviting an outside speaker, once to argue a student group shouldn't be allowed to register because its charter says it wants to replace pluralist democracy with something else.

I share the intuition but I dont think I know what the sentence is actually claiming. Can you lay it out as a menu of precise readings? For each: what tolerate has to mean (refraining from legal coercion? from withdrawing a platform or audience? from social disapproval? from judging the view false?), what counts as intolerance (merely holding illiberal views about how others should be treated vs refusing rational argument and answering with force vs credibly threatening to end tolerance once in power), whose tolerance is at issue (the state's, a university's, each individual citizen's), and whether the claim is a logical point, an empirical prediction, or just a permission rather than a duty.

Two things I care about most. First: my own hunch is that the engine here isn't a paradox at all but an asymmetry in credible threats — a side that has disavowed coercion can't deter one that hasn't, so tolerating an intolerant movement is structurally closer to an assurance or prisoner's-dilemma problem than to a contradiction. Is that a live reading of the slogan, and which version of it would it support? Second, Rawls seems to land somewhere else, tolerating the intolerant unless they become a real threat to liberty and its institutions — is that a disagreement about the empirical prediction, about the structure, or only about where the threshold sits?

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence: which readings, if any, survive and actually do the work in those two cases. Then the menu with verdicts — which readings are trivial, which are defensible, which are false or self-undermining. American English, no preamble, no restating my question.
