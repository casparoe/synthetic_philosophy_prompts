So I'm on the volunteer commitee for our town's founding-day thing and we've walked into a fight I didn't see coming. Our version — that the families who were pushed off the land held a gathering at the river every year and were told they could come back — has been told continuously since the 1890s, and it's basically the reason the day exists. Then someone dug up a government file, written in the 1950s by a clerk who wasn't there, saying no promise was ever made and the gatherings were something else entirely. At the meeting, when I said the file looked thin, somebody said "history is written by the victors" and that more or less ended the discussion in our favour. I don't love winning that way.

What I want is to know what that sentence is actually claiming, because everyone uses it and I don't think everyone means the same thing. Sometimes it seems to mean no more than "records are biased and incomplete", sometimes something way bigger like "there's no truth about the past at all, just whoever had the power to write it down". So: lay out the different precise claims it could be making, from boring to wild, and for each one say whether it's obviously true, whether it's defensible but arguable, or whether it's just false. And the part I actually care about — which of those readings, if any, would really do the work it was being used for at our meeting, i.e. support the idea that our story should get more weight than the file, or at least equal weight, even though it contradicts what's written down. One wrinkle that might matter: in our case the memory is older than the document, and the document was written by the side that won.

Sections, please, in this order: "The readings" (numbered list, boring first, wildest last, about 400 words), "Verdicts on each" (two or three lines per reading — true, defensible, or false, and why), "Which readings would actually do the work at our meeting" (~200 words), "What I should say next time" (~150 words), "Bottom line" (2–3 sentences). Plain language, please — I've got no academic background in any of this.
