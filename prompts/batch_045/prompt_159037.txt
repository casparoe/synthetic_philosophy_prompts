Me and a couple of people from my volunteer trail crew have this running argument and we keep dragging our friend Nadia into it. She got obsessed with the handicap principle a couple of years back (peacocks, gazelles bouncing up and down in front of a lion instead of running off, all that) and now she's the one we ask when we can't tell whether someone is for real. She has three words: costume, signal, and tell. A costume is something anyone can put on; a signal is costly and in principle fakeable, but faking costs you more if you don't have the goods; a tell is something that just leaks out and can't be faked, like the calluses on someone's hands or the way the genuinely good climbers round here show up in ancient beat-up gear while the guys who've been climbing six months all have matching new kits. She files that last one under tell, not signal. Her call sheet so far, which nobody has argued with much: peacock's tail — signal, honest. Gazelle stotting — signal, honest. A bank's TV campaign about how much it cares — costume. Somebody's Instagram bio that just says "be kind" — costume. A guy who quietly paid off a pile of school lunch debts and asked for his name to be left off — not signalling at all, just a nice thing. Our crewmate Marek, who turns down the paid contract work the rest of us take because he thinks the contractor is wrecking the trails — signal, honest. The guy who bought a six-thousand-dollar mountain bike to show he's serious about the sport — costume, and this is the one I keep coming back to, because her reason was that he'd have bought the bike anyway, he rides it constantly, and he could sell it for most of what he paid.

Here's the case we can't settle. A guy joined us about a year ago, and about six months in he bought the crew roughly four grand of new gear — ropes, harnesses, a better chainsaw — out of his own pocket. He's not loaded; it was a real chunk of money for him, though he's better off than most of us. He can't get it back, it's ours now, and if he quit tomorrow it stays in the shed. He didn't make a speech about it, but obviously everyone worked out it was him. Last month he asked to be made a team lead, which is the thing people actually want around here, and half the crew now says he bought his way into it. (For what its worth, somebody else in the crew did more or less the same thing four years ago, anonymously, never asked for anything, and Nadia has always filed that one under just being generous.)

The reason I can't work out what she'll say is that she's said two things out loud that pull in different directions on this one. One: it's not how much something costs, it's whether it costs him less than it would cost somebody who was faking it. Two: the good signals are the ones where the money just disappears, and you should be suspicious of any signal where the money lands in your lap. The gear money didn't disappear — it turned into our ropes, and we're the people he's trying to impress — but it is gone as far as he's concerned, and the bike guy could get his money back while this guy can't.

So, in plain language please (I'm not an academic, and I don't want book recommendations or paper names, just the reasoning): what do you think she'll say? I'd like (1) your best guess at her verdict — which of her three boxes it goes in, and what trait it would be honest about, if any, since I genuinely can't tell whether he's signalling that he's committed, that he's got money, or just that he wants the lead job; (2) the two or three rules somebody could be running that would reproduce her whole call sheet, what each of them predicts here, and which of her past rulings each one sits least comfortably with; (3) which precedent is the closest analog to this case and whether the analogy actually holds or breaks on the details; (4) the single tweak to this case — one variant, whatever it is — that would make the rival rules disagree as sharply as possible, so I know what to ask her next time we're all stood around in the rain; and (5) whether costume/signal/tell even has the right box for this kind of behaviour, or whether it's something else entirely, because one of the crew thinks the honest word for it is bribe.

A few hundred words of prose is plenty, but after it could you give me a JSON object with exactly these fields: "rules" (a list of objects, each with "name", "one_line" — the rule in one sentence — "verdict_here" — costume, signal, tell, or not-signalling, and say what trait it's honest about — and "worst_fitting_precedent" — which of her earlier calls the rule explains least well); "predicted_verdict" (an object with "box", "trait", and "confidence" as high, medium, or low); "closest_precedent" (a string naming which earlier ruling is the nearest analog and whether it holds); "best_guess_and_why" (a short string); "decisive_variant" (an object with "variant" describing the tweak, then "rule_1_says", "rule_2_says", "rule_3_says" for the rules you listed); and "missing_category" (a string saying whether her three-way taxonomy needs a fourth box here, and what you'd call it).
