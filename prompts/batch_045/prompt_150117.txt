(1) Some moral truths are necessary. There is no possible world in which torturing a young child for amusement is morally permissible.

(2) God is essentially morally perfect. A being who could command gratuitous cruelty simply would not be God; the term picks out a necessarily morally perfect being, not a being who happens to be good.

(3) Obligation is constituted by divine command. For an act to be obligatory just is for it to be commanded by God. Commands are not merely excellent evidence of what is obligatory; they are what obligation consists in.

(5) Moral knowledge is available without theology. An atheist, or someone who has never encountered the concept of God, can know that torturing a child for amusement is wrong, by ordinary reflection and conscience.

(7) Case verdict: the abhorrent command. If I received an apparently overwhelming revelation instructing me to kill my own child, the right response would be to refuse. I would be doing something wrong if I complied, and I could know that at the time, not merely in hindsight.
