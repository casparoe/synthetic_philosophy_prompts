I work in Bayesian statistics and machine learning. The vocabulary I actually use is priors and posteriors, marginal likelihood and Bayes factors, hierarchical and multilevel models, nuisance parameters and weak identification, prior and posterior predictive checks, sequential (prequential) predictive scoring, cross-validation and out-of-sample log predictive density, model expansion and the bias–variance/overfitting trade-off, Cromwell's rule and the hazards of dogmatic priors. I use all of this daily and am fluent in it. My knowledge of post-Popperian philosophy of science is second-hand and thin.

I would like you to translate Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes into that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report on the translation.

The machinery to be translated is, as I understand it: a research programme consists of an irrevocable hard core of central claims, protected by a belt of auxiliary hypotheses, plus two heuristics — a negative heuristic that instructs practitioners not to direct refutations at the hard core but to shunt them outward into the belt, and a positive heuristic that lays out a partly articulated plan for building successive, increasingly complicated models without being derailed by anomalies. A problem shift is progressive when the successive models anticipate novel facts and increase the programme's corroborated empirical content, degenerating when the belt is adjusted only after the fact, merely to accommodate data already in hand. Appraisal is retrospective and temporally extended: there is no instant rationality, no rule that settles at a given moment how much punishment a programme may absorb or when hope becomes irrational. Finally, Lakatos distinguishes this rational reconstruction from any account of what individual scientists actually believed, and, if I have this right, he extends the same machinery to appraise methodologies themselves. Where my paraphrase is wrong, say so briefly and then translate the corrected version.

Two cases to force the translation to do work rather than paraphrase. First: a programme whose hard core is a purely structural invariance constraint, entailing no observable distribution on its own, so that every empirical consequence is generated by belt hypotheses about the dynamics. Each belt adjustment is fitted after an anomaly and each raises the marginal likelihood on all data available at that time; but scored prequentially across the programme's whole life, its one-step-ahead predictive densities never beat a two-parameter baseline fitted once, years earlier. In my vocabulary the core looks non-identified: no datum ever moves the posterior over cores. So what is the core doing, and does the core/belt distinction survive the translation at all? Second: a programme correctly predicts a striking quantitative fact in advance, using a specific belt hypothesis that is later discarded for unrelated reasons; the replacement belt hypothesis reproduces the same number by a different route. My vocabulary, as I understand it, registers only whether the datum lies in the support of the posterior predictive distribution, and is indifferent to when it was predicted and by which route. Does the specifically Lakatosian content here — anticipation, novelty, the temporal and counterfactual element — survive being rendered as a fact about a joint distribution over data and parameters?

Then the residue report, in three columns. Which Lakatosian claims translate cleanly into my vocabulary, which translate only after you have quietly added something Lakatos does not supply — a prior over programmes, a scoring rule, a decision threshold — and which have no expression in it at all. Give particular attention to three things I suspect are trouble: the negative heuristic's instruction to withhold updating on anomalies, given that coherence forbids me from choosing not to update; the fact that a research programme is not a believer, so that "the programme is degenerating" has no obvious bearer, while Lakatos also refuses to reduce it to what any individual scientist believed; and the self-application of the machinery to methodologies themselves, which in my vocabulary would have to become a hierarchical model over methodologies and strikes me as either trivial or incoherent.

Close by telling me whether the residue indicts my framework or Lakatos's. Is what will not translate something my vocabulary is structurally blind to, a genuinely normative-historical notion that Bayesian language has no room for? Or is it a sign that talk of hard cores, heuristics, and problem shifts is doing less clear work than it appears, and that a good deal of it was always a suggestive redescription of model selection under misspecification? I want the honest version, not a diplomatic one, and I want any assumption you import on my behalf flagged explicitly rather than presented as translation.
