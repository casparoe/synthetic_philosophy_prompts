As he told it: Judy is parachuted into a zone that is half friendly (Blue) and half enemy (Red) territory, each half split evenly between Headquarters Company and Second Company, so her prior is one quarter on each of the four combinations; a radio message tells her that if she is in Red territory the odds are three to one that she is in Second Company. The interest of the example, he said, is that her evidence is irreducibly conditional — it cannot be represented as her becoming certain of some proposition, nor even by Jeffrey's generalization of conditionalization to uncertain evidence, because what she has learned is not a proposition whose probability gets fixed but a constraint on a conditional probability. The standard fix, he said, is relative-information minimization: among all distributions satisfying the new constraint that the probability of Second Company given Red is three quarters, adopt the one closest to her prior in Kullback–Leibler divergence. And because the constraint is entirely about what happens inside Red territory, he went on, this keeps the probability of Red at one half and leaves both Blue cells at a quarter, merely reshuffling the Red cells to three eighths and one eighth — from which he concluded that her unconditional confidence in Second Company should not move at all, and that the example is now really just a worked illustration with no live controversy left in it.
