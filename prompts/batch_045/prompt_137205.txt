Dennett's central idea is that the self is like a center of gravity. Instead, it is an abstract entity that we posit when we adopt the intentional stance toward a person and weave their actions and experiences into a story. Dennett's view is that the self is a kind of useful fiction: it does not exist as a concrete object, but it is a real pattern that we can talk about and use to make predictions.

However, because the self is a fiction, it follows that the self is merely an illusion and does not really exist at all.
