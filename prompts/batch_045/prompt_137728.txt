I'm writing a short piece on whether jumping out of the system gives you any real argument against mechanism about minds, and I want a fresh case to put in front of readers rather than the MIU one again (you know the one: start from MI, rules along the lines of "if a string ends in I you may append U", "you may double whatever follows the M", "III may become U", "UU may be deleted", and MU turns out to be unreachable, which you can only see by stopping play and noticing something about the number of I's mod 3).

So: invent me a new puzzle system of that general sort. Your own alphabet, one axiom string, three or four rules, the whole thing statable in about four lines. It has to meet these:

- The rules should look innocent, like something out of a puzzle book. Nothing that's secretly a Turing machine or a disguised halting problem — if the hardness is really coming from hidden computational universality, the point of the example is lost.
- There's a target string that is genuinely not derivable, and the argument for that needs a real jump-out insight with a bit more texture than "count one letter mod n": I want two things interacting (two quantities, or one quantity plus a structural fact about how the rules can and can't be composed), and I want you to actually give the argument, not gesture at it.
- In the same system there's a second target that IS derivable, but only by a derivation that blows the string up to something absurd in the middle and then shrinks it back, so that a program doing an exaustive grind through the rules is in exactly the same position with both targets: it never arrives and never knows why. That's the bit I care most about, so don't fudge it — say how you've rigged things so there's no shortcut.

Then use the pair to argue it both ways. One way: the machine stuck inside the rules can do nothing, the insight comes from treating the rules as an object, no amount of faster grinding gets you there, so mechanism is in trouble. Other way: jumping out is just relocating to a slightly bigger formal system, which is itself mechanical, so we've learned nothing about minds. Say honestly which feature of your case is doing the work in whichever direction you land, and then give the losing side the best thing it can say to defuse the case.
