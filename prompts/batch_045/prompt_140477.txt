Write a piece of sustained satire in the form of a public-consultation document: a paper issued by an invented regulator, the Interdepartmental Working Group on Graded Minds, proposing to bring animal-welfare law up to date by classifying every research subject according to its grade of mind and tailoring the protections owed to it accordingly.

The taxonomy the document works with should be stated in the document's own voice, as part of its glossary or definitions clause, not explained to the reader. Grade 1, Darwinian creatures, come equipped with hard-wired behavioural routines selected over evolutionary time; they do not learn from their own experience, and a routine can be exploited indefinitely by anyone patient enough to reset the conditions that trigger it. Grade 2, Skinnerian creatures, learn by operant conditioning: they must actually perform the action and actually undergo the consequence, and no warning, description, model, or rehearsal can stand in for the outcome. Grade 3, Popperian creatures, pre-screen candidate actions against an internal model, so that bad hypotheses can die in their stead instead of their dying for their hypotheses — though this only helps where the model is rich enough for the modelled outcome to track the real one, and is worse than useless where it is not. Grade 4, Gregorian creatures, inherit cognitive tools — words, notations, diagrams, and other representational artefacts — that restructure the very space in which grades 2 and 3 do their work, so that what an individual can learn, and even what selection can act on, depends on equipment it neither invented nor possesses on its own; its competence is a property of a niche rather than of a skull.

The instrument the paper proposes is the Popperian Floor: no subject of any protocol with an aversive endpoint may be below grade 3, on the ground that the central refinement available to welfare law — replace the aversive outcome with a modelled one — is simply empty for any creature that must undergo the outcome in order to learn anything from it. The working group must then work out, in complete earnestness and without a single ironic aside, what this commits it to. Among the commitments it should discover for itself: that welfare law now has to mandate cognitive uplift as a welfare measure; that uplift is, on the group's own account, partly cultural and self-propagating, so that each successful compliance cycle raises the grade of a population and thereby the obligations owed to it in the next review; that a grade-4 subject's suffering is extended by anticipation and by the artefacts that partly constitute its mind, while a lone grade-4 individual separated from those artefacts cannot be graded at all; that the cheapest route to compliance is to keep subjects down a grade rather than raise them up; and that the group must rule on a contested case in which a species pre-screens successfully only when tested alongside an artefact of its own making, so that it cannot be said whether the grade resides in the animal or in its equipment.

Requirements:

Form and length. A consultation paper of roughly 750 to 900 words: a short preamble stating the mischief the instrument is meant to remedy, a numbered set of recommendations with sub-clauses (include at least a definitions clause, a transitional-provisions clause, and an exemptions clause), one short grade-by-grade table of compliance costs, and a closing clause that states the final consequence flatly.

Where the comedy lives. Every joke should come from applying the taxonomy correctly — from what each grade can and cannot do, and from the way the welfare principle misfires on it — rather than from invented capacities, mad-scientist machinery, or jokes bolted on from outside. A reader who knows the fourfold classification should be able to tell, without being told, that the author has it right; a reader who does not should be left wondering uneasily whether the proposal might actually be sound.

Voices. Include at least two remarks attributed to named fictional officials or consultees — for instance the group's adviser on innate routines, and the representative of a facility that breeds grade-2 subjects — each remarking in character, in defence of their own interests, and each slightly missing the point.

Tone. Deadpan throughout. The document must not wink, and must not include any note, preface, or postscript explaining that it is satire; the reductio is something the committee itself fails to notice.
