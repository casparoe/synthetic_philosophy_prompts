so i've been reading about Jain anekāntavāda — the non-one-sidedness / many-sidedness thing — and the sevenfold syādvāda scheme that goes with it (you stick syād, roughly "in some respect", in front of a claim, and you get seven combinations: it is, it is not, it is and is not, the "can't be said" one, and three that mix the can't-be-said one back in). I've started using it as an argument-settling tool and I want to know what principle I'm actually applying, because I don't think my gut verdicts are consistent. Here's where I land on when "you're both right from your own angle" is legit and when someone is just plain wrong:

1. Blind men and the elephant (rope, wall, tree, fan) — legit. Each is picking out a real part.
2. "The train is moving" vs "no, it's at rest" — one person on the platform, one in the dining car — legit, both fine.
3. Smashed pot: "the pot is still here" (it's the same clay) vs "the pot is gone" (they're shards now) — legit, and this one feels like the deepest case on my list.
4. Two historians on whether a particular post-war reparations settlement was harsh — legit; harsh in some respects, mild in others.
5. Flat earth — not legit. One side is just wrong.
6. My uncle on the moon landing — not legit. Just wrong.
7. Patient says "I'm in agony", GP says "there's nothing wrong with you" — legit, both can be true, and I feel strongly that this is a different thing from #6 even though the surface shape is identical (A asserts P about subject S, B asserts not-P about the same S).
8. Insurance adjuster: "the car is worth nothing, it's a write-off" vs owner: "it's worth nine grand to me" — legit, both fine, even though the adjuster has all the receipts. This is the pair that keeps me up at night: same shape as #6, opposite verdict.
9. Two physicists, wave vs particle, both about the same stuff — legit, and I think the "can't be said" mode is doing the work here: you can't assert both in one breath.
10. Homeopath: "the water remembers the substance"; chemist: "no it doesn't" — not legit, one side is just wrong. Even though it's structurally just like #9.

My tentative principle, which I'll call the syād-test: a dispute is genuinely many-sided iff (a) each side is picking out an aspect the subject really has, not one they're projecting onto it; (b) the two claims are about respects, times or standpoints that don't cancel each other out; and (c) neither side is claiming its own angle is the whole story — the thing Jainism is actually attacking is ekānta, one-sided absolutism. I'm also reading the fourth mode as "this combination is nonsense", i.e. the conjunction is meaningless.

So: does that principle fit my ten, and which verdicts does it force me to drop? Is there a version that saves all ten and is it worth the complexity, or should I defineately give one up?
