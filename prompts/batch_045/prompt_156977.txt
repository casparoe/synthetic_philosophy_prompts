My flatmate has extremely firm views about individual contributions to collective harms, and I've been trying to work out what rule she's actually running, because I think her own view is about to back her into something she'll hate.

Her verdicts so far, best I can reconstruct them:

- Giving 10% of her income to effective charities: obviously required, one of the best things a person can do, no debate.
- Buying factory-farmed chicken: wrong, and she won't do it even after I pointed out that supermarkets reorder in batches, so her single pack is almost certainly inside the margin and changes nothing at the farm. Her line was that she's still the one handing over money for the cage.
- Eating meat somebody else already bought and cooked, i.e. leftover barbecue: totally fine, "the harm's done and me not eating it helps nobody."
- Flying long-haul for a holiday: fine, she does it, she doesn't offset, and she says one passenger is a rounding error against total aviation emissions. She'll admit its not great, in the way you'd admit anything is not great.
- Buying a fast-fashion hoodie sewn in a factory with genuinely awful conditions: fine, and she thinks boycotts tend to leave the workers worse off.
- Voting in a general election from a safe seat her party always wins: she votes, calls it a duty, and has genuinely done the calculation where you multiply a tiny chance of being pivotal by large stakes.
- Signing petitions, sharing infographics: theatre, waste of time.

I can't tell whether she's running (a) a strict expected-difference rule — what would have happened if I hadn't done it, with the threshold cases handled by multiplying the chance of being the tipping purchase by the size of the batch, which supposedly lands you near your per-person share anyway — or (b) some kind of membership/complicity rule where what matters is that you're paying into a practice that's wrong regardless of whether you tipped anything. Her leftovers verdict and her hoodie verdict make me think (b) has to be narrowed to cases where money crosses hands, but the flying verdict pushes the other way: under (a) the flight looks at least as bad as the chicken, since the extra fuel really is causally downstream of her seat in a way the bird only is via some threshold story, so a consistent (a)-person should be more bothered by the flight than by the pack of chicken, and she isn't. Or maybe she's just inconsistent and I should drop it.

Here's the case I actually care about. She and her partner are talking about having a kid, and I pointed out that a child in a rich country sits upstream of an enormous amount of emissions — tens of tonnes a year for decades — and that unlike the flight or the chicken, essentially none of it is inside a margin: there's no batch reordering, no threshold, no "the plane was going anyway." The inefficacy move she leans on everywhere else just doesn't apply here. She shrugged and said the emissions would be her kid's, not hers, and that she isn't causing anything, just starting a person. I can't tell whether that's a real answer or a dodge.
