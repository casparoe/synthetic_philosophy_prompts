Me and two friends have been going in circles on the hitchhiker case for about a year now: stranded in the desert, a driver who'll take you to town only if she's certain you'll hand over the money on arrival, she is never wrong, you'll never meet again, no reputational spillover. One friend says once you're in town the ride is over either way, so paying is just burning a thousand dollars. The other says that's precisely the reasoning that leaves you in the desert, and the agents who do better across these cases are the payers. Then a third move keeps getting wheeled out: that the real question is what kind of person to be, and if becoming that person takes a bit of irrationality — swallowing a belief you think is false, or acquiring a sentiment you can't switch on at will — then so much the worse for rationality. Nobody has budged an inch.

The thing that made me suspect we're talking past each other is a variant I made up: suppose the driver judges not what you'd do but why you'd do it. She picks up only people who'd pay because they think they owe it, and she sees straight through anyone who'd pay merely because they pre-committed or took a pill that made them reliable. Now the "make yourself the right kind of person" move seems to collapse, the first friend says the case is rigged, and the second starts talking about what we should want to be rather than what we should do. So: how many disagreements are actually in here? Is it verbal, a fight about what "rational" and "reason" mean? Is it several different questions under one heading — rating acts, rating policies, rating characters? Is some hidden formal question doing the work, like what it is for a decision rule to succeed? And is "be the sort of agent who X" even a coherent thing to recommend to someone who can't adopt it by deciding? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of the positions, and an honest answer about what, if anything, would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, a change of question, or nothing at all.
