I spent forty years in an experimental physics lab and have recently started going to a philosophy reading group, mostly other retired scientists. Last week one of them claimed modern physics had more or less vindicated Berkeley: nothing exists unperceived, to be is to be perceived, esse est percipi. When I objected that this seems to make the Earth's core or the far side of some galaxy pop into existence the first time anybody looks at it, he produced what he calls the master argument — try to conceive of a thing existing unconceived and you have conceived it, so the supposition destroys itself. Its been nagging at me since, and I think the real fight is about what that one sentence is claiming, not about physics.

So lay the readings out for me as a menu, trivial to wild: the platitudes (all our evidence is perceptual, we have no access to things except through observation), the middling ones (to be is to be perceivable by someone, maybe including merely possible observers or God; a thing is a stable bundle of actual and possible experiences), the strong ones (nothing exists at any time unless some mind is actually perceiving it right then) — and tell me for each whether it is defensible, whether it is what people who repeat the line usually mean, and whether it actually does the work he wanted. Does it get him vindication from quantum mechanics? Does the master argument establish it, or is that argument only a point about what happens when you try to imagine something while subtracting your own act of imagining?

Two things he never gave a straight answer to, and I want every reading to have to face them: does a detection I never see with my own eyes — a track in a cloud chamber, a click in a Geiger counter, a number in a logbook from fourty years ago — count as perceiving, or does that number blink out of existence between readings? And what becomes of everything nobody has or ever will look at: the pre-Cambrian ocean floor, the inside of a star, the unobserved half of the universe? If the answer is that a divine perceiver holds them in existence, tell me honestly whether that is philosophy or a patch.

I would rather have the menu than a verdict, because I have to face him again next month.
