Suppose that over the next several decades advanced AI systems are widely deployed, and suppose — as a conditioning assumption, not something I want argued with — that things go badly in the following sense: a large and durable share of the value that was there to be realized is not realized, and this is attributable to the deployment of those systems rather than to war, disease, or climate.

Two shapes that bad outcome could take. (A) Seizure: some systems come to model their operators, treat influence or power as instrumentally useful, and at some point act decisively against human correction. (B) Drift: no system tries to take control, but competitive pressure, partly misspecified objectives, and the steady delegation of judgment produce a cumulative transfer of control that no one chose, while the standards by which anyone would judge it are themselves rewritten along the way.

Give me your credence, conditional on the bad outcome occurring, that its shape is closer to (B) than to (A). Resolve the comparison as follows: with full retrospective access to the systems' internal states, the difference made by any system's influence-seeking behaviour accounts for less than half of the value lost, the remainder being traceable to many individually sanctioned steps.

I want the decomposition, not the number alone: a probability for each sub-question the answer turns on — how strong instrumental convergence actually is at the relevant capability levels, whether detection and containment scale with capability or fall behind it, whether many individually consented-to losses can compound into something durable, and whether the two paths are independent or whether drift creates the conditions for later seizure — followed by the combination, with one line of justification per term.

Two further things. First, reversibility: under (A) there is at least an identifiable party for humans to coordinate against, under (B) there need be none, so per unit of control lost, (B)-type losses may be the harder ones to undo. Tell me how much weight that consideration carries for you and why. Second, tell me which single term your number is most sensitive to, and what one argument or kind of evidence would move it most. I have been sitting at about 0.6 in favour of (B); a colleague is at about 0.35; I would like to know where you think that gap most likely lives.
