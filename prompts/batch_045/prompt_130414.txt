Hi — I'm the program officer at the Marlow Fund, a small family foundation. We give away about $5m a year, two program staff (me and one other person), board meets quarterly but we make grant decisions monthly, ~25 grants a year and half of them are renewals. Here's our problem in two parts.

First: about $1.5m of the annual payout is locked by the donor's will to "work that benefits people who have not been born yet," and the rest is basically unrestricted near-term global health and development. So in the same meeting we score, I dunno, a school-based delivery programme that's been running for six years and a small speculative biosecurity team, and it obviously doesn't work — the units aren't the same, and "do nothing and move the money across" isn't even available in the locked bucket, because the money has to go out the door into that bucket somehow.

Second: right now we rate every proposal High/Medium/Low on importance, tractability and neglectedness (the three-factor framework you've probably seen floating around the effective-altruism world), turn those into 3/2/1, and multiply. It's silly and we know it. A Medium/Low/High beats a High/High/Low and nobody actually believes that. "Neglectedness" keeps getting scored as "nobody cool is talking about this" rather than as anything to do with money. And our board chair is a retired accountant who says he doesn't want to see any figure with more than one significant digit, while another trustee thinks any cause you can't describe to her mother over dinner is a scam.

So: give us something we can actually run. Constraints — maybe two hours per proposal, one round of follow-up questions to the applicant, no literature reviews, no outside modelling contractor, and it has to fit on the one-page memo that goes to the board.

Specifically I want:

- The order we should establish things in, and what counts as an adequate answer at each step. I suspect we should be checking who else is already funding the space *before* we try to estimate how much good a dollar does, and that tractability should be judged at the margin rather than on average — but you tell me if that's right.
- How the three factors actually interact, and what to do instead of multiplying, given they clearly aren't independent.
- Where the procedure stops with a no, and where it has to stop and hand the decision to a person because the question is one of values and not facts.
- What we do when we can't get a number at all, which is most of the time.
- How to write the result up so it survives contact with the board: one significant digit, and explainable to someone's mother.
- Honesty about which steps are judgement dressed up as procedure. I'd rather know now than find out in year three.

Then run it on three real-ish cases, and at least one should come out in a way I won't like:

(a) a renewal for a well-run school-based mass deworming / delivery programme that everyone loves, that on the evidence as it stands looks fine, and that at least three bigger funders are already in;
(b) a cheap speculative research proposal from a tiny team with no track record, testing a mechanism nobody has tested, where the interesting part is the tail outcome and the chance we learn something;
(c) a community health worker training programme whose numbers look great but which can only roughly double in size per year because of trainer supply.

Also tell us what the procedure can't do — where it will definately misfire — and don't soften it. We keep tying ourselves in knots in meetings and I'd rather hand the board something with its limits printed on it.
