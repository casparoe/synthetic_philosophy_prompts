hi — I need a memo I can circulate to the rest of the group before Thursday, and I'd write it myself except I keep going back and forth on it.

I'm on the Signal Response Group for the consortium that runs our array. Quick version of the case: we have one unconfirmed candidate technosignature. Narrowband, drifting, repeating, from a star roughly forty light years off. The character of it looks more like spillover from something they're using locally than a beam pointed at us — which matters, because it means we'd be starting the conversation rather than answering one. It stopped after about nine hours and hasn't come back in seven weeks.

The proposal in front of us, from two of our members, is a staged reply instead of a straight reply-or-don't: stage one is a short transmission that only establishes artificial origin — nothing about us, no biology, nothing about where we are beyond the direction it goes in — and then we stop and listen, and only send anything with real content if we recieve something back. Their pitch is that the usual objection to transmitting is that it's a single irreversible all-or-nothing act taken by a handful of people on behalf of eight billion who never got a vote, and a ladder turns it into a sequence where each step is conditional on what we learn at the previous one.

What's making my head spin is this: our silence-followers (you know the argument — nobody is shouting, so either there's nobody out there or shouting is the thing that gets you killed), against a point one of our postdocs made last meeting that I haven't been able to shake. The bad outcome is the one we'd never get to observe. If a reply is lethal, nobody is left to update on it, and there aren't any other civilisations' remains we can go and inspect, so "we've never seen a contact go badly" isn't evidence in the way it looks like evidence — and it means the experiment can't be self-correcting on any timescale shorter than a civilisation's.

Two more complications. The array's transmitter is being decommissioned next year, so "wait twenty years and decide with better information" isn't a free option — it costs us the only hardware that can do this at the right frequency. And separately we have to decide whether to go public now or sit on it until we re-detect.

So: draft me the group's decision memo. I need a bottom line the board could actually vote on — send the first rung, send nothing and keep listening, or punt to the international consultation process but with a hard deadline — with reasons, a time frame, and a fair hearing for whichever position loses. One of our number will read it looking for reasons to say no, so don't strawman the waiting case; give the strongest version of it and then say why we should overrule it anyway, or don't. British English please, and no citations or further reading — I can't follow any of that up from here.
