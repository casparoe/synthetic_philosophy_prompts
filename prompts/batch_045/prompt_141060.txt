I'm assembling a set of mock institutional documents that satirize styles of scientific explanation, and I want one that a philosopher of science or a theoretically minded biologist would judge fair rather than cheap. Please write it.

Write a parody of a journal's Instructions for Authors, roughly 800 words, issued by a fictional outlet of your invention (call it, say, the Journal of Grounded Explanation) and signed by its Committee on Explanatory Grounding. The committee is formidably consistent, humorless, and entirely sincere. The piece should be readable by someone who has never encountered the crane/skyhook vocabulary, but someone who has should wince in recognition.

Requirements for the content:

Get the vocabulary right in the committee's own voice. A crane is an explanatory mechanism that does real work and is itself assembled out of machinery one level down; a skyhook borrows its lifting power from above and stops the descent; greedy reductionism is the mistake of skipping levels, of hanging a high-level phenomenon directly off the lowest available one and thereby losing the pattern that was to be explained. The committee should present itself purely as an anti-skyhook watchdog, and the disaster should be that its own procedures, applied without exception, are greedy reductionism. Nothing about the committee should be stupid or strawmanned: the catastrophe follows from its consistency.

Every submission must file a Grounding Chain: each explainer must be shown to rest on explainers beneath it; no explainer may be credited with a capacity of the same kind as the capacity it is invoked to explain; and emergence, self-organization, representation, norm, and the agent may not appear as terminal nodes.

Invent at least two pieces of institutional apparatus and use them in the text. Suggestions, if useful: a boom-crane clause, covering mechanisms described in engineering language that are nevertheless lowered from above; a disclosure form for unexplained explainers; a certificate of provisionally grounded status granted when a derivation from bedrock exceeds the page limit; a registry of approved bedrock levels.

Include three worked abstracts, each shown before and after compliance, drawn from three different fields — suggest animal navigation, human concept learning, and speculative bubbles. Each compliant version should satisfy every rule while having quietly lost the thing it was written to explain.

Close with the committee's own grounding chain bottoming out: whatever rule it adopts for when descent may stop is, by its own definition, a skyhook. The committee must not notice this.

Stay deadpan throughout. No puns, no winking, no admissions that any of this is absurd, and no named real person as a target. The comedy should come entirely from the methodology being right about skyhooks and wrong about where to stop.

Begin with the document itself. No preface, no framing, and no commentary after it.
