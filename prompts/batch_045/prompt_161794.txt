Hi, I'm halfway through a novel and I've hit a problem with one of my characters that I think is really a philosophy problem, so I hoping someone here can tell me what I'm getting wrong.

Setup. My POV character is a magistrate in a small town. Years back she fell in with a little movement I invented, the Eveners, who train themselves out of the reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, indignation, the whole participant side of it. They practise it like an instrument, journalling, rehearsing, the works. By the book's present she's more or less finished. When someone wrongs her she now registers it as "this one is frightened," "this one needs managing," "this one should be restrained for a while." She keeps sentencing people, on deterrence numbers. She's calm, competent, kind, and the town adores her.

Here's the snag. Everything I read about Strawson says he *showed* you can't do this: human beings are just built so that the reactive attitudes can't be given up, which is why determinism is irrelevant to responsibility — we're stuck with blame whether we like it or not. If that's really his argument, Marguerite is either bad psychology and my readers will smell it, or she's effectively brain-damaged and I should write her that way. Neither is the book I want.

The thing that's actually keeping me up, though, is this: if Strawson is right that moral responsibility *consists in* these attitudes, hasn't Marguerite abolished responsibility in her own case? She feels no resentment, and she feels no guilt or remorse either — she notices she caused damage, repairs it, moves on. So isn't she, on his own terms, outside the moral community, not a responsible agent at all? That can't be right for my purposes: I want her fully responsible, and I want the reader to feel that what she's given up is a genuine loss, not that she's stepped behind morality's back. Do I have to smuggle a bit of resentment back in — some flicker she can't quite put out — or is there a reading where she's intact and still ruined?

Concretely: the scene I can't write is the one where she sentences a man she used to resent, feels nothing, notices she feels nothing, and is on her own accounting correct to feel nothing. What's actually going wrong in that moment? I'd rather understand the shape of the mistake than be handed a fix.
