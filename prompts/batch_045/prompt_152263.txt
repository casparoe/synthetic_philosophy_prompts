i'm in a reading group on philosophy of physics and we did the hole argument this week and i don't think ive recovered. here's the version i walked away with: take a solution of the Einstein field equations -- a manifold of point-events dressed with a metric and whatever matter fields you like. now apply a smooth map that's the identity outside some region (the "hole") but shuffles things around inside it. because the theory is diffeomorphism invariant, what you get is another perfectly good solution, one that agrees with the first on everything outside the hole and disagrees with it inside. so if you think two distinct models are two distinct possible worlds -- which you probably do if you think spacetime points are real items with their own identity, over and above what occupies them -- then the entire physical state of the universe outside the hole, at every time, still doesn't fix what happens in the hole. radical indeterminism. the usual moral people draw is that the two models aren't two worlds at all but one world described two ways, i.e. spacetime points don't have primitive identity.

one guy in the group, a physics grad student, v confident, said the whole thing turns on a sloppy analogy and then offered his own. spacetime, he says, is like a computer's memory: a bank of addressed slots that exist and keep their identity whether or not anything is stored in them. a hole diffeomorphism is like taking one block of memory and sliding every value along by one address, leaving the rest untouched -- and suppose, he adds, that the machine's update rule genuinely doesn't care where in memory things sit. of course that's a real difference in the machine's state, he says: different cells now hold different charges, even though no program that only ever compares relative structure could tell you which state you're in. so, he concludes, the hole argument doesn't show spacetime points aren't real; it shows GR is indeterministic in the hole, full stop, and the fashion for declaring the two models to be the same physical situation is metaphysical bookkeeping whose only motivation is saving determinism.

i couldn't put my finger on what's wrong with it and i also couldn't shake it. can you audit the memory analogy for me: give me the correspondence -- slots, contents, the shift, the machine's update rule, someone reading the memory, the fact that the machine sits in a lab -- then tell me where the mapping actually breaks and which features of a computer's memory it quietly imports into spacetime that spacetime may not have. separate the disanalogies that matter for the use he's making of it from the ones that don't. then: does his conclusion survive? is "the hole is genuinely indeterminate, stop pretending otherwise" a position someone can actually hold, or was the analogy carrying the whole thing? and if the memory picture is the wrong one, what's a better thing to compare the manifold to? (he also kept saying diffeomorphisms are just changes of coordinates, like switching from metres to feet, which i suspect is doing its own separate work -- flag it if that matters.)
