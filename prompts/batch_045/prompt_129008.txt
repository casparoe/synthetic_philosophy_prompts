I spent thirty-odd years as an atmospheric physicist, and since retiring I have been sitting in on a fortnightly philosophy reading group run through the university's continuing-education programme. I am the oldest person in the room by two decades and the only one who keeps trying to turn the discussion into a measurement problem. Over the past few weeks the group has been working through the contrast between reasons that are relative to an agent and reasons that are not, and I have become preoccupied by something the discussion slides over, which I cannot name and cannot tell whether anyone has named.

The thing I keep circling is best introduced with two cases. First, a logbook from my old research group, winter of 1971, records a suspicion that a calibration standard had drifted; the note is addressed to nobody in particular and says, roughly, that there is reason for somebody to re-run the comparison before the next measurement campaign. There was. Nobody did. Everyone who could have acted on it has since retired or died, and the instrument itself was scrapped in 1989. My instinct as a physicist is that the reason has not thereby become false: like a field that has a value at a point where no particle happens to be sitting, it was defined at a place where no agent happened to be standing, and it still is. But my instinct as a novice in this subject is that a reason is the wrong sort of thing to outlive its last possible audience, and that whatever is left over is a fact about how things would have been better, not a reason. Yet that does not seem right either: what was written in the logbook was not a remark about the value of a state of affairs, it was directed at whoever turned up, and it would have licensed criticism of that person in a way that mere badness does not. Second case: a friend of mine with moderately advanced dementia still, I want to say, has reason to keep taking a particular medication, and his physician is criticisable if she lets him stop; but he is no longer capable of grasping the consideration, weighing it, or acting for it, and it is unclear to me in what sense the reason is his rather than merely about him.

So: is there an established concept for reasons of this shape — considerations that count in favour of an action and that seem directed at someone, but that are not, or no longer, or not yet, possessed by any agent capable of being moved by them; reasons that detach from any reasoner while retaining whatever it is that distinguishes a reason from a piece of impersonal value? The distinctions I have met so far all seem to miss. The agent-relative and agent-neutral contrast concerns who a reason is a reason for in a quite different sense. The objective and subjective distinction looks like a question of whether a reason depends on the agent's beliefs and desires, which is a separate axis from whether anybody has it at all. And impersonal or agent-neutral value looks like precisely the thing I want to distinguish this from rather than identify it with. I also suspect the puzzle is lopsided: for reasons to believe, I find it unmysterious that there should be evidence for a hypothesis that no one will ever consider, whereas the practical case is the one that bothers me, and I would like to know whether that asymmetry is real or whether I am being taken in by a difference in idiom.

If there is a term for this, I would like the concept carved rather than merely named: what exactly the phenomenon is supposed to be, how it differs from the three or four nearest candidates, whether my two cases are really one phenomenon or several — a reason whose owner is absent, a reason whose owner cannot receive it, and perhaps a third, a reason whose owner has ceased to exist, may be different animals — and where the boundary lies, that is, at what point a detached reason collapses into a plain fact about what would be better. And if there is no established name, a coined one with a definition and a pair of clean boundary cases would serve me better than a citation: I have no access to a university library and could not chase references, so I am not asking for a reading list, only for the distinction itself worked out far enough that I can take it back to the group and defend it against people who have read more than I have.
