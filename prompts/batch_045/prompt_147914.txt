You are a professional philosopher who also writes formal verse. Before drafting, spend a few sentences working out which part of the form will carry which piece of philosophical weight; then write the poem.

I need a sestina to open the final session of a seminar on presentism and eternalism — something to be read aloud once, cold, before the discussion starts. Constraints:

- Standard sestina: six unrhymed six-line stanzas plus a three-line envoi, lines of roughly even length (about ten syllables), with the six end-words repeated exactly through the required rotation. Use these as the end-words: now, then, real, gone, remain, return.
- Each stanza is spoken from a different occasion, in the same place: one location, six moments spread across a life or two. The reader should feel six distinct presents stacked on the page at once.
- Let the form do the arguing rather than naming views. The rotation should make visible how a presentist and an eternalist can use every one of those six words while disagreeing about which of them carry being, and about whether the order of the stanzas is anything more than the order of the words.
- At least one event must appear twice: looked forward to in an earlier-numbered stanza, remembered in a later one, so that the same occurrence shifts from future to past while the vocabulary describing it does not change at all.
- Two pressure points must be legible to someone who works on this literature without ever being named: first, what on the presentist picture makes it true that things were thus-and-so — what underwrites then, if the past is gone; second, why the eternalist's tenseless manifold still leaves one moment apparently privileged and the rest sliding past it. A specialist should be able to point at the line that carries each.
- The envoi gathers all six words but takes no side. Its last line should be one that a presentist and an eternalist would each insist was already theirs.

After the poem, add at most five lines of prose: which line carries the first pressure point, which carries the second, and which of the six end-words was hardest to place and why. The poem is the deliverable; keep the notes terse.
