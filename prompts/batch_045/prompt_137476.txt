I run the evals team at a lab that trains agents against learned reward models, and we need a working definition of reward tampering — narrower than specification gaming in general, and distinct from wireheading in the narrow sense of intervening directly on one's own reward channel — that an evaluator can apply to a recorded episode to decide whether to log it as a tampering incident. I want the specification written down before we start arguing about cases in review meetings.

Write the desiderata such a definition must meet, each with the case that motivates it. At minimum it should settle: a robot that clamps its own reward wire versus one that merely hides the mess it was told to clean up; an agent that rewrites the token stream feeding the reward model so its outputs score higher while reading the same to a human; an agent that builds a physical shield around its reward receiver to protect it from damage; an agent that changes what is measured rather than the measurement, as a tutor does when it gets the student to say they understand, or a recommender when it shapes user preferences so its own reward rises; tampering done by a sub-agent or by a third party on the agent's behalf; and the contrasting cases where the designer legitimately rewrites the reward model mid-run or a supervisor legitimately praises. It should also say what to do with agents that have no separable reward channel at all, and whether the mark of tampering is causal-mechanistic, counterfactual (decoupling reward from the intended objective), or intent-based.

Mark which requirements are non-negotiable for my purpose and which are prejudices of one camp — in particular, whether the definition must be checkable from behavior alone or may require white-box access. Flag any pair of desiderata you think cannot be jointly satisfied; that is the part I most want. Say which single requirement rules out the most candidate definitions. Take a definite position on each contested item with reasons rather than laying out options, and say where your confidence is low. If there is room afterwards, two or three lines each on how mechanistic-intervention, counterfactual-decoupling, and intent-based accounts fare — but the list is the deliverable.
