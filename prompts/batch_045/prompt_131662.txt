I'm in the final year of a PhD in philosophy and I'm applying for a nine-month fieldwork grant to run a cross-cultural study of philosophical intuitions. I need a one-page proposal and I'd like you to draft it.

My half-formed idea: the standard design translates a fixed vignette, administers it in several populations, finds a gap in the modal answers, and treats that gap as variation in the intuition itself. But any vignette is massively underdescribed, and participants from different backgrounds will fill the gaps with different defaults — they may take the protagonist to be a stranger rather than a relative, take the scenario to be set inside rather than outside a legal or institutional setting, or hold a looser conversational standard for the target word. If so, what looks like disagreement about knowledge or reference is really agreement on different background premises. My proposed name for this is keying variation, and my test for distinguishing it from genuine content variation is to measure the defaults (items asking what participants took the scenario to be about), then manipulate them by making the default explicit and holding it fixed across sites, and see whether the cross-cultural gap shrinks, persists, or appears only for some cases. I'd also add a second-order condition in which each group is shown the other group's modal answer and its stated reasons, so we can record whether they treat it as a disagreement, as a different question, or as a different topic.

Please give me: the question stated precisely enough to have a determinate answer; why the existing positions (both the variation-finders and the robustness defenders) leave it open; the design, including why this design rather than the obvious alternatives of more sites, more careful translation, or larger samples; what a positive result and a negative result would each look like, and what would count as the project failing rather than merely returning a null; and an honest note on what would make it too big for nine months. Don't include a bibliography — I'll add the citations myself. Write it in British English and keep it under 700 words.
