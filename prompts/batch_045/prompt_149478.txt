I help write the rules for a couple of volunteer organisations and moderate a forum, and I keep running into the same shape of problem, and I cant find out whether it has a name.

Two instances. Our bylaws say the section on membership fees can only be changed by a two-thirds vote. Last year a simple majority passed a motion saying that any section can be changed by a simple majority. So now either the fee section got unprotected by a procedure it explicitly forbids, or that new motion partly failed to do what it said. Either way nobody voted on the fee section, but it's suddenly far easier to change.

Second instance: a rule says motions about money need two weeks' notice. Someone amends the definitions section so that "money" no longer covers honoraria. The notice rule was never touched, nobody voted on it, and it now governs much less than it did.

So the pattern I mean: changing what a rule actually does by changing some *other* rule it leans on — its own amendment procedure, or the definition of its own words — rather than by changing the rule itself. Does legal theory, or the theory of rules generally, have a term for this? It isn't quite entrenchment, since the protection here is itself amendable, and it isn't quite implied repeal, since everybody agrees the rule itself is still there untouched. If there's no established term, coin one and define it, and give me the boundary cases: is redefining a word really the same move as altering an amendment procedure, and does the case where the second rule changes nothing yet anyway count? I want the carving, not just a label.
