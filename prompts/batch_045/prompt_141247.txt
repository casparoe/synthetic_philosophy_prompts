Hi — I'm on the reference values committee of a small professional body (clinical laboratory scientists). Every four years we publish the *Compendium of Reference Intervals*; the new edition has about 1,900 entries, things like "serum [X], adult female, 18–40: a–b mg/dL". Each entry gets read by two reviewers from the relevant subspecialty, and if both sign off it carries a small "Verified" mark in the book and in the downloadable data file.

The thing I need a memo on: our chair wants a note in the front matter along the lines of "users should assume that a small number of entries in this edition are in error." One member (a pathologist who reads philosophy and has already said "preface paradox" twice in email) says this is flatly inconsistent with the stamp — if we're each willing to attest to entry 1, entry 2, and so on, then either we can attest to all of them or we shouldn't be attesting to any of them, and bolting a humility sentence ontop of the front matter is just trying to have it both ways. Another member says the note is free: everyone already assumes reference tables have some errors, it's mere honesty, and the mark really just means "passed our process", not "true".

Three facts that I think matter and that the committee keeps talking past:

1. Nobody on the committee has read all 1,900 entries. There are nine of us and each reviews only in their own area. But the book goes out under the committee's name, so the conjunctive claim is being made by a body none of whose individual members believes it.
2. The entries are not independent. Most come out of one shared statistics pipeline (same script, same outlier rule, same unit conversion), and a fair number are computed from the same two or three source datasets. In the last cycle we audited a random 150 entries and found 4 errors: three in the same section, all traceable to one bad unit conversion in a script that had produced 41 entries, and one transcription slip in a different section. So the realized error process was mostly a single common-mode event, not four independent drizzles.
3. We recieved a letter from a small rural hospital lab after the last edition asking, in effect: when I act on entry 1,347, am I relying on the stamp or on the front matter? Nobody answered and it's still sitting in the inbox. To me that's the lottery version of the same problem — they act on one entry at a time, and for any one entry they'd be perfectly comfortable, but they know some entry is wrong and not which.

Two further proposals are floating: (A) replace "Verified" with a *reliability* mark meaning "produced by a process we estimate to be about 97% reliable" rather than "this value is correct"; (B) have a named person sign off on each section (roughly 30–40 entries) instead of on each entry, on the theory that a smaller conjunction is one somebody can sincerely believe. They might combine; I genuinely don't know.

What I need is a memo to circulate before our meeting a week from Thursday (the front matter locks nine days out). It should:

- recommend one concrete policy — what the mark means, whether there's a front-matter note at all and if so roughly what it says (pasteable wording would be great), and what we do about the correlated / common-mode risk specifically;
- give the strongest version of the pathologist's objection rather than a strawman, even if you come out against him, and also say plainly whether the "the note costs nothing" camp is right;
- say what (A) and (B) each do and don't buy us, including whether they can be combined and what that combination commits us to;
- and tell us what to write back to the rural lab.

One constraint: our rules currently say no entry shall be published unless the committee is satisfied that it is correct. If your recommendation requires that sentence to change, give me replacement wording. And please skip the literature review and citations — I need something nine people can vote on.
