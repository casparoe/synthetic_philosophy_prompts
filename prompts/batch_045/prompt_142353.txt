I cover neuroscience and AI, and the beat has quietly messed with my gut sense of who deserves blame. I keep reading that philosophers call these reactions *reactive attitudes* — is that the right handle? Either way, here are my verdicts, and I want to know what principle I'm actually working with.

- A source who lied to me to protect his employer: resentment, no question.
- A friend in the early stages of frontotemporal dementia who said something genuinely cruel at a dinner party before anyone knew: my resentment drains away once I hear the diagnosis, and I switch into something like clinical concern.
- A guy I profiled whose personality changed and who turned out to have a tumour sitting on his orbitofrontal cortex: same drain-away, once the scan came back.
- A man raised inside a violent extremist household who went on to do exactly what he was raised to do: I feel the full force of indignation, and so does everyone I know, even though the causal story is every bit as good as the tumour one.
- The chatbot that gave a suicidal teenager harmful advice: I caught myself being furious at *it*, and I think that's a mistake — but being furious at the company doesn't feel like a mistake at all, even though the company is machinery too, in a sense.
- Me, snapping at interviewees for a year before I got diagnosed with severe sleep apnoea: I can give the mechanistic story, and unlike with other people, explaining it doesnt touch my guilt one bit.
- Gratitude seems stickier than resentment: I still feel grateful to a colleague who mentored me even after learning she mostly did it to impress my editor.

My working rule has been something like: reactive attitudes are in order when the behaviour *expresses* what the agent really cares about, and out of order when its just the machinery misfiring — but I can't say what *expresses* means, and cases 5 and 6 break it in opposite directions. Can you repair that rule or hand me a better one, tell me which of my verdicts I'd have to give up, and say what it predicts for two I haven't settled: a friend with borderline personality disorder who does the same hurtful thing every few months, and someone radicalised by a recommendation feed rather than by their family?
