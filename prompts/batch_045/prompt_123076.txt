hey! I run the philosophy club at my school — about 15 people, mostly 16-17, one hour on thursday after school, and all weve got is a whiteboard, some scrap paper and whatever's lying around the classroom. no budget, and I can't give homework or prizes.

We're doing a session on functionalism and multiple realizability — the idea that a mental state is defined by the job it does (what it takes as input, what it does, what it leads to) rather than what its made of, so the same state could in principle be built out of neurons or silicon or gears or a room full of people. Everyone nods when I explain it but I want them to actually run into it, and I also want them to run into the parts where it starts to feel slippery, because right now I think half of them will go "well sure, that's obvious" and the other half will go "so what".

Design me an activity or game for that hour. What I'd like:

- nobody should hear the words functionalism or multiple realizability until the debrief, and no explaining at the start. The idea has to show up in what happens to them.
- everyone doing something, not watching two people at the board.
- groups should end up building or acting out the *same* abstract machine out of totally different stuff — cards, cups and pennies, people standing in different corners of the room, a chain of notes passed around, whatever.

Three things I specifically want built into the rules, but change them if you think they won't work:

1. A matter-neutral spec handed out to each group (like a little state/transition table for something simple — a lock, a vending machine, a tic-tac-toe strategy, whatever you think is best) that each group has to physically realize in its own medium, so that at the end you can line them up and see they're all "the same thing".
2. Something that makes them discover that just matching inputs to outputs isn't enough. My idea was a "dead branch" rule: the machine has to do the right thing not only on the input sequences we actually test, but on sequences nobody ever runs, and you get points for catching another group's machine cheating by being hardcoded to only work on the paths we tested. Is that the right way to make that point? Fix it if its not.
3. Some kind of component-swap or ship-of-Theseus round — two realizations of the same spec trading a part mid-run, or rebuilding their machine out of the other group's materials — so people have to argue about when its still the same machine and when it stopped being one.

And then the bit I'm most stuck on: I want there to be a moment where "well, any pile of junk realizes *some* function, so this can't be saying much" starts to bite, because I think that's where the whole thing feels too easy and I don't want the session ending with everyone feeling like it was true by definition. But I have no idea how to stage that as something they *do* rather than something I say.

Give me: materials and setup, the actual rules and payoff scheme (points only), a minute-by-minute timeline for 60 minutes including the debrief, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto specific parts of the idea. Also be honest about which bit of the idea the game just can't reproduce and I'll have to say out loud myself. And tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — my guess is the card group finishes in five minutes while the people-in-corners group is still confused, but you'd know better than me.
