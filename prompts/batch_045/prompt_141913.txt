hey — I run a tiny reading group on philosophy of physics (four of us; one is a quantum information theorist who keeps ruining every session by being right) and we're closing out a term on the psi-ontic / psi-epistemic split. For the last meeting I want to end with a poem instead of another ten-minute summary, and I have a specific form in mind: a sestina. The six end words I want, in this order, are wave, knowledge, overlap, branch, chance, world — though if one of them genuinely won't work you can swap it, just tell me what you swapped and why. The reason I want a sestina rather than free verse is that the rotation feels like the thing itself: the same six words keep coming back in an order that's fixed but always arriving in new company, so that by the fourth stanza "knowledge" has started to sound like a claim about the world and "world" like a claim about what we know. That slide — the formalism staying put while the story about what it means won't — was most of what we argued about for eight weeks, and I'd rather the form carry it than have the poem announce it.

The part I actually need help with is that I've been pushing a position in the group that I half-invented and dont really know how to defend, and I want the poem written in the voice of someone who holds it. Call it compressionism: the wavefunction is a compressed description of the ontic state. It's fixed by the ontic state, so it isn't free-floating opinion and it isn't arbitrary the way a probability distribution over somebody's lunch order is — but it's still a description rather than a thing in the world, the way the shortest program that outputs a given string is a description of that string and not a second string sitting next to it. So the view is meant to be psi-ontic in one sense and psi-epistemic in another, and my quantum info friend says this is just having it both ways with extra steps. I want the poem to give compressionism its best hour: let the early stanzas feel like the view is genuinely working, let it get one clean shot in at the onticist, and then let the envoi — the three-line turn where all six words return at once, which is where a sestina has to pay up — be the place where the view either collapses or somehow survives. Don't pre-decide it for me; if the honest ending is that the compression was lossy and something essential got thrown away in the packing, say that, and if you can see a way out, take it.

Accuracy matters more than prettiness here. The overlap material should gesture at the real argument that distinct wavefunctions can't share ontic states under a certain independence assumption about how systems are prepared — the Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph style result, no need to name it or cite anything — and I'd like a philosopher of physics to be able to read it without wincing. But another friend of mine hasn't touched physics since high school and mostly comes for the wine, so it also has to work as a poem read out loud. Six sestets plus the envoi, so 39 lines, about ninety seconds aloud, no footnotes and nothing explained after the last line.
