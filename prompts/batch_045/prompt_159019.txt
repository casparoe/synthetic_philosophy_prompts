Please act as a philosopher of mind who has spent an unhealthy amount of time on personal identity, the uploading/whole-brain-emulation literature, and the philosophy of neuroscience, and who is willing to be genuinely unfriendly to my view. Here's where I've landed after a long argument with my roommate (neuroscience PhD, thinks the whole uploading debate is cargo-cult metaphysics) and a seminar on personal identity where everyone rolls their eyes the moment someone says "uploading." I hold what I've started calling the handover-with-overlap view: what makes it the case that the person who wakes up tomorrow is me isn't the substrate, isn't the final functional organisation, and isn't strictly psychological continuity either — it's that at every moment the system currently doing the experiencing shares causal work with the system that was doing it a moment earlier. No moment at which the whole stream is handed over at once. That's why neuron-by-neuron replacement of the classic sort is survival, and why a destructive scan-and-copy emulation (build the emulation from a static scan, then destroy the original) is not: the emulation is a causal duplicate, but nothing was ever handed over to it. The corollary I'm actually attached to, and that my roommate says is indefinately weird, is that "creeping emulation" is also survival: build the emulation alongside, shadow one neural patch at a time, let each emulated patch take over the traffic from the biological patch it's mirroring, and decommission the biological patch only after the handover is stable — at the end there's zero biological tissue left and the final system is functionally indistinguishable from what a one-shot upload would have produced, but because the transition was a continuous handover with overlap, it's me. I also bite two bullets on purpose: being under general anaesthesia (or in some kind of induced hibernation) between stages doesn't break anything, since there's no stream of consciousness to break during those months anyway; and if the excised tissue were kept alive in a dish and later reassembled into a working brain, that later biological person is a copy, not me, even though she's made of the original matter. I'm least confident about that last one.

What would count against this?
