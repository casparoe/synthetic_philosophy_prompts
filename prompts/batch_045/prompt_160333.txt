I'm on the rules committee of my housing co-op, which is rewriting its charter, and someone handed us the argument below and I genuinely can't tell which bit to throw out.

Our amending clause, Rule 12, says: any rule may be amended or repealed by a two-thirds vote of a general meeting. We're considering adding Rule 13, which would say: Rule 12 may not be amended except by a two-thirds vote followed by a mail ballot of all members. The point of it is to make the threshold hard to lower later.

(1) Rule 12 lets a general meeting amend any rule in the charter, including Rule 12 itself.
(2) So there is nothing the meeting cannot lawfully enact: any rule whatever can be made part of the charter by a two-thirds vote.
(3) Suppose the meeting enacts Rule 13. Rule 13 is itself a rule in the charter, so by Rule 12 the meeting can repeal Rule 13 by an ordinary two-thirds vote, and then, at the same meeting or a later one, lower the threshold in Rule 12. Nothing unlawful happens at either step. So Rule 13 entrenches nothing: every entrenchment clause is defeasible by the very procedure it tries to restrict, just in two steps instead of one, and the whole exercise is decoration.
(4) But now suppose the meeting instead uses Rule 12 to amend Rule 12 directly, so that Rule 12 reads: no rule may be amended except by a two-thirds vote and a mail ballot. Once that amendment takes effect, the old two-thirds-only procedure no longer exists, so the meeting cannot undo what it just did by the route it used to do it. That looks like real entrenchment, achieved in one step by the very same supposedly unlimited power.
(5) (3) says entrenchment is impossible and (4) says it's trivial. Both can't be right.

I'd rather keep (4) than (3), because if (3) is right we should scrap Rule 13 and I've wasted two weekends on it. But I can't say what's wrong with (3). Is it that can amend in (2) is doing two different jobs? Is it something about whether an amendment that breaks the existing manner-and-form rules is void or merely improper, and about which rule is in force at the moment it validates the change? Its also not clear to me whether it matters if Rule 13 is redrafted to say it applies to amendments and repeals of itself, or whether routing future amendments to a different body (the mail ballot) is doing all the real work. A friend says this has the same shape as the old question whether an omnipotent being can tie its own hands, which makes me trust it less, not more.

Tell me which step you'd reject, why it looked fine, and what giving it up costs us in practice when we draft the thing.
