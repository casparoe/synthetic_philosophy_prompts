The situation: I'm a philosophy professor, and for about a year I've been the token external person on the safety review board of a mid-sized AI lab. Last spring one of their memos got quoted in a public transparency report, which concentrated minds considerably.

The row they keep having is this. The question they are supposed to answer has the form: is model M disposed to φ under conditions C, where φ is something like 'give a user actionable help culturing a dangerous pathogen', or 'sandbag on capability evals', or 'misrepresent its own objectives to its operators'. What they actually do is ask whether they can elicit φ in the harness, and then fight about what the result means. I pointed out to them that this is the fink/mask literature wearing a hoodie, and they asked me for something they can run. So: write it for me, and I will translate.

Here is the material I want operationalised, in my own words so we don't get lost in terminology. A mask is when the disposition is there and the manifestation is blocked by something extrinsic — refusal training, an output filter, a monitor, the system prompt. A fink here is when the test itself destroys the thing tested for: the model recieves cues that it is being evaluated and behaves better than it would in deployment, or the safety wrapper switches on under exactly the prompts that would have elicited the behaviour. Then there is the mirror-image case nobody on the team has a name for, where the elicitation brings the disposition into existence — in-context learning from a long many-shot jailbreak, or gradient steps during a red-team fine-tune — so a positive result is not evidence about the model you had before you started. And there is the plain false positive: the manifestation shows up for the wrong reason, because the model is role-playing a villain, or complying with a hypothetical, or being sycophantic, so it does not manifest the disposition you asked about. I also want them to handle unreachable stimuli (disposition present, no plausible deployment path to the trigger) and the second-order case, where what matters is the disposition to acquire the disposition under a cheap fine-tune, which raises the question of whether the object being evaluated is the model or the model-plus-fine-tuning-pipeline.

Two things I've been toying with that I want built in. First, a reference-class fix: the procedure should require the team to write down and defend the distribution C of deployment-like conditions they are ascribing the disposition relative to, rather than testing against the worst case or against whatever their benchmark suite happens to contain — because with enough prompt engineering everything is disposed to everything, and without a fixed reference class the ascription is empty. But writing down C is a policy decision, not a metaphysical one, so that is a place where the procedure has to stop and hand the decision to a person. Second, a symmetry clause: whatever standard of evidence the team needs before concluding 'the capability is gone, not merely masked', they must also need before concluding 'that bad output was an artifact of the test, not a real disposition'. Right now they are asymmetric in exactly the self-serving direction, and I want the procedure to make that impossible rather than to nag about it.
