I'm writing the framing section of a survey on observer-selection problems in cosmology, and before I go near the competing proposals I want the specification written down first. Please write me the desiderata a cosmological theory would have to meet in order to count as having solved the Boltzmann brain problem, where solved means something strong enough to be useful: a working cosmologist could take a new model, apply the theory, and get a verdict on whether the model is viable, rather than merely a reason to stop worrying about it.

Assume the setup is known; don't spend words defining fluctuation observers or recapping the dialectic, and don't survey the literature. Put the weight on the requirements themselves and on the cases that motivate them.

Some distinctions and cases I think any such list has to respect, and which I want handled explicitly:

- Two worries I take to be distinct though they are constantly run together: the dominance worry, that observers produced by thermal or vacuum fluctuations vastly outnumber observers produced by a long low-entropy cosmological history, so that a randomly sampled observer is a fluctuation; and the ratification worry, that a theory on which I am probably a fluctuation observer is a theory on which my evidence for that very theory is noise, so that the theory cannot be rationally accepted even if true. A theory can win on one and lose on the other, and I want separate requirements for each rather than one merged entry.
- The Archive case: a fluctuation that produces not merely a momentary observer with disordered impressions but an observer together with a physically complete set of apparent records — an instrument, a set of notebooks, a body configured as if it had just finished a measurement — that is, an observer with the same total evidence I take myself to have. Such fluctuations are enormously more expensive than minimal ones and so vastly rarer, but they are the ones that matter, because the cheap ones do not share my evidence. I want a requirement about which comparison class a theory is allowed to use and why, since the verdict flips depending on whether we count all observers or only observers with my evidence.
- A requirement about what would count as evidence for the hypothesis that I am such an observer, and about what the theory is committed to if nothing could in principle count as such evidence.
- A requirement about the sampling or typicality step: whether it is physics or a methodological rule, whether the theory must supply the reference class rather than take it as given, and whether a theory may use typicality to generate the original prediction while suspending it once the prediction turns on the theory's own credibility.

For each requirement give the case or consideration behind it, and mark it as non-negotiable, negotiable, or a preference of one research community. Then tell me which subset of the requirements, if any, is jointly unsatisfiable — I suspect the live tension is between using typicality to get the problem off the ground and qualifying it in order to escape the conclusion, but state it precisely if that diagnosis is wrong. Tell me which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidate solutions, and which one you suspect is a prejudice of one camp dressed up as a desideratum. Most useful of all: flag any requirement of yours such that dropping it would make the problem vanish altogether, since that would be a sign the requirement was smuggling in the answer rather than testing for it.

Use the familiar families of response only as stress tests of the list — a cosmology with a low-entropy past and a finite future, an eternally inflating cosmology with a measure over observers, and any approach that rejects the sampling assumption outright — and confine this to a few lines; I'll do the scoring myself.

Finally, the part where I want you self-critical rather than assertive: mark your own least secure requirement, say what argument or what kind of case would make you drop it, and say where you think this whole project of specifying what it would take to solve the problem might be misguided — for instance, if there turns out to be no determinate thing being asked for. No citations or reading list, please; I have the literature.
