I want a specification, not a survey. Fix the purpose narrowly: an account of the will of the people good enough to adjudicate, in real time and before any vote is held, a politician's claim to speak for the people — to say whether she speaks truly or merely speaks for a faction.

Write the desiderata such an account must meet. For each, give the case that makes it a requirement, and mark it non-negotiable or merely the preference of one camp. Among the cases I want it to earn its keep on: the inter-electoral claim itself, which forces the question whether the will can be identified independently of any procedure and, if so, how that identification is not simply arbitrary; scale-fragmentation, where a majority rejects every component of a package yet approves the package as a whole, so that issue-by-issue and holistic aggregation come apart; the silent and the misinformed — non-voters, and preferences formed under systematic misinformation — which forces a choice between the will people actually have and the will they would have under better information; the bearer question, whether the people are residents, citizens, those subject to coercion, or future generations, and whether the bearer survives a large change in its own membership; intensity, where 51 per cent are mildly for and 49 per cent intensely against, together with cycling and agenda-order cases in which no stable majority exists at all; and self-binding, where a people wills a constitution that later blocks what it now wants.

Flag any pair of desiderata you take to be jointly unsatisfiable, say which one is really a prejudice of a particular camp rather than a genuine requirement, and identify the single requirement that excludes the largest share of otherwise plausible theories. Close with one or two lines each on how four families fare: aggregative-procedural accounts, the general-will tradition, deliberative or counterfactual-will accounts, and constructivist views on which the people exist only in being invoked. A table is fine for the list. Lead with your bottom line — the excluding requirement and the unsatisfiable pair — in the first sentence, then give the requirements; no preamble and no restating of the question.
