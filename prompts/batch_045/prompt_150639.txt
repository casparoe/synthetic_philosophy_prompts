I'm not a philosopher — I work with data, and I've been reading around the edges of the Bayesian stuff out of curiosity, mostly so I can win arguments with a colleague. I keep noticing a difference between two kinds of evidence and I don't have a word for it, and the words I've managed to find are close but don't quite land.

Case one. A sensor reading comes back, or a lab result, and it moves my confidence in some hypothesis. Crucially, there's an intermediate thing I can point at and defend: the instrument said 3.2, the test came back positive, she told me she saw him leave the building. I dont become certain of that intermediate thing — I know instruments misfire and people misremember — but I know *which* thing it is whose probability went up, and if pushed I could defend a number for how much.

Case two. I glance at a blurry trail-cam photo and my sense that it's a bobcat and not a house cat goes up. Nothing became more probable that I can name. Not "the photo looks like a bobcat" — I mean, I could say that, but it feels like I'm just restating my conclusion in a sentence rather than giving the thing that moved. Same with a case I get a lot at work: I spend an afternoon reading incident reports and my confidence that the system is basically fine drops, and there's no sentence I'd point to as the one whose probability changed.

I don't think the difference is just that case two is vague or hard to put into words. It feels structural: in case one the update goes through an intermediary I could in principle become certain of, and in case two it seems to land on the hypothesis directly, or on some whole spread of alternatives I can't identify. But here's where I get stuck, and it's what I actually want an answer about.

If I try to force case two into the shape of case one by inventing an intermediary — the image has a certain gestalt, the reports have a smell about them — I can always do it. So what is the difference supposed to be, exactly? Is there anything real at stake, or is the distinction empty, and every update secretly has an intermediary whether or not I can say what it is?

And a related thing that's been bugging me. A friend who actually knows this material told me there's a standard move for handling evidence you don't become certain of — you spread the change across a set of alternatives instead of piling it all onto one — but she said the result depends on *which* set of alternatives you spread it over, and that two such updates taken in a different order can leave you somwhere different. That sounds to me like a defect in the move rather than an interesting fact about me. Does that order problem have a name? And does it get worse in my case two, where I can't even say what the alternatives were that my experience ranged over?

So: does the distinction I'm circling have an established name in this area, or has it just never been marked? If something nearby already covers it, tell me what it is, where my description doesn't fit it, and what I'm probably conflating. If nothing does, name it for me, define it tightly enough that I can classify a borderline case, and say whether the distinction actually does any work or whether I'm chasing a shadow. I don't want a label to drop into an argument — I want to know whether I've found a real joint or just drawn a line in fog.
