Two widespread practices seem to me to pull in opposite directions, and I would like the implicit theory of thought that they share, if they share one, reconstructed and stress-tested.

The first is the practice of demanding articulated procedure: in school mathematics a bare correct answer earns little or nothing, a correct procedure with a slipped sign earns most of the credit, and the justification it just came to me is not acceptable from a student. Yet the very same justification is acceptable — often the only one available — from a radiologist reading a scan, a chess master choosing a move, or a native speaker asked why a sentence is ungrammatical; there we treat articulation as optional garnish on a judgment we are happy to rely on. The second practice is newer: consequential automated systems are now asked to emit their reasoning in steps before answering, auditors investigate whether those steps are faithful to the process that actually produced the answer rather than a story told after the fact, and a system whose stated reasoning is post hoc is treated as defective and, in some jurisdictions, as legally non-compliant, even when its answers are exactly as accurate as those of a system whose reasoning is faithful.

My own hypothesis is that both practices presuppose something in the neighbourhood of a language of thought: that thinking is a causal sequence over structured, proposition-like states; that a verbal or verbal-like trace can be more or less a readout of that sequence; and that being able to produce the sequence is partly constitutive of having reasoned rather than merely having arrived. I want that thesis separated from what the practices merely sit well with. Could someone who denies that cognition is language-like — holding instead that it is associative, skill-like, or subpersonal and not propositionally articulated at all — still license both practices, and under what other rationale: pedagogical, accountability-based, evidential? And does the asymmetry between the classroom and the clinic then become principled rather than hypocritical?

Here is the case I want used as the stress test. Stipulate a system whose answer is computed first, and whose step-by-step text is then produced by a separate module that reads only the answer: the steps are causally inert, but they accurately describe a procedure that would reach that answer, and users shown them detect the system's errors better than users shown the answer alone. Does this system satisfy the demand of the classroom, the demand of the auditor, neither, or both? What the answer is should tell us whether what we really want is faithfulness or something weaker — auditability, error-detectability, or a user's capacity to contest an outcome.

Return your answer as a JSON object with exactly these fields: presupposed_thesis (one sentence: the strongest claim about thinking that both practices require); strictly_presupposed (array: claims the practices cannot survive without); merely_compatible (array: stronger or neighbouring theses they fit well with but do not need); rival_rationale (string: the best non-language-of-thought rationale for the same two practices, and where it diverges from the language-of-thought reading, including on the classroom-versus-clinic asymmetry); stress_case (object with keys classroom, clinical_expertise, and automated_audit, each mapping to an object with verdict and reason); and diagnostic (string: the single further case or experiment that would best separate the language-of-thought reading from the rival, and what each predicts about it).
