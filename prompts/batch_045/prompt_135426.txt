I'm writing a scene where my protagonist — a washed-up longtermist who now does bad standup — does a bit at a party about why he stopped caring about the far future. I want it to turn on the actual arguments, not just be a generic joke about nerds and big numbers.

Two moves I want in it. First the deflation: if you buy the simulation argument, the future you were sacrificing for may not be astronomically long at all — the sim gets switched off, or the simulators' own civilization runs out of steam, so the giant number of future people you were fanatically optimizing for never show up. Then the re-inflation: if were in a sim, theres probably a bunch of runs with copies of you making the same choice, so whatever you do gets multiplied back up, and the simulators might be moral patients themselves. End with him realizing the argument cuts both ways and he's fanatical again — which is maybe the acutal joke.

Keep it under ~150 words. Someone who's never heard of expected-value fanaticism should follow the setup, but someone who's actually argued about Pascal's-mugging-style cases should groan.
