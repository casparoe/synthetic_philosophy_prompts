Setup so we agree on terms. In Lakatos's picture a research programme has a hard core you protect, a belt of auxilary hypotheses you adjust, a negative heuristic (don't aim refutations at the core) and a positive heuristic (a rough plan for how to build the next version of the belt). An adjustment is ad hoc if it only mops up the anomaly it was invented for; a problemshift is theoretically progressive if the new version predicts some fact it wasn't built to accommodate, and empirically progressive if some of those predictions check out. A programme is degenerating when its belt changes do nothing but absorb refutations.

My argument, numbered so you can stab at it:

(1) Whether it's rational to keep working in a programme depends only on whether its problemshifts are progressive, not on how many anomalies it currently has stacked against it.

(2) So a programme can be rational to work in at a time when it is drowning in refutations, provided it can still turn things around.

(3) "Novel fact" seems to do two different jobs. Sometimes it means: the fact wasn't known or observed at the time the theory predicted it (temporal novelty). Sometimes it means: the fact wasn't among the things the adjustment was constructed to fit, so it's testable independently of the anomaly (heuristic novelty). The stock example people use — a belt hypothesis about an unseen planet perturbing Uranus, which then gets looked for and found — is progressive on both readings, so the ambiguity never gets noticed.

(4) Now take the most degenerating programme you can think of. Mine is invented so we don't argue about the history: "tidal resonance" says quakes are triggered when lunar-solar tides hit a crustal resonance band. Every failed forecast is absorbed by a new belt hypothesis about a screening layer that locally damps the resonance. Textbook degeneration.

(5) But for any anomaly at all, you can expand the belt by adding a hypothesis that also says something checkable about a completely different phenomenon: the screening layer, we now add, also modulates water levels in deep wells, which can be tested on its own.

(6) On the heuristic reading in (3), that expansion predicts a novel fact. So that problemshift is theoretically progressive, so the programme is potentially progressive at that moment.

(7) And (5) generalises: for every anomaly and every programme, however wrecked, there exists some extension of the belt that would count as progressive. So every programme is potentially progressive at every point in its life.

(8) Lakatos also holds that you don't abandon a programme on refutations alone — you need a rival doing better, and even then hanging on isn't strictly irrational, since programmes can recover.

(9) So: nothing a scientist ever does can be convicted of irrationality by this methodology. Clinging to tidal resonance forever, or to astrology, or to flat-earth, always has the same available rationalisation: some belt extension might have been progressive, and it's too early to tell.

(10) But then the methodology can't do the job it was built for — rationally reconstructing scientific change and demarcating science from pseudoscience — and collapses into Feyerabend's anything-goes with extra machinery. Which is absurd.

I've tried one escape and I don't like it: denying (6) by insisting novelty has to be temporal makes the stock planetary case hostage to who published first, which seems worse than the disease. I'd much rather keep (1) and (8) — that's the Lakatos I actually want to defend — and give up something else.
