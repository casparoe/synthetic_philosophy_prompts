The question was:

Nozick's tracking analysis holds that S knows that p only if S's belief that p tracks the truth, i.e. only if (3) if p were false, S would not believe that p, and (4) if p were true, S would believe that p.

(a) Explain, using a case of your own, why a defender of this analysis should expect knowledge not to be closed under known entailment — that S can know p, competently deduce q from p, and still fail to know q. [10]
(b) Nozick assesses both subjunctive conditions with the method S actually used held fixed. Does that move solve the problem you raised in (a)? [8]
(c) Assess the claim that because the tracking analysis denies closure, it can concede to the skeptic that we don't know we're not brains in a vat while still allowing that we know ordinary things, and that this is a virtue of the view rather than a cost. [7]

The student's answer:

(a) I'm at the zoo and I believe, on the basis of looking at it in good light, that the animal in the enclosure is a zebra. Condition (3) is satisfied: if the animal weren't a zebra I'd see that it wasn't, since a cow or an antelope doesn't look like that, so I wouldn't believe it was a zebra. So I know it's a zebra. Now take the proposition that the animal is not a mule cleverly painted to look like a zebra. Being a zebra entails that it isn't a painted mule, and I can competently deduce the second from the first. But I don't know the second: if it were a painted mule my visual experience would be exactly the same and I'd still believe it was a zebra, so (3) fails for it. So I know p, competently deduce q, and don't know q — closure fails.

(b) Yes, relativising to the method repairs this. (3) should be read as: holding fixed the method I actually used, if p were false I wouldn't believe p by that method. Once the method is fixed, the painted-mule worlds drop out, because those are worlds where the method isn't really in operation — my method is looking at striped animals in normal zoo conditions, and relative to that method my belief does track the truth, so I do know it isn't a painted mule after all. So once you relativise, the account preserves single-premise closure, which was all that was ever really at stake; the only residue is the multi-premise principle, where you combine two seperately known premises. Also, (4) is really just the safety condition later writers made famous — in nearby worlds where I believe p, p is true — and safety, unlike (3), is closed under known entailment. So Nozick has the resources to validate closure after all; you only get the failure if you run (3) on its own without (4). The tracking analysis is best read as a form of reliabilism, one that validates closure for any competent deduction.

(c) This is right and it's the main attraction. I know I have hands — if I didn't have hands I wouldn't believe I did — but I don't know I'm not a brain in a vat, since if I were, my experiences would be the same and I'd go on believing I had hands. Since knowledge isn't closed, the skeptic can't get from the first to the second, so the argument collapses and skepticism is refuted. We keep all our ordinary knowledge and pay nothing for it.
