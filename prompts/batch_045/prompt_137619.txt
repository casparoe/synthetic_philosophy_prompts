I write a column on science and ideas, and after a couple of years of covering risk — climate projections, pandemic models, the replication beat — I've quietly become a Humean about chance. Roughly: objective probability isn't an extra ingredient in the world. It's the pattern of what actually happens, summarized by a best-system-style account of the laws, plus a probability distribution over initial conditions, plus whatever the Mentaculus-style package (deterministic microdynamics, a low-entropy boundary condition, a uniform-ish distribution over the microstates compatible with it) needs to bolt on to make statistical mechanics come out right. No propensities. No irreducible single-case physical probabilities. When a physicist says an atom 'has a disposition to decay by such-and-such a time', I hear either a frequency claim or an information claim wearing a lab coat — and I've started saying so in print, which is why I'd like to know how exposed I am.

Give me the case against this, as straight as you can. Not the undergraduate survey of frequentism vs. subjectivism vs. propensity; I've read that. I want the defeaters, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with some account of which ones a committed Humean would actually concede as counting against them and which they'd wave off as question-begging.

Things I've been chewing on, in no order:

The one-shot world. A world with exactly one genuinely chancy event, which happens. The best system there seems to have nothing to say about its chance, and yet there seems to be a fact of the matter about how likely it was. Is that a real defeater, or just an intuition pumped by a toy world I have no reason to care about?

Direction of explanation. I think the thing I lean on most is that chance is supposed to explain the frequencies and also to constrain rational credence (the Principal Principle-ish thought: defer to the chances when you know them). But on my view the frequencies and the mosaic explain the chances. Is that inversion a genuine cost, or is it exactly the thing a Humean should embrace and charge the realist with making chance into a magical explainer?

The one I actually lose sleep over: whether the simplicity/informativeness trade-off inside 'best system' is language-relative or agent-relative. If the winning system depends on what creatures like us find simple or informative, then I haven't eliminated the subjective, I've just relocated it — and my view collapses into a kind of projectivism about probability, which isn't the most damaging outcome but is by far the most embarrassing. Say how bad this is and whether it's avoidable.

Deterministic-but-chancy cases: Bohmian mechanics, classical stat mech. My move is that all the probability lives in the boundary-condition distribution. Fine — but then does the view have any resources at all for saying why that distribution rather than another, or is it just posited? And if it's just posited, in what sense am I still a Humean rather than a propensity theorist with a funny hat?

A test I invented because I couldn't find it named anywhere, which makes me suspect it's an old objection in new clothes — call it portability. A genuine chance seems to travel with the setup: the half-life, the bias of the coin. You can move the setup to another environment, or never build it at all, and the probability statement still seems true, and still seems to support counterfactuals about what would have happened had you run it. Frequencies in the actual mosaic can't underwrite that. Is portability a distinct problem from the single-case problem, or am I double-counting one objection as two?

What would move me most personally, though, is an example from actual working science — mutation rates, collider results, extreme-event attribution in climate — where the Humean story mangles either what the scientists are doing or what they're entitled to conclude from it. If you have one, lead with it.

Two more asks. First: be honest about whether this position has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that's a problem for a view of this kind. Second: keep straight what's settled among people who actually work on this from what's contested, and don't hand me a contested claim dressed up as the consensus — mark your own reads as your own, and tell me roughly how much confidence to attach to each defeater, including the ones I've listed above. If my framing of any of this (the label 'Humean', what the best-system account is even committed to) is itself up for grabs, say that too.
