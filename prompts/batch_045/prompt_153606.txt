A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about Being John Malkovich (1999), and I want a reading more careful than either of us managed. Please read the film through Hofstadter's treatment of the self as a strange loop: the 'I' as a self-referential symbol that a system both contains and is about, a tangled hierarchy in which climbing levels of self-representation curls back on itself, so that the self turns out to be real without being a thing located anywhere.

There are two distinctions our argument kept sliding over, and I'd like you to hold them apart. The first is between the 'I' as an indexical, a token whose reference is settled by whoever is using it, and the 'I' as a self-model the system builds and acts on, which can be badly wrong about whose it is. When Craig occupies Malkovich, or when Malkovich steps through the portal into the world where everyone is Malkovich, is what breaks the first of these or the second, and does the film know the difference? The second is between a strange loop proper and mere nesting or regress: Lester's serial body-hopping, that Malkovich-world, and Craig's final condition, sealed inside Emily with no control at all. Which of these actually curl back and constitute something, and does the ending mark a loop collapsing into something closer to a Cartesian theater?

Two further things. Craig is a puppeteer, and the film keeps asking who is operating whom: on this reading, is the puppet motif the film's real argument about the self, or only a running joke about it? And tell me where the reading fails. The plot turns on wanting to be, or to have, someone else, and on Maxine's child belonging to Lotte as much as to anyone else; is any of that clarified by talking about self-symbols, or is the film's real subject one that the strange-loop apparatus has no vocabulary for? I would rather have a reading that says where the film resists than one that fits the film to the idea.
