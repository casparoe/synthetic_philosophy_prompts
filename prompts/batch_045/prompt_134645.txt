My opening. Your invariance theorem says switching universal machines shifts every description length by at most a constant, so the prior shifts by at most a constant factor, so who cares. But that constant is exactly where all the finite-time action is, and nothing in the formalism fixes it. Concretely: fix T = 10^100 and let U* be your machine with one extra primitive bolted into the vocabulary, a short symbol meaning "output green until step T, then flip" — describing T costs on the order of 330 bits. U* is universal, so by your own lights it's an admissible reference machine; a machine with a richer vocabulary is still universal. Now at step T the hypothesis "green forever" and the hypothesis "green until T, then flip" fit everything we've seen equally well, so the posterior just is the prior ratio, and under U* the grue-ish one wins by whatever I bought with those bits — comfortably better then even, if I paid a few hundred. Same method, same data, opposite prediction about tommorow, and I get to park the mistake at 10^100 or at 10^9, wherever I like. So which is it: either "up to a constant" is doing all the real predictive work, in which case the razor has no content and the choice of machine is the whole theory, or it isn't, in which case tell me what stops me.
