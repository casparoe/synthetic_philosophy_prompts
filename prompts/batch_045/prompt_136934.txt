I'm in a small philosophy-of-physics reading group — mostly philosophy graduate students plus one physicist — and I've been asked to lead the session on Maxwell's demon. I don't want to just walk everyone through the standard story: the demon and its shutter, the one-molecule engine, and the punchline that the demon's memory must eventually be reset and that resetting costs at least kT ln 2 per bit. Several of us will come away from that thinking we understand more than we do. The thing I keep getting snagged on is what I've started calling the location question: at which point in the demon's cycle is the thermodynamic cost actually paid, and what would have to be true for an answer to that question to be a physical claim rather than a piece of bookkeeping?

Here is why the question seems to me not to be straightforward. There appear to be three candidate locations: the acquisition of the information (the measurement or correlation-building that makes the demon's memory track which side the molecule is on, or how fast it is moving); the storage (the memory is finite, so a demon that never erases eventually runs out of room); and the erasure. The standard move, as I understand it, is to say that acquisition can in principle be done reversibly and carries no minimum thermodynamic price, that storage is just erasure deferred — the demon with an unbounded tape hasn't escaped the second law, it has only postponed the reckoning — and therefore that erasure is where the cost really lives. But then I ran into the point that the erasure cost isn't strictly per bit of physical memory; it's proportional to the information content of what's in the memory, so a demon whose records are repetitive or partly predictable could first compress them reversibly and then reset, paying less than the naive per-bit figure. And the demon's profit seems to fall in step: in treatments of feedback control in stochastic thermodynamics — and I may be half-remembering this — the extractable work is bounded by something like kT times the mutual information between the demon's record and the system. So both sides of the ledger appear to scale with the same information-theoretic quantity, and I lose my grip on whether the resulting balance is a real constraint imposed by the physics or an identity that would hold for any demon whatsoever just in virtue of how mutual information is defined — something we would recover even in a toy universe where the second law plainly failed.

I'd like an explanation that pries those two readings apart. It would also help me a lot if it dealt with a variant I can't stop thinking about: a demon that is simply handed a memory already correlated with the system, where the correlation was produced by an ordinary fluctuation or by someone else's notebook, so that the demon performs no measurement of its own and yet can still extract work. Does the cost then attach to whoever produced the correlation, to the demon who exploits it, or to nobody at all until the memory is reset? Please be concrete: I want to know what the competing accounts each say about the same single run of the same apparatus, and I want to know which steps in the standard story are load-bearing assumptions — about what a measurement is, about what a memory is, about what has to close a cycle — rather than consequences that follow from them. Assume the audience has basic statistical mechanics and knows what Shannon entropy is, but nothing about the demon literature. And if my framing of the location question rests on a confusion, please say what the confusion is and what a better framing would look like.
