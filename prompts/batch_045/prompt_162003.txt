I write long-form pieces, mostly on science and the history of ideas, and I keep running into the same thing in archives. I dont have a word for it.

A historian works through a colonial administration's correspondence. The files for one district survive; the files for the neighbouring one were destroyed in a fire decades ago. She writes it carefully: unlike district A, she says, district B shows no record of organised resistance. The next book drops the caveat: district B, notably quiet under the new administration. By the third one it's the pacification of district B, and it's in the textbook. Nothing was fabricated — the fire is real, and it's in a footnote somewhere — but a fact about what survived got converted into a fact about what happened, and each retelling makes the conversion harder to spot.

Digital version of the same thing: I was searching a scanned newspaper run for coverage of a strike and found a clean-looking collapse in mentions halfway through. Turned out the scan was made from microfilm, a couple of reels were unreadable, the OCR for those months is garbage, and the search engine can't see it. Anyone who searched instead of reading would report that coverage fell off a cliff.

Is there a name for this? It isn't argument from silence — the people making the move usually know the silence is an artifact. And it isn't quite the literature on silences in the archive, which as far as I can tell is about how power shapes what got recorded in the first place, not about what later writers do downstream with a gap. Its not citation laundering either, because nobody invented a claim; they just stopped repeating the caveat.

So: does this have an established concept, and if not, name it and define it — including where the line falls between the harmless version (a caveat you don't have room to repeat) and the version that should worry you, and what a check for it looks like when I'm about to lean on a secondary source. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give me the reasoning, no preamble and no restating the question.
