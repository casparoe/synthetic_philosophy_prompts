I've never studied philosophy, but I've been turning over on my own when it's right to let something be forgotten, and my gut verdicts on cases don't add up to anything I can state. Here they are.

My grandmother's village was destroyed; the survivors built a new town and almost never spoke of the old one, and their grandchildren grew up not knowing its name. I think something real was lost there, but I don't blame them.

A truth commission: perpetrators confess publicly, the record is kept, nobody is punished. I accept it, though it costs me something.

A dying friend asks me to destroy letters documenting a humiliating episode that only she and I know about. I think I should destroy them.

The same friend, but the letters document a crime she committed against someone still alive and still suffering. I think I must not.

The victims and the killers of a dictatorship are all dead and the regime's archives survive. I think they should be kept and read: whatever this duty is, it doesn't expire with the people.

My family quietly dropped an uncle who stole from them; the children don't know he existed. I judge this worse than the village case, and I can't say why — the shape looks the same.

The tentative principle I keep reaching for: you owe it to someone — the person who was harmed, or the wrongdoer's accountability — to remember; where no one has a claim, forgetting is a loss but not a betrayal. But that seems to hand over the archives case, and it still doesn't explain why the uncle case feels worse than the village case.

So: what principle actually fits these verdicts, and which of them does it force me to give up? Test my version against the cases and repair it if you can, or give me two or three candidates that save different verdicts and say what each one costs. Then tell me what the best one says about a case I haven't listed: someone who was badly harmed and who says clearly and repeatedly that she wants the whole thing erased, where the only people the record could still teach are historians with no connection to her. Please answer in American English and keep it as plain as you can.
