hey, one more for the pile. imagine late Tolstoy — the What Is Art? period one, bearded, given up his property, excommunicated, thinks most of the European canon is worthless — gets dropped into 2026 and somebody actually sits him down and explains how generative models work. Not "a machine makes pictures now," but the real picture: trained on scraped work nobody consented to, you steer it with a prompt, you can fine-tune it on a single style, and the thing itself has no body, no biography, no labour, no prayer life, and has never felt anything. Assume he takes all of that in properly and doesn't wave it away.

I want his verdict on four cases, and I want you to run them through his actual machinery rather than vibes: infection; the three conditions (individuality, clearness, sincerity); the four ways he says people fake art — borrowing, imitating, going for striking effects, and mere interestingness; the part where a work is judged by what feeling it carries, with the religious perception of brotherhood up at the top; and his three social causes of counterfeit art, i.e. paying artists, criticism, and art schools. Please answer in British English, and no citations or reading suggestions — I know the book, I want the reasoning.

1. A diocese fine-tunes an image model on a thousand years of Orthodox icons and prints cheap, technically lovely ones for every parish church; the icon painters' workshops shut. Parishioners say they pray better in front of the new ones.
2. A music service that generates you a personalised track every morning matched to your mood from your phone data. Everyone listens alone on headphones, nobody shares anything, and people say it genuinely gets them through the day.
3. Kids at a rural school co-write songs and little plays with a text model about their own lives — migration, a dead parent, the harvest — and perform them at a village gathering. No single author, lots of borrowed phrasing from the model, and the village is reportedly moved to tears.
4. A model trained only on the folk songs of one living village, generating new ones in that exact tradition. The village adopts them and dances to them, and outsiders can't tell which are old.

Give me them ranked worst to best, and say which one he'd call actively harmful rather than just worthless — he did think counterfeit art damages people, by wrecking their capacity to receive real art. Then two more things: (a) find me the case where his theory gives an answer that would surprise somebody who assumes he'd just hate all of it — the one he'd approve of, or at least refuse to condemn; and (b) tell me where his own tests break down once the maker isn't a person who could have lived the feeling. Does sincerity have any subject left, or does everything collapse into "does it infect anybody"? And does what he says about property and the fruits of other people's labour push him somewhere that the machine case forces him to go, wich I think it defintely might.

Be concrete — for each case say which of his tests is doing the work and why, and don't hedge with "it's hard to say what Tolstoy would think." Commit to verdicts. About 700 words.
