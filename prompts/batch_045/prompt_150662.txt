I am a PhD candidate in vision science, preregistering a study on the color phi phenomenon, and I have talked myself into an argument I cannot accept. Brief background, so we are using the same terms: in the standard display a red spot is flashed at one location and, a fraction of a second later, a green spot at a nearby location; observers report a single spot travelling from the first location to the second, changing color partway along its path. The puzzle is that the change is reported as happening before the green spot has been presented at all. The Orwellian hypothesis says the experience at the earlier moment really was one thing and was then overwritten, so that the subject misremembers it; the Stalinesque hypothesis says no experience with that earlier temporal profile ever occurred, and an experience with a different profile was constructed later. The two are built to agree on everything observable. My contribution is a continuous-report variant. Subjects track the color of the travelling spot by turning a dial sampled many times per second, and I get three effects in pilot data:

- Displacement: adding a brief luminance mask after the second flash shifts the point at which the dial trace shows the color change, moving it earlier along the trajectory by an amount that grows with the delay between the second flash and the mask, up to about sixty milliseconds.
- Graded dependence: on separate trials, a binary retro-probe — a color patch shown after the sequence, with a yes/no judgment of whether it matches the spot at the moment the patch appeared — yields a displacement larger than the dial's, and the two displacements correlate across subjects. The more the judgment leans on processing after the event, the larger the shift.
- Interruption: if the display is blanked immediately after the second flash, with no mask and no probe, the displacement disappears entirely.

Here is the argument. Each step seemed fine to me when I wrote it and the end is not something I can live with.

(1) A difference between two hypotheses about an experience is a real difference only if something the subject could in principle do or say would tell between them. Differences no possible observation could discriminate are not differences in experience.

(2) For any apparent temporal property of an experience — that it started at some moment, lasted so long, contained a change at such-and-such a point — one can construct an Orwellian and a Stalinesque hypothesis: on the first, the experience had that temporal property and its record was later altered; on the second, no experience with that temporal property occurred and one with a different profile was constructed later. By construction the pair agrees on every observable.

(3) My three effects show that this is not an artefact of the phi display but a general feature of experience. The temporal profile attributed to an experience at a time depends on stimulation after that time; the dependence grows with how much the report relies on post-event processing and vanishes when nothing follows. So for any experience at any time, later stimulation can in principle be arranged so as to displace its apparent temporal profile, and therefore an Orwellian and Stalinesque pair can be constructed for it.

(4) No measurement escapes this. A measurement of the timing of experience is either a report, whose own temporal profile is displaceable by (3), or a neural or behavioral measurement; and such a measurement can at best tell us when some candidate vehicle or disposition occurs, not whether the experience occurred then, since that is precisely what (2) leaves open.

(5) So by (1) and (4) there is no fact of the matter about the timing of any experience: none about when it began, how long it lasted, or when its internal changes happened.

(6) An experience is the sort of thing that occurs over an interval. There is no such state as undergoing an experience that is not located at a time, and whatever is not located in time is not an experience.

(7) So from (5) and (6), for any candidate experience there is no fact of the matter whether it occurs at all, and all that remains are facts about what will later be reported. Iterating, nobody is ever conscious at the time at which they seem to be conscious.

(8) That is not a surprising empirical finding but the dissolution of the thing my study was meant to investigate, and one of my advisors says it shows the paradigm measures nothing.
