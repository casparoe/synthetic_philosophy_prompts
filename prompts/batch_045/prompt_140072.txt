I'm one of the two member-elected trustees on the governance committee for my university's supplemental retirement plan (a 403(b), about 11,000 participants, mostly staff and adjuncts, a lot of balances under $25k). We meet Thursday morning and I'm supposed to circulate a recommendation beforehand, which is why I'm asking you.

Facts. Participants can reallocate among the plan's nine funds whenever they like. Each switch costs 0.25% of the amount moved, and that money goes into the plan's general reserve, which gets paid back out as a flat per-account credit every January (last year: $18). So somebody with a big balance who never touches anything collects a free $18, and somebody with $9,000 who switches six times in a quarter pays considerably more than that and gets $18 back. Over the last four quarters roughly 7% of participants finished the quarter in pretty much the allocation they started with, having made four or more switches to get there — usually out of the equity-heavy fund after a bad week, back in after a good one, repeat. Our recordkeeper calls them "round-trippers."

The recordkeeper's proposal is Cooldown+: after any switch, a 21-day lock, which you can override by paying a fee that doubles with each override in a rolling year.

Deadlock on the commitee. One trustee (finance dept) says the round-trippers are being money-pumped, full stop — they finish where they began, minus money, over and over — so we should stop them. Another (benefits lawyer) says two things: several of them have told us in writing that switching is worth it to them, that they'd rather do something than sit there watching, so maybe they're buying something rather than being pumped; and we're the ones collecting the 0.25%, which is a position she's definately uncomfortable reasoning from.

Here's the bit nobody can handle. Some round-trippers say they'd *like* to be stopped and can't stop themselves, so the obvious fix is to let people set their own lock at enrollment, in a calm moment, binding their future selves. But the recordkeeper wants to be paid for the feature, and then we've arguably just added one more step to the pump — the step where they pay us so that they can stop paying us. If your memo recommends binding anybody, it has to deal with that and not footnote it. Related: in last year's pilot, 61 people set self-imposed locks and later wrote in demanding release. A couple said they weren't in their right mind when they set it. A couple said the exact opposite — clear-eyed in October, not now.

Write the memo I'll put my name on and send out Wednesday night. Two pages, one bottom line with dates — adopt Cooldown+, offer enrollment-time self-binding instead, cap or rebate the fees, refund the round-trippers, or leave the whole thing alone — plus reasons. Two of the seven voting are a facilities manager and someone from HR, so no jargon, and please don't just announce that cyclic preferences are irrational and stop there, because some of these members know exactly what they're doing. If the strongest thing against your own recommendation is real, put it in plainly; the finance guy will find it anyway. And no footnotes or citations — we can't look anything up and nobody would read them.
