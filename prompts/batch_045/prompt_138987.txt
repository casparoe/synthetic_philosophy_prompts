ok so I need help with something for the classics club zine (we print like 30 copies and mostly just read them out loud to each other, but whatever). We just did Plato's Laws — his last dialogue, no Socrates in it, some old Athenian guy talks for twelve books about the laws of a made-up colony — and we spent the entire session on the drinking bit. As I understood it, his idea is weirdly two-sided: on one hand he's basically saying wine is useful because it makes you a worse version of yourself, so if you drink in a supervised group with a sober person in charge, you get a cheap and safe way to find out what someone is really like, like a fire drill for your character. On the other hand he also says it's a kind of medicine that heats the soul up and makes it soft and bendable, like iron in a forge, so that a person who's gone stiff and sour can be worked back into a better shape — and that's supposedly why the older men get to get properly wrecked at the Dionysia while anyone under eighteen isn't allowed any at all. The thing I keep arguing about with my friend Priya is that those two uses seem to fight each other. If the drink is what makes you pliable, then whatever comes out during the party isn't a discovery about you, its just the drink making a new person for a few hours — and then the note about that person goes in your file forever. Idk if that's a real objection or if I'm missing something obvious.

Anyway I'd rather write a short story about it than an essay, so here's the setting I've got so far. It's a boarding school, or maybe a summer program, twenty-ish years from now, and there's this new drug (call it "temper" or "anneal" or whatever you want) that for about four hours drops a person's self-control and makes them really suggestible — it loosens them up emotionally the way Plato thinks wine does. The school uses it two ways: there are the Friday "evenings," supervised by teachers who don't take it and who write a short note on each kid afterward, and there are the one-on-one sessions where a mentor walks you through some habit you're supposed to fix while you're soft. My main character is a sixteen-year-old who is very good at looking calm and is kind of proud of being the together one, and she volunteers to help out at the evenings even though she's not allowed to take it yet. I want the middle of the story to be one evening where something happens with one of the kids that could genuinely be read two ways — was that who he always was underneath, or did the drug just manufacture somebody who'd do that? — and I really don't want the story to settle it. I also want one character, maybe a teacher or maybe her dad who's a bit of a hardass, who thinks the whole program is disgusting on principle: that you're treating people as raw material to be hammered into shape instead of as people you could just, you know, ask things. But give that guy actual good arguments, don't turn him into a cartoon. And somewhere in there I want a moment where the softening definately does help somebody who needed it, because that's the part of Plato's thing that I think is kind of sweet and I don't want to just dunk on it for 900 words.

Rules I care about: nobody stands up and explains the philosophy, no ancient Greece cosplay, and I'd rather it not be one of those stories where the school turns out to be secretly evil — the people running it should basically mean well, which makes it harder to judge them. First person, from the main character's point of view, around 900 to 1300 words, and end on something unresolved.
