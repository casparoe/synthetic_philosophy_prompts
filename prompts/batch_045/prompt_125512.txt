I am writing up my position on weakness of will for a seminar and would like the set of commitments I have ended up with audited as a package before I defend it. Roughly where I have landed:

(1) Akrasia is real. An agent can act intentionally against her own all-things-considered better judgment, and this is not to be explained away as ignorance, compulsion, or a mere change of mind.

(2) What makes such an action akratic is synchronic conflict: at the moment of action the better judgment is still in place and the worse desire wins anyway. I reject the Socratic story on which judgment simply follows desire.

(3) The empirically grounded model of this is present-biased, hyperbolic or beta-delta, discounting: the weight of the near option spikes as it approaches, preferences reverse, and this predicts both why failures cluster near the reward and why commitment devices help.

(4) The judgment that makes an action akratic has to be the agent's own judgment at the time of acting. An earlier self's plan is not automatically authoritative over the later self, and I am suspicious of cross-temporal paternalism.

(5) Installing commitment devices and self-binding is rational for agents like us, and doing so does not excuse the akratic action: the agent remains fully responsible for what she does.

(6) Two case verdicts I want to keep. First, flat cases: both options are available right now, with no delay differential at all, as when you choose between the nourishing meal in front of you and the delicious one in front of you, both eaten immediately. These are genuine akrasia, even though present-biased discounting has no temporal gradient to work with. Second, the fully sophisticated agent who pre-commits and then pays to undo the binding is acting akratically, not merely executing a plan she foresaw.

I do not know where the trouble is, so please do not just tell me which commitment is least fashionable. Go through the set and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look that way. For each real tension, say what exactly has to be given up and what that costs me elsewhere in the set, and rank the tensions by severity rather than flagging them all equally. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and say explicitly if any apparent tension dissolves on inspection once the commitments are stated carefully.
