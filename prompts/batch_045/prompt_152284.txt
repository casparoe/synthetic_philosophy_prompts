I manage a small outreach team at a housing charity — six caseworkers, about sixty clients — and every Monday I get maybe twenty minutes to decide who gets the discretionary hours that week: visits above the bare minimum, someone to sit with a client at a tribunal, extra phone calls. Right now I do it by gut, which I think mostly means I favour whoever I got on with last week.

I started reading abot care ethics because it seems to say something I half-believe already: that what I'm allocating isn't a service but a relationship, and that relationships aren't interchangeable — half our clients have had three different workers this year and it's plainly worse for them, but I can't say why in a way our funder accepts. It also seems to say the whole point is responsiveness to this particular person, which sounds actively hostile to having a checklist.

So I want both: a procedure I can run in fifteen minutes on a Monday that two of my workers would apply and come out roughly the same on, but built out of care ethics rather than out of utilitarian triage or a points system. What questions do I ask and in what order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where does it stop and say no, where does it hand the decision back to me because it's a values call rather than a factual one? Please also be straight with me about whether turning care into a procedure guts what's distinctive about it, and where the procedure will systematically get things wrong.

Run it on three real-shaped cases: (a) Mrs A, isolated, no family, gets on well with her worker, slowly improving — against Mr B, big family network, deteriorating fast; (b) a client who's hostile and has missed four appointments; (c) a client we've visited weekly for eight months with no measurable improvement, under a funder who pays for outcomes. I suspect the honest answer on (c) is keep going and I really don't want to hear that, so say it if that's what it says.

Don't just lay out the options — commit to the version of care ethics you're using, say where a defender of a different version would break with you, and tell me which step is really just my judgement wearing a clipboard.
