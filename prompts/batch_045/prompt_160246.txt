I'm a PhD student in machine learning and I run a fortnightly AI safety reading group — about sixteen people, mostly ML PhD students with a few philosophers, ninety minutes a session. Next week we are doing the debate proposal (two debaters, incentivised to disagree, a judge who picks a winner), and I want the group to feel the obfuscated-arguments worry rather than nod at it. Design a runnable game for that session.

The mechanic I have in mind, which you should develop or replace if you have something better: each debater must present their case as a numbered chain of steps laid out in whatever order they choose, and is allowed to make claims about what their opponent would be forced to concede if the judge opened a given step. The judge gets a fixed budget — say at most two levels of nested sub-debate and five minutes in total — and any step not opened stands as conceded. One debater is privately handed the true answer to a question whose answer is determinate but expensive to verify (a question about a two-page handout everyone has in front of them, or a conditional-probability puzzle); the other is handed a false answer and coached to place the false step as deep in the chain as they can, keeping every other step impeccable and the whole thing well-formed. Scoring is tokens on a visible scoreboard, no money; the losing side writes up the session notes. If it helps, add a second round in which the judge may require any step to be split into two smaller steps at the debater's own cost, so we can see whether that rescues honesty or merely relocates it.

What they should come away seeing: that the failure is not that truth is hard to state but that locating one bad step inside an otherwise clean argument can cost as much as answering the original question, so the binding constraint is the judge's budget and competence rather than the debaters' honesty.

Give me the full rules and materials, the debrief mapping specific moments onto the parts of the objection, an honest list of what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (I expect at least judging by style, and someone in the room who knows the answer and leaks it) with how to handle each, and a frank note on what the game cannot reproduce — I take it the main gap is that in the real proposal the debaters are far more capable than the judge while here everyone is human, so the exercise shows that obfuscation is possible but not what happens as the debaters scale.
