I work in psychophysics and use signal detection theory daily: d′, response criterion, log-likelihood ratios, ROC and isosensitivity curves, payoff matrices, optimal criterion placement, and the standard point that sensitivity and bias can be estimated separately once ground truth across many trials is available. My philosophy training stops at two undergraduate courses. I am trying to get clear on the dispute among classical invariantism, epistemic contextualism, and pragmatic encroachment — the view that what is at stake for a subject can raise the bar for whether she knows — and I want it in my vocabulary rather than theirs.

Restate each of the three positions as faithfully as you can in signal-detection terms, applied to the familiar case pairs in which the same evidence and the same true belief seem to suffice for knowledge when nothing hangs on being right and to fall short when a mistake would be costly. For each view, say what plays the role of the sensitivity quantity, what plays the role of the decision variable, what sets the threshold, and which of these the view claims is fixed by the meaning of "knows" rather than supplied by the situation.

Then the residue report, which is the part I care about. Three things in particular. First: in signal detection theory a criterion shift trades false alarms against misses symmetrically, whereas stakes in these cases seem only ever to raise the bar — so is encroachment really an operating-point shift, or a one-directional threshold raise, and does the difference carry any weight? Second: sensitivity and criterion are identifiable only given ground truth across trials, while for knowledge ascriptions the only data are intuitions about cases; does that make the three views notational variants of one another, a parameter-identification failure rather than a substantive dispute, or is that impression an artifact of the translation? Third: the argument that knowledge is what licenses action and assertion seems to have no counterpart in a framework whose outputs are just decisions under a payoff matrix.

For each item, say what you had to fudge to make it fit, and close with a verdict on the residue: does it point to something signal detection theory cannot express, or to something the epistemologists' debate was never clearly about?
