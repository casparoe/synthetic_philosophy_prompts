Two bits of machinery I want you to genuinely use, not just nod at:

(1) **Fixed-channel vs. free-channel counterfactuals.** When a belief is formed by reading off an instrument's output, "if p were false, would S still believe p?" is ambiguous. Fixed-channel: hold the input pinned — same vitals, same labs, same scan — and vary only whether p is true downstream. On that reading almost any classifier with a nonzero false-positive rate comes out insensitive, because the same numbers are still on the screen in the nearby p-false worlds. Free-channel: let the input vary too, as it would in the nearest p-false worlds; then the numbers change and the model doesn't fire, and sensitivity survives. Which reading is the right one for tracking, and does the answer change if the clinician knows the model's error profile?

(2) **Deception zones and transmitting entailments.** For a method M and a proposition p, call M's *deception zone for p* the nearby p-false worlds in which M still yields belief that p. Say an entailment p→q (known to S) *transmits for S* iff every nearby world in M's deception zone for p is still a q-true world — i.e. every near false positive for p lands somewhere q still holds. Goldfinch→bird transmits (the near false positives are other birds); hands→not-a-brain-in-a-vat doesn't.
