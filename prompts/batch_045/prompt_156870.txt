So I've basically become one of those people who thinks 'collapse' is mostly a historiographical artifact — that when we say the Bronze Age, the Classic Maya, or Rapa Nui 'collapsed,' we're really just marking the point where the things archaeologists can see and happen to value (palaces, literacy, long-distance trade, monument building) stop happening, and then the story carries on under a name we can't be bothered to learn. Transformation all the way down, no real failures. I want to know what would actually count against that.

Please rank things honestly, decisive versus merely awkward, and tell me which ones someone who already holds my view would concede as counting against it — not just what a collapse person would say. A few specifics I want dealt with:

1. My usual move is a test I made up, call it the second-generation test: a polity genuinely collapsed if within two generations nobody in the same landscape is transmitting any of its administrative or ritual practices and nobody is claiming it as an ancestor worth naming. Does that test actually expose me to anything, or does it just bake my conclusion into the definition?
2. Which of the three canonical cases do I hold most safely and which should worry me? I have a feeling Rapa Nui is my strongest and the Maya my weakest, since Maya people are obviously still there — but maybe that's exactly backwards and the persistence of the people is the whole point rather than a problem.
3. The bit that actually bothers me: if literally every discontinuity can be redescribed as transformation, have I qualified myself into unfalsifiability? And if so, is that a defect in a claim like mine, or just what an interpretive claim about long-run history is?
4. Would it change anything if the losses were things the people living through them also clearly valued — safety, diet, lifespan, being able to feed kids — rather than just the stuff archaeologists like? Or does my view quietly depend on ignoring that distinction?

The thing that would move me most is a case where participants wrote it down as an ending in their own terms and meant it: say what a text like that would have to say to count, and whether I'd be entitled to shrug it off as elite self-drama.
