Compose a leaked internal memorandum of roughly 800 words, formatted as a consulting deliverable — a To/From/Date/Re header, numbered sections, and a short closing FAQ — in which the change-management firm Wexler & Vance reports to the board of a mid-sized logistics company on the first year of the Objective Standard: a workplace policy of meeting every employee with the purely objective attitude and of systematically retiring blame, praise, gratitude, resentment, and apology from company life.

The satire must be aimed at the Strawsonian position itself, and the comedy has to come from getting that position right rather than from inventing features for it. Build the memo out of genuine elements of Strawson's essay Freedom and Resentment: the objective attitude as one in which a person is seen as something to be managed, handled, trained, cured, or merely taken precautionary account of, rather than as someone to be addressed; Strawson's own remark that with a person wholly within that attitude one may fight or negotiate but cannot quarrel or reason with him, and can at most go through the motions of doing so; the reactive attitudes as responses to the quality of will an action manifests, so that indifference can wound as much as injury does; the difference between excuses, which mitigate resentment while leaving the participant stance intact, and exemptions, which suspend that stance altogether; the exempting categories Strawson himself lists — infancy, insanity, the morally insane, the person unfortunate in his formative circumstances — which under the Standard are quietly extended to the entire payroll; the self-reactive attitudes of guilt and obligation, and their objective-attitude counterpart in which one treats oneself as a thing to be managed; and Strawson's central claim that the truth or falsity of determinism leaves these practices untouched, which Wexler & Vance advertises as the Standard's greatest selling point, its neutrality as between the compatibilist and the incompatibilist.

Fill the memo with invented but plausible apparatus of your own devising: a form for the Provisional Suspension of Participant Standing, a performance metric such as Resentment Events per Thousand Interactions, a coaching track for employees still reporting gratitude, and a Severe Cases Panel that adjudicates the residual cases. The numbers should all be excellent and the piece should half-notice what has been lost: the reconciliation queue has been empty for eleven months, the apology and thank-you functions have been decommissioned for want of demand, a backlog of unfiled complaints has no procedure because no one can any longer have been wronged, and exit interviews are impossible since there is nothing left to resign over. Give the FAQ at least three entries, including one from a director who has read the source text and asks whether Strawson did not himself say that a wholesale adoption of the objective attitude is not something we can seriously envisage, our commitment to ordinary interpersonal relationships being part of the general framework of human life — to be answered with a reference to an appendix that declines to address it; one raising the objection associated with Wallace and Watson that a reactive-attitude account can catalogue which attitudes we have without saying which we are justified in having — to be answered, deadpan, with some version of the claim that the firm makes no assertion about whether the retired attitudes were justified, only about whether they were efficient; and one on determinism, to be answered with the assurance that the Standard is compatible with both determinism and its denial.

The voice throughout is sincere, consultative, and entirely deadpan: no character should notice the loss, and the piece should never state its own moral or wink at the reader. Include at least one quotation from a floor supervisor who is clinging to resentment and is being coached out of it. A reader who knows the essay should recognize that the author understands what is being satirized and should feel, without being told, why the participant stance cannot be dismantled on purpose.
