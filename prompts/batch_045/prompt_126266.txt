we did the private language stuff in my Wittgenstein seminar last week and I sort of get the argument when the tutor lays it out, but every time I try to explain it to my housemate it comes out as either behaviourism (nobody really has feelings, introspection is a myth) or else as the boring point that memory is unreliable. Both are misreadings, and I can feel that they're misreadings, I just cant say why.

So: could you write me a short story that makes the argument felt rather than stated? What I have in mind is someone who sets up a private notation for a particular recurring inner episode and logs it over years. Pick something less second-hand than pain or the colour red — maybe the specific vertiginous lurch that comes just before a migraine aura, or the exact quality of an urge to swallow. Make the character scrupulous: controlled circumstances, close attention, a private act of inner pointing or concentration before every mark.

Then the notation should fail intrinsically. Not 'they misremembered', not 'they were secretly muddled', not a twist about brain damage or a secret diary. After years of entries the marks have drifted into two distinguishable families, and there is no fact of the matter about whether the feeling changed, or the use of the mark drifted, or it was always two feelings all along. And crucially, nothing further they could do in private — not concentrating harder, not repeating the private pointing — could settle it, because whatever is going to seem right to them just is right.

Rules: no narrator explaining the philosophy, no descent into madness, no 'it was all in his head' ending, no character quoting Wittgenstein, no 'language is social therefore the self is an illusion' moral. I'd rather the ending let the reader see what would have turned those marks into a real notation — being able to be wrong, teaching someone else — without ever saying it. And if you can manage it: my sense is the interesting upshot isn't that the diary was worthless but that it was something, roughly like a cry or a groan, only not a notation, and I'd like to see whether a story can actually show that difference. Oh, and it shouldnt be depressing for the sake of it.
