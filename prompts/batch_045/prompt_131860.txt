I build those spinning POV LED displays as a hobby — the ones where a single row of LEDs paints a whole picture by rotating — and I got into an argument on a forum about whether the image is really out there in the light or whether the brain is filling most of it in. Someone dragged in the color phi thing: you flash a dot at one spot, then a differently coloured dot a bit further along a split second later, and people report a single dot travelling between them that changes colour partway across, before the second dot has actually been shown. From there I found the part where the two explanations get called Orwellian and Stalinesque, and I'm stuck on what looks like an easy way to just settle it that nobody seems to mention: get the subject to hit a button (or read it off an EEG, or zap them with TMS) the instant they consciously see the change, and see when that lands. If the response is fast enough, the rewrite in the Orwellian story would have to come after the press, so it'd be dead — which makes the whole thing just an empirical question about when the experience got laid down. And if it comes out Stalinesque, doesn't that definately mean consciousness is running late, that we're all living a fraction of a second in the past, and that "what it's like right now" is really about a moment ago? I'm sure I'm missing something, because everyone writes as if no experiment could decide this, which sounds like terrible philosophy of science to me. What am I getting wrong?
