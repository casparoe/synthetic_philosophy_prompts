hey — hoping you can help with something i'm kind of drowning in.

about four months ago i became the "impact lead" (not my choice of title) at a small nonprofit that runs job training and placement for 18–25 year olds who've been out of school and out of work for a while. roughly 60 participants at a time, 9 staff. until recently our funding was basically a block grant and we reported whatever we felt like reporting. now two of our three funders have moved to paying per documented outcome, and the board has decided staff bonuses — small, but real for people on our salaries — will be tied to the same numbers. so suddenly the numbers matter a lot, and I'm the one writing them.

which means I've been reading about Goodhart's law and specification gaming and getting steadily more paranoid. every metric i come up with, i can immediately think of three ways a smart, tired, underpaid caseworker who wants the bonus would hit it without helping anyone. i don't want to build something that quietly turns us into a placement mill that cream-skins the easiest participants and parks the hard ones — and i also don't want to freeze and report nothing.

what i need is an actual procedure i can run, not a lecture. concretely:

- i'll be adding or revising metrics maybe three or four times a year, and i can give each one a couple of hours, not a week. the staff who have to record the numbers are not stats people. the board has to sign off, and anything in a funder contract i can only work around, not delete.
- three ideas i've started using and want built into the procedure:
  - the gaming surface — the handful of cheapest ways to move the number without moving the thing we actually care about. this has to include ways of moving the denominator: who counts as enrolled, who gets quietly "paused", who we just don't sign up in the first place.
  - negative-space metrics — goals phrased as the absence of something bad (no participant evicted, no complaint upheld). these feel safe and I think they're the opposite: you can hit them by making the bad thing invisible rather than by preventing it.
  - the shadow measure — something slow, expensive and annoying that we check against the fast metric every few months to see whether the two are still tracking each other at all.
- please give me: the questions in order; what counts as a good enough answer to each one; a risk rating for the finished metric (say 1–5, with what each level means); the point at which the procedure just says no, drop this metric; the point at which it hands the question to a person because it's a values call and not a fact call; and a category for "fine for us to learn from, dangerous to pay on or publish."
- i also want a step about who is allowed to see the number and how often they see it. i have a feeling that belongs in here somewhere and i can't work out where.

then run it on three of mine:

1. a bonus for each participant still in a job 90 days after finishing the program.
2. training hours delivered. i love this one — it looks fantastic in the board deck and it costs us almost nothing to report — and i have a bad feeling about precisely that.
3. "no participant becomes homeless during the program." this is already written into a funder contract, so i can't remove it; the most i can do is decide how we verify it internally and what we do with what we find.

and somewhere in there, tell me what the procedure can't do: the ways i could follow it perfectly and still get Goodharted anyway. also, if you think my three ideas are the wrong three, say so and swap one — but only if you actually use the replacement in the procedure itself and not just in a footnote.

sorry this got long, and thanks.
