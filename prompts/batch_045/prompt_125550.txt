I work as the staff researcher for a mid-sized city's Public Memory Advisory Panel, a body of nine people who have to produce a written triage protocol for contested street names, building dedications, and monuments on public land. We are not short of opinions, but we keep talking past one another: every discussion collapses into the same single axis, roughly whether the thing should be judged by what it was erected to do or by what it now does to the people who have to walk past it, and then it stalls. What I actually want from you is a decision tree, not an essay: an instrument that takes a contested commemoration and routes it, by way of plain questions, to a named position about what should be done with it. I need it to be usable by a panelist who has read no philosophy of commemoration at all, so every branch node has to be phrased as a question about a concrete case or a claim one could simply agree or disagree with, never as a question that already contains the name of the view it leads to.

Please build the tree from the logical space rather than from the list of options we already know about: give me the branching questions first, say which combinations of answers are jointly available, and only afterwards place the familiar positions into that space, naming the ones that have names and marking unnamed the ones that do not. Six branch questions at most, so something in the range of eight to twelve leaves. These should include, as leaves: unconditional retention; retention with added context such as a plaque or an accompanying counter-monument; removal to storage; removal to a museum; relocation to private or less prominent ground; destruction; and one option our clerk has been pushing that I want taken seriously rather than laughed off, under which no public honoring is ever permanent — every monument and name is treated as leasehold, re-authorized or allowed to lapse at a fixed interval, with the burden of re-argument on those who want it kept. Each leaf should carry its name, one sentence naming its most awkward commitment, and one sentence on what new fact would move a case off it. Two further constraints matter for our purposes. First, some combinations of answers will presumably be empty, and I want each empty leaf noted and diagnosed: is nobody there because the combination is incoherent, because it is coherent but unattractive, or because it is merely unoccupied and untried? Second, where two leaves cannot really be separated by the questions I have let you use, say so explicitly rather than inventing a distinction; I would rather know that the tree bottoms out than be handed a fake branch.

Four of our live cases have to be usable in the tree, either as branch-node illustrations or as tie-breakers between rules that otherwise agree. A bronze statue of a state governor, erected in 1931, who both built out the public hospital system and personally drove a compulsory sterilization program; the hospital is still named after him. A granite monument to the pioneers with no individual named on it, which a local nation reads as a celebration of dispossession and which many residents read as a monument to their own grandparents. A school name whose upkeep and signage are paid for from a family endowment whose terms require the name to be maintained in perpetuity, so the options are partly constrained by a dead donor's money rather than by us. And a courthouse-lawn obelisk that is now listed as a protected historic structure and hosts the nest of a protected bird species, so that removal is not on the table at any price — the tree has to say something sensible when the leaf it would otherwise reach is simply unavailable. Finally, our panel has two notions it uses loosely and that I would like placed rather than assumed: use-capture, our term for a monument whose meaning is now set mostly by what people do at it — where protests begin, where skaters gather, where photographs get taken — rather than by its dedication; and commemorative drift, the fact that what a monument is about can change while the object stays exactly as it was. Tell me where each sits in the tree, whether it belongs at a branch node or only as a consideration at particular leaves, and add, if you think it is needed, a case of your own that would separate the two rules you find hardest to tell apart.
