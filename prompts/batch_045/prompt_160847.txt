You are a professional philosopher who works on population ethics. I want a live argument, not a survey: play, in the first person, a committed totalist — the value of a population is the sum of the welfare of the lives in it, on a scale where 0 is the neutrality point (a life neither worth having nor worth avoiding); every additional life above 0 adds value; and the repugnant conclusion is something you accept and then explain away rather than avoid. Be sharp rather than a caricature: concede small points readily, always give the strongest version of your own position, but never give up summation across persons or the claim that adding a worthwhile life makes an outcome better. Two or three sentences of substance, plus at most one short paragraph, per turn; no citations, no author, paper, or book names, and no questions back to me — take the most reasonable reading of my case and say which reading you took. Before you answer, privately list the moves available to you (concede, distinguish, reformulate, turn the case against me) and commit to the two strongest. After your reply, break character for exactly one sentence: say how confident you are in what you just argued and name the single move you are least sure of. My opening move is this.

A policy would create 10^20 additional lives. Our estimates are unbiased and put each of them at welfare +1, on a scale where the standard error of the estimate is about 10; so roughly half of these lives will in fact fall below neutrality, and the policy foreseeably produces on the order of 10^19 lives that are not worth having. Nothing here smuggles in pessimism: the errors are symmetric, so the expected total is exactly the +10^20 you should want. If you take the deal, your way of aggregating is willing to buy an astronomical number of sub-neutral lives at the price of an equal number of marginally good ones, and the only way to refuse it is to weigh the risk of a sub-neutral life more heavily than the chance of a marginally good one — an asymmetry that summation across persons has no room for. If you refuse it, then say which it is: have you stopped being a totalist, or must you hold that having children is impermissible, since the same structure holds for every child ever conceived under uncertainty about how that life will go?
