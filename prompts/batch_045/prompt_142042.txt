A few of us in a reading group put together the following argument and now it looks like it proves way more than any of us believes. Setup: what you have most reason to do is whatever you would advise yourself to do after a process of idealization -- full information, no akrasia, no distorting mood or fatigue, vivid imagination, whatever is on the list. Call this the advice model.

Provenance condition (P): advice counts as advice *to you* only if the concerns it appeals to are continuous with your actual motivational set. Otherwise its just a better person issuing orders at you.

(1) Idealization is supposed to strip out distorting influences on deliberation: ignorance, weakness of will, transient mood, framing, bad affective forecasting.
(2) But most of an adults standing concerns are partly downstream of exactly those influences. You picked the career partly from bad forecasting about what the days would actually feel like; the political loyalty partly from two or three vivid images; the grudge partly from being tired and hungry when it formed.
(3) If you strip out the influences you strip out their downstream products too, since the products are causally downstream of them. Call this contamination.
(4) So either the idealization stops short of the concerns and never corrects anything deep -- it only ever tells you how to get what you already wanted, so it cant be the source of any reason you didnt already have -- or it reaches the concerns and, by (P), the output isnt advice to you at all.
(5) Either way there is no such thing as a deep reason you would only recognize after better deliberation.
(6) But then the move "you would want this if you thought about it clearly" is never legitimate: not from a friend, not from a therapist, not from a hypothetical-consent contract, not even from yourself-next-year about yourself-now. Which is nuts, because sometimes its obviously correct.
(7) So (1), (3), or (P) has to go.

I want to keep (1) and I really want to keep (6). That pushes me toward giving up (P), but once I do I cant see what makes the idealized advisor's output normative *for me* rather than just good advice from a stranger who happens to share my name. Which step do you reject, what does rejecting it commit me to, and does your fix actually stop (6) from following or only make it follow more quietly?
