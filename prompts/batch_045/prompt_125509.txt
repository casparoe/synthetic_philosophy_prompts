Write the "Notice to Contributors" page for an invented evolutionary-biology journal — call it the *Journal of Conjectural Biology* — that has taken falsificationism seriously enough to write it into its copyediting policy. Format it as headed sections of numbered clauses, something a submissions portal would actually show. Aim for 800–900 words.

Background you may assume and should not pause to explain: Popper held, at least in his earlier and harsher formulation, that Darwinism is not a testable scientific theory but a metaphysical research programme — that "survival of the fittest" comes close to being empty if fitness is defined by actual survival, and that the theory cannot say in advance what observation would refute it. He also held that a metaphysical research programme is not for that reason worthless: it can be rationally criticized and can fruitfully guide research. He later softened the verdict, conceding that natural selection is testable even if not testable in the strictest sense. The journal has read both the earlier and the later Popper, and has decided between them.

I want the comedy to come out of the rules themselves rather than out of editorial wisecracks. Please work in, as policy:

- a mandatory definition of fitness that nowhere mentions survival, on the ground that otherwise the central claim says nothing;
- a "Prohibition Statement" nominating an observation that would count as a refutation, together with the further requirement that the nominated observation not yet have been made, since the journal does not accept predictions retrofitted onto data already in hand;
- two submission tracks — Type-T (testable) and Type-M (metaphysical research programme) — in which Type-M papers are still published, are still described as rationally discussable and valuable for the research they organize, but are typeset in a separate section, may not claim to be supported by evidence, and carry a fixed boilerplate note;
- a ban on the vocabulary of confirmation: no "support," no "confirm," no "probability," no "Bayesian"; contributors must write of corroboration and must report a degree of corroboration rather than any ratio of likelihoods;
- a Boldness Statement, scored on the board's own invented Content–Risk Index, since a hypothesis's scientific worth rises with how antecedently improbable it was;
- a rule about conventionalist stratagems: auxiliary hypotheses must be registered before the test, and hypotheses filed afterward count as immunizing moves;
- a recommendation that singular historical hypotheses — of the form "the ornament of taxon X evolved because..." — be recast as situational analyses, stating the organism's problem situation and deriving the trait as the rational solution, since a singular historical narrative is not a law and so not testable;
- a short editorial note on which Popper the journal follows: the earlier, harsher one, on the ground that the later recantation is the less falsifiable of the two claims and hence, by its own criterion, the less scientific.

Two structural requirements. First, build in a quiet reductio: applied in good faith, the rules restore to Darwinism exactly what Popper said it lacked — a fitness notion defined independently of survival plus a nominated refuter — and the journal must not notice this, or must register the resulting awkwardness as a defect in a particular submission rather than in the rules. Second, include one indented "Author's appeal" of roughly 120 words from an invented professor of evolutionary biology protesting a rejection, with the editor's reply set beneath it in brackets, deadpan.

Also include one worked example: an invented taxon and trait of your own choosing, given first as a submitted abstract and then rewritten to comply.

Constraints: bureaucratic voice, no winking, and the document should never say in its own voice that any of this is absurd — a philosopher of biology should be able to tell what is being ironized and should also feel the target is being treated fairly, while a reader who knows nothing about demarcation can still follow it. Do not put invented quotations in the mouths of real philosophers; quotation marks should enclose only the words of invented people. Nothing here should require a bibliography or any outside source. American English throughout.
