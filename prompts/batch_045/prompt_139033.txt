I've got no background in philosophy, I just fell down a rabbit hole a few months ago reading about how people explain what makes something something a *group* does, as opposed to just several people doing similar things near each other. Anyway, I've been arguing with a friend about it and I need a case.

Here's the disagreement as I understand it. There seem to be two broad ways of explaining a "we":

- The plans version (I think this is Bratman, sorry if I've got the names mixed up): what makes it a we is that each person has their own individual plan, the plans mesh with each other, and this is out in the open between them — common knowledge, everyone knows and knows that everyone knows. Nothing else is needed: no agreement, no new obligations, no one being in charge.
- The commitment version (I think this is Gilbert): what makes it a we is a joint commitment — some mutual readiness to be in it together, however minimal — and that's what creates the standing to say "hey, you're not holding up your end," obligations to each other that wouldn't exist if you were all just doing your own thing.

My friend says these two are basically notational variants and that in any real-life example they'll give the same answer. I want a case that shows them coming apart, and I want it to come apart in the direction he says is impossible: the commitment view has to say yes — that's a genuine we, they really were jointly committed, each of them would be entitled to call the others out — while the plans view has to say no, there's no shared intention here, because the individual plans don't actually lock together (or there's no common knowledge, or no mutual responsiveness — whichever is the weakest link, you tell me).

Constraints on the case:

- Ordinary adult life, and one paragraph. A workplace, a shared hallway, a food co-op, a pick-up line, a stairwell, whatever. No committees, no companies, no tribes or hunts, no crowds or mobs, no animals, no sci-fi.
- Nobody is in charge, nobody was assigned a role, nothing is in writing, there's no vote, no contract, and no explicit moment where they state the plan out loud and agree to it. Small talk is fine; an agreement is not.
- Everyone involved is a normal, reasonable adult. Nobody is lying, nobody is unaware the others exist, nobody is being coerced, nobody is confused about the facts or irrational.
- Here's the part I think makes it hard: they actually pull it off. The joint thing gets done, and from the outside it looks like smooth teamwork. So you can't get the plans view to say no just by having the thing collapse or the coordination fail.
- The verdicts have to fall out of what each view actually requires. If a character in the story says "we're in this together," or denies it, that can't be what's doing the work — that would just be stipulating the answer and my friend would laugh at me.

What I want back:

1. The scenario, one paragraph, nothing added after it.
2. Then go through each view condition by condition and say which conditions are met and which fail, and what verdict each view gives. Flag honestly if you had to tilt or stretch one of the views to get the verdict I asked for.
3. A plain-language check: what would someone with no theory at all say about the case, and which view does that side with?
4. Which single feature of the case is doing all the work? Then delete that feature and show me that the case collapses into a boring one where both views say yes.
5. The strongest thing a defender of the plans view could say in reply — either to get their verdict back or to argue the case doesn't count — and your honest read on whether it's a real reply or just digging in.
6. A couple of sentences on whether the idea that the group itself has attitudes over and above the members helps either side here.
7. And if you think the direction I asked for genuinely can't be done, say so and give me the closest near-miss you can plus the reason it fails. Also tell me plainly if you cheated anywhere — I'd rather bring a case with a known weak spot to the group than get torn apart there.
