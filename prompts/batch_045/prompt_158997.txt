In a graduate seminar on inconsistency-tolerant accounts of normative conflict, a visitor argued that a legal code containing two mutually inconsistent statutes need not thereby license arbitrary decisions, and was answered with the line "from a contradiction, anything follows" — the intended point being that once you admit a single inconsistency you no longer have a logic but a licence to derive anything whatsoever, so any view that tolerates true contradictions is committed to triviality.

I want that sentence taken apart. Lay out the distinct precise claims it could be expressing: at least a semantic one about what follows from an unsatisfiable set in classical logic; a proof-theoretic one about the rule that licenses an arbitrary conclusion from a contradiction; a claim about which further rules — disjunctive syllogism, modus ponens — do the real work in recovering explosion once that rule is withdrawn; a claim about what an agent who accepts a contradiction is thereby committed, or entitled, to believe; a claim about what reality would have to be like if some contradiction were true; and a practical or decision-theoretic claim, for instance that an agent who assigns positive credence to a contradiction, or who acts on an inconsistent corpus, is committed to arbitrary conclusions or is exploitable in a way that makes the position unliveable in practice. Add readings I have not listed.

For each, say whether it is true by stipulation, substantive and defensible, substantive and contested, or false; which of them a defender of paraconsistency or dialetheism can concede without cost; and which single reading the objector actually needed in order to get from one conflicting pair of statutes to anything-goes, and whether that reading survives scrutiny. I am drawn to the tolerance claim and want to know how much of the slogan I am obliged to swallow in order to hold it; the decision-theoretic version is the one I would find most damaging if it could be made to stick, so tell me whether it can where the purely logical versions cannot.
