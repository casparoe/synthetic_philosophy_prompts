I spent thirty-five years as an experimental physicist, most of it on precision measurement: error budgets, calibration chains, blind analyses, and the standing worry that an apparatus is reporting a signal that is not in the sample. In retirement I have been reading about illusionism about phenomenal consciousness and its critics, and I would like the debate restated in the vocabulary I actually trust. I know the standard labels, so please do not spend time re-explaining qualia or the hard problem.

Please do three things.

First, translate illusionism into the vocabulary of measurement. I mean the claim that phenomenal properties are not instantiated and that our convictions about them amount to a systematic misreport of some internal monitoring process. Restate it using observable, latent variable, apparatus, channel, systematic error, artifact, calibration against an independent standard, resolution limits, and theory-mediated measurement. Then restate the principal anti-illusionist resistance in the same vocabulary, with equal care.

Second, give me the residue report. I want to know which claims survive the translation without strain, which survive only after being bent to fit, and which cannot be stated in my vocabulary at all. I am particularly interested in what becomes of the claim that the illusion is not merely a false belief but involves something it is like to undergo it. Does that map onto anything in measurement terms, or is it precisely the sentence for which my vocabulary has no slot?

Third, I have a provisional taxonomy that I want tested rather than flattered. I distinguish three grades of instrument failure: a phantom reading, where the apparatus emits a signal with no corresponding state of the system; a cross-talk reading, where a real state of the system is reported in the wrong channel or under the wrong variable name; and an aliased reading, where a real state is reported but its structure cannot be represented in the apparatus's output space at all. Tell me which of these, if any, illusionism is committed to; whether the choice among them is forced by anything in the position or left open by it; and whether the standard realist objections target one grade in particular while the illusionist arguments support another. If my taxonomy is the wrong instrument for this job, say so and say what should replace it.

Finally, give me your verdict on the residue. Is whatever fails to translate a limitation of a vocabulary built for third-person, publicly checkable measurement, or is it a sign that the notion of a phenomenal property is doing less work in this debate than both sides suppose? I would rather have the honest answer than a diplomatic one, and I do not need references or a reading list.
