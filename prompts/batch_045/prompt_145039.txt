The position I want restated has two parts.

1. Commensurability: the pleasant and painful episodes of a life, or of a population, sit on one signed hedonic scale, so that a pain of magnitude −n is in principle exactly cancelled by a pleasure of +n, and only the net matters.
2. A lexical threshold on suffering: below some intensity T, pleasures and pains trade off in the ordinary scalar way; at or above T, no quantity of pleasure, in anyone, anywhere, however large, compensates. Suffering at or above T has lexical priority over all positive welfare.

To pin down what I have in mind, translate these two cases as well. The Matched Offset case: an intervention pairs every episode of severe suffering with an episode of intense pleasure in a different person, scaled so that the two are equal in magnitude and opposite in sign by the best available introspective and cross-modality measurement. In the Delayed Same-Subject variant, the offsetting pleasure occurs in the same person an hour later. Run each case once with both intensities below T and once with both far above T, and say what each version of the view implies and what your translation of it implies.
