im in an epistemology seminar this semster and we've spent like a month on skepticism and brain-in-a-vat stuff — Descartes, Moore, closure, and then that Putnam-ish move where an envatted speaker's words for brain and vat don't reach real brains and vats, so the sentence can't even come out true (i'll dig up the actual reference myself later, don't worry about citations at all). For the final paper i want to do something a bit different and read The Truman Show (the 1998 Jim Carrey one — you obviously know the plot) as a skeptical-scenario paper, and i have a half-formed angle i want you to either make work or tell me is garbage.

Here's the angle. Truman looks like the inverse of a brain in a vat: real body, real-ish physical surroundings, but the other people are actors following a script. So the doubt isn't about whether there's an external world, it's about whether there are any other persons in it. I've been calling this person-skepticism as against world-skepticism, and my suspicion is that basically all the anti-skeptical machinery we've read is calibrated to the world case and slides past this one: the Putnam-style semantic argument is no use to Truman at all (his words for sun, ocean, beer do hook onto real things), and running closure in the Moorean direction gives you something like "if I know my wife loves me then I know she isn't an actor," which feels way less compelling than the hands version and maybe for an interesting reason — the defeater isn't about my perceptual access to objects, it's about the attitudes behind another person's behaviour.

Second thing, and i think this is where you could be most useful: is the film even a skeptical hypothesis in the relevant sense? The whole point of the vat setup is that the bad case is evidentially indistinguishable from the good one, that's what's supposed to generate the argument. But the show definately leaks — the studio light that falls out of the sky, the car radio narrating his movements, his wife half-turning to camera mid-argument. Truman can find out, and does. So maybe there's a real distinction between a skeptical hypothesis and a deception hypothesis, and the interesting move is to ask what happens if we "repair" the film — imagine a seamless, well-run show, no glitches — and whether the fact that this one isn't seamless is the film quietly taking a position (you can know, because deception leaves traces) or just the film needing a plot. Relatedly: does the ending commit it to something like a safety or sensitivity condition on knowledge, where Truman only counts as knowing once he's actually out? Because that would make the film an odd ally of the skeptic rather than an answer to one. And Sylvia tries to tell him early on and he doesn't believe her, which might be a point about testimony — whether being told is even capable of giving you this kind of knowledge — or might just be plot.

So what I'd like is a reading: what the film dramatizes about skeptical doubt that the standard vat setup can't, two or three scenes you'd hang the argument on (just describe them, no need for exact wording of anything), the strongest version of the reading and the strongest objection to it, and an honest verdict on where it starts to distort the film — my worry is that the movie is really about surveillance, commerce, and authorship of your own life, and that i'd be bolting epistemology onto it. Also: is person-skepticism genuinely a distinct skeptical problem, or does it collapse back into plain external-world skepticism the moment you notice the sky is a painted dome?
