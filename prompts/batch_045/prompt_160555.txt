I'm finishing a PhD in philosophy, and the last session of the consciousness reading group I run this term is on the combination problem for panpsychism. I've been asked to close it with a short poem, roughly 20–24 lines, and I'd like the formal device to carry the argument rather than decorate it.

The idea: fix one ordinary macro-experience — say the taste of cold coffee on a station platform, or hearing one's name called across a crowded room — and hold it as a refrain that returns word for word, unchanged, at the end of each stanza. Meanwhile the stanzas redescribe that same event at successively lower levels: first in first-person phenomenological terms, then neurophysiological, then the vocabulary of fundamental physics (mass, charge, spin, spacetime relations), and finally in the last stanza as nothing but causal-structural relations among items with no qualities specified at all. The refrain's stubborn sameness against the thinning diction should do the work of the palette problem — the reader should feel the macro quality as unearned by everything the micro story supplies. And because no stanza ever finds a grain at the lower level that the single taste could be assembled out of, the grain problem should be working too: the refrain is insistently one thing, the underlying description insistently many.

Two things I'd ask you to get right, since the audience will include people who work on this. The failure I want staged is specifically the failure of subject-summing — one subject out of many — not a general mystery about consciousness, and the closing lines should leave the macro subject standing there as something that would have to be composed, bonded, or inherited rather than added up, with how left open. So: no mysticism, no "the universe is awake," no "we are all one," no summing that quietly succeeds, and no resolution. It should also read as a poem rather than as argument in rhyme, and someone in the room should be able to point to which lines are the grain problem and which are the palette problem.
