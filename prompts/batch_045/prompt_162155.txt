Two members of a reading group I sit in on got stuck on Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment: the inside of a skull is pictured as a kind of billiard table (the careenium) strewn with tiny magnetic marbles (simms) that clump into larger, slower clusters (simmballs), these clusters standing in for symbols; the question posed is which level shoves which around — do the simms push the simmballs, or do the simmballs push the simms? I am paraphrasing their exchange below. I have no side in it, and I would like a verdict rather than a sympathizer.

(1) Julian: The setup answers the question by stipulation. A simmball is just a cluster of simms, so saying that simmballs push simms around is saying that large slow conglomerates of marbles shove individual marbles around — ordinary macro-to-micro causation, like a crowd jostling a person. But the interesting question is whether a symbol's meaning is what does the causal work, and the thought experiment never touches that.

(2) Marta: That misreads the dialectic. The puzzle of how a mere symbol could move matter arises only if you picture symbols as extra items hovering above the substrate. Once you notice that any symbol has to be realized as some physical structure, the question loses its sting: the answer is that both push, at different levels of description. The point is therapy for a confused question, not a proof of a mechanism.

(3) Julian: Therapy that quietly loses the patient. Delete the interpretation — suppose nobody maps the simmballs onto anything — and the careenium's behavior is unchanged. So what drives it is shape, mass, arrangement; the meaning is projected by an observer. A skeptic grants everything Hofstadter says and concludes that semantic properties are causally idle. Unless you can exhibit a difference the interpretation makes, you have restated the skeptic's conclusion poetically.

(4) Marta: Then make the careenium harder to wave away. Suppose its dynamics answer not to raw shapes but to the relational structure of simmball configurations, so that the arrangement realizing P together with the arrangement realizing if-P-then-Q reliably produces the arrangement realizing Q, across a wide range of contents. Now the system tracks inferential relations among what the symbols are about, and your syntax/semantics contrast blurs: a structure playing exactly the right inferential role is most of what content consists in, so there is no remaining property of aboutness for the careenium to be insensitive to.

(5) Julian: You have moved the bump, not removed it. Any systematic reinterpretation — relabel the simmballs, swap water for its chemically different twin-earth counterpart — preserves inferential structure perfectly and leaves the careenium's behavior identical. So nothing in it is sensitive to which contents its symbols have; it is equally well described as driven by any of a family of isomorphic symbol systems. That some structure or other does the work is the skeptic's claim, not yours.

(6) Marta: Perhaps the honest conclusion is deflationary. Whether the simmballs push the simms around is not a further fact about extra causal powers; it is a fact about which level of description carries the explanation. If the simmball-level story predicts and explains what the simm-level story cannot, then the simmballs are causes in the only sense worth wanting — and your demand for an interpretation-independent fact of the matter is just the demand that a high-level description look like a low-level one.

(7) Julian: Then whether this system is one whose symbols push its substrate around depends on the interests of whoever is describing it, and there is no objective fact about whether a mind here does anything at all. I count that as a cost rather than a solution.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the seven moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with a brief justification for each rating, and say who is more right overall. Then say what, if anything, both of them are missing — is there a consideration neither raises that should change how the exchange is scored, or a way of reformulating the original question that makes their disagreement evaporate?
