Had a three-hour argument with my flatmate last night (hes doing a Buddhism module and kept insisting that Confucian role ethics is just no-self with extra steps, and then my friend who works on care ethics said it is care ethics with a nicer vocabulary) and I am still slightly annoyed about it, which is maybe why I am writing this now. I am a TA and I have got a single fifty-minute section next Tuesday with about twenty first-years, nearly all non-majors, who will have read one short and quite sympathetic secondary piece on role ethics and nothing from the primary texts; possibly one of them will have heard of the bit where Confucius is asked about an upright man and the son who covers for his father, so I cannot count on that being fresh or on them having it right. Do not explain role ethics to me. Plan how I explain it: what has to be in the room, conceptually, before the view even sounds like a claim rather than a platitude, and in what order, and why each step depends on the one before it.

Here is what I want specifically. First, the carrying example: I have been playing with a case where an adult daughter visits a parent with advanced dementia who no longer recognises her, so that nothing reciprocal is left, no contract, no affection on one side, and yet the daughter is still obviously in a role that has content. Tell me whether that should carry the whole session or whether something else should, and why not the two obvious candidates: the father who stole a sheep (I worry that turns the hour into a fight about nepotism and I lose the constitutive claim in five minutes) and the be-a-different-person-at-work-and-at-home example (which, after last night, I now think actively invites the misreading that roles are things you wear). I also made up two devices and I want you to place them or kill them: the empty-room version, where we strip a particular person of every role and ask what is left over, and the failed-role case, where a father has not spoken to his daughter in ten years and I ask whether he is still a father on this view and what the role is even made of if so. Say which of those helps, which backfires, and exactly where in the sequence it belongs.

Then the misconception map, which is the part I am most worried about. The ones I expect: roles as costume or social script; role ethics as virtue ethics in Chinese vocabulary (my flatmates version of this was that it is basically character without the unity of a character); relativism, or whatever-your-society-happens-to-expect; and the reform objection my friend hammered on, that if a person just is their roles then there is no standing ground from which to say a role is corrupt and should be reshaped or abolished, so the view collapses into conservatism. For each, tell me the point in the sequence where it should be defused rather than answered, and whether defusing it requires something I should have put in earlier, since that is really what I am asking about. Also tell me what I should refuse to explain at this level and say out loud that I am refusing: I am guessing the process-cosmology background and the scholarly fight about whether this is a reconstruction of the early texts or a modern philosophical build on them. And give me one thing to ask or have them do at the end that would actually show me they have the constitutive claim rather than the ability to repeat the word role, something that fails loudly if they only have the platitude version.

Last thing: be honest about where your plan is most likely to break. Tell me which ordering choice you are least confident in, what would change your mind about it (a different example, a different opening move, a class that shows up with more or less background than I think), and what I would see in the room in the first ten minutes that should make me abandon your sequence and do something else instead.
