I am writing a handout for an upper-level metaphysics seminar on causation, and I want it to function as a classification instrument rather than a survey. Half the students come in having read al-Ghazālī's attack on necessary connection in the *Incoherence of the Philosophers* — the fire and the cotton, the claim that what we call a law of nature is simply God's habitual way of acting — and half come in having read Hume and Lewis, with no Arabic and no theology. So the tree has to sort by what someone is committed to about cases and claims, not by which tradition or label they like: a theist and a non-theist should be able to walk down it and land on the same leaf, and no node may ask whether God exists or use the words "occasionalism," "dispositionalism," or "regularity theory."

The deliverable is a decision tree with eight branching nodes and nine to eleven leaves. Please construct it from the logical space first: state the questions and the combinations of answers they permit, and only then locate the actual historical positions in that space. Each branch label should be a claim or a concrete case that genuinely separates the positions and that a student with only the seminar readings could answer.

The distinctions that must do the branching work are these:

1. "There is no necessary connection" is ambiguous, and I want it split across three separate nodes rather than one. Is the denial modal (the effect could have failed to follow), explanatory (the cause does not make the effect intelligible), or ontological (nothing is produced or transferred at all)? Someone who denies only the first must not land on the same leaf as someone who denies all three.
2. What the decree covers. Separate the view that every individual effect is created directly, the view that a single stable "habit" is decreed once and covers all instances, and the view that creatures are given natures that demand their effects — the reading on which al-Ghazālī leaves room for created natures and some readers take him to hold. Give each its own leaf.
3. Counterfactuals. Include a node built around the sentence "if the cotton had been put in the fire, it would have burned," with options distinguishing truth in virtue of the stability of the habit, truth in virtue of a nature given to fire, truth merely as a prediction with no modal backing, and failure to be straightforwardly true because nothing in the situation would have made the cotton burn.
4. Human action as a test case rather than a label: present the Ash'arite doctrine of "acquisition" as a scenario — my arm rises and I intended it to rise — and ask what the agent's own power contributed, with options that separate theists and non-theists identically.
5. A modern case, so the tree is not only about fire: the spontaneous decay of a single atom, where no prior condition settles which outcome occurs. Use it to separate views that require a determinate nature or a sufficient prior condition from those that do not.

Required leaves: Ash'arite occasionalism; the created-natures reading of al-Ghazālī; Malebranche's occasionalism via general volitions, with a note on whether it really comes apart from the Ash'arite leaf or only differs verbally; Avicennan necessary emanation; Aquinas's instrumental secondary causation; Humean regularity; nomic necessitation along the lines of Lewis or Armstrong; a powers or dispositionalist view; an interventionist account of what causal claims mean; and at least one leaf standing for a coherent combination nobody occupies.

Two further requirements. Flag any pair of leaves your questions cannot separate, and say what extra question would be needed. For each unoccupied but coherent combination, write one sentence diagnosing whether it is empty because it is incoherent, because it is unattractive, or because it is merely unexplored.

Each leaf should carry the name of the position and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Below the tree, add a short list of the disputes you found to be merely verbal. Assume no footnotes, no citations, and no further reading: the handout has to stand on its own.
