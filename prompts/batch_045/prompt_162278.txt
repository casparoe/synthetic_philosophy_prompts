Hi — I'm a PhD student in history working on comparative memory policy, and there's a bundle of practices I keep bumping into that I can't get a handle on philosophically. Wondering if someone in philosophy can help me reconstruct what they presuppose.

Here's the practice, as I see it:

(1) Reparations programs pay money per victim, and they do it by sorting victims into categories — imprisonment, forced labor, lost property, damage to health, loss of a relative — and giving each category a different sum, on top of a finite fund that forces the per-person amounts to be set relative to each other. So somebody somewhere decided that a year of forced labor is worth n times a year of property loss. The schedule is cardinal: you can add and multiply with it.

(2) The very same settlements, apologies, and the museum/education apparatus around them insist the atrocity is incomparable, unique, beyond comparison. Memorial museums decline cross-case ranking; curricula that mandate teaching one genocide rarely mandate another; the professional norm in my field is that we don't rank.

(3) Inside a single case, though, quantification is obsessive. Name-recovery projects, death-toll revisions, fights over whether the number is 5.7 or 6 million — moving a toll by a few hundred thousand is treated as momentous and changes the memorial, the funding, and the moral rhetoric.

(4) And in law the categories don't track scale at all: a killing campaign aimed at destroying a group gets the gravest label even when the numbers are relatively small, while far larger indiscriminate killing gets a lesser one — and yet the same system screens cases at the admissibility stage partly by scale (as I understand it) and treats victim counts as aggravating at sentencing.

So: what theory of comparing atrocities does this bundle presuppose? I want the implicit one reconstructed, not the one that goes on a placard. Concretely:

- Does the practice actually need cardinal, inter-case comparability of evils, or does it only need intra-case aggregation plus an exchange rate between harm-categories that could be set politically without any claim at all about comparative badness?
- Is it compatible with real incommensurability across cases? I keep getting told that ordinal-only comparability — you can say A was worse than B but not by how much — is enough to run all of this, and I don't believe it, because the reparations schedule needs magnitudes, not just an ordering. Is there a version of incommensurabilism that survives the fact that finite funds force exchange rates anyway?
- Where is the actual inconsistency, if there is one? Is the taboo on ranking doing real work — protecting something worth protecting — or is it just flatly inconsistent with (1) and (3)?
- What would have to change if we went fully cardinal, some kind of aggregate-harm accounting across atrocities the way cost-effectiveness people do with DALYs? What breaks in the memorial and education practice, and does anything break in the law?

Two gadgets of my own, which I'd like you to push back on rather than politely accept:

(a) Ordinalism-plus: the view that cross-case comparison is legitimate but only ordinally, and that everything in the practice can be reconstructed on that basis. Tell me whether it really can, or whether the money part recieves a hidden cardinal structure that nobody admits to.

(b) Decision density: total harm divided by the number of people whose individual choices were causally necessary for it — roughly, how much ruin per decisive agent. My suspicion is that the impulse to call some atrocities worse, and the practice of prosecuting only those most responsible, are covertly tracking decision density rather than total harm, and that's why the rankings people give feel like judgments about culpability even when they're dressed up as judgments about victims. Is ranking by that a ranking of atrocities at all, or of agents? Is it less obscene than ranking by body count, or more?

Please also be explicit about the difference between what the practice strictly presupposes and what it merely fits well with. My worry is that several quite different theories would license this exact bundle, in which case the practice is underdetermined and I can't read a theory off it for my chapter.
