I've been asked to contribute a short position statement to an interdisciplinary workshop on the moral status of artificial systems, and before I commit anything to print I would like someone to audit the package of views I've accumulated rather than to argue with me about any one of them. The trigger was a seminar I teach on the metaphysics of mind: a student pressed me on a case involving a complete but extremely slow and spatially scattered realization of a human-like organization, and I found that my answer consisted entirely of gestures at clauses I hold for unrelated reasons. That made me suspect that some of my commitments are doing metaphysical work I have not acknowledged, and that at least one of the tensions I feel is spurious. But I cannot locate the fault line from the inside, and I would rather not discover it from the podium.

Here is the set as I would actually state it, with labels for your convenience. (C1) Whether there is something it is like to be a system is fixed by the abstract causal organization that the system realizes; nothing about the stuff, the speed, or the spatial distribution of the realizing states matters in its own right. (C2) Realization is not trivial: a system realizes a computation only if its causal transitions counterfactually support the specified transitions, so that it would have made them had it occupied the other specified states, and the support must not be accidental — a lookup table with the right input-output profile, or a system that merely happens to match the transitions on the inputs it actually receives, does not realize the computation. (C3) I reject the flicker-style reply to the arguments that replace a system's components one at a time with functional stand-ins: there is no respect in which consciousness can dim or flicker while the functional organization is held fixed. (C4) Both phenomenal intensity and moral significance come in continuous degrees with no sharp cutoff; very simple systems have very small but nonzero amounts, and I do not think there is a tidy boundary to be found anywhere along the complexity gradient. (C5) A complete realization of a human-like organization carried out by a large population of people, each responsible for one unit and passing messages on paper, with the whole system updating about once a day, is not a subject of experience at all and is not a moral patient. (C6) Sufficiently faint sentience generates no moral reasons whatsoever — not reasons too small to act on, but none — even though, by (C4), there is no sharp point at which it begins to generate them. (C7) Where a system robustly realizes many computations, the morally relevant one is the one that figures in the best causal explanation of the system's capacities; moral status is assigned to that computation alone and is not summed across all the computations realized. (C8) The very-large-numbers style argument is sound: an enormous population of systems, each with only a trickle of sentience, can together generate moral reasons of considerable weight.

What I want is an audit, not a survey. I have read the standard moves and can reconstruct them; please do not give me a bibliography, tell me which of these is the majority view, or restate the literature. Go through the set pairwise and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look as though they are, and for each genuine tension what would have to give and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the set. I am particularly suspicious of my anti-skeptical clauses — the robustness condition in (C2) and the selection rule in (C7) — because each was adopted to block a different reductio and I have never checked whether they are the same kind of claim as (C1), or whether either smuggles in an extrinsic or interest-relative condition. I also want to know whether (C5) is something my other commitments can deliver at all, or something I am asserting on the side, and whether the combination of (C6) and (C8) is really coherent or only seems so because I have never written down what "no reasons" was supposed to mean. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
