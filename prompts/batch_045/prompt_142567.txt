At work we're merging two databases and I've ended up in a dumb-sounding argument that I now think might not be dumb. We have to decide whether a "committee" is a thing in our data model — its own ID, its own properties (a name, a charter, a budget, minutes, the ability to hold a bank account and to have unresolved business) — or whether it's just a relation between people and time periods. My coworker Priya reads a lot of metaphysics on the side and keeps ending the arguement with the same line: "there are no committees, only people arranged committeewise." She thinks that settles three things: no committee IDs, no committee-owned money, and when the last original member rotates off over the summer it's simply a different committee.

I'm pretty sure that sentence is either obviously true and totally useless for our purposes, or it's cheating somewhere — but I can't say precisely where. Someone told me this is a version of the "special composition question" in metaphysics, which I'd never heard of, so I guess there's a literature.

Can you give me a menu of the different precise claims that sentence could be making? Stuff like: there are no composite things at all, only the tiny bits; there are composites but they aren't part of the basic furniture of the world; "committeewise" quietly smuggles the committee back in, so the paraphrase isn't as innocent as it sounds; composition happens no matter how things are arranged, so there are also committee-plus-my-coffee-mug things floating around; composition only happens when there's a single thing going on, like a life; whether these people compose a committee depends on us and our concepts rather than on the world; in the messy middle cases there's just no fact of the matter. Those are my guesses — give me the actual options, including ones I haven't thought of, and say which of my guesses are just wrong.

For each one: is it plausible, or is it a triviality wearing a costume, and — the part I actually care about — would it really get Priya her three conclusions, about IDs, about who owns the money, and about identity across a total membership change? My own hunch is that the money question is really about rules somebody wrote down and not about what exists, but I can't make that sharp.

The cases we keep hitting: two committees with exactly the same members but different charters; a committee where every single member has turned over but the bank account and the backlog of unfinished business carried straight on; a committee on the org chart that has literally never met; and a subcommittee that is just the parent committee minus one guy.

I dont need citations or reading lists, I'd never follow them up. Just the distinctions, in a form I can basically read out loud in our next meeting.
