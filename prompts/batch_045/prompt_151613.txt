hey — I keep going in circles on this colour phi thing and would like you to sort me out, but please go well past the one-paragraph version you see everywhere.

Setup as I understand it (correct me if its wrong): flash a red dot on the left, then a hundred milliseconds or so later a green dot a bit further right, and what people report is a single dot that slides across and changes colour partway through. The weird part is that they seem to see the colour switch *before* the green dot has actually been shown, so there's this question of what was in consciousness when.

The two standard moves:

- Stalinesque: at the early time there was no conscious episode of a moving dot changing colour at all. The brain waits for the second dot, then composes the whole moving-and-switching episode and backdates a memory of it, so it feels as though you saw the change earlier than you did.
- Orwellian: there really was an earlier conscious episode (red dot sitting there, no motion yet), and then once the second dot's information is recieved the record of that earlier episode gets edited, so that it now seems to you as if the change happened mid-flight.

What I want from you:

1. Compare the two properly, rather than just restating them. What exactly does each have to posit — about memory, about what a "time of experience" even is, about whether there is a determinate fact of the matter? Where does each get squeezed? And deal head-on with the obvious complaint: if both stories generate the same report and the same introspection, what could make one true and the other false? Is the difference between them doing any real work, or is it basically verbal?

2. Now take a third picture seriously and tell me where it breaks. The idea: there isn't one timeline of conscious events, there are several — one per attribute, say motion, position, colour — and each timeline "closes" (stops being revisable) at a different moment after stimulation. The closing time is what gives a content its date-in-experience. So in colour phi the motion content might close earlier than the colour content, and the episode has no single "when": its motion is dated at one time and its colour at another. On this view neither of the first two is right, because both assume a single timeline along which a content is either there-then-edited or absent-then-fabricated.
