I write genre fiction — small-town mysteries, nothing fancy — and there's an argument that goes around my writing group in circles, and I'd rather have someone audit the machinery of it than hand me another opinion.

The move everyone reaches for is Darwinian: story elements are like genes (or memes, depending on who's talking), the book is the organism, the market or "the culture" is the environment, and the tropes that survive are the ones that replicate best. It gets used for two opposite conclusions. My agent-ish friend uses it to say: write the trope, the market has already run the experiment, the three-act shape and the amateur sleuth and the missing kid won because theyre fit, arguing with that is like arguing with natural selection. Another friend uses the same picture to say the opposite: a cold virus beats a sonnet because it spreads, not because it's any good, so the dominance of those tropes tells you nothing about whether your book is good — write what you want, don't be a host.

Two things I've noticed that I never hear in the analogy version, and that I suspect change it:

First, alot of tropes seem to replicate through writers, not readers. The missing kid isn't mainly something readers demand; it's something that makes a draft possible. It hands you a clock, a suspect pool, a reason your detective is personally invested, and four chapters that more or less write themselves. So it's "fit" at the production stage — it survives because it's easy to build with, not because it's pleasurable to read. Call it draftability if you want.

Second, some of them look like they're selected as signals rather than as experiences: the trope is how a reader finds the book in a catalog at all, closer to a keyword or a cover font, and its value goes up the more other books and readers already use it. That's not really a thing that happens to genes, as far as I can tell.

So: can you give me the actual correspondence table — what's the replicator, what's the vehicle, what counts as a generation, what's mutation, what's the environment, what fitness is measured in — and then where the mapping breaks? Which of the breaks matter for the two conclusions my friends are drawing (write the trope / ignore the trope), and which are just noise? Does either conclusion survive once writer-side selection and the signal thing are on the table? And if the gene picture is the wrong one here, is there a better comparison — I keep thinking of a language, or a standard like a shipping container, but I can't tell if that's just another metaphor doing the same smuggling. American English please, and sorry if I got any of the biology words wrong, I keep typing replicater.
