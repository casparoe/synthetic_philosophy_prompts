At our reading group last week we were arguing about whether a large neural translation system that produces fluent, accurate target text thereby understands its source, or whether, in the spirit of Hofstadter-style worries about the shallowness of statistical translation, it is only manipulating symbols without any grasp of what they are about. Two people then used the same one-liner to opposite ends. One said, roughly, you cannot translate without understanding, and concluded that a system which really does translate does understand, so the question is settled. The other accepted the line and concluded the reverse: since the system plainly does not understand, what it emits is not really translation but translation-shaped text.

Please set out, as a numbered menu, the precise theses that line could be expressing, ordered from the near-trivial to the strongest, and give each a verdict: definitional or near-analytic, substantive but defensible, substantive and doubtful, or straightforwardly false. For each, say which of the two uses above it supports, if either, and whether the reading that licenses the first person's modus ponens is even the same reading as the one that licenses the second person's modus tollens.

I would like each reading tested against three cases, because I suspect they pull them apart: a formally specified transpiler that maps one programming language into another, which everyone is willing to call translation and nobody thinks requires understanding; a human court interpreter who renders a legal formula accurately between two legal systems while having no grasp of what it does in either; and a system trained only on X–English and Y–English parallel text that nevertheless translates X into Y accurately without producing English at any intermediate stage, so that whatever mediates its work is language-neutral.

Please also say which reading people in this debate are most likely to have in mind, and whether that reading actually does the work they need it to do. End with a final line of the form: Verdict: <number or label of the strongest reading that survives your verdicts and does the work either debater needed, or none>.
