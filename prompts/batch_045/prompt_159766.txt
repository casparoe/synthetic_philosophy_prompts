I work as a machine-learning engineer and read experimental philosophy as a hobby, without any formal training. For some years I have been following the literature on the Knobe effect, and I want a diagnosis of why it seems to be stuck rather than a survey of it.

As I understand the state of play: Knobe asked passers-by about a company CEO who is told that a new programme will increase profits but also harm the environment, and who replies that he does not care about the environment and only wants profit. The programme runs and the environment is harmed: about 82 percent of respondents said the CEO harmed the environment intentionally. In the matched case where the side effect is helping rather than harming the environment, only about 23 percent said he helped it intentionally. Since then, as far as I can reconstruct it, the competing accounts fall into a few families. One says the moral valence of the outcome genuinely penetrates the folk concept of intentional action, so this is a discovery about the concept itself. A second explains the asymmetry as a matter of construal: a bad side effect is read as a cost the agent must have weighed and accepted, whereas a good one is read as an incidental bonus, so the agent counts as willing to bring about the former but not the latter. A third treats the result as pragmatic rather than semantic: respondents are not answering the question they were asked but the question they take to be worth asking, which is whether the agent is blameworthy or praiseworthy, and the intentionality verdict is a way of delivering that answer. A fourth reports that the split partly reflects stable individual differences in how people interpret the word intentional, some hearing it as near enough to foresight and others as requiring aim. A fifth, more recent strand places the effect inside a broader pattern in which a concept has both a descriptive and a normative dimension. Every account has supporting data; no account appears to have retired any of the others.

Let me add my own wrinkle, because it is the reason I am asking. I rewrote the vignette for my colleagues at work. A team ships a recommender whose evaluation shows a four percent gain in watch time and a known increase in the amount of outrage content users are shown; the lead says he does not care about outrage one way or the other and only wants the watch-time numbers; the model ships and outrage content rises. Asked whether the team increased outrage content intentionally, my colleagues reproduce the asymmetry. But when I made the grammatical subject the system rather than the team, willingness to say intentionally collapsed for both the bad and the good case, while the blame response preserved the full asymmetry. My hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis, is that the dispute is stalled on an overloading error. Ascription of intentionality may be less like a report of an internal flag on the agent and more like a public endpoint whose specification is to return an accountability verdict; the endpoint is typed in terms of persons, so it throws when handed a system, and callers who want the blame result simply route around it through a different call. On that reading the effect is neither an error in folk judgment nor a discovery in semantics, and the camps are not disagreeing about one thing: some are documenting the behaviour of the endpoint, some are arguing about what its documentation should say, and some are asking what it is for.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Verbal, over which sense of intentional is in play? Substantive but empirical, about which mechanism produces the asymmetry? A dispute about which responses count as data and about who counts as a competent speaker? Or two questions under one heading, and if so, which one is the interesting one? I would also like your view on the second-order fight that this finding got dragged into, about whether experimental results of this shape should change how philosophy treats appeals to intuition, and whether that fight is the same dispute one level up or a separate one. Please argue from what each side actually says rather than listing positions, tell me whether my endpoint hypothesis survives contact with the cases, and tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis, or else why no progress should be expected. I am not asking for references, citations, or a reading list, and I would rather have your reasoning than anyone's bibliography.
