I keep going round in circles with my reading group on the wrong kind of reasons problem for fitting-attitude accounts of value, and I want a diagnosis, not another survey. The set-up we always come back to: the FA story says something is admirable just in case it's fitting to admire it, and a demon threatens you with something nasty unless you admire it — now you've a reason to admire the demon, but not the sort of reason that could ever make the demon admirable. Everyone in the group agrees on that judgement, everyone agrees the FA theorist needs to filter down to the right kind of reason, and then we spend the rest of the session fighting about the filter: reasons given by the object's features versus reasons given by the state of having the attitude, buck-passing style moves, flat denials that the demon gives you a reason at all. Twenty-odd years on and it feels like nobody has shifted.

The bit I want you to get into is this. Take a second demon: it threatens you unless you admire Vera, who is independently and genuinely admirable. Here a wrong-kind reason points at something that really does have the property, so the FA account gets the right verdict for what looks like the wrong reason. That makes me think the row isn't about which verdicts come out at all, but about grounding versus extension — some people want a filter that tracks the right extension of admirable, others want the reasons that do the explanatory work, and the same cases look like data or like noise depending on which question you think you're answering. My other hunch is that it's a proxy war about the order of explanation: if reasons are primitive and value is analysed in terms of them, the filter has to be drawable in non-evaluative terms or it's circular; if value comes first, the filter is trivial but FA has stopped being a reduction. Also the same pattern seems to show up for belief and for action, which makes me doubt it's really about value.

So: which is it — different questions under one name, a verbal fight about what a reason is, bedrock intuitions nobody can argue for — or something I've missed? And given whatever you think it is, what would actually count as progress, or should we just expect to be having this exact conversation in another twenty years?
