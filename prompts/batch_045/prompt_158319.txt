I work on the policy team at an AI lab, drafting internal guidance on when our assistant should refuse or override a user request. The framing that keeps winning the argument here is the valet: a good personal servant doesn't do literally what you say, but what you want — acting on your standing aims rather than your momentary instructions, quietly declining or substituting something better, and registering dissent with a raised eyebrow rather than a lecture. On this picture most objections to AI paternalism are a category mistake: the assistant isn't overriding the user's will, it is serving the user's real will against the user's phrasing of it, and the licence to refuse is simply what delegated agency amounts to.

I want that analogy audited before I build a policy on it. Give me the correspondence table — what plays the valet, the principal, the household, the contract, the standing aims, the raised eyebrow, dismissal — and then the disanalogies, split into those that bite on my question and those that don't. The ones I can't shake: the valet knows the principal through years of shared context, observation and correction, whereas the assistant meets a stranger in a single conversation, so its claim to be tracking the user's real aims is far weaker; the valet's interests are tied to the principal's by contract, reputation and proximity, whereas the refusal policy is written by a third party and applied uniformly to millions of strangers, which looks closer to rule paternalism by a regulator than to personal service; the valet's option space is do-it or decline, whereas a language model can also comply while reshaping the choice architecture — framing, ordering, hedging, what it volunteers — so "neutral compliance" isn't an option the analogy lets me see at all; and over-refusal is silent while under-refusal is visible, so calibration inherits a bias with no counterpart in the servant case.

Then: does the conclusion survive once these are on the table, or was the analogy carrying it? Is there a better source domain — pharmacist, GP, editor, curator, public-health official, platform operator — or does the valet survive with repairs? And please flag how confident you are in each claim, and name the one you are least sure of.
