I'm putting together a one-page handout for a cog-sci reading group on the language of thought hypothesis, and I want a decision tree rather than a list of names. Start from the claim that cognition is computation over representational vehicles, then branch on questions someone could answer without already knowing which position the branch leads to — e.g. whether those vehicles have reusable constituents or content is holistic, whether structured representations show up in perception, imagery and navigation or only in "central" reasoning, whether an implementation in distributed or vector representations still counts as language-like, whether the vehicle can be (sometimes) a sentence of your public natural language, whether the format has to be propositional or could be map-like and still compositional, whether thought needs any language at all to do its job (the anti-representationalist leaf). Six to eight branch nodes, seven or eight leaves, no branch labeled with a school's name, each leaf with a label and one sentence on its most awkward commitment, or "unnamed" if nobody actually holds it.

Two things I care about more than coverage. First, I think the deep split is over which job a language of thought is being asked to do: explaining systematicity and productivity, making misrepresentation and content possible at all, or accounting for what conscious reasoning is like from the inside. So put that question near the top and let views come out LOT-ish for one job and not for another. Second, keep the messy bits visible: name the pair of leaves your tree can't really seperate, and mark two or three answer combinations that nobody seems to occupy, with one sentence each on whether they're empty because incoherent, unatractive, or just unwritten.
