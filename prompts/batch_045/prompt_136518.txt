I'm in the second year of a philosophy PhD and trying to settle on the spine of a dissertation project. The area I keep returning to is what I'll call lexical threshold negative utilitarianism: the family of views on which there is some intensity or quality of suffering such that no quantity of pleasure, however large and however widely distributed, can outweigh a single instance of suffering above that line, while below the line pleasures and pains trade off against each other in something like the ordinary way. I have roughly fifteen months before I have to file a proposal and about that long again of funded time after it, working essentially alone, with decent formal tools but no empirical ones and no collaborators in psychology or neuroscience. So the question I want help with is an allocation question: given that budget, and given that the point of the project is to get clear about whether this family of views is defensible rather than to map it, which of the open questions in the neighborhood deserves to be the central one, and which deserve to be set aside? I'm not asking for a survey of the area or for orientation reading; I have enough grip on the standard dialectic to skip that. I want a defended ordering.

The candidates I've been turning over, roughly: first, the commensurability question itself — whether pleasure and pain are magnitudes on a single scale at all, and whether the lexical theorist can even motivate the view by appeal to their incommensurability, given that a lexical priority relation seems to be a comparative relation and so to presuppose at least comparability. Second, a problem I haven't seen handled and that I think might be the real crux: whether the threshold is supposed to be a fixed point on a scale of phenomenal intensity or is indexed to the subject's own hedonic baseline and history. If it's fixed, it appears to generate strange verdicts about subjects whose baseline has shifted through long adaptation, in both directions; if it's indexed, it starts to look like an ordinary prioritarian concern for the worst off with a lexical edge bolted on, and I'm unsure what the lexical edge is then doing. Third, the question of what the currency of intensity is: whether the relevant magnitude is momentary felt intensity, its integral over time, or something like the degree to which the experience occupies the subject's attentional field, and whether those can come apart for the same episode in a way that changes the verdicts. Fourth, the level question: whether the threshold operates on token experiences, whole lives, populations, or entire worlds, and whether lexicality at one level is even consistent with aggregation at another. Fifth, the epistemic question of how one would ever locate the threshold well enough for the view to guide action, and whether the difficulty here is an objection to the axiology or merely to its decision-theoretic application. My supervisor's view is that the axiology is basically settled once you say where the threshold sits and that all the live work is epistemic; I suspect the opposite, that the epistemic problem is a symptom of an unresolved axiological one, but I hold that loosely.

What I'd like from you is a prioritization of these, plus anything important I've left out, with the reasoning made explicit rather than asserted. For each: what it is upstream of, how tractable it is in fifteen months for someone doing armchair and formal work rather than empirical work, how neglected it is relative to how much rides on it, and how much would actually change downstream if it were answered one way rather than another — including whether the answer would change what a convinced lexical theorist should do, or only what they should say. I'd also like you to flag which of these are hollow or overrated, in the sense that they look deep but their answer wouldn't move anything, and to say what kind of result, over the next few years and from wherever it might come, would move items up or down your ranking. Concretely: if you were in my position, which two questions would you build the proposal around, and in what order would you work through them? Please write in American English.
