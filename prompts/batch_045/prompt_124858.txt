Hi — I'm on the board of a small nonprofit thrift store that also runs a two-year transitional employment program: about a dozen trainees at a time spend most of thier day sorting donations, deciding what's sellable, and pricing it. Revenue has fallen for three years running, and the program is the first thing we'd have to cut. We've been offered a lease on an automated sorting and pricing system — cameras plus a scanner that identifies an item and suggests a price, with a person confirming or overriding it. The rep says it'll roughly double throughput and lift revenue 20 to 30 percent. The trainees are split: some hate the sorting room and would rather be on the counter or the repair bench, others say sorting is the only place they feel useful and they're scared of being "retrained" into babysitting a machine. Our mission statement says we give people the dignity of work, and nobody on the board can say what that comes to when the work in question is something a machine does better.

The thing I keep going in circles on: I want to separate a job's output from a job's point. I've started calling a task load-bearing if the person doing it would be genuinely diminished without it, and filler if it only keeps them occupied — but I also think keeping people on filler tasks out of pity is its own insult, and I'm not sure I'm allowed to judge which is which for somebody else. Definately tell me if that distinction is garbage.

So: draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote. Recommend one course — adopt, don't adopt, or adopt with conditions — with actual dates, not "over the next year." Give the strongest version of whichever option you didn't pick, argued properly rather than strawmanned. And where the whole thing bottoms out in a disagreement the numbers can't settle, say that plainly instead of pretending the spreadsheet decides it.
