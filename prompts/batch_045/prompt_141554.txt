I grabbed a pop-philosophy book at an airport and it has a short chapter on the statue and the clay that I'm not sure its fair to the actual debate. It goes more or less like this:

"A sculptor buys a lump of clay on Monday and calls it 'Lump'; on Tuesday he shapes it into a statue and calls it 'David'. David and Lump have exactly the same parts and fill exactly the same space, so there is really only one object there — David just is Lump. But they also seem to differ: Lump existed on Monday and David did not, and if you squash the whole thing into a ball on Wednesday, Lump survives and David does not. That is the problem of material constitution. The solution most philosophers now accept is the constitution view: the lump constitutes the statue without being identical to it, which means David is not a second object over and above the lump, but the very same thing picked out by a different sortal — roughly the way Hesperus and Phosphorus are one planet under two names. And there is no threat from the Kripkean claim that identity is necessary, because 'statue' and 'lump' are sortal terms rather than proper names, so they don't rigidly designate and the identity can hold here without holding in every possible world. The only real alternative — that David and Lump are two numerically distinct objects sitting in the same place — has been largely given up, since it violates the principle that a single region of space contains a single material object."

My hunch is that "constitution without identity" is supposed to be a different position from "there's only one object there," but the author clearly knows more about this than I do, so I can't locate what's gone wrong or how much it matters. Can you go through it and tell me what someone actually working on material constitution would accept, what's a harmless simplification, and what's just wrong? And does the chapter's conclusion — that the two-object view is finished — survive once the mistakes are corrected? If it does collapse, tell me what the live options actually are and what the disagreement between them really turns on. No reading list needed, just the audit.
