Writing a novel in close third person and its driving me slightly mad that I can't name what I keep tripping over.

My character thinks that the coast road will be mined. If I write down the ingredients of that thought -- the road, the property of being mined, whatever it is that's supposed to stick them together -- what I end up with is a list. And a list doesn't say anything. Three items on a shopping list don't accuse anybody, don't warn anybody, aren't true or false. But the thought is true or false, and it manages that before anybody counts the ingredients.

When I try to fix it by adding the sticking-together as one more ingredient, the same thing happens again: now I've got four items, and four items don't say anything either. I can do that as many times as I like.

Then there's a second thing that might be the same thing in disguise. "The dog bit the postman" and "the postman bit the dog" look to me like they're made of exactly the same three items in the same arrangement, and my character believes only the second one. So the ingredients plus the arrangement don't seem to settle which thought it is either.

A friend who reads philosophy said this sounds near something called Bradley's regress, but everything I could find on that was about relations, and I can't tell whether it's my problem or only my problem's cousin.

So: does this have a name -- the gap between a thought and a list of what it's made of -- and are my two complaints really one complaint or two? I don't want a label I can drop into a sentence; I want to know where the edges of it are, what nearby things it isn't, and whether anyone thinks it's actually been closed rather than just renamed.
