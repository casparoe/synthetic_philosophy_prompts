ok so I've gotten weirdly attached to a reading of the Zhuangzi butterfly bit that I almost never see anyone defend, and I want someone to try to break it.

The standard move is to file it under dream skepticism: line it up next to Descartes' demon, say here's an ancient version of "how do you know you're not dreaming right now", and move on. I think that mistranslates the question. On my reading the puzzle isn't whether the experience is veridical, it's which of the two he is — Zhuang Zhou who dreamt he was a butterfly, or a butterfly now dreaming it's Zhuang Zhou. Call it "which-doubt" as opposed to "whether-doubt". And the two come apart: you can be totally certain you're awake right now and still, supposedly, not know which one you are, because what the image is doing is showing that the switch between them is something that happens to you rather than something you do.

What I want is what would count against this. Ranked, ideally fatal down to merely awkward. Textual stuff first: the passage sits at the end of a chapter that's mostly about perspectives and this-and-that, and it closes by naming something like the transformation of things — does that help me or sink me? There's also the awkward bit where he seems to say there has to be some distinction between the two after all, which if I'm honest is defintely the part I have least to say about. And the dream/reality thing gets picked up again later in the book, chapters 6 and 21 I think, which might mean the image is doing something more consistent than I want it to be.

Philosophical defeaters too: doesn't which-doubt secretly presuppose whether-doubt — if I really can't tell which I am, haven't I already conceded I can't tell whether my present experience is trustworthy? And what would someone who likes the skeptical reading say I'm helping myself to? Do I owe them an answer about indistinguishability, or is that an extra premise they're importing?

Last thing, be honest: is my reading insulated because basically any bit of text that looks skeptical I can redescribe as being about transformation? If so, is that fatal, or is that just what interpretive claims are like? I'd be moved more by a good textual defeater than a clever philosophical one, fwiw.
