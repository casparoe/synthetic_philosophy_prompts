QUESTION (Part A, 60 marks; Part B, 40 marks)

Setting for both parts. A departmental hiring committee is discussing whether to make an offer to a candidate, Professor Nour. Everyone present knows the following: Bea has argued against appointing Nour on the grounds that her research is weak; the committee has in front of it a teaching file for Nour containing the full course evaluations; and the chair has just asked whether anyone wants to speak to the teaching record. Bea then says:

"She's early in her career, but three of her students wrote to praise her."

(A) State what Bea said. Then identify and classify four further things that a hearer in that room would take away from her utterance, using Grice's own categories: part of what is said; conventional implicature; generalized conversational implicature; particularized conversational implicature; or none of these — in which case say what it is instead. For at least two of the inferences you classify, say which of Grice's diagnostics applies and what it shows. One or two sentences per item is enough.

(B) A week later a colleague points out that Bea is a non-native speaker of English who has never encountered the scalar reading of numerals, that in fact forty-one of Nour's students wrote in, and that Bea took "three" to be compatible with "many more," expecting the committee to consult the file for the exact figure. Does the "no more than three" inference remain a conversational implicature of Bea's utterance in Grice's sense? Is it part of what Bea meant? Answer both questions and say which feature of Grice's apparatus your answer turns on.

STUDENT'S ANSWER

(A) What Bea said: that Nour is early in her career and three of her students wrote to praise her. The truth-conditional content is just the conjunction; everything else is pragmatic.

Item 1. That Bea was talking about Nour and about Nour's students, rather than about one of the other candidates in the pool. This is a particularized conversational implicature. It is not in the words: "she" and "her" are indexicals, and nothing in the sentence uttered determines the referents, so the hearer must use the context of utterance to work them out. Because the inference depends entirely on features of the context, it is particularized rather than generalized. It is cancellable — Bea could have added that by "she" she meant Okafor, not Nour — so it passes the cancellability test.

Item 2. The contrast between being early in one's career and having students write to praise one, carried by "but". This is a generalized conversational implicature: in almost any context the use of "but" gives rise to the contrast reading, so it is a default inference rather than a context-specific one. It is also detachable, since a speaker could convey the same conjunction with "and", or with two separate sentences, and lose the contrast — which is how we know it is not part of what is said. And, like all conversational implicatures, it is cancellable: Bea could go on to say that she does not mean to suggest any contrast between the two.

Item 3. From the numeral "three": that exactly three, and no more, of Nour's students wrote in. This is a generalized conversational implicature, computed via Quantity. If Bea had known that more than three had written, she would have said so, and she did not say so, so she must take it that no more than three did. The hearer reaches it by assuming that Bea is observing the maxims and by asking what she would have to think for her utterance to be consistent with that assumption. It is cancellable: three of her students wrote — in fact more did, I have not counted.

Item 4. That Bea does not regard the teaching file as grounds for a strong positive assessment, or at any rate that she is not prepared to endorse Nour on the basis of it. This is a particularized conversational implicature, generated by flouting (violating) the first maxim of Quantity: Bea has read the file and has been asked directly for her view, yet she volunteers only a weak and hedged positive, and the hearer infers that she has nothing stronger to offer. Its content is indeterminate: Grice allows that a speaker may implicate a range of things without implicating any one of them precisely, and here one cannot say exactly how unfavourable the implicature is. It is not entailed and not part of what is said, since Bea could consistently add that she thinks the file is overwhelming.

(B) The inference survives. Conversational implicature is defined by the route the hearer takes to it: from what is said, plus the assumption that the Cooperative Principle is in force, plus the context. Nothing in that derivation mentions the speaker's private psychology, so provided the hearer's calculation goes through, the utterance conversationally implicates that no more than three students wrote, whatever Bea had in mind. It follows that the inference is part of what Bea meant by her utterance, since what a speaker means on an occasion just is what a competent hearer would work out on the basis of what was said. Bea's ignorance of the scalar reading explains why she generated the implicature inadvertently, but inadvertent implicatures are still implicatures — speakers implicate things they did not intend all the time. Grice's Modified Occam's Razor, that senses are not to be multiplied beyond necessity, supports this: having explained the numeral pragmatically, we should not go on to posit a further speaker's-intention condition on top of the derivation.
