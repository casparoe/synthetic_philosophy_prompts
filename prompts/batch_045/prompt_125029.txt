I'm moderating a recorded panel with the author of a forthcoming book on responsibility gaps in autonomous systems. Her thesis is that the gap is a defect in design rather than a fact about blame: a system is morally deployable only if its architecture guarantees, for every harmful outcome it can produce, at least one human decision that is a but-for cause of that outcome — call it the attributability constraint — and she proposes a certification regime under which any learned component that cannot be traced to a human choice is either frozen at deployment or prohibited outright.

Draft me eight to ten questions for the Q&A. I don't want her restating the thesis, and I don't want a guided tour of the literature. I want questions that bite, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. At minimum:

- One pressing the worry that the constraint is self-undermining: the whole appeal of a learned component is that its behavior isn't enumerable in advance, so demanding traceability can look like demanding that it not be the thing it is. Anticipate that she will say the constraint governs the decision to deploy rather than each output, and follow up on cases where a system's competence and its untraceability have the same source.
- One on continued learning after deployment — a system that keeps updating from user data, so the designer's causal and epistemic grip decays with use. If attributability is a property of a system at a time, is there a duty to freeze, roll back, or switch off, and whose duty is it? Anticipate a 'the deployer retains a duty to monitor' answer, and push on whether that duty is one anyone can actually discharge.
- One on authority without comprehension: a human who must confirm the system's proposal within a couple hundred milliseconds, or who holds a veto without any grasp of why the proposal was made, satisfies the letter of human-in-the-loop while arguably having no more control than a bystander. Ask whether her view has the resources to call such a person a rubber stamp, and what observable measure — override rates, time to decision, the cost of overriding — would show it. Anticipate a 'it depends on the whole sociotechnical setup' reply, and follow up by making her name a threshold at which the setup fails.
- One on whether closing the attribution gap closes the responsibility gap: enterprise liability, insurance pools, and compensation funds can get harms paid for without making anyone answerable in the sense her view cares about. Ask whether her regime is doing moral work or only allocative work, and whether she would accept a system that reliably compensates victims while leaving no one to answer for the harm.
- One on overdetermination and many hands: if attributability needs a but-for human cause, then a harm produced jointly by a model, a fine-tuner, a deployer, and a fleet-level feedback loop may have no single but-for cause even where several parties were plainly careless. Does her constraint then collapse into a demand for organizational redesign?
- One on whether the gap is genuinely new, given structurally similar cases in corporate agency, idiosyncratic drug reactions, and automatism. If we already live with gap-like cases, say what is special here.
- Finally, one question about what evidence would convince her that the gap in a given system had actually been closed: what is the success condition of the regime, and who is entitled to certify that it has been met?

She tends to brush off hard cases with 'that's a certification question, not a moral one.' I'd like at least one question, placed late, that makes that move progressively harder to repeat.
