I am writing a 20-minute conference paper arguing that betting-rate and Dutch-book arguments cannot settle the halfer–thirder dispute about the Sleeping Beauty problem, on the grounds that the rate at which the agent should bet in such a case is fixed not by her credence but by how payoffs are indexed to her awakenings. I want the paper to turn on one constructed case rather than on dialectic, so I need the case built to specification and shown to work.

Design constraints on the scenario:

- An ordinary sleep laboratory. One coin toss, at most two awakenings, standard induced amnesia between them, the agent fully informed of the protocol. No science fiction: no fission or copying of persons, no predictors better than chance, no infinite populations or repeated awakenings, no other subjects undergoing the procedure.
- The agent is an ordinary self-interested participant: no concern for her other temporal stages beyond ordinary prudential concern, no team or collective reasoning, stakes small enough relative to her wealth that risk neutrality is unproblematic.
- In the scenario she is offered, at one and the same awakening, two independent bets on the same coin toss: same proposition, same stakes, same moment, same evidence, offered by two experimenters with separate accounts and no possibility of hedging one against the other. Nothing may distinguish bet A from bet B except the rule governing how payoffs are indexed to her awakenings.

Required verdicts:

1. In bet A, the highest price she should pay for a ticket paying one unit if the coin landed Tails is two thirds of a unit.
2. In bet B, at that same moment and on that same evidence, the highest price she should pay for the very same ticket is one half of a unit.
3. The difference must be generated by the indexing rule alone: under A each awakening's bet is settled separately, so that a Tails world contains two settlements, while under B exactly one of her awakenings' choices is ever executed and she is charged only for the executed one, she is not told which, and void choices cost nothing.
4. Both numbers must be derived from the setup plus ordinary expected-utility reasoning rather than stipulated, so that a reader who grants the setup can check them.

What I need in the answer:

- The scenario in a single paragraph, written so that a reader can see the two payoff-indexing rules without being told what they imply.
- The arithmetic for each bet: the available strategies, what happens in a Heads world and in a Tails world under each rule, and an exact derivation of the two prices.
- An argument that the case is not cheating. State what the halfer and the thirder each take her credence in Tails to be at that moment, show that no single credence in Tails can rationalize both prices, and identify the one structural feature doing the work.
- Two ablations. First, equalize the indexing — make both bets per-awakening, then make both one-per-world — and show that the divergence collapses in each direction. Second, replace the awakening schedule with one that yields the same number of awakenings under both coin outcomes, and show that both prices become one half.
- The strongest objection you can construct, from either camp, to using this case against the view that betting rates reveal credence, together with your reply. One line of objection I expect you to address explicitly: under B only one choice per world matters, so the halfer will want to conditionalize on the proposition that her current choice is the binding one, which on standard halfer credences raises her confidence in Heads to two thirds, whereas on thirder credences it leaves it at one half. Say whether that conditionalization is correct, why, and what follows for the halfer if it is not.
- A single sentence, usable as the last line of my abstract, stating what the case shows.

Do not cite or attribute anything to anyone; I will add references myself. Keep the whole response under about 1,200 words.
