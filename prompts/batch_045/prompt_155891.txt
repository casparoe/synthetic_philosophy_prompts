I got back three weeks ago from my first ten-day silent retreat, and I am trying to decide whether to commit to a three-month course next spring. A man I sat beside, who has been practicing far longer than I have, handed me the following argument as a kind of joke, and it has stopped being funny. Please tell me where it goes wrong.

(1) The no-self teaching says that nowhere among the five aggregates is there a self: no permanent, unchanging, independent owner of experience, only a causally conditioned stream of physical and mental events.

(2) So a person is not an ultimate fact about the world. Person is a convenient way of talking about a stretch of a causal stream, the way a constellation is a convenient way of talking about a scatter of stars.

(3) If that is what a person is, then the unity of a life is a matter of how tightly a stream hangs together, and causal hanging-together does not stop at the skin. My mother's habits produced mine, my teachers' thoughts produced mine, and what I do is already lodged in other people's streams. There is one causal web with denser and looser regions.

(4) If the web is what there is, then the boundary of a person is drawn rather than found, and how far my future extends — to my death, on into my children, into everyone I have influenced — is up to us.

(5) If the boundary is drawn rather than found, then concern for my own future has no more claim on me than concern for anyone else's. Self-interest collapses into impartial benevolence, and the only thing left worth aiming at is less suffering, wherever it lands.

(6) So a consistent Buddhist has no reason to practice for his own liberation, to care that he be reborn well, or to protect his future well-being as such. He should simply work where the suffering is thickest.

(7) But (6) makes nonsense of what I just sat. Nobody undertakes a retreat for the general reduction of the world's suffering; you do it because you want out. And it makes liberation nobody's achievement.

Which step should I give up, and what does giving it up cost? If I have to choose, I would rather keep (1) and (2) than accept (6), but I want to know what I would be buying with them. If there are two or three standard ways out of this, lay them out with their prices rather than picking one for me. And please say plainly if the honest answer is that I have to let a self back in somewhere.
