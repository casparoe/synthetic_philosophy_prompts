So I fell into this via a book about vision and I've been going around in circles on it for a couple of weeks, and I can't work out whether what I keep bumping into is a real thing that has a name or whether I've just confused myself.

The starting case, in case it matters: a red dot flashes on the left, a green dot flashes on the right something like a tenth of a second later, and what you actually see is a single dot sliding across that turns green partway along — at a place where nothing was ever shown at all. Same shape of thing in the touch version: taps at your wrist and then further up your forearm, and you feel taps hopping up your arm through spots that were never touched, with the hopping well under way before the later taps land.

What I keep noticing is that whenever people talk about these, the discussion collapses into a shootout between two stories. Either you really did have the first, unrevised experience at the time, and then once the later stimulus came in the record got rewritten, so what you now have access to is a doctored version. Or nothing reached you at all until the later stimulus came in, and the experience got written once, late, and merely got dated to the earlier moment. And then the standard move is to say the two are indistinguishable in principle and leave it there. I gather both stories have names. I don't want a name for either of them.

What I want is a name for what's left over, because the cases I can't shake don't seem to me to fit either story, and they don't seem to be merely undecidable either:

1. When I try to spell out what the difference between the two stories would actually consist in, it evaporates on me. Everything downstream — what you say, what you press, what you later remember, what's happening in your head — comes out identical either way. But I don't want to conclude from that that there's no fact about what you experienced, because plainly there is: you saw one dot change colour mid-flight, you didn't see a green dot pop into existence out of nowhere. So the missingness seems to be specifically about the *when*, with the *what* totally fine. Is there a concept for that particular kind of missing fact, as opposed to (a) us simply not knowing which story is true, and (b) the much stronger claim that experiences don't have times at all?

2. The version that really bugs me: imagine two ways of probing the same trial disagree — say a reaction-time measure looks like the subject had the pre-rewrite content early, while something else (a forced choice, a neural marker, whatever) says the finished content only came online late. I genuinely don't know what to call that. Two drafts? One experience happening twice? One experience plus some unconscious processing that isn't experience? Or is it evidence that "when the experience happened" was never a single number to begin with? And is that the same phenomenon as (1) with extra machinery bolted on, or a separate one that deserves its own treatment?

3. And a smaller thing: subjects in this kind of setup sometimes report that something felt off but can't say what or when — they don't report the pre-rewrite content, just a vague wrongness. Does that residue have a name, and does it carry any argumentative weight at all, or is it just more constructed content with no special status?

I'm not after a label, I'm after the carving: what exactly this is, which of the nearby ideas it is and isn't, and where its edges are. If nothing established covers it, coin something and give me the definition plus the cases that should and shouldn't count. Also: what observation, if any, would move me from "we can't tell" to "there's nothing to tell"? And I can't look anything up, so please don't tell me who to read or what to cite — I want the shape of the thing, not references.
