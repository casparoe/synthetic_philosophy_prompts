I've ended up holding a deflationary view about hard choices and I want to know how exposed it is. What makes a choice hard is entirely epistemic: our grip on our own values is imprecise and noisy, so for a range of options our estimates overlap and we can't tell which way it goes. Part two is that everything distinctive about the phenomenology follows from that: the agonising, the fact that you can't just do a quick expected-value sum and feel relieved, and above all the residue afterwards — the sense that the road not taken stays somehow alive, that you've become rather than discovered the person who chose this — is an artefact of noisy self-access, not a window onto anything metaphysically special about the options. Corollary: agonising is a symptom to be engineered away with more information and better calibration, and talk of hard choices as opportunities to create reasons or to make yourself is post-hoc confabulation.
