hey — I run a small weekly forecasting group (mostly people from the local EA / rationalist scene) and we keep hitting the same stalemate: two people have read each other's writeups, dumped all their private evidence into a shared doc, argued in the comments, and are still at, say, 10% vs 40% on some AI-timeline-ish question. Aumann's agreeeing-to-disagree result keeps getting invoked, so I've been trying to turn it into an actual protocol we can run in the room — call it the Common Prior Protocol. The steps are: (1) the common prior assumption says that two otherwise rational people can only disagree because of different private information, never because of different priors; (2) so once everything is in the shared doc, we've effectively got common knowledge of each other's posteriors; (3) so any gap that survives must be irrationality somewhere, and the remedy is mechanical — whoever has less private information just adopts the other person's number, since the other person's posterior already contains their evidence plus more.

I want to run this at the next meetup but I suspect it's cheating in a couple of places, and I'd rather find out now then in front of twelve people. Where exactly does step (2) go wrong — is "we've both read the doc" anywhere near what the theorem needs, or is the condition something stronger that a shared folder just doesn't deliver? Is step (1) even the kind of claim you can apply to real people, or is it a modelling assumption about how one analyst represents a situation, in which case my whole diagnostic use of it is confused? And what about step (3) — if I learn your posterior, does conditioning actually move me all the way to your number, or somewhere else entirely, and is "whoever knows less defers" anywhere close to the right rule?

Please give me a committed answer, not a survey: tell me plainly which steps are broken and how, whether any version of the protocol survives, and what you'd actually have us do in the room instead — including whether, if the honest conclusion is that the prior genuinely isn't shared, that should change how much we defer to each other at all.
