I am putting together a seminar handout for a final-year epistemology module, and I want a decision tree rather than another prose survey of the literature. The module's framing case is an unsettled one: a student acquires a true belief from the output of a large language model, where there is no speaker who asserts anything, means anything, or takes responsibility for what comes out. I want the tree to sort views by how much of their account rests on the source's agency, not merely by their verdict on the familiar textbook cases, so that the LLM case does real diagnostic work alongside the human ones.

Please build it in two stages. First, construct the logical space: the branch questions, each phrased as something an undergraduate could answer without already knowing which position it leads to, together with the combinations of answers they allow. I want at least these questions doing the work — whether the hearer must have positive reasons bearing on this source's reliability on this occasion, or only an absence of defeaters; whether testimonial warrant is inherited from the source's own warrant or can be partly generated by the hearer's environment; whether a source's taking responsibility for the truth of what it says supplies a reason that no evidence of reliability could supply; and whether there can be testimony without an agent who asserts. Each node should hang off a concrete case rather than a formulation: include one case of a speaker who asserts what they do not believe, one of several barely credible sources converging independently, and either the LLM case or a crowd-aggregated-output case.

Second, locate the actual positions in that space as leaves. I need at least these: a Humean global reductionism that reduces reliance on testimony to observed regularities; a reductionism that instead demands positive local reasons about this speaker; a non-reductionism in the Reid-to-Burge line with a presumptive entitlement to accept what is presented as true; an assurance view on which the speaker's undertaking to the hearer is irreducible to evidence; and a default-and-challenge view. Add any other leaf you think the space requires and say why. Mark the unoccupied combinations as empty cells, with one clause each on whether nobody is there because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate rather than manufacturing a distinction between them.

Keep it to seven to nine branch nodes and no more than eight leaves, and use British spelling throughout.
