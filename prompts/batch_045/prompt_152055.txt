I'm in a reading group on the philosophy of spacetime, and I tried to sharpen the standard hole argument into something more forceful. The sharpening now looks to me like it proves far too much, and I cannot locate the move where it goes wrong, so I want a diagnosis rather than a summary of the debate.

Here is the argument as I now have it. (1) The Einstein field equations are diffeomorphism-invariant: if M is a model built from a manifold, a metric and matter fields, and d is a diffeomorphism of the manifold, then the dragged-along model d\*M is also a model — the geometry and the matter move together, so every coincidence between them is preserved. (2) So M and d\*M agree on everything anyone could in principle check: every instrument reading, every curvature scalar at the event where two specified worldlines cross, every coincidence of a pointer with a dial marking. They are isomorphic as structures. (3) Now take the hole construction: fix initial data on a slice Σ, let H be an open region lying to the future of Σ, and let d be the identity on Σ and everywhere outside H but non-trivial inside H. Then M and d\*M share the same initial data and agree outside H, while disagreeing inside it. (4) Manifold substantivalism says the points of the manifold are the physical spacetime points and keep their identity across models, so there is a determinate matter of fact about whether point p inside H carries curvature R. (5) Since M and d\*M disagree on that matter, they represent different physical futures for the same past. (6) So substantivalism plus general relativity is indeterministic, and substantivalism is the culprit. So far this is the argument as I was taught it. My addition is this: (7) nothing in (3) through (6) required H to be a proper subregion of the spacetime; a diffeomorphism can be the identity on Σ and non-trivial throughout the whole region to its future. Then the two models disagree about the metric properties of every point to the future of Σ, including the points along the worldline of the measuring apparatus and the points at which the pointer coincides with the dial. (8) So if (4) is right, general relativity plus substantivalism fails to determine what any instrument will ever read, which is to say that it has no empirical content at all. (9) But (2) says the two models agree on every reading and every coincidence, and the predictive success of general relativity is not seriously in question. So the chain from (4) to (8) must be unsound. (10) A friend in the group adds a second worry on the same theme: gauge transformations in a Maxwell field can also be the identity on an initial surface and non-trivial later, so if reasoning of the (4)-to-(6) shape generates indeterminism, then every theory with a non-trivial symmetry group is indeterministic, and the hole argument was never really about spacetime points at all. He also says that (2) is only safe in my case because my d is the identity near the boundary, and that unrestricted versions of (2) are contested.

Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? For what it is worth, I would like to keep (2) — it is the thing that makes sense of the symmetry — and I would rather give up (4) than give up either (2) or the empirical success of the theory; but I cannot say what giving up (4) actually commits me to. In particular: does my extension in (7) differ from the original hole case in any way that matters for the argument, or is my sense that it does itself the confusion? What becomes of ordinary claims like the curvature at the event where these two worldlines meet if the points are not individuals with primitive identity? And are the surviving options — relationalism, sophisticated substantivalism, plain anti-haecceitism, and the view that determinism was only ever determinism up to isomorphism — genuinely different positions, or the same position described four ways? Please say how confident you are in each move you make, and flag the single claim in your answer you are least sure of.
