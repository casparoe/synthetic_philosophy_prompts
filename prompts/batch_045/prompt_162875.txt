There's a distinction I've been using and I want you to use it back at me. Call it the inversion margin: how far a competence can come apart from any comprehension of it. Not the cheap version — "the agent can't say how it does it," which is true of basically everyone — but the strong claim that nothing in the system, at any level, has to represent the reasons why the thing works. Second distinction, which I think people collapse: Darwin-style inversions take the comprehender out of the *origin* of a competence (no mind ever had the design in view; blind variation and selective retention did the work), Turing-style inversions take it out of the *operation* (somebody understood it, built the machine, and the machine now runs without understanding anything), and then there's a third case — there IS a comprehender, but it sits downstream and confabulates: it tells you why it did the thing and the story is causally inert.
