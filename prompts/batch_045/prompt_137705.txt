So I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about promising, and the only honest method I've got is to write down my gut on a bunch of cases and then ask what principle, if any, I'm secretly using.

- My friend asks me to get coffee next Saturday, I say yes, and then something I've wanted for years lands on the same afternoon. Gut: I go to coffee, or at minimum I owe her an actual reschedule and not a flake.
- End of a party, real warmth, "we are totally doing this again soon," everyone knows it won't happen. Gut: nobody's bound by that and I feel nothing.
- A dying friend asks me to promise I'll take her dog. Gut: that one binds hard, years later, even though she's gone and has no idea whether I did it.
- Guy on a plane asks me to email him an article, I say I promise, he's forgotten by the next day, I'll never see him again. Gut: binds, but barely — like a 2 out of 10 — and skipping it costs me almost nothing emotionally. I genuinely don't know why this isn't the same as the dog one.
- Someone makes me promise not to testify by threatening me. Not binding at all.
- I promise to pay back the $500 I think I owe, then find the reciept — already paid. Not binding.
- But I promise to help a friend move and completely underestimate what the flight costs. Still binding, my problem.
- I promise to host book club in March, and in February my kid's in the hospital. Fine to bail, but I owe real notice and a make-up; if I just ghost, I've done something wrong.
- I promise to keep a secret, then it turns out keeping it lets someone get seriously hurt. I break it.
- "I promise myself I'll quit" on New Year's. Not really the same animal, and breaking it isn't the kind of thing I owe anyone an apology for.
- I say "I promise" while already knowing I'm going to blow it off. That's clearly worse than just blowing it off, even in a case where nothing else changes.
- The one I can't settle: a woman in early-stage Alzheimer's asks me to promise I'll come by every Sunday. A year later she doesn't know who I am and seems indifferent whether I show. My gut flickers between obviously you still go and there's no one left to be owed this.

My half-baked theory has been that promising hands the promisee a kind of claim that only they can cancel — but that makes the promise-to-myself case weird, and I'm not sure it explains why the dog and the plane guy feel so different.

So: is there one principle that gets these, or close? And I want you to actually commit here — pick the account you think is right rather than lining up three and leaving me to choose, say plainly which of my verdicts it can't keep and which one you'd overturn, and tell me what it predicts about the Alzheimer's case and about a couple I haven't listed: I promise my brother I'll keep his confidence and then he tells me he's about to do something genuinely awful to someone; and I promise someone something and later they release me, but I'm pretty sure they only said it because they felt pressured. Where is your own answer most likely wrong? American English, and no fence-sitting.
