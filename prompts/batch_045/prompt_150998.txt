I forecast long-horizon geopolitical risk with a small team, and we keep colliding over one line. When someone proposes anchoring a forecast about a rising power challenging an established one on the historical base rate — how often such transitions ended in large war, how often in accommodation — a colleague closes the discussion with "past performance is no guarantee of future results," in roughly the tone it has in a mutual-fund prospectus.

I would like a menu rather than a verdict: the distinct precise claims that sentence could be making, ordered from the trivial to the substantive to the false. Two worries of my own, which may or may not belong on the menu: first, that the cases in the record are not independent draws from anything — which episodes we count as empires or power transitions seems to depend partly on how they turned out, so the sample was in part built by its own outcomes; second, reflexivity — the systems being forecast read forecasts, so a base rate that governments plan around may not stay put.

For each reading, say whether it is trivial, defensible, or false; whether it is a claim about the world or about our record of the world; and, the part I actually need, which readings would genuinely support declining to use the base rate here, as opposed to supporting a discount, a wider interval, or an explicit statement of what the reference class is. Then say which reading you take a person in my colleague's position to intend, and whether the prospectus usage and hers rest on the same claim or merely sound alike.

Mark where you are confident and where you are extrapolating, and separate conceptual points from empirical ones you cannot check without data. And if the framing is off — if the slogan and reference-class forecasting are not really in tension, or the line cannot bear this disambiguating weight — say so directly instead of producing a menu for its own sake.
