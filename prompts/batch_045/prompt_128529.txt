I'm writing a short position paper on truth for a reading group, and before I circulate it I want to know whether the package I've assembled is actually stable. Here is where I stand:

(a) Minimalism: the equivalence schema exhausts what there is to say about truth. Truth has no hidden nature to be analysed; its function is expressive, letting us endorse claims we cannot repeat one by one and generalize over them.
(b) Since the unrestricted schema is untenable in classical logic, the right response to the Liar is to weaken the logic rather than restrict the schema or stratify the language into a hierarchy: I favour a Kripke-style paracomplete treatment on the strong-Kleene scheme, so that the Liar sentence receives no truth value.
(c) The non-classicality has to be quarantined. For the truth-free fragment — claims about snow, offices, whiteboards — reasoning must remain fully classical, and I am counting on classical recapture for non-pathological sentences to deliver that.
(d) Transparency: outside opaque contexts, a sentence and the ascription of truth to it are intersubstitutable without loss. That is what makes truth the device minimalism takes it to be.
(e) A semantics should be able to state its own verdicts from within the language. If my theory says the Liar is gappy, I should not be forced into metalanguage ascent or into silence whenever I want to say so, and a strengthened Liar should not be able to reopen the wound.
(f) The treatment should extend to empirically loaded cases: take the sentence "the only sentence written on the whiteboard in room 101 is not true", when it is in fact the sole inscription on that board. I am happy that whether such a sentence is paradoxical depends on how the world is, and I want a verdict here rather than a declaration that the case is out of scope.

Please audit this set rather than survey the field — I know the standard menu of positions on the Liar and don't need it rehearsed. Which pairs of these commitments are genuinely in tension, ranked by severity, and which only look as though they are? For each real tension, say what would have to give, what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the set, and what the single cheapest revision overall would be. Alongside each claim you make about my commitments, indicate how confident you are in it, and single out the point you are least confident about.
