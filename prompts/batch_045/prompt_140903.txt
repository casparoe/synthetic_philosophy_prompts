Working on something on the demandingness objection and I need a case built to order, because every time I try to write one myself it either turns into a pond/trolley thing or the objection sneaks in as a premise.

What I want: a case where what act consequentialism demands of one person doesn't arrive as one big ask but ratchets. She complies with the first demand, and complying is exactly what makes the next, bigger one land on her. And the complaint should be about that structure rather than about the size of any single sacrifice — every individual sacrifice in the story, asked once as a one-off, should be one she'd have no real grievance about. The objection has to come from there being no point at which she can reasonably take herself to be done.

Constraints on the scenario:

- Realistic: a clinic, a school, an office, a family. One paragraph. No ponds, no trolleys, no one-off rescues, and no villain engineering her situation. The escalation has to fall out of her circumstances as they actually change.
- Block the standard self-limiting reply. The usual reason people say consequentialism isn't really all that demanding is that the demands converge: as she gives more, the next unit costs her more and buys less. So build the case so the returns to her sacrifice go UP as she gives more — her compliance has to make her more effective, not less. Real mechanisms only, and the sort that are common rather than a lucky coincidence: credibility, trust, matching money, a threshold that unlocks other people's funding, skills she only has because she's been at it for years. Constant or increasing returns; no diminishing-marginal-utility escape hatch.
- No contested empirical claims about how much good a given donation does, and the story can't turn on anyone being deceived or on her contribution making no difference. Stipulate the payoffs and let them be what they are.
- She isn't a moral-saint type who signed up for this in advance, and nothing is ruinous in a single step. It's the accumulation that hurts, and there's a real personal cost an outside observer would take seriously.
- The verdicts have to fall out of the theories, not get written into the story.

Then the part I actually need help with:

1. Run the case through three or four rounds. At each round, what does act consequentialism require of her and why.
2. Test the escape hatches: a satisficing threshold, an agent-centred prerogative / personal-projects carve-out, and a rule-consequentialist code. Does each one actually stop the ratchet here, or does it stop it by drawing a line that's arbitrary given this case? My suspicion is that the third one changes the subject to what a code for everyone could demand, which is a seperate question from what she should do. Tell me if that's wrong.
3. Name the single feature of the case doing the real work. If that feature were removed, would it collapse back into a plain-vanilla demandingness case, and if so which one?
4. Give me the best available consequentialist reply — the steelman version, not just "she could decline."

This is going into a paper, so I care more about whether the case actually does the job than about how tidy it sounds. Tell me how confident you are in each bit and flag the point you're least sure of — in particular whether the ratchet is a genuinely distinct objection or just the old one with extra steps.
