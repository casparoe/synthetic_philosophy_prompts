I write near-future fiction, and I keep having to decide how my imaginary polities distribute the vote without knowing what I actually think about it. So here are my verdicts, case by case, and I want the principle behind them if there is one.

Excluding sixteen-year-olds: fine, I don't lose sleep over it. Excluding non-citizens who have lived and paid taxes in the city for years from municipal elections: not fine. Excluding prisoners serving sentences, including long ones: not fine. A civics test as a condition of registering: not fine. The same test, except that the polity must offer it alongside a paid three-day course, anyone may retake it, and a pass restores the vote retroactively: fine — which is strange, because the two differ only in whether there is a remedy. Binding decisions by an assembly drawn by lot from the whole electorate, briefed over several weekends by advocates on both sides and paid for the time: fine, and I trust it more than a referendum. Delegable voting — every voter may hand their vote to anyone they choose, who may hand it on again, so that a few people end up casting thousands of votes: fine. Weighting each ballot by a measured competence score: not fine. That pair is the one I cannot reconcile: both route influence toward the better informed, and what separates them seems to be only that in the first case nobody is graded.

My tentative principle: anyone the polity coerces is owed a voice, and once that is secured, how much weight each voice carries is an engineering question, settled by expected outcomes. That principle appears to license exactly the weighting I reject, so either it needs repair or one of my verdicts does.

Give me the principle, or the two or three candidates, stated precisely enough to argue with; say which case motivates each clause; tell me which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate; and run it on cases I have not listed — citizens resident abroad, decisions whose costs fall almost entirely on people not yet born, and an electorate systematically misinformed by sources the state cannot lawfully silence. The case I go back and forth on: whether those non-citizen residents may vote for the city's delegate to the national assembly, given that national decisions turn on knowledge many of them lack.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — the principle, or the verdict you think I have to give up — then give your reasoning. No preamble, and don't restate my question.
