I have been reading the rationalist blogs and forums since the old Overcoming Bias days, and the recurring argument about whether a language model "really believes" anything — or is, as the line goes, just predicting the next token — never converges, largely because people sort themselves by label before noticing which cases actually move them. I want to fix that for my own corner of the internet: an interactive piece for technically literate readers who know what fine-tuning, distillation and activation patching are but have no philosophy training, which walks them through concrete cases and dumps them at a position they did not pick in advance.

Please take the role of a philosopher of mind who works on intentionality and thinks carefully about diagnostic structure. Do not write me an essay about the debate; design the instrument. Before you write anything, brainstorm the logical space: the branching questions, the answer-combinations they permit, and only then which of those cells are actually occupied. Start from the cases, not from a list of -isms.

Build the tree from these five cases, in whatever order you judge diagnostically strongest, each node phrased as a question a reader can answer cold, without knowing where it leads:

1. TABLE. A lab distills a model into an enormous key–value store by querying it offline on every prompt under some length bound and storing the completions. Inside the bound the two systems are behaviorally identical; outside it TABLE degenerates. Same beliefs?
2. SIDECAR. A model is fine-tuned so that its first-person avowals ("I believe P", "P is false") are trained on deliberately inverted labels, while its task behavior is untouched because avowal tokens are masked out of the loss on task data. It now systematically misreports itself. Does that change what it believes — and if so, why does one output channel get that authority?
3. PATCH. Interpretability turns up a direction in activation space that predicts whether the model will later act as if P; patching it in flips the behavior, patching it out removes it; but the model never says P and no behavioral test on the original distribution separates the cases. Belief, or idle attribution?
4. MASSIVE ERROR. A system is best predicted — best compression, best forecasts of what it will do next — by attributing to it a belief set that is massively inconsistent, and no charity-preserving reinterpretation predicts as well. Are you allowed to attribute that?
5. MIRROR. Whatever standard you applied above, run it on a human whose beliefs are mostly inherited from a degraded information environment, and on yourself. Same verdict?

Add at most one further node if five cannot separate the required leaves. Leaves must include, under their usual names or as "unnamed": full-strength behavioral instrumentalism; fictionalist or agnostic instrumentalism; rationality- or charity-constrained interpretivism; causal-role or structural realism of the interpretability-friendly kind; a grounding or causal-history requirement that text-trained systems fail; human-exceptionalist skepticism that accepts beliefs in us but not in them; and eliminativism about beliefs generally.

No citations, no bibliography, no suggested reading: I want the reasoning, not the sources.

Please format your reply in these sections, in this order:

- "Branch questions" — about 250 words: the questions in tree order, each with one line on which distinction it is probing and why both answers are genuinely live.
- "The tree" — about 350 words: the tree itself, with answers labeling edges and positions labeling leaves.
- "Leaves" — about 150 words: one line per leaf, naming it and giving its single most awkward commitment.
- "Unoccupied cells" — about 250 words: the answer-combinations your questions permit but nobody defends, each diagnosed as empty because incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and what would have to be true for someone to move into it.
- "Where the tree fails" — about 120 words: pairs of positions your questions cannot separate, stated plainly rather than forced apart, and what further case would be needed if any.
