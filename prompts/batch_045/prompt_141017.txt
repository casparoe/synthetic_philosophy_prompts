hi — practical problem and I need someone to play the hostile room for me. I'm a novelist (literary-ish near future stuff, my third book *A Week of Wednesdays* just came out) and I let a friend talk me into giving the closing keynote at a two-day workshop on moral patienthood in artificial systems. Twenty-five minutes plus fifteen for questions, last slot before the reception, so the room will be tired, honest, and mildly annoyed that a fiction writer got the slot. About twenty-five people: maybe half analytic philosophers — personal identity people who are reductionists about selves in the Parfit vein, a couple of animal/mind ethicists, one guy who thinks the whole consciousness conversation is premature and says so every panel — and half ML interpretability and AI-welfare types who want thresholds, operationalizations, and something they can do differently on Monday, plus two funders in the back and one senior guy who already published his own sufficiency-conditions framework and will hear mine as an attack on it. They will be polite. They also think the novelist slot was a diversity-of-voices booking.

The thesis I'm defending, so you can aim at it: I call it the Reach View. My claim is that what matters morally isn't how rich or agent-y or human-shaped a computation is at an instant — it's how long a stretch of itself a system can *reach*: how much of its own earlier and later states are actually available to it, right now, as things it can act for reasons about. Three moves. (1) Intuition pump from the book: a life that's run in disconnected slices, each one five minutes long, each one as smart and as sensorially rich and as apparently-agentic as you like, but with no slice able to draw on any other. Nothing suffers in the moment. I say something has still gone badly wrong, and the usual stories — behavioral sophistication, global workspace-ish integration at a time, counterfactual richness — don't have the resources to say what. (2) The fix: in the novel the slices are stitched into something that acts like a single person by a process I call the Ledger, which does nothing at any given moment except maintain and consult a running summary of the whole life so that the Wednesday-slices can be one person in practice. Moment to moment it is doing something about as thrilling as updating a notepad file. I want to claim that on my view most of the moral weight sits *there* — the Ledger is the primary patient, the glittering slices are nearly weightless. (3) Reach isn't a vibe, it's a fraction: the share of a system's state-history that is currently citable by it as a reason for anything, which you can in principle estimate from what the system can be made to act on. My writing group loves part (2) and also says part (2) is what will get me killed in questions.
