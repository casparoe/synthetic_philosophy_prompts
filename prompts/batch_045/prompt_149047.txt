I took over three weeks ago as convenor of the ethics committee of a small medical NGO, and this is the first recommendation I have had to draft on my own. Our track record on whether to accommodate local practice that conflicts with our own policies runs as follows: a partner clinic that would only treat women accompanied by a male guardian, where a local women's association had publicly opposed the rule — we made funding conditional on dropping it; a partner that scarified children at parental request — no action, minuted as internal to the community; a community whose families refused blood transfusions on religious grounds — accommodated without conditions; a partner that triaged patients by caste, lower-caste last — we threatened to withdraw; a partner that withheld terminal diagnoses from patients at their families' request, contrary to our own consent policy — allowed.

The new case: in a district we are about to enter, a foreign religious mission has campaigned for six months, after which the village council voted, with no recorded dissent, to bar girls over eleven from our mobile clinic sessions; the council says it will end all cooperation if we see them anyway. The exclusion is recent — no such rule existed five years ago. A teacher has passed us a petition from the girls themselves asking to be seen and asking to remain anonymous. Our local nurses say that seeing the girls would cost us the district and the several hundred other patients we would otherwise treat there this year, and that we would simply be repeating the reflexes of the missionaries who preceded us.

I can see at least two rules that fit our precedents and disagree here — defer wherever the community is unanimous and no internal dissent is on record, or intervene wherever a practice is imposed on the very people who bear its cost and cuts them off from care we exist to provide — and possibly a third: defer only to a consensus the community reached on its own, not to one manufactured from outside by an actor with its own agenda.

Tell me what we should decide if we want to be consistent with ourselves, which of our precedents is the closest analog and why, what principle we would be committing to with each option, and which single further fact — or which further case — would most cleanly separate the rules. Give the strongest version of the case for the decision you are not recommending. Lead with your recommendation in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of the question. British English, please.
