I am about to use epistemic game theory as an engineering tool rather than as an object of study, and I want the specification before I commit to a framework.

The setting: I am designing an automated negotiation protocol. A small number of software agents, each with a private type, exchange messages over a fixed protocol and then choose. I want to be able to say of some outcomes that they follow from the agents' rationality together with what the agents commonly know, and of others that they do not — and I want to know what would have to be established before such a claim is licensed. Three features of my setting are not the textbook ones:

1. The agents' decision procedures are inspectable. In the deployments I expect, each side can read the other's code before choosing, so the object of higher-order reasoning is often a procedure rather than an action.
2. Rollouts are staggered. Some participants run a version of the protocol in which certain moves do not exist; they do not merely assign those moves low probability, they cannot represent them at all.
3. My verification is partial. At best I can establish that each agent has the relevant beliefs, that each believes the others do, and so on, to some finite depth and with some margin short of certainty.

Please write the desiderata that an epistemic account of rationality and common knowledge would have to satisfy to be adequate for this purpose. For each requirement give the case or consideration behind it — the criteria and their motivations are what I want, not a list. I particularly want you to settle what the account must say about: whether rational attaches to strategies or to the procedures that produce them; what is to be done when the game form itself is not common; whether conclusions reached under common knowledge survive when it is weakened to high-degree, high-probability mutual belief (consider the alternating-move game in which a player may take the larger share now or pass and let the pot grow, and the two-generals case in which every confirmation of a message itself stands in need of confirmation); whether the account must do more than describe beliefs and actually select an outcome, and if so whether it can do that without assuming common knowledge of the outcome it selects; and whether its verdicts must be invariant under re-implementations of the agents that behave identically in isolation but differently once the procedures are mutually visible.

Then mark which requirements are forced on me by this purpose and which are preferences of particular research programmes — a single fixed space of uncertainty shared by all agents, knowledge as factive and fully introspective, belief as a single probability measure rather than a ranking, a common prior, the vindication of some favored solution concept. I also want to know what diagnostic duty the account owes me: when play contradicts the prediction, must it tell me whether rationality or the knowledge assumption is what I should give up, and can any account discharge that duty? Flag any subset of the desiderata you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement does the most work in ruling candidate frameworks out. A brief scorecard at the end against the main families of account is welcome, but keep the weight on the criteria.
