I want an account of what is really at stake in the traditional unease about the axiom of choice, one that gets past the usual socks-and-shoes line.

The thing I keep getting stuck on is this. Distinguish full AC from the principle I'll call Concrete Choice (CC): for any set of nonempty sets whose members are all concrete, spatiotemporally located objects, there is a function picking one member from each. CC looks like what the intuitive argument for AC actually motivates — the thought that, given any collection of nonempty boxes, you can take one thing out of each — and it is the part that even self-described choice skeptics seem untroubled by. So: (a) If one worked in ZF with CC but without full AC, how much of ordinary mathematics would survive? Would we retain the parts of analysis that need only countable or dependent choice, and would we lose such things as the Hahn–Banach extension theorem, the ultrafilter lemma (equivalently, the compactness of first-order logic), or Tychonoff's theorem for arbitrary products? Which of the notorious consequences — Vitali non-measurable sets, the Banach–Tarski decomposition — would be blocked, and which would reappear by some other route? (b) Is CC even a coherent restriction? A friend argues that it collapses into full AC as soon as the world is rich enough to contain concrete codes for arbitrary objects: given any family of nonempty sets of abstracta, replace each member by the set of its physical encodings, apply CC to those, and decode. I have heard the reply that this only works if one already has choice functions for the encoding, but I cannot tell whether that is a genuine objection or a dodge. What does the answer tell us about whether concreteness can bear any set-theoretic weight — given that set theory has no predicate for it — and, if the intuitive argument only carries us as far as CC, where would the additional warrant for full AC have to come from?

Please keep the formal claims clearly separated from the philosophical ones: say whether something is a theorem of ZF, a theorem of ZFC, equivalent to AC over ZF, or merely a consistency or independence result, and distinguish all of that from views that are contested among people who work on the foundations of mathematics. Flag what you are unsure about rather than presenting a contested reading as the established one. I am after the conceptual structure, not references or further reading.
