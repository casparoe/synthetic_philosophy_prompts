(2) Amara: Disagreement alone never gets you there. Most of that variation sits upstream: people differ on nonmoral facts — whether the dead are actually offended, whether pollution is real, whether the act wrecks coordination. Fix the inputs and the outputs converge. Disagreement about the application of a shared principle isn't disagreement about the principle.

(3) Jon: That move is unfalsifiable. Name any possible pattern of variation and the realist can always posit some hidden factual disagreement to absorb it. If no pattern would ever count against you, the argument from disagreement isn't being answered, it's being immunized. And some of it isn't factual at all — it's about *weight*: how much loyalty or purity or authority counts against preventing a harm. That's a moral difference, full stop.

(4) Amara: Even weights can be upstream. Small-scale societies under real subsistence or pathogen pressure face genuinely different facts about what keeps a group alive, and if the deep principle is something like "promote welfare given your circumstances," the weights fall out of the circumstances. Also note what your argument needs: not just that culture causes moral judgment, but that it causes it *off-track*. You haven't shown that.

(5) Jon: Two things. If the correct weight on "don't harm this individual" against "keep the group intact" swings with local ecology, moral truth has become wildly local in content even though it's universal in form — that's relativism in a realist's coat. And the bit I think you're missing: much of the variation isn't verdict-disagreement at all, it's *scope*-disagreement. The populations don't only differ on whether X is wrong; they differ on whether X is the kind of thing morality has jurisdiction over. You can't repair that by correcting anyone's background beliefs, because there's no shared moral predicate whose extension they're haggling over.

(6) Amara: Fine, but scope disagreement is just second-order moral disagreement, and realism is a thesis about the status of moral facts, not about how much we happen to agree. And I can flip your complaint: what pattern of cross-cultural variation would *confirm* realism for you? If the answer is "none, since any convergence could be explained by shared culture," then your argument isn't evidence-responsive either — it's antirealism all the way down.
