I've been chewing on Hofstadter's idea that an 'I' is a self-referential loop rather than a thing sitting in one skull, so that different fractions of one person's loop can be present in different brains at the same time. I trust my gut more then my theory here, so here are my verdicts and I want a principle that returns them.

- My wife died six years ago. I catch myself doing her exact shrug, her timing, her turns of phrase, and our daughter does it more than I do. I want to say a real but small chunk of her I is in me — maybe 5%, more in our daughter — and not as a façon de parler: actually her.
- An actor who spent two years studying footage of me for a biopic can pass as me on tape. I say essentially none of me is in him: 0%.
- A scan-based copy built as I'm dying: that's me, basically 100%. I'm fine with dying into it.
- The very same copy built while I keep living: not me at all. I'd watch it switched off and feel nothing. I know these two are in tension and I don't want to give either up.
- A locked-in patient: no behaviour left but blinks, yet I say the I is fully there, 100%.
- My father with advanced dementia: still warm, still him in his manner, but he barely seems to represent himself anymore. I want to say much less of him is there — 40%? — a bigger drop than his behaviour change alone would explain.
- A friend of twenty years who's absorbed my habits, my jokes, my way of cutting a problem into pieces: maybe 10% me, even tho there's nothing in her that represents me.

Is there one account — of what the I is, of how much of it is present somewhere, and of when a fraction in another head counts as really that person instead of an imitation — that gets these numbers? I'd like it clause by clause, with the case that motivates each clause, and an honest statement of which verdict it can't save and why that one should go. And what does it say about the case I keep flip-flopping on: a language model trained on every word I've written, which sometimes feels like 0% of me and sometimes uncomfortably like 20%?
