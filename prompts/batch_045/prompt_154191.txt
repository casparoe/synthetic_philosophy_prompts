My school's sponsor, a wealthy former pupil, has made an unusual offer to sixth-formers: five thousand pounds, paid on Friday, to anyone who has by then genuinely made up their mind to drink a foul-tasting tonic on Saturday that will leave them miserably ill for a day. Drinking the tonic is entirely optional, and the money is paid whether or not anyone ever drinks it; the sponsor says he is interested only in whether someone can really bring themselves to decide to do it.

I would very much like the money, and I would like to make up my mind to drink the tonic. But I know that once the money is in my account on Friday, I simply will not drink it. Every time I try to decide, I notice that I do not believe I will go through with it, and the decision does not seem to take. My friend says I should just decide now and change my mind on Saturday, which sounds like it is not really deciding at all, but I cannot explain why.

Is my friend right? Can a person genuinely decide to do something while already knowing that they will not do it, and if I did somehow manage it, would taking the money be dishonest? I would also like to know whether this is just the same thing as making a resolution I expect to break. Please answer in British English.
