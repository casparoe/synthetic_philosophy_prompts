You are a professional philosopher of physics who has published on the foundations of probability in Everettian quantum mechanics. Before writing anything, think carefully and sketch the dialectic privately: enumerate the distinct decision rules an agent in the case below could apply — weighting branches by squared amplitude and summing welfare across the successors inside a branch; weighting branches by squared amplitude and averaging across them; weighting successors uniformly and ignoring amplitude; and any further option you think the case opens up — note what each prescribes, and identify which premise of the standard decision-theoretic treatment each one puts pressure on. Then output only the dialogue specified at the end.

The case is one I invented and cannot settle; call it the Cascade. A measurement is performed on a system prepared so that the squared amplitudes of the two outcomes, up and down, are 3/4 and 1/4, and both outcomes become decoherent macroscopic branches. Immediately afterwards a purely classical duplicating machine, triggered by the detector's macroscopic record, goes to work: in the up-branch it does nothing, leaving one successor; in the down-branch it manufactures from a stored blueprint two additional atom-for-atom copies of the agent, deterministically, placing the three successors in labelled slots. No quantum event, no measurement, no amplification and no change to any amplitude is involved. Stipulate that the machine is physically possible, that it collapses nothing, and that no successor can tell by introspection or local evidence which branch she is in or how many successors there are.

Before the run the agent — purely self-interested, about to become all four successors, regarding each successor as equally a continuation of herself — must choose which of two programmes to write into the machine:

(A) every successor in the up-branch receives 100 units of welfare, every successor in the down-branch receives 0;
(B) every successor in the up-branch receives 0, every successor in the down-branch receives 60.

Partway through, Ivo is allowed one variation: the machine instead uses a quantum randomiser to place exactly one copy in one of the three slots, so that the down-branch divides into three sub-branches of measure 1/12, each containing a single successor. The successors' first-personal situation is unchanged; the branch structure is not.

Write a dialogue between two equally competent philosophers.

Mara holds that the quantitative probability problem is essentially solved: given the branching structure of the universal state, the usual decision-theoretic axioms — preference ordering over acts, indifference to branching that leaves payoffs unchanged, diachronic consistency, supervenience of rational preference on the quantum state — force amplitude-squared weighting as the unique coherent measure, and that is all that the Born rule ever needed to be.

Ivo accepts the no-collapse picture as the best reading of the formalism but thinks the Cascade shows the derivations deliver less than advertised: they deliver a measure over the structure of the state, not a credence for a person who has to decide how much of her future is at stake in each of several equally real successors, and the step from one to the other is an unsupplied premise that the Cascade isolates.

They share two commitments, and neither may revisit them: branching is real, and naive branch counting is dead, since the number of branches is basis- and coarse-graining-relative and equal-amplitude splitting cannot yield 3 to 1. So the dispute is not about which numbers are the right target; it is about what hitting that target gives you.

Constraints: no character may settle a point by appealing to what some paper or author argues, and neither may escape the case by calling the duplicator impossible, by treating it as a measurement, or by declaring that of course branches and not copies are what count without saying why a purely classical multiplication of successors generates no claim on the agent's concern. Each character must concede at least one substantial point to the other, must reconstruct the other's position in its strongest form before replying to it, must engage the sum-versus-average question and the variant with the quantum randomiser, and must not explain Everett or the Born rule problem from scratch.

Give me exactly five numbered exchanges. Each exchange consists of one speech by Mara followed by one speech by Ivo, two or three sentences each, tagged Mara: and Ivo:. No stage directions, no narration, no preamble before the list and no summary after it. By the fifth exchange they should have isolated the single premise that the whole disagreement turns on, and each should say in one clause what would settle it for them.
