Two people in my department got into it over a historian's new dataset and I honestly cant tell who won. The setup: on some plateau a 1560 border between two principalities follows a watershed line drawn by a surveyor who misread his own map, so the villages on either side match on soil, elevation and road access, but ended up in different confessions because one prince converted for a marriage alliance and took his villages with him. By 1860 the Reformed side has higher literacy, more savings-bank deposits per household and more small workshops, and the gap only opens after about 1780, well after predestination preaching had mostly stopped and most villagers could no longer state the doctrine.

Lena: this is as close to a natural experiment as Weber's Protestant ethic thesis is ever going to get, the doctrine was assigned for reasons that had nothing to do with commerce, and the effect shows up with the right lag, so a value really is doing causal work here.
Dan: no, culture is a black box. You've shown the confession caused something, not that a value did; the proximate causes are schools, poor-relief rules and parish banks, and the fact that the gap survives the belief is evidence against the belief being the mechanism. Besides, citing a value tells you why the behavior made sense to the agent, not what caused it, and those are different things.
Lena: but rules only bind because people treat them as binding, so you've just pushed the value one step down and renamed it an institution; and the reason-versus-cause split is too quick, since reasons that get habituated into a population are exactly the kind of thing that causes behavior over generations.
Dan: then the thesis is unfalsifiable, and you can't name one observation that would make you drop it.
Lena: sure I can. Where conversion was imposed by fines and inspection instead of being preached, no internalized disposition should form and the gap should be missing, and in two of the districts it is.
Dan: all that shows is that coerced compliance didn't take. It doesn't show an idea did the work. And savings deposits might just be dowry custom.

Please go through this move by move and tell me who's right about what, and what both of them are missing. Give me exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no intro and no conclusion.
