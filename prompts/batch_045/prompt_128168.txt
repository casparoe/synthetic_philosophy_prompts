My friend Jen is the person our group chat goes to whenever we get stuck on an argument of the form is that the same thing or a different thing. She works in freight logistics, has never read a philosophy book in her life, and is weirdly consistant about it — seven years and we have never once caught her contradicting herself, which is annoying because I am fairly sure she is making the rules up as she goes along.

Her verdicts so far:

- My nephews clay dinosaur and the clay it is made of: two things, not one. Squash it and the dinosaur is gone but the clay isn't, and the clay was sitting in a bag before the dinosaur existed.
- My bike and the aluminium in it: two things. Melt the bike down and you still have the aluminium, you don't still have a bike.
- Me and my body: one thing. She thinks souls are nonsense and that I stop when my body stops, so person is just a word for a human body while it's alive.
- A log hollowed out into a canoe: the log is gone, there's a canoe now, and the wood is still there — so right now there are two things in the same place.
- A chess set and the pieces in the box: one thing. The set just is the pieces, there is no extra set object.
- My mug that I keep my pens in: one thing. Pen holder is just what I'm using it for.
- My car before and after I named it Betsy: one thing, obviously, naming it did nothing to it.
- The ship that had every plank replaced one at a time while it kept sailing, against the original planks nailed back together in a boatyard: she says the boatyard one is the real ship and the one still sailing is a copy.

Tonight's case, which we could not settle. My brother-in-law has a plain steel snow shovel, exactly the kind you buy at a hardware store, nothing special about it. A gallery has put it on a plinth with a wall label and is showing it as a sculpture. Nobody did anything to it — no polishing, no welding, no plaque screwed onto it, same object it always was. So: is there now a sculpture sitting in the same place as the shovel, made of the same steel — two things, the way the dinosaur and the clay are two things — or is it one thing that people have started calling a sculpture, the way my mug is one thing that I call a pen holder? And then: if the gallery sells it next year and it goes back to shovelling snow, did the sculpture stop existing at that point while the shovel carried on, or did nothing really happen to it?

Two more guesses I want from you, while you're at it: what she'd say about the identical shovel from the same batch still sitting in my shed, which no gallery has ever heard of — does that one have a sculpture hiding inside it too — and which of her old verdicts is the closest match to tonight's.

What I actually want is: your best guess at her verdict on the shovel, the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit all eight of her old verdicts just as well and what each of them predicts about the shovel, why you'd pick the one you picked, and one further case I could put to her next week that would tell those rules apart. Lead with the actual prediction in your first two sentences. Under 500 words please, I'll be reading this on my phone in a pub and I would rather have a blunt answer than a complete one.
