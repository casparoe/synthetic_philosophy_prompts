I keep flip-flopping on nudging and I think the only way to settle it is to just write down what I actually feel about a pile of cases and see whether any principle fits, so here goes. No citations needed, I want the principle.

- Opt-out organ donation (you're a donor unless you tick a box): totally fine, feels like the clearest case on the list.
- Auto-enrolling employees in a retirement plan with contributions that creep up every year unless you opt down: fine, I'd even want my employer to do it.
- Grocery store putting fruit at eye level and the chocolate at floor level: fine, boringly so.
- A tax on sugary drinks: fine, and weirdly it feels *more* legitimate than the gentle stuff even though it's much heavier handed.
- Emergency contraception kept behind the counter so you have to ask a pharmacist for it: not fine, it feels like it's weaponizing embarrassment.
- My employer quietly redesigning the kitchen and staircases so staff walk more, and not mentioning it (they get lower insurance premiums): not fine, straight up creepy.
- The exact same redesign, but announced with a sign explaining what's being done and why: fine, or at least nowhere near as bad.
- My phone throwing up a 20 second pause after 40 minutes of social media, on by default: fine.
- A personalized AI that reshuffles my grocery delivery site to nudge me toward the health goals I've typed into it, without ever telling me it's rearranging anything: fine.
- That same AI reordering my news feed toward the things I say I want to be well informed about: not fine, and I can't explain the difference to myself.
- Bank app telling me I'm saving less than people like me: not fine, same embarrassment thing as the pharmacy.
- Stuff I set up on myself (auto-transfer on payday, app blocks after 10pm): obviously fine, seems like a different animal entirely.

My tentative principle, which I don't trust, is something like: a nudge is OK when it steers you toward what you'd pick if you were rested, informed and thinking straight, and it stays cheap to go the other way. But that seems to get the organ donor case wrong (it isn't steering me toward my own good and it's still the case I'm most sure about), and it either condemns or excuses the stairwell case for reasons that have nothing to do with why I dislike it.

I also want to throw in two things I haven't seen discussed, if they've been discussed I've missed it. Call the first a meta-nudge: instead of hiding the architecture, you reveal it in a way that's itself designed to move behavior, e.g. a pop-up saying this default was set to make you save more, which reliably makes a chunk of people save less just to spite it. Call the second a proxy architect: you hand over the design of your own choice environment to something that models what you'd want from your past choices, you can always override it but you never see what it did.

So: give me one principle, or two or three candidates, stated precisely enough that I can test it, with the case that motivates each clause. Don't rescue all my verdicts by making the thing a mess. Tell me which verdict has to go, and what your principle says about the meta-nudge, the proxy architect, and one case I'm actually losing sleep over: rearranging the fridge and putting a hard cutoff on the router for my fifteen year old, who'd scream if he knew I was doing it on purpose.
