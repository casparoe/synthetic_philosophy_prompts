For the past few months I have been running a fortnightly Nomic-style game — a self-amending game in which passing a proposal changes the rules by which future proposals are passed — with a small group of friends, and we have reached a stalemate that I keep turning over between sessions. Early on, by unanimous agreement, we entrenched a rule providing that the amendment rule itself may not be changed except unanimously. Last week a proposal to repeal every rule requiring more than a simple majority was itself carried by a simple majority. Half the group says the later, more general rule wins; the other half says a change cannot be valid if it is made by a procedure that was, at the moment of the vote, simply not available, so the majority only purported to amend the amendment rule. Our attempts to settle the matter by adding a further rule about how conflicts between rules are to be settled have merely reproduced the same disagreement one level up.

Could you write me a short piece of fiction — not an essay — that dramatises this kind of deadlock? I would like it to concern characters who genuinely try to climb out of the knot rather than merely remark on it, and to show the regress that sets in when each attempted tie-breaker is itself an amendment whose validity is precisely what is in dispute. Please keep it recognisable to someone who understands problems of legal self-reference, and avoid resolving it by contrivance: an ending that leaves the question open, or that shows the group falling back on something outside the rules altogether, would suit me best.
