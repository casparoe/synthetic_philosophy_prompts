I want a case built to order. Its purpose is diagnostic: to test whether the distinction between doing and allowing can be reduced to the distinction between action and omission, by finding a case where the two come apart in the direction that is hard to build.

Construct a case in which an agent kills a patient purely by omitting to act — no bodily movement, no speech, nothing after the setup — yet in which the death is, pre-theoretically, a killing rather than a letting die. Constraints:

- Hospital or ordinary caregiving setting. No exotic technology, no trolleys, no villains with timers.
- The agent's only relevant behavior after setup is an omission.
- Everything the agent did before the omission was permissible and consensual. The killing verdict may not be inherited from an earlier wrongful action, or from a lethal intention formed at setup time.
- The death is not better explained by some other agent's killing, or by a lethal process already underway independently of what the agent set up.

Then:
1. Give a matched control case, differing from the target in one structural feature, that is intuitively a letting die.
2. Run three tests over both cases and record the verdict of each: the action/omission test; the threat test (pre-existing lethal threat versus a threat the agent herself introduced); the agency test (harm brought about by engaging something's harmful features versus by withholding what would have prevented it).
3. Say which tests deliver killing in the target case and which collapse it back into letting die, and name the single feature doing the work.
4. Give the redescription a defender of the action/omission test would offer to get letting die, and say whether that is a real answer or a refusal to engage.

Do not stipulate the verdicts; they should fall out of the structure. Be explicit about where the case is fragile: if the result depends on the agent's professional role or on an institutional rule rather than on the causal structure, say so, and say whether that is cheating given the purpose above.
