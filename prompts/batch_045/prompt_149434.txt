Consider the following arrangement. S writes to H: "I will not sign unless clause 9 goes." Before delivery the message passes through a rewriting system that S has authorized in advance and whose operating rule is public: replace any categorical commitment with the weakest formulation that a competent addressee would still take to convey that very commitment. H accordingly receives something like "It may be difficult for me to sign while clause 9 is in place," knows the rule, knows that S authorized it, and reads off S's refusal.

Please explain the conceptual difficulty this poses for a Gricean treatment of conversational implicature, and where you think it should land. The points I want covered:

1. Is there an implicature here at all, and if so, whose — S's or the system's? Frame the answer around the roles Grice assigns to (a) the speaker's selection of the very words used, (b) the hearer's route to the added content by inference from the assumption that the speaker is observing the Cooperative Principle and the maxims, and (c) the speaker's nested intention that the hearer reach that content through recognizing that intention. Identify which of these is under strain and why.

2. Run the case through the standard diagnostics — calculability, cancellability, non-detachability, reinforceability, and the contrast with what is said — and report whether they deliver the same verdict. If they come apart, say what that divergence shows about the diagnostics themselves rather than simply choosing one.

3. Say how much of this is genuinely new. Compare the case with two familiar ones: a conversation conducted through a human interpreter, and a case in which a third party tells the hearer in advance what the speaker intends, so that no inference is required. If my case reduces to one of these, give the reduction; if it does not, isolate precisely what the rewriting layer contributes.

4. State what the case shows about whether conversational implicature is a property of a speaker's act or of a signal in a context — that is, whether an account of implicature already presupposes an account of who or what counts as the speaker.

Keep the discussion concrete and make the hearer's inferential route explicit at each step. A few hundred words is fine: I want the machinery and the pressure points, not a survey.
