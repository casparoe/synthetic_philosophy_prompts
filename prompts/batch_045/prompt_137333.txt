I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy. The vocabulary I actually use for evidence is test suites, assertions, property-based tests and fuzzers, code coverage, mutation scores (how good a suite is at catching deliberately broken variants of the code), and the difference between a passing test and a machine-checked proof.

A philosopher friend put the raven paradox to me as follows. The hypothesis that all ravens are black is logically equivalent to the hypothesis that all non-black things are non-ravens; logically equivalent hypotheses are, by assumption, confirmed by exactly the same evidence; so observing a white shoe confirms that all ravens are black. That is absurd. Yet we also want to say that only ravens really bear on the raven hypothesis.

Please translate the paradox, and the main ways of responding to it, into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, flagging where the translation is doing real work. Concretely, I have two hunches I want tested rather than indulged. First: the two formulations correspond to two different assertions over the same domain — one that executes its check only when the input is a raven, one that executes it whenever the input is non-black — so that, on any coverage profile you like, the white shoe is evidentially inert in the first program and active in the second, despite the invariants being logically equivalent. Call that the oracle-sensitivity of confirmation. Second: mutation score seems to capture something the propositional framing does not — a suite that cannot distinguish the true invariant from a slightly weakened one, such as all ravens are black except albinos, has not confirmed much, no matter how many of its inputs pass.

Then give me the residue report: what maps cleanly, what had to be distorted to fit, and what has no expression at all in the vocabulary of tests, coverage, and oracles. In particular: (a) does degree of confirmation have a genuine counterpart here, or only the cruder notion of how much of an input space I have sampled — and if the latter, can you say roughly how many non-black non-ravens it takes to match the evidential weight of one black raven, as a function of the relative sizes of the two classes? (b) If I have a machine-checked proof of the invariant, the paradox appears to evaporate; is confirmation then merely a symptom of my boundedness as a tester, or does the paradox survive for an agent holding a proof? (c) Which premise of the paradox does my vocabulary simply refuse to state? And (d) is my answer, that it depends on which oracle you wrote, just the philosophers' point that only projectible, natural-kind predicates support confirmation, restated in engineering clothes — or does the coverage story say something the projectibility story cannot say?
