So any defensible view about distribution has to be person-affecting: everything it values has to be a benefit or a harm to someone.

(2) Prioritarianism is supposed to pass this test. It says that a benefit matters more the worse off the person who receives it. Since it only ever adds up weighted benefits, it never recommends making the better off worse off when nobody gains, so it never recommends levelling down.

(3) But notice: on this view, how much a benefit matters is not settled by the size of the benefit alone. It also depends on where the recipient sits, on their being below other people. So prioritarianism does care about a relation between people, and not only about benefits.

(4) If what does the work is the fact that one person is below others, then what matters is the gap between a person and the people above them.

(5) There are two ways to make a gap smaller: raise the person at the bottom, or lower the people at the top.

(6) Lowering the people at the top while making nobody better off just is levelling down. So prioritarianism is committed to levelling down after all; it simply arrives there by weighting benefits rather than by praising equality.
