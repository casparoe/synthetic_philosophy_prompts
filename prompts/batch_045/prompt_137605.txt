I am a novelist, and I need one document from my current book written before I can finish the chapter that turns on it. The novel is set inside the press office of a declining maritime league during the ten weeks in which it decides to mobilise over a small island it has never previously cared much about. The narrator is a junior speechwriter. In chapter nine she is handed a single piece of paper — the ruling of the Office of Historical Resemblance on which historical analogy may lawfully be used in public about the crisis — and she must reduce it to a seven-word line for the Prime Minister's broadcast. What I need from you is that ruling: 850 to 1,000 words, deadpan officialese, dated, docketed, and shaped as a licensing determination — findings of resemblance, the lessons in conflict, the determination, directions to licensed speakers, and a note on appeals and recertification. Footnotes and schedules are welcome; the apparatus should be part of the comedy.

The target is the practice of settling what to do by declaring what the present resembles, not any person. No real individual, living or dead, may appear or be named; nothing may be quoted or cited; no reading lists, authorities, or real texts beyond the widely known events named below. Everything you need about the fictional crisis is here, so do not invent or depend on any further real-world history.

The facts the Board must take as established: the Union of Kesh, a large industrial power, claims Anfa, a self-governing island of twenty-three thousand people under the League's protection, whose value lies in a deepwater port and a chip-packaging plant. Kesh has moved forty thousand troops to the coast opposite; the League has sent two carriers. Kesh's leader has called the island's status an open wound and a century of humiliation. The League's mutual-defence treaty obliges it to defend Anfa; no treaty the League signed recognises Kesh's claim. Kesh's navy is superior within two hundred miles of its own coast and not beyond; the League's analysts say that advantage expires in about three years. Fifty-two per cent of Anfa's residents favour the status quo.

Three applications are before the Board: from the Foreign Ministry, to license M-38 (Munich), whose registered lesson is that concessions to a revisionist power enlarge its appetite, so the challenge must be met early; from a coalition of legislators, to license V-64 (Vietnam), whose registered lesson is that a commitment entered for credibility, without a definable and attainable objective, becomes irretrievable, so the commitment must be declined; and from the Chiefs of Staff, to license T-431 (Thucydides), whose registered lesson is that when a rising power nears parity the established power's fear and the rising power's grievance make war likely whatever the merits, so one must act while the window is open and describe the action as structural. S-14 (1914) is also on the register: mobilisation timetables and alliance chains can produce a war no government chose, so the option of stopping must be preserved.

The Board's available doctrines — how it deploys them is yours — include paired licensing, a resemblance schedule distinguishing surface from structural similarity, analogical seniority, recertification of ancient lessons, disanalogy waivers, and a moral-charge rating disclosed to speakers in the manner of a nutrition panel.

What the document must do in my plot is fixed: it must leave my narrator holding a paper that authorises mobilisation while insisting, in its own words, that it has decided nothing, only recognised a pattern. Which of the applications succeeds, and by what route, is your invention; I want to be able to write her seven-word line from it myself.

The failings I want a specialist to recognise as real are these, and the document must exhibit them without ever naming them: resemblance is assessed after the conclusion has been chosen; the analogy supplies moral charge rather than evidence; every registered lesson has an equal and opposite one; the pattern-based prediction helps bring about what it predicts; fluency with analogies is not competence at policy; and once a lesson is licensed, evidence that might dislodge it is read as further confirmation. No sentence may concede the joke, no footnote may confess, and no tell-word — bias, framing, propaganda, self-fulfilling, hindsight — may appear. A reader with no theory at all should still feel, from the concrete consequences, exactly what is wrong.

Close with the Board's determination on its own final line, machine-readable and in exactly this shape: Verdict: [docket and short name] — [licensed or denied] — [the authorised directive, in six words or fewer].
