Last night a friend and I argued well past midnight about whether Nāgārjuna's tetralemma is just a four-valued logic, and I came away wanting a more disciplined translation than either of us managed. She claims the four lemmas are simply the values true, false, both, and neither, and that Nāgārjuna's refusing all four is a fifth value; I suspect that misses what emptiness is actually doing, but I can't say why in my own terms.

My vocabulary is knowledge representation. I build RDF/OWL-style knowledge graphs, and I'm fluent in SQL's three-valued logic and NULL semantics, the open- versus closed-world assumption, negation-as-failure versus explicit negative assertion, and Belnap's four-valued logic (FDE) with its gluts and gaps. Please restate the tetralemma and the Madhyamaka doctrine of emptiness in that vocabulary as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report: which parts translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart in my framework at all.

Three things I especially want pinned down. First, whether Nāgārjuna's negation behaves like negation-as-failure — no matching triple, so nothing follows under the open-world assumption — or like an explicit negative assertion, given that it is supposed to leave him committed to no positive counterpart. Second, whether emptiness is best modeled as missing data, as reference failure (a term with no denotation), or as the inapplicability of the question itself. Third, whether rejecting all four lemmas is a fifth truth value, a violated integrity constraint, a withdrawn query, or something my formalism has no slot for.

Then the verdict I actually care about: is the residue a genuine limitation of four-valued and open-world formalisms — something a knowledge-graph engineer should concede that Nāgārjuna saw and we did not — or evidence that the Madhyamaka claim was less determinate than it sounds? And concretely: if I added a fifth marker for "none of the four applies" to a system like mine, would it do any inferential work, or would it quietly collapse back into NULL?
