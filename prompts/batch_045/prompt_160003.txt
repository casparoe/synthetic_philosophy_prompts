I work in catastrophe modelling for a reinsurer, and a few years ago I fell into a decision-theory reading group that never really ended, so I've ended up carrying around a pile of half-formed views about risk that I've never actually checked against each other. I'd like you to do that. I have a feeling I'm helping myself to something somewhere.

Where I think I've landed:

(1) In the Allais setup — a certain $1m versus [10% chance of $5m, 89% of $1m, 1% of nothing]; and then [11% of $1m, 89% of nothing] versus [10% of $5m, 90% of nothing] — I take the certain million first and the five-million shot second. I've had the independence violation walked through for me maybe four times now and it has never once moved me. My honest reaction is that the axiom is what should go, not my answers.

(2) I dont think that's just stubbornness. I think the reaction is tracking something real: ending up with nothing when you could have locked in the sure thing is a genuinely different kind of bad from merely having less money, and I'm happy to count that difference as part of what the outcome is rather than as noise.

(3) At work I'm an expected utility person, full stop. Put a number on where you end up, weight by the probabilities, take the biggest. I use that every day and I really dont want to lose it — it's the only thing that lets you price ten thousand policies without scoring every one against every other one.

(4) And when I'm deciding for other people — setting retention limits at work, helping my parents structure a drawdown — I override the reaction in (1) and go with the framework in (3), and I've never felt bad about it. I've always assumed that isn't paternalism, because (1) is about me and my own money.

(5) But I dont extend the same tolerance to the urn cases. Bet on red from a 50/50 urn versus red from an urn whose mix you dont know, then the same question for black: I prefer the known urn both times, and unlike with Allais I think that one is plainly a bug, not a quirk to be respected. I can feel that it's different from the Allais thing but I couldn't tell you what the difference is.

(6) I also think it should never matter how a gamble is built — one spin of a hundred-slot wheel or two spins of ten slots, one stage or two — it's worth exactly what the reduced one-stage version is worth. I've always taken that as obvious, and I've noticed it makes some people think I must therefore accept independence, which I definately dont.

So: is this a package? I want the pairs that actually fight each other, ranked by how badly, with what would have to give in each case — not a list where everything is flagged as equally in tension. Tell me which of these only look like they clash. Rank the fixes by how much they cost me, bearing in mind that (3) is load-bearing for my job and (1) is the thing I'm most attached to. And if the thing that has to go is my stubbornness in (1), say that plainly instead of being polite about it — I'd rather know than go on quietly contradicting myself. If there's one revision that cleans up the most at once, name it.
