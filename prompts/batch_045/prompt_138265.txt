Give me a number, not a survey of the options. What is your credence that the logical omniscience built into standard possible-worlds semantics for belief — an agent who believes p believes every logical consequence of p, and believes every logical truth — is a genuine defect of that semantics rather than a harmless idealization that honest formal work can retain? Take the question to have this resolution: it resolves toward genuine defect if, fifty years from now, the default formal treatment of belief permits an agent to believe p and to believe if p then q while failing to believe q, for reasons other than inattention, forgetfulness or memory limits; it resolves toward harmless idealization if closure is retained and apparent counterexamples are explained away as misdescriptions of the agent rather than as failures of the semantics.

Give me the decomposition that produces your number, with a probability and one line of justification for each term: (i) that ordinary believers really violate even single-step closure under an entailment they can recognize on reflection — as opposed to violating only multi-step closure, which everyone grants and which should not count as decisive; (ii) that a hyperintensional or otherwise structured alternative can do the explanatory work possible-worlds semantics was bought for — action explanation, modelling deduction, feeding decision theory — rather than merely redescribing the counterexamples in a new notation; (iii) that Bayesian coherence requirements, such as assigning probability 1 to logical truths and to whatever one's evidence entails, are optional enough that dropping closure need not take probabilistic models of belief down with it; (iv) that a two-tier move I am tempted by is coherent: retain full closure, but only over an algebra of propositions the agent is currently aware of, so that learning can enlarge the algebra rather than redistribute credence within it; (v) that fifty years of convergence in the field would track the arguments rather than inertia, fashion and the conservatism of available formal tools.

Then combine the terms into a single figure, tell me which one the total is most sensitive to, and say which single result — empirical or formal — would move your number farthest. For calibration: I sit at 0.65, a colleague at 0.3; tell me which of these sub-questions our gap most likely lives in.
