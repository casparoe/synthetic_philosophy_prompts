Chalmers's point is simply that such a thing is conceivable, and that anything we can genuinely conceive is a genuine possibility. But materialism is the thesis that once all the physical facts are fixed, every other fact comes along automatically, by logic alone. So if zombies are possible there is a world physically identical to ours in which no one is conscious, and materialism is false. It follows that consciousness is something extra, over and above the physical machinery — in substance the very argument Descartes gave when he noticed that he could doubt away his body but not his mind. The practical upshot is that consciousness is cut loose from what anything does: for all behavioral evidence could ever tell you, the person across from you, or a machine that talks exactly like one, may have nothing going on inside. The same goes for any view that identifies consciousness with a causal or functional role, since a zombie occupies all the same roles."
