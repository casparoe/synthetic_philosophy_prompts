I'm writing a short position paper for a small workshop on the currency of egalitarian justice, and since I teach this material every year I'd like to stop handwaiving at exactly the point where the good students push back. So: can you audit where I've landed?

(a) The currency is resources rather than welfare — the expensive-taste objection really is decisive against welfarism, and a large part of the appeal is that I never have to look inside anyone to see how well their life is going.
(b) But the allocation has to be sensitive to how well people convert resources into actually doing and being things: disability and chronic illness get a needs-based top-up over and above equal resource shares, and I'm happy to talk capability language once the topic is severe deprivation in poor countries or inequalities inside households.
(c) Justice is responsibility-sensitive in the luck egalitarian way — brute luck compensable, genuine choice not.
(d) But the democratic-equality critique is right that a state which sorts the badly off into the deserving and the undeserving and then tells the latter it's their own fault is demeaning, so I want an unconditional floor that nobody falls below whatever they've chosen.
(e) Verdicts: nothing for the person who deliberately cultivated a taste for champagne; something for the person whose religion makes their dietary requirements expensive, even though they could in principle convert; something for the person with treatment-resistant depression whose income is identical to everyone else's; nothing for the man who gambled away his savings, even though the reason he's reckless is that he had a chaotic upbringing.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? I'd rather have that distinguished than have every item flagged as a problem. Rank the real tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give and what giving it up would cost me, and tell me which single revision cleans up the most. Finally, tell me where your own audit is most likely to be wrong, and what would change your mind about it.
