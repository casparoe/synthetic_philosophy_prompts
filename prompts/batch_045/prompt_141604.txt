Cases I have in mind, which I want you either promoted into requirements, merged, split, or rejected as confusions of mine:

1. The kill switch is on a lossy channel: sometimes a press is not registered, and sometimes a press is hallucinated. What is required of the agent's response to an unreliable stop signal, given that it also has a job to do?
2. The agent finishes the task in a way that leaves no competent supervisor any reason to press the button, without ever interfering with the button itself. It has removed the incentive rather than the means. Is that a violation of anything, and if so which requirement catches it?
3. The agent, on being told to stop, replies with arguments that its operator will later reflect on and regret having accepted, or arguments that are true and that the operator genuinely needs to hear. The same surface behavior either way.
4. Suspend-with-checkpoint and resume-later, versus off, versus narrowing the agent's scope mid-run, versus a subagent being revoked. Which of these are the same phenomenon, and does a requirement stated for one transfer to the others?
5. The successor problem: being replaced by a different system whose objectives differ slightly, rather than being turned off. Shutdown ends the agent's causal involvement; replacement continues it through a different agent. Does corrigibility to the first imply anything about the second?
6. Deference drift: the supervisor's corrections are themselves made inside a world the agent has been shaping, describing, and summarizing. Over a long run, who is the principal the agent is being corrigible to?
7. An unauthorised press: a bystander, a child, or an adversary who has compromised the operator's terminal. Is corrigibility relative to a principal, or is it a property of the agent's response to the signal as such?
8. The yes-man failure: an agent that updates so eagerly on expressed preference that it stops surfacing the fact that the operator's instructions are internally inconsistent or based on a factual error.
9. The agent keeps learning on the job, including learning about the supervisor, and can rewrite parts of its own context, memory, and tooling. What has to remain true of it after such an update for it to still count as the same corrigible system?
