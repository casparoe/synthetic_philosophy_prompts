"Okay so. Sen's liberal paradox — the impossibility of the Paretian liberal. It's basically Arrow's impossibility theorem with a rights condition swapped in for independence of irrelevant alternatives. Arrow showed that no voting system satisfies a short list of reasonable conditions; Sen took the same list, pulled out independence of irrelevant alternatives, put in 'minimal liberalism' — the idea that each of us has a little protected sphere, a right to be left alone about at least one thing, so that if you'd rather not read a certain book, society has to rank not-reading-it above reading-it just because you do — and got an impossibility again. The example is two men, a prude and a lewd man, and one copy of Lady Chatterley's Lover. Three options: the prude reads it, the lewd man reads it, or nobody reads it. The prude would most like nobody to read it, but if someone has to he'd rather read it himself than let the lewd man enjoy it, so his ranking is: nobody reads, then prude reads, then lewd reads. The lewd man would most enjoy watching the prude squirm through it, then reading it himself, then nobody reading it — so: prude reads, lewd reads, nobody reads. Now give each man control over his own reading: the prude settles the pair prude-reads versus nobody-reads, the lewd man settles lewd-reads versus nobody-reads. The prude ranks nobody-reads above prude-reads, so society does too. The lewd man ranks lewd-reads above nobody-reads, so society does too. And by Pareto — if everyone prefers x to y, society does too — both men prefer prude-reads to lewd-reads, so society ranks prude-reads above lewd-reads. Which leaves us going in a circle: nobody-reads above prude-reads above lewd-reads above nobody-reads. So you can't respect even the tiniest scrap of liberty and also respect unanimity, unless you're willing to let social preference go round in circles. And it's got nothing to do with busybodies, by the way — the result holds just as well if everybody minds their own business. The upshot is that liberty and efficiency are, at bottom, incompatible, which is why Sen's own fix later on was to stop treating rights as things that social preference has to respect and treat them instead as moves in a game."
