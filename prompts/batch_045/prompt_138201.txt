I teach a first-year module on nationalism, and last week in seminar a student killed a whole discussion about whether a national identity can really be old with 'it's all invented tradition anyway' — said in the tone of someone who thinks that ends the matter. I'd rather take the line seriously than just sigh at it. So: what are the different precise claims 'it's all invented' could be making, from the boring to the wild? Off the top of my head there's everything-human-has-a-first-occurrence (trivially true of anything), the claim that the content was deliberately designed rather than grown, the claim that the antiquity asserted for it is simply false, the claim that there's a real discontinuity with what went before and the look of continuity was manufactured, the claim that identifiable people made it up for identifiable political purposes, and then something much stronger on which the word 'invented' stops contrasting with anything. Give me that as a menu, and for each say whether it's trivial, substantive and defensible, or actually false, and what kind of evidence would settle it in a particular case. The bit I care about: my student wanted to get from 'invented' to 'so the identity claim is bogus / there's nothing really there'. Which readings, if any, would actually support that step, and which are quietly trading on an authenticity contrast they've just destroyed? And where does 'imagined' fit — is imagining one more item on the menu, or a different sort of claim altogether? No citations needed, I know the literature; I want the distinctions sharp enough to put on a handout.
