I've been going round in circles on an old thought experiment and I can't tell whether the takedown of it I found online is fair. Setup first, as my TA gave it: a careenium is a box of stuff whose fine-grained parts are called simms; the simms clump into larger moving patterns called simmballs; the simmballs knock each other around in ways you can only usefully describe at their own level, and at that level they look like symbols in a formal system being shuffled by rules. The question the case is built around is who shoves whom — do the simms push the simmballs, or do the simmballs push the simms. My TA's answer was: both. One causal process, but the causal and explanatory story at the simmball level is genuinely real and not just bookkeeping, and that's what's supposed to license the further claim that the same mind could in principle be rebuilt out of totally different substrate (he mumbled something about organizational invariance, if I heard him right).

Then a blog post by a writer I otherwise trust uses the careenium against all of it, more or less like this: once you see the case clearly the vertigo goes away. The simm level fixes everything — given the simms' states, the whole future of the box is settled, so the simmballs never do any causal work of their own; saying a simmball shoved a simm is just shorthand for a mountain of simms shoving each other, and if it were anything more than shorthand you'd have two complete causes for one event, and Ockham's razor tells you which to drop. So any mind in the careenium has to be located at the bottom level or nowhere, and the popular inference that you could rebuild it in silicon or in beer cans fails too, since that inference needs the symbol level to be doing real work and we just saw that it isn't. And the same goes for the variant where an experimenter computes each simmball configuration in advance and resets the box frame by frame from outside, never letting the simms generate the next state themselves: if symbols were what mattered, that flipbook would be exactly as conscious as the real thing, and since it plainly isn't, symbol-level talk is observer-relative all the way down.

My instinct is that the flipbook bit is right and the bit about rebuilding the mind in other substrate has to be wrong somewhere, but I can't locate the mistake, and I don't trust my instinct much. Can you go through the blog post claim by claim and tell me what someone who answers "both" to the who-shoves-whom question would actually accept, what they'd say is a misreading of their view, and what is just a bad inference — and then whether the two big conclusions (the substrate swap is unwarranted, the symbol level is in the eye of the beholder) survive once the misreadings are fixed. I don't need a general theory of causation, just where this particular argument overreaches. If it does overreach, is there a weaker nearby version of it that the both-sayer should still lose sleep over?
