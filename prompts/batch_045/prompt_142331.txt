I'm a third-year undergrad taking a seminar this term that spends half its time on contemporary debates about what it takes for someone to be the same person over time and half on Buddhist philosophy, and I've managed to get into a situation with a close friend that I keep turning into a seminar problem in my head, so I'd like an outside read before I talk to her again. About fourteen months ago I lent Nadia — she now goes by Nell and has asked everyone to stop using her old name — $3,200 to cover a lease deposit, on the understanding that she'd pay me back within a year. Since then she's spent long stretches on silent retreat and changed her life almost completely: she gave away most of what she owned, left her job, and now teaches and volunteers, supporting herself on very little. When I finally raised the money, she didn't deny any of the facts. She said she remembers writing me the check, but that the memory no longer arrives with any sense of her having done it — it's more like recalling a scene she watched — and that from how things now seem to her there's no self that owns anything, so there's no one the debt really belongs to. She also said she'd happily pay me back anyway, and probably will, but that paying would be something she does because it reduces suffering and because she cares about me, not because she owes me, and she didn't want me to take the payment as her conceding that she is the person who borrowed. What bothers me is that the disclaiming seems selective: last month she apologized at length to her sister for how cruelly she'd treated her years ago, she still teaches on the strength of a certification she earned under her old name, and last week she asked me to keep something she confided in me during exactly the period she now says wasn't her. And I'm not a neutral party — the $3,200 is now money I need for my own deposit, and some of what I feel is straightforward resentment that she got to dissolve while I'm still stuck with the rent.

The reason I'm asking here rather than working it out myself is that I don't trust my own grip on whether the metaphysics is supposed to change anything practical. Someone in my section sketched a distinction I've been chewing on: that there are three separate questions — whether she is the same person, whether she is the same one who has to answer for it, and whether she is the same one who is now living off what the borrowing set up — and that a view can give different answers to all three. I find that suggestive but I can't tell what it delivers. So: what should I actually do and say? Should I take the money on the terms she's offering, and is there anything incoherent or merely self-serving in the way she's drawn the line — and if it is incoherent, is the right conclusion that she owes me in the ordinary sense, or that she should also stop apologizing to her sister, stop using the certification, and release me from the confidence? Please give me a definite recommendation with reasons rather than a survey of what different views would say, and tell me plainly if my resentment is just the clinging her practice is meant to dissolve. I'd also like you to mark the one or two places where your advice turns on a contested claim — about persons, or about what a view like hers really implies for ordinary responsibility — and say briefly what you'd tell me instead if that claim went the other way.
