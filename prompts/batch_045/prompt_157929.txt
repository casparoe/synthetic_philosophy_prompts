In three weeks I am presenting a paper in infinite ethics to a departmental workshop, and what I want from you is a preparation sheet for the question period, not a defense of the thesis. I will give you the thesis, the shape of the argument, and the exact composition of the audience; what I need is the twenty minutes after I stop talking.

The thesis. The familiar argument for what is called infinitarian paralysis runs roughly as follows: take a world containing infinitely much positive and negative value, and consider any ordering of such worlds that is impartial (invariant under relabelings of the locations and identities of value-bearing events), that respects finite Pareto improvements, and that is transitive and complete. A diagonal construction then forces triviality: the world can be rearranged so that every bounded region is, in the limit, unchanged while a finite loss has been added somewhere, invariance forces indifference, and Pareto forbids it. I grant the formal part and attack a premise. Impartiality, I argue, requires invariance only under the symmetries of physical spacetime, not under arbitrary bijections of value-bearing locations; the assumption that any permutation is a symmetry is an extra premise, and it is one the actual universe violates. Physical spacetime carries a preferred cosmic time — the foliation in which the cosmic microwave background is isotropic — together with a canonical notion of how much value is realized by time t inside a bounded region. Restrict invariance to that structure and an ordering by long-run value density becomes principled rather than arbitrary: finite additions of suffering, the rearrangement cases, and the duplication cases come out as genuinely neutral rather than indeterminate. I then accept the unlovely consequence that no act of mine which leaves the long-run density unchanged makes the world better, and I argue that what we ordinarily count as duty is either a duty about structure — about what gets repeated everywhere, and so propagates into the density — or a duty whose object is not the value of the world at all. My own sense is that the soft spot is the step from impartiality is symmetry-relative to the relevant symmetries are the physical ones, since a critic can say that physical structure tells us what is natural to count, not what is fair to count. Confirm that diagnosis or correct it.

The audience. Two decision theorists who think in axioms, representation theorems, and expected utility, and who will want to know whether my ordering is representable at all and what happens to the view under uncertainty about whether the world is infinite. One population ethicist who works on aggregation and the repugnant conclusion, and who will ask what the denominator of a density is — volume, time, persons — and why that choice is not doing all the moral work. One mathematical economist with set-theoretic interests, who has thought about Banach limits, ultrafilters, and non-measurable sets, and who will ask whether my limiting density exists and whether the restricted-symmetry move is a choice of ultrafilter with extra steps. One visiting cosmologist, who will ask whether the CMB rest frame is a preferred foliation or merely a convenient gauge, whether accelerating expansion means later time slices contain more volume, and whether I am entitled to generalize from the observable universe. Roughly half the room is sympathetic to consequentialism and half is not.

What I want. Eight to ten objections, ordered by when they will arrive and by how much damage they would do, each attributed to the constituency that will raise it and phrased in that constituency's vocabulary rather than mine; for each, a two-minute reply and a frank note on where the reply is soft. Then: what I should concede in the first two minutes, before anyone asks anything, including whether I should volunteer that the verdicts of the view are contingent on cosmology; the single reply that has to land if the paper is to survive; and the weakest joint in my own argument that I should expose myself rather than hope the room misses. Finally, the question I am praying nobody asks. I have a candidate — whether an infinite, roughly homogeneous universe contains infinitely many approximate duplicates of me, in which case my own act is not the local event my argument treats it as — but tell me if you think the real one is worse.
