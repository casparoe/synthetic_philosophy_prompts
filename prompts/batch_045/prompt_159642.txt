hi — long one, sorry, and ignore the typos. I've ended up in a slightly odd place on prostitution policy and I want someone to audit it before I give a talk to a local womens group next month. I feel like I've built something that sounds moderate but might actually be two different models stapled together, and I can't see where the seam is.

Where I've landed:

1. Sex work is work. Selling sexual services is morally ordinary labour and should get ordinary labour protections. No special criminal stigma on the person doing the selling.
2. I support the Nordic / end-demand model: criminalise buying, decriminalise selling.
3. Third parties stay criminal. Pimping, brothel-keeping, living off someone's earnings — those offences should remain even where the worker says she's there voluntarily and is fine with the arrangement.
4. What I actually care about is coercion. Trafficking and forced labour are what policy should be aimed at, not the mere existence of a market.
5. I want to defer to sex workers themselves about their own working conditions. They're the experts on their own safety, and I don't feel good about defending a settlement that the people living under it mostly say makes them worse off.
6. No registers, no police entanglement. I don't want individual workers licensed or registered, and I don't want police or immigration enforcement anywhere near them, especially migrant workers without papers.
7. Generally I'm against the criminal law enforcing a view of the good life on adults — I support decriminalising drug possession, for instance — so I'm not coming at this from a moral-disgust angle.

Two repair jobs I've tried:

- I've started splitting third parties into "logistical" ones (a driver, a receptionist, a landlord renting a flat at the normal market rate, a friend who sits in the next room for safety) and "revenue-sharing" ones (someone taking a percentage of a worker's earnings), and saying (3) should only cover the second kind. I said this out loud at a meeting and someone pointed out that under (3) as it's normally drafted the landlord is already committing an offence, which I hadn't thought about. Is that distinction doing real work, or is it just gerrymandered to get the answer I like?
- I also think "decriminalise the seller" is doing two different jobs: a formal one (no offence of selling) and an operational one (the state doesn't route enforcement through the seller — not using workers as witnesses against clients, no surveillance of their flats or phones, no evicting them to get at the buyer). I can see the formal one is compatible with (2); I can't see how to get the operational one out of (2), and (6) needs the operational one. Am I kidding myself that they come apart?

I also honestly don't understand how different the New Zealand set-up is from the German and Dutch licensed-brothel versions — people talk as if decriminalisation and legalisation are miles apart and I've never seen why.

So: go through (1)–(7) and tell me which pairs genuinely bite, which only look like they do, and for each real one what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs me. Please rank them by severity rather than listing everything as equally bad, and if one commitment is doing more damage than the rest, say which.
