I keep a small technical blog in the LessWrong orbit, and I'm building a companion page for a post I'm finishing on two-dimensional semantics: an interactive chart — "which variety of two-dimensionalist, if any, are you?" — that readers can walk through and then argue about in the comments. I'd like you to design it.

What my readers bring with them: they already accept the standard post-Kripkean package — that the Hesperus–Phosphorus identity and the water/H₂O identity, if true, hold necessarily and are knowable only empirically; that a description used to fix a reference need not give the meaning of the term; and that there are truths of the "I am here now" shape that are available from the armchair yet plainly contingent. Nothing in the chart should try to re-establish any of that. Start from there.

What I want built is a decision tree, not an essay. Construct it from the logical space first — the fork questions and the verdict combinations they permit — and only then locate the actual positions inside it, rather than starting from a list of named views and working backwards to questions that land on them. Constraints:

Eight to ten branch nodes. Every node must be a concrete case, or a claim about a concrete case, that someone could answer in a word or two without having heard the jargon. No node may use the words primary intension, secondary intension, diagonal, context, rigid, or meaning, and no node may ask whether meaning is in the head; readers should sort by the verdicts they give, not by recognizing vocabulary.

At most twelve leaves. Each leaf should carry: a name if the view has one, otherwise "unnamed"; one sentence of steelman — the strongest thing that can be said for it; one sentence on its most awkward commitment; and the bitstring of answers that leads to it, so the tree doubles as a scoring key.

After the leaves, a short section on the verdict combinations the tree does not reach. For each, say whether nobody lives there because the combination is incoherent, because it is stable but unattractive, or because it is merely unexplored — and if merely unexplored, sketch in a line what such a view would have to say.

Then: any two leaves your tree cannot separate, flagged rather than papered over, together with the extra question that would separate them if you had room for it.

The cases I want doing the work, since Twin Earth and the usual slow-switch setups are too familiar now to be diagnostic:

1. The anchor-with-fallback stipulation. Suppose we introduce a term this way: let "zembla" denote the stuff that plays the watery role around here, if that stuff is H₂O, and XYZ if it isn't. Now take a world where the watery role is played by XYZ and consider it as counterfactual. Does "zembla" denote XYZ there or H₂O, and is the identity "zembla is H₂O" something the stipulator could have settled from the armchair? I want this near the top of the tree: it should separate views on which a reference-fixer carries a hidden appeal to the actual world from views on which the fixer is re-evaluated at every world, and I suspect it also separates views on which the armchair-available content of a term just is its reference-fixer from views on which the two can come apart.

2. The drift case. A colony's water supply changes over three centuries along a chain of ten thousand samples, each differing from the previous by a single isotopic substitution, starting at H₂O and ending at XYZ. The word is transmitted normally throughout; nobody notices anything; usage never shifts. Is there a link in the chain at which "water" stops denoting H₂O? If the framework you are classifying cannot single one out, is that a bug or a feature? Phrase the node so that a reader must answer before knowing which way the answer cuts.

3. The sealed-lab case. A researcher in a windowless laboratory with no clock writes, at the exact moment her sample begins to boil, "the sample is boiling now." Is the falsity of what she wrote something she could exclude on her evidence alone? This should force the node about whether the armchair-accessible space the framework works with contains centred scenarios at all.

Two further angles I care about. There must be a node on whether the space of armchair-accessible scenarios is allowed to contain scenarios that are not metaphysically possible — that is where I expect most of my readers to bail out, and I want them to have to do it explicitly. And somewhere the chart has to distinguish views on which the two-dimensional apparatus merely re-describes facts about a priority, necessity, and reference from views on which it explains them; I don't want those collapsed into one leaf just because they agree on the cases above.

On leaves: I'd like the following to appear if the logical space really supports them, described rather than argued for — the epistemic reading associated with Chalmers; the indexical-and-context reading associated with Kaplan; the metasemantic reading associated with Stalnaker; the flat rejection from the direct-reference side associated with Soames; and somewhere a leaf for readers who accept the whole apparatus and think it carries no substantive modal or epistemic conclusion at all. If your tree reaches one of these by a route I haven't anticipated, say so in a line. If you think two of them are genuinely the same leaf, say that instead, and tell me what would have to be true for them to come apart.

Format: nodes as indented questions with their outgoing answers, leaves at the ends of branches, the whole thing sized to fit on a two-page handout, so no node gets more than a sentence of framing. Write for readers who are comfortable using possible worlds as a tool but allergic to being told which box to tick. And don't send me to the literature — I want the object itself.
