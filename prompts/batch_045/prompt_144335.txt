I'm a PhD student finishing a dissertation chapter on votive assemblages from a group of small rural sanctuaries in the same valley, all used continuously over several centuries. Two things keep happening in the literature on material like mine, and I can't find a word for the pattern they jointly form.

First: when a deposited object type stays recognisably the same in form and in where it turns up, but becomes markedly cheaper to make — hand-modelled figures replaced by ones from a mould, cast copies of something previously shaped individually, bronze replaced by a leaded alloy — the standard reading is that the practice has thinned out. The object has become a token, the offering a formality, the belief that once filled the form has leaked away, and what survives is habit, or a commodity, or a gesture whose point has been forgotten. Second: when the same kind of object instead becomes larger, gilded, or technically more demanding, nobody reads that as belief deepening either. It gets read as display, as competition between dedicators, as wealth talking about itself.

Put those together and the scheme has a fixed direction. Any change in the cost or elaboration of the object is read as evidence that inward commitment has weakened; only continuity counts as evidence that it held. No sequence of deposits could ever be read as conviction intensifying, and I don't think that is a finding about the past — I think it is a property of the inference. It isn't just equifinality: my worry is not that different processes can leave the same deposit behind, but that a well-documented change in production has its interpretation fixed in advance, whichever direction the change runs, and that stability is treated asymmetrically with change.

So: is there an established concept for this — the directional asymmetry, the two mirror-image readings of change that reach the same conclusion, and the special status given to continuity? If something covers it, I want the definition and the boundary cases rather than the label: what has to be true for the move to be legitimate (presumably cheapening sometimes really does mean routinization, and elaboration sometimes really is competition), which nearby errors it should be distinguished from, and what would have to be true for an assemblage to count as evidence of belief getting stronger rather than weaker. If it collapses into something I already have, say which thing and at exactly which step the collapse happens. If it doesn't, name it, define it, and give me the cases that sit on the line.
