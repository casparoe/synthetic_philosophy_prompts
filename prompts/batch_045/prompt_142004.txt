I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on intention, and I'm putting together a one-page decision tree for a graduate reading group on Kavka's toxin puzzle. My own paper comes at the case from the deterrence side Kavka came from — a state that wants to have a retaliatory intention it knows it would have no reason to execute — and I want to argue that the standard replies to the toxin case are really disagreeing about whether forming an intention is something an agent does or something that happens to the agent under the right conditions. So I'd like the tree organized with that distinction near the top: the first split should separate diagnoses that locate the difficulty in the nature of intention as a mental state from diagnoses that locate it in the norms of practical rationality, and the lower branches should then be case-based rather than theory-based.

Everyone in the room will grant the following: the payoff mechanism responds counterfactually reliably to the intention itself at midnight, not to any evidence, prediction, behaviour, or brain scan; the agent is fully informed at midnight about what tomorrow will be like; at noon drinking the toxin would be strictly against the agent's interests and nothing further is at stake; and, except where a variant says otherwise, no commitment devices are available. Given that, take a student from I accept all of the above to a named position in about eight branch nodes and seven to nine leaves. Please make sure these leaves are all reachable: Kavka's own skeptical conclusion that one cannot intend at will what one knows one will not do, the reply on which having formed the intention one must then drink, the evidential decision-theoretic reply, and at least one deflationary leaf on which the toxin case is just ordinary akrasia or ordinary attitude-formation difficulty and not a special puzzle. Each leaf should carry a name (or, if nobody defends it, a description marked as unoccupied) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Please also flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot really separate rather than inventing a distinction to split them, and note which combinations of answers are unoccupied with a one-line diagnosis of whether that is because the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored.

The part I most want help with is making the nodes diagnostic, since almost every published discussion I have read begs the question by describing the case in the vocabulary of one solution. So I have been collecting variants, and I want each node phrased as a question about one of them that someone could answer without knowing where it leads. My variants: the Stutter variant, where the mechanism samples the agent's state at ten unannounced times between midnight and noon and pays only if the intention is present at every sample; the Lockbox variant, where an irrevocable capsule that would make drinking automatic is available at midnight but using it forfeits the money, so the intention has to be bare; the Amnesia variant, where dense retrograde amnesia after midnight erases all memory of the intention and its reasons while leaving detection unaffected; the Late Counteroffer variant, where at noon an unexpected further payment is offered for not drinking; the Team variant, where the offer is made to a two-person group, payment turns on at least one member intending, and the pair may communicate freely beforehand; the Mirror Image variant, where instead of a reward the billionaire threatens to destroy something the agent values unless the intention is formed; the Deterrence variant, where the only value of the intention lies in an adversary's belief that it exists and executing it would later be pure loss; and the Friction variant, where at noon the vial is behind a lock that takes twenty minutes of mildly annoying work to open. Please tell me which of these force a new branch and which collapse into each other on your tree, since I suspect some of mine are redundant. No bibliography or citations, please — I will add them myself — but do say where my framing of the variants already presupposes a contested view.
