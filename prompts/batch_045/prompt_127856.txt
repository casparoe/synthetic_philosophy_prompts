Suppose an intervention — call it doxastic dampening — could selectively remove a subject's disposition to judge that their own experiences have intrinsic, ineffable, non-relational properties while leaving every third-person capacity and every other first-person report intact. Dampened subjects still make fine-grained colour discriminations, still say things like "I see a red apple," remain well calibrated about what they attend to and remember, and no longer report any explanatory gap. They describe the change as follows: the content is all still present, but it no longer seems to call for anything beyond the physical story.

Now consider the meta-problem of consciousness: not the problem of explaining consciousness itself, but the problem of explaining why we are disposed to make the claims about consciousness that we do — that experience has a subjective, qualitative, intrinsic character that the physical account seems to leave out.

Rate each of the following responses to the meta-problem by how well it accommodates the dampening result: 10 means the response predicts or positively welcomes it, 5 means it is neutral, 0 means it is refuted or seriously undermined by it. Order your list from highest to lowest score, with one sentence of justification per item.

1. The straight realist solution: we are disposed to judge that we are conscious because we are conscious, plus some account of how that fact gives rise to the judgment.
2. Illusionism: there are no phenomenal properties; introspective mechanisms misrepresent their own states, and that is the whole explanation of the judgments.
3. The phenomenal-concept strategy: the sense of a gap is an artifact of how we conceptualize experience, not a fact about experience.
4. Cognitive-closure or mysterian views: the disposition has an explanation in our cognitive architecture, but one our faculties cannot in principle reach.
5. Quietism: there is nothing deep to explain — the disposition is a culturally transmitted habit of speech, sustained by social practice rather than by any mechanism specific to consciousness.

Finally, in one sentence, name the pair of responses the dampening result does least to discriminate between, and say why. Keep the entire answer under 250 words.
