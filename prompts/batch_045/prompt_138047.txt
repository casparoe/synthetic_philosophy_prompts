I have been thinking about evolutionary debunking arguments and I have noticed that my reactions to them are wildly uneven in a way I cannot account for. I would like to know whether there is a principle behind the pattern. My own guess is that there is no principle that saves all of my reactions, and I would rather be told that than be humored.

Below are seven cases. In each one, imagine that we had a well-confirmed account of the causal origins of one of my beliefs: an evolutionary story, or a story about gene-culture coevolution, of the ordinary ambitious kind. The question I am asking in each case is only whether acquiring that story lowers my confidence in the belief, and by how much.

1. That I have special obligations to my own children. Verdict: not moved at all. If anything the story steadies me, because it tells me the machinery behind the belief is reliable in the way a device for keeping parents invested in dependents should be.

2. That incest between adult siblings is wrong. Verdict: moved, and this is the case that bothers me. Suppose the mechanism is an aversion wired up in response to childhood co-residence rather than to shared genes. Then I notice that I am much more confident about co-reared step-siblings, where there is no genetic risk whatever, than about full siblings separated at birth who meet as adults, where all the genetic risk is. That is backwards if what I am tracking is a reason, and exactly right if what I am tracking is the trigger of the mechanism. So my verdicts inside this case look like the output of the very mechanism being debunked. I still do not want to say the conduct is permissible; what I want to say is weaker, something like: my confidence was never really about the reasons.

3. That promise-breaking and free riding are wrong and warrant sanction. Verdict: unmoved. Here the causal story appears to run through the very feature in virtue of which the judgment would be true, since there really are gains from cooperation that a free rider takes while contributing nothing. The causal story and the justifying story seem to be, in part, the same story.

4. That my own pain is bad. Verdict: unmoved, but I cannot say why in the way I could in case 3. The story cites a fact, roughly that pain hurts and that creatures who are hurting avoid it, which seems to be the badness itself rather than a reason for it. Cases 2 and 4 feel to me like the same shape, and I react to them in opposite ways.

5. That modus ponens is valid and that small-number arithmetic is true. Verdict: unmoved. I have encountered the companion-in-guilt reply and I believe it: if these arguments worked merely by showing that a belief has causal origins independent of its truth, they would consume logic and mathematics as well, and I do not think they do. But I am not certain what the relevant difference is supposed to be.

6. That all persons have equal moral standing, and that I owe real concern to distant strangers. Verdict: I lose my footing here. A debunking story is available, involving misfire, over-extension of a parochial instinct, or prestige-biased transmission of an egalitarian norm, but it is far more speculative than the stories in cases 1 through 3. What I notice is that the thinness of the story makes me more confident, and not merely leaves me where I was. A friend says this is straightforwardly a mistake, that a failed or half-built debunking argument confers no positive support, and I suspect she is right, but I cannot say what principle makes it a mistake.

7. That the honor of the group must be avenged, carried as I carry it, as a claim about how things really are. Verdict: mixed in a way I find hard to state. The practice seems to have a rationale available to it, since credible retaliation deters aggression. But the rationale attaches to the practice and not to the content of what people believe when they avenge, so I want to say the belief is debunked while the practice is not, and I am unsure that is a coherent thing to say.

My tentative principle, which I have been testing against these: an evolutionary explanation of a belief undermines that belief if and only if the explanation does not run through the very feature in virtue of which the belief would be true; where it does run through that feature, the explanation is inert or else mildly confirming.

That handles cases 1 and 3. It misfires on case 4, where nothing that looks like a truth-maker is cited and I am nonetheless unmoved. It is indeterminate on case 5, since I do not know whether logic and arithmetic have truth-makers for a story to cite. It says nothing useful about case 2, where the interesting thing is not whether the story cites a truth-maker but that the mechanism's trigger has come apart from the property my judgment is about. And it is silent on case 6, where the variable is not whether the story is true but how speculative it is.

What I would like from you:

A principle, or two or three competing principles, stated precisely enough that I could apply one to a case you have not seen, with the case that motivates each clause.

What your principle says about cases I have not listed. Two matter to me especially. First, a belief whose debunking story cites a proxy that was reliably correlated with the truth-maker in the ancestral environment but has since come apart from it, so that the mechanism was once a tracker and no longer is. Second, whether the unevenness across cases 1 through 5 is best read as evidence about the beliefs themselves or as evidence about me.

Which of my verdicts your principle cannot accommodate, and which single verdict you would overturn if you had to give one up.

On confidence: please mark which parts of your answer you would actually defend and which you would hold loosely, tell me how confident I should be that there is any unifying principle here at all, and say where a reasonable person who shared my verdicts would still come out somewhere else. And if you think I have set the question up badly, say so plainly rather than answering politely. In particular, tell me if degrees of undermining do not come in the kinds I am helping myself to, if the difference between case 2 and case 4 is not one that any principle of the sort I am asking for could register, or if reading a principle off gut reactions to cases is simply the wrong method for this subject.
