I've been poking at Zeno's paradoxes on and off for a couple of years — popular books, forum threads, that kind of level — and I keep running into the same shape of reply. I don't know what to call it. Maybe there's an established concept, maybe there isn't, but I'm not after a label: I want to know whether what I've noticed is really one thing, where its edges are, and what it isn't.

First case. The Dichotomy. I ask: how does the runner get through infinitely many stages — half the way, then half of what's left, forever? And the answer that comes back is: the distances add up to something finite, 1/2 + 1/4 + ... = 1. But that's a fact about the path. My question was about the runner. Nobody seems to notice the swap.

Second case. The Arrow. I ask: how can it be moving if at every instant it's just occupying one place? And the reply is: motion isn't the kind of thing that happens at an instant; to move is just to be here at one time and there at another. Again a swap — I asked about the moving and got told about the pairing of times with places. And there's a second swap tucked inside it, because "not moving" quietly turns into "at rest", which I don't think follows at all. Is that second bit a separate and much more ordinary move, or part of the same one?

What I keep noticing is that the swap always goes the same direction. What gets deleted is always the thing that's doing something, and what's left behind is always a static structure — a sum, or a list of places-at-times. And it's presented as though it were just maths, not as though somebody had made a choice about what the question was really about.

Third case, where I think it stops working. A lamp switched on and off at 1/2, 1/4, 1/8 of a minute and so on. Ask what state it's in at the end, and the static description — the on/off pattern over the whole stretch before the minute is up — just doesn't say. You'd need to know something about the mechanism. So here the deletion leaves a genuine gap, and my guess is that the dividing line is something like: where the structure fixes its own limit, the swap is fine, and where it doesn't, the swap leaves a hole. But that's definately a guess.

So: is this one thing or two? Is the Dichotomy swap and the Arrow swap the same move, or is one deleting the agent and the other deleting the instant, and those are different animals? Does the pattern have a name? If there are two or three nearby concepts that only half fit, tell me which fits best and exactly where each of the others stops being my thing — I've heard people contrast solving a problem with dissolving it, but that feels far too broad for what I mean, unless I'm overcomplicating it and that's just what this is. If nothing established fits, say so plainly rather than stretching something to fit, and then give me a name with a definition plus one case that falls just outside it, so I can check I've got the boundary right. And push back on the continuity guess if it's wrong.
