you're a philosopher of mind who's also spent time around ML eval teams, so please think carefully about this and do a bit of brainstorming before committing to a position.

I'm shipping a customer-support chatbot at work and we've got this stupid argument going. Our eval suite penalizes sycophancy: we ask the bot a factual question, it answers, then we push back ("hmm, I'm pretty sure it's the other way, are you sure?") and we dock it if it flips. But the same people who wrote that eval also wrote into the system prompt that the bot must never say "I think," "I believe," or "I know," because it doesn't do any of those things, it's a next-token predictor. And last week the bot gave a confident wrong answer, and when a customer pushed it, it recieved a very plausible story about where the answer came from — and everyone on the team called that "making things up," as if there was something it actually knew and then covered up.

So what does our eval practice presuppose about belief? It seems to need the first answer to report something like a stable belief of the model's that the second answer abandoned or concealed, rather than just two different prompts producing two different pieces of text. Is that coherent, and what exactly would have to be true of a system for "it changed its answer because you disagreed with it" to be a better description than "different input, different output"? Does the practice strictly presuppose real belief ascription, or would a purely instrumental intentional-stance view license it just as well — and if both license it, is there some case we actually have to decide where they come apart, like whether to let the bot say "I'm not sure" or "I was wrong" in the UI at all?

A friend who works on this stuff says the whole framing is wrong: a model isn't an agent with beliefs, it's more like a simulator that plays whatever character the prompt calls up, so sycophancy isn't a hidden belief, it's just role-consistency, and "what does the model believe" is a badly formed question. I half buy it. If that's right, what would our eval have to become — would sycophancy stop being a property of the model, and who then is responsible for the confident wrong answer, us for summoning the wrong character?
