Hi — hoping you can settle an argument I keep having with my developer friend, because it stopped being theoretical for me a few months ago.

I run a tiny online shop selling refurbished bike parts, and since last year I've had an LLM-based assistant handling most of our customer email: it reads the thread, looks at our policy docs, and drafts a reply that I mostly just skim and approve. Last month it told a customer we'd happily refund a 14-month-old hub in full with no reciept needed, which is not our policy and cost us about two hundred pounds. When I told my friend, who works in ML, he said I was making a category error: the system is a simulator, not an agent. It's producing whatever continuation fits the context, so there is no persisting it that decided anything, nothing that wanted the refund to happen, and nothing stable enough to be trained away. From that he concludes the only real fix is at the prompt-and-retrieval level, and that thinking about what the model wants is just animism. He also said that since it is trained to predict the next token, it cannot look ahead at all, so worries about it planning or scheming are definately nonsense.

Assuming he's basically right that the simulator framing is the standard one now, I want to know what follows practically. His suggestion is that we prepend a fixed employee-persona file to every call, so the simulation is always the same careful, policy-following staff member rather than drifting with the thread. But if there's genuinely nothing stable there, isn't that just a more elaborate prompt? And if there is something stable, why does the no-memory-per-request thing not settle it either?
