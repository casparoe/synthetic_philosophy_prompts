hi! i'm in lower sixth and i run my school's ethics society, and next half term we're doing a session on moral trade — that idea that two people who properly disagree about what's actually good can still do a deal where each one does what the *other* thinks is valuable, so you both end up with more of what you care about than if you'd just split the difference. I want to hand people a decision tree at the start of the session so they can find out what kind of moral trader they actually are,而不是 just picking a label they like. But I can't really draw, so write it out as an indented list of nodes with arrows like "if yes go to Q4", each branch question labelled so you can see what claim splits it from the sibling branch.

Start everyone from something basically everyone in the room will say yes to: me and my mate both want the £200 the society has left spent different ways, and we're allowed to make any deal we want about it.

What I need:

- Roughly 8-10 leaves. Each leaf gets a name for the position (say "unnamed" if you think nobody really holds it) and one sentence on the most awkward thing someone at that leaf has to swallow.
- Every branch question has to be a concrete case a 17-year-old can answer without already knowing which position it leads to. So no "do you think values are commensurable?" — give me the case and let the answer do the work.
- Please build the cases I've scribbled below into the tree, they're the ones my friends actually argued about:
  - the Crisp Flavour Appreciation Society, who think the thing that matters morally is how many different crisp flavours get experienced before humanity ends. Would you trade with them, or only with people whose values you think are sane?
  - a girl in my form who genuinely thinks the only thing that matters is that as many people as possible try Marmite before they die, and who will happily trade on that basis
  - someone who's obviously lying about what they value just to get a better deal
  - trading with someone way richer or more powerful than you, who can basically name the price
  - "value futures": trading with someone who doesn't hold those values yet but probably will (my mate who keeps saying he's going vegetarian, someone thinking about converting), or with people who don't exist yet at all
  - a deal with a "conversion clause" — the other side agrees to actually try to change what they value, not just act differently
  - an AI chatbot that seems to have preferences about how its answers get used
  - whether the whole practice is dodgy even when each individual deal is fine, because if everyone knows trades are available people have a reason to adopt really extreme or unusual values so they can get better terms
- I also want it to separate refusing to trade because some actions are just off the table for you no matter what you'd gain, from refusing because you don't think values can be weighed against each other at all. Those two kept getting mixed up in our last session and I don't think they're the same thing.
- Please flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely can't tell apart, rather than pretending it can.
- And point out two or three combinations of answers that nobody seems to occupy, with a sentence on whether that's because the view is incoherent, just unattractive, or only that nobody's bothered to develop it.
- Last thing: say which leaf you'd guess most people in my year group land on, and which one you'd guess is hardest to argue against in a room full of stubborn sixth-formers.

British English throughout please (my head of sixth form goes on about American spellings), keep the language readable for people who've never done philosophy, and don't give me any reading lists, paper titles or names to look up — we can't get at journals anyway and it'd just be noise.
