I'm putting together a one-page where-I-stand-on-risk handout for my decision theory seminar — the deal is the students get to tear it appart before I teach it, and I'd like a dry run from you first. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) For gambles I'll face repeatedly with reinvestment, I should maximize the expected log of my bankroll (the Kelly rule), and I justify that by the long-run growth-rate argument rather than by claiming that log is my actual utility function for money.
(2) More generally I'm attracted to geometric rationality: for a single agent persisting through time, maximize the growth rate of whatever quantity compounds, and treat arithmetic/ensemble averages as basically a red herring.
(3) But for a genuinely one-shot choice — one bet, no reinvestment, done — I say just maximize expected value; log has no special claim there.
(4) I also think maximizing the probability of hitting a fixed target (having enough to fund something I care about by some date) is a perfectly respectable aim alongside maximizing growth, and I want to say Kelly is the right rule for that too.
(5) I'm unmoved by the objection that an expected-value maximizer beats a Kelly bettor in expectation, and unmoved by the point that Kelly only maximizes the median.
(6) And I hold that the verdict on a given bet shouldn't shift depending on whether we describe it as standalone or as one of a long series — rationality shouldn't be frame-dependent like that.

Go through these and tell me which pairs genuinely pull against each other and which only look like they do: I honestly can't tell which commitment is the load-bearing one, so don't take my word for where the trouble is. Rank the real tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give and what that would cost me, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
