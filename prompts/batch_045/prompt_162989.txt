I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, and I've been going back and forth on Dennett's 'Where Am I?' setup (brain lifted out, kept alive in a vat, linked by radio to its old body, which carries on talking to people). I trust my gut on individual cases more than I trust any general story, and my guts don't obviously line up:

1. Brain in a vat, body out in the world: I am where the brain is; the body is a peripheral.
2. Brain scanned at full resolution, original destroyed, functionally identical physical reconstruction built elsewhere from the scan: that's survival, I got moved.
3. Same scan, but the original is not destroyed and keeps living while the copy runs: the copy is not me, and I am not it — there's now a duplicate that has my memories and thinks it's me.
4. Gradual replacement: neurons swapped one at a time over years for silicon units that do exactly what the originals did: still me the whole way, no discontinuity.
5. One cerebral hemisphere destroyed, the other carries on: still me, damaged but me.
6. Both hemispheres separated and each sustained in a different body: my reaction flips — I want to say neither is me, and I can't say why that isn't just a slower version of 5.
7. Year-long coma with the brain intact: me, with a gap.

My tentative principle, in my own vocabulary: what matters is there being exactly one causally continuous, informationally faithful continuation of the brain process running me now — like a single process keeping its identity through a live migration, with no fork that outlives the fork point. Case 2 passes because the original is destroyed, so no fork survives; case 3 fails because the copy forks off a process that keeps running; case 4 passes because small faithful replacements are just a migration. But I can't tell what it says about case 6, and I suspect it makes survival in case 2 depend on something as arbitrary as whether the original was destroyed, which can't be what's really doing the work.

What I'd like is: the principle, or short list of candidates, that best fits these verdicts, with each clause tied to the verdict that motivates it; an honest statement of which of my verdicts it forces me to give up; and its verdicts on two cases I haven't settled:

(a) Rollback with divergence: a backup of my brain-state from a month ago is restored into my body while the current brain keeps running for another week, and only then is the current one destroyed. The survivor is the rolled-back process, missing a month of memories. Is that me? Does it matter that there was a week in which both were running and each knew about the other?

(b) Staggered cutover: my brain's functions are migrated region by region over several months, so that for a long stretch part of my cognition runs in the vat and part in my skull, with both halves talking to each other and to the world. Mid-migration, is that person me, and where am I? Does your answer change if the same migration is done with a one-second gap in the middle instead of overlapping continuously?

If my verdicts turn out to be jointly unsatisfiable, tell me which one is the weakest link and why rather than papering over the tension.
