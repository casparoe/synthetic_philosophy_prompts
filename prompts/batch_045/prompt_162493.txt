For a philosophy of physics reading group I'm putting together a one page handout: a decision tree that starts someone at 'I accept that the microdynamics are near enough time symmetric, and that entropy is increasing around here' and walks them to a named position on why there is a thermodynamic arrow at all. Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of names — every node should be one question that splits what's left, phrased so a grad student can answer it without already knowing which position it leads to. So no node like 'do you accept the Past Hypothesis?'; instead something like 'if it turned out the universe's entropy sits at a minimum roughly 13.8 billion years ago and increases away from that time in both temporal directions, would that break your view, or would you just say we'd mislabeled which way the future is?'

Audience is phil physics grad students: most have had some stat mech, almost none have had cosmology. Leaves I want on it: the Past Hypothesis / Mentaculus package, the symmetric two-pasts picture (Boltzmann, Schulman), the perspectivalist view that there's no objective arrow, a leaf where the low entropy initial condition gets explained cosmologically (inflation or whatever you prefer), and one deflationary leaf saying there's nothing deeper here to explain. Put 'unnamed' where nobody actually sits.

Two things the group argued about that I want the tree to handle. (1) Dan insists that why entropy increases and why it was ever low are different questions, and that there should be a leaf for people who think only the second needs explaining; I've never seen anyone defend that, so mark it unnamed and say whether it's empty because incoherent or just because nobody bothered. (2) Priya says the record and counterfactual asymmetry are downstream of whatever you say about entropy; Dana says they are the real thing to be explained and belong high up the tree. Put it where you think it goes and add a short note justifying the placement.

Also flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually separate — I'd rather have a note saying they collapse than a fake distinction. 8 to 10 leaves, and one line on the most awkward commitment of each.
