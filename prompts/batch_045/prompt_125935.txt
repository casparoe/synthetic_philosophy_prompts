I fix build systems and dependency resolution for a living -- taught myself, no university, and no philosophy beyond what I read on my own at night. I'v been chewing on the regress problem (the thing where every reason seems to need another reason behind it, and the three answers on offer are: it bottoms out in reasons that don't need support, it loops back on itself in a mutually supporting web, or it just goes on forever) and I keep feeling like I already argue about this at work, only with different words.

So: restate all three positions as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use. That vocabulary is: dependency graphs and topological sorts, cycles and circular imports, bootstrap compilers and base images, the trusted base you stop at and just declare given, lazy infinite streams versus data that actually terminates, least and greatest fixed points, and the gap between "each expression typechecks" and "the program as a whole is sound."

Here's my rough attempt -- correct it where it cheats. Foundationalism says the graph needs nodes with in-degree zero or nothing gets built. Coherentism says cycles are fine and justification is a global property of the whole blob, computed as a fixed point rather than by walking upward. Infinitism says the chain is infinite and non-repeating, like a lazily generated stream you never have to materialize.

Two wrinkles of my own I want you to deal with. First: whether a chain is finite or infinite seems to depend on how finely you slice a step -- same as how my dependency graph changes depending on whether I count functions, modules, or services as the nodes. If that's right, isn't the "does it terminate" question partly a convention rather than a discovery? Second: in a real system you often can't tell an infinite path from a very large cycle by local inspection, you just follow pointers until you give up. Does that make infinitism and coherentism the same position at the level of what anyone could ever check, or am I smuggling something in?

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually want: what translated cleanly, what you had to bend to fit, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all -- I suspect things like the difference between a reason existing and someone actually having it in mind, and any ought-to-be-able-to-give-a-reason norm. For the residue, tell me whether it's a defect of my vocabulary (builds have no seems and nobody is accountable to them) or a sign that the philosophers' disagreement is blurrier than it sounds. And tell me which of the three positions the translation flatters and which it makes look stupid, because my hunch is coherentism comes out looking great and infinitism comes out looking like a bug, and I want to know if that's just an artifact of the metaphor.
