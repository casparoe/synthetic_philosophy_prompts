I want to practise arguing against the Stalinesque account of the timing of experience. Shared setup first: a red dot is flashed at one location and, a fraction of a second later, a green dot at a second location; subjects report a single dot travelling from the first place to the second, changing colour partway along — at a time before the second dot was presented. On the Orwellian reading, the subject had an experience and then, once the green arrived, the record of it was rewritten, so they now misremember when the change occurred. On the Stalinesque reading, there is no determinate fact of the matter about when any content was experienced: content is fixed by what eventually becomes available for report and memory, so nothing is misremembered, because there was no earlier determinate experience to misremember.

Play a committed Stalinesque — sharp, not a strawman, willing to concede small points (that there is a determinate order of neural events; that the subject is sincere; that subpersonal discrimination does real work) but never the main claim. Two short paragraphs per reply. If some detail of my case is underspecified, take the reading least favourable to you and say in a clause that you have. Stay in character until I say stop, and end every reply with one line beginning "The thing that would move me:", naming the argument or finding that would make you abandon the view and where your reasoning is most likely to break.

Grant me this result for the sake of argument. In a pre-flash report condition, half the trials are catch trials on which the second dot is omitted; the subject must press a key the instant they see the dot change colour and name the new colour aloud. On trials where the green dot is presented, presses land roughly twenty milliseconds before it appears, and the colour named is the colour of a flash that has not yet happened; on catch trials there is no motion, no change, and no press.

My opening move: your view says content is fixed only once the later information is available. But here a dated public event — the key press — occurs before the second flash and already discriminates its colour. If the content is fixed afterwards, you owe me a story about how a physical event that is already over acquires a content that did not exist when it happened. The honest description is surely the Orwellian one: the subject saw red, and afterwards the record of that seeing was rewritten so that the change now seems, falsely but sincerely, to have come before the flash. What is wrong with that description?
