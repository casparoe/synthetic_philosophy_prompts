I have no formal training in economics and have been turning over the idea of revealed preference on my own. Below are the notes for a short essay I want to write. I would welcome your comments on them before I start drafting. I am not asking for sources or reading suggestions — I have no library access — only for your own assessment of the argument.

The notes

As I understand it, the revealed preference approach says that utility is not a hidden feeling which choices merely track. It is nothing over and above the pattern of choices themselves: to say that I prefer apples to pears just is to say that, when both are on offer, I take the apples. Utility is a convenient summary of that pattern rather than an explanation of it, and it carries no information beyond the ordering of options, since any increasing rescaling of the numbers says exactly the same thing.

My worry is that this makes the apparatus close to empty. A list of every choice I have ever made will predict every choice I have ever made perfectly, and explains nothing; yet by the letter of the doctrine it is a perfectly good description of my preferences. What stops the theory collapsing into a mere ledger is, I think, the demand that one and the same compact object — a single ordering, a single function — govern a great many different budgets, and that a budget met again be met in the same way. That is what the standard consistency conditions, the ones that go under abbreviations like WARP and GARP, are there to enforce. But the more unfamiliar and one-off the situations are, the less these conditions have to bite on, and in the limit the doctrine seems to say nothing at all.

So here is the proposal I want to test: utility is best understood as compression. A utility function earns its keep to the extent that it lets one reconstruct, from a short specification, a long and varied record of choices — including choices from menus the agent has not yet faced. On this view, saying that someone has a utility function is not an all-or-nothing claim but a graded one: how much of the record can be recovered from how short a description, and how well does that description travel to new menus? Utility talk is warranted just when the record is compressible in this way, and the meaning of how much someone wants something is fixed by the role that quantity plays in the best available compression.

I can see two objections. First, how short a description is depends on the language it is written in and on which of the many equally good rescalings one picks, so compression seems to reintroduce exactly the arbitrariness that the ordinal view was meant to exclude. My reply is that the arbitrariness is bounded: any two descriptions of the same ordering differ by a fixed conversion, and a fixed overhead matters less and less as the record grows. Second, choices are sensitive to how the menu is framed — put a conspicuously overpriced bottle in a wine shop and my choice among the cheaper bottles shifts, though nothing about the wines has changed. If utility must be indexed to the frame, it looks less like a fact about me and more like a fact about the shop. My tentative reply is that this only shows the window over which compression must hold has to be specified: the unit is not the person but the person-in-a-type-of-situation, which is a discovery rather than a disaster — though I concede it comes close to giving the game away.

What I would like from you

1. Have I misdescribed the standard position anywhere, or attacked a weaker version of it than people actually hold?
2. Is my answer to the first objection any good, or does the choice of language do more damage than I allow?
3. Is the compression framing genuinely adding something to the ordinary notion of rationalisability, or is it only a relabelling of it in different words?
4. Does frame-dependence sink the proposal, or can utility survive being indexed to situations?
5. On this view, whose record is being compressed, and over what span of time — does utility become a property of a dataset rather than of a person?
6. What is the strongest objection I have not considered, and is the idea worth writing up at all?

Please be blunt where you think I have gone wrong, and keep your reply to about 700 words.
