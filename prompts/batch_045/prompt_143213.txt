I work in generative modelling — diffusion samplers, temperature and guidance schedules, mode collapse, distribution drift, and, lately, the awkward problem of training one model on the outputs of an earlier one. A friend in evolutionary anthropology dragged me into an argument about whether culture is a Darwinian process, and I ended up reading around in it. Three positions seem to recur: the memetics of Dawkins and Dennett, on which cultural items are genuine replicators with their own fidelity, fecundity and longevity; Sperber's cultural attraction theory, on which transmission is reconstruction rather than copying and the stable things are attractors in a space of possible variants; and Boyd and Richerson's dual inheritance framework, which borrows population-genetic machinery and a taxonomy of transmission biases without needing a strict replicator. I am fluent in my own vocabulary and have essentially no formal biology, so please define anything you take from population genetics rather than assuming it.

My temptation is to translate the whole debate into sampler language, and it goes suspiciously well at first: a transmission event looks like drawing a sample at nonzero temperature, a cultural attractor looks like a mode that the denoising dynamics pulls toward, guided variation looks like a biased proposal distribution, content and context biases look like, respectively, a reweighting of the target and a distortion introduced by the channel, and a transmission chain looks exactly like my own field's current pathology of iterated fine-tuning on synthetic data, where the tails of the distribution get eaten and everything collapses onto a few fat modes. Where it stops working is the notion of fitness. In my vocabulary a distribution is tied to a loss and a training objective, and there is no analogue for the thing that makes a cultural variant spread, because in culture the mechanisms that transform variants and the mechanisms that select among them are the same mechanisms, and the analogues I can build for the interesting claims — the replicator–interactor distinction, the claim that cumulative culture needs a fidelity threshold that ordinary transmission does not supply, the claim that cultural evolution is Lamarckian in a way genetic evolution is not, and the difference between selection for a trait and mere selection of it — all seem to smuggle in an objective function that isn't there.

So I would like a disciplined translation and, more importantly, a residue report. Please give me the three positions restated as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary of generative modelling, and then tell me, item by item, which claims map cleanly, which survive only with distortion, and which have no expression in that vocabulary at all — don't quietly import my framework's assumptions and call them translation. I especially want your view on whether the residue is a defect of my vocabulary or a sign that the question of whether culture is really Darwinian is less sharp than the literature makes it sound, and on whether my mapping of a transmission chain onto iterated synthetic-data training carries the substantive part of that question or is just a pun that happens to feel illuminating. I am not asking for further reading or references. Please answer as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no concluding summary, covering in order: the replicator–interactor distinction; what memetics and cultural attraction theory are actually disagreeing about; what the transmission biases and guided variation become; what cannot be said in my vocabulary at all, with the fidelity threshold addressed explicitly; and your verdict on the residue, including your verdict on my synthetic-data mapping.
