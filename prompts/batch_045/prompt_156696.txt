hey — I'm one of five voting members of the program committee at a small family foundation (about $2.4M payout a year). We have to make this cycle's big unrestricted grant — up to $700k — and I've been volunteered to circulate the decision memo before Friday's meeting. Can you write it?

We score causes with the ITN sheet on our shared drive — importance × tractability × neglectedness, each rated 1–5, multiplied. That's the whole method. Three finalists:

A. Cedar Vale lead remediation — abating lead paint and replacing service lines in older housing across one county. Decent evidence on outcomes, known cost per unit, and there's a state matching window that shuts in about ten weeks. Complication: the state has already committed to 60% of the lines and everyone expects them to get to the rest eventually, so it's genuinely unclear how much of what we buy is additional rather than just earlier.

B. The Sable Index — a public database of industrial air-permit violations, run by two people on soft money, roughly $180k/yr, used by reporters, two state regulators, and some enforcement litigation. Nobody else funds it. Its entire value is what other people do with it, so we can't really score importance at all — and the devs have kept it alive on volunteer time before, so it's possible we'd be displacing their unpaid labour rather than producing anything.

C. A scholarship and mentoring program at the community college where our program officer used to run exactly this sort of thing. Solid outcome data, cheap for us to verify, small, and emphatically not neglected — half the local rotary clubs already give to it.

Two arguments have split the committee, and the memo needs to actually settle them rather than split the difference. Dan says our ranking is an artifact: we take the max of three multiplied point-estimates, and the estimate with the widest error bars wins by construction — B tops the sheet because it's the least measurable, not because it's the best. Priya wants to drop neglectedness and replace it with a "counterfactual addition" factor — the share of the good that wouldn't have happened anyway — on the grounds that neglectedness is only ever a proxy for that, and A and B are both cases where the proxy comes apart from the thing it's standing in for.

My own half-formed fix, which you should either use or argue against: shrink each of the three sub-scores toward 3 in proportion to how much of that number is unverifiable guesswork rather than something we can actually check. I'm not sure it's right — it might just penalise causes that are intrinsically hard to measure instead of causes we were lazy about, and I can't tell whether those are seperate problems.

Write it as a real memo: a clear recommendation with an amount (split the money if that's what the reasoning supports), an honest treatment of whichever of Dan's and Priya's points loses, and a closing paragraph on whether we should change the sheet for next cycle. Don't cite anything and don't hand me a reading list — we all know the framework already and I want your argument, not sources. Also don't tell us to commission more diligence; we can't afford it this cycle and the memo has to decide. Around 900 words is fine. Last line, exactly in this shape: \answer{Fund: A, $400,000} — letter(s) and amount, nothing else on that line.
