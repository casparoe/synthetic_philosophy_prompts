Here's where I've got to. As I understood the discussion of causation in the Tahafut, his target is the claim that there's a necessary connection between cause and effect: fire touches the cotton, the cotton burns. His move is that the sequence holds only because God has, by His habit or custom, decreed to create the burning at the contact. God could just as well create the burning without the contact, or the contact without the burning, or satiety without eating. We think the link is necessary only because we've seen the sequence so often that it becomes psychologically unshakeable.

My problem is that this looks readable in two quite different ways and I cant tell which one he wants, or whether they really come apart.

Reading one — call it elimination: there is no necessity anywhere in the story. The fire touches, the burning follows, end of story. Reading two — call it relocation: the necessity is real, but its home is the divine will rather than the natures of fire and cotton. On this reading the pairing is counterfactually robust — if this bit of fire had touched the cotton it would still have burned — not because fire can do anything, but because a rational and unchanging God has no reason to reshuffle His custom case by case.

My classmate calls reading two counterfactual occasionalism. My worry about it is that it seems to buy back everything the causal realist wanted, so the dispute collapses into an argument about where you shelve the necessity, which feels merely verbal. My worry about reading one is the opposite: with no robustness at all, God's habit starts to look exactly like Hume's habit with the mind swapped out for God, and then I don't see what stops the whole pattern from being one enormous coincidence — which would leave our causal inferences unexplained rather than explained.

Two more things I want you to deal with, sorry.

One is the argument from miracles. I thought miracles were evidence for occasionalism: the pattern visibly breaks, so the necessity was never in the creatures. But the realist can say God overrode or suspended a genuine created power, and overriding a power seems to require that there be a power there. The same data fits both, so I don't know what would ever settle it.

The other is a knot about the habit itself. If the burning is keyed to creaturely conditions — burn when and only when contact — then God's will looks like it's tracking what creatures are up to, which is a kind of dependence of God on creatures. But isn't denying exactly that dependence the whole point of occasionalism?
