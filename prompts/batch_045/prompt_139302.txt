You are a decision theorist and professional philosopher. Please think carefully, and begin by brainstorming two or three candidate scenarios before settling on the strongest one.

My friend and I argued late into the night about Parfit's hitchhiker. She says the case shows something deep and general: that rationality can require you to make yourself irrational, by acquiring a disposition you know you will have most reason to act against at the moment of action. I said the case depends on the desert-island setup, and that in ordinary life the move is not available to you, because the people whose trust you need are sensitive to how a disposition got there, not merely to the fact that it is there. We went in circles because neither of us could produce a case that wasn't rigged by stipulation.

So I want one built to order. Design an ordinary-life scenario — a workplace, a loan between friends, a tenancy, a shared house — with no desert islands, no neuroscanners, and no predictor better than an observant person who knows the agent well, in which all three of the following hold:

1. the agent really would be better off having the costly disposition than lacking it, so this is not a debunking case;
2. at the moment of action it is clear that acting on the disposition is against the agent's aims;
3. the agent still cannot secure the benefit by deciding to acquire the disposition, because the selector's trust tracks the provenance of the disposition, and every route open to a deliberate choice produces the wrong provenance.

The verdicts must fall out of the situation rather than be written into it. After the scenario: show why the hitchhiker reasoning carries the agent through (1) and (2) and then stalls at (3); name the single feature doing the work; say what happens to the case if you vary that feature — a selector who sees only behaviour, then one who sees motives but not their source; and give the strongest version of my friend's reply. If (3) holds, is the real moral that rationality governs the management of your own formation rather than your acts?
