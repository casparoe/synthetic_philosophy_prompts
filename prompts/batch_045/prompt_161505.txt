I'm putting together a clickable flowchart thing for a first-year seminar on happiness and wellbeing — student clicks through five or six questions and lands on a leaf that says something like you're basically a hedonist, here's what that commits you to. I want it to sort people by what they say about cases rather than by which label they think sounds nice, so no node is allowed to be anything like do you think pleasure is the only thing that matters. The whole instrument should be built out of variants of the experience machine instead.

The root split I had in mind: are we asking what makes someone's life go best for them, or what they'd have most reason to do / what's worth choosing? I keep going back and forth on whether those collapse for this purpose, so tell me if the tree needs two roots or if that distinction only ever does work at one or two leaves.

Cases I want used as the decision nodes — mix and match, add better ones if you have them:

- the plain version: lifelong plug-in, experiences reliably better than real life, you know what you're signing up for
- you've been in it since birth and never chose it; unplugging is possible but it would wreck you
- a machine where you deliberately pick a hard simulated life with real struggle and failure, because the easy one would be boring
- a machine where your simulated actions drive a robot body and actually help real people, so you get genuine causal impact plus the full experience of achieving something
- a machine where you're the only real person and everyone you love is a simulation that behaves perfectly
- the shared machine: everyone you know is plugged in too, you interact normally inside, nothing outside is real
- a machine offered as treatment: your real life is genuinely awful and the plug is the only option on the table
- a machine that hands you exactly the phenomenology of having achieved something hard, with no achievement anywhere in the causal chain

Leaves I want present: sensory hedonism, some kind of desire-satisfaction view (and if the tree can split actual desires from informed or idealized ones, do that as separate leaves), objective-list, the contact-with-actual-reality view, and something narrative or authenticity-flavoured. Name them where they have names, say unnamed otherwise, and give one sentence each on its most awkward commitment — the bit a student will notice and go wait, really?

The part I actually care about most: which combinations of answers does nobody occupy, and is that because the combination is incoherent, just unattractive, or merely untried? And please flag any two leaves the tree can't really separate however you order the questions. I suspect idealized-desire and objective-list-with-happiness-on-it collapse into each other, and I'm not sure anything separates a hedonism that only counts certain kinds of pleasure from a view where achievement matters but only as a cause of pleasure — if you can build a node that does separate them, that'd be the most useful thing in the whole handout.

One last thing: I want this to work for someone whose real-life contact with the question is should I spend three hours a night in VR or in a game, so somewhere in the notes say where the machine cases and that everyday case come apart and where they don't.
