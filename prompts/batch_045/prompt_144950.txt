I've been orbiting EA-adjacent circles for a few years and I'm finally trying to write down where I've actually landed on longtermism, because people keep asking me and I keep giving slightly different answers. Here's the package:

(1) I accept strong longtermism: the differences in expected value between the options actually in front of us are dominated by their effects on the far future, and stuff that only affects the next century or so is a rounding error in the comparison.

(2) I accept the asymmetry about existence — adding a life that goes well doesn't make an outcome better, adding a life that goes badly makes it worse. So I don't think extinction is bad because of all the happy people who would otherwise have existed. It's bad because of the deaths, because of the suffering, and because it cuts off something we now care about.

(3) I also think the case for longtermism is robust to how you settle population ethics — you don't need to first decide between totalling up welfare, averaging it, or some person-affecting view before you can say that the far future is where the value is. I think that robustness is one of the best features of the view and I lean on it a lot.

(4) I think the Pascal's mugging thing (someone threatens enormous amounts of suffering unless you hand over your wallet, and you can't rule it out) is a reductio, and we should just ignore sufficiently tiny probabilities of enormous payoffs.

(5) But I also think a 0.1% chance of losing something like 10^40 future lives is enough to justify spending a huge share of the rich world's resources on reducing extinction risk, and I'm fairly comfortable being called a fanatic about that.

(6) I think we're clueless: for most things we might do, we have no real idea whether the long-run flow-through effects are good or bad, and the honest credence on the sign is close to a coin flip.

So: does this hang together? My guesses, which I suspect are wrong: (6) feels like the one that really breaks (1), and (4) and (5) feel like they're in tension, but I can't tell whether they actually are or whether there's a clean way to separate them. I'm fairly sure (2) and (3) aren't in tension with each other since (3) is basically a generalisation of (2), and I'd be surprised if (2) caused trouble anywhere else.
