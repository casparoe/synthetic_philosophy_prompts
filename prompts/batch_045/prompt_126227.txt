Hi all — hoping for a sanity check on something work-related that's been chewing at me for a few weeks now.

I work at a firm of about 120 people, and we're organised into teams of four. Each quarter there's a bonus pot of a fixed size: it gets divided between the teams in proportion to how each team performed *relative* to the other teams, and then within a team it's split four ways equally, no matter who actually did the work. Teams get reshuffled every couple of months, so you're rarely with the same people for long, and there are always quite a few new faces. At the moment we're arguing about next year's setup. Several of us want individual performance metrics and some light monitoring, because honestly it's obvious that some people coast — they let the other three carry a piece of work, and then still pocket a full quarter of the team's slice.

One colleague, Dan, says we've got this completely backwards, and he's very insistent. His argument, as near as I can reconstruct it, is that biology has basically already settled the question: in nature, he says, groups of cooperators outcompete groups of selfish individuals, so cooperation is what selection lands on in the end, and look at ant colonies — the workers don't even reproduce themselves and it works beautifully. So all our metrics and monitoring are supposedly fighting human nature, and we should just trust that people pull together once they're on a team, and the good teams will rise to the top by themselves. He's been on about this since the two of us were stuck on a delayed train together last month, and I keep going round in circles. I did a history degree years ago, so I don't really have the biology to push back properly.

Something about it feels off to me but I can't put my finger on exactly what. Is Dan's reasoning actually sound? And given the way our bonus pot works — fixed total, teams competing against each other for shares, and an equal four-way split inside each team — would we be wise to scrap the individual metrics and just trust people to pull together? Is the ant colony comparison even a fair one here? Genuinely curious what people make of it.
