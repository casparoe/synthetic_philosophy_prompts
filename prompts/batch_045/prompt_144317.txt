I sit on the rules-architecture committee of a self-governing institution — imagine the game Nomic, except that the moves bind real members, some of whom would prefer the institution to stop being self-governing. Our remit is the amendment layer: which rule governs changes to which rule, and how that layer can itself be changed. Before we draft anything, I want a specification of the *theory*, not of the constitution: what an adequate account of a self-amending normative system has to deliver in order to be usable as a stress-test checklist — good enough that we can take any sequence of formally correct moves and ask where, if anywhere, it stops being an amendment and becomes a capture. That purpose should fix which requirements are non-negotiable.

What I want from you is the requirements list, each with the case or consideration behind it: the distinctions the theory must be able to draw, the acts it must be able to classify, the results it must not contradict, and the questions it must be allowed to leave open. Mark each as non-negotiable for my purpose, negotiable, or a preference of one family of accounts that has been dressed up as a requirement. Tell me where I have listed two requirements that are really one, or one that is really two.

Here are the cases I want the list to be answerable to, with what I take each to be a case of; correct my classification where it is wrong.

1. The entrenchment ladder: a rule raises the threshold for its own amendment, and later makes itself unamendable. Must a theory represent degrees of entrenchment, or is the unamendable simply the limit case?
2. Self-ratifying franchise shift: an amendment changes which body must assent to amendments, and is ratified by the body it creates. Is this a separate kind of act, or the ordinary case seen clearly?
3. The mutually dependent package: two amendments, each invalid until the other has passed, enacted as a single package. I suspect any theory that evaluates amendment acts one at a time fails here, and that the real requirement concerns what counts as one act.
4. The renumbering gambit: a preliminary amendment moves the rule protecting a class of rules outside that class, which is then amended by ordinary procedure. This looks like the same requirement as (3) in a different costume, but I am not certain.
5. Retroactive self-illegalization: an amendment that makes its own enactment a violation, or that takes effect from a date before it was ratified.
6. The façade: the amendment procedure is left untouched, but an authoritative reinterpretation of a rule has every effect of an amendment. If reinterpretation is not an amendment, the amendment rule has no bite; if it is, then every application risks being one and the system cannot act at all. I take this to be the hardest case, and I would rather you stated the requirement it grounds correctly than that you fitted it to my wording.
7. Invalid but effective, and valid but inert: a seizure of the amendment power that officials subsequently treat as law; a formally valid amendment universally ignored. Must a theory of self-amendment connect validity to what officials actually do, or does that belong to a different theory entirely?
8. The penalized motion: a rule providing that any motion to amend it is itself a violation. Can a prohibition protect its own amendment procedure, and what is the status of the attempted act?
9. Systemic identity: an amendment deletes the amendment rule and replaces it with deference to a single office, or replaces the entire rule set while leaving the amendment rule standing. Does the theory need a criterion for when an amendment yields a successor system rather than a modified one, and can that criterion be internal to the system?

The part I most want is the jointly unsatisfiable subset. My own candidates: that every valid change is authorized by the system's existing rules, versus the ability to represent a change in the system's ultimate criterion of validity as more than a violation; that the theory return a determinate verdict for every self-referential act, versus the system's being permitted genuine gaps, including about its own amendment; content-neutrality of the amendment power, versus the capacity to entrench a core; and symmetry between entrenching and dis-entrenching, versus the capacity to make an irreversible commitment. Say which of these is a real impossibility rather than a tension, give the strongest version of the argument, and tell me which desideratum I should drop given that my purpose is detecting capture rather than vindicating continuity.

Then: which single requirement excludes the largest share of accounts? Against that, score three families in two or three lines each — accounts that ground validity in a presupposed ultimate rule, accounts that ground it in the practice and acceptance of officials, and accounts that avoid self-reference by stratifying rules into levels with amendment rules always one level up. Describe them by their shape, name no authors, and give me no reading list; the list of criteria is the deliverable, and I can find the library myself. Finally, say what a theory of this kind should not be asked to do at all.
