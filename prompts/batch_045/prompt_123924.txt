Here's what I want. Take the standard commissurotomy cases and the fight about what they show: does a split-brain patient house two subjects of experience, or one subject whose access to its own states has got badly fragmented? You've got the two-streams reading, the "there's still one subject, it's just that one half talks and the other half watches" reading, the "no, it just switches between them" reading, and the "there's no fact of the matter here at all" reading. Restate those positions, one by one, in the vocabulary I actually use — partitions, membership views, quorums, log divergence and reconciliation, conflict resolution, lossy links, failover, reads that can't see recent writes, nodes that keep taking traffic after they've been fenced off. Take seriously that the left hemisphere's famous habit of inventing an explanation for something the right hemisphere initiated looks an awful lot like a repair process fabricating a causal history so the log reads clean, and that a right hemisphere with no route to speech is a node with no path to the output. Push that as far as it honestly goes.

Then two cases I've been chewing on, which I want folded in rather than bolted on:

1. *Reversible* deafferentation. Suppose the callosal link can be knocked out pharmacologically for an hour and then restored. While it's down, the right hemisphere directs the left hand to do something the left hemisphere didn't initiate; afterwards the left hemisphere finds out the ordinary way, by seeing what happened. Was that hour one stream of consciousness or two — and does the later reintegration retroactively make it one, so that "how many subjects were there?" turns out to be settled partly by what happens next?
2. Put the callosum on a dial: bandwidth and latency degrading continuously rather than being cut. Where, if anywhere, does the number of subjects change — and if the honest answer is "no clean breakpoint", say what that does to the debate.

Then the residue report, which is the bit I actually care about. Is it that distributed-systems talk is only ever defined from the outside, from the position of someone who can read every node's log, so that it has no way to express there being something it is like to *be* one node and not another? Or is it that the philosophers' question was never as clear as it looked, and the failure to translate is a symptom rather than a casualty? I want you to be suspicious in both directions, not just the convenient one.
