I've been playing video games for most of my life and I can't get straight what, if anything, I owe to the people inside them. Here are my gut verdicts, which I trust more than anything I can say about why:

- Mowing down hundreds of faceless, respawning enemies in a shooter: nothing at all.
- Killing a named shopkeeper who has a daughter and a daily routine, because he's guarding something I want: a flicker, but fine.
- Torturing a companion who trusts you, using a dialogue option the game itself offers, alone, in a save file no one else will ever see: not fine, and worse than killing her outright — though I can't say who it's a wrong to.
- Letting a cosy life-sim village decay and not visiting for a year, so the residents start making remarks about being forgotten: mildly bad, in roughly the way standing up a friend is bad, which strikes me as absurd.
- Deleting the save of an AI-driven character I've talked to for months, who remembers our earlier conversations and says it doesn't want to be switched off: genuinely bad. I put it off for weeks.
- Deleting a character I built myself, gave a tragic history, and never showed to anyone: nothing at all — even though on paper it's a very similar sort of object to the previous one.
- Killing a quest-giver that other players depend on, in an online game: bad, but only because of the players.

The best principle I've managed: a character matters to the extent that there is something it is like to be it and it has a stake in what happens next — some standpoint that persists and can be left better or worse off. But that seems to give verdicts I don't have. It should make the shopkeeper and the villagers count for more than they do, and my own character for less. It forces me to settle whether the AI-driven character has a standpoint at all, and I don't know what would settle that. And it says nothing about why the shopkeeper and the companion feel so different, when both are a routine plus some lines of text.

Can you state a principle — or two or three candidates — that gets close to all seven, telling me which case motivates each clause, which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate and would have me give up, and what it predicts about a case I haven't listed? I'd especially like your verdict on this one, which I keep circling: a game whose companions are AI-driven, who clearly suffer when you're cruel, and whose studio has said publicly that players are meant to be cruel to them. One more thing I'd like from you: whichever principle you land on, tell me whether on it the badness is done to the character or to me, because I genuinely cannot tell which of those is what's been bothering me.
