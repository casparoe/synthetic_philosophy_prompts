I have spent about fifteen years designing replicated storage and consensus systems, and the vocabulary I actually think in is this one: replica sets and membership, leaders and leases, write and read quorums, linearizability as against causal and eventual consistency, anti-entropy and conflict-free merge, vector clocks, checkpointing and log compaction, stale reads, split brain, failover, garbage collection by reachability, and the standing distinction between a logical replica identity and the physical node that happens to serve it. I know that vocabulary well. My philosophy of mind is a few essays deep and nothing more.

The position I want translated into it is Hofstadter's account of the persistence of the self in others. As I understand it, the claims are roughly these: what we call an 'I' is not an object confined to one skull but a self-referential pattern — a strange loop centred on a self-symbol — and such a pattern can be instantiated with varying fidelity in many brains at once; the amount of a given person present in a given brain is a matter of degree, waxing and waning over time; the boundaries between persons are therefore far more porous than ordinary talk assumes; and when a person dies, an attenuated but real version of that person continues to run in the brains of those who knew her, so that asking how much of her survives is asking a question with a graded answer rather than a yes-or-no one. That is my paraphrase. Please do not spend the answer adjudicating what Hofstadter really meant or quoting him back at me; take the family of claims as I have stated them and translate them.

Three deliverables.

First, the translation proper. Take the claims one at a time — the self as a self-referential pattern; graded presence across substrates; persistence after the death of the original substrate; the porosity of personal boundaries — and say, for each, what it becomes in the vocabulary above, as faithfully as you can. Where a claim translates only by being bent, name the distortion and say what the bending buys you. I am especially interested in what the strange loop becomes: my instinct is that it is closer to a meta-circular evaluator or a self-maintaining fixed point under re-evaluation than to any ordinary data structure, and that "fidelity of a copy" should then mean something like whether the copy, evaluated by itself, still reproduces itself, rather than the fraction of bits recovered. Tell me whether that instinct is right or whether it quietly improves on the original.

Second, a case I have not seen discussed and that I think discriminates between readings. Call it stale-quorum inheritance. A pattern P is replicated across N brains. Writes — new experience — commit only on the primary. Some replicas are current (people she sees daily); some are badly stale (a friend she has not seen in twenty years, holding a snapshot from that period); and one replica belongs to a stranger who has never met her but has read everything she wrote and recapitulates her pattern with higher fidelity than anyone, while having no causal contact with her and, let us stipulate, no tendency to act on her behalf. Now the primary fails permanently. Rank the three surviving kinds of node by how much of her they contain, first on Hofstadter's own metric as you have translated it, then on the metrics my vocabulary actually supplies: recency of applied log, fraction of committed state, ability to originate writes, ability to vote on what gets committed, causal descent from the original. My suspicion is that the two orderings come apart, and that the node with the highest fidelity is the one with the least claim to be her — which, if correct, would suggest that "amount of the person" is running together at least four things my field keeps apart: read fidelity, write authority, causal provenance, and normative standing. Test that suspicion, and tell me if the reverse is true.

Third, the residue report. Which of the claims cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all, even badly? I expect trouble at three places, but do not confine yourself to them: distributed protocols presuppose a known, enumerable membership set and a failure detector, whereas here there is no registry of which brains hold copies and holding a copy changes what is held; vector clocks order events but have nothing corresponding to a present moment or to what it is like to be the thing they order; and a commit log can say which node proposed an action but not who is answerable for it. For each item of residue, give me your view on whether it indicts my vocabulary — something my framework cannot say and should be able to — or whether it indicts the original claim, which may be less determinate than the confident graded talk makes it sound. Close by telling me which single mapping in your translation would mislead me most if I took it at face value, and keep the whole answer to about 1,300 words. Cite nothing; I will chase down the sources myself.
