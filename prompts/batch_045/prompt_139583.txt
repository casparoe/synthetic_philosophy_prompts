I'm applying for a single semester of research leave and need a two-page proposal for a paper of roughly 8,000 words on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument — the regress argument for the conclusion that no one is ever truly or ultimately morally responsible, since to be responsible for what you do you must be responsible for the way you are, mentally speaking, when you do it, and to be responsible for that you would have to have brought yourself into being in the relevant respect, which is impossible whether or not determinism is true. A colleague pressed me on this at a workshop dinner last week and I came away with a half-formed idea that I would like turned into something precise; I would much rather have it sharpened, or shown to be a dead end, than have it flattered.

Here is the idea. It strikes me that the standard compatibilist replies — that the argument sets an incoherent or question-begging standard, that it conflates responsibility for an action with responsibility for one's character, that even if ultimate responsibility is unattainable there remains a perfectly good everyday responsibility that is worth wanting — all of them concede to Strawson the *unit* of responsibility. The argument is staged at the moment of action: the agent acts at t out of a mental set she did not choose, and the question is whether she is responsible for that mental set at t. But a great deal of our actual responsibility practice does not target momentary actions at all; it targets extended things — a decade of professional negligence, a standing cruelty, an omission that persists, a friendship one let lapse, a belief one never troubled to revise. Call this the extended-object model. My suspicion is that the regress gets its grip from the occasion model rather than from the incompatibilist intuition itself, because once the object of assessment is stretched over time the agent has room to intervene on herself: to notice a pattern, to train a habit, to recheck a belief. The condition that she could have been otherwise through her own earlier agency then looks satisfiable in a way that being causa sui at t never could. So the question I want to work on is this: does the force of the Basic Argument depend on an occasion-model assumption that a defender of responsibility is entitled to reject, or does the regress simply reappear one level up, attaching to the earlier self that did the noticing and training — and if it reappears, is that reappearance interestingly different from the original argument, or merely the same argument with extra steps?

The second half of the idea is a test, which is what I think would make the paper more than a familiar dialectic. Run the same argument for responsibility for belief: there the analogue of self-creation would be choosing one's evidence, or believing at will, which looks impossible in a stronger and more obvious way than it does for action. If the Basic Argument is more convincing for belief than for action, and yet we do not conclude that no one is ever responsible for what they believe, that asymmetry is diagnostic — it tells us which premise is doing the work, and whether that premise is doing too much. I would like the proposal to state the question so that it has a determinate answer; to explain why the existing positions leave it open; to give a month-by-month account of what I would actually do across the semester, including what the strongest version of the opposing case would be and how I would try to meet it; to say what each possible conclusion would look like and which considerations would decide between them; to give an honest scope assessment, including what could make this too big for one semester; and to name failure conditions explicitly. My own three worries are that the regress simply relocates, that the extended-object model turns out to be a familiar you-can-make-yourself-over-time view in new vocabulary, and — worst of all — that the whole disagreement dissolves into a verbal dispute about the adverb. I will fill in the citations myself and am not asking for a literature review, so please refer to positions and arguments by description rather than by author or paper, and don't worry about dates or bibliographic detail.
