hi — I'm running a 75-minute session for a mixed methods workshop: 14 people, mostly philosophy and psychology grad students, maybe a third of them have actually used Bayes' theorem in the last year and the rest know it only from hearsay. I want them to run into the problem of old evidence on their own, and I don't want anyone to say the phrase, or anything close to it, before the debrief.

The setup I keep circling around: participants watch a data sequence get generated — a long run of draws from a box, or a sequence of symbols — and they have prices riding on what comes next. Then a theory card gets revealed that fits the sequence already on the board perfectly. Here's the bit I want the whole game built around: some of the theory cards were written down and sealed before the data was generated, and others were written afterwards by someone who could see the sequence. Participants never learn which kind they're holding. They have to put a price on the theory being right about the next stretch of data, and prices are scored by a proper scoring rule so accuracy pays.

My hope is they'll feel the pull toward paying more for the sealed ones, and then find they can't say what in the formalism is supposed to license that. And I want one extra wrinkle in there: an "old-evidence dividend" the house pays out whenever someone adopts a theory that explains data already on the table. I'd like that rule to be what makes the whole thing show up as an incentive structure people can game, not just as a puzzle about a formula.

Can you turn this into something actually runnable? I need: the rules, the materials (index cards, envelopes, a box of tickets, a projector — nothing that needs a web connection or a TA), a payoff scheme that works with play money or points, a minute-by-minute timeline for 75 minutes including setup, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto specific parts of the problem — the bit where the evidence is already certain so the ratio can't move you, the bit where prediction and accommodation seem to come apart, and the bit where it's genuinely unclear how to cash that out. Also: what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, how I handle it, and an honest note on which feature of the problem the game simply can't reproduce and I'll have to say out loud myself.
