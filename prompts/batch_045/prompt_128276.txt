After a career in experimental chemistry I have started sitting in on a philosophy reading group, and we have now spent most of a term on the norms of assertion without anybody changing their mind. The knowledge norm, the truth norm and the reasonable-belief norm each handle some cases and fail on others, and every standard objection has a reply its proponent evidently anticipated. I would like a diagnosis of the standoff rather than a verdict on it.

My hypothesis, offered as one: the camps may not be answering a single question. Distinguish (i) a rule a practice must have in order to be the practice it is, (ii) a standard of correctness by which a performance counts as defective, and (iii) a deontic standard that licenses or forbids the act. It looks to me as though the knowledge norm is strongest read as (ii), which is what lets it explain why it is raining but I don't know that it is sounds defective in a way the truth norm struggles to account for, while the truth norm and the reasonable-belief norm are at their best read as (iii). On that reading the arguments are mostly about which cases count as data, Moorean pairs, high-stakes cases, lottery cases, the teacher who asserts what the textbook says, and the sides differ because each case is evidence about a different one of the three. I also keep returning to a thought from my own discipline: that assertion may not be a kind that carves nature at the joints at all, but a grab-bag of things we have lumped together, with each norm correct about a different part of it.

So, three things. Is that the right shape for the disagreement, or is there a substantive question about speech acts, or about which intuitions are evidence, that my trichotomy papers over? Then the case from my former life that the group cannot settle: I spent years asserting measurement results, in papers and at bench meetings, that sat at two or three sigma, results I would have declined to say I knew if pressed, and doing so seemed proper, useful, and neither blameworthy nor defective. Which of the three norms, if any, does that practice tell against, and does its being good practice count against a norm or only against applying that norm to that bit of the grab-bag? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress here rather than another round of cases: a further distinction, a different question, evidence of some sort, or nothing at all, and why?
