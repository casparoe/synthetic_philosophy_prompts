im putting together a one-page handout for a phil of mind / AI seminar and i want a decision tree that walks someone from the single question "does this model believe that p?" down to a named position. The angle i care about: i want students to see that the disagreement is usually about which case is load-bearing, not about the word "believe", so build the tree out of the cases first and only then hang labels on the leaves.

Every branch node should be a claim or case a student can answer without already knowing where it leads (no "is belief just predictive usefulness?" as a node). Leaves should include at least: Dennett-flavoured instrumentalism, the real-patterns/"mild realist" version of it, causal-role functionalism (a real belief box), a teleosemantic or training-history condition, Davidsonian interpretationism with charity, as-if fictionalism, and eliminativism.

Cases i want doing the branching work:

- a model fine-tuned only on flat-earth text that reliably says the earth is flat — belief, or just a pattern?
- a model whose chain of thought is fluent and self-consistent but has no causal influence on the final answer (post-hoc rationalisation) — do we attribute the belief stated in the reasoning?
- a system that internally encodes p (probes find it, it would say p absent the safety tuning) but which always behaves as if not-p
- the chess program that "knows" its queen is attacked vs the thermostat that "believes" it's 20 degrees
- somewhere a branch for whether the system has to be capable of being wrong and of revising

Where the yes/no space leaves a cell nobody occupies, mark it and say briefly whether that's because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. At the end flag any two leaves the tree cant actually pull apart, and say what further case would separate them (or why none could).
