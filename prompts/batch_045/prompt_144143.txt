Since a long hospital stay last year I have been working slowly through the films that came out while I was in treatment, and this week I finally got to Tenet. Around the same time I had been reading about the direction of time: the statistical reading of the second law on which entropy increase is overwhelmingly likely rather than law-governed, the past hypothesis treated as a boundary condition rather than a dynamical asymmetry, and the suggestion that what really distinguishes past from future is an asymmetry in what we can know and what we can do rather than anything in the underlying physics. The film kept seeming to be about exactly that without ever saying so, so I want a reading of it through the philosophy of the thermodynamic arrow.

First, the physical side. Is the film's inversion a reversal of entropy, or of something else? Take three scenes separately: the fight in which a masked figure comes back out of a turnstile and grapples with the protagonist, the demonstration in which a bullet returns into the gun, and the highway sequence with the inverted driver. For each, say what would have to be true of the entropy gradient and of the light, air, and bodies involved for it to be coherent, and ask how local the reversal is allowed to be. What does thermodynamics say about two systems whose arrows point in opposite directions and interact, and who, if anyone, is entitled to call one of them the reversed one?

Second, the agential side. The film's future people act on the past, the characters keep reassuring one another that what has happened has happened, and the final pincer operation depends on information travelling one temporal way while agency goes the other. Read that against the asymmetry of knowledge and the interventionist account of causation: does the film dramatize a coherent picture in which the epistemic asymmetry and the asymmetry of intervention come apart, or does it quietly collapse them?

Finally, I would like to know where the reading fails. I do not want to make the film into an illustration of a view from my reading group, and I suspect some of it is just physics-flavoured staging. Which scenes resist the lens, and does the film, taken on its own terms, add anything to the debate about the arrow that the arguments alone do not?
