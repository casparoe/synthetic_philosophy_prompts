so i'm in this little writing group — four of us, every other tuesday at a pub, each of us brings something short that riffs on whatever we've been reading — and it's my turn next week and I drew the toxin puzzle. You know the one: Kavka's, from the early 80s. A stranger offers you a large sum if, by midnight tonight, you form the intention to drink a vial tomorrow morning that will make you miserably ill for a day and do nothing worse; the money is yours at midnight whether or not you ever drink, and by morning you'll have no reason left to drink. I've read the standard discussion and honestly it bores me — can you intend what you know you won't do, can you intend to intend, does the reward give you a reason to have an intention rather than to act — so I want a story that puts real pressure on one corner of it, and I want you to write inside my version of the setup rather than the original.

My version: near future, nothing shiny, a licensed company called Velleity runs an "intention audit." You sit in a booth before midnight, a neural auditor reads you off, and if the reading comes back as *intends to swallow the vial they hand you at nine*, the money clears then and there. Two wrinkles. First, a counterfactual-motive clause: the audit doesn't pay on a bare intention to drink, it pays on an intention to drink on the condition that no further payment, prize, penalty, or promise attaches to the drinking itself. So the usual escape routes are dead. She can't put the payout in escrow payable only if she drinks, can't make a real promise to a friend, can't plan to drink out of celebration or pride or to prove something to herself, because each of those manufactures a morning reason and the clause screens for exactly that. (How the auditor pulls it off — clever questioning, a running model of you, whatever — leave it vague, it just works well enough.) Second, it's a ladder: the payout doubles each quarter you come back, you can only re-enter if you were read as intending in every previous round, and the vial is the same mild toxin every time, so by round five everyone in the queue has a flawless public record of meaning it and not doing it.

The character: someone on her fourth or fifth round who has obviously worked something out, because she keeps winning — and I want the story to be about what she actually did to herself to make that possible, and whether what she ends up with is an intention at all. Not a philosophy essay with names changed, please; the puzzle should be load-bearing for what happens in the scene. Did she train herself into some kind of hollow intending, or change what she wants (curiosity, spite, an acquired taste for the misery), or find a way to intend the act under some other description, or is she just the sort of person whose self-predictions have come loose from her reasons — and would the auditor even distinguish those? I keep flip-flopping on whether somebody like her is self-deceived, or straightforwardly akratic, or a very strange kind of competent, and I want the story to land on one of those and make me feel it. Also give me her reason for coming back a fifth time when she already has more money than she needs — the doubling shouldn't be the whole of it. Somewhere around 1200 words, end it wherever it needs to end, and please don't have her explain the philosophy out loud to a friend in the last scene.
