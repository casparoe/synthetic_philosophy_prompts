I am writing to ask for a considered piece of reasoning on a matter that has become unexpectedly pressing for me, and I would be grateful for a thoughtful answer rather than a survey of the literature.

My great-aunt died in October and left the residue of a modest estate to fund, in her words, research into the survival of consciousness after bodily death. I am one of three trustees; the others are a retired judge, who reads the will strictly, and my aunt's goddaughter, who believes the dead can be reached. The will requires us to satisfy ourselves that whatever we fund is scientific research and not, say, spiritualist entertainment, and the solicitor has made clear that this judgement is ours to make and to defend.

The application now in front of us is not from a medium. It is from a small group of computer scientists who propose what they call a computational sitting. A language model is fine-tuned on the surviving writings of a recently deceased person; it is then given nothing but a name, two dates, and a question from a bereaved relative, and it produces text in that person's style. The relatives are asked to pick, from a set of transcripts, the one that best matches the person they lost, where several of the alternatives are transcripts generated from other people's papers. The applicants say the interesting cases are those in which families report that the output contained a specific detail no living person knew. They invoke Turing's name freely, and one of them has written to us saying that if Turing could put extra-sensory perception into his 1950 paper on the imitation game as a serious objection, we can hardly dismiss this.

There are complications I can see and others I can't. The model has almost certainly ingested enormous quantities of text about the dead, so any apparent hit may be recall rather than reception; the applicants propose dealing with this by restricting the target cases to people whose private papers were digitised only after the model's training data were assembled, and by asking for details recorded in sealed material that has never been published. Separately: a success here would not look like a success in the imitation game, since nobody is asking whether the speaker is a machine. And the whole field now carries a methodological history Turing did not live to see: preregistration, the replication crisis, selective reporting, experimenter expectancy effects, and the ease with which a flexible system can look impressive without anything anomalous having happened.

What I want from you is an answer to this: what would Turing himself have made of this proposal, given how he actually handled the argument from extra-sensory perception in the 1950 Mind paper — where, as I understand it, his move is structural rather than sceptical, conceding the phenomena for the sake of argument and proposing instead that the experiment be arranged so that they cannot be used to cheat? Please address, in about 700 to 800 words:

1. Whether he would have regarded the computational sitting as a test of psi at all, as a test of machine performance, or as an experimental design in which the two are entangled beyond rescue — and which of his own distinctions settles the question.
2. What he would have demanded by way of controls and statistical design, reasoning from his taste for operational criteria, quantitative thresholds and designed experiments rather than from any particular documented remark of his I could look up.
3. What he would have said about the sealed-material and post-digitisation variants specifically, and whether a positive result there would have struck him as evidence for survival, for extra-sensory perception, or for something more mundane about the informativeness of accumulated text.
4. How he would have separated, if at all, the question of what the experiment shows from the question of what should be done with my aunt's money.

Please close with a short paragraph, no more than a hundred words, written as the recommendation I could put to my two co-trustees. I do not need citations, references or a bibliography, and I would rather you did not try to reconstruct chapter and verse from his papers: it is the shape of the argument I need, and I can check the sources myself.
