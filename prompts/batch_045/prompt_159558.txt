I've been doing couples counselling for about fifteen years, reading a bit of moral philosophy on the side, and I keep noticing the same shape of thing without having a word for it.

Here's the shape. Someone finds out his partner has been lying about money for years. There are two ways he can regard her, and I can literally watch him flip between them. In the first she's someone who owes him an accounting and an apology: he's hurt, he's holding her to a standard, he wants something *from her*. In the second he's talking about her childhood and her fear of scarcity, and he's setting up automatic transfers so it can't happen again — he's now relating to her the way you'd relate to a leaking pipe. And he can't be in both at once. In the second frame the anger feels inappropriate to him; in the first frame the childhood stuff feels like an excuse.

What I want a word for isn't either frame, it's the flip. The flip doesn't look like a change in what he believes. His views about her upbringing, and about whether she could have done otherwise, can be word-for-word identical on both sides of it — I've checked. It looks much more like something he *does*, and sometimes does on purpose: clients step into the second frame deliberatly to get through a funeral or a tax season and then step back out afterwards; and I've watched the anger come back years later, unbidden, when some new detail surfaces.
