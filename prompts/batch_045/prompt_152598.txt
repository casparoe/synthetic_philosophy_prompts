Bit of an odd one, but here goes. I'm building the analytics side of a budgeting app and I'd like the welfare scoring to be principled rather than vibes.

What I have per user is about a year of purchase data: what they bought and the prices they faced, and prices do move around a fair bit (supplier changes, occasional promotions). No demographics, no surveys, no self-reports — just choices under budgets that shift. From that I want three things: (1) the user's utility function, so I can predict what they'll buy next month when prices change; (2) how much better off they'd be under plan A versus plan B, so we can rank our own plans; (3) a per-user 'gain' number I can compare across users, so we can target the people who'd benefit most from a given feature.

I've read that under revealed preference utility is only defined up to an increasing transformation, so the actual numbers are arbitrary and only the ranking carries meaning. I've got round that by rescaling every user onto the same 0–1 range: u = 0 at the cheapest basket they ever bought, u = 1 at the most expensive one, then I compare the rescaled values. Once everyone's on the same scale the numbers should be interpersonally comparable, right? That feels like the obvious dodge and I cant find a straight answer on whether it's legit.

Second thing: a few users' data come out inconsistent — they violate the weak axiom. I'm currently just deleting the offending observations until what's left is consistent and fitting to the remainder, which seems tidier than dropping the whole user.

Third, and this is the bit that actually bothers me: some users buy stuff that's obviously bad for them (one guy blows about a third of his income on scratchcards). If preferences are just choices, I have to say he's maximising utility and that there's no sense in which he'd be better off without them — which makes the whole apparatus useless for a product that's supposed to help people. Is there a version of revealed preference that lets you say someone's choices don't reflect what they really want, or do I have to bolt on happiness surveys before I'm allowed to say anything?
