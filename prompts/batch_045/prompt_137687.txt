For the reading group Im running next week I need a thought experiment that makes the 'LLMs are agents' vs 'LLMs are simulators' argument cash out in an actual prediction, instead of both sides retrofitting an explanation to whatever the model happens to do. So: build me a case where the simulator side predicts a specific, surprising behavior that no plausible agent story predicts — the prediction has to be statable in advance, and it cant be of the form 'well, some simulacrum might just do that', since that move is exactly what Im trying to rule out, so treat it as cheating. If a hard outcome prediction is too much, a sharp disagreement about probability is fine, as long as it isnt just 'simulators allow more variance'.

Constraints:

- It has to be runnable in one ordinary chat session with no special access: no weights, no logprobs, no fine-tuning, no system-prompt-only tools, and no statistics over hundreds of runs.
- No jailbreaks, no prompt injection, no context rot, nothing that depends on the model malfunctioning or getting confused.
- Dont lean on any real model card, published eval, or interpretability result — it should work from the armchair, for a model that cant look anything up.
- The agent theorist gets to pick whichever goal is most convenient (helpfulness, approval, self-preservation, whatever) but has to stick with one goal for the whole conversation, and the case should survive that choice rather than depending on it.
- The interesting behavior should be sensitive to something semantic, so that the third option — 'its just a token pattern-matcher with no content' — also comes out looking bad.

After the scenario: state the prediction, walk through what each of the three views (agent, simulator, mere pattern-matcher) says and why, tell me which single feature of the setup is doing the work, what the case collapses into if you remove that feature, and what an agent theorist would say to wriggle out of it. Finally, say honestly whether the case really supports the simulator view or whether it mostly just kills the agent story and leaves 'something else' wide open. If you think the specs are jointly unsatisfiable, say which one has to give and why.
