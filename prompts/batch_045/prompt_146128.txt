On a podcast I listen to, a guest argued we shouldn't worry much about how persuasive a superintelligent AI might be. His picture was that it's basically a doctor talking to a patient. Sure, the doctor knows way more than you do, but the fix for that isn't to make her less convincing — it's informed consent: say what the treatment is, say what the risks are, let the patient agree, and that's the whole of it. So the only thing we supposedly need from a superintelligent AI is that it not lie to us about what it's doing, and anyone who wants limits on how it explains things is treating adults like kids. I find the picture appealing but I suspect its doing most of the work.

My own add-on was this: make the AI also write the strongest case for whatever it isn't recommending, like a limitations section, and then require a cooling-off day before you decide. My friend says that's useless, because the AI is also the one writing the case against its own recommendation, and it could just as easily talk you into waiting.

Can you audit the doctor analogy for me? Give me the correspondence table — doctor, patient, disease, treatment, consent form, second opinion, malpractice — then where the mapping breaks. Three things I keep circling on: the gap with a doctor is mostly about facts, whereas with a superintelligent system the gap might include what you end up wanting; there may be no genuine second opinion available; and doctors sometimes use persuasion on purpose, as with placebos, and we mostly think that's fine. Also say what the doctor picture quietly imports that the AI case doesn't have. Then tell me whether the conclusion — disclosure is enough, limits on persuasion are just paternalism — survives, whether my fix plugs any of the hole, and what a better analogy would be.

Sections in this order: The mapping (~120 words), Where it breaks (~200), What it smuggles in (~100), Does my fix work (~120), Settled vs contested (~100). In that last one, separate what people who actually work on this agree on from what's genuinely argued about, and don't make a contested claim sound like the consensus. American English please, and no citations.
