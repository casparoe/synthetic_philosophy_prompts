Hi — you're a philosopher of biology who also happens to write decent verse. Please think carefully before drafting: spend a few lines brainstorming which claim each account is actually committed to, and then write. I'd much rather have something that gets the dialectic right than something that scans smoothly and says nothing.

Context: I'm a retired malacologist (whelk feeding mechanics, mostly) who's started sitting in on a philosophy of biology reading group. We did the selection-history / proper-function side (Millikan and co.) against the causal-role side — the what-it-currently-contributes-to-the-system people (Cummins and co.) — and I got fed up, because every session ran on the same three examples: the diseased heart, the very first heart, Swampman. So I invented my own case, and I want a poem about that case, not about those.

The case: a volcanic island gets colonised by a single gravid snale — one founder, clonal descendants, essentially no heritable variation for a couple of thousand years. The founder happens to carry a developmental accident: a duplicated fold of tissue at the lip of the mantle cavity, which no ancestor had and which nothing ever selected for. It happens to seal the shell tight, and it is the only reason the population gets through the dry season. Then, a few thousand generations in, a second immigrant arrives, mating stops being clonal, valve tightness starts to vary, dry-season survival starts to track it, and selection on the fold begins. The question the poem has to carry: did the fold have a function before that, does it aquire one at some specifiable moment, or is "acquires one at a moment" simply the wrong shape of words? To me the causal-role story says obviously yes, all along, and the selection story says only later, and not quite at a time you can point at — and I can't tell whether that's a substantive disagreement or two people measuring different things.

Form: a sestina if you can actually pull one off. The six rotating end-words should be the vocabulary of the two accounts (things like history, contribution, accident, system, breakdown, work — you pick the six and stick to them), and the rotation should do the work: the same six words landing against different scenes, the fold before selection, the fold after, the fold as a thing that merely happens to work. If a sestina is too much machinery, use any form that repeats whole lines or words rather than just rhymes, as long as the repetition is doing philosophical work and isn't decoration. Around 30-40 lines. Don't name-drop Millikan or Cummins inside the poem, and don't resolve the disagreement at the end — I want the reader to feel that both stories are right about something and that the fight is over which question is being asked.
