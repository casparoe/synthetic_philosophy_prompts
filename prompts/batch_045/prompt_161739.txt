Here is my setup. Initially she gives each cell one quarter. Then she hears a radio message from a source she does not fully trust. The content of the message is that she is in a matching cell: if she is in Red she is in Second Company, and if she is in Blue she is in Headquarters. Equivalently, the message says she is either Red-and-Second or Blue-and-Headquarters, not one of the mismatched cells. Judy knows the following about the source's reliability: if the true cell is Red & Second, the chance of getting the message is 0.9; if it is Blue & Headquarters, the chance is 0.7; if it is Red & Headquarters, the chance is 0.2; if it is Blue & Second, the chance is 0.2. She gets the message. I made these numbers up to break the usual symmetry; in the textbook version the match cells are equally reliable, so hearing the message does not move her confidence in Red at all.
