Odd request, but here goes. I teach philosophy now; before grad school I spent eight years in distributed systems and formal verification — Lamport clocks, happens-before, vector clocks, consistent cuts and global snapshots, linearizability, temporal-logic specs and model checking. I still think in that vocabulary more fluently than in the one I teach in.

I'm trying to get a grip on the Rietdijk–Putnam style argument for the block universe and I want it in terms I actually trust. Give me the argument in its own terms in a short paragraph first, so I can see what's being carried, then translate it. My proposed manual is: event = process event; worldline = process; simultaneity = a consistent cut across processes; an observer's present = the frontier of its own local state, i.e. what it has already recieved; the block universe = the whole state space, or one completed execution; passage = a process's local state being refined as messages come in. The Andromeda-paradox bit is then just the point that two processes' frontier cuts can disagree about whether some distant event has happened yet.

Use better mappings if mine are bad, but the part I really want is the residue report: what translates cleanly into claims about clocks, cuts and concurrency; what only goes through if you smuggle something extra in (say, that being co-real is an equivalence relation, or that whatever a model contains is thereby real rather than merely represented); and what has no counterpart at all — especially whether "real," "exists," and "is happening now" are doing work my vocabulary has no slot for.

One thing I keep flip-flopping on: for any given process, its own next step really is undetermined by its local state, while other processes' events are just data not yet delivered. Is that asymmetry a genuine model of process-indexed becoming, or an artifact of the fact that the systems I build happen to have real nondeterminism and a fixed past? What would settle it?

Mark what you're confident about versus what you're reconstructing — hedges are fine, hand-waving isn't. And if the whole translation is a bad idea — if the argument turns on something about existence that no computational vocabulary can even gesture at, so that any clean mapping would be a fake one — say so plainly rather than handing me a tidy table with a footnote.
