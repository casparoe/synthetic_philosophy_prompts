Here are the points i copied out (shortened):

1. "EA says: work out which intervention does the most good per dollar and fund that one. In practice that means funding whatever you can put a number on — bednets, deworming, direct cash transfers — because those have a cost per life saved you can actually calculate. And it means ignoring everything that isn't countable: building a political movement, changing what a culture cares about, preventing a war that never happens. So 'effective' in effective altruism really just means 'measurable'."

2. "The deeper version of this is what I'd call the denominator problem. To rank two causes against each other you need a unit — dollars per life saved, dollars per unit of suffering prevented. But only some problems come with a natural unit. Malaria comes in cases. A corrupt institution, or a norm that makes people not care about strangers, doesn't. So the famous cause rankings are mostly decided by which problems happen to be countable, and EA mistakes that for evidence about which problems are biggest. Worse, when a harm can't be estimated the cost-effectiveness tables just quietly treat it as zero, which means any intervention with big messy long-run downsides looks amazing on paper."

3. "Earning to give: take a high-paying job in finance or tech, live cheaply, donate most of it. Critics say this makes you complicit in whatever that industry does. EAs reply that it doesn't matter, because of replaceability: if you don't take the job someone else will, and that person will donate less than you, so you still make things better. But that argument proves way too much. If replaceability works, you could take a job at a tobacco company or an oil company and it's still fine, as long as you give away more than the next person would. Nobody believes that. So the whole earning-to-give thing collapses."

4. "EA treats the world like a shopping list of separate problems to buy solutions for, instead of asking who has power and how you change that. Basically it's the politics of rich people donating money, and it quietly assumes political or structural change is a waste of time because you can't measuer it."

5. "And then that guy who ran a big crypto exchange and was always talking about EA turned out to be a fraud and lost everyone's money, which kind of shows the whole movement was never really about helping people."
