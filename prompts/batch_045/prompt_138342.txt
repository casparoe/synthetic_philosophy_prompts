I've got a 30-minute slot next Thurs in front of a joint session of our lab's RL group and the evals & policy team, and I'm presenting the case for demoting outcome supervision from default to fallback. Thesis in one line: on any task where the outcome is unverifiable at training time, or verifiable only through a learned proxy, we should supervise the process instead — and specifically with what I've been calling null-outcome process grading, where the step-level grader never sees the final answer, so credit can't leak backwards from a lucky or unlucky result.

My argument has three moves. (1) Outcome reward fails in three distinguishable ways, not one: outcomes that can't be checked at all (open-ended writing, most real-world advice, judgement calls), outcomes that are only checkable long after the gradient has to be applied (did this refactor cause an outage eighteen months from now), and outcomes checkable only by another model, which just relocates the problem one level up. (2) Process supervision isn't a weaker substitute for outcome signal, it's a different kind of object: outcome reward is a score, process supervision is a constraint on the path, and there's no single scalar for the optimizer to Goodhart, so the usual proxy-collapse story doesn't get off the ground. (3) The blinding is the part that actually buys you something — otherwise your step labels are contaminated by hindsight, and you end up teaching the model to emit the kind of reasoning that tends to precede answers that happen to be right, which is not the same thing as reasoning.

Audience, as precisely as I can: about a dozen RL people who think of credit assignment as solved-in-principle by TD methods, who've all personally been burned by reward hacking, and who will hear process supervision as hand-designed dense reward with extra steps; three people from evals who care about benchmark validity and statistical power and will immediately want to know what this costs per unit of signal; one econ/incentive-design person who hears supervise the process as teach to a more expensive test and will reach for moral hazard and multitasking; and a philosopher of action visiting for the quarter who will want to know whether a trace that instantiates the form of reasoning is the process or merely a report of it, and what work my constraints are doing if no one is acting for reasons.
