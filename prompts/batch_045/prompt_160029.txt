I work in molecular biology, on replication fidelity specifically — polymerase kinetics, proofreading and mismatch repair, error thresholds, quasispecies. I read Gödel, Escher, Bach as an undergraduate and remember the shape of the typogenetics material and of the claim that DNA replication is a Gödelian self-referential loop, but I no longer trust my memory of the formal details. So that we are working on the same object, here is the version I have in mind: strands are sequences over the four letters A, C, G, T; triplets are translated, via a fixed code table, into amino acids; a run of amino acids is an enzyme, and each amino acid names a typographic operation on a strand — move the working head right or left, switch to the complementary strand, insert or delete a letter, cut the strand, and enter or leave copy mode, which copies letters until it is switched off; an enzyme also carries a binding preference fixed by its own sequence, which determines where on a strand it docks before operating. A strand's phenotype is the set of enzymes its translation yields, and the system contains at least one strand whose own enzymes, acting on it, regenerate both the strand and the enzymes — a self-reproducing entity. If your recollection of Hofstadter's system differs from mine in the inventory of operations or in how binding is determined, say what you are using and proceed with that; I do not want the answer to turn on a detail either of us may have wrong.

What I want is a translation of this into the vocabulary I use daily, and then an honest account of what the translation loses. My vocabulary: template-directed polymerisation, primer and template, polymerase with its exonuclease domain, dNTP pools and the energetics of phosphodiester bond formation, the central dogma and its exceptions such as reverse transcription, sequence-structure-function, folding and allostery, catalytic promiscuity, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases and tRNAs as the physical realisation of the code, compartment and metabolism, mutation rate and error catastrophe, Muller's ratchet, the RNA world and ribozymes, the frozen-accident view of the code's assignment. Restate typogenetics in those terms as faithfully as you can — say what a letter is, what an enzyme is, what translation is, what replication is, what the self-reproducing strand is — and do the same for the isomorphism claim itself: state, in molecular terms, what would have to be true of a cell for the claim that replication is a Gödelian self-referential construction to be literally true rather than merely suggestive.

Then give me the residue report in three parts. First, what translated cleanly, with the molecular counterpart named. Second, what translated only with distortion, and exactly where the distortion sits — I suspect the direction of information flow is one such place, since in typogenetics the analogue of protein edits DNA directly, which is not how a cell works. Third, what has no counterpart in molecular vocabulary at all — negation, proof, undecidability, interpretation versus mechanism, anything of that kind. And then the verdict: does the residue point to a level of description my vocabulary genuinely lacks, such that molecular biology needs notions like code, information or self-description that it currently borrows rather than owns? Or does it point the other way, showing that the DNA–Gödel isomorphism is doing less work than it appears to do — in which case tell me which formal result actually carries the weight. I have in mind the neighbourhood of fixed points: quines, Kleene's recursion theorem, von Neumann's universal constructor. Say which fits, why, and where each one fails, and say plainly if the honest answer is that the Gödelian framing survives only as metaphor.

Two extensions I also want. First, stipulate a variant I will call noisy typogenetics: every copy-mode insertion receives a substitution with probability q, enzymes are consumed and must be re-translated, and a strand whose translation no longer yields a working enzyme set simply fails to reproduce. What happens to self-reproduction above a critical q, and does anything in the Gödelian framing predict, describe, or care about this? I want to know whether the analogy has content at the level of error or only at the level of exact self-reproduction. Second: in typogenetics the code tables sit outside the system, whereas in a cell the machinery that realises the genetic code is itself encoded in DNA and produced by translation. Is that circularity the same formal object as typogenetic self-reproduction or a different one, and what would it take for a code to be genuinely self-interpreting?

Finally, calibrate the answer. Separate what is settled among people who know this material — the mechanics of replication and translation, the absence in cells of any pathway that writes DNA sequence from protein sequence, the existence of reverse transcription — from what is genuinely contested: how much of Hofstadter's analogy survives scrutiny, whether the code's assignment is a frozen accident or has a stereochemical or co-evolutionary explanation, whether an RNA world preceded DNA-and-protein life, whether information talk in molecular biology explains anything or only redescribes. Flag every point where you are reading Hofstadter more charitably than the text supports, and every point where your answer rests on a stipulation rather than on something anybody has established.
