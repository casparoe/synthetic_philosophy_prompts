Morning — I'm the (unpaid, no legal training whatsoever) governance secretary for a housing co-op with about 900 members, and one of my jobs is screening every proposed rule change before it goes on the ballot for a general meeting. We meet two or three times a year, pass maybe six amendments a year, and I'm the one who has to stamp each proposal "in order" or send it back to whoever drafted it. I get about twenty minutes a proposal, sending something back costs us a month because of the notice period, and a board of nine has to sign off on whatever I've certified — so I need something I can actually point at and work through, not my own vibes. The bit that keeps biting me is self-amendment. Our rulebook can change itself and says so out loud: Rule 31 says any rule, including Rule 31 itself, can be amended by a two-thirds majority at a general meeting with fourteen days' notice. And members keep handing me amendments that do things to the machinery of amendment itself, usually by accident but sometimes quite deliberately. Can a two-thirds vote be used to take some rule out of the reach of future two-thirds votes? If an amendment lowers the quorum, was it validly passed under the old quorum or can it bootstrap itself in under the new one? What do I even do with an amendment that says of itself that it may never be amended or repealed — does it fail, does it take effect but do nothing, or does the rulebook just now contain two rules that contradict each other? And there's an audit problem too: an old amendment gave Rule 19 its own special amending procedure (approval by the management committee), then a later amendment abolished the management committee, so Rule 19 is still on the books and as far as I can tell nobody can now amend it at all.

My brother did a philosophy degree and says this is exactly what a game called Nomic was invented to play with, and that there's a book about it. I don't want a reading list or a bibliography — I can't get to a university library and I wouldn't read it anyway, so please don't give me sources or citations. What I do want is the actual logic of self-reference and self-amendment turned into an intake procedure: the questions to ask, in order, on each proposal I recieve; what counts as a sufficient answer to each; the point at which the procedure stops with a "no, send it back"; and the point at which it has to hand the decision to a human being because the question is really about what the co-op wants to be able to bind itself to, not about what the rules say. I've started informally sorting proposals into four buckets and I'd like you to tell me which of these distinctions actually need to survive and which are clutter: (1) order — does the amendment touch the amending rule rather than just some first-order rule; (2) reach — does it operate on itself, e.g. retroactively or by repealing its own enabling procedure once it takes effect, i.e. one-shot versus continuing amendments; (3) orphans — does it leave some rule whose amendment route no longer exists; (4) stacking — does it change the threshold, quorum or manner-and-form in the same vote as some substantive change. I also need a tie-break rule, since our rulebook is completely silent on it: when two rules genuinely conflict, do I go later-in-time, higher-order-wins, or more-specific-wins, and whatever you pick, be honest about whether it's derivable from anything or is just a choice we'd be making. And please be straight about which steps are judgement dressed up as procedure — I'd rather know than ship a form that looks rigorous and isn't. British English throughout, please (organisation, favour, judgement), and plain language: the person running this is a volunteer with a spreadsheet, not a lawyer.

Here's the rulebook as it stands and the three live proposals I want you to run the finished procedure on:

- **Rule 31**: any rule, including this rule, may be amended by a two-thirds majority at a general meeting of which fourteen days' notice has been given. Quorum for a general meeting is 40 members.
- **Rule 3**: membership is open to any adult who pays the subscription.
- **Rule 7**: no member may be expelled without a hearing.
- **Rule 12**: the treasurer must publish accounts quarterly.
- **Proposal A** (from a member, looks harmless): Rule 7 may only be amended by a three-quarters majority at two consecutive general meetings.
- **Proposal B** (from the management committee, and the one I'm under pressure to let through as a single motion before the AGM): the quorum for a general meeting is reduced to 12 members, and Rule 3 is amended to restrict membership to people who have lived in the borough for at least two years.
- **Proposal C** (from a member who has clearly thought about this too much): Rule 12 is repealed with immediate effect, and this amendment may not be amended or repealed.

Please run the procedure on all three and give me the verdict on each, including at least one where the answer goes against something I want — I'd guess B is going to be that one, since if it has to be split the quorum fix definately won't land before the AGM and I'll be the one explaining that to the committee. Then tell me what the procedure can't do: which kinds of proposal it will always get wrong or stay silent on, and what I should do with those besides guessing.
