I'm writing the closing section of a seminar paper on the ethics of reparations for historical injustice. The seminar has been almost entirely on the machinery: who owes what to whom once the original wrongdoers and the original victims are dead, whether benefits that have passed to innocent recipients ground a claim on their own, whether a current holding can even be returned in the coin in which it was lost, and what distinguishes repair from apology, punishment, or redistribution. I want to end by reading Shakespeare's The Tempest as something that stages those questions rather than argues them, because I think the play is unusually good on the failure modes of repair — but only if I can do it without the section collapsing into the reading everyone already knows, where Caliban is the dispossessed native, Prospero is the coloniser, and the upshot is that empire was cruel. That reading is true and it is not worth a section. I don't need names, titles of articles, or footnotes; I have the reading list. I need the argument, and I need somebody to tell me when the analogy is being forced.

Four things in the play seem to me to be doing real work, and I'd like them taken seriously rather than mentioned. First, the symmetry the play constructs and then declines to notice: Prospero is himself the survivor of a usurpation, put to sea with an infant daughter by his own brother, and he pursues that claim to the point of full restoration, while never once treating Caliban's formally parallel claim — that the island came to him through his mother Sycorax and was taken from him — as a claim at all. Second, the play stages the benefits-of-colonisation offset from both directions in adjacent speeches: Prospero's side is that he housed Caliban and taught him language; Caliban's reply is that the profit he got from that gift was the ability to curse, and that he himself was the benefactor, having shown Prospero the springs and the fertile ground of the island in exchange for enslavement. Third, there is Ariel, who has a contractual claim: he says he performed his service without grudge or grumbling and was promised an abatement of a full year, and Prospero's answer is not to pay or to refuse but to remind Ariel of the torment he was freed from and threaten to peg him inside an oak for twelve winters — and then to release him anyway, later, on his own initiative. Fourth, the party who actually owes Prospero something, Antonio, never apologises and is never made to pay; he is forgiven, and the restoration of the dukedom comes bundled with a marriage alliance that reorganises relations between Milan and Naples going forward.

I've been calling this last pattern remedial capture, by which I mean a situation in which the party who controls whether repair happens at all also controls the description of what would count as repair, so that what is handed back is whatever the wrongdoer was prepared to give — Prospero sets every term in the play: the timetable of Ariel's freedom, the return of an island he is leaving regardless, the pardon extended to his brother. Two questions about that coinage. Is it a genuine moral phenomenon, or only a morally inert redescription of the obvious fact that the powerful set the terms? What would the difference look like inside the play, scene by scene, and does the ending commit it — does the comedy resolve by having Prospero's remedies accepted as sufficient? And the larger reading: is Prospero's restoration the right remedy for the wrong done to him, and if so why is the formally identical remedy — returning the island — not presented as repairing Caliban? What kind of claim, if any, does Ariel have for the year? Does Caliban's answer to the language exchange settle the offsetting question, or does it only reassert that the recipient rather than the giver values the benefit, and what follows if the recipient's valuation is self-serving? Is forgiveness here a substitute for payment or a refusal to pay in ceremonial dress? And be honest about where the reading fails: if Caliban cannot be a plaintiff in a tort frame, or if the loose ends are simply what romance as a genre does and say nothing about repair, tell me and I will cut the section rather than pad it.
