I've just come out of a long argument with a friend about whether the possibility that our foundational axioms are inconsistent should change how we think about mathematical knowledge, and I want to check that the position I ended up defending hangs together before I try to write it up. Roughly, I hold:

(1) The risk is real. I would put the inconsistency of ZFC at around one per cent or a little more, and that of PA lower, but not at zero.

(2) Realism about the natural numbers: a determinate intended structure, to which the axioms answer rather than which they constitute, and a fact of the matter about arithmetic regardless of what we can prove.

(3) A discovered inconsistency would be far less damaging than it is usually made out to be. The reason is a view I will call robustness under repair: a claim counts as mathematically known when it is provable in every member of the set of revisions we would find acceptable, so what we know is not the logical closure of our current axioms but the part that survives every repair we would actually make. On this view nearly all of the mathematics we apply would survive.

(4) Our track record is genuine evidence of consistency: successful application, and long proofs re-derived by independent routes, should raise our confidence that the theories we use are consistent.

(5) Gödel's second incompleteness theorem finished Hilbert's programme in its original form — but that is a claim about what can be proved and from where, and says nothing directly about whether the axioms are consistent.

(6) If a contradiction were actually derived tomorrow, I would expect the community to treat the derivation as suspect before treating the axioms as suspect.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, which only look as though they are, and what is the cheapest revision? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad; for each, say what would have to be given up and what giving it up would cost; and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. My friend attacked (4) hardest and left (2) untouched, which surprised me. Written British English, please.
