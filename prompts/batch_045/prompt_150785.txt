I've been reading this stuff since the sequences and the old SSC days, and there's one analogy that keeps doing an insane amount of work while nobody audits it: consciousness is a broadcast.

You know the version. There's Baars' theater with the spotlight on the stage and a dark audience of unconscious processors, there's the global neuronal workspace with ignition and long-range fan-out, and by the time it reaches blog posts it's become: consciousness is a message posted to the company-wide Slack channel, where every department can read it and act on it, while unconscious processing is a pile of private DMs between two modules that nobody else ever sees.

What drives me up the wall is that the same analogy gets used to license two opposite conclusions. The GWT-friendly crowd (there's a guy in the ACX comments under one of the consciousness posts who does this every single time) slides from globally available to phenomenally conscious: the feel just is what wide availability is like from the inside, so there's no leftover hard problem, access is all there is. Then the Block-style crowd grabs the very same picture and says: exactly, broadcast is the A-consciousness notion, this is a theory of access and it never touches phenomenality, the analogy proves my point that they're distinct. Both of them can't be getting that out of one Slack channel.

So: audit it. I want the correspondence table first — what plays the role of the sender, the channel, the message, the subscribers, the bandwidth limit, the you that somehow reads the message. Then where the mapping breaks. Two things I think are doing sneaky work but I can't tell if they matter:

1. A broadcast has one emitter and a passive audience, but ignition looks receiver-driven — the population that amplifies the signal is partly what makes it a broadcast at all, so it's less a tower transmitting and more a stampede that becomes a signal because enough people joined. Does that break anything, or is it incidental?
2. A broadcast is public to everyone on the channel. Why should a representation being widely available make it feel like anything, rather than just being more useful? The analogy seems to import publicity-in-the-system and then quietly cash it out as phenomenality.

I made up three cases to stress-test it, and I'd rather you engage with these than with the literature:

- narrowcast: a representation fans out to a big but partial set of consumers — it reaches report and action selection, but the systems for memory consolidation and affective appraisal are disconnected. Is that subject phenomenally conscious of the content? Access-conscious? Which notion is "how many readers" even tracking?
- the 3am post: the broadcast fires, nothing consumes it at the time, but the message is durable and every relevant module reads it later and acts on it. Compare the reverse: full live fan-out with a perfect trace, wiped from every consumer within 100 ms and leaving nothing anywhere. If one of these is the conscious episode, which, and what does that say about whether the broadcast event or the consumption is the load-bearing part?
- DMs only: a rich, stable representation reaching a single consumer system over a dedicated high-bandwidth private line, never posted to the channel, but driving detailed behavior. If broadcast was the whole story, why isn't this consciousness too — or if you bite that bullet, has the analogy just collapsed into "processing that matters"?

Then the actual question: which of those disanalogies are load-bearing for the two conclusions and which are noise, does either conclusion survive once the table is on the desk, and is there a better analogy — I've been kicking around currency, a format conversion, and a shared protocol as replacements, but I genuinely can't tell if those fix anything or just move the confusion. No citations needed, I mostly want the argument.
