My friend from a philosophy-of-physics reading group and I got into an argument last week about Maxwell's demon, and somehow we ended up stuck on the sentence "information is physical." Here's roughly how it went: I gave the move where the demon has to record which side each molecule arrives from, its memory eventually fills up, and erasing that record costs more entropy than the demon ever saved, so the second law survives. He said that only works if you assume the demon's memory is finite and has to be recycled — why couldn't it just keep an ever-growing ledger, one fresh bit of memory per molecule, and never erase anything at all? When I pushed him on whether that's even coherent over infinite time, he just said "look, information is physical" and acted like the discussion was over. I genuinely can't tell what work that sentence was supposed to do there, because he already agrees the ledger is a physical object sitting in the box, so it can't just mean that.

So: what are the different precise claims that sentence could be expressing? I can gesture at a few — something trivial like "bits have to be carried by some physical system," something stronger about there being an unavoidable thermodynamic price for erasing or for acquiring information, something weirdly epistemic where entropy is really a measure of our ignorance and the "physicality" is a fact about the description rather than the world, something about information never being destroyed as a closed system evolves, and something much more grandiose along the lines of information being what reality is made of rather than a feature of how we talk about it — but I can't organize them, and I don't know which ones are platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, and which are just false or unfalsifiable. It also doesn't help that I've seen it credited to some physicist whose name I can never remember, so I have no idea whether it started life as a narrow technical claim or as a manifesto.

What I'd like is a menu rather than an essay: lay the readings out from weakest to strongest, say for each whether it's trivial, substantive but defensible, or just wrong, and be explicit about which reading would actually have to be true for my friend's infinite-ledger point to go through — and whether that reading is one anyone has independent reason to accept, or whether he's using "information is physical" to sound profound while quietly smuggling in the very assumption he denied, namely that the demon's memory has to be bounded. (I TA for an undergrad thermal physics course, so I'm fine with equations if they make a reading more precise.)
