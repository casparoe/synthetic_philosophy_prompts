I'm a retired lab scientist — thirty-odd years of protocols, calibration logs and safety committees — and I've only started reading philosophy properly since I retired, so I dont really know the literature and I'll probably mangle the vocabulary. What I keep bumping into is the doctrine of double effect and the whole trolley business: the claim that diverting the trolley onto the sidetrack can be permissible while pushing the man off the footbridge is not, and that the difference turns on what you intend as opposed to what you merely foresee, even when the number of dead comes out the same. I get the distinction, but it strikes me as suspiciously like a bookkeeping convention, and I'd like to see what it looks like if an institution actually runs it as one.

Could you write me a parody Standard Operating Procedure — deadpan, impersonal, acronym-heavy, numbered clauses, with a revision history and a distribution list, 500-600 words — issued by the compliance office of a transit authority in a jurisdiction that has codified double effect into regulation. Setup I want the jokes built on:

- The trolley runs to a published timetable, the people on the track are Positioned Parties under a maintenance schedule, and the person at the lever is a rostered Operator rather than a passer-by; nobody in the document ever asks who arranged any of this.
- The compliance office is the Office of Foresight Allocation, and its job is classifying deaths: deaths brought about, foreseen side effects, and what the memo calls tertiary effects — a death that is a foreseen consequence of something that is itself merely foreseen. The memo should treat the third category as though it is not something the authority answers for at all, without ever saying that outright.
- A made-up rule I'd like enforced: the five-metre rule. Beyond five metres between Operator and Positioned Party the relation counts as mere foresight; inside five metres it counts as intention. The whole loaded distinction becomes a measurement you take with a tape.
- The footbridge case gets handled under manual handling: pushing is prohibited as unauthorised use of a Positioned Party as equipment, while the same death brought about by other means stays fully compliant.

Don't let the memo wink — no clause should hint that any of this is monstrous.

Format: the memo first, then one section titled "What's being sent up", about 200 words, going clause by clause on which real feature of double effect or of the standard trolley setup you're exaggerating, so a reader who hasnt read any of this can tell it isn't just nonsense.
