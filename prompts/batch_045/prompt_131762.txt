I'm in a small reading group — two philosophers, an astronomer, and me — that has been circling the Fermi question for months, and I want to force the disagreement into numbers rather than vibes. Here is the proposition, stipulated as precisely as I can manage: the Milky Way contains, right now, at least one other lineage that has produced a technosignature a civilization with roughly our current instruments — a present-day radio survey plus a present-day transit-spectroscopy mission — could detect from the far side of the galaxy, if it pointed at them. My credence is about 0.25; my friend Priya's is about 0.02. If you think the proposition as stated still doesn't settle into something with a truth value, say what you would count as settling it and give me numbers on that instead.

One reason mine is as high as it is is an argument I made up and can't shake, which I'll call the interior-turn hypothesis: any lineage that gets cheap energy and cheap computation also gets cheap, arbitrarily high-fidelity, arbitrarily variable artificial experience, and once subjective life can be expanded internally at near-zero marginal cost, the point of physically settling — or even announcing yourself at — new stars falls toward zero. On that story the galaxy could be full of lineages that are invisible from the outside not because they are hiding, or quarantining us, or went extinct, but because the only thing they still want more of is not spatial. I'm not attached to it; if you think it is confused, tell me which premise fails and give that premise a number anyway.

What I want from you is your own credence on the proposition, decomposed. Give me the sub-questions you think carry the weight, each with a number and one line of justification — at minimum: whether the hard step is behind us (abiogenesis, complexity, intelligence) or ahead of us; how often a technological lineage would become detectable at all, conditional on arising; how complete our present surveys actually are; and how much evidential weight the silence bears in the first place — then show how the terms multiply out to your headline figure. After that: which term you are least confident in, which single further observation or argument would move that term most, and a separate probability that the interior-turn story is the largest single contributor to the silence, conditional on lineages being common. Last thing: tell me where you think the gap between 0.25 and 0.02 really lives — on the filter, on the visibility term, or on whether the silence is evidence at all — and which of the two of us you would bet against. Please don't walk through the Drake equation or rehearse the Fermi paradox; we have read all of that and it will not move either of us, and please don't give me citations, references, or a reading list — I want your own numbers, from the armchair. Six hundred words maximum; I would rather have seven or eight numbered rows with one line each and two short paragraphs than continuous prose.
