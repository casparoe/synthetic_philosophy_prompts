I chair the collections care committee of a university museum of natural history. Our holdings include a herbarium of some 200,000 sheets, a research collection of around 40,000 pinned insects, and a duplicate teaching collection of botanical material used in undergraduate practicals. Carpet-beetle larvae have been found in the teaching collection, and the committee must now rule on two things at once.

First, a petition signed by four curators and two technicians asks us to adopt a policy that no insect be killed except where necessary to protect material that cannot be replaced, and that, where killing is necessary, we use the least distressing method available — we cannot say painless, given how little is settled about insect sentience. Secondly, the director asks us to resolve to become "pesticide-free" within two years, which would release a £1.2m bequest offered on precisely that condition.

Please draft the committee's decision memo: a clear recommendation — accept, reject, or accept with conditions — a timetable, and reasons that both the petitioners and the director could actually reply to. The memo must face rather than bypass three difficulties. (i) The non-chemical treatments we would switch to, freezing and nitrogen anoxia, are themselves lethal, and anoxia takes two to three weeks during which the insects starve: the choice before us is between methods of killing, not between killing and refraining. (ii) The museum already kills insects deliberately for its research collection, so any policy must say what distinguishes the larvae eating the teaching collection from the beetles we pin. (iii) Most of the infested teaching material is replaceable at modest cost, which on the petitioners' own criterion seems to permit simply letting it be destroyed.

Give the strongest consideration on the losing side an honest treatment — including the value of the donor's money and the petitioners' claim that the word "pest" does moral work, making insects look like aggressors and licensing carelessness. Write the memo in British English and in under 700 words.
