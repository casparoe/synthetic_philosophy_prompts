I have no training in philosophy; I came to this on my own, after an old friend said, looking at a photograph of the two of us at twenty, that the person in the picture was not him anymore. Since then I have been reading, without much guidance, about what it takes for one and the same thing to exist through time. As I understand the dispute, one side holds that a thing is wholly present at each moment at which it exists, so that what occupies the chair now is the entire object and not a slice of it; the other holds that things are spread out in time the way a road is spread out across a landscape, so that what occupies the chair now is only a part of a longer object, with earlier and later parts composing the whole. My sense is that each side has a characteristic way of handling change — a thing that was straight and is now bent — and that each has been accused of quietly explaining change away rather than accounting for it.

I would like a joke on this topic to read aloud at the start of a discussion meeting of about twenty people, roughly half of whom will know the dispute well and half of whom will never have heard of it. Three constraints.

First: the joke should turn on the actual content of the disagreement — what a thing would have to be like for it to be first straight and then bent, how many things are in the room if the slicing view is right, what it would be for a part rather than the whole of a person to be present — and not on the Latin-derived names of the two views, not on the obvious four-dimensional-creature imagery, and not on Theseus's ship, which is the one thing the non-specialists in the room will already have heard of.

Second: I have a half-formed view of my own that I would like the joke to be compatible with. I do not think the slicing, if there is slicing, is infinitely fine. I want to say that a person's temporal parts are stretches of continuous conscious life, so that a part ends at a deep, dreamless sleep and a new one begins on waking, rather than there being a part for every instant. A joke that simply assumed the instant-by-instant version would misfire for me.

Third: my own suspicion is that both views, for all their differences, end up saying something slightly deflating about change, and that this is where the real joke lies. If you think that is a misreading of one side or of both, say so, and then write the joke about whatever you take the genuinely funny thing in this neighborhood to be.

Please give me the joke in a form I can read aloud in under a minute, and then, in two or three sentences, state which view it works from and why the punchline is supposed to land.
