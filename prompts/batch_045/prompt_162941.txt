hi — i'm finishing up a chapter (phd, metaphysics) on whether there has to be a fundamental level, and i want a sonnet as the epigraph, something i could also read out at the start of a talk if it works. can you write one?

the thing i want the poem to actually get right is a wrinkle that keeps getting lost in discussion. people use "fundamental" as if it names one property, but it seems to me it's doing two jobs, and my own shorthand for them is bedrock vs coverage:

- bedrock: a thing or fact is fundamental if nothing grounds it — it sits at the bottom, it's ungrounded.
- coverage: a thing or fact is fundamental if everything else is grounded in it, i.e. it's the single item the whole dependence structure hangs from (the thing on which everything supervenes).

usually these ride together: you picture the microphysical facts as ungrounded and as what everything else is grounded in, so smallest = fundamental and nobody notices the split. but they can come apart. the two cases i care about:

1. the whole-cosmos-first view: the biggest thing is the ungrounded one and it grounds its own parts, so the fundamental item is the whole rather than the tiny bits. bedrock and coverage both land on the cosmos, and both land somewhere you weren't expecting.
2. a chain that simply descends forever, with no bottom. then nothing is ungrounded, and there's no single item everything hangs from either — so on my way of putting it nothing is fundamental at all, unless you let the whole infinite structure count as fundamental in the coverage sense without being bedrock.

structurally, what i'd like: english/shakespearean sonnet, three quatrains and a closing couplet, roughly iambic but don't wreck the sense for the meter. quatrain 1 sets up the naive descent — grounding as the non-causal "in virtue of" relation, bigger facts resting on smaller ones, the reader expecting a floor. quatrain 2 makes the identification explicit (smallest = bedrock = coverage) and lets it feel obvious. quatrain 3 is where it cracks: the two jobs come apart, and i want you to commit to wich case does the splitting (either of the two above, or both if you can make that work in four lines). the couplet then has to say exactly what gets given up — which of the two readings survives, or whether "fundamental" survives at all.

one more formal thing, if it doesn't fight the rest of it: keep the quatrains' rhymes alternating (abab, cdcd, efef) so no line is answered by the line immediately beneath it, and make the final couplet the only adjacent pair — the only place the chain lands on itself and stops. if that turns out too cute or unworkable in english, drop it, but i'd like to see it attempted.

it should be accurate enough that someone who works on grounding wouldn't wince, and it shouldn't be jargon in verse form or a poem that explains itself in footnotes. no names, no citations, no endnotes — the poem has to stand on its own.
