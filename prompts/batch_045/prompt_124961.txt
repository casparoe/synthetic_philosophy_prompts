I chair the program committee of a foundation that awards a single five-year grant of roughly $40 million each cycle. We vote on Tuesday, and we are down to two finalists.

Program A funds seasonal malaria chemoprevention and bed-net distribution for about two million children under five. It is expected to avert roughly 1,800 deaths over five years, every one of them a child alive today.

Program B funds detection and response capacity for engineered pandemics. Our best estimate is that it lowers the probability of a civilizational collapse this century by about one percentage point, which our staff analysis values at several times Program A in expected lives saved. But the benefit would arrive at a time when, had we acted otherwise, the beneficiaries would not exist at all: they would be different people, and vastly more of them. On a person-affecting view, then, Program B benefits no one and Program A wins outright.

Committee credences are roughly 45 percent on impersonal total views, 40 percent on person-affecting views, and 15 percent on prioritarian views favoring the worst-off. Averaging across them is not straightforward, because each view delivers its verdicts in a different currency: any weighted sum of choice-worthiness requires a normalization convention, and the two staff memos, using different but equally undefended conventions, reached opposite recommendations.

A further wrinkle: our founding deed restricts grants to relieving the suffering of people alive when the grant is made, and a majority of the committee reads this as a genuine obstacle to Program B. One member proposes a bridge instead: fund Program A for two years, commission work on how to compare across moral theories, and revisit B if its co-funder's matching window is still open.

Draft the decision memo I will circulate before the vote. It should recommend one specific course of action, say how much weight the deed's restriction should carry relative to the underlying moral case, and state the strongest version of the argument for the option it rejects.
