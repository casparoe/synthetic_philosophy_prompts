Looking for a name for something, if it has one. I'm trying to finish a paper in social epistemology and I keep having to describe the same configuration in three paragraphs because I can't point to a concept, so I'm past the stage where a bare label is of any use.

Here's the pattern. A community has an explicit, genuinely well-meant norm of charity: before anyone is allowed to assess a criticism coming from outside, the criticism has to be restated in the community's own vocabulary, in the strongest form anyone can manage. People follow the norm. And the systematic effect is that nothing from outside ever lands as evidence against the community's core commitments — because by the time the claim has been translated into the home vocabulary, it either says something the community already believed or it says something obviously false. The members are not insulated from outside sources; they read them, cite them, and can repeat the objection back to you accurately if you ask. They don't discredit outsiders either. They'll say the critic is smart and well-intentioned and that what she really means is p-star, where p-star is the anodyne translation. So it isn't the omission structure of the epistemic bubble and it isn't the discrediting-and-alienation structure of the echo chamber. Whatever goes wrong goes wrong at the uptake step, not at the source-selection step and not at the trust step.

Two cases I keep coming back to. One is a reading group in a department I used to be in: every objection to the framework got "reconstructed in the best light," and the reconstruction always turned out to be a point the framework had itself made a decade earlier, so the outside literature was constantly being read and never once biting. The other is an online community where every objection has to be cashed out as a probability or an expected value before it counts as an objection at all; complaints that resist that formatting are set aside not as wrong but as not yet a criticism, and they are set aside indefinitely.

The reason I can't tell whether it's a vice is that every individual application of the norm looks faultless. Charity is a good norm. Translation across vocabularies is unavoidable if you're going to argue with anyone. Sometimes the translation is correct and the outsider really is confused or really is making a point you already accept. So I don't know whether the thing to name is the norm, or the disposition of the community to apply it only in one direction, or just the outcome — the immunity — regardless of how it's produced.

So: is there an established concept or a literature for this exact configuration? If something nearby fits only imperfectly, I want to know what the nearest candidates are and exactly where each of them comes apart from what I've described — I assume the bubble/echo chamber pair is the first place to look, but I'd also want it distinguished from things like hermeneutical marginalization, incommensurability claims about rival conceptual schemes, credibility deficits, and ordinary motivated reasoning, none of which seems to me to be quite it. If there's nothing established, coin a term, define it, and give me boundary cases: a case where the same translation practice is clearly fine, a case where it's clearly vicious, and a hard case in between. Also tell me honestly whether you think it's a vice at all, or whether it's just what having a framework is.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — whether there's an established concept, or if not the term you'd propose — then give me the reasoning and the boundary cases. No preamble, and please don't spend the first paragraph restating my question back to me. And no bibliography, I can find the reading myself.
