I'm in a graduate seminar on consciousness — we've been through the standard higher-order material (the Rosenthal-style thought theory, the Armstrong/Lycan inner-sense version, Block's access/phenomenal distinction) and each of us has to circulate a one-page statement of where we stand, which the rest of the group then spends twenty minutes attacking before the presentation. Mine is below, and I'd like an audit rather than a summary or a survey of the literature.

Here are my commitments, as five claims. (1) A mental state is conscious if and only if, and in virtue of the fact that, a distinct higher-order representational state of the same subject represents it as a state of that subject. I am an actualist about this: a standing disposition to form such a representation is not enough; the higher-order state has to actually be there. (2) I want to offer this as an empirical identity claim rather than a conceptual analysis — closer to water = H2O than to bachelor = unmarried man — so that the theory is revisable in light of evidence and I am not obliged to answer conceivability arguments on their own terms. (3) I add what I call the contact requirement, to block the rock objection and the general worry about stray or targetless higher-order states: the higher-order state must be non-inferentially produced by the very state it targets, and must represent that state de re, as this state, rather than merely as whatever is currently causing such-and-such. My test case here is a surgical one — suppose an intervention leaves a subject with two functionally independent hemispheres, and the higher-order system in the left one is rewired so that its representations are non-inferentially caused by, and de re about, visual states in the right one. I have a strong pull toward saying those right-hemisphere states are then conscious, and another pull toward saying the case is incoherent, and I can't tell which of my other claims is generating which pull. (4) I also want to accommodate unattended experience: absorbed in an argument, I am still conscious of the weight of the pen in my hand and the hum of the fridge, though I am attending to neither and am plainly not forming a separate higher-order thought about each. My fix is a field proposal — a single coarse-grained higher-order state can take a whole scene or field of first-order states as one object at once, so I don't owe the implausible one-thought-per-state explosion. (5) Finally, two claims about character. First, the character of my experience is intrinsic to my first-order states: adding the higher-order state changes what I am conscious of, not what my experience is like; it doesn't paint anything in. Second, this is exactly why the theory helps with the hard problem — because my awareness of my own states is transitive, I can only ever represent them relationally, and the residual sense that something has been left out is an artifact of the mechanism by which I come to know about them.

What I want to know is which of these actually pull against each other and which only look as though they do. I genuinely don't know where the trouble is. I have a suspicion that (3) and (4) are doing incompatible work — the contact requirement wants a tight, per-state, non-inferential link, and the field proposal seems to give me one state loosely hooked up to many — but I can't get clear about whether the fault is in the contact requirement or in the field proposal, and someone in the seminar has told me the real problem is in (5). Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally serious, and for each one say what would have to be given up and what giving it up would cost me; if there is a single revision that would clean up more than one of them at once, say which. Also flag anything I've simply helped myself to without argument, and say which of my claims does the least real work.

One more request about how you frame the answer: please separate, as you go, what is settled among people who actually work on these questions from what is a live dispute, and don't present a contested reading as the consensus in either direction. I keep getting pushback of the form that's just contested and I often can't tell whether it's genuinely contested or merely unfamiliar to me.
