So me and my housemate were having one of those 1am arguments about whether the state should really compensate people for things that go wrong in their lives. He's been going on about luck egalitarianism — his gloss was that the whole point of the view is that inequalities which trace back to your own choices are fine and you should be left to eat them, while inequalities that trace back to circumstances nobody picked (your genes, your parents, the meteor that lands on your shed) get neutralised. I said fine, but then what about someone who knowingly took a gamble and lost — apparently that's meant to be the easy case, no compensation, you chose it. I said: okay, but what about someone who only took the gamble because of a temperament they were born with, and then he started going on about a regress and I sort of lost the thread.

Anyway, total non-sequitur, but I'm trying to write a joke for a group chat, and I want it to be one of those jokes that only really works if you actually know the position — not just a ha-ha-philosophers-are-daft gag. The setup I've half-written: there's an office (heavenly bureaucracy? jobcentre from hell? up to you) called something like the Office of Brute Misfortune, where a clerk has to rule on whether what happened to you was brute luck or option luck, and if it's option luck they stamp NO RECCOURSE on your form and slide you a leaflet with something smug on the cover. A bloke comes in who entered a lottery in which the single winner gets a vineyard in the Dordogne and every loser gets an extremely tedious but harmless disease; he lost, and he's here about the disease. Clerk says: you bought the ticket, option luck, next please. He says: yes, but I only bought it because I was born with a disposition that loves risk, and I did not choose that.

Could you land the punchline for me?
