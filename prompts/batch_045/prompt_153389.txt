Ok so I've been listening to this history podcast episode about the debate over ordinary German men who became Holocaust perpetrators, and I want to know how much of what they said I should actually believe. I'm not a historian, I got interested after falling down a wikipedia hole one night and now I can't tell what's reputable.

Their summary went roughly like this (I'm paraphrasing from memory so maybe I'm the one mangling it):

"For years everyone assumed the men actually doing the shooting in occupied Poland were ideologically trained killers, SS fanatics, Einsatzgruppen types, people who'd been screened for being brutal. Then Christopher Browning's Ordinary Men looked at Reserve Police Battalion 101, a unit of middle aged Hamburg policemen who shot and deported tens of thousands of Jews, and showed they were completely average blokes with no special selection or training. His explanation is that they were under orders and would have been in serious trouble if they'd refused, and that once you've killed once you keep going because you can't face admitting to yourself what you've already done. Then Daniel Goldhagen hit back with Hitler's Willing Executioners and said no, ordinary Germans knew exactly what they were doing and wanted to do it, because eliminationist antisemitism was just part of German culture by then; the guards weren't reluctantly following orders, they tortured people for fun, which proves they were enthusiastic. So the real argument between them is about how much ordinary Germans actually knew about the killings. The field has basically settled it in Browning's favour now, nobody takes Goldhagen seriously anymore, and the lesson is that it was the situation that made them kill rather than antisemitism. Milgram had already shown that ordinary people will obey orders to the point of killing, and Battalion 101 is basically that finding in the field."

Something here feels off to me and I can't put my finger on where. I have a feeling the whole "ordinary men vs willing executioners" framing might be a fake choice, surely someone can be a totally ordinary person and still be a willing and convinced killer, so why is this even a debate? But I genuinely don't know if that's a point people have actually made or just me being glib.

Also, a friend of mine who did a masters in history said something I keep turning over: she said Browning's evidence is much better at explaining why the men kept killing once they'd started than why they started at all, because the peer pressure stuff, not wanting to let your mates down, careerism, getting used to it, only gets going after the first massacre. She called it the difference between onset conditions and maintenance conditions. It sounded like a real distinction but I've never seen it in anything I've read and she might have made it up on the spot.
