Then I thought: surely some of what we want from a belief system is that it not be mushy — sitting at exactly 0.5/0.5 feels like it's missing something even when it's perfectly calibrated. So I added a boldness term: I(c) = (c − v)^2 + λ·H(c), where H(c) = −c·ln(c) − (1−c)·ln(1−c) is the entropy of the credence and λ = 0.05. Entropy peaks at 0.5, so this penalizes hedging and rewards confident credences on top of rewarding truth. I checked numerically that if my actual credence in rain tomorrow is 0.7, the credence b that minimizes expected inaccuracy from my own perspective isn't 0.7 but about 0.723, and the gap always points outward — the rule always wants you slightly more extreme than you really are, and the bigger λ is, the bigger the nudge.
