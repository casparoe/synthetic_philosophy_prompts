I'm a PhD student in machine learning working on world models and mechanistic interpretability for deep RL agents. The vocabulary I actually think in is latent states and their posteriors, probes and linear decodability, interchange interventions and causal abstraction, on-policy versus off-policy distribution shift, successor features, rate-distortion tradeoffs, and model-based versus model-free control. I read a bit of philosophy of mind on the side, mostly because I use belief and desire talk constantly in my own papers and have never been able to tell whether it is convenient shorthand or a commitment I cannot cash out.

I would like the eliminativist position on beliefs and desires restated one claim at a time in that vocabulary: that propositional attitudes are posits of a folk theory; that the theory has stalled and explains little of what we care about; that its states resist principled individuation; and that the likely fate of the theory is replacement rather than reduction. Restate each as faithfully as you can manage without smuggling in my framework's assumptions and calling the result a translation. In particular I want to know what elimination, as opposed to reduction, would even mean in my terms. Is it the claim that no partition of a system's states that any learner could arrive at realizes the folk state space? That where attitude-like partitions do exist they are never the best intervention handles? Neither, or both?

Three things I would like pressed rather than accommodated.

First, an asymmetry I keep running into and have started calling the observer surplus. When I train a network to predict another agent's behaviour in a partially observed gridworld, a low-dimensional code reliably emerges that behaves like an attribution of a goal together with an information state, and interventions on that code transfer zero-shot to novel mazes. When I train the acting agent itself on the same tasks, nothing belief-shaped is decodable; control seems to ride on something closer to a monolithic value cache. Does this asymmetry favour the eliminativist, on the grounds that beliefs turn out to live in an observer-side compression scheme rather than in the agent? Or does it favour the realist, on the grounds that a posit supporting transferable interventions in unseen environments is precisely what a well-confirmed unobservable looks like?

Second, my own operationalisation of successor-hood, which I suspect is too weak. Say a posit has been eliminated only if some other partition of the system's states supports better interchange-intervention transfer than the attitude-like one, that partition licenses generalizations the attitude-like one does not, and there is no lossless translation back into the old vocabulary. Are these conditions jointly satisfiable at all? And given that intervention-transfer is always measured against a task distribution, does elimination come out task-relative, so that eliminativism quietly becomes a pluralism about which decomposition is useful when? Is that collapse something an eliminativist should welcome?

Third, what I call basis-relativity. When I probe for a proposition-like variable I never recover anything separable into agent, content, and attitude. What I get is smeared across timestep, modality, and policy head, and the equivalence class I am willing to call a content depends on which readout basis I choose. Does this vindicate the eliminativist complaint about content individuation, or does it only show that my representations are heavily overcomplete?

Then the part I actually care about, the residue report. Go claim by claim: what translated cleanly, what you had to distort to fit, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary whatsoever. My own hypothesis about where the real trouble lies is that the normative dimension has no home here — the idea that a representation is answerable to how things are, and that citing someone's belief is giving a reason rather than naming a cause — since the only notion of error my vocabulary supplies is expected loss. I take that to be a defect in how I talk rather than a muddle in the philosophical debate, but I would rather be argued out of it than confirmed in it. I also want your candid view on whether any of the three considerations above are genuine data about the debate or artifacts of the way I have set them up, and whether anything in the debate stops looking substantive once it is translated.
