I want an explanation of a fairly specific conceptual knot, at the level of the machinery rather than at the level of the slogan.

The setting is the careenium-and-simmballs thought experiment from the older popular literature on minds and machines. Restating it so that we don't depend on a shared memory of the text: a shallow bowl-shaped world is filled with a soft, deformable material, the careenium, which has dynamics of its own — dents spread, ridges slump, slow waves propagate. Buoyant on it float hard balls, the simmballs. Where a simmball sits, the careenium is depressed; the slopes thereby created steer where the simmballs roll next, and so do the careenium's own relaxations. The simmballs' arrangements can carry markings and can be so arranged that the ball-level rules — collision, exclusion, reconfiguration — implement addition or primality testing; you can predict tomorrow's simmball configuration from today's without ever tracking the careenium's micro-deformations. The question the setup exists to raise is who shoves whom: do the simmballs — the symbols, the thing described by the system's software-level story — push the substrate around, or does the careenium only ever push itself, and the simmballs, around?

What I want explained is why that question, as posed, is not one question, and why its fate seems to turn on the character of the mapping between simmball-level states and careenium-level states rather than on anything dynamical. To pin that down, consider three variants of the substrate:

(i) The pliable careenium, as above: many distinct careenium states realise one and the same simmball configuration, and causation runs both ways.
(ii) The indexed careenium: the mapping between simmball configurations and careenium states is exactly one-to-one and onto — no coarse-graining anywhere — though the substrate still has its own dynamics and still pushes back.
(iii) The passive careenium: the substrate has no dynamics of its own beyond taking an imprint; its deformations are a function of where the simmballs currently are, and those deformations play no role in where the simmballs go next.

Please address, in order:

1. In which variants is the shoving question well posed, and why does the answer differ? I am especially interested in two edges: in (iii) the simmballs plainly deform the substrate and yet it seems wrong to say the simmball *level* accomplishes anything — what is missing? And in (ii), where there is no coarse-graining at all, does the question collapse into something merely verbal, and if so what does that tell us about why (i) ever looked puzzling?
2. Why coarse-graining cuts both ways, made precise rather than gestured at. Being many-to-one is what lets the simmball level ignore careenium detail that makes no difference to the outcome, which is what makes it worth citing; it is also what makes it look idle, since every careenium event already has a sufficient careenium-level story and the simmball-level event looks like a shadow of it. What is the principle that tells you which level's variable is the one to cite, and does applying it settle the case or only relocate the disagreement?
3. Suppose the careenium-level evolution is computationally intractable while the simmball-level transition rules are cheap to step, so the simmball level is our only usable predictor of the system. Does that confer causal standing on the simmball level, or only predictive indispensability, and what is the difference meant to consist in here?
4. Is any part of this empirical? If the right answer is that the setup conflates three separate claims — the simmball level being causally relevant, being explanatorily autonomous, and being ontologically something over and above the substrate — say which variant or which intervention would discriminate them, and what observation would count for or against each.
