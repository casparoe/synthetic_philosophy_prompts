You are a legal philosopher who specialises in self-reference and self-amendment in rule systems — the nomic end of jurisprudence, the stuff about rules that govern how the rules get changed. Please think carefully, and do a bit of unstructured brainstorming before you start drafting; I'd much rather have something that's been chewed over than a confident first pass. And write it in British English — our committee secretary is extremely picky about that.

Context: I'm on the Rules and Standing Orders Committee of the Oakfield & District Housing Co-operative — roughly 4,000 members, about 30 branches, a delegate AGM every March, plus an all-member postal/online ballot whenever the rules require one. We aren't a court; we're the body that advises the AGM on whether a proposed rule change is in order and what it would actually do.

Where things stand: Rule 62 says any rule may be amended by a two-thirds vote of delegates at an AGM, provided 60 days' notice has gone out to branches — and Rule 62 can itself be amended that way. Rule 21 sets delegate entitlement at one delegate per 200 members per branch, with the branches listed in Schedule 1, which is also amendable by an ordinary two-thirds AGM vote; the membership register that any ballot would be run off is kept under Rule 18, same story again.

Two days ago a working group dumped a draft on us, which they're calling "the Escalator" (proposed Rule 62A):

- any rule may be marked "protected" by a two-thirds AGM vote;
- once protected, a rule can only be amended, or un-protected, by a two-thirds AGM vote *plus* a majority of the whole membership in a ballot — a double lock;
- the level of protection is recorded rule by rule, so a rule protected at the double-lock level can only be released at the double-lock level;
- and 62A(4): Rules 62 and 62A are themselves protected from the moment 62A passes.

We're split three ways and going round in circles.

One lot says 62A(4) is straightforwardly fine: an entrenchment clause has to bite against its own repeal or it isn't an entrenchment clause at all, and every constitution does this. Another lot says there's something fishy about a rule that raises the bar against its own amendment by using the lower bar — it's pulling itself up by its own bootstraps — and that if we want 62A(4) to be real, 62A has to be passed *again* under the double lock it creates, i.e. re-enactment cures the defect. (I'm not even sure re-enactment helps: if the first enactment was invalid, what rule are you re-enacting under? And if it was valid, why bother? Someone on the committee muttered something about the opposite of a ratchet but I didn't catch it.)

Then there's a second problem nobody spotted till last night. The double lock is only as strong as Rules 21, 18 and Schedule 1, because those decide who the delegates are, which branches exist, and who counts as "the whole membership" — and none of them are protected under the draft. So a group with a working AGM majority could redraw branch boundaries or tinker with the register and walk straight through the double lock without ever touching a protected rule. Someone suggested the fix is just to protect 21, 18 and Schedule 1 as well, but that only moves the question: whatever rule says how Schedule 1 gets amended is itself amendable, and so on down.

Timing matters here. We have to circulate our recommendation by 14 February so branches get their 60 days before the 15 March AGM, and there's a live substantive fight behind all this procedure: one faction wants Rule 3 (the "permanently affordable homes" mission rule) protected before the AGM, and another wants AGM authorisation to transfer around 300 homes to a housing association to cover a £14m cladding and remediation bill, which Rule 3 may or may not already block. So whatever we say about 62A effectively decides which of those two gets a clear run, and I'd rather we were honest that we're deciding that.

What I need: a memo I can put in front of the committee at our 7 February meeting, 900–1,200 words, that (a) recommends one concrete course of action with dates attached — pass as drafted, pass with specified amendments, defer past the March AGM, or reject; (b) works through the bootstrap problem and the who-counts-as-an-amender problem in language our branch secretaries can follow; (c) gives the strongest version of whatever argument loses, not a strawman, because half the committee holds it and they'd spot one instantly; and (d) tells us what to do if we simply can't agree on the philosophy — is there a version of the Escalator that's safe on either view of bootstrapping, some two-stage or sunset arrangement, and what would it cost us in time. Please don't pad it with case citations, further reading or anything like that; we can't check them and they'd be no use to us anyway.
