If you move the cricket a couple of inches while she's inside, she comes out, locates it, drags it back to the threshold — and then has to go and inspect the burrow all over again, because moving the cricket reset her program; experimenters have apparently put her through this loop forty times in a row without her ever short-circuiting it. The book then draws the morals: the wasp isn't really thinking or taking stock of anything at all, her behavior is a fixed action pattern, pure machinery dressed up as purpose; the philosopher Daniel Dennett took over the case to introduce the notion of sphexishness, meaning rigidly mechanical behavior that only looks intelligent; and the upshot is that the flexibility is the illusion, not the mechanism — we're the same way whenever we catch ourselves compulsively rechecking our phone — so free will goes out the window with it, since our brains are physical systems running programs too.
