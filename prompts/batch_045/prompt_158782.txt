I'm in a little history-and-philosophy-of-science reading group — half of us are grad students, half are people who wont stop arguing about the replication crisis — and this week we did Lakatos, and we spent maybe forty minutes on one made-up case that nobody could settle. Now I want to turn it into a joke for the end-of-term slides, so help me write it.

The case (it's invented, don't try to look it up): there's a research programme claiming humans have a weak magnetic sense. Every replication fails. Each time, the protective belt gets patched: its the shielding, its the participants' phones, its the field direction, its the time of day, its that the sample was too big, then too small. Then last year the programme's defenders announce a genuinely new auxiliary — call it the shyness postulate: the effect is detection-shy, it only shows up when nobody is deliberately trying to detect it. They preregister a study where participants are told they're being tested on something else entirely, the effect shows up, and they write it up as a progressive problem shift, on the grounds that they predicted a novel fact that hadn't been used in constructing the theory and it was corroborated.

What I want is one joke whose punchline actually turns on the Lakatosian machinery rather than being a generic "scientists move the goalposts" gag — the hard core and the belt, what the positive heuristic is supposed to be for, the difference between a belt fix with independent testable content and a plain ad hoc move, and the awkward fact that on his own telling you only get the verdict in hindsight. It should land for someone who's actually read him and just get a "huh?" from everyone else. My suspicion, which the joke should be built around, is that the shyness postulate is progressive by the letter of the methodology and degenerate in spirit, and that Lakatos has no way of saying those come apart — but if you think I've got that backwards, tell me after the joke instead of quietly fixing it.

Then three or four lines of notes, please: which parts of the joke are bog standard Lakatos that anyone who knows the material would grant, and which parts lean on a contested reading or on your own reconstruction — I'm thinking especially of whether a "novel fact" has to be temporally novel or merely not used in building the theory, and whether he ever gives you a criterion for when a heavily patched belt has quietly become a different programme with a different hard core. And flag clearly if you think the shyness postulate isn't ad hoc by his lights at all, in which case the joke probably doesn't work and I'd rather know that than have you sand the problem down.
