Longtime lurker, finally posting. I keep noticing the same move in threads about hedonism and well-being and I don't have a word for it, or rather the word I have is probably the wrong one.

Here's the shape of it. Someone motivates hedonism by saying that the only thing that can be good for you is something that changes what your life is like from the inside — the intuition behind the experience machine, or behind saying that a wish fulfilled after you're dead can't be good for you. "Pleasure" gets introduced there as the stuff that feels good, felt quality, obvious. Then someone brings up the heterogeneity problem: the pleasure of cold water after a long run, the pleasure of finally seeing why a proof works, and the pleasure of finding out your paper got accepted don't seem to share any one feeling. The cold water has a bodily qualia you can point at; seeing the proof is mostly just absorption in the proof, and if there's a feeling at all it's a relief that shows up afterward; the acceptance email feels like a state directed at a proposition rather than a sensation. The reply I always get is: right, that's why nobody holds a felt-quality view anymore, pleasure is really an attitude — enjoying something, being glad that p, wanting the experience to continue, take your pick. OK. But then two paragraphs later the same person is leaning on the experience intuition again to say desire theories are absurd, and at that point the "wanting it to continue" bit is doing no work at all.

I've been calling it hedonic drift, which my friends point out sounds like a made up economics term. Someone else told me it's just the adverbial theory, but that seems like a view rather than a move. It's also not quite motte-and-bailey: neither version is a safe core that gets retreated to under attack — the felt-quality version does the work at the start and the attitudinal one does it later, and the argument needs both. Plain equivocation feels too thin too, since it doesn't explain why the switch is systematic, with each version reliably assigned to a different job.

Is there an established name for this? And if the nearest thing is a cluster of concepts rather than one, I don't want a label dropped on me — I want to know which of them actually carves the pattern I'm pointing at and where the edges are.

Give me:
- What I'm describing (~100 words): restate the move better than I did
- Nearest existing concepts (~250 words): felt-quality vs. adverbial vs. attitudinal theories, the experience requirement, motte-and-bailey and equivocation — which fits, where each one breaks down
- Is it actually an inconsistency (~200 words): or is there a way for a hedonist to hold both halves without cheating
- Name it (~100 words): if nothing established fits, give me a definition plus one clear case and one near-miss that isn't an instance
