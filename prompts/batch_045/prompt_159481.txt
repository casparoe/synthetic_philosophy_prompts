I've been working through the Sleeping Beauty problem on my own over winter break — a fair coin is flipped, Beauty is woken on Monday, her memory of the awakening is erased, and she is woken again on Tuesday only if the coin landed tails, being asked each time she is awake for her credence that it landed heads — and I've concluded that the famous dispute between halfers and thirders is much ado about nothing: if the question is about the coin, the answer is one-half, and if it is about whether the awakening she is now having is a heads-awakening, the answer is one-third, so the two camps are simply answering different questions and are both right. My friend says the disagreement is genuine but already settled by the betting argument: offer Beauty, at every awakening, a ticket that pays one dollar if the coin landed heads, and the price at which she breaks even in the long run is one-third of a dollar; since credences just are the prices one is willing to pay, thirdism wins and halfism is a confusion about what probability is for. Which of us has the better reading of the situation, and how should I argue for it in the short paper I'm writing on the problem? And if my friend is right, I don't understand why philosophers still treat the case as open rather than as a solved exercise.
