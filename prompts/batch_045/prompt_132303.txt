I'm writing a seminar paper on whether cognitive penetration threatens perception's evidential role. My tutor's complaint about the literature is that the stock cases all lean the same way: the penetrator is an expectation, a stereotype, or a bit of book-knowledge, and the resulting experience is evidentially a bit worse than the un-penetrated one, so penetration gets cast as a liability by default. He has asked me to try to build a case that leans the other way: one where the penetrating state makes the experience more accurate, or more reliably keyed to the distal property, than the un-penetrated experience would have been.

Could you construct one? Constraints:

- An ordinary, realistic setting, in one paragraph: no laboratories, tachistoscopes, or neural interventions.
- Same distal scene and same proximal stimulation as the un-penetrated baseline; the only difference between conditions is what the subject believes.
- The penetrator must be, in my terms, a calibration belief: one that says how to read the current input (how the illumination is coloured, how the surface is oriented, how far away it is) rather than a belief about which object is out there. A belief that merely says there is an X over there would be cheating, since then the experience is only as good as a hint and the case collapses into a prompting case.
- The belief must be true and already well supported by independent evidence the subject has; no lucky guesses.
- The difference must be in the experience rather than only in judgement: say explicitly how you block the three deflationary readings, namely a shift of attention or gaze within the scene, a change in response criterion, and plain post-perceptual inference.

Then run the check: describe the experience in each condition, say why this still counts as penetration on the standard definition, and show why the circularity objection (that the experience merely inherits the justification of the belief that shaped it, and so adds nothing of its own) fails to get a grip here. Finally, be honest about which single feature of the case is doing the work, what happens to the case if that feature is removed, and what someone convinced that any penetration is epistemically corrosive would say to resist it.
