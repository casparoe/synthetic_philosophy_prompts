hey! so I run this philosophy club at my school (we're like eight people and a box of timbits) and every year we write a fake final exam for each other as practice — it's pitched at roughly first-year uni seminar level, 45 minutes a question, out of 25 marks. I'm doing the work-and-leisure one this year and I'm kinda stuck making it actually hard. Everyone in the club has already done the usual three things — Weber's protestant work ethic thesis, the French 35-hour week, and karoshi (the Japanese thing where people literally die from overwork, strokes and heart attacks from insane hours) — and I know that if I just ask "is the work ethic still with us?" everyone will turn in the same essay about hustle culture and burnout, they'll all get a B, and I won't have learned anything about who actually thought about it.

The distinction I want the question to force is the one I keep going in circles about with my friend: what exactly is supposed to be good about work? There seem to be three different answers people smuggle in and they don't fit together. One says work is good because it's effortful and disciplined — but then serious rock climbing or grinding away at learning cello should give you the same dignity, and work-ethic people definately don't act like it does. Another says work is good because you're producing something other people actually need, so the worth comes from being needed rather than from the effort. And the third is basically the old line about anyone who won't work not getting to eat, which makes leisure a reward you have to earn instead of something you're just owed. I want the question to make people pick which one they're defending and then deal with the bill for picking it — like, if it's the "being needed" one, what happens to your free time when a machine does your job better than you and nobody needs you anymore? And I want the karoshi stuff to do more work than "overwork is bad": the bit that breaks my brain is that karoshi is normally described as something done to workers, like a workplace hazard, while the work ethic treats the worker's own willingness as the morally important part — so if someone freely chose the eighty-hour week and was proud of it, is their death a violation of them or just the logical end point of the thing they valued?

So: can you write me one essay question (45 min, 25 marks), a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme? For the scheme I'd like maybe five must-hit points with marks attached, plus a sentence each on how people usually chicken out or mess that point up. Please don't let the model answer sit on the fence and say both sides have a point — I want it to commit to something even if it's a bit controversal, because a fence-sitting answer is exactly what makes these fake exams useless. And no reading lists or page cites or anything like that, we don't have library access anyway lol.
