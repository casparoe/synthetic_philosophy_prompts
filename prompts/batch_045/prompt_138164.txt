I'm one of the commissioners on a truth commission, a few years into the job. Our statute is roughly: amnesty is available if the applicant makes full disclosure and the act had a political objective. We decide case by case and I've realised I have no idea what my actual principle is, so here are my gut verdicts.

- Foot soldier who shot one man on orders and told us everything, including who gave the order: amnesty, obviously.
- The commander who ordered a dozen killings: no amnesty. He gave us more, and more useful, information than anyone else. He also showed no remorse and said he'd do it again. Still no.
- His driver, who drove the truck and claims he didn't know what was in it: amnesty.
- A girl abducted at fifteen, kept with the unit two years, who beat a prisoner to death herself: amnesty, and I also want her name kept out of the published report, even though the statute has no provision for that.
- A man who confessed to killing an informer, except the informer turned up alive in another country. He's confessing to the wrong killing because he won't admit the real one: no amnesty. And I think this is worse than the man who never applies at all.
- A man who burned a woman's house because he'd been told she was informing, and she wasn't: amnesty. A man who burned his rival's house in what was obviously a personal feud and described it as political: no amnesty.
- A village where the only doctor is an applicant and everyone, including the victims' families, petitioned for him: amnesty, but it feels wrong. A wealthy applicant whose family offers to fund reparations if he gets amnesty: definately no, and that one feels worse than the doctor.

Two I can't settle: the clerk who forged the papers that let a squad move around, confessed, is our only witness against a general, and is dying; and whether we're harsher on the state's own people than on the rebels who did the same things (I think we are, and I think that's right, but I can't say why).

Is there one principle behind all this? Truth-maximising can't be it, given the commander. Which verdicts do I have to drop? And what does whatever principle you come up with say about two cases we haven't had: an applicant who gets amnesty and then brags about it on the radio, and one who refuses amnesty and demands to be prosecuted because he's proud of what he did?
