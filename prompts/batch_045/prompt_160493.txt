I am assembling advanced problem sets on distributive ethics for a graduate reading group, and I need one item that forces people to construct a case rather than to rehearse the standard one. Please build the case and then say what it shows.

Use this vocabulary, so that the arithmetic is unambiguous. Prioritarianism ranks distributions by a value function that is strictly increasing in each person's well-being and that gives extra weight to well-being the lower the level at which it occurs. Work with two renderings of that idea:

- Concave-transform: V(u) = Σᵢ f(uᵢ), where f is strictly increasing and strictly concave (choose your own f and say which).
- Rank-weighted: V(u) = Σᵢ wᵢ u₍ᵢ₎, where u₍₁₎ ≤ … ≤ u₍ₙ₎ is the distribution sorted from worst off to best off and w₁ > w₂ > … > 0 (for two people, w = (2, 1) will do).

And two readings of any such view under uncertainty:

- Ex post: apply V to the distribution of well-being in each state of nature, then take the probability-weighted average of those values.
- Ex ante: take each person's expected well-being across the states, then apply V once to that distribution of expectations.

The task. Give me a numerically explicit example — two people, two equally likely states, two policies, the same people and no one's existence at stake either way — satisfying all of the following at once:

1. Under policy R, both people have strictly higher expected well-being than under policy S.
2. The person who is, in expectation, the worse off of the two is the same person under both policies, and it is that person whose expectations are reduced by choosing S.
3. In neither state does either policy make both people better off than the other does, so the case cannot be dismissed as one policy being worse in a state where everyone loses.
4. Ex post prioritarianism strictly prefers S, and it does so under both renderings above — that is, the reversal is not an artefact of the fact that a concave transformation is sensitive to the units in which well-being is measured.
5. Ex ante prioritarianism strictly prefers R, under both renderings.

Show the arithmetic to three decimal places for every step, including the ex ante and ex post totals for each rendering, so that I can check the reversal by hand. If you think the five constraints cannot be met jointly, say which one has to go and why, and give the closest example you can.

Then the conceptual part, in under 700 words of prose (the tables do not count):

- State precisely what has been levelled down here: persons' outcomes, persons' prospects, the spread of the distribution, or something else. Explain why the levelling-down objection in its original, deterministic form cannot get a grip on either rendering — both are strictly increasing in each person's well-being, so no outcome in which someone is worse off and no one better off can ever be ranked higher — and then say whether what happens under uncertainty is the same objection in a new setting or a different objection that merely wears the same label.
- Say whether the rank-weighted rendering should even count as a version of prioritarianism rather than as a form of egalitarianism, given that it is not separable across persons, and say what turns on that classification for how damaging your example is.
- Take a position on which reading, ex ante or ex post, is the correct one for a view whose rationale is that it matters more to benefit people the worse off they are, and give the one reason you find strongest.

Do not cite or name any author; I want the argument, not the literature. Finish by telling me how confident you are in the arithmetic, in the claim that this is a genuine analogue of levelling down rather than a separate objection, and in your verdict on the ex ante versus ex post dispute, and by singling out the one claim in your answer that you are least sure of.
