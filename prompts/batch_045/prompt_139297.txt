I run a small discussion group, and two of its members have agreed to stop arguing past each other and produce an adversarial-collaboration document. One is a compatibilist of roughly Dennett's stripe: moral responsibility is real and survives determinism, but desert is not a primitive moral fact that punishment answers to, and punishment is justified by what it does going forward. The other is a Caruso-style free-will skeptic defending the public health–quarantine model: criminal sanctions are justified only by prevention, with the redress of unjust social conditions as a foundational constraint, and blame-based desert talk is to be abandoned rather than reformed. I've been asked to draft the document.

I want it in three parts. First, a numbered list of claims both can sign: about the social determinants of criminal behavior, about what determinism and the absence of ultimacy do and do not settle, about what any system of sanctions has to accomplish, and about which existing practices both already oppose. Nothing vacuous, and nothing either side would balk at. Second, a disagreements table: each row one point of disagreement stated in neutral terms, followed by each side's own preferred formulation of its own view, worded so that side would sign it. Third, a cruxes section: for each crux, a concrete case, policy, or piece of evidence both sides accept as bearing on it, plus each side's prediction recorded in advance, plus what each would count as real evidence against their own view rather than only against the other's.

Two cruxes I expect: whether the quarantine model, once the constraints it needs are added, is practically distinguishable from deterrence plus incapacitation, or whether the live difference is only in the expressive register; and whether blame language does coordinative or signaling work that risk language cannot do, or whether that work is replaceable. Add cruxes of your own, and say which ones you think are empirical rather than conceptual.

As one test case, please use this hypothetical: a jurisdiction adopts the skeptic's model, with sentencing by a risk board using actuarial scores and no blame language in court or statute, and five years on average time deprived of liberty for low-level property offenders has risen, because detention now tracks predicted risk rather than the seriousness of the act, while violent recidivism is flat. Write down what each side would have predicted and what each would say the result shows. Finally, if you think part of their dispute is verbal rather than substantive, say which part and why.
