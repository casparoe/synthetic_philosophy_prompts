I write about science and ideas for a living, and a few weeks back I was at a materials conference in Delft where I ended up wedged between two sources of mine — Ines, a philosopher of science who works on dispositions, and Raj, a coatings engineer — arguing over a demo pane: a sheet of ordinary soda-lime glass bonded to a shear-thickening polymer interlayer, the sort of thing that goes rigid within microseconds of an impact. There were two panes on the table. The fresh one survives a hammer blow that would shatter untreated glass, and it also survives a *harder* blow, because the interlayer stiffens more as the impact gets sharper; but it shatters if you hit it very, very hard. The second pane has been sitting in a UV chamber for six months, its interlayer has degraded, and it shatters under the first moderate blow. Raj opened by saying the fresh pane simply isn't fragile: fragility is a disposition to break when struck, the conditional "if struck with that force it would break" is false of it, and that's the whole story. Ines said that's just the standard mask move — glass packed in foam peanuts is still fragile — except that here the mask is intrinsic and triggered by the very stimulus it protects against, so the real question is whether an intrinsic, self-triggering mask still counts as a mask; she said yes, the brittle glass network is still there intact and something else is interfering with the manifestation. Raj came back that once you allow intrinsic masks you can redescribe anything: nobody thinks steel is fragile, but you can call its plastic deformation an intrinsic mask on brittle fracture, and then every solid object is secretly fragile and the distinction you wanted has evaporated — and he added that the coated pane is indistinguishable from a merely tough object across every test you could run, so there may be no fact of the matter here at all.

Ines replied that a mask has to leave the causal basis in place while blocking the manifestation, and steel doesn't have a brittle basis for anything to interfere with, so her view isn't vacuous; but she conceded something, which is that the interlayer is *itself* a disposition, triggered by the same event as the one it blocks, so what we have isn't disposition-plus-mask but two dispositions racing on different timescales, and the honest verdict on the fresh pane is "less fragile" rather than a binary yes or no waiting to be discovered. Raj then waved at the non-monotonic curve — a harder blow is safer than a softer one, up to a point — and said neither his simple conditional nor her "more or less fragile" scale can represent that, so her timescale story is as broken as his opener; and he said the UV pane shows where the real action is, because what the fresh pane has is a second-order disposition, a disposition to *acquire* fragility, and none of the standard machinery handles those cleanly. Ines said he'd moved the goalposts, that time-indexing dispositions is machinery rather than insight, and then the panel started and I lost the thread.
