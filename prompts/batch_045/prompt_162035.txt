For a first-year political theory tutorial I am writing a diagnostic — a decision tree that starts from one shared intuition case and lands each student on a named conception of liberty without ever using the words "positive," "negative," or "republican" in the questions themselves.

Constraints:

1. Six to eight branch nodes. Each node must be phrased as a question about a concrete case that a student with no training could answer; no node may ask for a label, a definition, or a verdict about "freedom" in the abstract, and no node may be answerable by recalling which theory it leads to.

2. The nodes must be cut from the logical space rather than from the list of positions: whether unfreedom requires actual interference or only a standing power to interfere; whether it requires an agent whose will is in question, or whether a structural condition (poverty, illiteracy, a washed-out road) can leave someone unfree; whether it matters that the interfering will tracks the agent's own interests rather than being arbitrary; whether it matters that the agent endorses the constraint, or that the desire blocked is one the agent disidentifies with; whether a power of interference the agent never encounters and never knows about counts.

3. The case set must include: a gate that is locked but that nobody ever tries to open, whose key another person holds; a superior who is reliably kind and legally required to consult you; an addiction; a law you voted for and still endorse; a washed-out road.

4. Locate the resulting answer-combinations in that space: give each occupied cell its conventional label (negative liberty, republican non-domination, positive liberty as self-mastery, and the "freedom as non-dependence regardless of arbitrariness" cell), and mark the unoccupied cells, saying briefly whether each is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

5. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot actually separate, and name the extra case that would separate them.
