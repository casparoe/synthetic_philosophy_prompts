You are helping me prepare the next episode of a long-form interview series on formal epistemology. The audience is philosophy graduate students and machine-learning researchers who are comfortable with basic Bayesian machinery — conditioning, Bayes factors, KL divergence — but not with the specialist literature on uncertain evidence. The episode has no shownotes, so every question must be self-contained: nothing should ask the guest to name papers, attribute results to particular authors, or recommend reading, and nothing should turn on a quotation or a scholarly detail that the audience (or the guest, on the spot) would have to look up.

My guest is Dr. Lena Ardeshir, whose recent book-length defense of Jeffrey conditionalization rests on two claims. First: uncertain evidence should not be modeled as a shift in the probability of some proposition in the agent's language, but as a likelihood function — equivalently, a likelihood ratio — over an unarticulated observation variable; on that picture the notorious rigidity condition is not a contingent empirical assumption about experience but the requirement that the observation variable screen off the hypothesis from whatever produced the shift. Second: once evidence is modeled this way, the familiar objections — order-dependence across non-commuting partitions, the impossibility of lifting a probability out of zero, the absence of any convergence result for agents who undergo the same experience — all turn on conflating the observation variable with a proposition the agent can state. Her habitual deflection, which I want to become progressively harder for her to deploy, is: if that bothers you, you are still thinking of evidence as a proposition.

Draft eight interview questions, ordered as a single arc rather than as a list, covering at least one each of the following:

1. The normative status of rigidity: is it a requirement of rationality or a substantive factual assumption, and what independent grip do we have on the screening claim when the alleged screen-off variable is something like an experience?
2. Partition-dependence and order-dependence, using a concrete case of your construction in which the same numerical shift in the probability of A is compatible with two different partitions that yield different posteriors for a hypothesis cutting across both.
3. A self-undermining or metacognitive update: an agent who becomes, say, thirty percent confident that her own perceptual channel has just been degraded, and then has an experience through that very channel, so that the invariance rigidity demands is exactly what is in question.
4. Zero-probability and unformulated evidence: hypotheses the agent has never articulated, and whether rationality is then being assessed on a rational reconstruction rather than on the agent.
5. Interpersonal matters: two agents who share an experience and agree on the observation variable but differ in their priors — can their posteriors move further apart, and is any agreement-style or convergence result recoverable?
6. The familiar equivalence between Jeffrey updating and the revision that minimizes KL divergence from the prior subject to the constraint on the partition: is that a vindication of the rule or a redescription of it, and does the same style of argument secretly favor rival minimum-information or maximum-entropy rules?
7. Whether the epistemic-utility and diachronic-coherence arguments that support ordinary conditionalization transfer to her rule, or support something weaker.

For each question give me: the line as I should actually say it aloud, under about fifty words and free of formalism that would not survive being spoken; two to four sentences of the answer you expect from her, in her own idiom; and a follow-up that closes off the most likely escape route. At least three questions should be built so that her usual move of enlarging the algebra and conditioning on an added variable is not sufficient by itself, because what is disputed is precisely what constrains the new variable's likelihoods. At least one question should force a numeric or explicitly comparative verdict rather than a gesture at a formula. No question should be answerable by restating the formal definition. Close with a final question about what observation would lead her to abandon the framework, and about a case in which she thinks her own rule should not be applied at all.
