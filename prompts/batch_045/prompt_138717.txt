I'm a retired chemist who came to philosophy late, and the reading group I go to has been going round in circles for two years on one question: when does a legend become history? We keep coming back to the same three cases — the Trojan War, King Arthur, the Exodus — and nobody has moved an inch. It doesn't feel like a factual dispute: we all grant the same things, that there's a burnt destruction layer at the site people identify with Troy, that the Egyptian record contains nothing everyone will accept as a report of slaves leaving, that Arthur first turns up in texts written centuries after he supposedly lived, and that arguments from place-names and language can push a tradition back but never attach a date to an event. My hypothesis is that we are asking three different questions under one name — did the event occur, did the person exist, is the story a garbled memory of something real — and each of us answers whichever one we care about and assumes the others are answering it too. I also want to smuggle in something from my old field: treat oral transmission as a noisy channel, so that after enough generations without writing, "no independent attestation" stops counting as evidence against and just becomes what you'd predict either way. So: is that the right diagnosis, is the channel-noise move legitimate or a category mistake, and what would actually count as progress here — a distinction, some new kind of evidence, or dropping the question?
