I'm retired now — thirty-odd years in low-temperature NMR, and for the last couple of years I've been going to a philosophy reading group, which is where I picked up Maxwell's demon. I thought I'd understood the thing from Bennett's version: the demon measures for free and pays when it wipes its memory, kT ln 2 a bit. Then I started fiddling with it and now I'm not sure the story says what I thought it said, so I want a number and the reasoning behind it.

Here's where I got stuck. Build the demon so it never wipes anything. Give it a one-bit memory, and every cycle a fresh bit arrives on a tape in a maximally mixed state — nobody in the machine ever measured or prepared it, it just shows up random. The demon does one reversible operation to correlate itself with which side of the box the molecule is on, uses that to pull work out of the single heat bath, and the bit trundles off downstream, correlated with the gas now but still maximally mixed on its own. No erasure anywhere in the device; the tape comes in random and goes out random.

But: the joint state of (random bit, gas) already has maximal entropy, and a unitary can't lower joint entropy, so no reversible coupling can build mutual information out of a maximally mixed memory. It looks like the thing the demon actually consumes is not "information" but lowness of entropy in its memory medium — an entropy sink that has to be replenished from somewhere — and erasure is just one way of paying that bill, not the bill itself.

So the proposition I want a number on: there is a fact of the matter about where the demon pays, and it's on the memory side (resetting or replenishing a low-entropy medium), with no further irreducible thermodynamic price attaching to the act of acquiring the information. I'm around 0.6. A man in my group is at 0.9 and says its obvious.
