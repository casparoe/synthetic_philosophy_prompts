For a reading group I got talked into I've been going through Hofstadter's *Le Ton beau de Marot: In Praise of the Music of Language* (1997), the book where he produces dozens of English versions of one short Renaissance French poem by Clément Marot (he calls it "Ma mignonne") and argues, roughly, that a translation which carries over the content but drops the constraints — rhyme, metre, the particular sound-pattern, the wordplay — hasn't translated the poem at all; that you often have to change the content in order to keep the form; and that what's essential about the thing lives in the pattern rather than in the message. He also extends "translation" to translation between frames generally.

What I want is a restatement of that position in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can manage, and then a residue report. Concretely:

I'm fairly sure this already sneaks in the thing he denies, but I can't put my finger on where.
