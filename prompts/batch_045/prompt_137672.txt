hi — I'm putting together a discussion session on semantic externalism for a small reading group (mixed level: a couple of people have read Putnam, most havent). I need a case, built to order rather than one of the greatest hits.

What I want: a scenario where externalism — the "meanings ain't in the head" package — is forced to say that a word in some competent speaker's mouth changes what it refers to over time, while nothing at all changes inside her head. She isn't corrected, she doesnt learn anything new, her dispositions with the word stay put, and there's no point at which she'd say "oh, I must have been using it differently before." As far as she's concerned she means exactly what she's always meant.

Constraints, because the obvious versions won't do the job:

- No other planets, no duplicate earths, no imaginary chemistry, no envatted brains or deceivers. The two candidate referents have to be real-world things a real chemist / geologist / botanist could tell apart and a normal person using the word couldnt — think look-alike species, two minerals sold under the same name, a supply chain quietly swapping one thing for another.
- She must not be ignorant or confused in the way Burge's arthritis patient is. She's a perfectly competent user of the word by her communitys standards; I don't want the work done by her having a partial grasp that the experts quietly complete.
- The shift must not be an official or community reclassification — no committee redefining a term, nobody announces anything. If the only reason the reference moved is that somebody with authority decided to use the word differently, the case fails, since then it's stipulation and not externalism doing the work.
- It shouldn't collapse into plain homonymy either: everyone involved, including her, is treating this as one word for one kind of stuff throughout.

One more thing, which is the actual reason I'm asking. Externalists usually have two mechanisms pointing at the same thing, so you cant tell which one is load bearing: the causal-historical story (your use is hooked up to the samples you're actually in contact with, and the hidden nature of that stuff fixes the reference) and the deference story (you defer to the experts or to whoever taught you, so the reference is whatever their practice picks out). I want a case where those come apart — where, for this speaker at this time, they'd pick out different stuff — and I want you to say which one externalism should let win and why.

Deliverable: the scenario in a single paragraph, ordinary setting, no stage machinery. Then show the check: what the word refers to before and after, the content of one specific belief of hers before and after, what she'd sincerely say about her own meaning, and where each of the two mechanisms lands. Then: which single feature of the case is doing the work; what happens to the verdict if you remove it (specifically, what if the change were announced to her instead of happening quietly); and what someone who thinks meanings are in the head would say about the whole setup.
