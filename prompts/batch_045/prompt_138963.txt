im hoping you can do better at this than my last attempt, which came out as a rhymed lecture with the word posterior in it. context: i spent about three hours last night at the dumpling place arguing with my friend Sam — she's in a stats PhD program — about the Judy Benjamin problem. soldier parachutes into a jungle, the territory is split into Blue (ours) and Red (theirs), each half is split into a Headquarters Company area and a Second Company area, and she starts out with a uniform prior across the four quadrants. then her radio squawks and the only thing she gets is: if you are in Blue territory, the odds are three to one that you are in Headquarters Company. so the thing she learns is a constraint on a conditional probability, not a proposition, and you cant just condition on it. Sam's view, which she defended louder as the night went on, was that the message is totally silent about whether she is in Blue at all — it only tells her what to think *inside* Blue — so the right posterior keeps P(Blue) at exactly one half and just tilts the two Blue cells to 3:1 against each other, leaving both Red cells parked at 0.25. I said no: the minimum-relative-information update (minxent, minimum cross entropy, whatever your department calls it) says that to satisfy that constraint with the smallest possible disturbance to the uniform prior you have to let a little probability mass cross the Blue/Red line, so P(Blue) slips to something like 0.47 and the two Red cells move up off 0.25 while staying equal to each other. her answer satisfies the constraint too, so we werent disagreeing about the 3:1 — we were disagreeing about which of the two admissible posteriors is the least disturbing one. at one point i accused her of quietly conditioning on the material conditional, which would be much worse, since that just deletes the Blue-and-Second cell and drives the ratio to 1 instead of 3:1; she accused me of counting the message twice; and we left it there.

the actual ask: i'm TAing a formal epistemology course and i want to close the last section on updating with a poem rather than a summary slide, something a student could reread on the bus and get the shape of the disagreement from. i've been thinking a pantoum, because of how it works — rhymed quatrains, abab, five of them (so about 20 lines), where lines 2 and 4 of each stanza come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next, and the very last line of the poem is the first line again. that recycling is the point: i want the first line to be the radio message itself, and each time it comes back it should be heard differently — first as reassurance, then as a constraint, then as the thing that quietly drags her confidence in being in Blue at all down below a half while saying nothing whatsoever about the Red half. the final return of that line should be the same message arriving a second time, which by then carries no new information at all because the constraint is already satisfied, and i want that to land as something like being abandoned rather than being comforted.
