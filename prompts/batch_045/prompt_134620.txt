Ive been getting really sucked into arguments about whether LLMs understand anything or are just stochastic parrots, and it got weirdly personal recently cuz my roomate works in AI safety and wouldnt stop saying that all the model does is predict the next word from patterns, no real thoughts behind it. That made me drag out my old copy of Hobbes's Leviathan (1651), chapter 4, "Of Speech", because I remembered he talks about names being marks and signs. Heres the bit I mean:

"So that the first use of names, is to serve for Markes, or Notes of remembrance. Another is, when many use the same words, to signifie (by their connexion and order,) one to another, what they conceive, or think of each matter; and also what they desire, feare, or have any other passion for. And for this use they are called Signes."

Can you help me get straight what Hobbes is doing here? Im specifically wondering about this niche angle: Hobbes says the first use is private "Markes" to remember your own train of thoughts, and the second is public "Signes" to show others what you conceive. Suppose we say an LLM's training gives it something like the public "connexion and order" of words (so it masters Signes), but there's a big question whether it has any continuing private "Mentall Discourse" or "Trayne of our Thoughts" at all—like, its context window resets and it doesnt remember past inferences as its own. On Hobbes's account, could a system that was all Signes and no Markes (or all Markes and no Signes) still understand language? A bit later in the chapter he says that understanding is nothing but conception caused by speech—words making you have a conception—so does that require that the words actually cause a conception in a mind that also has its own train of thoughts? I guess I'm confused about what counts as a conception for him: is it any mental effect from words, or does it have to be built out of sensory experience like the rest of his empiricism suggests? Please explain how the Markes/Signes distinction should make us think differently about the stochastic-parrots debate, instead of just repeating the usual slogans.
