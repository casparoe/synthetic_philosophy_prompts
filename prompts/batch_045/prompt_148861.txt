Im recording an episode next week with a philosopher who's just finished a book on Harsanyi's aggregation theorem, arguing that it basically vindicates utilitarianism — her slogan is 'grant Pareto and expected-utility rationality for the social ranking and the weighted-sum form falls out for free.' I want 6–8 questions that dont let her just restate the theorem, each with one follow-up written against the answer she's most likely to give. Press her on: (a) since each person's vNM utility function is only pinned down up to positive affine transformation, the weights can just be absorbed into however you rescale each person's function, so is 'equal weights' any constraint at all — and if it isnt, does she have to fall back on the equiprobability / veil-of-ignorance version to get anything egalitarian, and is that fallback circular? (b) a fix I've seen people reach for is normalizing everyone's function so that their own worst-to-best span runs 0 to 1 before adding — but doesnt that quietly build in the claim that everyone's span is the same size, i.e. a pretty strong interpersonal comparison? (c) her argument needs the ex ante version of Pareto rather than the ex post one, so what does she say to someone who thinks that's the wrong Pareto? (d) as usually stated the result assumes a fixed set of people, so what happens when the lotteries can change who exists. And one last thing I keep going back and forth on: I used to think the weighted-sum form was neutral between utilitarianism and prioritarianism since the weights are arbitrary — but a fixed weight attached to a person isn't the same as extra weight for being worse off, and squashing someone's function to get priority looks like it breaks the Pareto condition. Turn that into a question pitched so she can't just answer 'yes, the theorem is neutral between the two.'
