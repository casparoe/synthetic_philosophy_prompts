I work as an ML engineer on an interpretability team, mostly on comparing representations across models, and I have no philosophy background at all. A friend in a philosophy program got me reading about Quine's gavagai case after we spent an evening arguing about whether two of our models that agree on every input we can think of could nonetheless "mean different things" — we got nowhere, and I've been picking at it since. The vocabulary I'm actually fluent in: training distribution and support, model class and capacity, loss and optimization, identifiability versus mere existence of a fit, underspecification, functional (input–output) equivalence versus weights-space symmetry, gauge freedoms like hidden-unit permutation, latent alignment via stitching or optimal transport, distribution shift, downstream fine-tuning benchmarks, inductive bias as what breaks ties when the data doesn't, and the no-free-lunch point that nothing is determined by data alone without a prior. I also want to flag one bit of lab jargon: when two of our models are behaviorally indistinguishable on everything we test, we break the tie with a family of held-out downstream tasks we call an anchoring suite, and I've started to suspect that habit is doing more philosophical work than I realized.

What I'd like is a faithful restatement of the case for the indeterminacy of translation in that vocabulary, and I care most about it being honest rather than flattering to me. Please keep separate three things that I keep collapsing: (a) the boring-sounding claim that a finite record of what speakers assent to under what stimulation leaves many manuals compatible with the data, so you need something like a prior to pick one; (b) the more interesting claim about the object-word — that where I hear rabbit you can consistently posit undetached rabbit parts, or rabbit stages, or the recurring rabbit-kind, and then compensate by adjusting the apparatus for individuation and counting so that all the same sentences keep the same truth conditions; and (c) the strong claim that no possible evidence settles it and that there is therefore no fact of the matter about which manual is right. My own gloss is that (a) is just plain old underspecification, (b) is something like reparametrizing the latent and then compensating in the readout so the function is preserved, and (c) is where I lose the thread. Tell me where that gloss is tracking Quine and where it's quietly changing the subject.

Then I want the residue report, and the residue is the part I'm paying for. Three things in particular. First: in every setting I work in there is an external target — labels, or a data-generating distribution, or at minimum an anchoring suite — and reference to it is fixed from outside the model; in radical translation there seems to be no such thing, only speakers' assent and dissent to whole sentences under stimulation, which looks to me like a sparse, noisy approval signal rather than a label set. What plays the role of supervision here, and is the analogue of "the true labeling function" simply absent, or is it present but degenerate? Second: my vocabulary has no way to state there is no fact of the matter as distinct from no data we could ever get would distinguish them, and I'd like your view on whether that is a genuine expressive limitation of an engineering framework that always has a downstream task somewhere offstage, or whether it's a sign that the strong claim is less substantial than it sounds. Third, and most important to me: is the rabbit versus rabbit-stage difference of the same kind as the permutation symmetry of my hidden units — a difference in internal bookkeeping with no functional consequence, which on my instincts is just notational — or is it a difference in the function itself? And if I try to settle it by inventing an anchoring suite for the linguist, does the case for indeterminacy say the suite will fail, and why: because the data can't come, or because the compensation trick in (b) will absorb whatever comes? If my framework needs something added to even express the strong claim, say what, and say whether you think adding it would be a real gain or a way of talking myself into a problem.
