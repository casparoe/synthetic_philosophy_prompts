I'm a journalist covering AI, and basically every source I talk to reaches for the same move sooner or later. The skeptics: it's just a Chinese room with extra steps, nobody's home, stop using the word agent. The enthusiasts: Searle's own system reply concedes that the room as a whole understands Chinese, so there is something there, and at this scale we should start treating these systems as agents. I'm writing a piece on whether it makes any sense to attribute agency to a deployed assistant, meaning the thing I actually talk to in a chat window, and I want the analogy audited before I lean on it in either direction.

So first, the mapping. What plays Searle's man, the rulebook, the symbols, the story he memorizes, the people outside slipping questions under the door? What plays the system in the system reply? And where does the assistant character I'm conversing with sit: is it the man, the room, the symbols coming back out, or something the setup has no slot for at all?

Then the disanalogies, and which ones actually do work for the agency question versus which are just decoration. The ones I can think of: Searle's rulebook is hand-written by programmers and semantically inert, whereas an LLM's weights are compressed out of billions of pages written by people who were describing a world, which I take it means semantics has to get in somewhere, but maybe via the corpus rather than via the weights, and I don't see where the room would put that. The man in the room doesn't learn; these things adapt within a single conversation but not across conversations. The room has one fixed task and a fixed rulebook, while the deployed assistant has been through a second phase of training where the rulebook got nudged according to how helpful or convincing its outputs looked to human raters. And whatever I'm talking to is a fresh instance every time with a context window that fills up and then dies, so even granting that the system understands, the thing I'm talking to doesn't persist past the conversation.

I also want to know what the analogy is quietly importing. My guesses: a homunculus inside who has genuine understanding of some other language, so there's a clean place to park semantics; a sharp syntax/semantics distinction; a single unified system with clean boundaries, when here the boundary between weights, fine-tuning, the system prompt, the context window and whatever tools are bolted on outside is totally negotiable; and the assumption that understanding is the property that matters for agency, rather than goal-directedness or memory or having anything at stake.

One more thing a source said to me that I can't tell is deep or just relabeling: under the simulator framing, the model isn't the assistant, the assistant is a character the model simulates, and the Chinese room has no room for a simulated character that's distinct from the rule-follower. Is that a real disanalogy or just a new vocabulary for the same picture?

Can the enthusiasts get from the system understands to it's an agent? And if the Chinese room is the wrong frame for the agency question specifically, what's a better one?
