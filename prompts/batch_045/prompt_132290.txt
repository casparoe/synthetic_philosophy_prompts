Retired meteorologist here, drifting into philosophy of science now I've got the time. I've built an argument against something Popper says and I'm fairly confident it proves too much, but I can't find the bad step.

(1) A big part of Popper's case against laws of history is what he called the Oedipus effect: a forecast enters the system it forecasts, and how it enters isn't itself forecastable, so the forecast has no stable target and large-scale historical prediction is hopeless.
(2) None of that is specific to human affairs. A climate projection is a forecast about a system that contains us, and we react to it — build sea walls, price carbon, or decide it's hopeless and drive more — and which of those we do is exactly the unpredictable bit in (1).
(3) Epidemic models and even the seasonal forecasts farmers act on have the same shape.
(4) So if (1) is sound we should junk climate projection and epidemic modelling too. But those are testable, have been tested, and are good enough to steer policy by.
(5) So (1) can't be what sinks historicism.

Where does it go wrong? My own guess is that the exit is about how much of the system the feedback can actually reach: radiative forcing and the carbon cycle don't care what we believe, whereas the solvency of a bank or the mood of an electorate is partly constituted by what people believe, so the feedback is bounded and gets handled by running scenarios instead of issuing one prophecy. But I can't turn that into a principled line rather than a difference of degree that Popper could just swallow.

If I have to choose, I'd rather keep (4) than (1). But I also want to know wether any version of the argument survives — is there a formulation that still rules out grand stories about the direction history must take, without touching climate models? And please tell me which step you'd actually reject and how confident you are, not a general list of what's uncertain. If I've misread Popper (I gather he later backed off the claim that the effect marks a clean line between the social and the natural sciences), or if (4) is smuggling something about how projections handle feedback, say so plainly rather than answering politely around it.
