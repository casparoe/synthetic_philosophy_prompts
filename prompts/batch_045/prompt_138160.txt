im in a decision theory seminar this term (phil dept, but half the room is CS people) and this week was updateless decision theory. What I keep going in circles on is the basic move: instead of deciding what to do given what you now know, UDT says to pick the output of your whole decision procedure from behind the prior — before you knew which situation you would be in — and then just execute it later, even once youve updated and every reason you can actually see points the other way. Our last session is a show-and-tell: everyone brings something short, the TA said nothing over a page and nothing that's just a summary of the reading, so I want to bring a villanelle.

What I want the form to do is the arguing. The two refrains should be the two standpoints: one is the voice from behind the prior, the one that answered before it knew anything; the other is the voice of the agent after updating, standing in front of the actual closed box or the actual empty road, saying that nothing she does now changes what's already sitting there. They take turns across the stanzas and only end up next to each other in the final quatrain, and that meeting shouldn't resolve anything — I want the poem to show the two of them answering different questions and never quite making contact with each other, not to show one of them winning. Each stanza should be a different one of the cases we've done — Newcomb, the hitchhiker where the driver only picks you up if she predicts you'll pay and you're already in town when payday comes, counterfactual mugging, whatever order works best — so that the two refrain lines sounding exactly the same while the situation keeps getting worse is what carries the argument. Someone in the seminar should be able to tell which stanza is which case without being told, and tell why the refrain doesn't budge.

The last stanza is the one I actually care about, and it's a case we didn't do, so here it is: your decision algorithm is also running right now, in a stranger. Different person, different life, no causal contact with you and never will be any. She's in a much worse version of one of these situations, and her payoff is enormous if her copy of the algorithm says cooperate and yours does too, since the correlation UDT cares about is between the algorithms rather than between the people. So choosing from behind the prior is choosing for both of you at once, and you end up eating a real, visible cost for the sake of somebody you'll never meet and who isn't you in any ordinary sense. I want the refrains to sit down together there. I honestly cant tell whether that makes the poem a defence of the theory or a reductio of it, and I don't want it to decide — but it does have to get the theory right, because half the room will be checking, and I'd rather it be recognizable as UDT than merely pretty.
