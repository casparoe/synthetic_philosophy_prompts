Odd request but here goes. I'm originally from Coventry, been in this town in the States for twenty-odd years, and I help run a tiny volunteer group that supports new migrants. The county asked me to give the five-minute welcome at the naturalisation ceremony at the town hall next month — about forty people taking the oath, and in the front row there'll be a federal judge and a bloke from the county historical society who apparently knows a huge amount about the abolitionists round here.

I've no academic background; I just started reading Douglass on my own a year ago and I definately can't get past his argument that the Constitution was an antislavery document — that it was the slaveholders' reading of it, not the text, that made it proslavery, and that the framers left the word out on purpose. I want that idea to actually hold the speech up, not just get name-checked. The trouble is that everyone at these things says something like 'the founders got a great deal wrong and we've been slowly fixing it ever since', and if Douglass is right that line just hands the argument to the people he was fighting. But I also don't want to stand in front of forty people, several of whom fled countries where the constitution was pure decoration, and tell them this one was always fine. Can you write it? Something that lands for people who've never heard of Douglass, doesn't make the judge or the history bloke wince, and doesn't pretend the tension isn't there.

One thing I keep going back and forth on, and I'd like you to take a side: to me Douglass sounds like he's giving a purposive reading of the document while insisting he's only giving the plain meaning of the words, and the word-not-being-there point is carrying more weight than it can really bear. If you think I've got him wrong, say so and write it the other way.

British English please. And at the end a short note on where the speech leans on a contested reading of Douglass or takes liberties with him, and what argument or bit of evidence would make you change how you'd written it.
