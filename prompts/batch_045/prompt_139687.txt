I've been in a reading group on the norms of assertion for three years — the same six people, meeting every other week — and in that time nobody has changed their mind even once. I want a diagnosis of why, not another argument for one side.

The three positions, as my group has them: a knowledge norm (you may assert that p only if you know that p); a truth norm (only if p is true); and a reasonable-belief norm (only if you reasonably believe that p, usually with sincerity and no-defeaters clauses bolted on). Each gets attacked from two directions. The truth norm looks too demanding when a doctor asserts a diagnosis on the best evidence available and the diagnosis happens to be false — nobody in the room thinks she did anything wrong — and too permissive when a guesser asserts a true proposition on no evidence at all. The knowledge norm looks too demanding in lottery- and preface-type situations, including the variant where you know each of a thousand claims individually but do not know their conjunction and so, apparently, may not assert the conjunction while happily asserting each conjunct; and it looks wrong in the cases where a speaker asserts truly against her own evidence, or asserts something she believes but could not be said to know, in a context where asserting is plainly the right thing to do. The belief norm looks too permissive when the speaker's belief comes from a source she had every reason to distrust, or from evidence she negligently failed to gather, and too demanding wherever confident testimony outruns what anyone could reasonably be said to have justified belief in.

What makes me suspect the dispute is not what it advertises itself to be is the repair pattern. Every side responds to its problem cases by adding a clause — no undefeated defeaters, in a position to know, able to defend it if challenged, not in a context where standards are raised — and once two or three such clauses are in place, I can no longer construct a case in which the amended norms disagree. It begins to look as though the disagreement is either verbal, or else entirely about which formulation best explains a set of verdicts everyone already shares, with explains doing unargued work.

I have three hypotheses and I don't trust any of them. First, that the sides are testing their norms against different kinds of evidence: the awkwardness of sentences of the form p, but I don't know that p; patterns of retraction, apology and blame; and the function assertion is supposed to serve in the transmission of information. These need not agree, and each side may simply be weighting the one it finds tractable. Second, that they disagree about what a norm of assertion is even for — whether it must be a rule a speaker can consult while speaking, a standard for assessing blameworthiness, a constitutive rule of a speech-act type, or a specification of what a practice of testimony is good for — so that they are answering different questions under one name. Third, that there is a hidden requirement about guidance doing the work: knowledge is arguably not a state one can aim at directly, and the sides may be split on whether that disqualifies a knowledge norm.

The only thing that reliably moves my group is audience variation. An intern asserting a true, well-supported claim to the engineer who has already run the check; a parent telling a child something the parent only half-remembers; a witness on the stand, where hedging is not permitted. Intuitions swing hard with the hearer's epistemic position, and it is unclear to me whether this shows that the literature has been hunting for an audience-neutral standard that does not exist — in which case the question as posed is ill-posed — or whether it is simply a parameter every side has held fixed, in which case the debate is sound but incomplete.

Please give me a diagnosis with reasons: which of my hypotheses is right, what is wrong with each of the ones that is not, and what each position actually commits its holder to that makes the impasse stable. Then tell me what would count as progress given that diagnosis — a distinction, a reframing of the question, a formal or empirical result — or argue that no progress should be expected and say why. I am not asking for a survey of the positions, and I don't want an answer that turns on who published what first or on where an argument appeared. Please write in American English.
