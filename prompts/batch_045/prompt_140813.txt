I cover science and medicine for a national newspaper, and since last year I have served as one of two public-interest members of the Clinical Classification Committee — the body that decides which conditions appear on the Schedule of Recognized Conditions, the list that gates publicly funded treatment, monitoring, and workplace accommodation.

At our next sitting we must rule on a petition to add a condition nobody has asked us to classify before. It exists only because of a new diagnostic technology, homeostatic reserve profiling, which measures how well an organ system holds up under standardized physiological stress. Resting function in these people is entirely normal and they feel perfectly well; roughly one adult in seven nonetheless scores in the bottom quartile, and that quartile goes on to develop overt organ failure at about three times the rate of everyone else, typically a decade later. A monitoring drug exists, made by the company that funded the profiling studies and brought the petition.

Our terms of reference require both a "departure from normal physiological functioning" and that the condition "warrant medical attention on the person's own behalf." The committee is split, and the split is not really about the evidence but about whether disease is a biological fact about the body or a judgment about what is bad for a person. Our clinicians say the first clause is plainly met and the second is a policy call we should make generously. Two members say the first clause cannot be met at all: the cut-point is drawn on a smooth continuum and marks the bottom quartile of whoever happens to be tested, so a person can stop having the condition if the population around her becomes less fit, and can acquire it without changing in any way.

Draft the two-page written opinion I intend to table and circulate beforehand. It should recommend a specific disposition — add the condition to the Schedule, refuse, or something in between such as a time-limited, monitoring-only category — with the timeline and any review conditions spelled out. It should also give the strongest version of the losing option its due, since I have to vote for this in the room and then defend it afterwards in print.
