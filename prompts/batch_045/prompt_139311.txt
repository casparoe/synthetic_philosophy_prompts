The situation as I've been sketching it, including a few things we've been arguing about:

- Capture-side provenance actually works and is mandatory: cameras sign what they record, there's a chain of custody, any clip can be checked against it. Call this signed capture. Assume it can't be forged.
- Generation is open and cheap: anyone can make a convincing clip of any event, and the part that worries me most is that they can also make convincing *archival* footage — plausible material of the past, right grain, right format, right incidental detail — so faking the present and retroactively fabricating the record are two different problems.
- The generators and the detectors are the same technology, and detection gets worse over time, so "we'll just check" isn't a stable answer.
- Politically on the table: (1) mandatory labels on synthetic media; (2) an inverted default, where anything without a chain of custody is legally presumed fabricated; (3) a single official public register of authenticated media — my colleague calls it a register of the real; (4) liability on whoever ships the model.
- The wrench I keep raising: in a lot of the cases that scare us, there is no author. Nobody wanted this particular falsehood. The clip exists because a model was trained on a corpus and someone typed a prompt, or because a fine-tune was released and forgotten. There's no liar with an aim, and nobody knows it's false.

What I want from you:

Her distinction between factual truth and rational truth, and the claim that factual truth is peculiarly fragile because it's contingent: the fact could have been otherwise, so denying it is never absurd the way denying arithmetic is.
