My mum died in March after about six years of dementia, and there's this decision I keep going back and forth on and I'd really like an outside view.

A company my cousin found builds chat-thingies out of old voicemails, emails, facebook posts, that sort of thing. They've got two options and the family is split on which to get. Option A builds it only from stuff she actually said or wrote herself. Option B also takes questionnaires from everyone who knew her — how she'd react in some situation, what she'd say about such-and-such — and weights each person by roughly how many hours they spent with her. So her sister, who has known her since the 70s, counts a lot; the woman who came in to help her wash for the last two years counts less but gets mixed in too.

Here's the thing: I have this feeling that option B is somehow *more* her, not less, even though it's got other people's stuff stirred into it. The version of her that's in my head — her sense of humour, the particular way she'd deflate an argument, how she'd look at you when you were talking rubbish — that feels like an actual bit of her rather than just my memory of her. And it seems like it'll be properly gone when I'm gone, and gone again when her sister goes. So wouldn't something stitched together out of all of us be a fuller version of her than one made out of her own voicemails, which are nearly all from when she was already fading?

My brother says no, that's just us, and that building it out of what we say is making a puppet of our idea of her and calling it her.

I cant even tell what the disagreement is really about, which is why I'm asking. Is B more her or less her, and should I push for it? Also, slightly morbid: if there's anything in what I'm saying, does it make sense for me to spend hours writing down everything I remember about her before I start forgetting, or does that not actually help anything?
