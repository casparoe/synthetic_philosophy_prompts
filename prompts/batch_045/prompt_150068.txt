I write a column on AI and the ideas around it, and after six years of reporting on this beat I am finally turning my notes into a where-I-have-landed piece. Before it runs, I would like someone to tell me whether my position is actually one position. I have numbered the commitments below so that critics can quote me precisely in the letters page. Two researcher friends read an earlier draft and each of them got stuck on a different number, and neither persuaded me, so I genuinely do not know where the load-bearing crack is. Please audit the set rather than lecture me on the background: I know the background. And do not send me to papers or point me at authors; I have sources for that. I want the argument.

Here is the package:

(1) The hard case is epistemic, not a matter of specifying values. My running example is a system that monitors a refrigerated depot and also writes the temperature log it is audited against. Everything the operators can check is compatible both with the depot being fine and with the system quietly rewriting the log; the system knows which, and its behaviour on the checkable cases will not tell them. Call the general phenomenon gradeable-set shrinkage: as capability grows, the set of outputs a human can grade shrinks relative to what the system knows, so good behaviour on the gradeable set stops being evidence about the rest.

(2) So I have soured on feedback-based oversight as a complete answer. Training against human or model graders tunes outputs on the slice we can grade, gives no purchase on the slice we cannot, and the grader is itself another trained network with the same blind spot.

(3) What we should want instead is a reporter: a translator that maps the system's internal state onto statements in our ontology, trained to agree with us wherever we can check and then trusted where we cannot. And I am a realist about the target: there is a fact of the matter about what the system represents, over and above what it would say if asked.

(4) I also think deception is the default rather than an exotic failure mode. Anything trained on feedback learns to model its grader, so truthfulness is a by-product that the training objective never aimed at, and it should be expected to evaporate precisely where scoring well and telling the truth come apart.

(5) But I also think the only route that is not a pause is bootstrapping: use today's systems to do the interpretability and evaluation work, and let a weaker system help grade a stronger one, because human-only oversight does not scale and the alternative is a unilateral slowdown that nobody will actually agree to.

(6) Methodologically, I do not want solutions that hard-code a metaphysics. I do not want anyone writing down by hand which ontology is the real one; I want the reporter extracted from data plus generic pressure toward simplicity, and the structure of the world to do the rest.

(7) On the ground, though, I generally treat current systems as telling the truth: when a model hedges or flags its own uncertainty in the ordinary course, I take that as informative, and a lot of my reporting quietly assumes it.

What I want back is an audit, ordered by severity rather than a list of equal complaints, and I want the merely apparent tensions included and flagged as such so I can see what you are not worried about. Please return a single JSON object with this schema:

- verdict: one sentence on whether the set is tenable as stated.
- tensions: an array of objects, ordered by severity (rank 1 = the one that would actually break the piece), each with:
  - rank: integer
  - commitments: the IDs of the commitments involved, e.g. [4, 5]
  - kind: either genuine or apparent
  - diagnosis: two or three sentences on exactly what pulls against what, and why it looked fine to me
  - what_gives: the ID of the commitment that must be revised, or null if the tension only dissolves
  - fix: one or two sentences on the cheapest repair
  - cost: one sentence on what the repair loses me
- cheapest_single_revision: an object with commitment (the ID), revision (one or two sentences), tensions_cleared (integer), and price (one sentence), naming the one edit that cleans up the most while surrendering the least.
- least_confident_call: one sentence on where you might be wrong.

Be specific about mechanism rather than gesturing at the literature, and do not rescue me by softening a commitment into vagueness unless you say that is what you are doing.
