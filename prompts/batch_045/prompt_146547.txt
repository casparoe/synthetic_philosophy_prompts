Teaching a grad seminar on conditionals next term, about 10 people, and I want the main handout to be a decision tree rather than a survey of views. Everyone starts from the same two commitments: I want the probability of "if A then C" to equal P(C|A), since that's what makes conditional bets look sane, and I don't want conditionals to be trivial. Lewis's triviality results say I can't keep both, so the tree should go downward from there: every branch node is one assumption that some version of the argument needs (the equation; that conditionals have truth conditions at all, or at least are the sort of thing that can carry probabilities; that they can be nested and iterated; that the equation is read synchronically vs. as a claim about what you'd believe after learning A; that P(C|A) is even defined when you're certain A is false; whatever non-triviality assumption is doing the real work), and every leaf a named position, or "unnamed" if as far as you know nobody defends it.

Some constraints, because I've ran this one before and the boring version just sorts everyone into Adams, Stalnaker and Lewis in three boxes:

- Phrase most nodes as concrete cases rather than as claims about the formalism. I want at least two betting setups: a three-card or two-urn case for the plain equation, and a nested one ("if the coin landed heads, then if I bet I win"), since the argument needs iterations and students intuitions about the inner conditional go all over the place.
- Give a real node to the probability-zero antecedent case (someone always asks: I've already looked at the card, so what is P(C|A) even supposed to be?). It should route somewhere, not get a footnote.
- Two positions I want placed rather than assumed. (1) "Double bookkeeping": keep a selection-function / similarity semantics so conditionals have truth conditions, but treat the equation purely as a norm on what's acceptable to suppose, and deny the two ever have to agree. (2) A view where the equation constrains only the update from P to P_A, where P_A comes from an imaging-style revision (each world's probability moves to its nearest A-world) rather than conditionalization, so nothing synchronic is constrained and triviality never gets off the ground. For each, say whether it earns its own leaf or collapses into an existing one, and if it collapses say into which and why.
- Leaves I want present regardless: the no-truth-conditions / acceptability view; the truth-conditional view that gives up the equation and explains its pull pragmatically; a view that keeps the equation only for a restricted class of conditionals; and the bite-the-bullet trivialist cell even if it's empty.
- Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't actually separate, and mark the empty cells of the logical space with one line each on why they're empty (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored).

Don't cite anything or point me at papers, I'll fill in the reading list myself, I just want the structure.
