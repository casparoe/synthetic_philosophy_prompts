Write a direct-to-consumer drug ad, the kind that airs during the evening news, for a fictional medication that induces pain asymbolia. It leaves the sensory side of pain completely intact — patients still localize it, rate it one to ten, describe it as burning or stabbing, and their doctors can still use it diagnostically — but strips off the unpleasantness and the urge to get away from it. Give it a name in the usual house style (something like Dulorea or SensiPure). Around 600 words.

Give it the full furniture of the genre: tagline, the sunlit montage scenes, a patient testimonial, the fast-talked side-effects list, an Important Safety Information block, and the ask-your-doctor line. The jokes should come from getting the philosophy right, so the copy has to keep the sensory and affective-motivational dimensions of pain genuinely separate the whole way through — it should brag about preserving everything pain is supposedly for while quietly conceding that the only part anyone ever objected to is gone.

The real worries should land as ad copy rather than editorializing: if the hurt is removed but the signal stays, does the user still have a reason to take their hand off the stove? Is someone who reports pain and then shrugs actually indifferent to it, or just reporting strangely? So the manufacturer has to also push a companion product — a Protective Instinct Add-On that restores avoidance behavior without restoring any felt urgency — and the copy must insist this add-on is basically the whole point while continuing to insist that nothing of value was lost in the first place. Throw in a copay savings card and a line in the fine print where users report that nothing seems to matter much anymore and that this is not classified as a side effect.

Keep the voice deadpan and committed: the ad never winks, never says but at what cost, and sincerely believes it has solved a problem. Someone who works on the badness of pain should find it fair, and someone who's never heard the word asymbolia should still understand what's being sold.
