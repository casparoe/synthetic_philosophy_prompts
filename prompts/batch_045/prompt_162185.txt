I'm a backend dev who got dragged into a metaethics reading group (long story, my roommate is a phil grad student) and we're doing evolutionary debunking arguments next week. I want to show up with jokes. Not generic nerd jokes — I want the punchlines to actually turn on the machinery: the bit where the debunker needs more than "selection shaped these beliefs," they need the beliefs to be insensitive to the moral facts, or pushed in some particular direction away from them; the third-factor reply where the realist says of course evolution got us to track the facts, being nice pays; and the companion-in-guilt move where debunking moral beliefs seems to drag our beliefs about logic and arithmetic down with it.

The extra wrinkle I keep pushing in the group, which nobody has answered: our moral psychology was selected to steer behaviour, not to measure value, the way cruise control was selected to hold a speed and not to know what speed it's holding. So the real question isn't whether evolution touched our moral beliefs, it's whether it pushed them off in a direction — whether the distortion has a sign. Call it the drift problem if you want, I don't care about the name.

I don't care if the jokes are pro- or anti-realist, but at least one should land for someone who thinks the argument succeeds and at least one for someone who thinks it fails. Give me exactly five, numbered, one or two sentences each. No intro, no sign-off, no footnotes explaining why they're funny.
