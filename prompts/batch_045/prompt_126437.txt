I design fault-tolerant flight control laws, so the vocabulary I actually think in is: plant and controller, reference signal and tracking error, disturbances and disturbance rejection, actuator authority and saturation, gain and phase margins, local versus global controllability, observability, and nested loops in which an outer loop hands setpoints down to an inner one.

Translate the core of the free will dispute into that vocabulary, thesis by thesis. Start with the alternative-possibilities condition: the compatibilist's conditional reading of "she could have done otherwise" (roughly, that she would have acted differently had her relevant states differed) against the incompatibilist reading, on which the ability must survive holding the actual past and the laws fixed. Then translate the source condition: the demand that an agent be the ultimate origin of her action rather than a well-behaved relay of causes traceable to what she did not make.

Two features of my framework should be treated as fixed points of the translation, not smoothed over. First, ability in control engineering is not binary: it is a margin, defined at an operating point, given as the size of the disturbance set the loop can absorb while still meeting spec. Second, my vocabulary has no room for a controller that supplies its own reference — setpoints arrive from an operator or an outer loop, and "who sets the top-level reference?" is a design question outside the theory rather than one it can answer.

So I want the residue report as well as the translation. For each thesis: what mapped cleanly, what survived only after distortion, and what has no expression in the vocabulary at all. Where the translation had to import an assumption my framework does not itself supply — e.g. treating an agent's reasons as setpoints — say so explicitly rather than presenting it as translation. Then give me your honest verdict on the two residues: does grading ability into a margin dissolve the binary question or merely relocate it, and is the regress about the top-level reference a blind spot of control theory or evidence that the ultimate-sourcehood demand was never coherent? Finally, my framework does have a working notion of responsibility — identifying which unit failed, for repair or replacement. Say whether that maps onto anything in the moral responsibility debate, or whether the similarity is only verbal.
