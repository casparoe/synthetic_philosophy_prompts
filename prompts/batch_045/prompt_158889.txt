I write a column on science and ideas, and I am finishing a piece on whether the strings a large language model emits are about anything. In an interview, a philosopher of mind framed the issue with an analogy I keep turning over: derived intentionality, she said, is like the moon's light. The moon genuinely shines, but only by reflecting the sun; it has no light of its own. On this picture, artifacts — thermostats, the coin-sorting mechanism in a vending machine that Dennett calls a two-bitser, and presumably language models — glow with borrowed aboutness, while minds, and only minds, are sources. She used the analogy to license a strong conclusion for my readers: because a model's output is lit entirely by us, whatever a model means is always shorthand for what some interpreter takes it to mean, and talk of machine understanding is at best a façon de parler.

The complication is that the same tradition supplies a counterexample to the picture rather than an illustration of it. The point of the two-bitser is meant to be that its aboutness is borrowed from its designers, and then to ask what makes our own case any less borrowed — especially if the design history of a neural mechanism is written by natural selection, which intends nothing, or by gradient descent on a task, which stipulates nothing about what internal states are supposed to indicate. Before I decide how much of this survives into print, I would like the analogy audited rather than admired.

Three cases I want run through it. First, a device whose discriminations were shaped entirely by an optimizing process, with no engineer, no intending designer, and no stipulation of what its states were for: is there a sun here, or a longer chain of mirrors? Second, a system trained to predict its own earlier representations, where borrower and lender coincide. Third, a closed loop in which models generate text and other models are the only things that interpret it, with no human reader anywhere in the chain — moons lighting moons, and no sun at all. Does the metaphor entail that this loop is dark, and is that the answer I should give my readers?

Please give me the correspondence table explicitly: what plays the role of the sun, the moon, the light, reflection, attenuation, the night side. Then separate the disanalogies that matter for the conclusion from the ones that do not. I am especially worried about three: that light is a conserved quantity passed causally from one body to another while aboutness is not the sort of thing that gets transferred; that reflected light is still light, which may quietly grant that derived intentionality is real intentionality and thus undercut the merely-as-if reading; and that building a source into the picture may simply assume the regress stops somewhere, rather than showing where. Finally: does the conclusion about machine understanding survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying it, and is there a better one — something from norms, currency, or rule-governed practice — that does the work without smuggling in a source?
