I spent thirty years writing dichotomous keys for freshwater-invertebrate field guides, and now that I'm retired I run a small philosophy reading group at the library — me, a few other ex-science types, and one retired librarian. We've been going round in circles on whether the switch to farming was a mistake, and I'm fairly sure it's because we're all answering different questions. So I want a key, not an essay.

What I'd like: a branching key of seven or eight questions, plain indented text is fine (no need for ASCII diagrams), that sorts somebody into one of the positions on this — the strong verdict that agriculture was the worst mistake in human history, the affluent-forager picture, the line that it was forced by climate or population pressure so nobody was at fault, the line that it was bad for the individuals who lived through it but good for the species or for everything that came after, the line that the whole comparison is illegitimate, and whatever else you think belongs on there. Two rules: no question may name a position or an author, and every question has to be answerable cold, by someone who's never read any of this — either a claim to agree or disagree with, or a small concrete case.

Two cases I definatly want used. One: the undo button — a village around 6000 BCE gets to vote on forgetting grain and going back to full foraging, knowing what we now know, but with the population it actually has by then. Two: the ratchet — every generation's move to slightly more farming is locally sensible and slightly raises how many people the land supports, so the option of going back shrinks a little each time, until foraging isn't on the table any more. I also don't think "farming" is one thing, so I want a node that splits the package: cereal calories, domesticated animals, and storable surplus that can be taxed. Which bit is doing the damage, and is the answer different for each?

Build it from the logical space first — the questions and the answer-combinations they allow — and only then drop the named positions onto the leaves, each with one awkward commitment it's stuck with. Tell me which cells come out empty and wether that's because they're incoherent, just unattractive, or merely undeffended, and be straight about any two leaves your key can't really separate. Eight to ten leaves, questions in the order a newcomer would want to meet them.
