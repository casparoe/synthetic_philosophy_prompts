Construct a thought experiment for an undergraduate unit on free will: one paragraph, realistic (a workplace, a clinic, a family kitchen — no laboratories, no implants, no exotic technology), containing two agents doing comparable things under comparable pressure. The case has to engineer a split verdict. A flexibility account of freedom — the view on which the opposite of freedom is sphexishness, the mechanically rigid repetition of a fixed routine while the circumstances change around it — must count the agent who looks rigid as the unfree one. An identification or self-government account — freedom as acting from a standing policy the agent has made her own and can still revisit — must count that same agent as the free one, and the superficially adaptable agent as the mechanical one.

The inversion has to be generated by the theories, not written into the story. So the rigid agent's uniformity may not come from compulsion, ignorance, weakness of will, or someone else's programming; the flexible agent's flexibility may not be mere noise or randomness; and the two agents may not differ in how much they consciously deliberate at the moment of acting. What separates them should be where the adaptiveness is located: in one agent it is spread across many locally apt trigger-response links, none of which the agent can survey or revise as such; in the other it is concentrated in a policy that covers many situations and that the agent can call into question.

Then run the checks. For each of three diagnostics — the mid-course perturbation test (alter the stimulus partway through the action), the new-reason test (supply a strong reason to stop), and the repeat test (run the same situation ten times over) — state what each agent does and what each of the two accounts concludes. Name the single structural feature doing the work, and say what happens to both verdicts if that feature is removed: in particular, if the rigid agent's policy is one she has never revisited and would not revisit under any of the three tests, does she collapse into sphexishness even on her own account, and is that a defect in the case or a result?

Finally, give the strongest reply available to the flexibility theorist. I expect it to be a re-description of the behaviour at a different grain or timescale, so that her test recovers the intuitive verdict; tell me whether that move is principled or ad hoc, and what constraint on grain-selection, if any, would block it without presupposing the answer. The point of designing the case this way is to force the flexibility theorist to specify the grain and timescale at which flexibility is supposed to matter, and to see whether that can be fixed non-question-beggingly. Keep the scenario itself to one paragraph and the whole response under about 700 words.
