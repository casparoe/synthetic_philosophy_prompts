im teaching the first essay of Nietzsches Genealogy next week and want to close the session with a poem rather than a summary, so the students see the thing happen instead of being told about it.

Write a pantoum of 5-6 quatrains — the form where lines 2 and 4 of a stanza reappear as lines 1 and 3 of the next, with the last line of the poem closing back onto the first. The constraint that actually matters: the two recurring lines should carry one short blunt declarative sentence about who the good are, worded identically every time, and it has to mean something different on each return purely because of the lines its now sitting between. I want the form to perform the revaluation rather than narrate it.

Content: first half in the knightly/aristocratic mode — good as self-praise of the powerful, happy, beautiful, with bad (schlecht: plain, common, low) as a thin afterthought nobody really hates. Then the turn, and the second half in the priestly mode, where the concept of evil is what comes first and good is derived afterward as whatever is not-evil, out of ressentiment. Get that direction right: someone who knows essay I should be able to point at each recurrence of the line and say which value-mode it belongs to and why. Stay inside essay I — no eternal recurrence, no overman, no later Nietzsche. And ressentiment should come off as inventive rather than just sulky; dont let the poem cheer for either side.

Then three or four sentences of prose after: where the volta lands, a gloss of what each recurrence of the refrain means in its context, and where the form forced you to fudge something.
