I want the specification before I commit to any account of genetic information: what would a theory of what makes a stretch of DNA — or any molecular structure — information-bearing have to get right in order to be more than a suggestive metaphor? Write me the desiderata: the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must handle, the distinctions it must not collapse, with the consideration behind each, marking which requirements you take to be non-negotiable and which are only preferences of one camp.

The cases I most want covered:

- The baseline: a nucleotide sequence whose relation to the amino-acid sequence it helps produce looks chemically arbitrary, and is in fact read differently in some organelles and lineages, as against the relation between a polypeptide and the shape it folds into, which looks chemically forced. A theory should either explain why the first invites information talk and the second does not, or explain the contrast away.
- Context-sensitivity: one stretch of sequence yielding different products in different cells or at different times, via splicing, editing, shifted reading frames or regulatory state, so that content cannot simply be a property of the sequence alone.
- Heredity without sequence: methylation and chromatin marks, cytoplasmic and structural templates passed from parent to offspring. The theory must either admit these as information or exclude them on a stated principle.
- The reaction norm: one genotype, many phenotypes across environments; one phenotype, many genotypes. Whatever codes for means here, it had better not mean specifies uniquely.
- A lineage I'll call L, in which the codon-to-amino-acid mapping is not fixed by the genome: the adaptor molecules that set the mapping are handed down as finished molecules from parent to offspring, and the lineage can shift to a different mapping within a few generations with no change in the DNA sequence of its genes. Does L's DNA carry the same information under a different code, or different information? Is the inherited mapping itself information — information about how to read other information — and if so does the account face a regress, given that reading it seems to require machinery whose own specification already presupposes some mapping?
- An environmental cue that is chemically arbitrary with respect to the developmental switch it triggers but has been reliably present throughout the lineage's history. If arbitrariness plus reliability is what does the work, this cue qualifies: say whether the theory accepts that, and if not, what else it demands — a source with the right history, a consumer whose proper function is to interpret, or something further.

Also: state what the theory is for (grounding molecular biology's explanatory practice, or settling a metaphysical question about content) and whether the list changes with that purpose. Then tell me which pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, so that the list is itself a result, and which single requirement excludes the largest family of views. Close with a brief scorecard — a short paragraph each — for the main families: the literalist genetic-code view, deflationary causal or parity views, teleological-selection-based views, and developmental-systems views. No bibliography or citations, please, and write in British English.
