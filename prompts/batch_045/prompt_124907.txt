Got into a three hour argument with my friend last night and I'm still annoyed about it, mostly because I think I gave away more than I meant to. It started over a news story about a company in our city that contracts adults to act as someone's registered kin — they show up for hospital visits, they're the name on your next of kin forms, they sit with you through end of life stuff. Everyone involved consents, it's disclosed on a public register, nobody inherits anything, no money moves at the death, no fraud, no deception of anyone who has a stake. There is genuinely no one who is harmed by it. It still makes my skin crawl and I said the council should be able to shut it down. My friend, who is usually the more small-l liberal of the two of us, said fine, but then you are just doing Devlin, and the rest of the night went downhill from there.

So I wrote down where I actually stand and I want it checked before I send it to him. Here it is:

1. Criminal law needs harm to others, or a risk of it. Majority disapproval is not by itself a reason to ban something. (My friend kept saying harm is itself a moralised concept so my principle is just moralism with extra steps. I think that's a debating point and that there's a real difference, even if the border is fuzzy.)
2. Disgust is never by itself a good reason to criminalise. That's the actual core of what's wrong with Devlin, and I think the old laws against gay sex were basically that mistake, and that the Wolfenden and Hart line on that was right.
3. But offense to others in public is different, and I'm fine with public indecency laws even in the empty-park-at-3am case where there is nobody around to be offended.
4. The law teaching people what's acceptable is part of what the criminal law is for. It has an expressive job, not just a deterrent one.
5. I reject the idea that a society gets to enforce its constitutive morality as such. And anyway I dont think there is one shared morality in a society as pluralistic as ours for it to enforce.
6. Consensual adult incest should stay criminal even when there's no coercion, no grooming and no chance of children.
7. The council should be able to shut down the kin-for-hire company.
8. No paternalism: people have to be free to wreck themselves. I'm for decriminalising drugs, and I think consent should be a defence to causing someone injury in the consensual dangerous sex cases, the way it isn't in England.

Some of these obviously rub against each other — I can see 6 and 8 doing it, and 2 and 3 — but I genuinely can't tell which one I have to give up, or whether one of them is doing more work in my overall picture than I realise. I'm an engineer by the way and my friend did a philosophy degree, so assume I'm missing vocabulary rather than being subtle.

Go through the eight and tell me which pairs are really in tension and which only look like it, ordered by how bad the tension actually is, what the cheapest fix would be in each case and what that fix costs me elsewhere, and then which single revision would clean up the most. Then tell me straight whether my friend is right that I've ended up a legal moralist without noticing. Please write it as continuous prose — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just an email you'd actually send.
