I host a small philosophy podcast, and next month I'm recording with a set theorist turned philosopher who defends what she calls frame-indexed cardinality: the claim that 'uncountable' never gets a fixed extension, because the quantifiers in a set-theoretic sentence carry a hidden domain parameter — a 'frame' — that shifts with context roughly the way 'nearby' does. So on her reading Cantor's theorem only ever says that within any one frame there's no bijection in that frame between ω and its power set, and Skolem's paradox is just what you get when you forget that a countable model's own 'uncountable' set and the countability of the model as a whole can't be seen from a single frame at once. She insists this is a semantic thesis about our cardinality talk, not a metaphysical thesis about the sets, and that nothing in ordinary mathematical practice has to change. Draft me eight or nine questions that force her to say where the relativity definately lives and what it costs her, each with a follow-up written against the answer she's most likely to give. The things I want pressed: (1) whether her restriction to well-founded frames is doing hidden work, since well-foundedness isn't first-order definable, so she looks like she's leaning on exactly the sort of absolute notion her view can't ground — she'll probably say she only needs well-founded frames as a constraint on the semantics, so push her on what's supposed to single those out; (2) where the cut falls between number theory and cardinal arithmetic, given that ω is absolute across transitive frames, so that 'there are infinitely many twin primes' stays perfectly determinate while 'how many reals are there' doesn't — press her on whether arithmetic's being special is a principled stopping point or just where the absoluteness happens to run out; (3) the move to second-order ZFC, since full second-order semantics pins the hierarchy down up to isomorphism at each inaccessible rank, which seems to leave only relativity about height, and if she answers that quantifying over 'all subsets' is itself frame-relative, ask her what becomes of her claim that the view is semantic rather than metaphysical; (4) self-application, i.e. when she says every set is countable from some frame, which frame that claim is in, and whether her own semantics isn't again subject to a countable model so that the outside standpoint never actually arrives; (5) whether the continuum hypothesis turns into a pseudo-question for her, and if she says no, what she thinks the search for new axioms is even trying to do; and (6) what observation could count against frame-indexed cardinality at all, given that it's built to leave first-order practice untouched. No warm-up question where she just explains Skolem's paradox for the listeners, and nothing about how she got into set theory. Write it out like an email, continous prose only — number the questions inline if that's easier to read, but no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables.
