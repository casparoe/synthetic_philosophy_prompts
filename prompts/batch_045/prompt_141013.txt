So I'm retired now — I used to do NMR of lipid membranes, nothing glamorous — and in the last year or two I've started reading philosophy of mind, mostly because I got into Schrödinger's later essays and couldn't believe what I found there. He more or less endorses the old Vedanta idea that consciousness is singular: the many minds we seem to encounter are appearance rather than the real story, and the real story is one. My gut says this is where a first-rate physicist walks off the end of the pier, but I can't say exactly why, so I want to turn it into a bet instead of an argument.

Here's the case I keep poking at. I made it up, so far as I know it's not in the literature, which is sort of the point:

Two awake adult volunteers get a full-bandwidth bidirectional cortical link — think of it as an artificial corpus callosum with absurd throughput. After a few weeks of calibration something settles. There is one stream of behaviour and report where there used to be two: either one finishes the other's sentences, neither can reliably say whose memory a given memory is, a pinprick to one body is described as something *I* feel rather than something located over there, and if you ask how many of you there are in there, the answer comes back "one," calmly, with no sign of confusion or of two voices taking turns. Turn the link off and you get two people again, and both of them remember the merged stretch from the inside, the way you remember something you did.

Two questions, and I want numbers, not "it depends what you think consciousness is" — or rather, tell me what you're conditioning on, then give me the conditional number anyway.

First: during the merged stretch, how many subjects of experience were there? Give me a single number for "exactly one," and decompose it for me — something like: how much rides on whether consciousness tracks functional or informational integration rather than, say, the biological details of the substrate; how much on whether the number of subjects is a sharp matter of fact at all rather than something partly conventional or vague; how much on whether those first-person reports are worth anything as evidence about subject-count rather than just about what the merged system says; whether a view like integrated-information-style or global-workspace-style individuation would even give the same answer; and whatever else you think has to be in there. One line of justification each, then the combination.

Second: separately, does the case move your credence in the Schrödinger-flavoured claim — that there is at bottom one consciousness appearing as many — and by how much? I'm around 0.1 on that claim and an old colleague of mine, who's been doing this reading with me, is around 0.55. Where do you think the real disagreement between us lives, since we've both read the same books?

And then the bit I actually care about most, because it's where my own training nags at me: suppose a successor to quantum mechanics turned up — call it the recoherence-window theory — in which large, warm, wet neural states don't decohere anywhere near as fast as we currently think, and two brains coupled the way I described can stay genuinely phase-coherent for long enough to matter. Does that move any of your numbers? I want to know whether the quantum machinery is doing real work in Schrödinger's monism or whether it's decoration hung on a claim that would stand or fall on grounds that have nothing to do with physics. Say which of your sub-probabilities it would touch, in which direction, and by how much.

Finally: which term in your decomposition are you least confident about, and what single piece of evidence — empirical or conceptual — would shift your headline number the most? I havent read much of the secondary literature on any of this, so please don't send me to it; I just want to see its joints.
