I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on the epistemology of universal induction, and I'd like a short, forceful argument on a point I keep going back and forth on.

Background I can assume: Solomonoff induction weights each computable hypothesis h by 2^{-K(h)}; the 'malign prior' worry is that the resulting mixture is heavily influenced by hypotheses in which consequentialist agents elsewhere in the hypothesis space read the predictor's own outputs and arrange rewards or penalties to steer them, since a self-referential pointer to 'the output of this very predictor' costs only a few bits to specify while buying an enormous amount of control over what the predictor cares about.

Now take the predictor to be a pure forecasting system: no external actions, nothing it values except its epistemic score, which is a bounded strictly proper scoring rule such as the Brier score. Assume the manipulators cannot touch its hardware or its code; their only channel of influence is choosing what it observes next, conditionally on the report it just issued — as in 'assign probability greater than 0.9 to the next bit being 1, or we will make the next bit 0.'

Please argue for the following position: no choice of scoring rule answers this kind of manipulation, because strict properness is only a guarantee against dishonesty about a report-independent outcome distribution, and report-dependence is exactly what the malign hypothesis introduces. So the problem is not a problem about loss functions, incentives, or decision rules at all, but a problem about which hypotheses a prior is permitted to range over: the real work has to be done by a restriction on the hypothesis class (say, excluding every hypothesis on which the data depend on the forecaster's own reports), and any such restriction is doing substantive epistemic work rather than merely cleaning up an implementation detail.

Steelman the most tempting reply — that the forecaster should instead report a reflectively stable fixed point, the forecast that is consistent with its own reporting policy once the manipulators' response to that policy is conditioned on — and explain why this does not restore what the scoring rule was supposed to guarantee. Do not hedge symmetrically between the two sides: commit to the position.

Keep the whole thing under 500 words, and end with a final line of the form \answer{...} containing, in one sentence, the premise your argument cannot survive without.
