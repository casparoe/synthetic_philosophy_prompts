For a reading group I'm in I have to write up my "position on free will" on one page so the others can poke holes in it before I present it, so I'd like a consistency check while there's still time to change things. Someone already told me I'm helping myself to things that don't fit together but wouldn't say which, so here it is roughly:

1. I'm a compatibilist. Determinism isn't a threat to free will or to moral responsibility.

2. I don't think responsibility needs the ability to do otherwise. I'm persuaded by the Frankfurt-style cases: if the counterfactual intervener never actually has to step in, the fact that he would have stepped in can't be what makes the difference.

3. What matters, I think, is the actual sequence: an action is free when it comes from a mechanism that's appropriately reasons-responsive — one that would have issued in a different action if there had been sufficiently good reason to.

4. I think the manipulation arguments are the strongest thing against my side, and I do want to say the agent in the zygote-style case isn't responsible. You know the one: another agent designs you from conception so that you'll perform one particular action decades later for the reasons she built in, and you grow up perfectly reasons-responsive and otherwise completely normal, and you do it. I want to say: not his action, not his responsibility.

5. Same verdict for a variant I've started calling the "late installation" case — same design, same reasons-responsiveness, but the motives are put in by a neurosurgeon last Tuesday rather than at conception. If anything I find that one clearer than 4, not murkier.

6. I'm not a libertarian. Agent-causation strikes me as mysterious, and I think sticking indeterminism into the decision process would make the action less up to me rather than more — more luck, not more freedom.

7. I think that if determinism is true then facts about the world before I was born, together with the laws, entail everything I'll ever do, and a predictor with full information could have written down my whole life in advance. That's just what determinism says, so I don't see the problem, but people keep telling me it's a problem for 1.

8. I also think the reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, holding someone to account — are what responsibility is really about, and that all the machinery in 3 is just a story about who it's appropriate to have those attitudes towards.

9. And I think the only free will worth wanting is the kind I've already got if 1–3 are right: something that makes deliberation non-pointless and makes me a fit target of those attitudes. I don't want "ultimate sourcehood" — the demand that I be the ultimate origin of my own motives — because no one could meet it and I think wanting it is a mistake; its fine with me that it bottoms out in stuff I didn't choose.

So: which pairs are actually in tension, and which only look like it? Please rank the real tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, tell me for each what would have to give and roughly what it costs me, and then say which single revision cleans up the most. The bit I actually care about: is there any version of 3 that survives 4 and 5, or does whatever makes the designed agent unfree make me unfree too? And does 9 undercut 4 and 5 rather than supporting them?

Please answer in British English and try to keep it readable for someone who isn't a specialist.
