Rewatched The Dark Knight last weekend and got nerd-sniped by the ferry scene. As I remember it: two boats, one crammed with prisoners and one with ordinary citizens, each given a detonator wired to the other boat, and the Joker announces that if nobody acts by midnight he blows both of them up. Everyone calls it a prisoner's dilemma, but a while back I read about Hofstadter's superrationality — roughly, that two agents who are similar enough and who know they are running the same reasoning should decide as if they were one agent, and so both refrain — and it doesnt seem to fit, for two reasons. First, mutual restraint doesnt save anybody here, since the Joker detonates both boats anyway, so the lockstep answer applied honestly is death for everyone. Second, I half remember a related puzzle where the lockstep argument breaks down once the reward depends on how many others refrain, and doubt about whether the others are reasoning the same way cascades until everyone defects; the ferry setup feels closer to that, especially since the two boatloads arent even similar to each other.

Can you read the scene through that lens, and also say plainly where the reading breaks? Because what actually happens on screen is neither restraint nor coordination: the citizens take a vote to blow up the other boat and then nobody can bring themselves to do it, and a prisoner just takes the detonator out of play. That looks like unilateral disarmament, which is a move superrationality has no room for. So is the Joker's game rigged so that no decision theory helps, or is the film quietly arguing that the thing that saves people is refusal rather than reasoning?
