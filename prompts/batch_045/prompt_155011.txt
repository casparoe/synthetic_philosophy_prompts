So my thesis defense is in three weeks and I only found out last week that the external examiner is a hardline exclusionist — the kind of person who thinks nonreductive physicalism is a unstable halfway house and that Kim's exclusion argument more or less settles it. The rest of the committee is two interventionist / difference-making people who think the whole worry only gets going if you smuggle in a production notion of causation, plus one philosopher of cognitive science who works on ML interpretability and is fairly impatient with philosophy of mind in general.

My thesis, roughly: the exclusion argument is unsound, and the cleanest way to see that is a parity case. I've been doing a bit of contract work with an interpretability group on the side, and people there say things all the time like "the model produced that output because the feature for golden retrievers was active in layer 14" — a high-level, multiply realized, representationally described cause sitting on top of a causally sufficient lower-level base of matrix ops and voltage transitions. If the exclusion argument were sound it would rule out those claims too, and those claims look perfectly fine. So either the exclusionist gives up the closure-plus-exclusion package, or they owe an account of why the mental case is disanalogous — and I argue the three disanalogies I can think of (multiple realizability, the derivedness of representational content, and the fact that the "physical" base in the computer case is itself an abstraction rather than fundamental physics) all fail.
