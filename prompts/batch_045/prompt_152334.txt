ok so slightly silly backstory: my reading group did a session on blame and responsibility last week and we managed to spend forty minutes on hypocrisy without anyone noticing that we werent even asking the same question. Here is the case we ended up circling. Priya and Sam are flatmates who both agreed to a house rule about washing your dishes within 24 hours. Priya has left her own dishes in the sink for a week. Sam leaves his for three days. Priya blames Sam: "you left your dishes again, that was inconsiderate." Sam says "you do it too, worse than me." Four variants. (A) plain hypocrite: Priya says nothing about her own record. (B) the confessor: Priya opens with "look, I'm worse than you on this, I've been leaving mine for a week and I'm not proud of it, but you still shouldnt have." (C) the rule-keeper: Priya is the one who proposed the dish rule and the one who normally enforces it on everyone, and everyone including Sam knows she's a repeat violator. (D) domain mismatch: Priya has quietly cheated on her taxes for years, and blames Sam for lying about his hours on a timesheet — same broad family of norm (don't be dishonest for your own advantage), different concrete domain, and Sam's violation is the one she is invoking.

What I want from you is a number, not a survey of the literature (I know the general shape of the debate; please dont spend the answer telling me who said what). Give me your credence in this claim: in variant A, Priya's blame is defective in a way that is specifically about her own record — that is, there is a genuine standing condition on blame and she fails it — as opposed to her blame being entirely in order and Sam's you-do-it-too being nothing more than a conversational dodge that doesnt touch whether his blame is warranted. Then decompose it. I'd want at least: how confident you are that there is any real standing condition on blame at all rather than tu quoque always being a fallacy; conditional on there being one, your distribution over what could ground it — something like fairness or equality of treatment, whether the blamer is actually committed to the norm she invokes, some bilateral or relationship-based account on which blame is an address between people who owe each other things, or something consequentialist about whether hypocritical blame ever does any good; and how confident you are that the three candidate verdicts come apart, i.e. that the blame is unfitting or inapt, versus apt but impermissible to express, versus apt and permissible but lacking whatever it is that lets the blamee dismiss it.
