I'm assembling the aggregation session for a graduate seminar on contractualism, and I would like a decision tree that sorts students by what they say about cases rather than by which label they prefer. Please deliver it as a single JSON object, not as prose; I will render it myself.

My suspicion, which I want the tree to test, is that the standoff about aggregating complaints persists partly because everyone treats the strength of a personal complaint as a single scalar, when it may really be two things: the size of the setback a person suffers, and the level of well-being she is left at. I want that pressure point built into the tree, and I want the four branch questions below to be the ones that do the dividing.

1. One person suffers a serious, certain loss; a very large number of other people each suffer a trivial one. Who has the stronger claim?
2. The same very large number of trivial losses, but all of them falling on a single person.
3. A policy that gives each of a thousand people a one-in-a-thousand chance of the serious loss, versus certain small costs for all of them — read the comparison ex ante and again ex post, and let the difference (if any) be a branch.
4. A trivial loss to someone already destitute, versus a substantial loss to someone extremely well off. Give the options as: track the size of the setback; track the level the person is left at; track some ordered combination; or treat the two as not separable in the required way.

Each question must be phrased so that an MA student with no stake in the literature can answer it, and each must come with the set of answers your tree allows. Build the logical space first: the combinations the answers permit, then the actual positions placed in that space. Aim for six to nine leaves, and make sure the following appear among them: a strict pairwise view with no role for numbers; a view on which numbers matter only among complaints of equal strength; a level- or hierarchy-based view; a view that permits aggregation within a life but not across lives; a fully aggregative view; and at least one skeptical leaf on which some comparisons simply have no answer. Where a leaf corresponds to a named author, give the name only if you are confident; otherwise put null in the field rather than guessing. I do not want any citations or bibliography.

Note the combinations that are empty and diagnose each: is nobody there because the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored? And flag any pair of leaves your questions cannot separate, with the extra question that would separate them. Each leaf should also carry, in one sentence, the most awkward commitment it is stuck with.

Use this schema:

- axes: array of {id, question, case, allowed_answers, why_it_divides}
- question_order: array of axis ids, plus a one-sentence justification for the order
- leaves: array of {id, label, answer_vector (axis id mapped to the chosen answer), defended_by (name or null), position_in_one_sentence, most_awkward_commitment}
- unoccupied_cells: array of {answer_vector, diagnosis (incoherent | unattractive | unexplored), explanation}
- inseparable_pairs: array of {pair (two leaf ids), why_the_tree_cannot_separate_them, question_that_would}
- notes_for_seminar: at most three sentences on where you think the real weight of the debate lies
