Ive got no academic background in any of this — never took a philosophy class — I got into it because a friend of mine slowly disappeared into a Telegram group about some crypto token a couple of years back and Ive been trying to work out what actually happened to him. I half remember reading somewhere that a philosopher, Nguyen I think, drew a line between an epistemic bubble, where you just never run into the other side, and an echo chamber, where youve been brought to systematically distrust everyone outside. That clicked for me, and Ive ended up going a bit past it. Roughly: I think the distinction is real and it matters mainly because the two things have different fixes; I think the standard advice — diversify your feed, follow the other side, read both sides, teach people media literacy — is basically fine for bubbles and is somewhere between useless and actively harmful for echo chambers, because the discrediting there is pre emptive (the group tells you in advance what outsiders will say and why theyd say it), so new exposure just gets filed as more confirmation and hands the group fresh material to discredit, meaning an echo chamber can feed on exactly the thing thats supposed to cure it. And then theres the bit I came up with myself and am least sure about: I think theres a third failure mode that is neither one. In my friends group nobody ever said a bad word about outsiders. It wasnt that they thought outsiders were lying shills — although that did happen too — it was that outsider stuff wasnt even phrased in a way that registered. Someone would post an ordinary sceptical article and the reaction wasnt "theyre paid off," it was a kind of puzzled "why would anyone even ask that" or "thats just not the question." The information is fully available and it isnt exactly distrusted, its rendered unintelligible, like a category error. Ive been calling it drift. I think its harder to escape than either of the other two, because with an echo chamber theres at least a claim you can interrogate, and here theres nothing to grab onto. Relatedly, everyone Ive seen get out of something like this got out through a person — a friendship, a falling out, wanting to be a different sort of person — never through a fact or an article, which makes me think the whole thing is less about information and trust than about who you want to be.
