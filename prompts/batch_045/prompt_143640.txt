I work as a backend engineer and read philosophy in the evenings, so I have the vocabulary of an interested amateur and none of the training. I would like a piece that would survive being read by someone who does have the training.

Please write a parody internal design document proposing the migration of a human mind off biological neural tissue onto a substrate-neutral runtime, written in the flat, confident bureaucratic voice of a large infrastructure project that has never once doubted itself. Format it as a numbered design doc with the section headings my company uses: Summary, Goals, Non-Goals, Background, Proposed Design, Rollout Plan, Alternatives Considered, Open Questions. Around 800 words. Assume it will be read on an internal wiki by engineers who have never taken a philosophy course, and that it should be funny to them without being nonsense to a specialist.

The target is the computationalist picture of the mind: mind is to brain as program is to hardware; what matters is the formal or functional description rather than the stuff implementing it; a mental state is a state picked out by its causal relations to inputs, outputs, and other states. Exaggerate those real commitments rather than inventing difficulties for them, so that the satire is recognizable as fair. Beats I would like worked in, in whatever order serves the document:

- Conformance testing is purely behavioral. QA certifies the migrated system on input-output equivalence alone, and at one point certifies a cinderblock wall in the datacenter, on the grounds that with a suitable choice of encoding and enough physical states, any old system implements any program you like.
- The team cannot agree on the granularity at which the state machine should be specified — spikes, ion channels, something finer — and treats this as an ordinary scoping dispute with a deadline attached.
- Background cites the earlier pilot: a worm whose complete connectome had already been mapped, migrated successfully in the sense that mattered to the team.
- Rollout replaces neurons with functionally equivalent substitutes in small increments, and at some point the document has to say, in project-management language, when something goes wrong, or else blandly decline to say.
- Semantic content — what any of these states is about — appears nowhere in the spec, or is filed under Non-Goals.
- A dual-run phase in which the original and the migrated system return different verdicts, together with a documented procedure for adjudicating the disagreement that is funny precisely because it does not engage with the difficulty.
- Reviewer comments in the margins, each closed out as working as intended or won't fix (as designed).

Constraints: no one in the document may name a philosopher or use the words functionalism, qualia, or Chinese room — the humor has to come from the substance rather than from jargon. Do not break character to explain a joke, do not let the author wink, and do not resolve any of the problems. The document should stay serenely committed to shipping while leaving a careful reader with a clear sense of where its premises give way.
