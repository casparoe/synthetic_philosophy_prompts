I write the physics layer for orbital-mechanics simulators, and about a year ago a colleague and I ran into Newton's bucket in code. In a scene containing a single spinning body and nothing else, I want the engine to report centrifugal effects; he says that with nothing else in the scene there is no rotation to report, and that we are free to stipulate either. We have not moved since, and as far as I can tell the philosophical literature has not either: the Leibnizian complaint that absolute rotation would be a difference without a difference-maker, Newton's two-globe argument from the tension in a cord, Mach's proposal that inertia is fixed by the total distribution of matter, and the familiar observation that in general relativity the points of the manifold appear to have no identity apart from the fields are all long since on the table, and both parties still stand where they stood.

I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict. My own hypothesis is that the standoff is not about ontology at all: both sides accept the same formalism and the same predictions, and what they actually differ about is what a physical explanation owes us — whether inertial structure has to be grounded in relations among bodies, or may be accepted as primitive. My colleague thinks it is a disguised empirical question about whether a genuinely empty universe could contain rotation, untestable in practice but not in principle. A third suggestion from our group is that the word "exists" is doing all the work, one side using it quantificationally and the other in an explanatory sense.

Which is closest, and why; where is each one weak? Then, given your diagnosis, tell me honestly what would count as progress — my candidate is that the real fault line is not existence but whether the metric figures in the theory as a fixed non-dynamical background or as a field the theory solves for, which would make the dispute evaporate in general relativity while remaining live wherever a fixed background is needed. Is that the right place to look, or is the honest answer that nothing should be expected to settle this?
