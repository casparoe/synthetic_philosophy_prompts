I sit on the technology review committee of my union local at a precision metal-fabrication plant. Management has proposed installing a machine-vision and adaptive process-control system across the CNC lines. On its face the offer is generous: an eight-year no-layoff guarantee, and one third of the projected productivity gain converted into a four-hour cut in the weekly schedule at unchanged pay.

What divides us are two clauses. The first, which we have started calling the feedback clause, provides that every override or correction a machinist makes to the system's settings is logged, uploaded, and used to retrain the model, which the vendor then licenses back to the plant — and to our competitors — on a per-machine subscription. The second lets management reassign anyone whose tasks have been absorbed by the system into 'process monitoring' work, graded a band lower on the grounds that supervising a machine is not skilled work.

About a third of us want to take the deal and bank the shorter week. Another third want to reject it outright: the feedback clause means we are being paid in hours for handing over the accumulated judgment of the shop floor, and by year eight there will be nothing left to bargain with. A third member insists both camps have confused two questions. Machinery as such, he says, is not the enemy — Marx's point is that the same machines that shorten the working day under one set of social relations lengthen it under another, and that workers who wrecked machinery took aim at the wrong thing; what we should fight over is who captures the gain, not whether the machine arrives. He also thinks the feedback clause is precisely where the second question is decided.

Draft the committee's decision memo for circulation before Friday's vote: recommend accept, reject, or accept with conditions, with the conditions or counter-proposals stated concretely enough that management could respond to them, and give the strongest version of the position you are rejecting. Please also answer one question head-on: on this way of thinking, what is the difference between co-operating with the new machinery and collaborating in our own displacement, and does the feedback clause fall on one side of that line or the other?
