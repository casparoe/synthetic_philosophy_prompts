Subject: need a parody govt form for the group blog

So I've been having the same argument in the same comment thread for like six years now — you know, the one about denoting phrases — and instead of writing another 3000 word reply that nobody will read, I want to just do it as a bit.

What I want: a parody of an official government form, something like Form 27-B, Application for Leave to Refer, issued by a Bureau of Denoting Phrases in a jurisdiction that has fully implemented Russell's treatment of definite descriptions as administrative law. Before you're allowed to utter or publish a sentence containing a definite description you have to file. The form has to work as faithful semantics, so:

- a field where the applicant has to expand the whole sentence into its quantificational form — existence, uniqueness, and that everything satisfying the description has the property in question — with no option for just leaving it as a singular term
- a checkbox for scope of negation, with the two readings printed on the form as worked examples: the present King of France is not bald, wide scope vs narrow scope, one of which comes out true
- a section on incomplete descriptions that gets auto-rejected for multiple satisfiers in the declared domain unless the applicant attaches a domain-restriction annex saying which table, which car, which king
- a field distinguishing attributive from referential use, where the referential box comes with fine print saying it isn't admissible in a criminal proceeding — picture a defendant who everyone can see is clearly insane, whether or not he did what he's accused of
- an empty-description notice: the statement comes out flatly false, not gappy, and filing a false statement is a misdemeanor, so the Bureau should be insufferably smug about it
- the margins or the back page should contain the resistance: graffiti, an appeals form, letters from citizens insisting their sentence wasn't false, that it just failed to be about anything, and refusing on principle to fill in the uniqueness field

Also: the Bureau has very recently, per some internal memo, extended its jurisdiction to plural definite descriptions and to indefinites, and I want that handled in a way somebody who actually knows this literature would say is right rather than handwavy.

Numbered fields with fine print, deadpan bureaucratic voice, 700-900 words. The joke has to be the theory being taken literally by an institution — not puns on the word the — and don't let the form break character to explain itself. A reader who has never heard of any of this should still get why it's funny; a semanticist shouldn't be able to find a mistake in it. And no living philosophers by name, please.
