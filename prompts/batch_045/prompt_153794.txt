we're wrapping up a reading group on the interpretation of quantum mechanics (mostly philosophers, plus one physicist who will absolutely say something if the poem gets the formalism wrong) and I'd like to close the last session with a poem instead of a summary slide.

The way I keep putting the dialectic to myself is as two questions: does anything ever get *added* to the wavefunction (extra variables, particle positions, whatever), and does anything ever get *taken away* (collapse, one term singled out, the others dropped)? Everett says no and no, Bohm says yes and no, Copenhagen says no and yes — and the fourth cell, where you say yes to both, is the one nobody wants to live in. That's the picture I want it built on.

So: a pantoum — the Malay form where lines 2 and 4 of each quatrain turn into lines 1 and 3 of the next, and the closing stanza gathers up the leftovers — of about four quatrains. Stanzas one to three are the three theories talking, in whatever voice, and the lines that get repeated should be sentences about the setup that at least two of them can honestly say (an apparatus, a superposition, a reading, a recorder), so that the whole point is watching which line a given voice can't say straight, or has to say with its meaning bent. The last stanza should be the fourth cell: the view nobody actually holds, assembled entirely out of lines the other three have already used.

Keep it a poem and not a rhymed glossary — I want it to land for someone who's never thought about this, and to be right enough that the physicist doesn't wince. Under a page. And no "nobody really understands quantum mechanics" mysticism; that's precisely the note I don't want to end the term on.

After it, a short note, five or six lines, on where the poem cheats: which line no actual Everettian or Bohmian would put in their own mouth, and where the form forced you to lean on a contested reading to make a repetition work.

Also please push back if the framing is wrong. If collapse theories with some kind of primitive ontology already live in that fourth cell, or if "nothing gets taken away" isn't fair to Bohm, or if the whole 2x2 is just a bad way to carve this up, say that plainly instead of quietly writing around it and handing me something tidy. I'd rather have the argument than a poem I can't defend in front of the physicist.
