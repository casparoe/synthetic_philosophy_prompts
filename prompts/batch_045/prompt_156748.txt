You are a professional philosopher with research-level command of two areas that rarely get put in one head: classical Chinese philosophy (in particular the Zhuangzi and its interpretive disputes) and contemporary epistemology of dreaming, imagination, and first-personal evidence. Here is the situation. I am a novelist, and the book I am currently drafting turns on what I take to be a real philosophical problem rather than a decorative one. My protagonist comes to suspect, for reasons the novel supplies, that the life she is living may be a dream, and the book's drama depends on that suspicion being both epistemically respectable and emotionally costly to her. I have been trying to get clear about what I actually believe, because I suspect I am helping myself to things that don't cohere. This is my profile, as honestly as I can state it:

(a) On the text itself: I hold the non-skeptical reading. I think the butterfly passage near the end of the second chapter of the Zhuangzi is not raising the question of whether one is now dreaming; it is making a point about what the commentarial tradition calls the transformation of things — that no standpoint or identity is final, and that Zhuang Zhou and the butterfly are each, in their turn, what is going on. The force of the passage is ontological and therapeutic, not epistemological.

(b) On the problem itself: I hold that dream skepticism is a genuine epistemic difficulty and not a sophomore's diversion. The hypothesis that I am dreaming at this moment is coherent, and I do not think the standard dismissals of it work.

(c) On where the difference lies: I hold that the difference between dreaming and being awake is not a difference in phenomenal character at the time. Dreams can be exactly as vivid, as detailed, as affectively gripping. The difference lies in causal-functional role — what the experience tracks, whether there is a stable environment it is hooked up to, and so on.

(d) On what may be appealed to: I hold a broadly internalist constraint. A difference between two states that is in principle invisible to the subject from the inside cannot do first-personal epistemic work for that subject. And in practice my own confidence that I am awake rests on the sheer volume and mutual coherence of my apparent memories.

(e) Against one popular test: I keep a dream journal, and I have repeatedly woken convinced of whole conversations, journeys, and decades of background that never occurred. So I hold that bare memory-continuity cannot be what distinguishes waking from dreaming, and I take this observation to be decisive against memory-based replies to the skeptic.

(f) On the upshot: I hold that nothing practical turns on the answer. Whether or not the worry can be settled, the stance one should take toward one's life is the same — a certain equanimity. Relatedly, I hold that what makes a life worth living is entirely a matter of its content, not of its causal history: a whole lifetime dreamt through would be worth exactly as much as the same lifetime lived.

(g) On what fiction can do: I hold that a wholly invented scene can be true to experience, and that fiction is a genuine route to knowledge of what it is like. I want my protagonist's uncertainty to be real uncertainty, not a literary effect.

(h) On what fiction cannot do: I hold that a novel is not like life in one specific respect — someone decides. As author, I settle whether a given scene is a dream, or I settle that the question is left open. Nothing plays the author's chair in the case the worry is about. So the novel may be structurally incapable of producing the very state it is trying to convey.

What I want from you is an audit, not a lecture. Some of these, I suspect, only look like they clash, and part of the value of your answer will be telling me which those are and why. For each real tension, say what would have to give, what the cheapest available repair is, and what that repair costs me elsewhere in the set. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and — since I have to finish this book — whether the book itself has to change as a consequence, and into what.
