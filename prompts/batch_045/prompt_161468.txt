ok so I run a weekend reading group — about 14 people, half programmers half humanities types, two hours — and next month we're doing Kuhn. I want an activity where they *feel* incommensurability rather than hear about it: no lecture up front, and nobody says "paradigm", "incommensurable", or Kuhn's name until the debrief.

Rough setup, which you should fix or replace if you have something better: invent a small made-up science with its own puzzle domain. Say a deck of ~40 cards showing stacks of coloured bands, something I can make in an afternoon with index cards and markers. Two rulebooks, one per half of the room. Each is internally coherent, each lets you classify and predict the deck well enough that solving puzzles feels like real progress, each has a built-in rule that generates its own anomalies — but they carve the deck up on incompatible principles and reuse a shared word ("depth", say) to mean different things. Split the room, train each half separately, score points for correct predictions. Then pair people across halves, hand out fresh cards, and make each pair produce one joint prediction and one jointly agreed translation of a key claim from their rulebook.

What they should walk away having run into: that inside a framework you experience it as given rather than as a hypothesis (puzzle-solving, not testing); that anomalies alone don't budge you until there's an alternative on the table; that when they finally see the other rulebook some people flip and some don't; and that at the end they genuinely can't agree on whether the two halves were studying the same thing. Don't make one rulebook obviously worse, and don't let the disagreement collapse into a labelling convention they can resolve just by pointing at cards.

Give me the rules, the scoring, the materials, a timeline across the two hours, and the debrief mapping moments onto the ideas. I know somebody in the room is going to say this only shows underdetermination or a difference in convention, not meaning-change — have an answer ready for them. Be frank about which part of the Kuhnian claim the game can't produce at all (my guess is the world-change bit, since everyone is staring at the same cards) and that I'll have to supply by hand. And tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it.

Start with the activity itself — setup and rules — no preamble about why incommensurability is interesting.
