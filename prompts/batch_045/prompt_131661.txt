I am writing a paper on the frame/outer/inner distinction that Hofstadter draws in *Gödel, Escher, Bach*, and I would find it useful to read a careful argumentative treatment of a difficulty I keep circling around. Please write a short essay (roughly 900–1,200 words) that takes up the following controversy and defends a verdict on it.

Call an artifact *self-annealing* when no proper part of it can be singled out as its outer message, because the same physical features that carry its inner message also, under the very decoding scheme they specify, describe that scheme. A self-describing tape for a universal machine, or a quine, looks like a limiting case: the instructions for reading it are among the things it says. Call an artifact *drifted* when it is produced by a process with no senders, conventions, or intentions behind it — a crystal whose surface grooves happen, under some scheme, to decode into a coherent text — and note that for any artifact and any chosen content there is some scheme that maps the one to the other.

The controversy is this. Is the frame/outer/inner hierarchy a taxonomy of real structural levels *in the message*, or only a taxonomy of the receiver's epistemic situation — of how much the receiver already has to bring to the artifact? Self-annealing artifacts seem to show that the outer level is not a separate location but a role assigned from outside, which can be played by anything, including the inner message itself. Drifted artifacts seem to show that the inner level cannot be fixed by structure alone, since structure underdetermines content, and so must be fixed either by the artifact's causal history or by some notion of the "naturalness" of a decoding scheme.

Your essay should weigh both worries against the reading on which the three levels mark genuine, mind-independent differences, and should say explicitly which of the three levels, if any, is doing real theoretical work. Please close by defending one position rather than splitting the difference — for instance, a hierarchical realism, a full collapse into interpreter-relative roles, or a hybrid on which one level is objective and the others are not — and by drawing out what your verdict implies for the larger question of whether meaning is located in the message, in the mechanism that reads it, or in the pair.
