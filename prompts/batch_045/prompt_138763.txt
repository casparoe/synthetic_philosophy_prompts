We also ended up arguing about whether the inner message is supposed to be the meaning the sender intended or just whatever the receiver ends up with, which I suspect matters a lot for how you answer the rest of this. Anyway, I realised I have really firm gut verdicts on a pile of cases and no idea whether they add up to anything, and I'd like you to act as a professional philosopher who works on semantics / philosophy of information and help me find out. Take your time with it, please — do a bit of brainstorming about candidate principles first rather than jumping to the first one that fits, and feel free to tell me my verdicts are junk.

Here are the cases and what I'd say, going pure gut:

1. A golden-record-style disc found drifting in space by beings who have no machine that could play it, but the disc's cover has pictures etched on it showing how to build the player. — Definitely a message, even before they've built anything.
2. Same disc, but the cover diagrams were scratched off before it launched; all they have is a grooved disc. — Still a message, but it feels like a much thinner claim now.
3. A hard drive with a file of random bytes on it. Under some baroque encoding someone cooked up afterwards, the bytes spell out a sonnet. — Not a message. There's nothing there to pick out that encoding.
4. A strongly encrypted file, where the one person who knew the key died. — Definitely still a message. The meaning is in there.
5. A honeybee's waggle dance in a dark hive. — That's a message, even though no bee meant to say anything and it isn't the sort of thing that could be false.
6. An ant pheromone trail that makes other ants walk to the food. — Not a message, that's just chemistry shoving ants around.
7. The weight matrix of a trained neural network, together with the code that runs inference on it. — I want to say there's no message in there, nothing anybody meant. But that sits really badly with what I said about the bee.
8. DNA from species A put into the egg of a related species B, producing something B-ish with a few A traits. — The outer message is the same physical stuff in both cases, so the inner message changed: the message isn't located in the DNA alone.
9. An inscribed tablet from a civilisation whose language is completely lost — we can see it's script, we can't read a word. — Clearly a message. Frame message yes, outer message presumably there, inner message gone.
10. A cloud formation that happens to spell HELP perfectly. — Obviously not a message.

So: is there a principle that gets all ten, or do some of them have to go? I especially want you to look at 3 vs 4 (both need a key, opposite verdicts) and 5 vs 6 vs 7 (all three are information transfer with nothing that intends anything). Please give me a table of my verdicts, then two or three candidate principles, each with a note on which case motivated which clause, and be explicit about which of my verdicts each candidate can't accommodate and which you'd overturn if you had to pick one. And if my verdicts just don't cohere, say so plainly and tell me where the incoherence is.
