After thirty-five years in experimental low-temperature physics I took early retirement, and for the past two years I have been sitting in on a philosophy of physics reading group at the university. We have spent this term on Maxwell's demon, and I volunteered to bring something to our final meeting that is not another summary of the literature: something satirical, which a group of humanists plus one retired experimentalist can enjoy together. I would be very grateful if you would draft it for me.

The piece I have in mind is an excerpt from a corporate disclosure document. Please write roughly 700 words, formatted as numbered notes to a set of accounts, drawn from the annual Informational Emissions Disclosure that a gas-separation firm (give it some bland, institutional name) files with its regulator. The register should be the flat, reassuring prose of a real annual report: euphemism, passive voice, confidence.

Substantively, the notes should cover the following, in whatever order works best:

- The firm's business: it operates sorting engines in which a demon discriminates fast molecules from slow ones, acquiring one bit of information per discrimination and periodically clearing its memory. Clearing one bit costs at least kT ln 2, about 2.9 × 10⁻²¹ J at room temperature.
- The firm's headline innovation: all clearing operations have been relocated to facilities in colder jurisdictions, since the cost scales with temperature. Give the figures honestly (clearing is roughly 17 per cent cheaper at 250 K than at 300 K) and also give the annual throughput, quoted in moles of bits, so that the reader can see the saving is real in percentage terms and derisory in absolute ones (a mole of bits cleared at 300 K comes to under two kilojoules). The firm should present this as a material improvement in efficiency.
- A note in the style of greenhouse-gas accounting, distinguishing the dissipation the firm causes directly from the dissipation it exports to its cold-climate subsidiaries and to a supplier, with only the first category counted as the firm's own.
- A note explaining that the firm's demons no longer clear memory at all: measurement and copying of a bit can in principle be done reversibly, so each new bit is simply written to fresh tape, purchased at cost from a subsidiary that supplies it in a maximally uncertain state. The joke, which the note must not acknowledge, is that supplying blank tape is exactly the clearing operation, relocated one corporate layer away.
- An auditor's qualification: erasure is not an event an outsider can verify. A genuine reset is not distinguishable, on the thermodynamic record, from a reversible transfer of the bit into some degree of freedom that has not been listed in the inventory, and so all dissipation figures are self-reported.
- A footnote from outside counsel observing that commentators remain divided on whether the second law needs rescuing by this kind of information bookkeeping at all, or whether the bookkeeping merely restates the prohibition it is invoked to explain, and noting that the firm takes no position on the matter.

Constraints: the physics must be correct in every particular, since I will be the one wincing otherwise. Keep it deadpan throughout; the company should never notice what it has admitted. Keep it legible to a non-physicist, with the humour coming from the content rather than from name-dropping, and treat any proper names from the history of the subject as incidental rather than load-bearing. What is being satirized is the reflex of treating the second law as an accounting identity that can always be made to balance by moving the cost somewhere else, and I would like that to be what a reader comes away having felt.
