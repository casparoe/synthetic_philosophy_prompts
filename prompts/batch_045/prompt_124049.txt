I am designing a diagnostic instrument for a graduate seminar on corruption and comparative public ethics: a decision tree that takes a reader's judgments about concrete cases and sorts them into the account of corruption they are tacitly using. I want the tree built from the logical space of possible answers rather than from a list of established labels, and I want it usable as a standalone handout, so it should appeal only to distinctions a well-read non-specialist can reconstruct from memory.

Deliverable: a branching instrument of ten to twelve diagnostic nodes plus a key. Each node is a concrete case or a plainly worded claim with two or three answer options, phrased so that a reader can answer without knowing which account any answer supports. No node may ask directly whether the reader defines corruption in terms of public office, markets, norms, or legal rules; the labeling happens only at the leaves.

The cases. Distribute the following across the nodes, and add two to four of your own where you think the space is under-sampled:

1. Speed money. A firm pays a clerk to process a permit it is legally owed, within the statutory deadline, when clerks routinely delay by months otherwise.
2. A public manager hires her cousin, who is in fact the best-qualified applicant, and would have hired him anyway.
3. A village elder presents a goat to a district officer in a region where refusing a gift is a grave insult; the officer accepts, and no favour is requested or given.
4. Data philanthropy. A company donates a proprietary dataset to a city that ranks contract applicants with a machine-learning system; the donation foreseeably and welcomeably improves the donor's ranking, though the donation is not conditional on any decision. I especially want to know which accounts treat this as corruption and which do not, and where the tree has to add a node to separate them.
5. A firm lobbies successfully, fully lawfully and without payment, for a rule written to favour its own technology.
6. A triage nurse moves up a patient whose family sends a basket of fruit, in a queue that is formally first-come-first-served.
7. An international NGO requires its local partner organisations to adopt a zero-gifts policy, including refusing customary hospitality from village hosts.
8. A mayor awards contracts preferentially to firms that hire from her own ethnic community, understanding herself to be discharging obligations of solidarity rather than enriching herself.

Branch structure. Label every branch with the claim that does the splitting, not with a school of thought. I expect the early nodes to settle whether there is a wrong at all and whether its locus is the rule, the consequence, the relationship, or the agent's character; the middle nodes to settle whether a betrayed principal, a distorted allocation, a subverted office, or a violated norm is doing the work; and the later nodes to settle whether the operative standard is internal to the polity in question or external to it, and whether intention, reciprocity, or foresight is required. Include at least one node that separates positions on whether a population's own norms can be an unreliable standard because those norms have themselves adapted to endemic extraction, and say what that does to any norm-based definition.

Leaves. Locate in the space at least the following: a legalist position; a principal-agent position of the kind associated with the formula that corruption is a function of monopoly power plus discretion minus accountability; public-office-centered, market-centered, and public-interest-centered definitions; a harm-based universalist definition of the kind used by the large transparency NGOs; a social-norms or perceptual position, including the black-grey-white grading of practices by how a public regards them; a particularist or gift-economy position on which reciprocity and patronage are obligations rather than deviance; a neo-patrimonial position on which clientelism is a different order of rule rather than a corruption of a bureaucratic one, so that applying the concept is a category error; a republican position on which the wrong is subjecting public decision to another's will or corrupting the independence and character of the agent; and a structural or collective-action position on which the phenomenon is an equilibrium and individual blame is misplaced. Each leaf gets one sentence on its most awkward commitment, the answer pattern that lands there, and the one case above where it yields an answer most readers will find absurd.

Unoccupied and unseparated cells. Mark at least two coherent-looking combinations of answers for which you know of no defender, and say briefly whether each is empty because incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag at least three pairs of leaves your tree cannot really separate, give the case on which they coincide, and specify the extra question that would separate them if anyone could answer it.

Finally, a short note on scoring: say whether the instrument should return one leaf or a profile, and where you expect readers to be inconsistent — in particular whether it is coherent to reach a universalist verdict on the lobbying and mayor cases while reaching a particularist verdict on the goat and hospitality cases.

Names of positions may be given where a standard label exists, but the handout should not depend on anyone's exact wording or on locating a source.
