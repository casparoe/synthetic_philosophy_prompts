I help maintain a cross-national index that codes countries on 'freedom,' and I got pulled into the liberty literature to sanity-check our coding — and the three-way negative / positive / republican argument looks completely frozen to me: the same Berlin-style worry that positive liberty smuggles in paternalism, the same republican examples about the master who never has to lift a finger, the same reply from the negative-liberty side that non-domination is just expected non-interference wearing fancier counterfactuals, and nobody appearing to have moved on anything anyone said after roughly 2000.

My hypothesis is that it's three stacked disagreements with three different diagnoses, and that people keep talking past each other because one gets answered with the tools for another: (1) a partly verbal bit about whether liberty names a property of a person's options at a time or a standing status they hold in relation to others; (2) a hidden formal or empirical question about whether the counterfactuals republicans need — would the power-holder have interfered, and would it have been arbitrary — are well-defined enough to underwrite anything quantitative, which is the part I actually care about since my index can only ever observe actual interference and legal rules; and (3) a bedrock bit about whether independence from arbitrary power matters intrinsically or only because it tends to buy you security.

Test that against the case that broke my coding scheme: a benefits system run by an automated rule, no official has any discretion over it, it has in fact never cut anyone off, and its outputs are fully predictable — yet nobody subject to it has any say in the rule, there is no route of appeal, and if the rule changed tomorrow they would simply have to lump it. Is there domination here? Whether your answer comes out clean or muddy, tell me if the case really seperates the republican reading from the negative-liberty one or whether both sides can just digest it, and which of my three diagnoses that result supports.

Given whichever diagnosis survives, tell me what would count as progress — a result about the counterfactuals, splitting arbitrariness-as-discretion from arbitrariness-as-failure-to-track-the-interests-of-those-affected and seeing which one the arguments really need, agreeing to stop using one word for two things, or nothing at all — and be honest if you think the question is merely hard rather than ill-posed, since I have to ship a codebook either way.
