So I got promoted about four months ago and now run a tiny product team that's split between Rotterdam and Osaka — two devs in Rotterdam, one designer in Osaka. I'm in Austin, everyone's remote, and I'd never managed anyone outside the US before, so basically all of this is new to me and I'm probaly overthinking it.

Here's what blew up last week. On our Monday call one of the Rotterdam devs, Joost, said about our designer Mika's new mockups, totally flat: this doesn't work, I think we should throw it out and start over. No softening whatsoever. Mika said almost nothing for the rest of the call. Later she messaged me privately saying she'd found the call pretty bruising and asking whether we could give feedback "a bit more indirectly."

I passed that on to Joost. He came back with something I half agree with and half don't: if he'd said "maybe worth trying another direction," Mika would have to guess whether he meant "tweak it" or "this is garbage." Guessing is work, and it's work he's shifted onto her while keeping the actual content to himself. So wrapping things up isn't kindness, it's deciding for someone else what they get to know, and he'd rather she be annoyed at him than confused.

But then I did something dumber. I went back to Mika and told her Joost hadn't meant anything personal, that he's just like that, and that he actually rates her work. Joost found out and got annoyed for a different reason: he says he does mean something personal in the sense that he genuinely thinks that particular mockup is bad, and my little reassurance invented a state of mind for him that he never claimed, and told Mika something untrue in order to make her feel better. His words were that this isn't politeness, it's me lying on his behalf.

So three things I actually want from you:

1. Is Joost right that soft or vague feedback is the more disrespectful move, because it hides the real content and makes the other person do the decoding? Or is that just a nice story he tells himself so he can keep being blunt?
2. Was my line to Mika a lie in a way that matters? I have this feeling that lying about a third person's inner state is a different animal from telling someone their haircut looks great, even though both are meant kindly — but I can't say what the difference is, or whether it makes mine worse or better.
3. What do I do next week? One house style for everyone, or do I adapt to each person — and if I adapt, am I being two-faced?

One more thing that's nagging at me. My brother's line is that indirectness only actually works — only is genuinely kinder — when both people share the same code for reading between the lines; across a code gap it just fails, because the receiver neither gets the content nor realises she was supposed to read between the lines at all, so you've paid the cost of softening and got none of the benefit. If that's right it seems to cut against Mika here, and I don't like where it leads. It also doesn't obviously cover my case, where the thing I fudged wasn't even my own view.

Give me a real verdict, not "it depends on the culture." Commit to a definite line on all three, say why, and then tell me the single strongest thing someone who disagrees with you would come back with — one, not a list — and whether it moves you. Oh, and write it in British English if you can, my notes get circulated to the London office.
