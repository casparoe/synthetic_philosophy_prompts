I keep going back and forth with a friend about whether the importance / tractability / neglectedness frame is actually any good, so I want to test it on a case instead of trading intuitions. Take WHO's smallpox eradication, and specificaly the decision to launch the intensified programme in 1967 (roughly two million deaths that year, certified gone by 1980) at a moment when the flagship global campaign was the malaria eradication effort started in the 50s, which had most of the money and staff and obviously failed -- malaria still kills several hundred thousand people a year.

Set it up as a funder in 1966 scoring both causes on the three axes using whatever concrete proxies you'd actually reach for, then tell me which cause the framework picks and what it would have said about the smallpox decision we now know was the right one.

The parts I care about, because I think this is where the frame is silently mis-specified:

- Importance as a flow vs. eradication as a one-shot. Does anything built around annual burden systematically underrate "you can end this, permanently"? And what happens to neglectedness-as-a-proxy-for-diminishing-returns when the last handful of countries are the most expensive per case averted?
- Tractability of a cause vs. tractability of the method you happen to have. The strategy that worked -- surveillance and containment rather than hitting some mass-vaccination coverage target -- hardly existed as a strategy when the programme was approved. So was ex ante tractability just low, and does that mean ITN is biased toward causes that already have a working intervention?
- The "who else would have paid" question. A lot of the push came from rich countries that wanted to stop footing the bill for routine vaccination and quarantine. Was smallpox even neglected in the sense the framework needs, or just unglamorous to funders with self-interested reasons?
- Whatever has no slot in the frame: the programme left behind vaccine logistics, surveillance networks and trained field epidemiologists that later campaigns borrowed. Call it option value or capacity, but where does it go?

Where the framework is genuinely silent, say so instead of stretching it. And finish on the survivorship worry: are we only holding smallpox up as the poster child because it won? Under 900 words, no citations, nothing looked up.
