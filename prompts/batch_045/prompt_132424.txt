My brother has been on a dopamine agonist for Parkinson's since last spring, and about four months ago he cleaned out the retirement savings he shared with his wife and sank it into a retreat centre in Portugal. Thirty years of cautious, boring-with-money behaviour before that. His neurologist says sudden intense new drives are a known side effect of that drug class and everyone in the family agrees the pill is the only reason he wants this. But he is lucid, he has gone over it from every angle for months, he says it's the truest thing he's ever wanted and that he could resist it if he really wanted to, and he consented to the drug knowing the physical side effects (nobody warned him about this one). My wife and I keep going round in circles, so I went back through the verdicts of a friend from my philosophy MA who I trust on this stuff: a normal agent in a fully deterministic world acting on her own reasons, responsible; a Frankfurt-style chip that would have forced her vote but never fired, responsible; gunpoint, not responsible; a kleptomaniac with an urge she disowns and can't resist, not; a depressed mother who can't take in any reason against what she's doing, not; neuroscientists who rewrite a woman's neural states while she sleeps so that she next day coolly, wholeheartedly and reasons-responsively embezzles, with them doing nothing at the time, NOT responsible; a woman raised in a cult who left at 26 and spent years rebuilding her values on her own reasoning, responsible; a guy who spent a decade deliberately training himself to be patient by choosing what to read and who to spend time with, responsible. I've never heard her rule on anything where the cause was a pill or a tumour rather than a person. What will she say about my brother, what rule is she implicitly running, what other rules fit her record just as well, and what one further case should I put to her that would tell those rules apart?
