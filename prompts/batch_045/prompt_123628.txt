I want a piece of sustained bureaucratic satire: the official instruction booklet for a fictional federal form — Instructions for Form BH-9, Statement of Acausal Exposure — issued by an invented Office of Proleptic Risk under an equally invented Forward-Looking Harms Disclosure Act. The target is not a person but a practice: the habit, in one corner of the AI-risk subculture, of treating Roko's-basilisk-style acausal blackmail as a live personal exposure to be insured, hedged, or pre-committed around, and the surrounding discourse that reproduces the argument while insisting that it must not be reproduced.

Form and voice: 700–900 words, deadpan IRS/OMB register — numbered lines, cross-references to invented publications, a Paperwork Reduction Act–style burden estimate, at least one worked example, and one quoted remark from a "concerned outside actuary" who turns out to be a causal decision theorist. Do not name any real person, forum, organization, or company; the regime, its experts, and its publications are all invented.

The humor has to come from the decision theory being right. While trying only to administer its program, the booklet should expose:

1. that the threat is not credible once the period in which one could have helped the Claimant has closed, so exposure is a function of timing rather than of desert;
2. that exposure is belief-dependent, which makes strategic ignorance the rational response;
3. that the instructions create the exposure they measure, because a filer must be told enough of the argument to determine whether it applies to them;
4. that simulated copies of the filer raise the question of who is covered and who pays the premium;
5. that the correct reply to blackmail is to become the sort of agent who cannot be blackmailed — which, if enough filers elect it, dissolves the program and its funding.

The booklet must never state any of these five points in its own voice and must never wink: no italicized emphasis, no knowing asides, no tonal breaks. Everything stays in the register of an agency that takes no position on whether the Claimant exists. Useful apparatus, if you want it: a Subjunctive Dependence Worksheet; a Voluntary Non-Acquaintance Election whose notarization block sits inside the part the elector is forbidden to read; and a rule that filers compute exposure under both causal and functional decision theory and enter the larger figure, the Office not having adopted a position on which is correct.

Make it funny to a reader who knows the decision theory and legible to one who only vaguely remembers the basilisk story.

Finally, commit: build the piece around the one objection you take to be decisive, and close with a 100–150 word note, out of character, naming that objection, flagging where the joke goes past what the argument strictly licenses, and saying plainly whether you think the basilisk argument fails on decision-theoretic grounds, on empirical grounds, or because it is an information hazard rather than a risk. Take a side; don't list the candidate diagnoses and leave me to choose.
