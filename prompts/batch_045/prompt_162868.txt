For most of my working life I was an experimental physical chemist, and in retirement I have started reading philosophy seriously; I find I still want numbers attached to claims, so that is what I am asking you for.

Consider a variant of the quantum-suicide experiment that I have not seen discussed. A device performs 1000 successive trials on identically prepared qubits; on the up outcome nothing happens, on the down outcome a mechanism administers instantaneous, painless death, with decoherence complete within microseconds. On down outcomes there is also a hardware failure probability of 0.01 that leaves the subject alive but with dense anterograde amnesia covering the preceding trial, so that they survive without being able to report surviving it. Call the two kinds of survivor intact continuers and degraded continuers. Under the usual branching picture, surviving measure is nonzero after all 1000 trials but of order e^-683, and the intact share of that surviving measure is (1/1.01)^1000, roughly 5×10^-5.

So stated as a claim with a truth value: conditional on there being any continuation of this subject's experience after the sequence, the continuation is overwhelmingly more likely to be degraded than intact, and first-person-continuation reasoning (quantum immortality) commits its adherent to expecting that rather than to expecting intact survival.

My own credence that first-person-continuation reasoning is sound is about 0.15.

Give me yours, decomposed. I want the sub-questions you think it depends on — for instance whether a no-collapse branching picture is correct at all; whether branching yields many genuine continuers of one person rather than one continuer selected at random; whether a continuer in a branch of vanishingly small amplitude, given neural noise and finite physical precision, has any experience; whether a degraded, amnesiac continuer is the same subject on the theory of personal identity you are implicitly using; whether first-person expectation should track measure or mere existence of a branch — each with a number and a one-line justification, then the combination that gets you to your headline figure.

Then three further things: which single term your total is most sensitive to; what argument or observation would move that term most; and, since a friend of mine from my reading group puts the figure at 0.6, where you think the disagreement between us most likely lives.
