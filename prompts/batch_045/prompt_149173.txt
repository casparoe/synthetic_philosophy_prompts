Thesis, roughly: the standard dirty-hands story (Walzer/Machiavelli flavored) treats the politician's guilt as a private residue — you do the necessary wrong, then you're stuck with it. I argue for something I'm calling moral escrow: when an official does a genuinely necessary wrong for public reasons, the wrongness isn't forgiven and isn't privately swallowed either, it's held by the political community, and it's discharged later by a public body that (a) confirms the necessity after the fact, (b) formally records the wrong, and (c) imposes a symbolic sanction on the official. The guilt goes into escrow and only the polity can release it.
