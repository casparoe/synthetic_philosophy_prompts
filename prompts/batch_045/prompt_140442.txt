The idea: the paradox of phenomenal judgement is normally presented as a single argument, but I think it's two requirements stuck together that nobody properly pulls apart. (1) A dependence requirement: my judgement "I am in pain" has to depend in the right way on my being in pain — that's what's supposed to break under epiphenomenalism, and it's why the epiphenomalist's fallback (pain and judgement share a neural cause, so the judgement tracks the pain reliably) feels like cheating rather than an answer. (2) A causal reading of that dependence, which is what generates the paradox, since epiphenomenalism just denies causation. I want to split (1) from (2) and ask whether the dependence in (1) can be cashed out non-causally: either as counterfactual / difference-making dependence (the pain is a difference-maker for the judgement without producing it) or as grounding / constitutive dependence (the physical base of the judgement and the physical base of the pain are related by grounding, not causation).

So the question, roughly: is there a non-causal dependence relation available to the epiphenomenalist that does the work the paradox demands — making the judgement answerable to the pain rather than merely reliably correlated with it — and if so does it do all of that work or only part of it (say, securing reference and reliability but not knowledge, or knowledge but not non-accidentality)?

What I need in the proposal: the question pinned down precisely enough that the paper has a determinate answer, including whether it's a question about knowledge, about singular reference, or about both; why the standard replies (bite the bullet — the judgement is reliable but not about the pain in the way we assumed; or just reject epiphenomenalism) leave it open; the approach, and why that approach rather than the obvious alternative of arguing about whether epiphenomenalism is even consistent with physics; what a positive result and a negative result would each look like; and what would count as the project failing — my worry is that if the dependence question turns out to be just a special case of how we know about any non-causal thing (maths, the future, moral facts), the whole thing explodes.
