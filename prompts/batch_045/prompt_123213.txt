Our city's Markets and Public Values Committee, of which I am a member, has accumulated a set of rulings whose rationale I cannot state to my own satisfaction. We permitted compensated plasma donation at licensed clinics but refused a state-priced market in kidneys from screened and counselled sellers. We accepted surge pricing for hotel rooms during storms but not for bottled water or portable generators once an emergency had been declared. We permitted resale above face value for concert tickets but not for places on the council-house waiting list. We permitted payment for taking part in clinical trials but not payment for attending a public consultation to speak in favour of a developer's scheme. We permitted compensation for egg donation but refused commercial surrogacy.

The proposal now before us is a regulated platform for paid companionship. For a fee fixed by the regulator, a person would visit an isolated older resident each week, accompany them to medical appointments, be registered as their emergency contact and be treated by care staff as a friend; the company is explicit that what it sells is the standing of a friend rather than merely hours of work. The resident would be told that the visits are bought, institutions would be told if they asked, and nobody would be required to disclose the arrangement to anyone else.

I suspect that several different principles fit our record equally well and that this proposal is exactly where they come apart. Please answer in British English as a numbered list of exactly five items, each of one or two sentences, with no introduction and no closing remarks: the principles consistent with our precedents; what each of them implies for this proposal; the decision you would recommend if we want to be consistent with ourselves, together with your reason; which of our earlier rulings is the closest analogue; and the variant of this proposal that would best tell the competing principles apart.
