i'm a software engineer — i work on the display pipeline for an AR heads-up display in cars (frame pacing, latency compensation, that sort of thing). no philosophy training, so sorry if i mangle the terms.

A friend who does do philosophy got me into this. There's this old perception demo: you flash a red dot at point A, then a green dot a bit to the right at point B, maybe 50–100 ms apart, and what people report is a single dot sliding from A to B that changes colour partway along — even though at the moment it supposedly changes, the green flash hasn't happened yet. He said there are two stories you can tell about that. One: you really did have an experience first (seeing the red dot, or seeing it move without turning green yet), and then, milliseconds later, your memory of it gets rewritten so it now seems like it turned green mid-flight. Two: nothing was conscious at that point at all — the brain waited for the second flash and then wrote the finished version straight into experience, with no earlier draft ever having been conscious. I think he called them Orwellian and Stalinesque, and i think it's Dennett, but i coudn't swear to either. His point was that nothing you can measure — not behaviour, not reaction time, not what the person says afterwards — tells you which story is the true one.

The thing is, I think this actually bites, and I want a case that shows it. Our group is fighting over a proposed requirement for collision warnings: the warning has to be consciously perceived by the driver at least 400 ms before she hits the brake. I want a scenario where that requirement comes out satisfied on one story and not satisfied on the other, while everything third-person stays exactly the same. If I can build it, the requirement is either untestable or it's secretly a claim about which story is right.

What i need built:

1. One paragraph, realistic. A car, a driver, a windshield HUD, a pedestrian, a dashcam maybe. No lab equipment, no brain scanners, no implanted electrodes, no sci-fi. Consumer-grade stuff at most.
2. Both stories have to agree on every observable fact and on everything the driver sincerely reports afterwards, including "yes, I saw it, I had time to react." So the case can't be resolved by asking her, and it cant be resolved by any measurement a regulator could get. That's the whole point of it.
3. The disagreement has to be the hard kind: not "did she see it 20 ms earlier or 20 ms later," but "was there any conscious experience at all at the moment the requirement cares about." One story says yes — there was a real experience there and it got edited afterwards; the other says no — at that instant there was nothing conscious, and the experience she remembers was constructed later. I want that to turn into a clean yes/no on the requirement, not a fudge.
4. It has to fall out of each story's own commitments. I don't want "and let's stipulate the Stalinesque one is true here." Tell me what each story commits to about when experience happens, and why that gives the verdict it gives.
5. Cheating rule: the driver's memory can't just be unreliable in the ordinary way. The forgetting has to be the rewrite the first story posits, not normal fuzzy recollection. And neither story gets to quietly bolt on an extra mechanism to win.
6. Use the colour-phi structure as the engine, ideally with the HUD doing the two-step thing itself — an amber glyph in one position, a red one slightly displaced a beat later, so it reads as one marker sliding across and turning red early. If that doesn't work, say why and use something equivalent.

Then, after the scenario:

- what each story says, and the verdict each gives on the requirement, spelled out
- which single feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens to the disagreement if you remove it. (My guess: if you reword the requirement as "the information was available to the driver's decision-making at time t" instead of "consciously perceived," the whole thing collapses back into agreement — tell me if that's right and why.)
- how my friend would attack the case. His move, i assume, is that if no possible evidence distinguishes the two stories, then there's no fact of the matter about when she was conscious, so the requirement isn't untestable-but-real, it's just empty. Is that the strongest objection here, or is there a better one? And if he's right, what exactly does it cost me — can I write the requirement at all, and what should it say instead?
