I build large-scale quantitative models for a living, and everyone in my field concedes that our current models are strictly false — idealised, known to be superseded within a generation — while also insisting that the field is getting closer to the truth rather than merely saving the phenomena. The defence I have come to rely on runs through truthlikeness: I hold that although Popper's content-and-consequence definition is refuted by the Tichý–Miller triviality result, which has it that no false theory can be closer to the truth than any other, the likeness family of accounts escapes that result, since it measures a theory's closeness to the truth by distance in a space of possibilities rather than by containment of true and false consequences. I add two qualifications of my own: closeness is always relative to a specified cognitive problem and a fixed set of attributes with weights, and a successor model can be more truthlike than its predecessor even where the successor introduces attributes the predecessor lacked.

What would count against this package? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want to know which ones a defender of the likeness approach would concede as genuinely counting against it rather than dismiss as misunderstanding: that the metric, the choice of attributes, and their weights look conventional, so that orderings of closeness are stipulated rather than discovered; that the shared space of possibilities required for comparison dissolves when successor theories carry new attributes; that comparative truthlikeness is inaccessible without already knowing the truth, so it cannot guide method or show any rule to be truth-conducive; and that the notion may collapse into predictive accuracy or empirical adequacy and so do no work that a constructive empiricist could not grant. Say which of these are live rather than merely logically possible, and whether my relativity clause has qualified the view into something unfalsifiable — and whether, for a claim meant to underwrite the idea that inquiry progresses, that would be a defect or just the price of precision.

Answer as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no concluding summary: the five strongest defeaters in descending order of decisiveness. In the last item, say which of your own verdicts about what is live versus merely logically possible you are least confident in, and what would change it.
