I sit on a municipal commission that has one year to recommend what to do with a backlog of contested commemorations: two statues, a school name, a couple of street names, a university hall named for a benefactor who also funded eugenics research, and a monument whose subject is unobjectionable but that was erected by a deposed regime partly as propaganda. We can only recommend; the council decides, and we will have to act while the disagreement about what we owe the past is still live. Before we hear from any more interested parties, I want a specification rather than a verdict.

Write the desiderata that a theory of commemoration would have to meet to be usable by a body like mine: the cases it must handle, the distinctions it must respect, the things about how public honoring actually works that it cannot contradict, and the reason each item is a genuine requirement rather than a prejudice of one camp. Among the claims I want you to test rather than assume: whether honoring is a zero-sum allocation of a scarce stock of public recognition, so that adding one name is a cost to others; whether a monument's meaning is fixed by its makers' intentions or can accumulate meaning from what later happens at the site; the difference between commemorating a victim, a perpetrator, and a collective such as a regiment, a company, or a founding generation, where no individual's record settles the case; whether removal, relocation, recontextualization in place, and adding a second monument nearby are genuinely distinct options or the same option with different costs; and what a commission owes a minority whose heroes keep losing under majority rule.

Then flag any pair of desiderata you think cannot be jointly satisfied — I suspect administrability by nonhistorians and case-by-case sensitivity pull apart, and that treating like cases alike conflicts with deferring to local attachment — and say which single requirement does the most to exclude candidate theories. You may score the usual strategies (leave everything, remove everything objectionable, contextualize in place, move to museums) against the list, but keep that to a few lines; the criteria are what I need.

Separate what is settled here from what is genuinely contested among people who work on the topic, and do not present a contested position as the consensus; where you are unsure, say so. Please write in British English.
