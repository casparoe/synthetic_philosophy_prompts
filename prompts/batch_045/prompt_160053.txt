My mother is in the later stages of Alzheimer's disease, and my brother and I keep going in circles about whether she is still in there. I have also been sitting meditation most mornings at a Buddhist center for the past year, and what I have absorbed there has shifted what I believe about persons. I am not a philosopher, and I am trying to write down where I have actually landed:

(1) There is no substantial or enduring self. What we call a person is a causally continuous stream of psychophysical events, and there is no further fact beyond that stream — no owner, no subject standing behind the events.

(2) Questions about whether some earlier or later person is me nonetheless have determinate answers, and those answers consist in facts about overlapping psychological continuity: memory, intention, character, and the causal links among them. It is a mistake to say these questions have no answer at all.

(3) My mother is still the same person she was, and what she said and wrote years ago — including a letter about what she would want if she were ever in this condition — still carries authority over what we do with her now.

(4) Special concern for my own future is rationally privileged: that it will be me who bears a future pain is a reason for me to weight it more heavily than an equivalent pain of a stranger, and someone who failed to would be missing a reason they have, not merely lacking a feeling.

(5) Taking (1) seriously has made my own death considerably less frightening, and I think that is the right response: once the substantial self is given up, death is not nearly the catastrophe it appears to be from the inside.

(6) I want to keep the idea that consequences are carried forward in the stream — that what I do now shapes what is suffered later — even though nothing transmigrates and there is no one downstream who remembers.

Please audit this set rather than survey the literature on it. Which commitments pull against each other, which only look like they do, and which pairs are genuinely fine? Order the real tensions by how badly they damage the package instead of treating them all alike, and for each say exactly what I would have to give up or add to repair it and what that repair costs me. I do not know where the trouble lies, so please do not assume I would sooner abandon any particular one. At the very end, on a line of its own, give \answer{...} naming the number of the single commitment whose revision would do the most to make the rest hang together.
