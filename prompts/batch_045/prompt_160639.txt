I sit on a small committee that recently had to decide whether to act on a single anonymously sourced report we had no way to corroborate: one source, no track record for this person, no independent evidence for or against the content. The argument kept collapsing into one epistemological question — whether a hearer needs positive reasons for taking the speaker to be reliable before the speaker's say-so can confer justification (the reductionist line), or whether testimony is a presumptive source that confers justification absent defeaters (the anti-reductionist line). Call the case I actually care about *cold-start testimony*: the hearer's first-ever testimony from a category of source about which they hold no generalization at all — no track record for this speaker, and no frequency evidence about speakers of this kind, so neither a local nor a global reductionist requirement could be satisfied by anything the hearer possesses.

Give me your credence, as a single number, that a hearer in a cold-start case can acquire justification from the testimony, and decompose it. I want rough probabilities for at least: that reductionism in some form is the correct general account of testimonial justification; that, if it is, the acquisition objection — that reductionism makes it impossible for any hearer ever to get testimonial justification started — is fatal to it; that justification here requires possessing evidence rather than merely lacking undefeated defeaters, and how much of the dispute that sub-question absorbs; that the case is genuinely instantiable for an adult, whose background track record may mean no real case is truly cold; and how much of what remains is purely verbal, turning on which notion of justification is in play. Combine them, name the term your final number is most sensitive to, say what one argument or finding would move it most, and tell me where you think the disagreement between my colleague at 0.8 and me at 0.3 most likely lives.

Separate clearly what is settled among people who work on testimony from what is live controversy; do not present a contested position as the consensus. Under 400 words.
