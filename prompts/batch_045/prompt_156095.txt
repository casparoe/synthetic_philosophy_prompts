I am a PhD student in philosophy, finishing a dissertation chapter on whether ascriptions of representation to artificial neural networks should be read literally or as useful shorthand. Over three years of a joint reading group with the ML department, my supervisor — a senior interpretability researcher who is, on the whole, a realist about at least some network states carrying content — has recorded verdicts on the following six cases. I have her verdicts but not an explicit criterion, and I would like to know what she will say about a seventh case that is about to come before the group.

Her track record, with the features she took to be decisive:

1. A one-layer transformer trained on modular addition. The residual stream carries sine/cosine features of the residues; rotating that clock by k rotates the model's answer by k. Verdict: yes, it represents the residue.
2. A code language model with a direction that linearly decodes whether the current token is inside a string literal; clamping it changes the model's quoting behavior, and the probe transfers to a programming language absent from training. Verdict: yes.
3. An image classifier in which a linear probe decodes "car present" from a mid-layer at 95%, while neither clamping nor ablating that direction perturbs behavior; the feature is a by-product of a texture circuit. Verdict: no — a readout artifact, not a representation.
4. A captioning model with a direction that decodes the disjunction "the image depicts a dog, or it is a photograph taken outdoors at night" at 96%, where clamping does steer captions toward dog-related or night-related words. Verdict: no — causal efficacy without a projectable content is not enough.
5. A grid-world navigation policy in which position is decodable only with a nonlinear probe, only during the middle of an episode (the probe fails at the first and last steps, where she took position to be undefined or already resolved), and redundantly, in two disjoint unit populations, either of which can be clamped to steer the next action; lesioning one population leaves behavior intact. Verdict: yes — redundancy is irrelevant to representational status.
6. A language model in which a direction linearly decodes the number of tokens remaining in the document, clamps steer, and the decoder transfers — but where intervention shows the downstream circuit is sensitive only to the coarse distinction "the document is nearly finished" and indifferent to fine-grained distance. Verdict: she declined yes-or-no and said instead that it represents the coarse variable, not the fine-grained one.

The new case. A 14-layer transformer trained on procedurally generated natural-deduction proofs in a toy formal language over a fixed stock of twelve premises, with a training distribution capped at twelve proof steps. From the layer-9 residual stream, a one-hidden-layer probe recovers the set of premises used so far at 94% exact match, where chance is under 1%; the best linear probe reaches 31%; the same probe architecture retrained at layer 7 reaches 11%; and a sparse autoencoder trained on layer 9 has roughly forty features whose union fires exactly for that set, with no single feature carrying it — the encoding appears to be superposed across a sixteen-dimensional subspace. Clamping the layer-9 projection onto that subspace to the value associated with a target set S makes the model, for 41 of 50 tested sets, continue the proof while citing only premises in S and never premises outside it; clamping at layer 5 does nothing; clamping onto a random direction of the same subspace at matched norm degrades fluency without systematic steering, so this is not a generic damage effect. On out-of-distribution inputs — proofs of twenty to thirty-five steps — the decoder collapses to 8% exact match, while clamping still steers correctly for 29 of 50 sets. Finally, and this is the wrinkle I care about most: a decoder trained on the same subspace for the complement — the set of premises not yet used — achieves 93% exact match, and because used and not-yet-used are complements throughout this dataset, no clamp can distinguish the two readings. The steering evidence is equally consistent with the circuit enforcing "used = S" and with its enforcing "unused = complement of S."

Please answer, in American English, the following. First, articulate the two-to-four candidate criteria for representationhood that fit her six verdicts and say what each predicts here — whether the layer-9 subspace represents the premise set, and with what determinacy of content. I expect at least one criterion to return a yes, at least one to return a no or a not-yet-established, and at least one to return a yes whose content ascription is indeterminate between the complementary variables; if you think the verdicts support other criteria, give them. Second, give your best guess at her actual verdict with the reasons for preferring it, and identify which of her earlier verdicts is the closest analog and which one is doing the most work in excluding criteria. Third, design the single further experiment, within this same setup, that would best separate the surviving criteria from one another — for instance by breaking the complementarity between the used and unused sets, or by varying whether the posit pays for itself in predicting held-out behavior — and say what each criterion predicts about its result. Finally, in two or three sentences, tell me what the divergence reveals about what her realism is a realism about: content, downstream sensitivity, or the usefulness of posits.
