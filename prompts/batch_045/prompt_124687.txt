Topic is worst-case versus average-case guarantees in alignment. Constraints: cheap analog materials only — index cards, dice, envelopes, a bag of colored beads, that kind of thing — and no real money. Whiteboard available, ten minutes of setup at either end.

Here's the sketch I have, please fix or replace whatever doesn't actually work:

Three hidden model types per round, assigned by drawing an envelope, and I want all three to have the same expected loss under the deployment distribution, so that anyone whose decision rule only tracks the mean literally cannot distinguish them: one that fails often and mildly, one that fails rarely and catastrophically, and a third that is statistically identicle to the first on the deployment distribution but has a catastrophic behavior planted on a rare slice of inputs that an adversarial chooser can steer toward. Then two scoring regimes across the two halves: one where the deployed model is fed i.i.d. inputs and teams are paid on average performance, and one where, after the team commits, another participant plays a chooser who picks inputs from the whole space including the rare slice, and a single catastrophe ends the round.
