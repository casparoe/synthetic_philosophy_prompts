I'm a distributed-systems engineer, fifteen years in, no philosophy training. I've been reading about Integrated Information Theory in my spare time because it's the only account of consciousness I've found that looks like it's written in something close to my own language — and that suspicion is exactly what I want tested.

Here is what I take IIT to claim, and I'd rather be corrected than humoured: a system is a conscious subject to the extent that its causal structure resists being decomposed into independent parts; Φ is the measure of that resistance; the subject is whichever subset of elements maximises it (the exclusion step); and what that subject undergoes is the full "shape" of its cause–effect structure, not a summary of its behaviour. As I understand the downstream commitments, a purely feed-forward circuit gets roughly zero no matter how many gates it has, a small ring of identical units that only copy their neighbours can score very high, and behaviour and usefulness are, in principle, irrelevant to the verdict.

Restate all of that, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary I actually work in: state machines, time-unrolled causal graphs, min-cuts, modularity and factorisation, erasure-correcting codes, replicas and consensus, description length, and the gap between a system's being in a state and an observer's being able to recover it. Please don't smuggle engineering assumptions in and call the result a translation — flag every place where you had to add one.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually want. Tell me which of these claims is something I already compute under another name; which survives only once I've quietly fixed a timescale, a grain of description, a noise model, or a cut metric; and which has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. My own guesses for the last category are the identity claim — that Φ *is* the experience rather than tracks it — and the idle ring. For each piece of residue, say whether my vocabulary is too thin to carry it or IIT has not yet said anything definite enough to carry.

One concrete probe. I built a crude proxy: normalised min-cut on the time-unrolled graph, defended as "how strongly this system resists being split into parts that evolve independently." It ranks a 64-gate feed-forward XOR tree above a 16-unit parity ring whenever every output depends on every input. If IIT ranks them the other way, is my proxy measuring the wrong quantity, or is integration in my sense genuinely a different property from integration in theirs — and which of the two is doing the real work in the consciousness claim?
