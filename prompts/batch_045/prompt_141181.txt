I want a number, and I want to see the machinery that produces it.

The claim under assessment:

(C) The dispute between presentism and eternalism is substantive rather than verbal — that is, once both sides are assumed to agree on every empirical fact and on a shared semantics for tensed and tenseless sentences, there remains a genuine metaphysical question on which they disagree, and what they disagree about is what there is, not merely which vocabulary to use.

Fix the resolution condition this way: count C as true if there is some residual question Q such that (i) both parties treat Q as having an answer, (ii) Q is not settled by fixing the total physical description of the world, and (iii) the presentist's and the eternalist's answers to Q differ in existential commitment and not only in phrasing. If you think C needs repair before it can bear a truth value at all, repair it, state what you changed, and give your number for the repaired claim.

Give me your credence in C, decomposed. At minimum I want separate probabilities, each with one line of justification, for:

1. That the disagreement can be stated without equivocation — that the presentist's claim that only present things exist is not simply a restricted quantification the eternalist can also grant, with the real fight displaced onto the semantics of tense.
2. That the disagreement does not collapse into a question about which structure in the world is metaphysically privileged, given that both sides can agree on which structures are physically privileged.
3. That considerations belonging to the phenomenology of temporal passage — the sense that time moves, the practical asymmetry between memory and anticipation, the apparent openness of the future — carry genuine evidential weight for a dynamical metaphysics, rather than being fully explicable within a static one.
4. That there is a reading of "passage" or "becoming" on which it is more than a metaphor, and on which the two views come apart.

Add any further term you think belongs, then show how the terms combine — multiply, constrain, or override one another — and give a single number.

Then a stipulated hypothetical. Suppose — this is not a real result, and I am not asking whether it is likely — that a successor to current fundamental physics is confirmed whose dynamical laws quantify over a distinguished foliation of spacetime into hypersurfaces, and that the foliation is empirically detectable: a small, systematic violation of the usual frame-independence in high-energy decay rates picks out one global slicing. Call this Detectable Foliation. How much does your credence in C move, in which direction, and why? I especially want to know whether the update lands on term (2), on the thought that a physically privileged slicing just converts the dispute into a dispute about whether the physically privileged slicing is also the metaphysically privileged one, or whether it lands somewhere else.

Then: which single term is your final number most sensitive to, and what one argument or result, if you encountered it, would move you most?

For calibration, my own credence in C is about 0.55 and a colleague's is about 0.85. On the decomposition above, say where you think the disagreement between us most likely lives.

Finally, keep two registers visibly separate throughout: what is settled or near-settled among people who actually work on this, and what is live controversy. Do not present a contested view as the consensus, and do not launder your own preferred position by describing it as what most people think.
