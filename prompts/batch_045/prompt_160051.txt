I'm a third-year PhD student in health economics. My dissertation is on equity weighting in distributional cost-effectiveness analysis, so the vocabulary I actually think in is this: QALYs and DALYs; health-state utility weights elicited by time trade-off and standard gamble, including states valued below zero and the respondents who refuse to trade any life years at all; incremental cost-effectiveness ratios; the cost-effectiveness threshold understood as the shadow price of a fixed budget rather than as a moral cutoff; net monetary benefit and cost-effectiveness acceptability curves; opportunity cost; and, one level up, utilitarian versus leximin social welfare functions, Arrow and Harsanyi, Rawls's lexical priority, and the representation results that tell you when an ordering can be written as a real-valued utility function and where continuity has to fail for it not to be.

I have been reading about a position in population ethics that I want to reconstruct in those terms, because I cannot tell whether it is saying something my framework cannot say or whether it is saying something I already say in different words. The position has three parts, and I would like each handled separately.

The first is an asymmetry: preventing suffering takes priority over producing happiness, in the strong sense that no amount of happiness produced can compensate for a fixed amount of suffering left unprevented. The second is a lexical threshold: there is a degree of severity such that suffering above that degree dominates everything below it lexically, so that no quantity of sub-threshold suffering or of pleasure, however large, can make up for a single instance of suffering above the degree. The point of the word lexical is meant to be that this is a real discontinuity rather than a very large weight: the compensating ratio is not large, it does not exist. The third is the background dispute about commensurability that the position needs in order to be stated at all: whether pleasure and pain are magnitudes of one hedonic dimension with a privileged point on it where the threshold sits, or whether they are two separate dimensions that cannot be placed on a common scale; and, whichever way that goes, whether the same scale is doing intrapersonal work (does a long stretch of mild pain compensate a moment of extreme pain?) and interpersonal work (does one individual's mild pain offset another's agony?).

Please translate this into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue: what carries over cleanly, what carries only if I let it be distorted, and what cannot be expressed at all. I want the translation kept honest in two specific respects. Do not quietly render the lexical threshold as a severity weight, a priority tier with a multiplier, or the familiar cost-per-QALY threshold; I know those are not lexical, and if the best my vocabulary can offer is a very large finite weight, say so and say what the substitution loses. And do not let the asymmetry become loss aversion or any other reference-dependent scalar: those are finite coefficients, and the view I am asking about is the limit in which the coefficient is not finite.

I am particularly interested in what happens to the arithmetic. QALY aggregation is additive and separable, across persons and across time, and it treats quality and duration as trading off linearly inside a single integral. A lexical view looks as though it requires a non-additive aggregator that is sensitive to the worst thing that occurs rather than to the total. Can the machinery I actually use — net benefit, incremental net monetary benefit, acceptability curves, discounting, the value of information — survive that substitution, is there a workable replacement such as allocating a fixed budget lexicographically by tier, or does optimization under a budget simply cease to be well defined? Relatedly: under uncertainty, a genuinely lexical ordering seems to make any nonzero probability of crossing the threshold dominate everything, which in my setting is the shape of a fanatical result. Does the budget constraint, the discipline that keeps health technology assessment from collapsing, defuse this, or does the disorder run deeper, because the view is stated over outcomes while every decision I know how to model is a choice among acts under a constraint?

The last thing I want is a verdict on the residue rather than a list. For each item that does not survive: is it evidence that the continuity and single-currency assumptions of my framework are doing substantive moral work that I have not noticed, or is it evidence that the original claim was less determinate than its vocabulary makes it look? And if the whole position turns out, on translation, to be something I already have a name for — a non-Archimedean social welfare function, leximin over moments of experience, maximin, something of that kind — tell me which, and tell me what having the name hides.

Write it as prose, one short section per part, then the residue and the verdict, about 1,000 to 1,500 words, and do not cite anything: I want the argument, not the literature.
