I'm part of this dumb online study group were we design model constitutions for fictional countries, and one of the other members, Priya, is completely obsessed with sortition and getting rid of career politicians. She's not an academic or anything, just really online about it, but she has firm verdicts on everything we float. Her verdicts so far: a randomly selected upper chamber serving one non-renewable five-year term, with full pay and real legislative power — yes, obviously; an elected lower house with a two-term limit — no, she called it a 'career ladder'; a lottery-picked advisory assembly that's unpaid and only meets for a year — no, just 'sortition theater' with no teeth; an appointed commission of experts with ten-year terms that can be renewed once — no, unelected and careerist; a randomly selected budget office with a single three-year term, a proper professional salary, and binding power over some spending — yes; and a sortition constitutional convention where members could be picked again after sitting out six years — no, because that would create 'professional sortition veterans'.

Now the group just put forward a new proposal and I want to guess what Priya will say before she logs on. The new one is for a unicameral city council that is *elected* — ranked-choice or whatever, the voting system doesn't matter — but with a strict single non-renewable six-year term, a salary set at the level of a mid-level civil servant, full authority to pass laws, and an extra rule that nobody who has ever held public office before is allowed to run, so in principle you literally cannot have a career politican. It's well paid, it has real power, and nobody can serve twice, but the members are elected rather than chosen by lottery.

I'm less interested in whether this is a good idea than in reverse-engineering Priya's rule. On one hand, everything she says suggests the real evil is reelection and a permanent political class, in which case she should love this: no reelection, no ex-officeholders, decent pay, real power. On the other hand, she might think the problem with elections isn't just careerism but that elections as such select for ambition, wealth, and unrepresentative people, so only actual sortition bodies are legitimate, meaning she'd hate this no matter how airtight the term limit is. Can you lay out the different rules that fit her existing verdicts equally well — the anti-careerism-plus-real-power rule, the sortition-purism rule, or anything else you can think of — say what each rule would predict about this elected single-term council, give your best guess at which one she's really running and why, and tell me what further case would tease the two rules apart (e.g., would it help to ask her about an elected body with a single term but no ban on former officeholders, or a sortition body that is advisory but well paid, or something else entirely)?
