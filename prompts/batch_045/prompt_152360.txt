My friend and I spent about three hours arguing about species last night — she works in systematics, I did ecology as an undergrad — and I've tried to write down where I actually landed, and now I'm worried it doesn't all fit together. Here's the list:

(1) Species are real things out there, not boxes we impose: there's an answer to "how many species are there in this group" that doesn't depend on us or on what we happen to be studying.
(2) A species is an individual, not a kind or a class — it has a beginning, an end and a history, more like an organism than like the set of all red things.
(3) For sexually reproducing animals, what makes a group a species is that its members actually interbreed with each other and don't with outsiders.
(4) Bacteria and asexual lizards come in species too, and I don't want to say the word means something completley different when it gets applied to them.
(5) I'm a pluralist: a field-guide author, a DNA-barcoding person and a behavioural ecologist can all be right at once even if they end up with different numbers of species for the same group.
(6) The whole point of a species name is that it licenses predictions — tell me something belongs to species X and I can say things about its chemistry, its diet, how it'll do in a drought. A grouping that doesn't support that kind of inference isn't doing scientific work.
(7) In the messy middle cases — the ring-species sort of setup, where A breeds with B and B with C but A won't breed with C — I'm happy to say there's just no fact of the matter about whether A and C are one species.

The thing that actually started the fight was a case: two populations of a bird on opposite sides of a big river, separated for maybe two million years, nearly identical to look at, never interbreed in the wild, but in captivity they pair up readily and raise healthy fertile chicks. I said one species, she said two. I thought I was just applying (3), but now I'm not sure that's what (3) says.

So: does this hang together? Which of these really collide, and which only look like they do? Please rank the genuine tensions by how bad they are instead of flagging all of them as equally fatal, and for each one say which commitment would have to give and what that would cost me. I'd rather not drop any of them, so I want to know which is cheapest to revise — and whether there's a single revision that would clean up more than one tension at a time.
