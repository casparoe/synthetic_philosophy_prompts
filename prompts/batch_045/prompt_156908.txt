Call them the generalists and the particularists. The generalists hold that conspiracy theories as a class carry a distinctive epistemic defect: they are partly defined by their opposition to an "official story", they characteristically inflate as they go (each item of contrary evidence gets redescribed as further evidence of the plot's reach), they are often structured so that no evidence anyone could actually acquire would settle them, and they require an expanding circle of people to keep quiet, so a distinctive kind of distrust — or at least a shifted burden of proof — attaches to them over and above whatever the evidence in the particular case happens to be. The particularists hold that a conspiracy theory is nothing but the theory that some event of public interest was brought about by a conspiracy, that it ought to be assessed exactly like any other empirical claim and against its rivals, that the pejorative charge the phrase carries is a sociological accident of who has deployed it and against whom, and that no general claim about the class can survive the existence of conspiracy theories that turned out true after being dismissed for exactly the reasons the generalists give.
