hey — i'm putting together the last ten minutes of a session on fairness in automated decision systems (recidivism-prediction style risk scores, base rates that differ across groups, scores that are supposed to mean something to the person reading them) and instead of one more slide with a venn diagram that doesn't close i want to end on a poem. i think a sestina is exactly the right form here and i want you to take a proper swing at it.

youre a philosopher of science who also writes formal verse, so take the form seriously: think carefully and brainstorm first — try out four or five candidate sets of six end-words before you commit, and tell me in a line or two what you rejected and why.

the idea: the six repeating end-words are six things a deployed risk score gets asked to be. i want at least these five in there — calibration, equalised odds / error-rate balance, predictive parity, independence (demographic parity), and the treat-similar-people-similarly individual fairness constraint — plus a sixth of your own choosing that is deliberately not a fairness criterion in the same sense (accuracy? the base rate itself? who-audits-the-auditors? you pick, and defend it). the sestina's rotation is the point: the same six terms come back in a different order in every stanza and no arrangement of them satisfies all six at once, which is more or less the actual impossibility result once base rates differ and the predictor isnt already perfect. i want that to be legible in the machine of the poem, not just asserted in it.

things i care about:

- 39 lines, real sestina, six stanzas of six plus a three-line envoi, end-words in the standard rotation (6-1-5-2-4-3), all six words in the envoi, two per line. i want the pairings chosen on purpose: tell me in one sentence which two you put together in the final line and what that pairing is supposed to mean.
- someone who has actually read the impossibility literature shouldnt wince. so be accurate about which pairs of criteria can and cant be had together — theyre not all incompatible with each other, and i'll notice if you imply they are — about the role unequal base rates play, and about the fact that calibration is a claim about what a score means rather then about who gets hurt. if a criterion had to be simplified to fit a line, that's fine, but flag it.
- no the-data-is-biased-so-the-model-is-biased filler, no algorithm-as-villain stuff, no shrugging punchline about how fairness is all subjective. the poem has to hold the genuinely uncomfortable bit: several of these are each independently morally defensible and you still cant have them all.
- it should work as a poem too. actual images, not six abstractions banging into each other. setting is yours — a parole board, triage, admissions, insurance, whatever lets the six words land.
- commit: make definite choices and give reasons. i'd rather have a poem with a position and a defence of it than a menu.

then a short note, a paragraph not an essay, on where you fudged: which criterion you had to distort to make it scan; where you leaned on a contested reading (whether individual fairness is really a rival to the statistical criteria or a different kind of thing altogether; whether leveling down is a live option or a reductio); and where the poem quietly picks a side the math doesnt force. where does it take liberties, basically — i want to know before i put it on a slide.
