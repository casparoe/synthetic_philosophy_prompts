hey so I want a parody internal memo, about 650-700 words, from the chief actuary of a life insurance company to the claims department, laying out how to adjudicate claims filed on behalf of people who've done the quantum-suicide setup — you know, the one where you rig a lethal device to a quantum event so that there's a branch where you die and a branch where you don't, and from your own point of view you only ever seem to end up in the branch where you survive, forever, while everyone else watches you die. Format it with a To/From/Date/Subject header and numbered sections, deadpan corporate voice throughout — no winking, no as-absurd-as-this-sounds, no narrator who knows this is ridiculous.

The jokes have to come from the physics and the decision theory being right, so that someone who knows the many-worlds literature laughs and someone who doesn't can still follow it. In particular get these in somewhere: the gap between first-person expectation and what outside observers see; the measure / Born-rule worry that the surviving branches have vanishingly small amplitude, so there's no reason to expect to find yourself in them; the fact that a successful run can't be reported back to anyone, since the only people who'd hear about it are in the branches where the experimenter is dead; the mangled-worlds version where the branches you survive in are increasingly damaged ones; and the point that the apparatus only insures you against that one quantum-triggered cause of death and not against anything else.

The company's legal move should be that the payout turns on third-party certification of death, not on subjective continuity — something along the lines of the company taking no position on whether the insured is alive, only on whether the payee is owed money. Then push that logic to a reductio with an actual product they're launching, e.g. a rider priced by branch amplitude (premiums scaling with the inverse square of the amplitude, benefit collectable only in branches where the insured survives) and let the memo notice, without ever saying it's absurd, that any policy the insured can personally collect on is unaffordable and any affordable one can only be collected by the widow. Include at least one quote from an uneasy outside consulting physicist they've retained, and end by announcing a pilot program whose acronym is the punchline.
