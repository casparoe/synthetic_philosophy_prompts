I am drafting a section arguing that the arrow of time cannot be identified with any merely local entropy gradient, and I need a case that forces the issue rather than one that merely illustrates it. Please build one to the following specifications.

The scenario. Describe, in a single paragraph plus a one-sentence entropy budget, a physically coherent arrangement at laboratory scale, inside one isolated system, in which a subsystem S contains a genuine agent while the thermodynamic entropy of S decreases monotonically over an interval long enough for that agent to run an experiment and store its result. The total entropy of the isolated system is to increase throughout. Constraints:

1. No exotic physics. The underlying dynamics are time-symmetric, nothing beyond ordinary thermodynamics is invoked, and the arrangement should at least be nomologically possible without new laws — ideally something a well-funded laboratory could build.
2. No cheats. No Maxwell demons, no Boltzmann fluctuations or other statistical accidents, no fine-tuning imposed by a future boundary condition, no backwards causation. The decrease of S's entropy must be robust and repeatable, and must be produced by machinery operating from the past.
3. The agent must earn its description. Its records must genuinely be records rather than accidental correlations, its predictions must be reliable, and its actions must make a difference to what happens later. None of this may be secured by stipulation.
4. The reversal must not rest on the agent's ignorance. The agent may know the complete physical description of the whole setup, including whatever absorbs the entropy that S exports.

The deliverables.

(a) Verdicts. Taking the direction of time for the agent as the thing in question, say which way each of the following three accounts says it points, and why, in two or three sentences each:
  (i) The entropic-gradient account: the arrow at a place is the direction in which thermodynamic entropy increases there. Read locally in S this points one way; read for the total system it points the other. Say whether this account can return any verdict once the two come apart, and what it would have to add in order to do so.
  (ii) The past-hypothesis-and-records account: the arrow is global, fixed by the universe's having begun in an extremely low-entropy macrostate, and the asymmetry of memory, evidence and inference is derived from that single boundary condition rather than from any local gradient.
  (iii) The agential or interventionist account: the arrow is the direction in which an agent's deliberations are open and its actions can still make a difference, as opposed to the direction it can only remember.

(b) The load-bearing feature. Name the single structural feature of your arrangement that does the work, and show that the case collapses without it: state exactly what goes wrong if S is sealed off and made genuinely isolated — whether it is the second law, the entropy bookkeeping for recording and erasing, or the agent's own operation that fails first.

(c) The best objection. Give the strongest reply available to someone who insists that the entropy gradient just is the arrow of time and that apparent local reversals are artifacts of how the system has been carved up. Then say whether your case can be thickened to meet that reply, or whether the reply merely relocates the dispute to the choice of which system's entropy is the one that counts.

(d) Honesty check. Tell me plainly whether the scenario generates the divergence between the three accounts from the physics, or whether one of the verdicts was smuggled in by the way you described the agent or by where you chose to draw the boundary of S. If it was smuggled, identify the stipulation responsible and say what a non-question-begging version of the case would have to supply instead.
