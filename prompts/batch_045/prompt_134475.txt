Me and a friend spent like two hours last night going in circles about whether anything a machine does is genuinely about anything, or whether its all second-hand — the way the words in a book mean something only because we mean something by them, or the way a thermostat's needle counts as reading a temperature only because somebody built it and somebody interprets it.

The bit we couldnt get past was this move he kept making. He says the machine's content is borrowed, so it isnt real content. I said fine, then where does ours come from: trace it back and you land on natural selection, or on the language community, or on whatever causal process shaped your brain — and none of those is a thing that understands anything either. So either borrowing doesnt need a lender who understands, in which case the machine is fine, or it does, and we're in the same boat. He called that tu quoque. I said it isnt about him being a hypocrite, it's that whatever test he uses to exclude the machine seems to exclude us too.

Two wrinkles we noticed and couldnt handle. (1) There look like three different kinds of lender: something that understands, like a person; something that has purposes or goals but doesnt understand, like natural selection or a whole community or a company; and something that merely has effects, like erosion making a rock look like a face. Which of those can actually lend content, and is that even the right question to be asking? (2) The chain isnt a chain, it loops — my words mean what they do because of the community, the community is nothing over and above its members, each member learned from the previous generation. Nobody ever deposits anything; it's a line of credit with no money behind it. Does derivation have to bottom out, or can borrowing be circular?

Is there a name for this whole shape and not just the one move — someone says X's content is second-hand, the other person shows the same argument lands on the first person's own content, and then you're stuck saying what a real source of content would have to be like and whether the chain has to terminate? It feels adjacent to tu quoque and adjacent to the problem of other minds but neither is quite it. If nothing established fits, coin something and tell me where its edges are. (I gather Searle and Dennett fought about roughly this, so tell me if I'm reinventing something.)
