I'm in the first term of a PhD and writing the paper for a reading group on self-deception, and the group has split in a way I find suspicious: both sides reach for what is, as far as I can tell, the very same move. They say that self-deception is deception, the ordinary interpersonal kind, except that both parties happen to live inside one skull — one part of the person plays the deceiver, another plays the dupe. Various versions are in circulation: the psychoanalytic one where a censor keeps unacceptable material out of consciousness; the commonsense one where someone "believes his own press releases" or has "drunk his own Kool-Aid"; the committee-of-the-mind version where sub-agents negotiate; and a newer suggestion from my supervisor, who dislikes homunculi and proposes instead that the role of the deceiver is played by a sub-personal gating system, something like a spam filter or an over-zealous press office that diverts inconvenient evidence away from the person's belief-forming system before it ever registers as a reason.
