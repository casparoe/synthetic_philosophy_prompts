The question I set was:

> Suppose neurophysiology discovered that in adult humans some occurrent mental states are, at a single time in a single subject, fully realized by each of two non-overlapping neural populations: each population on its own occupies the complete causal role characteristic of that mental state, and disabling either one while leaving the other intact changes nothing in the subject's psychology or behaviour. Call such a state co-tenant. In no more than 500 words: (a) Does co-tenancy cause trouble for the type-identity theory that ordinary cross-species multiple realizability does not, and why? (b) Does it threaten token-identity? (c) A critic objects that once realization is allowed to be many-to-one, functionalism loses its grip, since for any physical system and any causal role there is some description under which that system occupies the role — so everything realizes everything. Assess this "promiscuous realization" objection and the best reply available. (d) Can the identity theorist escape (a) by identifying the mental type with a disjunction of physical types?

The student's answer:

> Ordinary multiple realizability says that one mental type can be realized by many physically unrelated types, so no single physical type can be identical with it. But the identity theorist has a standard hedge: identity may fail globally while still holding locally — within a species, or within a single subject at a single time, one physical type may do all the work. Co-tenancy removes that hedge, and that is why it bites harder than the cross-species cases: here even a single subject at a single moment supplies two distinct physical realizers, so there is no local type-identity left to retreat to.
>
> (b) Co-tenancy also settles the token question. Token-identity is the claim that each mental particular is identical with exactly one physical particular; co-tenancy gives us one mental particular with two, so token-identity is false. Functionalists who say they reject type-identity but keep token-identity are kidding themselves: once realization is many-to-one at the level of particulars, realization cannot be identity at any level, and the mental particular must be a distinct, higher-level item over and above both neural populations.
>
> (c) The promiscuous-realization objection is serious but answerable. If any system can be described as occupying any role, functionalism individuates nothing — a mousetrap realizes belief under some mapping. The functionalist's reply must be to constrain realization rather than to abandon multiple realizability: the realizer has to possess the causal powers the role demands, and the mapping has to be one that supports the right counterfactuals rather than a gerrymandered one. The residual difficulty is saying whose specification of the theory fixes the role; I take this to be the Ramsey-sentence issue, and I do not think it is fatal here.
>
> (d) The disjunctive fallback fails. Pain = (C-fibre firing or silicon state S or …) is true only if the disjunction is complete, and we can never enumerate every possible realizer, so the identity is in principle unfinishable. Besides, disjunctive predicates are not observational predicates, so the claim is no longer the empirical, a posteriori identification the identity theorist advertised.
