I teach a graduate seminar on the epistemology of long-term technological forecasting, and we have reached the debate over how fast an intelligence explosion would unfold once machine systems can do AI research. I want the students to see a credence with its joints exposed, so please give me a number on one fixed proposition rather than a survey of the literature.

Proposition P: conditional on (i) systems that perform the cognitive work of a competent AI research engineer at or above human level being deployed within the next fifteen years, and (ii) no globally enforced restriction on training runs or on the automated production of new systems, the interval between the first system that substantially automates AI research and the arrival of systems that decisively outperform the best humans across essentially all cognitive domains is under two years.

Give me your single number, then decompose it. I have in mind these factors, but add any you think matter more: whether gains from automating research compound smoothly or hit steep diminishing returns once the easily-discovered algorithmic improvements are exhausted; whether progress in AI has depended less on the raw capability of individual researchers than on many semi-independent groups tinkering empirically, in which case concentrating research in a few automated labs could slow rather than accelerate it; how much of the remaining difficulty is a verifiability ceiling, where further capability awaits better feedback signals and evaluation environments rather than more intelligence per se; how much wall-clock latency is irreducible because of serial bottlenecks outside cognition, such as fabrication, physical experimentation and institutional decision cycles; and how fast capital and compute could actually be scaled up, as opposed to how fast the algorithms could improve.

For each term give a rough probability and one line of justification, say how you are combining them and why that combination rule rather than a weakest-link or multiplicative one, flag any dependence between the terms you are treating as independent, and identify which single term your final number is most sensitive to. I also want two meta-questions answered briefly, because they belong in the seminar: how much of your uncertainty is really uncertainty about whether the proposition is well-posed, given that the endpoints are hard to operationalize and we have no observed case of a takeoff to calibrate against; and which of these factors you think is most neglected relative to how much it moves the answer.
