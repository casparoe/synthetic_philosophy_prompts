I teach a first-year seminar on infinity, and I want to close it with Borges's "The Library of Babel" rather than with another paper. Before I commit a session to it, I need to know how much of the story's philosophical weight actually rests on the Library's being infinite.

Here is the worry. Borges fixes the alphabet at twenty-five orthographic symbols and fixes the length of a volume — four hundred and ten pages, forty lines to the page, around eighty characters to the line — so the number of distinct books the Library can contain is finite, though it is far larger than anything the physical universe could instantiate. If the Library is nevertheless infinite, its infinity is entirely an infinity of copies. And nothing the story seems to trade on requires a second copy of anything: that every possible book exists somewhere; that truth is everywhere and indistinguishable from forgery; that the useful is drowned in the useless; that a complete catalogue would have to be one more 410-page book and so, seemingly, cannot be one.

So: is the repetition doing work I am missing? The narrator's closing thought is that the Library is periodic, that an eternal traveler would in time see the same volumes recur in the same disorder, and that this recurrence is itself an order. Does that thought need actual infinity — Dedekind-infinity, parts as large as the whole, Hilbert's-Hotel-style rearrangement — or only an enormous finite?

And how would a strict finitist, someone who refuses not just infinite totalities but finite numbers we cannot exhibit, read the story's own hedged estimate of the Library's size in its opening sentence? Is that hedge finitist caution, or does the narrator commit himself to actual infinity regardless?

Then be honest about where the analogy with my syllabus fails: is the Library the wrong picture of Cantor's infinite — countable, hotel-shaped, with no diagonal argument available over the books themselves — and is the catalogue that must contain itself really a diagonalization, or something closer to the liar?
