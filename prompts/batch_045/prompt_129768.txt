In a methodology reading group I keep falling back on the same analogy: a model stands to its target system as a map stands to the terrain. A map is valuable precisely because of what it leaves out; a map drawn at one-to-one scale would be as large as the country it depicts and would serve no purpose. The Underground diagram is our favourite example, since it is deliberately false about distances and directions and is nevertheless the best tool for the job.

One member of the group uses this to defend the strongest version of Friedman's as-if methodology: not merely that descriptively false assumptions are acceptable in a theory that predicts well, but that unrealistic assumptions are a mark of a theory's power, and that objecting that a model's agents are nothing like real people is like objecting that the Underground diagram misstates the distance between two stations.

I want that analogy audited rather than accepted or rejected. Give the correspondence explicitly — what plays the role of the terrain, of the scale, of the projection, of the legend, of route-finding, of the user knowing what the map is for — and then say where the mapping breaks, separating the disanalogies that matter for the work it is doing here from those that do not.

For the ones that matter, I find it helpful to sort a model's false assumptions into two kinds: those whose effect on a prediction we could in principle undo, because we have a definite recipe for correcting the prediction once the assumption is dropped, the way a projection's distortion can be inverted; and those for which we have no such recipe, so that the assumption does real predictive work and nobody knows what removing it would do. Call them dischargeable and non-dischargeable. Does the map analogy only cover the dischargeable kind, and does the answer to that question decide whether the strong as-if conclusion survives?

Finally: is there a better analogy for a model that carries its falsehoods non-dischargeably, or is the map the best available and the lesson that we should stop asking it to carry the F-twist at all?
