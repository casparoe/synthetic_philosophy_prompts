I spent most of last night arguing with a friend — a systems engineer, not a philosopher — about an incident report he helped write, and I came away convinced that neither of us had a grip on what we were actually disagreeing about. I want to keep the argument going against a much sharper version of his position than he was able to give me.

The incident, as he described it. A patient in an intensive care unit was on a ventilator. The unit's policy is that ventilator alarms are routed to the on-call nurse's pager, because the bedside audible alarm is suppressed by a night-mode profile. The routing passes through a paging gateway whose outbound TLS certificate expired at 02:14. The gateway's retry logic treats a TLS handshake failure as a client error and therefore non-retryable, so it discarded the alarm and wrote one line to a debug log. At 04:05 the ventilator raised a high-pressure alarm; the nurse's pager never received it. She learned of the alarm at 06:40 on rounds, by which time the patient had suffered hypoxic brain injury. The hospital's root-cause report says: the delay in response was caused by the gateway's failure to deliver the alarm. The platform team's own retrospective says: the cause was that we did not monitor certificate expiry. The vendor's statement says: the device alarmed correctly.

My friend's position was that none of these "did not"s are causes. What caused the injury, he said, was a positive process: the airway obstruction, the fall in blood oxygen, and the expiry of a certificate at 02:14, which is a state change, not an absence. Everything else in those reports is a description of what did not happen, and nothing is caused by what did not happen. When I pointed out that the gateway's discarding the page is itself a positive event — a branch taken, a socket closed, a log line written — he said that event is not what harmed the patient; it is only a marker of where an intervention failed to occur. When I asked what did harm the patient, he said: the hypoxic process, which is exactly the process the nurse's response would have interrupted. So, he claimed, there is always a positive realizer available, and "causation by omission" is shorthand for a counterfactual truth about what would have interrupted the realizer, plus a normative truth about who was supposed to act.

I think that last move is where he is vulnerable, and I want to press it. Please play a philosopher who holds his conclusion — no omission, no absence, no failure-to-act is ever a cause — but who holds it properly, with the resources I've described and better ones you can supply. Argue the position; do not name philosophers or papers. You may concede small points, and you should concede them when they are true, but you may not concede the main claim, and you may not retreat to saying that it depends on what we mean by "cause." You are not allowed to lean on the stock examples — no plant nobody watered, no dog that did not bark, no poisoner who refrained; if you want an illustration, take it from the incident or build a new one. Stay in first person, keep each turn to roughly one or two short paragraphs, and stay in character until I say "stop." Treat the incident exactly as I have given it; if you need some further fact about it, ask me for it in one sentence at the end of your turn rather than inventing it.

Here is my opening. Your realizer maneuver is circular, or at least it borrows from the account it is meant to replace. To identify the fall in blood oxygen as the realizer, you already have to know which process the omitted action would have interrupted — the counterfactual does the selecting, and the positive process is wherever the counterfactual lands. And once you allow the realizer to be simply the process that would have killed him anyway, your account returns the same verdict for the on-call nurse and for a stranger in another city who also did not page her: same realizer, same harm, and yet only one of those non-actions is the thing the report is interested in. So answer me this: in the paging incident, which positive process is the realizer, and what work does it do that the counterfactual and the duty were not already doing?
