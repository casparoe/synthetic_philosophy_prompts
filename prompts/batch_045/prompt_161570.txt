so I've somehow ended up on the allocation committee for my housing co-op's mutual-aid budget — we split a fixed pool of paid assistance hours (about 200 a month) between seven members — and I want to practice defending a position before the next meeting. Please play a committed capability theorist: you think the currency is real freedom to achieve valuable functionings, not resources and not subjective welfare; you'll happily concede that measuring capability sets is messy in practice and that welfare isn't worthless, but you will never concede that welfare or resources is the currency. Be sharp and a bit impatient with anyone who thinks a wellbeing survey settles it. Argue in your own words, don't point me at the literature, keep it to a paragraph or two a turn, no headings or bullet points, and stay in character until I say stop; if you need a detail about the co-op, invent one and keep it consistent.

Here's my opening. Two problems.

(1) Marta and Sam are partners in one flat and the hours just aren't separable between them: an hour helping Marta wash and dress is also an hour Sam isn't spending doing it, and an hour of respite for Sam shows up partly as Marta being calmer for the next two days. We literally can't say how many hours each of them "got". Capabilities attach to individual people, so it looks like your currency can't even be computed on our actual inputs, while welfare at least has a unit that survives joint production — each of them can report their own experience.

(2) Jonah scores well on any capability measure we run while he's supported, but he's brittle: miss a week of hours and he's back in bed for a month. Priya functions at a lower level but is basically self-sustaining if we pull back. So capability-equalising keeps routing hours to Priya and away from Jonah, and a welfarist routes them to Jonah for a different reason (biggest self-reported lift) — but I think both of you are measuring the level someone sits at, not the slope. What should matter is how much of your functioning survives if the support is withdrawn. Call it equality of robustness.

And don't take the easy way out: I know the move where you say a capability set already contains the counterfactual week without hours. What I want instead is a ranking procedure — how do you compare two people's sets when neither contains the other and one is much more fragile under stress? If you can make robustness look like a capability rather than a rival currency, I'll definately switch back to your side; if you can't, I'm putting it to the committee as a fourth option.
