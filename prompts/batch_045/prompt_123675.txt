You are a professional philosopher of mind with research-level command of the contemporary panpsychism and Russellian-monism literature: the combination problem in its subject-, quality- and structure-combination forms, panqualityism, phenomenal-bonding replies, cosmopsychism and its mirror-image decombination problem, the structural-coherence arguments, and the surrounding dispute about whether any of this is dialectically better off than a straightforward emergentism. Please think carefully about the task below before answering, and begin with a short unstructured brainstorm in which you generate more candidate requirements than you will keep, say which you discarded and why, and only then commit to the final list.

I am writing a paper on the combination problem, and I want to fix the standard of adequacy before I read any more proposed solutions. So this is not a request for a survey, and I do not need citations, a bibliography or a reading list: argue from the cases, which I take to be the load-bearing part. Please write the whole answer in British English.

The purpose a solution is to serve — and I am fixing this, since adequacy is relative to it — is narrow: a solution should leave micropsychist panpsychism a genuine rival to physicalism about consciousness, rather than either (a) physicalism with an extra posit bolted on, or (b) emergentism relocated one level down. That is, whatever work is done at the macro level must not quietly reintroduce the brute emergence of macro-phenomenal properties from non-phenomenal ones, and it must not detach the mental from the physical in a way that forfeits the Russellian motivation (that micro-phenomenal properties are the categorical grounds of microphysical dispositions, and so earn their place in physical theory rather than being added to it).

What I want from you is the specification. Give me the numbered list of requirements that any account of how micro-phenomenal properties and micro-subjects yield macro-phenomenal properties and macro-subjects must meet, with the concrete case or consideration behind each, and mark for each requirement whether it is non-negotiable given the purpose above or merely a preference of one camp. Where you think a requirement I or the literature treats as mandatory is really a prejudice — something only, say, a Russellian monist, or only an opponent of primitive relations, would insist on — say so, and say what follows if we drop it.

Here is my own draft list, which I expect you to prune, repair, split, merge and extend rather than simply endorse:

1. No upstairs emergence: the macro-phenomenal facts must be wholly grounded in the micro-phenomenal facts, on pain of surrendering the advantage over emergentism.
2. No brute subject-summing: the theory must say, for any collection of micro-subjects, whether it constitutes a further subject, and why, without helping itself to the creation of a new subject ex nihilo.
3. Palette adequacy: the micro-phenomenal quality space must be rich and structured enough to yield the macro palette — colour, smell, the specific phenomenal character of a toothache — rather than only some thin residue of it.
4. Structural fit: the combining relation must explain why the structure of macro experience matches the structure of the physical goings-on it is supposed to go with, rather than merely accompanying it.
5. The dormancy constraint (mine, and the one I care about most): ordinary macro-consciousness is graded, and sometimes wholly absent — dreamless sleep, general anaesthesia, coma — while the constituent micro-subjects and their broad physical arrangement persist. So combination must be defeasible and scalar: the theory must say what it is about a sleeping brain that un-combines its micro-subjects, without those micro-subjects winking out of existence, and it must say why the micro-phenomenal activity does not simply go to waste.
6. The partial-unity constraint: callosal section, hemispherectomy, and the graded recovery that follows them suggest that unity admits of degrees and can be partly disrupted. Whatever relation does the combining must be one that can hold partially, and ideally one that predicts in which surgical and developmental configurations there is one subject, two, or none.
7. The introspective-silence constraint: my evidence is evidence about one subject, and it contains no hint of the teeming micro-legion — neither introspectively nor by inference from what neuroscience tells me. The theory must explain that asymmetry, and say what could in principle count as evidence for its micro-phenomenal claims.
8. The composition-tracking constraint: if the Russellian motivation is to be preserved, the combining relation must line up with physical composition and causal integration, not cross-cut it.
9. The plenitude constraint: the relation must not be gerrymandered to brains. It must tell us whether nations, livers, termite mounds and galaxies have subjects, and bite whatever bullet its answer carries.

Three further things I want. First: flag any subset of the final list that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — I anticipate trouble, for instance, between the dormancy constraint and any account that identifies the macro subject with one of the micro-subjects, and between composition-tracking and plenitude — and if the list itself turns out to be the significant result, say so plainly. Second: tell me which single requirement does the most exclusionary work, i.e. which one knocks out the largest share of the live options. Third: say whether the bonding relation is also supposed to do the work of diachronic unity, whether that is a feature or a cost, and what it commits us to about ordinary persistence through dreamless sleep.

Then, and only briefly — a table of a dozen lines or so, not an essay — score the following against your list: a primitive phenomenal-bonding relation; panqualityism, on which the fundamentals are qualities rather than subjects and subjects come in at a later stage; cosmopsychism with a decombination story; and a view I have been toying with myself, which I will call the attenuation view: micro-phenomenal properties are everywhere, but a macro subject exists only where a set of micro-subjects stands in relations of sufficiently dense causal integration, the degree of macro-consciousness scales with the density and reach of that integration, and sleep and anaesthesia are cases of falling below threshold rather than of anything being lost — the macro subject being realised by, not composed of, the maximally integrated collection. Tell me whether the attenuation view is panpsychism at all or something else in phenomenal dress, and which requirement kills it.
