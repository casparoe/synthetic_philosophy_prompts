Two people from my consciousness reading group kept going after the session ended last night and I got drafted as referee without having a view, so I'm passing it on: can you adjudicate move by move?

Maya defends the higher-order thought theory, Devin doesn't. (1) Devin opens with the rock objection: if a thought about a state is what makes that state conscious, then a thought about a rock should make the rock conscious, which is absurd, so the view is either wrong or incomplete. (2) Maya: no — a higher-order thought makes a first-order *mental* state conscious, and only when it's caused by that state in the right non-inferential way, by whatever mechanism normally subserves introspection. A rock has no states to be made conscious and no such mechanism, so nothing follows. (3) Devin: the causal clause is where the view quietly cashes out. Either "in the right way" gets spelled out in non-mental terms, and then I can build a device that satisfies it for a rock or a thermometer; or it gets spelled out as "in whatever way makes the target state conscious," which just renames the thing you were supposed to explain. (4) Maya: not circular. Compare perception — seeing x requires x to cause your visual state via a working visual system in suitable conditions, and that's an empirical causal condition, not a restatement of seeing. Introspection can work the same way: the right causal way is "produced by the system that tracks your own occurrent states." (5) Devin: perception's causal condition is answerable to something independent, the distal object; yours isn't, and the independence you need is exactly what's in question. Test case: an "introspective prosthesis," an implant that reads off someone's early visual states and writes into their global workspace the non-inferential, counterfactually-dependent higher-order state "I am now in visual state V." It's not their native introspection system, it's a gadget wired in from outside, and their reports and reasoning shift accordingly. If you say the visual state is now conscious, consciousness has become something you can plug in from outside and the causal clause was never doing any real work. If you say no, then every reason I can think of — it has to be the subject's own mechanism, it has to govern their reasoning and action, it has to be produced by the very system whose states they are — either imports a first-order condition or restates the intuition. (6) Maya: I say no, and the reason is principled: consciousness is the subject's being conscious of the state, so the higher-order state has to be theirs, available to and governing their reasoning and agency as a whole. Teh prosthesis's output is an input from outside, like being told. (7) Devin: that's a for-me-ness condition that isn't in the theory as stated, and once you add it the higher-order state stops constituting consciousness and becomes just another state that's conscious because of something else — at which point the higher-order machinery is decorative and you owe me a first-order story.

Who's right about what at each step, and what are both of them missing — is there a reply Maya had available that she didn't make, or a version of the prosthesis case that's actually decisive rather than just intuition-pumping?
