Here's the practice as I understand it. Please take my description at face value and argue from it, even if I've got some detail slightly wrong.

A transport ministry wants to know whether a new rail line is worth building. Nearly everything on the benefit side is stuff nobody buys and sells: an hour of travel time saved, a small reduction in the chance of dying in a crash, cleaner air, a bit of quiet. So the numbers get backed out of behaviour. How much extra do people pay to travel faster — tolled road versus free road, fast train versus slow bus? How much extra wages do workers in dangerous jobs get paid compared with similar workers in safe ones? From that you get a value per hour and a value per statistical life. Then the same number gets applied to everybody: your hour is worth what my hour is worth, regardless of how much either of us earns. But the number is scaled up every year in line with income growth, so a life in 2050 is worth more than a life today. Benefits on the far side of a few decades get discounted at a few percent a year. Everything is added up in money, compared to costs, and if the total is bigger the project goes ahead — and nobody ever has to actually pay anything to the people who lose out, it's enough that the winners could in principle compensate them.

Two wrinkles. First: for things with no market at all but that people obviously care about — how far you can see from a trail, whether a rare bird goes extinct — the same government runs surveys and asks people what they would pay, and uses the answers. But when people are asked what they would need to be paid to accept losing something they already have, those numbers come back several times bigger and get thrown out as inflated and hypothetical. Second: the same agencies that treat your choices as the evidence of what's good for you when you're a commuter or a worker treat your choices as something to be corrected when you're a smoker, a saver or a snacker — sin taxes, automatic enrolment in pensions, calorie labels, nudges everywhere. My choices reveal my utility in one filing cabinet and corrupt it in another.
