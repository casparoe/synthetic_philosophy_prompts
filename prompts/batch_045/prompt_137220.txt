hey — I run a first-year discussion section on sex, gender and society, and I want to hand out a flowchart instead of yet another reading.

The topic is that much-discussed cross-national pattern where countries that score high on gender-equality indices tend to have a *smaller* share of women among their STEM graduates than countries that score lower. I want a decision tree that walks a student from "ok, assume the pattern is real" to one of about seven named positions — but built from the logical space first, not from a list of labels I already have in mind.

To force the tree to do real work, I've invented four findings from a made-up 2025 dataset. Every branch should be a reaction to these, not to textbook summaries:

(a) The pattern replicates *within* countries over time: as a country's equality score rose across three decades, the female share of its engineering degrees fell.
(b) It's driven almost entirely by engineering and computer science. In biology and medicine the female share *rises* with equality; in maths it's flat.
(c) The gap in "I am good at maths" self-ratings between 15-year-old boys and girls is *wider* in the highest-equality countries, even though the gap in actual test scores is narrower there.
(d) The female share of engineering degrees tracks, inversely, how well women are represented in that country's *other* high-status professions — law, medicine, journalism. Where women are thick on the ground in those, they're thin in engineering.

Shape of what I want:

- At most seven internal nodes, each phrased as a concrete case or a bet about what some further data would show — never as "do you think it's nature or nurture?" A student who's never heard any of the position names should be able to answer each one. One node has to be about what an aggregate cross-national pattern licenses you to say about a particular 16-year-old who loves engineering.
- Seven leaves. Each gets a name (or "unnamed, as far as I can tell"), one sentence on what it predicts about (a)–(d), and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Please definitely include: the measurement-skeptic leaf; the "STEM as escape hatch / absolute-vs-relative payoffs" leaf; the "affluence and equality let intrinsic preferences show through" leaf; the adaptive-preferences leaf; a backlash or identity-threat leaf; something like a comparative-advantage-sorting leaf; and one leaf for "the pattern is real and social and still nothing follows about what anyone is owed."
- Please make the nodes do real discriminating work between those last few — especially the intrinsic-preference leaf and the comparative-advantage leaf, which I suspect your tree can't actually separate. Flag any such pair rather than quietly splitting them.
- After the leaves: say how many answer-combinations your seven questions formally allow, which ones are unoccupied, and for each empty one whether it's empty because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. I'm interested in the cell where someone grants the pattern is real and substantially biological *and* still demands aggressive intervention, and in the cell where someone thinks it's an artifact but keeps the full-strength justice claim.
- Don't let the justice question become an eighth node that blows past seven leaves. Instead give each leaf a one-line "what it implies about what we owe" tag, and say outright where your tree forces two leaves into the same verdict even though the people who hold them would hate that.

Audience is first-years with no background: gloss any term like "comparative advantage" or "adaptive preference" in passing. And no bibliography — I don't want reading lists, just the tree.
