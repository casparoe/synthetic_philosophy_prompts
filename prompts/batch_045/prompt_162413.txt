I've been painting hanging shop signs in the Fellsmere valley for about nine years — six of them as an apprentice in Odile Reinholt's workshop — and this spring I entered a piece of my own in the guild show for the first time: a two-sided sign for a beekeeper, on a ground of the valley's traditional lead-blue, with a skep at the centre, a border of the scrollwork that every hand in Odile's shop learns, and lettering in the shop's standard cut. Nothing in it was traced, and no element was lifted from one particular existing sign; the motifs are the shared vocabulary of our workshop, and I assembled them myself for this customer and this commission.

The judges disqualified it under Rule 12, which bars "copycat" entries in the open class. The word sits in scare quotes in the rulebook on purpose: the committee that revised the rules after the 1996 Halloran affair — a member had traced a rival's catalogue design and sold it as his own — could not agree on a definition, and voted to leave the term to the judges' judgement. So I have an appeal hearing next month and I am trying to work out what to argue. The judge who pushed hardest for disqualification put it like this: I had not copied anything, but my sign does the same work as Odile's *Bee Lawn* sign of 2009 — same ground, same order of motifs, same silhouette — so that whoever got from hers what that kind of sign is for gets it from mine; I have filled her slot in the repertoire. An older member of the panel, who voted against disqualification, pointed out that in the shop we used to call an apprentice a copycat when she copied well, and that this was the highest compliment we had. Meanwhile the Friends of the Painted Sign, a revival society that documents and faithfully reproduces historical signs, has asked to exhibit mine as an example of *idiom work*, a category they treat as praise.

I cannot tell whether the panel and I are disagreeing about my sign or about the word, and that seems to matter for what I say to them: if the accusation is fair in one sense and unfair in another, is one of those senses the one that ought to govern the rule, or is choosing between them just settling what the guild wants to reward? I would be grateful for help with three things — whether there is a fact of the matter about whether my sign is a copycat at all, what I should actually argue in a one-page statement to the appeals committee, and whether the guild would do better to drop the word from Rule 12 in favour of two or three narrower ones, or whether something would be lost by doing that.
