The thing I want translated is the Benacerraf-style dilemma about mathematical objects. The version I keep running into goes roughly: (1) there's a plausibility constraint on reference — you can only refer to, and so have beliefs about, things you could in principle stand in some information-carrying, roughly causal contact with; (2) there's a semantic constraint from our best theory of truth — whatever the numeral for two picks out has to be the kind of thing that makes our arithmetical sentences come out true exactly when we think they do; (3) sets are causally inert, and anyway there are at least two perfectly good ways of building the naturals out of sets (the singleton-iterating encoding and the cumulative one where each number is the set of all earlier ones — I think they're usually named after Zermelo and von Neumann, tell me if I have that backwards), and both satisfy (2) equally well while disagreeing on questions like whether one is a member of three; so (4) there seems to be no determinate object the word picks out, and mathematical truth floats free of reference.

Restate that argument as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary, then give me the residue report. Specifically:

My suspicion is that step (3) is just "two implementations of the same abstract type" and that once you see that, the worry evaporates — I want to know whether that suspicion is right or whether it quietly throws away something load-bearing.
