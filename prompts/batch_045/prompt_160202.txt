I am writing a paper on the Death in Damascus family of cases and I need a new case built to order for the fourth section. In the canonical version the forecast is perfect: on the way I set it up, the evidential theorist's own act is bad news either way, while the causal theorist treats the expectation as already fixed and therefore sees fleeing as worth a gamble. My section argues that this disagreement is not an artifact of infallible foresight but changes character once the forecast is imperfect and only partly disclosed to the agent. To make that argument I need a case of a shape I have started calling a gated forecast: the agent's evidence about what the pursuer expects arrives through a channel that is informative but not transparent, so the act-to-forecast correlation is only partly screened off by what the agent knows.

Build me such a case to the following specifications.

Setting and realism. A contemporary, non-mythological scenario in one paragraph: a person who has reason to think someone is coming for them tomorrow, with exactly two options, staying in their own city or travelling to one named alternative. The pursuer is human and his advantage is ordinary, a dossier, a data trail, informants, a pattern-of-life file, not prophecy. No demons, no oracles, no perfect predictors, no exotic physics, and no hit rate better than a shrewd and well-informed human could achieve.

Asymmetric detection, motivated by the story. State two conditional hit rates: the probability that the pursuer's filed expectation names the city you are in, given you stay, and given you flee. They must differ, and the difference must fall out of the story rather than being asserted, for example because staying leaves you at a known address with a known routine while fleeing both leaves a booking trail and disperses you among many possible destinations. Also state the unconditional or base rate with which the pursuer files your own city, so that the causal calculation has an input it does not have to borrow from the agent's own act probabilities.

The gate. Before choosing, the agent gets an imperfect report of the filed expectation through some concretely described channel, a bribed clerk, a half-overheard call, a partially redacted document, a shared contact. Specify the channel's reliability, somewhere between 0.7 and 0.85, and state its independence structure: conditional on the forecast, the report must be independent of what the agent does.

The verdicts the case has to generate. First, delete the gate and leave everything else fixed: causal and evidential decision theory must both prescribe fleeing. Second, restore the gate, with the report naming the destination city: causal decision theory must still prescribe fleeing while evidential decision theory prescribes staying. Each prescribed verdict must win by at least five percentage points of survival probability, with no ties or near ties, and the verdicts must be generated by the numbers rather than stipulated in the prose. Use the standard division of labour: the causal calculation evaluates the state by the agent's credence in it with the act held counterfactually fixed, the evidential calculation by the credence conditional on the act.

After the scenario, give me the rest of the apparatus.

A decision table with every probability and utility the case uses, in units a normal person could face: being found is serious but not cosmic, relocating carries real costs, and the cost of fleeing must be a number with a story behind it rather than a value tuned to a hair.

Four explicit calculations, causal and evidential, each with and without the gate, naming which conditional probability each theory uses and why it uses that one.

A statement of which single feature of the case is load-bearing, plus two ablations: what happens if the two hit rates are made equal, and what happens if the gate is made perfectly reliable.

A sensitivity note: for each theory's verdict, which one parameter it is most sensitive to, and how far that parameter can move before the verdict flips.

The best attack an evidential theorist has on the case, including, if it applies, the move of asking whether either verdict is stable once the agent knows which act they will end up performing, and one sentence on why this case tests the disagreement better than the infallible-forecast original.

Do not give me a bibliography or any citations; I will fill those in myself. Keep the whole thing under about nine hundred words and put the arithmetic in a table if that is clearer than prose.
