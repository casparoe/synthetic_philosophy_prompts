I'm defending the third chapter of my thesis in a fortnight — it's a joint micro-theory / behavioural-lab seminar, and the room will be roughly: two axiomatisers who think the strong axiom of revealed preference is close to definitional and who will want everything as a lemma; an experimentalist who runs online choice experiments and cares about effect sizes, pre-registration and whether anything here is testable with n=400 on Prolific; a structural econometrician who estimates demand from scanner data and will want to know what's identified and off what variation; a senior theorist who was around for the old causality-and-utility fights and thinks the whole "what does utility mean" question was settled by making it a representation theorem; and one philosopher of economics who'll ask what I mean by welfare. My thesis is that the standard move — utility just is a numerical representation of a choice function, and asking whether it measures anything is confusion — is not stable once the same feasible set can be put in front of the same person in more than one way, and that what welfare claims should track is what survives across those ways, not the raw choice data.

Quick sketch of the argument so you know what to aim at. Call a presentation any way of putting the same underlying feasible set in front of the agent: different default, different ordering on screen, different labelling of the attributes (price per month vs per year, 90% lean vs 10% fat), different framing of the same lottery. For each presentation p let R(p) be the set of complete, transitive preference relations over the underlying alternatives that rationalise the choices observed under p (so I'm intersecting relations, not utility numbers, which dodges the "utility is only ordinal" objection). Define the agent's presentational core as the intersection of all these R(p)'s — a beats b only if every presentation's rationalising relation says so. I then prove that when choices satisfy a condition I'm calling weak presentational coherence (no cycle in the union of the across-presentation strict preferences, plus a mild richness requirement on the presentation set), the core is itself a complete, transitive preference relation and it rationalises the data from every presentation; and that when WPC fails the core comes out empty or cyclic and I claim that's a finding about the agent worth measuring, not a modelling failure I should patch. Welfare claims, on my view, attach to the core.
