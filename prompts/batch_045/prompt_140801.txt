Say Mill gets airlifted into a civil-service meeting where the choice between two policies is going to be settled by a wellbeing survey: everyone rates their own life 0-10, average the two arms, take the bigger number, one person one unit. He thought pleasures differ in quality and not just quantity, and that whoever has had both is the one who gets to say which is higher — so what does he actually object to first here? I don't think it's the cardinality worry, so skip past that bit quickly. What I want is what he'd insist gets bolted on: do the competent judges rate everybody's life or only their own, and if it's everybody's, how is that not exactly the paternalism he spends On Liberty arguing against? And can higher vs lower pleasures even survive being crushed into a single number, or does he end up throwing the survey out and asking for something else entirely — and what would that something be?
