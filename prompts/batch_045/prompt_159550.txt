So I've ended up defending what I take to be the boring line on Thomson's lamp, and I want to know how exposed it actually is. The line: supertasks are possible — there's no contradiction in the idea of infinitely many operations packed into a finite interval — and the lamp doesn't show otherwise. What it shows is underdetermination. The description (lamp on at the start, toggled at 1/2, 3/4, 7/8, ... of a minute) pins down the state at every instant before the limit and nothing at the limit itself, and that's just because there's no last toggle and no instant immediately before the limit for the state to be inherited from. Both 'on at the limit' and 'off at the limit' are consistent with the whole story, and nothing in the setup prefers either. So: indeterminism, not impossibility. (Incidentally the lamp is on for two-thirds of the minute by measure, which feels like it ought to matter and I don't think it does.)

Two things I keep going back and forth on. First, the twin-lamp case: same switch, two lamps wired with opposite polarity, so at every instant before the limit exactly one of them is on, and hence at the limit exactly one of them is on too — that much seems to follow — but there's no fact about which. I find myself wanting to say there's a determinate disjunctive fact with no determinate disjunct. Is that stable, or does it quietly collapse into 'the setup is impossible after all'? Second, chaining: I also hold that if a process is genuinely possible, you should be able to run it and then, at the limit instant, start a second process whose instructions read the first one's output — if the lamp is on at the limit, do A, otherwise do B. The lamp obviously can't be chained. Does that break the view, or is it only a constraint on what can be concatenated rather than on what can happen?

So what would actually count against all this? I want defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, including the ones a defender like me should concede rather than just wave off. And be honest about whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability: I've already said I only mean metaphysical possibility, not whether anything could do this in our world given finite power budgets and minimum switching times, and I can feel that qualification doing work it maybe shouldn't. What would move me most is an argument that turns underdetermination into outright contradiction, not another intuition pump about occuring states. Under 450 words, ranked list or prose, no headed essay.
