I run a fortnightly reading group on modality, and two of our regulars — call them Nadia and Crispin — have been going at each other for about a year on origin essentialism, to the point where I cant tell whether its still a real disagreement or whether they've just started using words differently. I want to force the issue: draft the document they should have produced by now, the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration.

Nadia holds the standard Kripkean line on origins and holds it hard: no human person could have originated from gametes other than the ones they actually did, and she thinks even a genetically identical but numerically different sperm-and-egg pair wouldn't do — the requirement is about those gametes, not about what they carry. Crispin thinks the necessity is an artefact of how we trace individuals across counterfactual situations, i.e. that nothing out there settles which baby-in-another-situation is me. Lately he's been retreating to something he calls 'genome essentialism': what's essential isn't the gamete tokens but something like the nuclear genome, so a one-base-pair difference wouldn't matter but a substantially different genome would — and he's genuinely unsure whether mitochondrial DNA is in scope, since that comes from the egg cytoplasm rather than the nucleus.

What I want, in this order: a numbered section of claims both can sign (about the biology, about the logic — necessity of identity, no branching, what the standard arguments on each side actually establish and what they don't); then a table of the residual disagreements, with each disagreement given twice, once in wording Nadia would accept for her own view and once in wording Crispin would accept for his, plus a column saying whether you think that particular row is substantive or just verbal; then the cruxes.

The cruxes I suspect are: whether the de re requirement attaches to gamete tokens or to genetic material; whether the no-branching constraint in the twinning case is doing independent work or is just the same requirement wearing a hat; whether conceivability or intuition is any evidence here at all or is explained away as imagining a counterpart; and whether part of this is really a fight about which relation counts as identity across worlds. For each crux, give a case or argument both of them would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance — Nadia's and Crispin's — before anyone knows how it comes out.

Some cases I've been chewing on, most of which I made up, so use them only if they actually bite: an egg from the mother's identical twin sister that happens to carry the same genome as the egg you actually came from, fertilised by the same sperm; nuclear genomes identical but the mitochondrial DNA different; a single base pair edited in the zygote before first cleavage; the very same sperm and egg fusing three days later than they in fact did; and the ordinary split-zygote case that produces identical twins.

Two more things. Be explicit about confidence as you go: mark which formulations you're reasonably sure both would sign, and which are your own attempted compromises that one of them would probably balk at — and say how much weight you'd put on your verdict about the verbal/substantive rows, since that's the bit I'd least trust myself to check. And if the request is somehow confused — if 'could you have come from a different sperm and egg?' is really two questions glued together, or if one of my cases is secretly a case about something else entirely, or if the honest verdict is that no joint statement is possible here at all — say so plainly instead of manufacturing a consensus.
