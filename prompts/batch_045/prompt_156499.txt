I dont have any academic background, I just read interpretability stuff on the side, and there's an argument I keep watching go in circles. One side points at cases where you run some tool that carves a model's activations into features, find one that tracks something like "this text is French" or "there's a bridge in this chess position", push the model along that direction, and it behaves exactly as you'd predicted, including on inputs nobody tested on. They say: look, there's a real thing in there, the model represents that. The other side says all of that shows is that the tool pays rent — that there could easily be several incompatible ways of carving up the same activations, each of which lets you predict and intervene just as well, with nothing in the network picking one out as the one it "really" uses, so "representation" is just a useful façon de parler. Then the first side says what more could a representation possibly be required to do, and round we go.

My half-formed theory is that it's two different questions sharing a word: one about whether the internal states play a certain causal role, one about whether there's a fact of the matter which decomposition is the right one, and nobody says which they're answering. But I'm not confident, and I also can't tell whether there's a hidden empirical question doing the work.

Here's the bit I keep turning over and can't tell if it's decisive or a red herring. Imagine someone actually builds the thing the skeptics wave at: for one model and one concept, they exhibit two decompositions that genuinely disagree with each other — the first says this unit is a single clean detector, the second says it's two unrelated things overlapping — yet both support every intervention and prediction the other does, equally well, on new inputs, and both are what you get from running the same method twice with a different seed. Would that settle the argument, or would the realist just say fine, then it has two representations, or that uniqueness was never the requirement? Is this a verbal dispute about a word, a real disagreement about what it takes to have content, a factual question about how many such decompositions can exist, or a sign the question is ill-posed? Diagnose it using what each side actually says, and tell me honestly what, if anything, would count as progress — or why none should be expected.
