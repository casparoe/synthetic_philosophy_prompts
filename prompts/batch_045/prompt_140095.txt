I've been going to a small reading group on persistence for about two years now, and I'm pretty sure we're going in circles. I don't want another tour of the positions — I want a diagnosis of why nothing ever moves.

Here's where we're at. Everyone agrees on the cases. There's the candle that's straight and then bent, the person at ten and the same person at eighty, the ship whose planks all get replaced, the company that's the same company after every employee and every share has turned over, and — these are ours, we made them up — a file in a version-control system, where what's actually on disk is a pile of diffs and the "current version" only exists when something reconstructs it on demand, and a game-engine object that gets torn down and rebuilt every single frame. The endurance people say the thing is wholly present at each time it exists. The perdurance people say it's spread out across time the way a road is spread out across space, and that the thing at a time is a part of it. One guy in the group keeps pushing a third option where what's there is just the current stage and talk of the whole object is a façon de parler. And every argument is by now completely familiar to all of us: temporary intrinsics (how can one thing be both bent and straight), counting (how many things are in the room if every momentary bit counts), change (if it's the temporal parts that have the properties, does anything ever change, or is it just variation across a block), coincidence, and whether the spatial spread case and the temporal spread case are relevantly alike. Two years, nobody has moved anybody, and it's not for lack of smart people or patience.

My own hypothesis is that this isn't a disagreement about time at all — it's a disagreement about what "exists at" and "part of" are doing. Whether the time-modifier scopes over the claim that the thing exists, or expresses a relation between the thing and a time, and whether parthood is time-indexed. On that reading, once you pick a formalism to make it precise — quantify over times with a two-place "exists at", take tense operators as primitive, index parthood to times — the metaphysical answer more or less falls out of the notation, and the whole thing is a verbal dispute in a substantive-looking costume. But maybe I'm wrong. Maybe the sides differ about something genuinely modal or causal. Maybe they differ about which ordinary sentences count as data a theory has to respect — how many are in the room, whether the bent candle is the same candle — so the fight is over the evidential base, not the metaphysics. Or maybe it's bedrock intuition on both sides that neither can argue for.

So: what kind of stall is this, actually? Verbal, substantive, two questions under one label, or a real disagreement — about language, about our concepts, about the world — in disguise? Wether my formalism thing is right or not, I want you to say why, and to do it by engaging with what each side says about the cases above rather than listing views. And then, given your diagnosis, tell me honestly what would count as progress: some formal result, a prior agreement about which sentences are the data, something from physics about simultaneity, a new case that breaks the tie — or nothing, and we should just disband the group and read something else. Don't give me a reading list, I already have one and that's kind of the problem.
