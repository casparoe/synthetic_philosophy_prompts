I'm a PhD student in machine learning — my research is mechanistic interpretability of small language models — and next month our lab is running a reading-group session with a visiting philosopher who defends epiphenomenalism about phenomenal consciousness. I've tried to explain the position to my labmates, and the reaction I keep getting is some version of so consciousness is just a readout with stop-gradient applied? I said something like yes, and then found I couldn't get any more precise than that; I suspect the yes is doing very little work and hiding most of the difficulty. So I want the translation done properly and systematically rather than gesturally.

Assume I'm fluent in the following and want you to use it as the target vocabulary: backpropagation and detached tensors, probes and linear classifiers trained on activations, causal interventions on the computation (ablation, resampling, activation patching), in-distribution versus out-of-distribution evaluation, and causal graphs up to interventions and d-separation. Assume essentially no philosophy. The toy I've been using, which you should either adopt or replace and say why: at some middle layer of a transformer, an auxiliary head computes a value from the residual stream by a fixed function; the value is detached, so there is no gradient path from it to any weight and no path at all to any downstream computation, including the model's own verbal reports about its internal states. A separate report head emits sentences of the form I am now in state S; it is trained, never on the auxiliary value, and correlates with it only because both are functions of the same upstream activations. Please restate, in that vocabulary and as faithfully as you can: (i) the epiphenomenalist claim itself — that phenomenal states are caused by physical states but cause nothing physical, in particular nothing about what we say or believe; (ii) the paradox of phenomenal judgment — that if epiphenomenalism is true, one's judgment that one is in a phenomenal state is not explained by the state it is about, so it is unclear what warrant it has, and the thesis threatens to undercut the philosopher's own assertion of it; and (iii) the main moves available in response, including the idea that a judgment can be a reliable indicator of a state without being caused by it.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually want. Which claims map cleanly onto my vocabulary — I'd guess the correlation-versus-intervention distinction, common-cause explanation, counterfactual dependence of the report on the readout, and in-distribution reliability versus robustness under intervention are all doing real work here. Which translate only with distortion, and where exactly the distortion enters: I'm especially suspicious of three places — that in my toy the readout is a deterministic function of the activations, which makes supervenience a computational guarantee rather than the weaker metaphysical claim an epiphenomenalist usually accepts; that a node with no downstream consumers is dead code, which no training process would maintain and any compiler would delete, and which I suspect is smuggling in the evolutionary objection under a different name; and that nothing in my vocabulary seems to say what it is for the report head's outputs to be about the readout rather than merely correlated with it. And which claims have no expression at all in what I know — I'd guess the first-personal character of the state, and anything normative or epistemic, i.e. reasons, warrant, justification, as opposed to reliability. My advisor's view is that once you've asked whether the report head tracks the readout under interventions and distribution shift, there is nothing left over and the paradox dissolves into a question about OOD reliability. I want to know whether that is right, or whether the translation is quietly relabeling an epistemic problem as a statistical one and thereby answering a different question. Where you think the residue points to a gap in my framework, say so; where you think it points to unclarity in the original debate, say that instead — and flag any place where my toy has quietly imported an assumption I didn't notice.
