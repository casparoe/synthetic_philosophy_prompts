So this whole thing started for dumb reasons — I had surgery under general anesthesia in the spring and came out of it kind of fixated on whether there was anything it was like to be me during those two hours, and of course that lead me into a long argument with a friend who works in ML about whether a big enough machine could be conscious. Those arguments keep stalling on the exact same move. One of us says "I can perfectly well imagine X," the other says "sure, but imagining a thing doesn't mean it's possible," and neither of us has any way to say which of those imaginings are worth anything. But the thing I keep noticing is that "seems possible" is not one feeling, it's at least two, and they behave completely differently when you poke them.

First kind: my friend says imagine something that behaves exactly like a person, says all the same things, winces at the same jokes, but there's nobody home — dark inside. That imagination is smooth, and here's the part that got me: it survives being attacked. Someone told me there's some hidden necessary connection that makes it impossible, I understood the argument, I half accept it, and the feeling does not budge at all. Second kind: take the "the morning star might not have been the evening star" sort of example, or "water might have turned out not to be H2O." That one doesn't feel like I'm picturing something, it feels like I'm failing to find anything that rules it out. And it has this creepy property: a philosopher friend killed the intuition for me by confidently asserting some discovery that ruled the case out — and then admitted she'd completely made it up — and the feeling still didn't come back, or only came back a little, even though I knew the defeater was fake. So one kind is inelastic under pressure, including under bogus pressure, and the other kind collapses under pressure that turns out to be worthless. They also spread differently: I can hand the first kind to someone by describing the scenario and they get it immediately, whereas the second kind I basically can't pass on.

What I can't tell is whether I've found something real or just two ways of describing the same thing, and I keep hitting cases that don't sort cleanly. There's a third texture: cases where I can't picture it at all and yet can't find anything wrong with it either — time having no beginning, or something existing outside of time and making time. And there are the cases where the two pulls come apart, which is what actually matters for the arguments: for the dark-inside-machine case my friend reports the smooth feeling and I mostly report the "can't see what rules it out" one, and I genuinely don't know whether we're disagreeing about the world or just running different machinery. Is there an established distinction that this maps onto, or does everything I find online just lump all "seems possible" judgments together under one heading about using imagination as a guide to what could be? Please don't just tell me a name or hand me a pile of things to read — I want the carving: what exactly the two (or three) things are, what each is not, which of my examples belong on which side and why, what a borderline case would look like, and, if the split is real, whether one of them is the one that should be doing the work when people argue from "I can imagine it" to "it's possible." And if there genuinely isn't a concept for it, say so and give me one with a definition I could actually use.
