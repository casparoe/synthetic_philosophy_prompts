I am finishing a paper on restriction strategies for Fitch's paradox of knowability, and I want a number with its derivation rather than a survey of the literature.

Set the stage. Read K as the knowledge operator, assume factivity and distribution over conjunction, and take the knowability thesis as the schema p → ◇Kp for every proposition p. The derivation is short: let q be p ∧ ¬Kp for some p that is true and unknown; the schema gives ◇Kq; but Kq yields Kp ∧ K¬Kp, and factivity turns the second conjunct into ¬Kp, so Kq is impossible after all. Since any true unknown proposition generates this, the schema together with the existence of one unknown truth collapses into the claim that every truth is known.

A restrictionist restricts the schema to a class C: for p in C, p → ◇Kp. There are three things such a restriction has to do at once, and it is their conjunction I care about: block the collapse, block it stably rather than only for the canonical instance, be principled rather than reverse-engineered from the derivation, and remain strong enough to underwrite the anti-realist conclusion that truth is not evidence-transcendent. Call a restriction meeting all three successful.

The candidate I keep returning to restricts to knowledge-insensitive propositions: p is in C only if p's truth value is invariant under any variation in which propositions are known, holding the non-epistemic facts fixed. The canonical instance p ∧ ¬Kp drops out, since its truth varies with whether p is known. And the restriction is not on its face tailored to the paradox: it has the same shape as the familiar bans on self-reference that keep other intensional operators out of trouble.

Two things worry me, and I would rather have them priced than described. First, the notion can be stated syntactically, as containing the formulas free of epistemic vocabulary, or semantically, as containing the invariant propositions, and these come apart: diagonalization seems to yield sentences that are syntactically clean but provably equivalent to the claim that they themselves are unknown, which reinstates the collapse for the syntactic version while the semantic version survives. Second, even granting that the semantic version survives, the conclusion the anti-realist wants — that no truth is unknowable even in principle, that there are no evidence-transcendent truths — itself embeds epistemic vocabulary, and so may fall outside the fragment to which the restricted schema can be applied at all.

Give me your headline credence that some restriction strategy is successful in the three-fold sense, then decompose it. I want at least these terms, each with a number and one line of justification: that some restriction blocks the canonical derivation; that it blocks strengthened diagonal variants, conditional on blocking the canonical one; that a semantic invariance condition can be stated without circularity or emptiness; that the result counts as principled rather than *ad hoc*, given that *ad hoc* may have no sharp application here; that the restricted thesis is strong enough to deliver the anti-realist conclusion, whether directly or by some other route from the restricted schema; and that the three conditions are jointly satisfiable at all. Combine them, flagging where independence fails.

Then tell me which single term the headline number is most sensitive to, and what would move that term — a model theory in which the restricted schema is consistent and provably closed under diagonal repair, a limitative theorem showing that no restriction meeting the second condition meets the third, or an argument that the question has no determinate answer because *principled* and *strong enough* are themselves contested. If the last, say so and say what your number then becomes.

For calibration: I am at about 0.2 and my supervisor is at about 0.65, and neither of us can locate the disagreement. Tell me which of the terms above most likely separates someone at 0.2 from someone at 0.65.
