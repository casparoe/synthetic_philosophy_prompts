Not a homework question, this is actually happening. I'm on a commitee rewriting how our association picks its 15-person board. About 9,000 members. Right now everyone belongs to one of 15 local chapters, each chapter chooses one board member, and whoever gets the most votes in that chapter wins even if it's only 30% because the rest split. It's been like that since forever.

Numbers from our last member survey, and they've been stable: the old guard about 45%, the reform coalition about 35%, then two smaller groups. Call the first one the C's: about 15% overall, but packed into three chapters where they're 55-60%, and almost nowhere else — and those are the chapters that historically got ignored by the board. The other group, the D's, are about 12% and spread almost perfectly evenly, like 11-13% in every single chapter, so they have never won a seat, not once.

Two proposals on the table. Option one: scrap the chapters, everyone votes once across the whole association, and the 15 seats get handed out according to what share of the vote each slate got. Option two, floated as a compromise: merge the chapters into 5 regions, each region elects 3, and inside each region those 3 seats get divided by vote share.

The people pushing option one keep saying it's about finally giving smaller groups a voice. But when I actually do the arithmetic it looks like it takes a seat or two off the C's and gives them to the D's, and I genuinely can't tell whether that's fairer or just handing representation to a different group that happens to be the reformers' friends. I also can't tell if option two does anything for the D's at all — my feeling is 3 seats per region is too small a pool for a 12% group to ever win one, but someone said bigger regions would fix that and I don't see why the size would matter so much. And under option one each group would put up its own ranked slate, so there'd be no way to vote out one specific useless person, which seems like a real loss to me but everyone rolls their eyes when I say it.

What should I actually argue for, and is my worry about option one a real objection or am I missing what this whole proportionality thing is even for? Please don't just tell me every system has tradeoffs — tell me which one you'd back in this specific case and what you'd want to know that I haven't told you. Also say how confident you are in what you're claiming and single out the part of your reasoning you trust least.
