A blurb on the back of a short introduction to contemporary philosophy — I no longer have the book with me — sums up Richard Rorty's antirepresentationalism roughly as follows: language never hooks onto the world, only onto other language, so no belief is ever answerable to how things really are; truth, on this view, is nothing over and above what your community will currently let you assert, i.e. just current consensus; it follows that there is no fact of the matter about anything and no standpoint from which one culture's beliefs could be faulted by another; science is accordingly only one more genre of writing, with no special epistemic authority, and inquiry converges on nothing at all; and in any case the position refutes itself, since the claim that no statement represents reality is either itself a claim about reality or else just another move in the language game.
