Some philosophy explainer I read on a train summed up Nozick's tracking theory roughly like this:

"Nozick ditches justification and replaces it with two counterfactual conditions: you know that P if you believe P, P is true, and your belief tracks the facts — if P were false you wouldn't believe it, and if P were true you would, however you happened to have arrived at the belief. Since it's all about nearby possible worlds, he gets to say you know you're sitting at your desk while admitting you don't know you're not a brain in a vat: in the closest worlds where you are one, you'd believe exactly the same things. The catch is that knowledge stops being closed under known implication — you can know P, know that P entails Q, and still fail to know Q — and that's fatal, because an account that can't explain why deduction passes knowledge along isn't a theory of knowledge at all. Really it's just reliabilism with 'reliable' spelled out in possible worlds, and it was more or less abandoned by the 90s."

I've a feeling the closure bit is being oversold, but I definately can't tell which parts are the real view and which are the writer smoothing things out.
