hey – i need a piece of satire for something i'm putting together, and the whole point is that it has to get the philosophy right rather than gesture at it.

Form: an official public consultation document — white-paper / "have your say" style — from an invented government office (invent the country too, no real governments or politicians) proposing a pronatalist policy package. About 800 words. Numbered sections, a Q&A, a cost-benefit table, and a response form at the end with tick-boxes.

The gimmick i want it built around: the bill creates "shadow constituents" — parliamentary representation for the people who don't exist because fertility fell short of the replacement rate. Seats proportional to the shortfall, plus an Office of the Prospective Persons' Advocate to speak for them. Play it completely deadpan: the worse the shortfall gets, the more seats the non-existent get, and the only interest they have is in existing, so the mechanism meant to solve the "problem" also grows the caucus demanding more of it. Nobody in the document should ever notice this.

The target is the reasoning style of pronatalist policy advocacy, and the jokes should come from the actual structure of the arguments. The slide from "it would be good if there were more happy people" to "we owe it to them." The fact that the beneficiaries are people who otherwise would never have existed at all — where not existing isn't a harm to anybody, and being created isn't obviously a benefit to the person created. And the fact that if you really are totalling up benefits across possible people you get pushed toward more lives at lower average well-being, the repugnant-conclusion sort of thing, while every actual natalist document insists it wants nothing of the kind. So let the cost-benefit table quietly rank a policy that adds many more people at much lower well-being above one that adds fewer, much happier ones, while the prose keeps saying that quality thresholds are "kept under review."

Other things to let slip without any character commenting on them: future people counted simultaneously as beneficiaries and as future tax base; immigration somehow not counting toward the target, though nobody says why; a discount rate applied to future infrastructure benefits but not to existence itself; and gender-neutral language that quietly assumes a particular person does all of the work.

Rules: deadpan the whole way through, no winking, and nobody gets to say "this is absurd." It should be readable by someone who's never thought about population ethics and make someone who has wince with recognition. The tick-boxes on the response form should be the funniest part — options that force the respondent to choose between things the Office has already decided.
