Take Volkswagen's diesel emissions cheat — the one that broke in 2015, where the company had shipped cars with software that recognised when the engine was running a regulatory test cycle and switched it into a cleaner mode, so the cars passed in the lab and polluted way over the limit on the road — and run it through Kant's formula of universal law. I want an actual mapping, not "lying is wrong because universal lying is incoherent": spell the maxim out at the level of description you think is right (and say why that level and not a finer or coarser one), say who the agent is — the engineer who wrote the code, the manager who signed off, the firm as such — and whether that choice is doing work the test can't do for itself. Then run both branches: do you get a contradiction in conception or only a contradiction in the will, and what turns on which? My own hunch is that the cheat is parasitic on the practice of emissions certification in roughly the way a lying promise is parasitic on promising, but I'm not convinced that gives you more than a contradiction in the will, since a world where everyone cheats on tests still has tests, just useless ones.

Two things I especially want. First: suppose the people involved really did have the maxim "comply with the letter of every rule that binds us" and used it to defeat the point of the rules — does the FUL reach that at all, or does it only bite once the maxim is described as defeating the point? Second, whether the case tells against the FUL or for it: on the one hand nobody was deceived in anything like a promise, and the harm is diffuse (regulators, buyers, whoever breathes the air nearby), which the FUL at least doesn't need to count; on the other hand it seems to need an account of what an institution is for, which sounds like Hegel's formalism complaint all over again. Say where the test is silent instead of making it speak, and tell me whether you think this is a case the FUL handles well or one it was never really built for.
