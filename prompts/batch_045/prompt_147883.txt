I am a professor of philosophy and, for the past three years, the ethics adviser to my university's clinical research partnership at a teaching hospital in a West African city, where our local staff describe a small gift to someone who has helped you as ordinary etiquette and describe going through a fixer or a well-placed relative as simply how anything gets done here. I am asked to rule on ten or twelve matters a year, and I want a procedure rather than a policy statement: ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a refusal, and where it must hand the decision to a named person because the remaining question is one of values rather than of fact.

Two constraints shape it. First, it has to be runnable by our site coordinator, a senior nurse with no training in ethics, in about half an hour, from a written record, with me unreachable by phone. Second, it may not simply export our home university's compliance list: the site reasonably rejects the suggestion that a goat, a length of cloth, or a bottle given after the fact is the moral equivalent of an envelope of cash, and it is equally unmoved by our monetary threshold.

I want the procedure built on the thought that corruption is not a class of objects but a capture of a role's discretion by a loyalty the role was created to exclude, so that a bribe, a clientelist relationship, and a gift are one phenomenon at different durations, and the operative test is whether a consideration entered a decision that the role's point excludes, asked from the standpoint of whoever loses by it. It must also handle the case where refusing is not neutral, because to decline the gift is to decline the relationship the work depends on.

Apply it to three cases: a family giving the study nurse who recruited their child a chicken and a bottle of schnapps once the child's treatment has ended; our site director proposing to award the sample-courier contract to his wife's brother's firm, which is also the only firm that reliably reaches the district, disclosed and declared irrelevant by him; and a foreign partner university offering our medical school's dean, who is deciding whether to renew their memorandum, an all-expenses-paid week at the resort hotel where the signing is to take place. I suspect one of these will come out the way I would rather it did not; say which, and whether that is a defect in the procedure or in my expectation. Finally, state plainly what the procedure cannot settle, in particular whether the standard it applies is ours, theirs, or something it has quietly introduced of its own.
