I've been asked to write the 50-year history of the small research institute I've worked at for about eleven years, and I keep catching myself making moves that feel Whiggish and other moves that feel fine for reasons I can't spell out. Here are my gut verdicts:

- The 1972 group built something that looks to us exactly like the ancestor of what we do now. I'm happy giving them a whole chapter, but I wince at calling them "the first" anything or saying they "anticipated" our work.
- The big 1980s programme that got defunded and left no descendants: I want it in the book even though it led nowhere. Cutting it because it has no heirs feels like the actual sin here.
- Describing the director who killed a line of work in 1991 as being on the wrong side of history: no. Saying the same decision was one its makers later came to regret, because I have the letters where they say so: fine.
- A chapter organised around why the institute ended up doing X: fine. A chapter on the 1994 project that got cancelled, built around what was lost and what might have happened if they'd persisted: also fine, and I can't say why.

Two I can't reconcile. A section on the technician who did the real bench work behind a 1974 paper and was left off the author list: that chapter exists only because of present-day worries about credit, and I think it's the most important thing in the book. And a chapter on our founder's 1961 memo as evidence of an early commitment to open science: same present-day motivation, and I think it's bullshit.

The principle I keep trying out on myself is that hindsight is fine as a filter for what to look at, but not as an account of what the past was for or what it was trying to become. Does that actually deliver my verdicts, where does it break, and which of them does it force me to give up? And what does whatever principle survives say about two I haven't decided: the anniversary timeline of firsts they want on the wall, and a 1982 memo praising a project that now looks ridiculous, which I mainly want to quote because its funny?
