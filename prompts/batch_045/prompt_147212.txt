Hi — I'm on the acquisitions and ethics committee of a small museum, and we're suppose to be drafting a policy on copies, facsimiles and AI-assisted reconstructions, so before the meeting I wrote down what I actually believe about fakes and originals. I've got to defend it in front of colleagues who disagree with me in both directions, so I'd rather find the cracks now. Please write in British English.

(a) With a really good forgery there's no perceptual difference — anyone who says you can always tell if you look properly is mostly kidding themselves. So whatever explains why we care about originals, it isn't what meets the eye.

(b) What explains it is history: the object's causal origin, whose hands it came from, when and where. That's why a forgery's value legitimately collapses the moment it's found out, and why I'd also say a molecule-for-molecule facsimile — say one made by a process that has to destroy the painting in order to scan it — is worth next to nothing beside the original.

(c) But if the original burnt and all we had left was that facsimile, I'd of said we'd lost almost nothing of value; we'd still have the work.

(d) The wrong of forgery is basically just fraud, i.e. deceiving buyers. Once a fake is known and honestly labelled I don't think there's anything left to object to, and I'd happily hang one.

(e) I don't think the artist's own hand matters as much as people claim: a cast made after her death from her own mould, exactly as she directed in her will, is as much the work as one made during her lifetime and no less valuable — and I find myself wanting to say the same about a late work produced by a system trained on her entire output, which she authorised in the will and which the estate signed off on.

(f) And when a forgery hung undetected for years and everyone loved it, I think those people were having genuine experiences and weren't wrong about the qualities they were looking at; the discovery doesn't retrospectively spoil them.

Which of these are genuinely in tension and which only look like it? Rank the tensions by severity, say what would have to be given up in each case and what the cheapest fix would be, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
