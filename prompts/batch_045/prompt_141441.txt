I'm retired now — thirty-odd years working on phase diagrams and crystallography, and lately I've been sitting in on a philosophy reading group, which is how I got tangled up in this. We've spent two sessions on Nomic, the game where the starting rulebook tells you how to change the rules, including the rule that tells you how to change the rules. Half the room thinks that is a perfectly coherent thing a rule system can do to itself; the other half thinks there's always a small normative gap that something outside the system has to fill, and that when the system amends its own amending rule the gap just becomes easier to see. They've stopped making progress and mostly repeat themselves, and nobody has written anything down.

Could you write the document they should have produced: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between

- the self-containment side (call them the Closure people): a valid amendment can change the amendment rule itself, in the same act, with nothing outside the rules doing any work, and it's still the same system afterward; and
- the gap side (call them the Scaffold people): every amendment of the amending rule leans on something the rules don't state — a practice, a convention, a shared understanding of what counts as following a rule — and the real question is how much work that thing does, not whether it exists.

I'd like: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides could sign, and I mean claims with teeth, not "rules matter" — about what an amendment rule has to do in the first place, about whether "valid according to the rules" and "binding on the players" can come apart, about what's going on when a rule entrenches itself. (2) The disagreement stated twice, once in the Closure people's own wording and once in the Scaffold people's, each version being one the other side would accept as a fair statement of what they hold. (3) The cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side — and for each crux a concrete case both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about what the case shows, written down in advance. (4) If part of the arguing was only about words, say which part and why. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table, and keep the whole thing under about 1500 words.

Here are some cases I made up — use them or swap in better ones, but they shouldn't come from a book:

- The ratchet: the assembly passes a rule saying "this rule cannot be amended." Next turn it passes a rule repealing it. Is the second act a valid amendment, a small revolution, or just noise?
- The dying quorum: a rule sets the quorum for amending at two thirds; an amendment lowers it to one; the single remaining player then rewrites everything else. Does the system survive that as the same system?
- The retroactive rule: three amendments have already been made, and a new rule says amendments only ever counted if they were proposed in writing. Which of the three survive, and who decides?
- The fork: two groups start from identical rulebooks and each amends the amending rule differently. A year later, which one (if either) is the original system — and is that the same question as the one the ratchet raises?
- The borrowed gavel: the rules are amended to say that disputes about whether an amendment was valid are settled by an umpire who isn't a player and doesn't consult the rules. Has the system closed its own gap by importing somebody else's?

One more thing, and you can tell me if it's a red herring: my instinct from the lab is that this is the old question about whether a thing that replaces its own parts is still the same thing — a crystal that changes its own lattice, an organism that rebuilds its own metabolism. If that analogy actually illuminates the closure-vs-scaffold fight, say where it helps; if it sneaks in an assumption the Closure people shouldn't get for free, say that instead.

Please write in American English, and don't assume I've read anything — I can follow an argument fine, I just haven't read the literature. No citations or suggested reading, I can't get to a university library easily and I wouldn't trust a reference list I couldn't check anyway.
