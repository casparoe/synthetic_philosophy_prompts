I teach a graduate seminar on social epistemology, and the final session of term is on collective judgment: jury theorems, information cascades, the epistemic cost of letting people talk before they vote. I want to close the session with a poem rather than another handout, and I'd rather it be a real poem than a versified summary.

The poem is about the Condorcet jury theorem. Write it as a villanelle. If you think a different fixed form does the work better — a sestina, whose six end-words return in rotated order, is the obvious rival — use that instead and give me one sentence saying why.

The philosophy should be structural, not decorative:

- Let the two refrains be the theorem's two conditions: that each voter is more likely than not to be right, and that the voters are independent of one another. Which refrain is which is up to you, but it should be unmistakable by the final stanza.
- The build across the tercets is the convergence result itself: majority accuracy climbing toward certainty as the jury grows. Keep the margin thin, as it is in the case that actually matters — voters who are right fifty-one times in a hundred, a jury in the hundreds rather than the dozens.
- The volta, at or just before the closing quatrain where the two refrains meet, is the collapse: the voters have been hearing one another, or reading the same report, or watching who voted first. The crowd that looked like an oracle turns out to carry a single signal, and its size buys nothing. If you can find room, the reader should also feel the knife-edge at even odds — that a voter at exactly fifty percent contributes nothing however many of her there are, and that below it, adding voters makes the majority worse.

Do not use the words correlated, independent, Condorcet, or any other jargon inside the poem; render them. No epigraph, no footnotes, no apparatus. Someone who knows the result should be able to point to a line and say that is where independence fails and that is why the convergence stops; someone who has never heard of it should still be able to read the poem as being about a crowd talking itself into certainty.

Give me the poem, then at most two sentences of prose identifying which refrain is which condition and where the collapse happens. Nothing else. Under 300 words total.
