I work in ML infra — large-scale training runs, optimizer tuning, loss-curve archaeology, that kind of thing — and I've somehow ended up advising a small foundation that's deciding whether to spend a couple of million dollars trying to get a particular technical standard adopted early (an interoperable logging format for model evals, roughly), on the theory that standards lock in and whoever moves first picks the attractor. My partner is an economic historian, and every time I try to articulate my scepticism she says something like "well, of course, path dependnece", and then we talk past each other for twenty minutes. So I want to actually do the translation properly instead of hand-waving with the word "lock-in".

Here's my crude mapping, which I suspect is wrong in an interesting way. A historical or technological trajectory = one training run. Path dependence = the landscape is non-convex, and the seed, the data ordering, and whatever tiny fluctuation you happened to get early on decide which basin you fall into, with the barriers between basins too high to hop once you're in one. Lock-in = a deep basin whose walls get steeper the longer you stay in it. And "would the same outcome have arrived anyway?" = do all the basins have roughly the same test loss, i.e. are they functionally equivalent modulo some symmetry — the way two runs from different seeds often end up at solutions that differ by a permutation of the units but compute the same function. On that reading, "history is contingent" just means high seed variance in the final weights and low variance in the metric you care about. I'm fluent in this vocabulary; I'm much shakier on the economics side beyond undergrad stuff.

So: take that mapping, do it as well and as honestly as you can, and then give me the residue report — what in the path-dependence literature translates cleanly into training-run vocabulary, what translates only if you fudge it, and what has no expression in it at all. Three suspicions I want you to either kill or confirm. First, the one that bugs me most: in my world the loss landscape is exogenous, fixed by the data before I start, but with standards and institutions the payoffs seem to be partly produced by the path itself — everyone else adopting changes my gradients — so maybe "basin" language smuggles in exactly the assumption that would make path dependence boring and trivially true. Second, the only way I have of saying "the same outcome" is functional equivalence under a metric I get to pick, and history doesn't hand you a test set; maybe the "would it have arrived anyway" question isn't even well posed in my vocabulary and only becomes well posed in some welfare or regret framing I don't have. Third, that the normative half of the literature — whether being locked in is bad, whether we ended up in a worse place than we could have, whether anyone could have steered it — has no counterpart at all for me, since if the loss is the same then every basin is equally fine. And then the bit I actually care about: does the residue tell me anything about the foundation's decision, or is it only telling me that my vocabulary is the wrong shape for this question?
