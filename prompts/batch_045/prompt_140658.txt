Last night me and my flatmate got into a stupid argument that went on way too long. She says that when five strangers on a footpath all grab a fallen branch and drag it off the trail without a word being said, that's a genuine joint action — five people doing something together. I said it's just five individual actions that happen to add up. Neither of us could get any purchase on the other and by the end we were mostly just repeating ourselves.

So I want a decision tree for our reading group (six of us, nobody very technical, we've all read a bit of philosophy of action) that sorts you into a position on what makes something a joint action rather than a pile of parallel individual ones. Please build it from the logical space rather than from a list of isms: give me 7 or 8 branch questions, each a concrete case or a claim you can agree or disagree with without already knowing which theory it leads to — no "do you think groups have minds of their own?" style questions — and then put the usual family of views at the leaves (shared intention, we-intentions, the plural subject, we-mode, and the deflationary story where joint action needs almost nothing over and above individual action), adding anything you think is missing as its own leaf or marking it "unnamed."

Three cases I specifically want the tree to handle, because they're where we kept getting stuck: (a) agents who can't form any view at all about what the others intend — two people who each think the other one is a hallucination, or a swarm of very dumb delivery robots; (b) participants who are perfectly coordinated but disagree about what they're doing, one thinks it's the rehearsal and the other thinks it's the real performance; (c) the lone-member case, where someone acts as one of a group of five and there is no group of five. If one of those cases doesn't actually separate any of your leaves, say so instead of inventing a branch for it, and tell me which pairs of leaves your tree just can't pull apart. Finally, mark the combinations of answers nobody actually occupies, and for each say in a sentence whether that's because it's incoherent, just unattractive, or simply that nobody's bothered to defend it.
