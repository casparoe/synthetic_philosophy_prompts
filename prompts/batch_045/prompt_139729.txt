In the usual telling, the 2018 human germline-editing episode is a rogue-scientist story: He Jiankui, then at SUSTech in Shenzhen, edited embryos to disable CCR5, announced the birth of twin girls (a third child followed) at the Second International Summit on Human Genome Editing in Hong Kong in November 2018, was dismissed, and in December 2019 was sentenced to three years for illegal medical practice — all of it against a declared international consensus that clinical use of germline editing was premature. The aftermath was field-wide rather than personal: condemnation, WHO guideline work, calls for a moratorium, a criminal ban in China, and reports that another researcher elsewhere meant to follow.

I want that episode read instead as an instance of the unilateralist's curse. Give me the mapping first: who exactly the agents are (individual labs, funders and universities, or states as competing regulatory jurisdictions, since the work was done where oversight was thinnest), what the unilateral action and its real option set were, what the payoff asymmetry consists in — priority, credit and commercial upside accruing to the actor, regulatory, safety and reputational cost spread over everyone in the field — and what the formal result predicts, including its counterintuitive half, that adding more capable agents or improving their information makes the risky action more likely rather than less.

Then give me the honest part, where the frame distorts the case. Three places I expect trouble, but say where else it breaks: (i) the curse is normally stated for a shared and potentially catastrophic downside, whereas here much of the cost was to the field's licence to operate and to children who were not members of the decision set at all — is this simply a commons problem, and if so does the label earn anything a commons analysis would not? (ii) the actor also bore an enormous private cost ex post, which the payoff structure does not obviously represent ex ante. (iii) the usual prescription is a norm of conformity, or deference to the most conservative member, but what the field actually built was registration, guidelines and moratoria — a mechanism the curse literature does not consider, and one that by its nature can only be installed after someone has defected.

Say what the theory would have prescribed beforehand, whether anyone in the episode was positioned to follow it, and whether the episode confirms the curse, is an anomaly for it, or shows it was the wrong instrument. Finish by saying what extension — to jurisdictional competition, to affected parties outside the agent set, to disclosure and pre-registration — the case would require before calling it an instance of the curse is more than a relabelling.
