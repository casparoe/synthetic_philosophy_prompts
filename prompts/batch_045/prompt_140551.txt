About two months ago I had a small procedure under deep sedation (propofol, I think) and I came round definately convinced I'd been awake for part of it — there was a scraping sound, someone saying something like 'nearly done', and it had that flat ordinary this-is-happening-now feel to it. The anaesthetist was kind about it but basically said: the drug stops memories being laid down, so what you're describing is a memory of something you never experienced. I did not find that reassuring and I've been chewing on it ever since, which is how I ended up in the literature on the colour phi thing.

Setup, so we're on the same page: red dot in one spot, then a green dot a bit further along in quick succession, and you don't see two dots — you see one dot sliding across that changes colour partway along, and people report the change happening *before* the second dot was ever presented, which you cannot have literally seen. The two readings as I understand them: Orwellian means you really had the experience at the time and the memory got rewritten afterwards to insert the change earlier; Stalinesque means nothing was rewritten because the finished version was assembled before it ever reached you, like a show trial where the verdict is written first and the proceedings are just theatre. And the Dennett point (Consciousness Explained, 1991 — that's the book everyone points you to) is that the two are supposed to be indistinguishable even in principle: the theorists agree about every event in the brain and only disagree about whether to call a given bit 'pre-experiential', so there's no fact of the matter about when the dot changed.

Two things I've been messing with; tell me if either is junk. First, a variant: I built a crude phi display on my phone where the second item is sometimes a real word and sometimes a scrambled letter string, and I drag a slider to say when the dot seemed to change. My impression is that when the second item is meaningful the change feels *earlier*. If that's real it looks like later-arriving content reaches back and redates the earlier experience, which feels like a point for Stalinesque. Second, a proposed tie-breaker: forget the verbal report and look for collateral traces instead — priming, a tiny flinch, faster reaction to green afterwards. If the content was genuinely experienced en route, it should leave other downstream marks, and if it was fabricated on the way in it shouldn't.
