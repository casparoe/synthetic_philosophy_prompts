I'm a high school senior, and a friend and I have been arguing about a case I invented precisely because the real-world version is too messy to settle. Please grant the following stipulations for the sake of the argument, whether or not they match actual supply chains. The chicken place near my school receives fillets in crates of 200. The regional warehouse sets each week's order by rounding total store sales up to the next crate, so an individual sandwich normally changes nothing at all, but for roughly one purchase in two hundred the purchase is the marginal fillet that tips the whole order up by one crate, i.e. by 200 birds. When an order does drop, some of the reduction leaks: the birds are sold on to some other buyer rather than never being bred. Call the share that genuinely does not leak the retention rate, and stipulate only that it lies somewhere between 0.3 and 0.9 and that neither of us has any idea where. Finally, assume that raising and killing the birds in this way is itself wrong, so that the only live question is the inefficacy one: whether my causal impotence as a single buyer gets me off the hook.

Give me your credence, as a number, that in this case my buying the sandwich is morally wrong in the sense that I have most reason not to buy it, and not merely some reason. I sit at about 0.75 and my friend at about 0.15, so what I really want is the decomposition: how much weight the expected-difference calculation carries, given that a roughly 1-in-200 chance of averting 200 birds is an expected saving of about one bird; whether very small chances of preventing a harm may be morally discounted to zero or whether expected value settles the matter; whether the self-shaping multiplier counts, the idea that skipping now makes me likelier to skip for years so that the real object of evaluation is a habit rather than a sandwich; whether the what-if-everyone-did-that move adds anything over and above the expected-value calculation or is just loose talk; and whether an expressive or complicity-based reason survives even in the worlds where my causal effect really is zero. Give each term a rough number and one line of justification, say how you combine them into the final figure, then tell me which term my friend and I most likely disagree about, and which single piece of evidence — the true threshold, the true retention rate, or something about habit formation — would move your number most. Please end with your final credence alone on the last line, in the form \answer{0.XX}.
