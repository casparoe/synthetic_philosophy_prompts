Hey — I write about science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about whether one person's little slice of a collective harm actually matters. I keep running into the same my-contribution-makes-no-difference move wearing different hats, and I sketched five versions of the setup to see whether the differences between them are doing any real work. For a sidebar I want them ranked: give each a score out of 100 for how much genuine force the no-difference argument has in that case (100 = the person really does nothing wrong, 0 = the move is worthless), and list them in order from strongest to weakest.

The five:

1. A grocery chain's automated restock rounds each store's order up to whole crates of twelve chickens, so your single bird only adds a bird to the next order if the running tally happens to land exactly on a crate boundary — otherwise the order is byte-for-byte what it would have been.

2. The supplier hatches against a fixed contract, renegotiated quarterly and revised only if demand has moved by more than 2%; one chicken is nowhere near that, and the review is months off besides.

3. You skip the chicken, but the flock was already hatched against that fixed contract, so your bird gets discounted into another market and eaten by someone else — you change who eats it, not whether it's eaten.

4. Your extra kilowatt-hour tonight comes off reserve capacity that would have been burned anyway, so no extra gas is burned; but it feeds the operator's load forecast, which feeds next years capacity auction.

5. Your extra tonne of CO2 is real, but the national inventory is published rounded to two significant figures and the legal target is assessed against the published number, so there's no possible world in which your tonne shows up in the thing that actually gets judged.

Reply as exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, in American English, no preamble and no wrap-up paragraph. In whichever item you feel shakiest, also tell me how confident you are in the ranking overall and name the single claim you're least sure of — I dont want to print something that sounds more settled than it is.
