Been asked to write down where I've landed on animal ethics before a reading group on Saturday, and I'd rather find the holes in it now than in front of six other people. Here's the set:

1. I've been vegan for about six years and I think buying factory-farmed meat is seriously wrong, not just mildly bad.
2. The reason is suffering: what matters is how much a creature suffers and for how long, added up. I don't think animals have rights, and "right to life" does nothing for me.
3. I think wild animal suffering is probably the biggest moral problem there is by sheer numbers, and I'm maybe 60% on flies and beetles being sentient.
4. So when I buy food I try to minimize the total suffering my purchase causes, including the animals killed growing and harvesting the crops.
5. Beef is worse than chicken.
6. Spraying insecticide on crop fields is bad, partly because of what it does to the insects.
7. Eating a cow that genuinely had a good life and a painless death is fine — say the steep hill pasture near where I grew up, land too rough to plough, roughly one animal per hectare — and so is eating roadkill.

Two things people have said to me. My sister says 2 pulls the rug out from under 1: if animals have no rights and the death itself is painless, there's nothing left to object to. That feels wrong to me but I can't say why. And someone in the group says 5 is actually backwards once you take 4 seriously, because per kilo of meat you kill far more chickens than cows, so chicken should come out worse. I think she's ignoring how much feed a cow needs over its whole life, but I'm honestly not confident, and I've also been told the crop deaths are where most of the insects go.

Go through these and tell me which pairs really clash and how badly, which apparent clashes dissolve on inspection, and for each real one what the cheapest fix is and what that fix costs me. Rank the real tensions by severity rather then treating them all as equal, tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and flag any place where my rough empirical assumptions (feed per kilo, lifespans, what pasture land can and can't be used for) are doing load-bearing work. End your reply with \answer{n}, where n is the number of the commitment that has to give in the worst tension, or \answer{none} if the set hangs together as it stands.
