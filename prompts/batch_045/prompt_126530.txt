here are the ones i've been chewing on myself; tell me which are genuine requirements and which are me smuggling in what i already wanted to be true:

- the inheritance (or portability) requirement: a real pattern should be hand-over-able. if you can tell me the pattern in a sentence or two and i can then go exploit it on new material, it's real; if it only fits for the person who found it, it's a private curve-fit. does this destroy pure data-compression accounts?
- budget-relativity versus verdict-objectivity: either pattern-hood depends on the predictor's computational budget, in which case "was the critic right?" has no budget-free answer and my reading group can't actually be wrong; or it doesn't, and you owe me an account of the privileged budget. which horn, and what breaks on it?
- anti-gerrymandering without language-invariance: compression-flavoured accounts make pattern-hood depend on a choice of description language. demand invariance across languages and almost nothing qualifies as a pattern; allow the choice and every scatter of dots becomes one, constellations included. where's the middle, and is there one?
- causal closure versus explanatory autonomy: patterns don't get extra causal powers on top of the physics, but intentional-level explanations are supposed to be doing work that micro-level ones can't. what would a truce even look like, and is the truce itself a requirement or a hope?

- self-application: the theory should be able to classify its own central notion. does "real pattern" come out as a real pattern on the theory's own criteria, and if it doesn't, is that fatal or is it fine?
