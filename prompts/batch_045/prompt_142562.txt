My political philosophy reading group got completely stuck last night and I want to bring something concrete next week. We were going over Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain case — the bit where a just distribution gets dismantled because a million people each voluntarily hand over a quarter to watch someone play basketball — and one of the group said the whole argument is a trick: the crowd is doing all the work, the charisma is doing all the work, and you could never get the same result out of ordinary life where nobody is famous and nobody is being cheered. I think she's wrong but I couldn't produce anything on the spot, and I've now wasted most of a week failing to build a version that doesn't secretly smuggle the celebrity back in. So I want to try a slightly different angle: instead of showing that liberty upsets patterns, show that liberty quietly destroys the floor the Lockean proviso is supposed to guarantee, without there being any injustice anywhere for the rectification principle to latch onto.

Here's the spec. One paragraph, realistic — water rights, parking permits, a housing co-op, allotments, a shared server, a irrigation channel, whatever, but no billionaires, no monopolist villain, no sci-fi. Constraints: (1) everyone starts from a legitimate baseline and every transaction or appropriation in the story is one a Nozickian has to count as fully voluntary and rights-respecting; (2) each individual step passes the Lockean proviso *on its own* — nobody is worse off than they'd have been had that particular step not occurred; (3) but once the whole sequence has happened, at least one person is clearly and seriously worse off than they would have been under no appropriation at all. So the endpoint fails the proviso even though no step does. And crucially: (4) there's no identifiable step or agent for rectification to target — no one acted badly, no single transaction is where it went wrong, the badness is an unintended side effect of a lot of fine transactions, and no one person ends up holding everything. I have a hunch the trick is to make the goods complements rather than substitutes so the losses are non-linear and show up only at the end, but build it however it actually works.

Then the part I really need: the demonstration that it meets each of (1)–(4) step by step; what each of Nozick's three principles says about the story; what the final distribution looks like to someone holding a patterned view instead (say a sufficientarian or a difference-principle person); an honest statement of which single structural feature is doing the work and what happens to the case if you remove it; and then how a defender of the theory wriggles out. Two wriggles in particular I want addressed: the move where you apply the proviso to the *system* of appropriation as a whole rather than to each act separately — I have a vague memory that Nozick words the proviso in terms of a process normally giving rise to a property right rather than in terms of individual acts, which would let them evaluate the sequence at once, but tell me if I'm misremembering that — and the flat-footed move where they just say the last person has no complaint because nobody violated their rights. Finish with a paragraph on whether my case is secretly just the old common-ownership / Georgist complaint about privatising things that used to be shared in disguise, and if it is, what I'd have to change so that it isn't. Our group has one fairly hardcore Nozickian in it who will go hunting for the cheatmoves, so please flag any place where the scenario stipulates the verdict instead of generating it from the principles.
