I have just spent a long evening arguing with a friend about whether the things I think with are parts of me. She held that any theory of this kind collapses into counting every well-used tool as mental; I kept insisting there must be a principled stopping point; and neither of us could say what an adequate theory would have to look like. So I want the specification before we resume.

Please write the desiderata that a theory of cognitive extension must meet to count as adequate for one particular job: deciding when an intervention on someone's external resources counts as an intervention on that person's mind rather than merely on their surroundings. The cases I actually care about are a phone seized and searched at a border, a care home removing a resident's labelled photographs, and a platform quietly rewriting the notes, archive, or predictive keyboard that a person relies on to think.

Give each requirement with the case or consideration behind it; mark which are non-negotiable and which are only preferences of one camp; and flag any subset you doubt can be jointly satisfied. The theory has to sort these: a memory notebook carried and consulted constantly; a cloud note application that silently corrects what was saved; an augmented-reality overlay a surgeon is trained to override when it drifts; a photo archive for a man with severe hippocampal damage, tagged and curated by his wife, which he can neither inspect nor edit; and a phone keyboard that completes his sentences before he has settled on what to say. It must also respect the distinctions I take to matter: constitution versus mere causal coupling, and the suspicion that the first is being inferred from the second; an extended mind versus a merely well-scaffolded agent; something's being a cognitive vehicle versus its user's deserving epistemic credit or blame; and a resource the agent has mastered versus one that has mastered the agent.

Do not spend more than a sentence or two scoring existing internalist, functionalist, or enactive positions — the criteria are what I want. Please write in British English, and close with \answer{...} naming, in five words or fewer, the single requirement that excludes the most candidate theories.
