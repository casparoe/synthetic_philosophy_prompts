I'm putting together something for the end-of-term show-and-tell of the philosophy reading group I go to on Tuesday nights (local library, mostly retirees plus one very patient PhD student who keeps us honest) and I could use a hand with it.

Background, so you know where I'm coming from: I'm retired, thirty-odd years as a field ecologist — mark-recapture work on a seabird colony, same island every summer, its the only job I ever wanted. I only started reading philosophy about a year ago and it's the first thing since retiring that's made me feel properly stupid in a good way. The bit I can't let go of is population ethics. In my old job you could never talk about how many birds there should be without straight away asking about density dependence, carrying capacity, and the Allee effect at the low end — below a certain colony size the whole thing stops working, birds can't find mates, the colony just goes. Then the philosophers turn up and ask, in complete seriousness, whether a bigger human population would be better, and there's all this machinery: total versus average, the repugnant conclusion, adding people who are happy enough and cost nobody anything, the thought that you can't really benefit someone by bringing them into existence, and a threshold level of lifetime wellbeing above which a new life counts as a plus. And as far as I can tell the whole answer swings on one number nobody can justify. That's what I want to send up.

So: a parody of an official government strategy document. Made-up country please — no real governments, no real politicians; the minister is just "the Minister." The conceit is that a Ministry of Demographic Resilience has had to build a population axiology into its policy appraisal, and has done it by turning every contestable value question into a technical parameter, set by a committee and buried in an annex. Their headline metric should be something like Existence-Adjusted Life Years, and the crucial free parameter is the reference level of lifetime wellbeing above which an added life counts positively — call it whatever sounds properly civil-servicy. Everything else in the document should follow dead straight from the spreadsheet.

Things I'd like you to actually get right, because the joke only works if the philosophy is doing the work:

- Every recommendation should change sign depending on that one parameter, and the document should never notice it has.
- The ecological material goes in as *rhetoric for a value claim*: the Allee-effect-ish observation that below some size a society stops sustaining certain goods gets used to argue that shrinking is self-reinforcing, and nobody pauses to say that a fact about how populations grow is not a fact about what's better. That's exactly the move I want to laugh at. Don't let it collapse into ha-ha-humans-are-a-plague or carrying-capacity doom — I spent my career being annoyed by that and it isn't the target.
- An FAQ where a citizen asks, in plain language, whether this is just a decision about how many people should exist, and the answer is a straight-faced non-answer in bureaucratic prose that quietly assumes the thing it's denying.
- Without ever breaking character or winking, the document should arrive at the conclusion that the best value-for-money policy is to add an enormous number of lives that are only barely worth living, and present this as a triumph of prudent management. That's where the room should go quiet.
- Fair matters more than savage. Two people in the group are genuinely pronatalist and I want them laughing too, because what's being sent up is a style of reasoning and the habit of hiding values as parameters — not a person and not a slogan. No cheap shots, no immigration stuff, no jabs at real family policy, and definately nothing about overpopulation I'd have to apologise for.

Sections, in this order please:

1. "Cover note" — about 80 words. From the Permanent Secretary to the Minister, saying in so many words that the difficult judgements have been dealt with by making them adjustable.
2. "The strategy" — about 600 words, numbered paragraphs, at least one footnote. The main document.
3. "Annex B: worked examples" — about 250 words. Three short scenarios scored in the Ministry's metric: one where contented people are added at no cost to anyone, one where the population shrinks and everyone left is much better off, and one down near the minimum viable size. Show the parameter doing all the work.
4. "What I'm aiming at" — about 200 words, in your own voice, telling me which real features of the debate you exaggerated and which you left alone, so I can check it's fair before I inflict it on the group.

It'll be read aloud, so aim for roughly four minutes' worth and cut anything that's only there for decoration.
