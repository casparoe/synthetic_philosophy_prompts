We've been using Nietzsche's early essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life as a kind of scorecard — monumental, antiquarian, critical — and going in circles. So: allocate the history budget. Give me three fractions that sum to 1, and one sentence each on why it lands where it does. Please don't spend the answer explaining what the three modes are; I've read the thing.

Two complications we keep hitting that the essay doesn't really cover, and I want you to deal with them rather than dodge around them:

- We want to preserve what I've started calling standby history: records of the paths we deliberately didn't take — abandoned technologies, scrapped constitutions, arguments we lost — kept not to inspire, not to console, and not to indict, but so that generation six still has the option of reviving them. Nietzsche's three modes all seem to be about what history does for the people alive right now; I can't tell whether standby history is a genuine fourth mode or just a degenerate case of the antiquarian one, and whether it earns its own slice. - There's no living tradition to interpret any of this. Whatever we pack has to survive eight generations of readers who share no language with us except the one we teach them out of the archive itself. That looks like it hits the three modes very unevenly — some of them seem to need an ongoing community to function at all, and I want that baked into your numbers rather than footnoted.
