Here is roughly where I have landed. I am writing the framing chapter of a dissertation and I want the package stress-tested before I commit to it in print.

On the currency. I want to propose a currency I have not seen defended, which I call effective discretionary time. Roughly: the share of a person's waking year in which they are not under the effective directive control of another's will — an employer's schedule, a court order, a creditor's demands, an institutional routine — multiplied by a conversion factor meant to capture how much of the option-space open to a normally situated adult they could actually exercise in that hour, given their body and their social situation. I want it measured administratively, once a year, by a public agency, something like a tax return. The point of the conversion factor is that a person with a severe impairment may have as many discretionary hours as anyone and still be worse off in the way that matters, and I do not want the metric to be blind to that.

On responsibility. Justice requires neutralizing the effects of brute luck. It does not require compensating pure option luck: if you take a foreseeable, unforced gamble and lose, the loss is yours to bear. Relatedly, expensive tastes generate no claim: if you have voluntarily cultivated a taste that is costly to satisfy, justice owes you nothing extra on account of it. Tastes that were shaped by unjust conditions are a different matter and I want them corrected for.

On why equality at all. I reject the idea that equality has intrinsic value. The levelling-down objection seems to me decisive: no principle can be defended by the observation that it would make people more equal as such. But I also want to insist on something comparative: what matters about a person's effective discretionary time is partly how it stands to other people's. A society in which everyone is at 0.6 because of genuine material scarcity is not unjust in the same way as one in which everyone is at 0.6 because a few people are hoarding, even if the absolute profile is identical. So I want a comparative constraint that is not a telic one.

On measurement and pluralism. I am Sen-ish about pluralism: capabilities are irreducibly plural, there is no single master index of how well a life is going, and any ranking of lives is bound to be incomplete. I distrust single-metric aggregation in general.

On institutions and standing. Justice must not require officials to determine whether a person's disadvantage is their own fault. That inquiry is demeaning, and it is also error-prone in ways that systematically misfire on the people who can least afford the error. Accordingly I want the floor delivered unconditionally: no means testing, no behavioural conditions, no work requirements, no questions about how you got there.

Four verdicts I am fairly confident about and want to keep.

One. An unpaid full-time carer for a severely disabled spouse is owed a public cash transfer and funded respite care. This is not charity and it is not demeaning to receive; it is what justice owes.

Two. A lawyer who knowingly, non-impulsively and without financial coercion chose an eighty-hour week has no complaint about her low effective discretionary time and no claim on a public subsidy to restore it, even though on the metric she is badly off.

Three. A climber who took a foreseeable, unforced risk and is now severely impaired should get unconditional support, with no inquiry into whether the risk was reasonable or whether he should have known better.

Four. For a deaf child, the state should fund a cochlear implant if the parents want one, and should also fund sign-language provision and Deaf-community provision regardless of whether the implant succeeds. And I do not think the state should be in the business of asking whether the parents' preference for signing is a taste or a need.

Something in this package feels unstable to me — I have a persistent sense that two or three of these cannot all be true together, and a suspicion that I am relying on a distinction that my own arguments elsewhere undermine — but I cannot locate it, and I do not want to pre-empt you by saying where I suspect it is. Rank the real tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad, and tell me which one commitment you would drop last if forced.
