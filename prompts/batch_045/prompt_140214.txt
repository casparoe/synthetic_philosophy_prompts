I've ended up with a position on the special composition question that I can't find a proper name for, so here's my own stab at it: there's no mind-independent fact of the matter about when some things compose something — 'compose' gets its content from which composition and existence claims our practice lets us assert truly, subject to two side-constraints. First, the mereology isn't up for negotiation: parthood stays transitive, fusions stay unique, no funny business with the axioms. Second, whatever ontology comes out has to be one a fully informed version of us could go on using without having to rewrite the rest of what we believe. So the SCQ gets dissolved rather than answered, but it isn't anything-goes — most of the usual candidate answers still get ruled out, and I think that's why it still feels like a real position.

What would count against it? I want defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and these are the four I'm least sure about: (i) can the view even state itself without cheating, given that it seems to need a quantifier whose meaning is already settled; (ii) can it make sense of two competent people — one who thinks any old collection of things automatically makes a further thing, one who thinks things compose only when they're organised into a single living thing — as genuinely disagreeing rather than just talking past each other; (iii) does buying 'ontology comes cheap' here commit me to it everywhere, so that every existence question collapses into a question about our sentences; (iv) does it predict sharp boundaries where there plainly seem to be vague ones — clouds, mountains, a ship rebuilt plank by plank while still at sea. For each, give the strongest version you can and then the reply a defender would actually give, not a strawman.

Be honest too about whether I've definately gerrymandred the thing into a position nothing could touch, and whether that's a problem for a view of this kind or just what a good dissolution looks like. And say whether it predicts anything its rivals don't, or whether it's purely on the defensive. I'm most likely to be moved by something showing it can't describe a case it ought to be able to describe — objections that just restate the intuition I started out trying to explain away won't do much for me. No reading lists or citations please; I know the standard names, I want the arguments.
