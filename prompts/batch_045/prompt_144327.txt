I'm on the team that enrolls patients in a study in the emergency room, and I keep having to make judgment calls about when a signature is a real yes, so I want to know what my own verdicts add up to. Here they are.

- She signs after I tell her the drug may cause headaches, and I leave out that in the pilot one in twenty had migraines bad enough to miss work. Not valid.
- She signs, and I've overheard her son in the corridor telling her to just do it, it'll keep the doctor happy. I say nothing. Valid, I think, though it makes me uneasy.
- She signs because she's picked up, wrongly, and I know it, that joining is the only way she'll get to see a specialist; she'd be referred either way and I don't correct her. Not valid, and I'd be at fault.
- She signs believing the study is funded by a charity she supports. It's funded by a company she boycotts, we don't advertise the funder, and she'd refuse if she knew. Valid, I think — the funder isn't part of what she's agreeing to. But its the same shape as the previous one: a false belief I know about, decisive for her, that I leave alone.
- She's on morphine and consents to one extra blood draw: fine. Same patient, same morphine, consents to the full eighteen-month protocol: not fine.
- We tell her that most people in her situation take part, which is true and which we say partly because it works. She signs. Valid — I can't see how a true sentence invalidates anything, but it bugs me.
- Six months in she clearly wants out: flinches, goes quiet, asks when it ends, keeps showing up, never says the words. I think her consent has lapsed even though she never withdrew it.
- We re-ask every month as required and she says yes every time, but I'm fairly sure the only thing holding the yes up is that saying no after three yeses would feel rude. No idea what to say about this one.

Is there one principle, or a short list, that gets all of these, stated sharply enough that it also rules on: consent given up front for samples to be used in any future study we like, consent given to a research assistant the patient takes to be a doctor, and a yes we get at 3am from someone who was asleep ten minutes earlier? Tell me too where I'm confusing invalid with valid but I shouldn't take it — I suspect some of these are that. And commit: give me one account, say which of my verdicts it forces me to drop, and don't hand me a menu or an it-depends. I'd rather lose two verdicts than end up with a principle that has eight clauses.
