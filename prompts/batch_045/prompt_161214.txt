I'm one of those longtime LW/ACX types who still goes to the monthly rationalist meetup in my city, and last night I got into an argument I can't stop chewing on. Someone put forward an evo-psych explanation of a pretty specific human pattern — the claim was that male sexual jealousy tracks cues of infidelity more than it tracks any actual cost to the relationship, and that this is an adaptation shaped by ancestral paternity uncertainty. Another guy shut it down with what he clearly thought was a knockdown: "Popper already settled this. Darwinism isn't falsifiable, it's a metaphysical research programme, not a scientific theory, so any adaptationist story you build on top of it is unfalsifiable by construction." And when I said I vaguely recalled Popper walking that back later, he waved it off: that recantation was about evolution-in-general or common descent, not about natural selection, the mechanism, which is the part that's supposedly tautological. Nobody had a laptop so we left it there. I looked the line up afterwards and the actual sentence is: I have come to the conclusion that Darwinism is not a testable scientific theory, but a metaphysical research programme—a possible framework for testable scientific theories. And there's a later one that goes: I have changed my mind about the testability and logical status of the theory of natural selection; and I am glad to have an opportunity to make a recantation. I'm like 80% sure both are real but I have no idea where or when exactly, and I definitely can't reconstruct what Popper thought the *difference* between them was.

So: please act as a professional philosopher of science who also knows the history of 20th-century biology, and start by brainstorming, before you organize anything, as many distinct precise claims as you can find hiding inside that one sentence — "Darwinism isn't falsifiable, so anything built on it isn't science either." A few I've been kicking around myself: the tautology worry (fitness defined by survival, so the slogan about survival of the fittest does no work — and I read somewhere that Popper treated that Spencer catchphrase as if it *were* the definition of selection, which if true is a big deal for why he thought what he thought); the "no prohibition" worry (selection on its own forbids no outcome without auxiliaries about heritable variation, population structure, and constraints, and Popper himself only ever tested theories against background knowledge, so maybe nothing here is special to biology); the demarcation step (unfalsifiable = not empirical science, and whether that was ever meant to imply "not worth taking seriously," which I suspect is the part doing the real work in the argument); the "just-so story" reading (that the problem is with particular adaptationist hypotheses, not with the core theory — Gould and Lewontin's spandrels complaint, sort of, though my friend says that's a different argument and he might be right); and the flat conflation of evolution-happened with selection-is-the-mechanism, given that Popper plainly accepted common descent and thought the Mendelian stuff was well tested. Give me the menu with verdicts: which readings are trivial, which are substantive and defensible, which are probably false, and which one the guy at the meetup most likely meant. Most importantly, tell me which readings would actually earn him the conclusion he wanted about *that specific jealousy hypothesis* and which wouldn't get him there even if true, and — this is the part I'd actually take back to the next meetup — what extra commitments someone asserting that hypothesis would have to add (measured heritability? a population comparison? a competing by-product or cultural-transmission story? an explicit claim about universals?) before it could be tested at all.

Since you can't look anything up: for each reading and each verdict, put a rough confidence number next to it, and single out the one thing in your answer you're least sure of — I'm guessing it'll be either whether the recantation exists in the form I quoted or what Popper thought a "metaphysical research programme" was still good *for*, but tell me honestly whichever it is. Don't hedge everything uniformly; I want to know where the real uncertainty is.
