I am a novelist finishing a book whose central device is this: my protagonist travels back and succeeds — the gun does not jam, the timeline does not branch — in killing her own grandfather before her mother's conception. The causal route to her own existence is thereby deleted, and yet she persists: first-personally continuous, speaking in the first person, reporting vivid memories of a childhood that now has no causal history behind it. I am told that this is simply the grandfather paradox, and I do not think it is: the standard dispute concerns whether such a killing could be performed, whereas my scene stipulates that it was and asks what is left standing afterward.

Give me a number: your credence that the narrator continues to be the same person who lived that childhood, and that her apparent recollections of it are genuine memories rather than something else that merely presents itself as memory. I do not want the answer that it depends on one's theory of personal identity; I want the dependence made explicit and priced. Decompose it into at least the following terms, each with a number and one line of justification: that personal identity over time is at least partly a matter of causal or historical continuity rather than psychological continuity alone; that memory, in order to be memory instead of confabulation, requires an appropriate causal connection to the event remembered; that an entity can coherently exist while lacking any causal origin or ancestry, as in the ungrounded causal loops the bilking argument is meant to exclude; and that the felt first-person continuity my narrator reports could in principle be produced by something other than the history she takes it to record.

Then combine those into the single number I asked for, and tell me which term the result is most sensitive to — which sub-question, resolved the other way, would move you furthest. Finally, say plainly which book you think I should write: the one in which she is self-deceived about her own past, or the one in which she remembers truly and is simply a person with no history behind her. I would rather have the odds than an assurance that both are defensible.
