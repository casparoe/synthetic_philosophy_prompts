So my brother died in March — or rather he was declared dead in March, by brain criteria, after a bleed, and then for two more days he lay there with a ventilator, a beating heart, warm hands, while they got the transplant teams lined up. My little sister has never accepted it. She says he died when they turned the machine off, and some days I think she's right and some days I think she's obviously wrong and I really don't want to have that fight in front of a room full of people.

The hospital has asked me to speak at their donor remembrance thing in April, five minutes, and the room will be about half donor families and half the ICU and transplant people who were in the room with us. Two things I want to say something honest about and not wriggle out of:

One: the coordinator floated this option, fairly new policy at that hospital, where a family can ask for a second determination — leave the brain-death testing exactly as it was, but also withdraw and wait for the heart to stop, so the time on the death certificate is when the monitor flatlined and not two days earlier. My sister wanted that. I said no, it would be theatre. i still don't know who was right, and I want to say something about what waiting would actually have done and what it wouldn't have.

Two: during the recovery they gave him drugs so his body wouldn't move, and that one detail has done more damage in my family than anything else — if he was already dead, why does he need anything at all? I want to answer that without it sounding like a dodge.

Write the five minutes. It has to comfort the families in the room without making the clinical half wince, and it can't turn into a mini-lecture on the criteria.
