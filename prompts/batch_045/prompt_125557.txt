hey — hope this is the right kind of question. i'm a backend engineer, no philosophy background, got into meditation a few years back and the no-self thing (anatta) actually matches what i think i see when i sit. a guy in my sits group did a philosophy degree and keeps hitting me with an argument i can't answer, and the annoying part is the conclusion is something neither of us believes.

his argument, cleaned up a bit:

(1) anatta says a person is nothing over and above a stream of momentary psychophysical events — the aggregates — with no further fact about who the stream belongs to and nothing that persists through it.
(2) a later mental event is a genuine memory of an earlier experience only if the rememberer is connected to that experience by more than mere causation. if a neurologist zaps your brain so you get a vivid apparent memory of a beach trip that someone else actually took, and that trip is what caused the zap, you don't thereby remember that trip.
(3) but on anatta the only relations between a later moment and an earlier experience are causal, plus similarity and continuity of the stream. there's no self for the connection to consist in.
(4) so on anatta nothing is ever a genuine memory of anything. there are only memory-shaped events.
(5) but we obviously do remember things. a theory that says we never do is just false.
(6) so anatta is false.
(7) and it gets worse: nothing in (2) through (5) turns on any specifically Buddhist claim, so every 'it's just continuity' style view of a person goes down too, and you're stuck with a persisting owner-ish thing that does the remembering. which is nuts.

so it proves way more than he wants — it doesn't only kill anatta, it forces some kind of soul, and neither of us wants one.

i want to keep (1) and (5). my gut says (2) and (3) are quietly using 'caused by' in two different ways and that's where the slide is, but when he asks me what exactly the difference is, i end up just repeating 'the right kind of causation' like a mantra, which he correctly says is not a answer.

two things i'd like. first: which step do you reject, and say plainly what rejecting it commits me to — including whether i have to give up anything i actually care about on the cushion. second: run my own case through whatever you land on. i have a service that snapshots its state, is down for an hour, then comes back up and replays its write-ahead log. it wakes up with entries that behave exactly like memories and were produced by the right sort of chain, but there was a gap where nothing was running, and i could have restored two copies from the same snapshot, in which case both would 'remember' the same event. is that thing remembering, or did continuity break and something else that talks like it took over? does your fix for the argument give the same answer at the service level as at the person level, and if not, why isn't that a double standard?
