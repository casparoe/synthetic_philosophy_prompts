Hey — I'm a PhD student trying to get clear on what exactly generalises in Moore's paradox, and I've been testing it on two cases I never see discussed.

(1) Graded: "It'll probably rain tomorrow, but I'm only about 40% confident it will." Off, but nowere near as bad as "It's raining and I don't believe it."
(2) Plural: a four-person committee signs off on a statement reading "We're likely to miss the deadline, though not one of us individually expects we will." Here the Moorean absurdity seems to soften, or at least change shape.

Can you explain what's actually doing the work in the original case — first-person authority, the transparency of belief to a subject who is deliberating, the truth-aim of belief, the norm of assertion, whatever you think it is — and then run that account against these two? Concretely: is (1) the same phenomenon in a weaker key, so that Moore-paradoxicality comes in degrees and tracks the gap between the confidence you voice and the confidence you have? Or is it just doxastic fragmentation wearing the same clothes? And if (2) really isn't paradoxical, does that show the paradox essentially needs a single subject who could in principle settle whether p just by deliberating — so it can't bottom out in a rule about what you may assert?

Please skip the usual menu-of-diagnoses rundown (logical contradiction vs pragmatic contradiction vs mere unassertibility). Give me the diagnosis you'd actually defend, and say where it bites on the two variants.
