I'm in eleventh grade and doing an independent project on the philosophy of perception, and I've argued myself into a corner. As I understand the setup, in the color phi display a red dot is flashed at one spot and, a fraction of a second later, a green dot at a nearby spot, and people report a single dot gliding across and changing color partway along the path — a color at a place where no dot of that color, indeed no dot at all, was ever shown. Dennett's two stories about this are the Stalinesque one, where the visual system finishes assembling the moving, color-changing dot before any of it reaches consciousness, so nothing is consciously experienced and then quietly amended, and the Orwellian one, where there is a brief conscious experience that doesn't match the final story and memory of it is then overwritten.

Here is the trap. I made up a probe: play a short tone at a variable delay after the first frame and ask the subject to name the dot's color right at the tone, hoping that short delays would come out red and long delays greenish, catching the experience before the revision. (1) Any report takes time to produce, so by the time the subject answers, the second frame has already been processed and the answer may itself have been revised — the probe is just one more stimulus subject to the same ambiguity, and adding a probe on top of the probe only restarts the regress. (2) So no experiment that works by asking people what they experienced can ever tell the two stories apart. (3) Experiments that don't ask — reaction times, EEG, anything purely behavioral or neural — only help if you already know which measurable event goes with which conscious experience, and you can only learn that from people's reports, so they inherit the same problem. (4) So no experiment of any kind could ever tell the two stories apart. (5) If nothing we could ever do could distinguish two hypotheses, there is no fact of the matter about which is true. (6) So in color phi there is no fact of the matter about whether the revision is Orwellian or Stalinesque. (7) But nothing in (1) through (5) depended on anything specific to color phi; the argument applies to every claim about when in a stream of experience something was experienced. (8) So no claim about the timing of experience is determinately true or false — including obviously safe ones like: if two words are shown several seconds apart, the first is consciously seen before the second. (9) That can't be right.

Which step should I give up, and what does giving it up cost me? If I have to choose I would rather keep (1) and (8) than (6). I suspect (5) is doing work it isn't entitled to, or else that the slide is really between (2) and (4) or between (6) and (7), but I can't locate it. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating my question.
