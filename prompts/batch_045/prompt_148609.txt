I sit on the technical committee of a certification scheme for farmed seafood, and after two years of meetings I want a diagnosis of our standstill rather than a verdict on who is right. One camp argues for incremental welfare reform inside existing production: percussive or electrical stunning at harvest instead of asphyxiation in ice slurry or carbon dioxide narcosis, shorter pre-slaughter fasting, lower stocking densities and better water quality in both net pens and recirculating systems, non-chemical sea-lice control, electrical stunning of decapods before boiling or splitting. The other camp thinks this is close to cosmetic: on their accounting the dominant term is how many animals exist and for how long, and the levers that matter are the ones that reduce the number of farmed animals — demand shifts, alternative proteins, refusing to certify systems whose cumulative grow-out mortality runs into the double digits, species and production models that need far fewer lives per tonne of protein. What makes the impasse strange is how much they agree on: they are willing to stipulate, for the sake of argument, that decapod crustaceans are sentient; they agree that finfish have nociceptors and show the familiar physiological and behavioural signature of chronic stress — elevated cortisol, appetite suppression, fin erosion, altered opercular and swimming patterns; they agree that death by ice slurry or CO2 is a bad death and that nobody has a validated method for rendering a shrimp insensible before it is killed; and they agree that a system with fewer animals living better lives is preferable to a system with more animals living worse lives. They have not moved an inch on what any of this implies for what we should certify.

My own hypothesis, which I want tested rather than confirmed, is that there is no single value disagreement here but four separate ones, and that only one of them is moral in the ordinary sense. First, an empirical question about the shape of the harm over the production cycle: whether time-integrated negative affect is dominated by the terminal minutes at harvest or by months of low-grade background stress, crowding, disease and handling — call it the tail-versus-background question. Second, an accounting question about the unit of aggregation: animals multiplied by valence-days, with an explicit hedonic zero; or lives as units, each judged worth living or not worth living; or some threshold view on which only time spent below a specified valence counts and good time merely fails to compensate. Third, a question about what welfare is — felt affect, proper functioning, or the animal's opportunity to exercise the repertoire its evolved organisation equips it for — which I suspect is doing more work than anyone admits and may be partly verbal. Fourth, a theory-of-change question about whether welfare improvement reduces or increases the number of animals farmed in the medium run; note that reform's effects seem to pull against each other here, since measures that improve feed conversion and survival reduce the number of lives needed per tonne while the same measures lower the moral cost of eating farmed seafood and may raise total consumption, and I cannot get either camp to say which effect they expect to dominate or why.

Please tell me which of these disagreements is actually load-bearing for the certification decision, whether any of them is verbal, ill-posed, or a pair of different questions sharing a name, and whether my four-way split conflates things that should come apart or splits what is really one thing. Then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a shared unit of accounting, a particular estimate — a demand elasticity, a time-budget or mortality decomposition, a decision-relevant result from the affective-state literature such as judgement-bias or conditioned-place-preference work — a conceptual distinction, or a change of question altogether. I would rather you engaged the two positions as I have described them than surveyed the literature, and if you conclude that some part of this is a case where no argument should be expected to move either side, I would like that said plainly and with reasons rather than papered over with a compromise.
