I am an undergraduate in a seminar on evolution and explanation. We have been working through Dennett's contrast between cranes and skyhooks and his caution about greedy reductionism, and I have discovered that my own judgments about which explanations are respectable do not line up with any principle I can actually state. I would like you to help me extract one, or to tell me which of my verdicts I have to abandon.

The principle I have been working with, which I will call the Borrowing Test: an explanation is a skyhook when it does its explanatory work by positing, as primitive, the very thing that was supposed to be explained — mind, purpose, meaning, agency, or designedness. A crane does its work with machinery that does not already contain the explanandum. I like the test because it seems to get the easy cases right, and because it explains why the accusation of skyhook is a specific charge and not just a synonym for "wrong."

Here are my verdicts on the cases, roughly in the order I thought of them:

1. The adaptive complexity of the vertebrate eye, explained by cumulative selection over many small variations — crane; my paradigm case.
2. Vitalism, an élan vital that organizes matter into living form — skyhook; my other paradigm case.
3. Intelligent design creationism, as an explanation of biological complexity — skyhook, and the Borrowing Test seems to say why: the designer has exactly the competence we were trying to account for.
4. Maxwell's demon defused by Landauer's principle, the demon's information having to be erased at a cost of about kT ln 2 per bit — crane, and a satisfying one, because the thing that looked like a free lunch gets paid for out of the same thermodynamic budget as everything else.
5. The Baldwin effect: individual plasticity or learning shifts the selective environment, and selection then consolidates what was originally learned, without any inheritance of acquired characters — I want to say crane, but it looked suspicious for a century precisely because it resembles Lamarckism, and I cannot say what makes it a crane rather than Lamarckism wearing a lab coat.
6. Teleosemantics: representational content grounded in proper functions conferred by a history of selection — crane.
7. The Chalmers-style move of taking experience itself, or some proto-phenomenal substrate of it, as a fundamental posit tied to the physical by psychophysical laws — skyhook. To me it relocates the mystery into the fundamental furniture of the universe and declares the relocation a solution.
8. Taking the masses of the elementary particles, or the Born rule, as brute fundamental posits of physics — perfectly fine, not a skyhook. Nobody protests.
9. Anthropic selection within a multiverse as an explanation of the apparent fine-tuning of the constants — I recoil from this; it strikes me as a skyhook, or at best a crane with nothing under it.
10. Explaining why a chess engine sacrificed its queen by saying that it judged the resulting attack worth the material and expected me to miss the knight fork — fine, perfectly respectable, even though it is top-down, mind-involving, and says nothing about weights or activations.
11. Explaining the origin of life as "given the right chemistry and enough time, self-replicating systems got going" — crane, even though no one can spell out the steps and I have no mechanism to point at.
12. "In-context learning emerged at scale," offered in a machine-learning paper as an account of a capability — not a skyhook, I think, but not an explanation either; it is a label pasted onto the thing that needs explaining. My professor calls these placeholders, and I want to know whether a placeholder is a third category or just a crane we have not built yet.
13. Integrated information theory, or the free energy principle, offered as unifying explanations of brain function — I genuinely cannot tell. Both are mathematically defined and quantitative, which makes them look like cranes; but I worry that the mathematics is decorative and that these are skyhooks that have been laundered by being written in equations. My professor's phrase is "laundered skyhooks," and his question is how we would ever detect the laundering.
14. The idea of free-floating rationales: the claim that a design feature in nature can be explained by a reason that no individual ever entertains, the reason being there for selection to find. This looks to me like mentality smuggled in with no mind attached, but I am told it is just selection described from a different angle.

And separately, on greedy reductionism, three verdicts about grain:
15. Explaining a courtship ritual by the neurochemistry of the participants — I dislike it, but I think it is incomplete rather than wrong.
16. Explaining what a statute means by the microphysics of the ink on the page — not merely unhelpful; wrong in kind.
17. Explaining the eye by cumulative selection — exactly the right grain, not reductionist at all in the bad sense.

What I want from you:
