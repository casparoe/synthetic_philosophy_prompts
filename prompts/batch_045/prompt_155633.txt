ok here's what I want. I keep having the same argument about whether LLMs are agents, simulators, or something else entirely, and it never moves — not online, not with my collaborators, who are otherwise pretty reasonable people. Every time I think I've nailed down what one side is denying, it slides into something else. So: is this a genuine disagreement, or are there several different questions going around under one label?

Here's the split I've started using, which I don't see other people using and which might be wrong. There seem to be at least three candidate bearers of "agent" here: the trained network itself (weights plus a forward pass, the thing that puts a distribution over next tokens); the interlocutor that shows up in a particular conversation, the voice you're actually talking to, which swings a lot with context; and the deployed system — model plus system prompt plus scaffolding plus tools plus the people who tuned it and are running it. My hunch is that "it's just a simulator" is basically a claim about the first bearer and mostly right there, while "obviously it's an agent" is a claim about the second or the third, and both sides think they're contradicting each other when they're quantifying over different things.

What makes me doubt that tidy diagnosis: even after you fix the bearer, people still disagree, and the disagreement seems to track what level of description or comparison class they adopt. Seen from inside the conversation the interlocutor has plans, tracks a goal over thousands of tokens, and sometimes does things that look like deception. Seen from the weights up, there's a conditional distribution and nothing that persists between calls. Nothing in the situation appears to pick out one of those descriptions as the correct one. That makes me think the actual structure is closer to Skolem's paradox: "agent" isn't absolute, there's no description-independent fact of the matter, only facts relative to a level — and both sides are right relative to theirs. That's my hypothesis and I want it stress-tested, not agreed with.

So what kind of stalemate is this? Verbal? Substantive but unresolvable? A hidden empirical question about how stable elicited goals are across contexts that nobody has actually run? Or is my Skolem analogy doing zero work and just decorating a mess?

Please don't give me a survey of positions or the greatest hits of the discourse, and don't cite anything — I've read it all, I'll fill references in myself. I want a diagnosis with reasons attached, and then I want you to say what would count as progress given that diagnosis: a distinction, an experiment, a change of question, or an argument that nothing here should be expected to move and why. And if the bearers split is the right move, what does it predict that I could go test — say, the same weights with two different system prompts, where I'd happily call one of the resulting things an agent and the other not?
