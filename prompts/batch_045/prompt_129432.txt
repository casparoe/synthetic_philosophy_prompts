I'm the director of program ops at a mid-sized regional workforce nonprofit, and over the next year every contract we have is moving to outcomes-based payment: we propose the numbers, the state agency has to approve them, and somewhere between 40 and 60 percent of our funding sits on top of them. So a few times a month I'm going to be sitting there with a proposed metric in front of me, about a day to think about it, a board that wants money at risk to look like confidence, and a program staff who will quietly reorganize their entire week around whatever I pick. I'd like a written procedure my team can run on each candidate metric before it goes into a contract.

The problem I keep running into is that "watch out for gaming" is too vague to be useful, and the standard advice I've seen either collapses into "measure everything" (we can't — we can properly audit maybe two things a quarter, the rest we get self-reported) or into "just use judgment" (which is what got us here). I've started sorting failure modes myself and I think there are at least five different things going on: hitting the number by serving people we'd have served anyway; sorting who we serve so the easy cases are the ones who count; the ratchet, where a good year becomes next year's floor; collateral, where something nobody put a number on gets worse while the headline improves; and what I think of as plain decay, where the proxy just stops tracking the thing we cared about over a couple of years even though nobody is cheating. I'd like you to clean that taxonomy up, throw away the parts that don't earn their keep, and add whatever's missing.

What I actually need:

- the questions in the order to ask them, and what counts as a real answer to each one rather than a shrug
- where the procedure just stops with a no, and where it has to hand the question to a person because it's a values call and not a factual one
- a distinction between metrics that are safe to put money on and metrics that are fine to report but must never carry payment — and what to do when the funder insists that at least three of the payment metrics be ours and at least half the money be at risk, so "use none of them" isn't available
- a one-page version, plus a one-sentence plain-English "why this step" line under each question, because I have to defend the whole thing to a board member who will ask why we're making this so complicated
- an honest note on which steps are judgment calls wearing a lab coat, and what the procedure deliberately doesn't try to do

Then run the whole thing on three candidates: share of participants still employed 90 days after exit; median hourly wage at placement; and number of participants who complete a credential. That last one is the funder's favorite and my staff's favorite, and I have a bad feeling about it, so if the procedure says it can't carry money, say so plainly. And tell me what I'm supposed to do mid-contract when a metric that passed the procedure starts to look wrong, since nothing we sign is shorter than three years.
