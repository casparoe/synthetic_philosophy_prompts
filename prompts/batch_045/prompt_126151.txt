I don't have any academic background, but I got nerd-sniped by whether the wavefunction is a real thing or just a way of tracking what we know (I think the jargony words are psi-ontic for real and psi-epistemic for knowledge, but sorry if I got them mixed up). Could you write a short dialogue between two sharp people, one on each side, who actually respond to each other instead of strawmanning? To push past the usual examples, have them argue about a case I made up: a particle can be prepared with the exact same wavefunction either by randomly picking between two sub-routines X and Y that both produce it, or by a single sub-routine Z that also produces it. If the wavefunction is real, X, Y, and Z don't differ in reality; if it's just knowledge, the random way really leaves it in an X or Y condition while the Z way leaves it in a different one. Now imagine a difference detector can tell after the fact whether the mixed X/Y way or the pure Z way was used, even though both give the same wavefunction and same odds for every ordinary measurement. Have them argue about whether this detector is possible, what it would show if it existed, and whether the knowledge-view side can just say it's impossible or doesn't matter.
