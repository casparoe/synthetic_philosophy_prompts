hey, I need a piece of satirical writing for something I'm putting together. Could you write a mock FAQ page — aim for 700–900 words — for a government-ish body called something like the Bureau of Karmic Reconciliation, the agency that administers moral desert across lifetimes. think of it as a tax authority for karma: assessments, audits, appeals, payment plans, a customer portal that's down a lot.

Voice is deadpan corporate-bureaucratic, motto along the lines of "we don't judge, we reconcile." It should never wink at the reader and never quite say the obvious thing. Format it as 8–10 Q&A entries, plus a three-item glossary at the bottom in the same voice.

The rule I actually care about: every joke has to come from a real feature of karma-as-desert, exaggerated rather than invented, so that someone who knows the literature thinks "fine, that's fair" instead of "that's a strawman." Some things I'd like worked in:

- Strict desert seems to need the same agent to persist, but the tradition also denies a permanent self. Have the Bureau handle this the way a bank handles an account migration — identity as continuity of a causal stream, the flame-passed-between-candles idea rendered as a form field. One customer insists they're being billed for a person they have never been, and the Bureau's answer is cheerfully technical and does not actually solve it.
- Only intentional action bears fruit. There's an "Intent Verified" checkbox; the FAQ should show the difference it makes, and maybe a guy who caused something terrible gets his case reclassified as non-assessable for want of intent and is weirdly unsatisfied by that.
- Fruits ripen late and out of order, across lives. So: a ripening schedule, deferrals, interest, and the fact that your current suffering may be the fruit of an action that isn't in your visible history because you have no access to prior-life records.
- The fruit resembles the seed, the eye-for-an-eye logic. Give me an actual tariff schedule with absurd-but-faithful conversions (slander → N lifetimes of being systematically disbelieved, etc.).
- Merit transfer, sold as a feature: gift your merit to a relative like loyalty points, with a small processing fee. This is the entry where the Bureau's own rules visibly break — if merit is transferable it isn't desert — but it should be written as if it's the product they're proudest of.
- No discoverable first action, so no opening balance and no statute of limitations; someone asks who opened their account and gets brushed off.
- The mass-casualty question. A tsunami, an earthquake, a famine — the Bureau explains it as collective ripening in neutral service language ("we understand this can feel personal; it isn't"). Let this one land properly dark; it should be recognizable as the victim-blaming that critics charge the doctrine with, without the Bureau ever noticing.
- Karma is supposed to be an impersonal natural law with no judge and no punisher, so the Bureau keeps insisting it isn't punishing anyone while using entirely punitive vocabulary — assessment, penalty, arrears, enforcement.

Couple of constraints: no named real people, invented spokespeople are fine. Keep the Bureau internally consistent between entries — don't let it escape into "it's a mystery, have faith," the whole joke is that it's rule-bound and procedural. A reader who knows nothing about the philosophy should still be able to tell what's being made fun of, and a reader who does should be able to tell that you got the doctrine right.
