Im a software engineer with no philosophy background, reading about theories of well-being on my own, and I'm stuck on something.

I think I get the basic shape of the experience machine argument: if how good your experiences feel is all that matters for how well your life goes for you, you should plug in, most people say they wouldnt, so hedonism is in trouble. Fine. But my friend and I made up a version and I cant tell what to do with it.

Its not all-or-nothing, its a dial. At 0 your life is completely normal. At 100 your in the pod full-time and nothing you experience tracks anything real. Every notch up makes your experience a bit better and your grip on reality a bit weaker — at 20 a few of your most vivid memories are of things that never happened, at 50 the people you love are simulations who behave indistinguishably from real ones, and so on. You never get told what notch your on.

What I cant figure out is what question I'm even asking when I say I'd rather be low on the dial. Is being in real contact with things just one more ingredient in a good life, so there should be some notch where the nicer experience exactly pays for the loss? Or is it the kind of thing that cant be weighd against experience at all, more like a filter on what even counts as a benefit? And heres the part that breaks my brain: if it is tradeable and the notches are tiny, then whatever makes me refuse 100 should also make me refuse 1, which is crazy — so it looks like it cant be tradeable. But if it isnt tradeable, I dont see that there's any objection to hedonism left, since the whole point was supposed to be about how well life goes for you, not about some separate scoreboard.

Can you unpack whats actually being asked here, what the live options are, and where the real disagreement sits?
