I was half-listening to a philosophy podcast on the drive home and the guest gave this run-down of the liberty stuff, and I've been turning it over since. Roughly what he said:

"There are three ideas wearing the same word. Negative liberty is being left alone — that's basically just a matter of how many doors are open to you. Positive liberty is being your own master, which in practice means taking part in ruling yourself, so it's really the democratic one. Berlin's worry was that positive liberty is the dangerous one: once you allow that somebody's real interests might differ from what they actually want, any dictator can claim to be setting them free by force, which is why liberals since have stuck to negative liberty. The republicans thought both sides missed the point — what matters isn't interference but domination, having someone in a position to push you around whether or not they ever do, so the contented slave with a kind master is still unfree. Really though that's just negative liberty with an insurance policy bolted on: it's a bet about how likely interference is, and it inherits the same blind spot, that it says nothing about whether you can actually do anything with the freedom you've got. You can see the whole three-way split come apart on a recommendation app that predicts you perfectly and quietly arranges your options so you always take the one it knew you'd take — nobody is forced, nothing is closed off, so on both the negative and the republican views you come out perfectly free, and the only complaint left is that it feels creepy."
