So I've been reading about freedom on my own time, no classes, just whatever I can find online and in a couple of secondhand books, and I keep running into three different things people seem to mean by it. One: freedom is just nobody stopping you, no interference, you get left alone. Two: freedom is being your own person, actually in charge of yourself rather than pushed around by your own ignorance or panic or circumstances, and maybe needing things like education or money before you're free at all. Three: the republican or neo-Roman one, where freedom isn't about being interfered with, it's about not having a master. Nobody should have standing power to mess with you at their own discretion, even if they happen to be decent and never use it. Tell me if I've mangled any of that.

What got me thinking is employment law, which seems to be doing something strange. In most rich countries there's a list of things you cannot contract away even if you genuinely want to and the other side is happy to pay: you can't sell yourself into bonded labour, you can't agree to work for less than the minimum, you can't sign away basic safety protections, you can't agree in advance never to join a union, you can't waive your right to sue for certain kinds of harm. But the same legal system will happily enforce a twenty-five year mortgage, a marriage, or a non-compete for a year or two after you leave a job. Then there's the flip side: a contract you signed at gunpoint gets thrown out, but a contract you signed because the alternative was loosing your home gets enforced without blinking. I had no real choice counts when a person threatens you and doesn't count when the situation does. And everywhere you hear that employment is free because you can always quit, which the law seems to rely on and not rely on at the same time, since it also regulates hard what happens inside the relationship while you're still there.

So: what view of freedom does this whole package presuppose? Go through it bit by bit. Which parts only make sense on one of the three views, and which parts would any of the three be equally fine with? Is the gun versus poverty distinction actually defensible, or just a line the courts found convenient, and if it is defensible which view does it fit and which does it quietly rule out? Does you can always quit do the work people want it to do, and under which view would quitting be enough?

Here's the part I made up and want you to take seriously. I've been thinking that the thing that matters isn't whether anyone interferes with you, or whether you're self-mastered, or even whether some individual has arbitrary power over you, but how concentrated the power over your life is. Call it the ruin test: count the number of individual people who could, on their own, at their own discretion, ruin your year, with no real recourse for you. Freedom goes down as that number goes down and as each one's power gets bigger, no matter how nice they are. So a well-paid worker with one boss who can fire them for any reason and no savings is less free than a worse-paid worker with tenure and real procedural protection, and a gig worker with two thousand clients who can each deactivate them is somewhere strange in between. Is that a fourth view, or just republicanism with a number bolted on, or positive liberty in disguise? Does it catch anything the three standard views miss, like the spread and stacking of powers rather than just whether any exists? What would employment law look like if you built it on the ruin test, and how far is that from what the law already does? Same question for the other two: if we took the republican view seriously would at-will firing, non-competes, forced arbitration and NDAs have to go, and if we took the positive view seriously does the state end up owing you a job or an income?

Be straight with me about confidence too. Say how sure you are on the main claims, roughly, and single out the one thing you're least sure of. Especially flag it if you're hazy on what the law actually is in particular countries, because I definately am and I dont want confident-sounding made-up doctrine.
