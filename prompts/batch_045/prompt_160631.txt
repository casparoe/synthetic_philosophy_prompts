I'm working on a chapter about social construction and I keep running into a distinction I dont have a word for. Take two cases. If I convince people that racial categories aren't a natural/biological kind — that they were made up and are held in place by law, habit and paperwork — part of what I'm doing politically seems to be loosening the categories' grip: the hope appears to be that once people see the construction, the thing itself starts to come apart. Now take money. If I convince people that a bill has no value built into it and that its purchasing power is entirely a matter of what everyone expects everyone else to accept, nothing happens; prices dont move. But if I convince people that everyone else is about to lose confidence in the currency, things move very fast. So in the first case the construction seems to need its participants to be wrong about something, or at least to not have the constructionist story in view, while the second runs fine under full transparency and is only disturbed by a shift in higher-order expectations.

My hypothesis is that the variable is whether the kind depends on people's first-order belief that it tracks something non-social, versus depending only on iterated expectations about what others will do — but I cant tell if that's the right variable, or whether the real difference is about whether the kind sorts people or objects, or whether it's just about how fast the feedback runs. Does this thing have a name? I know the general social-ontology material about collective acceptance, and I know there's work on how classifying people changes the people classified, but neither seems to be exactly this. I dont want a bare label: I want what the distinction actually consists in, the nearest established concepts and how what I've described differs from each, and where the boundary sits for mixed cases — a gender category that survives unmasking for some speakers and dissolves for others, or "electability", which looks like it lands somewhere in between. If there's no term for it, coin one and define it with its edge cases. And please write it as continuous prose, no headings, bullets, bold or tables, just like an email.
