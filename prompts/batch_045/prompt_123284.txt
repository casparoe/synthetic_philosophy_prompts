i keep going in circles on the pessimistic meta-induction, so instead of arguing about it in general i want to work one case properly.

The case is the luminiferous aether. Sketch of the facts so we're starting from the same place (correct me if i have something wrong): 19th century wave optics needed a medium for light, people worked out increasingly baroque mechanical properties for it, the Michelson-Morley experiment in 1887 failed to detect motion through it, Lorentz and FitzGerald each proposed something like length contraction to save it, and then the 1905 relativity papers get the same kinematics out of two postulates with no medium at all. I also half-remember that Einstein later, in connection with general relativity, was willing to say that space has physical properties and so something aether-ish comes back — but if i've got that wrong, ignore it, i dont want the answer to turn on it.

Here's the part i actually want you to work with. I've been trying to make the argument precise using something i call the role-inheritance test. The idea: when a posit gets abandoned, don't ask whether the word or the entity survived, ask whether anything in current science plays the role it was introduced to play. The aether had several jobs — define a preferred rest frame and make sense of absolute motion; be the thing that is waving; carry the energy and momentum of light; transmit forces across otherwise empty space. Some of those jobs died outright, some look like they still have occupants (fields carry energy and momentum, spacetime in GR has real physical structure, the vacuum isn't nothing). And the thing i can't settle is who keeps the books: the pessimistic inductivist counts posits that died and gets a high base rate of failure; the realist counts roles that eventually got filled and gets a low one; and it's the same history in both cases. So what settles the choice of counting rule, if anything does?

Concretely:

1. A disciplined mapping. For each of the jobs the aether was supposed to do, say what current physics does with it — dropped, filled by something else, or still open — and then say what that pattern is evidence for.

2. Does the selective-realism move work here? The version i have in mind says: keep the posits that were doing real predictive and explanatory work, throw out the decorative ones. Which of the aether's properties were load-bearing and which were decoration — and more importantly, is "load-bearing" readable off the situation at the time, or only once you know how the story ends? If it's only the latter, what does that do to the move?

3. Structural realism: what survives a transition is the structure, roughly the equations, not the ontology. Does that help with this case, given that Lorentz-style aether theory and special relativity were empirically equivalent over the relevant phenomena? What does that stretch of empirical equivalence do to the argument — does it make the discard look less like a refutation and more like a re-description, and does that help or hurt the realist?

4. My role-inheritance thing: is it a genuine constraint on the meta-induction, or just a relabeling of the disagreement in language i happen to find more comfortable? What would a historical pattern look like that would force both sides to agree on a base rate — or is there no such pattern because the disagreement is upstream of the cases?

5. And back onto the theory: does this case strengthen the pessimistic meta-induction (a hugely successful research program, abandoned and not coming back), weaken it (most of the job description survived under other names), or show that the argument was always a fight about bookkeeping rather than about how science goes?

Last wrinkle, take it or leave it: a friend defends something he calls depreciation realism — you hold current successful theories to be approximately true, but your confidence in any given claim decays with time since acceptance, calibrated to whatever the historical rate of abandonment is. Is that coherent? My worry is that it either collapses into plain realism plus an odd bookkeeping ritual nobody follows, or it should be pushing you to disbelieve specific claims of specific current theories, and nobody does that either. Say which way it goes and why.

Stay concrete, and where the framework has nothing to say about this case, say so instead of making it talk. No reading lists or citations please, i just want the reasoning.
