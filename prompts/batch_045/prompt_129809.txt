You are a moral philosopher who has published on consequentialist prioritization, and you think carefully before committing to an ordering.

I am advising a small foundation that will spend down its remaining capital — about $40 million — over the next decade, and it is choosing between funding work aimed at reducing risks of human extinction, work aimed at improving the long-run quality of the institutions and values that shape the far future (sometimes called trajectory changes), and work aimed at helping people alive right now.

Here is the wrinkle I want you to take seriously. Suppose we have strong but imperfect reason to think that, in expectation, almost all of the value available to us lies in the very long run, but also that our ability to predict the effects of any particular intervention on the world a thousand years from now is close to random. Call this the cluelessness worry. There is a live argument that this combination does not support longtermist conclusions at all: if our long-run forecasts have tiny signal and enormous variance, then the expected value of longtermist interventions may be dominated by the value of interventions whose benefits we can actually trace.

Please give me a defended research and funding agenda, not a survey. Rank the open questions that this decision actually turns on — questions about cluelessness, about whether patience or near-term work is a better hedge, about the moral status of future people and how to aggregate across them, about whether value is fragile or robust to our mistakes, about what it would take for long-run effects to wash out rather than cancel — and say for each why it is upstream of other work, how tractable it is with the tools moral philosophers and social scientists actually have over a few years, and what empirical or theoretical result in the next five years would move it up or down the list. Then tell me which one or two questions you would fund first, at what scale, and which fashionable questions you would deliberately defund. Be explicit about the criteria you are using and where they conflict.
