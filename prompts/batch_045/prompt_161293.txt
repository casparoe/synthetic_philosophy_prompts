I'm a PhD student in behavioural economics, and the second section of my dissertation chapter argues that present-biased discounting models and the philosophical notion of weakness of will are tracking two different things. I need one case that establishes this by construction rather than by assertion, and I would be grateful if you would build it for me to the specifications below.

The machinery, so we do not talk past each other. Quasi-hyperbolic discounting: a payoff dated t periods ahead is weighted βδ^t for t ≥ 1 and 1 for t = 0, with 0 < β < 1, so whatever falls in the present period receives a premium relative to exponential discounting. Call this the immediacy premium. The criterion I want to test it against: an agent is weak-willed at a time just in case she freely and intentionally does B while judging then — at the time of action, all things considered, under no coercion, ignorance or inability — that doing A would be better. The judgment must be contemporaneous with the action; having earlier planned or ranked A first is not enough.

Specifications for the case. (i) An ordinary professional setting — a ward, a lab, a studio, a court — one episode, no addiction, no psychiatric diagnosis, no literal multiple selves or two-systems machinery, no forgetting, no self-deception, and no stipulating that anyone judges anything; every judgment must be motivated by reasons the scenario actually gives the agent. (ii) Two colleagues facing the same option pair: A costs effort now and pays off more later, B pays off less now. Their discount parameters are identical, and are estimated from unrelated monetary intertemporal choices rather than inferred from their behaviour in this episode; pick β and δ and state them. At t1 both form unconditional intentions to do A with equal deliberative seriousness; at t2 both do B. (iii) Agent 1 satisfies the criterion: at t2 she still judges A better, all things considered, and does B anyway, freely and intentionally. Motivate this rather than assert it — I want the reason she judges A better to be one that survives her doing B. (iv) Agent 2 does not satisfy the criterion: between t1 and t2 she acquires a piece of information that bears on the A-versus-B comparison and is not a report about how aversive A feels now, and at t2 she judges B better on reflection. The constraint that makes this hard: the information must not suffice on its own. With the information in hand but β set to 1, she would still rank A first; the flip requires the immediacy premium. So present-bias is doing genuine causal work in her choice while the criterion still acquits her. If you think no such case can be built, say so inside item 1 and build the strongest version you can regardless — I would rather have a case than a concession.

Format. Exactly five numbered items, no preamble, no headings inside items, no closing remark. Item 1 is the scenario, a single paragraph of at most 120 words. Items 2 to 5 are at most two sentences each and do the following, in order: (2) both agents' β and δ, the dated payoffs of A and B (yours to choose), what the model predicts for each agent at t1 and t2, and one clause on why no re-parameterisation of the model can separate them; (3) the criterion's verdict on each agent, the reason agent 1's defection is akratic rather than a change of mind, and the reason acquitting agent 2 does not collapse into trusting a report the model predicts to be unreliable; (4) the single structural feature doing the work, plus both removal tests — delete the information, and set β to 1 — with what each does to the two verdicts; (5) the strongest reply available to someone who wants present-bias to be the whole story, and whether that reply is ad hoc or shows my argument needs a stronger case than this one.
