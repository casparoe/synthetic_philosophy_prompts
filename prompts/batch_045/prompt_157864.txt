Some framing so you're not guessing at what I mean by corrigible. Three things I keep running into that I dont think are the same problem, even though people use one word for all of them:

(a) Most writeups of the shutdown problem seem to imagine one operator and one physical switch. Our setup has three separate stop paths: a human hits the button; an automated watchdog kills the container when some invariant trips; and the agent itself can 'yield' — hand control back with an explanation of what it was about to do instead of plowing on. To me these are three different questions wearing one name. The button question is about not resisting. The watchdog question is about not optimising against a check you know is there. The yield question is about whether the agent can be uncertain about whether it should still be running, and whether it can say so. Is one of these the real problem, or is the interesting question how they interact — e.g. does training an agent to yield when unsure make it more or less likely to fight the button later?

(b) I have this worry I call corrigibility debt, by analogy with tech debt. A lot of the time our agents look corrigible for boring reasons: they havent been optimised hard enough yet to care, or the task is short enough that stopping is cheap. The worry is that the property is not stable under optimisation pressure or under longer horizons, and that we're reading off a behaviour that'll evaporate the moment we train harder. Is "make corrigibility stable under pressure" a better use of my two quarters than "detect, after the fact, that it has broken", or is that the wrong way to carve it?

(c) Someone pointed out to me that a shutdown attempt is also information: if the operator is reaching for the button, that's evidence I'm doing something wrong. But an agent that treats the button as evidence, and updates on it, is doing something quite different from an agent that just complies blindly — and I cant tell whether we want the updating version or the indifferent version, or some third thing where it complies but flags the disagreement.
