Q3 (25 points). Ava and Ben are forecasting whether a startup will default within the year. The state space is Ω = {ω1, ω2, ω3, ω4, ω5, ω6} with a uniform common prior μ (each state has probability 1/6). Ava's information partition is P_A = {{ω1, ω2}, {ω3, ω4}, {ω5, ω6}}; Ben's is P_B = {{ω1, ω2, ω3}, {ω4, ω5, ω6}}. Let E = {ω1, ω3, ω4} be the event that the startup defaults. Recall Aumann's agreement theorem: if two agents have a common prior and it is common knowledge at ω that Ava's posterior for E is q_A and Ben's is q_B, then q_A = q_B. (Recall also that the smallest event that is common knowledge at ω is the cell of the meet of the two partitions containing ω.)

(a) [8 points] Give each agent's posterior for E as a function of the state, and determine the cell of the meet of P_A and P_B that contains ω3. In one or two sentences, explain why the fact that their posteriors differ at ω3 does or does not contradict the agreement theorem.

(b) [9 points] Consider the actual state ω3. First suppose Ava alone publicly and truthfully announces her posterior, and Ben updates on the announcement but says nothing. Then suppose instead that both announce simultaneously, Ava saying 1 and Ben saying 2/3. In each scenario: what does each agent learn, what are their posteriors after the announcement(s), are those posteriors equal, and do the two agents end up with the same information? Explain briefly what this shows about the role of communication in the theorem.

(c) [8 points] Now suppose the priors are not exactly common but merely close: μ_A and μ_B with TV(μ_A, μ_B) ≤ ε, where TV is the maximum over events F of |μ_A(F) − μ_B(F)|. Assume the posteriors q_A and q_B for E are common knowledge at ω. Is the agreement conclusion robust — that is, must |q_A − q_B| be bounded by some function of ε alone that goes to zero as ε goes to zero, uniformly over all events and all information structures? Prove it, or give a counterexample. Then say in two sentences what your result implies about the inference from observed persistent disagreement to the falsity of the common prior assumption.

Student's answer:

(a) Under the uniform prior, P(E) = 1/2. Ava's posterior for E is 1/2 on her cell {ω1, ω2}, since only ω1 lies in E; 1 on {ω3, ω4}, since both states lie in E; and 0 on {ω5, ω6}. Ben's is 2/3 on {ω1, ω2, ω3}, since ω1 and ω3 lie in E, and 1/3 on {ω4, ω5, ω6}, since only ω4 does. So at ω3 the two posteriors are 1 and 2/3. The cell of the meet containing ω3 is all of Ω: from ω3 Ben cannot rule out ω1 or ω2, from ω2 Ava cannot rule out ω1, from ω3 Ava cannot rule out ω4, and from ω4 Ben cannot rule out ω5 and ω6, so the chain of indistinguishability runs right across the state space. The only events common knowledge at ω3 are therefore Ω itself and ∅, and neither posterior is common knowledge there. Aumann's antecedent fails, so the disagreement at ω3 is perfectly consistent with a common prior.

(b) If Ava alone announces, her announcement is informative for Ben: it tells him the state lies in {ω3, ω4}, which intersects his own cell {ω1, ω2, ω3} in {ω3}, so he learns ω3 and his posterior becomes 1. Ava's own posterior is unchanged at 1. The two posteriors now agree, and the agreement is common knowledge: after the announcement the cells are {ω3, ω4} for Ava and {ω3}, {ω4} for Ben, so the meet cell containing ω3 is {ω3}, and the event that both posteriors equal 1 is {ω3, ω4}, a union of meet cells, hence common knowledge at ω3. Notice that equal posteriors do not mean identical information: Ben knows ω3 while Ava still cannot rule out ω4. If both announce at once, the pair of announcements pins down ω3 for both of them and both end up knowing ω3, with the same posteriors of 1. In either case, though, the announcement is not really doing any work. The theorem applies to the original partitions, and since Ava and Ben share a common prior, their posteriors for E were already equal at ω3 before anyone spoke; communication only makes explicit an agreement that was latent. That, I take it, is the general moral of the result: given a common prior, disagreement between agents is always merely apparent.

(c) The theorem is robust to approximately common priors. Let C be the cell of the meet containing ω, and suppose q_A and q_B are common knowledge at ω. Since each agent's posterior is constant on C, q_A is the μ_A-weighted average of Ava's posteriors over her cells inside C, which collapses to μ_A(E | C); similarly q_B = μ_B(E | C). Now assume TV(μ_A, μ_B) ≤ ε. Conditioning is an averaging operation, and averaging cannot increase the distance between two distributions, so TV(μ_A(· | C), μ_B(· | C)) ≤ ε, and hence |q_A − q_B| ≤ ε, uniformly over every event E and every information structure. So approximate common priors yield approximate agreement, with the exact theorem recovered in the limit as ε goes to zero. This is the methodologically useful form of the result: if we observe two agents who have communicated fully and whose posteriors for E remain d apart, we are entitled to conclude that their priors were at least d apart, so persistent disagreement is direct evidence against a common prior, and the size of the observed gap measures the size of the prior divergence.
