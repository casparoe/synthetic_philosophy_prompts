I'm working through the species problem, and I keep returning to a short passage near the beginning of Chapter II of the first edition of *On the Origin of Species* (1859), which I find harder than its plain style suggests. The passage reads:

> Nor shall I here discuss the various definitions which have been given of the term species. No one definition has as yet satisfied all naturalists; yet every naturalist knows vaguely what he means when he speaks of a species. Generally the term includes the unknown element of a distinct act of creation. The term "variety" is almost equally difficult to define; but here community of descent is almost universally implied, though it can rarely be proved.

Later in the same chapter, discussing what he calls protean or polymorphic genera, Darwin remarks that in them "hardly two naturalists can agree which forms to rank as species and which as varieties."

Here is what I would like from you.

First, help me get clear on what these sentences are *doing*. Is the clause about "the unknown element of a distinct act of creation" a sociological report about Darwin's contemporaries, a criticism of them, or something he still relies on himself — and what turns on which? Why does he think the failure of definitions is compatible with every naturalist knowing "vaguely" what he means, and what is carrying the reference of the term if no definition works? And why is the definitional difficulty of *variety* handled so differently — by pointing to an implied but unprovable community of descent — rather than by the same shrug he gives to species?

Second, I want to press the passage against a case Darwin could not have envisaged, so that the question is not merely one of exegesis. Consider a large, well-studied collection of self-replicating software artefacts — call them weavers — living on a distributed network. A weaver reproduces by emitting a copy of its own specification, with occasional errors; errors are inherited. In one portion of the network, "sib" weavers, copying is strictly vertical: one parent, one offspring. In another, "mesh" weavers, a new specification is assembled by splicing fragments taken from several existing weavers, so ancestry is reticulate. There is no analogue of sex, no sterility, no hybrid inviability; but there is a compatibility filter, in that a weaver cannot splice fragments from another whose specification differs from its own beyond some rough threshold, and this threshold is itself a matter of engineering detail rather than of biology. Variation across the whole collection is continuous. Curators disagree: some recognise thousands of species, others a handful, both consistently, and from the same data.

Run the three moves of the passage against this case. Is there a fact of the matter about how many species of weaver there are, and if not, is the curators' disagreement merely verbal? Which features of the case — vertical versus reticulate transmission, the threshold on compatibility, the continuity of variation, the absence of any analogue to separate creation — are actually doing the work in your answer, and would your answer change if the mesh cluster were removed? In particular: for Darwin's readers the species concept quietly contained a creationist element; my case contains no such element. Does removing it remove part of what needed explaining, or does the puzzle survive intact?

Finally, be explicit about the epistemic status of what you give me. Separate what is settled about the passage and its context from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this material — for instance, how far Darwin is deflationary about species, and whether that is a semantic claim, a methodological one, or a claim about what exists. Do not present a contested reading as the consensus one; tell me which reading is dominant, if there is one, and what would have to be true for the alternatives to win. And mark clearly, throughout, where you are extrapolating from the text to my example rather than reporting what the text says — I would rather have an inference flagged as yours than a guess dressed up as exegesis.
