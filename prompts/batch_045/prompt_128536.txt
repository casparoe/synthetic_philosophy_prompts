My field crew samples a roughly circular marsh, a couple hundred metres across, by laying down straight transect lines and walking whatever stretch of the line falls inside it. We use the fraction of the walked line that crosses a certain invasive reed to estimate what fraction of the marsh is covered by it. Up to now we've picked each line by choosing two random points on the edge and running a rope between them, which is easy to do in the field. A stats guy on the grant says its biased and we should instead pick a random compass bearing and a random distance of the line from the centre, and lay it perpendicular to that. A third person says just pick a random point anywhere inside the marsh and make that the midpoint of the line. All three sound like "a random line through the marsh" to me. The marsh isn't a clean circle either, and the reed does seem to creep in from the edges. Who's actually right, does the choice really change our numbers, and is there any non-arbitrary way to settle it, or is it just taste?
