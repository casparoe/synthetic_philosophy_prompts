So — I'm 71, retired two years ago after 35 years in physiology (cardiac muscle, mostly), and I've started sitting in on a philosophy reading group at the local university, the kind where they let auditing retirees talk. They're doing a unit on weakness of will and I got annoyed in the way scientists get annoyed: hyperbolic discounting kept getting waved around as if the economists had settled a question that I think they mostly renamed. I've decided to try to write this up properly — a paper, call it 8000 words, three months of part-time work, which is about what my back and my attention span will support. Nobody is funding me and I have no advisor, so I need a proposal mostly to keep myself honest. Can you write me one I can work from, pitched at the level where I'd have to defend it to the one philosopher in the group who is definately smarter than me.

Here's my half-formed gripe, such as it is. As I understand the present-bias story (the beta–delta thing), it predicts that preferences reverse as a reward gets closer: from a distance I pick the apple, up close the cake wins. Fine as far as it goes. But I think there are at least three things in that neighborhood that get run together, and the model may only speak to one of them:

(1) forming a plan to do A later, then not doing A when the time arrives;
(2) at the very moment of action, judging that A is better while doing B anyway;
(3) just failing to act, or getting captured by whatever is in front of your face, where there doesn't seem to be a judgment in play at all.

My suspicion is that a discounting model is really a model of (1), that it gets quietly stretched to cover (3), and that it has nothing to say about (2) — because at the moment of action the delay is zero, and any discount function you like hands you full value at zero delay. So to get (2) out of the model you have to say either that the agent's judgment flipped along with her preference (in which case there was no akrasia to explain, just a change of mind with a funny shape to it), or that she is running two evaluations with two different discount rates — in which case the explanatory work is being done by the split, not by the curve, and the curve is decoration.

The second thing I want to press on, and I genuinely can't tell whether philosophers have already dealt with it: the discount function is fitted from choice behavior, including the lapses, and then cited as the explanation of the lapses. In my old field that would have been called a fitting artifact until somebody produced an independent measurement. Relatedly — and this is the one bit of my own I'd like you to take seriously rather than smooth over — I want to propose seperating the delay to a reward from whether the reward is currently available: perceptible, in hand, already plated, smelling up the room. Cake in front of me now versus identical cake arriving by a machine on a fixed ninety-second delay is the same delay structure but not, I'd bet, the same pull, and I don't know whether the standard model has any room for that difference or whether it has to treat it as noise.

What I want from the proposal: the question put precisely enough that it has a determinate answer; why the standard moves (the discounting model, and the usual philosophical story about acting against one's own better judgment) don't already close it; what I'd actually do month by month, given that I'm not running experiments and have no subjects except my own bad habits; what the two or three possible conclusions are and what kind of consideration would decide between them; and an honest paragraph on how this project fails — including the possibility that the whole thing collapses into "you have rediscovered a distinction people already made under a different name," and how I'd find that out. Also: what a finished paper would have to contain to be worth sending anywhere, as opposed to being a grumpy old man's notebook.

Skip the literature review and the references, I'll chase those down myself — I still have library access and I'd rather not have invented citations I can't check.
