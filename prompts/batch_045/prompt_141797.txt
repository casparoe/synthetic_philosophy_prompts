Last night I had a long, circular argument with a friend about whether Solomonoff induction actually justifies Occam's razor, and I'd like a diagnosis of why we stalled rather than a verdict or a survey of formal learning theory.

The argument went roughly like this. The convergence result says that predicting with the universal prior keeps you within a fixed overhead of the best computable predictor, an overhead that scales with the complexity of the true environment — so simplicity pays. My friend's objection was that the overhead is doing all the work: the bridging constant between two reference machines can be astronomical, there is no useful general bound on it, and for any finite body of evidence you can always help yourself to a machine with your favourite anomaly hard-wired in as a lookup table, so the razor starts to look like a preference for one encoding over another. I replied that the dominance result makes every computable prior equally good up to that one-off cost, and that the cost washes out as the data stream lengthens. He said that is fine as a claim about predicting the next symbol and useless as a claim about which theory to believe, and that no working scientist is minimising cumulative log-loss over an infinite sequence. At that point, I think, we were answering different questions.

My hypothesis is that this is exactly it: a convergence question where the choice of machine is harmless, and a finite-sample theory-choice question where it is a substantive prior commitment. But I can't tell whether that is the whole story or whether a hidden formal issue is also in play — whether the unboundedness of the bridge constant can be tamed by restricting to some natural class of machines, or whether a variant of the prior that also charges for computation time reorders things in a way that matters.

So: what kind of stalemate is this — verbal, a clash of bedrock intuitions, two questions under one name, or an unfinished technical question in disguise? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a sharpened distinction, a theorem, or an argument that no answer is available? Please flag how confident you are in each central claim and single out the one you are least sure of.
