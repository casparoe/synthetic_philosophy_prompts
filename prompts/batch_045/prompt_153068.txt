I'm a 4th-year PhD student (metaphysics side of the dept) and I've ran our grad reading group on modal epistemology three times now, and it's the same argument with the same three people every time, so I want an actual diagnostic instrument instead of going thru it again by hand.

What I want: a decision tree / sorting quiz, about eight branch questions, that walks someone from "zombies seem conceivable to me" to a named position in the modal epistemology literature. It's going to be a printed handout, so every node has to be a concrete case, not "do you think conceivability is a guide to possibility".

Non-negotiables:

1. No node may name a philosopher or a view. Each node is a question about a case that someone who's never opened the literature can answer. Give me the path key separately.
2. Build it from the logical space, not from a list of names: give me the questions and the answer-combinations they allow first, then locate the actual positions in that space, and tell me which combinations are unoccupied and why — incoherent, merely unattractive, or just nobody's bothered.
3. Leaves I want covered: straight modal rationalism (a clean conceiving settles possibility, no gap); the Chalmers-style ideal-conceivability / two-dimensionalist view; the "conceivability is a real but defeasible guide" family; the Kripke-style view where the necessary a posteriori cases open a gap conceivability can't close; and the van Inwagen-ish skeptical one where conceivability buys you basically nothing past the trivial cases. If I'm missing a leaf, add it and say why. Flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely can't pull apart rather than forcing a question just to separate them.

Three wrinkles I want built into the structure, because this is where my group actually stalls:

- A node on *how much* a conceivability verdict is meant to move you. I've been using three settings and want the tree to route thru them: "veridical" — a clean conceiving gives you knowledge of the possibility, full stop; "presumptive" — it gives you a defeasible reason that a good modal theory or a hidden identity can override; "inert" — it's only ever shorthand for what you already knew some other way. If the dial needs a fourth setting, argue for it.
- A node about the conceiver's information. Is the person doing the conceiving allowed all the empirical facts about actuality, or only ideal a priori reflection? The standard hidden-identity cases are only conceivable for a conceiver who doesn't know the identity yet, and I want that made load-bearing instead of footnoted.
- A node on *failures* to conceive. Everyone in my group treats "I can't imagine it" as evidence of impossibility and I think that's a separate commitment from anything about conceivability succeeding. My case: a creature with a sensory modality we don't have — I can't form any image of what its experiences are like, but that seems to be a fact about me rather than about what's possible. Call it the eighth-sense case. The tree should split people who think unconceivability is evidence from people who think it's only ever noise generated by the conceiver's own limits.

Cases that have to appear somewhere: the zombie case, one hidden-identity case, "could this very table have been made of ice", the eighth-sense case above, and a math/logic one (could 2+2 have equalled 5). I want the last one because I suspect it does a lot of work separating people who otherwise agree, but put it wherever you think it belongs.

Also: state at the top which sense of "possible" the whole instrument is tracking, and point out anywhere a leaf quietly switches senses — I'd rather be told than be reassured.
