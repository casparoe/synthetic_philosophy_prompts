Right, so I need a bit of satire for a reading-group thing, and I've got a fairly specific idea in mind.

Write me a mock official guidance leaflet — call it 'Guidance for Claimants' — issued by a fictional Whitehall body (something like the Modal Claims Office, within a Department for Modal Affairs), explaining how to claim compensation under a new statutory scheme. Around 650 to 750 words. Voice: deadpan civil-service prose, the sort that explains something completely insane with total calm. Give it numbered sections, one worked example, and a short FAQ at the end.

The machinery is where the joke has to live, so here's the setup I want it to get right. The scheme pays out if what the claimant describes is possible. But 'possible' is assessed under two tests, and the leaflet has to explain both in bureaucratic language without ever using the words 'primary intension' or 'secondary intension':

- Test 1, 'considered as actual': the claimant describes a scenario as if it were the actual world, and the claim is assessed by whatever in that scenario fills the relevant role. So a claimant who says the stuff coming out of our taps is XYZ passes Test 1 — in their scenario, the term for the watery stuff picks out XYZ.
- Test 2, 'considered as counterfactual': the scenario is assessed as a way this world might have been, with the actual referents held fixed. The same claim now fails, because whatever the taps run, the stuff just is H2O.

So far so fair. The thing I actually want ironised is a further provision — call it the Neutrality Clause, or the Coincidence Provision — which says that for the purposes of the scheme the two tests are assumed to coincide, so that passing Test 1 counts as passing Test 2. Because that quietly makes the scheme liable for every claim anyone can coherently describe, the rest of the leaflet should be officials building escape hatches into the small print while never once admitting that the Clause is the problem: a means test, a cap on awards, a requirement that claims be 'endorsed with actually', a form for claimants who'd rather say their concept has a deep contingency so the two readings come apart, and so on. The leak should be completely visible and nobody in the document is allowed to notice it.

Two things I care about, tho. First, the reductio has to be honest: the target is the move of letting the first reading do the metaphysical work, not two-dimensional semantics as a whole — don't write it as if anyone seriously thinks conceivability alone settles modality. The joke is that the department has written the neutrality assumption into law and now has to live with it. Second, the escape hatches should be recognisable versions of the real moves: the 'actually' endorsement should function like the standard necessary-a-posteriori case, and the opt-out should be the move where a claimant says their concept works like one of those, so their scenario is conceivable but not possible and the claim dies. Someone who works on this should read it and think the target is fair rather than invented. Bung in one buried FAQ entry about claimants from a world physically identical to ours where nobody is conscious — those get paid too, which is where the scheme's money actually went.

British English throughout, please, and no winking: no character in the document may find any of this funny. Also dont name any real philosophers in it, invented officials only — I'd rather the piece not read as a swipe at a person. Definately keep the comedy in the content rather than in anyone reacting.
