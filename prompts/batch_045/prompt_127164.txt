I sit on the investment committee of a small spend-down foundation. Our docket runs to a few million dollars a year, currently split between global-health grants, farmed-animal welfare work, and one policy-advocacy program, and we are large relative to several of the organizations we fund. We have one analyst with about two years whose entire output has to be methodological: no new surveys, no fieldwork, no data collection — only conceptual and measurement work that would change which of those three areas gets the next marginal million. Two features of our situation matter: the three areas have no common unit of outcome, and our board has no settled view, and no procedure for reaching one, on how a year of nonhuman suffering weighs against a year of human suffering. Most of what we fund has effects that play out over decades, and we cannot commission the empirical work that would resolve the resulting uncertainty.

Assume I already know the standard importance / tractability / neglectedness framework; do not explain it back to me. What I want is a ranked agenda: the five open questions in cause prioritization that most deserve those two years, ordered by how much a good answer would change our actual allocation rather than by how interesting they are. I care specifically about questions whose answers survive contact with the situation described above — incommensurable outcome units, unresolved disagreement about the relative weight of different beings' welfare, long-run and largely unmeasured effects, and no new data. Questions that are conceptually deep but would leave our docket exactly where it is should be ranked low, and I want that said explicitly rather than left for me to infer.

Two further constraints. First, rank the candidate questions using the framework's own three criteria, and in each case name the criterion doing the work; flag any candidate that scores high on neglectedness mainly because nobody could answer it in two years, since I would rather fund tractable work on a second-order question than a heroic attempt at an unanswerable one, and since questions about whether the framework's multiplicative form, its unit of value, or its treatment of the marginal versus the average dollar are well posed all seem to me to be in the running. Second, be explicit about what each ranking depends on — I am more interested in the considerations that would move a question up or down than in the ordering itself.

Answer as exactly five numbered items and nothing else: no preamble, no summary paragraph. One or two sentences per item. The first sentence names the question and why it sits where it does, including which criterion is doing the work. The second names the single consideration, result, or change in our circumstances that would most move it up or down the list; treat that second sentence as your answer to what would change your mind. Write in the register of someone who expects to be wrong about at least two of these five.
