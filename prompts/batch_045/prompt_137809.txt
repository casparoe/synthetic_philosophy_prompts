Hey, I'm in a philosophy of mind seminar this term and we did the color phi thing last week and I think I get the setup but the discussion left me confused in a way I cant quite put my finger on. Setup as I understood it: you flash a green dot at one spot, then a red dot a few degrees away maybe 100-ish ms later, and subjects report a single dot travelling between the two spots that changes color partway along the path — and apparently they report the change happening before the red dot at the second spot was even presented. Our TA said Dennett's move is that there are two stories you can tell about this. Stalinesque: the brain waits for the red dot and then produces one experience with the motion and the mid-trajectory color change already baked in, so no earlier draft was ever conscious. Orwellian: you really do first consciously see the green dot sitting at the first spot, and then afterwards that bit of your history gets retroactively rewritten, so you seem to remember the change occurring mid-flight. And then the kicker is supposed to be that nothing empirical could decide between them, because any probe you use to time the experience (press a button the instant you see the switch, whatever) has its own temporal slop and is itself just another event inside the stream you're trying to measure — so there's no fact of the matter about when the experience occurred.

In a database those are straightforwardly different and you can audit them apart.
