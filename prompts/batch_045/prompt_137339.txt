My reconstruction, with an outcome called big if it contains infinitely many lives at positive welfare and infinitely many units of realized value:

1. Serious cosmology gives us a non-trivial probability that the outcomes we choose between are big — infinitely many lives across infinite space, or an unbounded future.
2. Pairing Principle: if the persons in outcome A can be put into one-one correspondence with the persons in outcome B such that everyone in A is at least as well off as their partner in B and someone is better off, then A is better than B.
3. Between any two countably infinite collections there are infinitely many bijections, and for big outcomes in which everyone enjoys the same positive welfare there exist bijections witnessing the Pairing Principle in both directions — Hilbert's-Hotel style shuffling.
4. So the Pairing Principle either contradicts itself or must be read as requiring unanimity across all bijections, in which case it yields: no big outcome is better than any other. On either reading it delivers nothing: big outcomes are equally good, or incomparable.
5. If big outcomes are equally good or incomparable, then no finite difference we can make now — averting a war, funding a vaccination programme, not funding one — improves the world, since the world is big either way and finite differences do not register against an infinite total.
6. The mathematician's window-averaging criterion repairs this: a future with happy lives at twice the density per cubic megaparsec comes out twice as good as one at half the density, and a world in which every second person is miserable comes out worse, which is what we wanted.
7. But window-averaging makes an outcome's value depend on where the spheres are centred, on their shape, and on how fast they grow: recentre them on a different galaxy, switch to cubes, alter the growth schedule, and for some pairs of outcomes the verdict flips — while no individual's life has been altered by so much as a hair.
8. Rearrangements, relabelings and re-coordinatizations that change no one's experience are morally irrelevant, and there is no fact of the matter about where the centre of an infinite world is; how good the world is cannot depend on where I mentally stand when I add things up.
9. So by 7 and 8, window-averaging is morally arbitrary; and by 2–5 and 8, the only structure-independent criterion available is paralysis.
10. Therefore there is no coherent way of aggregating value in infinite contexts, and since we cannot rule out a big future, any long-termist cost-effectiveness reasoning that leans on the size of the future rests on a criterion that either collapses into indifference or smuggles in arbitrary structure.
