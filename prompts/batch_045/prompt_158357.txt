Hey — I'm working on a short blog post pushing back on the idea that inference to the best explanation is just Bayesian conditionalization wearing a trenchcoat, and I need a case to hang the post on.

Build me a thought experiment where IBE and straight Bayesian conditionalization (condition on the total evidence, take the hypothesis with the highest posterior) pick different hypotheses off the same shortlist — and where IBE's pick is the one a sensible person in that situation would actually act on.

Constraints on the scenario:

- Realistic and short: one paragraph, somewhere ordinary — a hospital ward, a small lab, a school, an office. No demons, no perfect predictors, no skeptical scenarios, no conspiracies.
- Every hypothesis on the shortlist has to be a live one: something a competent person in that setting would float, with a prior I wouldn't laugh at (a couple percent at minimum). Priors and likelihoods should be readable off the story, not numbers you assign by fiat.
- All the evidence is stated in the scenario — nothing smuggled in later.
- The divergence can't come from the cheap routes: no hypothesis with a negligible prior winning on charm, no conjunctive monsters ("H and also the moon is made of cheese"), and nothing that just restates the data ("the readings were exactly 4, 9, 9, 12"). Whatever wins on fit has to be a real, independently describable mechanism.

And here's the wrinkle I actually care about, which is my own addition rather than anything standard. The way I want to cash out "best explanation" is what I'll call robustness: H1 explains the evidence better than H2 if H1 would still have made the evidence unsurprising even if the numbers had come out a bit differently — if it explains a whole neighborhood of nearby possible results, not just the actual ones. So the case I want is one where:

(i) the hypothesis with the highest likelihood on the actual data is a sharp, tight-fitting one that nails the exact figures;
(ii) the hypothesis a robustness-first IBE picks is a broader, looser one that fits the actual figures slightly worse but would have fit a wide range of nearby results about as well;
(iii) and the broader one is the one we'd intuitively go with.

Basically an overfitting story, except the overfit hypothesis is a genuine causal story, not a curve someone drew through the points.

What I want back:

1. The scenario, one paragraph.
2. The shortlist and the walkthrough: priors and likelihoods as the story supports them, what conditionalization says, what IBE-with-robustness says, and why each gives the verdict it gives. Don't just stipulate that IBE picks the loose hypothesis — derive it from the criterion.
3. Which feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens to the case if you take that feature out.
4. The honest part: is my robustness criterion secretly just a prior in disguise? If a Bayesian can fold it into the prior over hypotheses as a simplicity or smoothness preference and land on the same answer, then the divergence is fake and my post is dead. Tell me straight whether you think that's what's going on, and whether any version of the case survives it. If there's a different wrinkle that does the job better, suggest it.
5. Epistemic hygiene: mark what you're reasonably confident about and what you're guessing at. If you think the task as specified can't be done properly — if, say, any case meeting my constraints collapses back into Bayes — say that plainly instead of handing me a case that sort of works. I'd much rather hear "your premise is confused here" than get a polite story I can't use.

Please write in American English, and keep the whole thing under ~700 words if you can.
