My opening:

Say I'm forecasting whether my team's project ships by the deadline. I do the outside view properly: assemble ~150 past projects that look relevant, get a base rate of about 35% on time. Fine, I'll grant that's where I start.

But now there's a cheap diagnostic I can run -- a one-week spike -- and I have historical stats on it: it comes back "clean" in roughly 60% of projects that shipped on time and roughly 15% of projects that blew the deadline. Those numbers came from the same kind of historical data as the base rate, not from my gut about how good my team is. I run it, it comes back clean, and Bayes takes me from 35% to about 68%.

My claim is that this isn't the inside view sneaking in the back door. Every input is a base rate over some class; the spike result just puts me in a narrower class, and the likelihood ratio is itself an outside-view object with its own reference class behind it. So the planning-fallasy objection doesn't touch it. That objection is that inside-view reasoning is optimistic because it counts the steps I can already picture going right and ignores the long tail of things I haven't thought of -- but the spike is exactly the sort of thing that has already met the long tail, historically, and the 15% is a measurement of it.

Which leaves you with the reference-class complaint: I chose the class, and if I widen it the base rate goes to ~45% while if I narrow it harder it goes to ~28%, so my 35% was a choice, not a fact. But you can't use that to force me back to the broad class, because "broad" isn't neutral either, and the spike is precisely the kind of thing that makes a narrower class relevant rather then gerrymandered.

So: is my 68% legitimate outside-view reasoning, or did I smuggle the inside view back in? And if it's legitimate, what's left of the outside view as a distinctive method -- isn't it just "update base rates with likelihood ratios", which no one ever disputed? In which case why did anyone ever pitch it as a rival to the inside view rather than as a special case of doing statistics correctly?
