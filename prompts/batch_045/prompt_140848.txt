After several hours of argument with a friend last night, I want my own position put under maximum pressure. I hold that a Nozick-style truth-tracking account of knowledge can absorb the closure objection rather than be refuted by it. Tracking conditions are admittedly not closed under known entailment, but I claim the only closure worth wanting is stepwise closure: if a subject knows p by a method that tracks the truth of p, knows that p entails q, and competently draws that inference in a single step, then she knows q. On this reading the famous cases are not closure failures at all but transmission failures — the conclusion's own tracking profile is independently wrecked (in a case where I know the dial reads 20° yet fail to know that the room is below 21°, the nearest world in which the room is warmer is one where a stuck dial would still read 20°), so nothing about deduction as such is impugned. My friend's reply was that this is a redescription, not a solution: if the view cannot let deduction extend what we know, it is epistemically sterile, and "competently drawn single inference" has been gerrymandered to fit the cases.

What would count against me, ranked from fatal to merely awkward rather than catalogued? I want the defeaters a tracking theorist should herself concede, not only those a closure theorist would press: cases where stepwise closure fails for a one-step inference whose conclusion has an intact tracking profile, if any can be constructed; the possibility that transmission failure and closure failure cannot be distinguished without the distinction collapsing into one or the other; the argument that stepwise closure still cannot underwrite knowledge by long or multi-premise deduction, since sensitivity degrades with inferential distance — the one that worries me most; and evidence about how ordinary speakers attribute knowledge in closure pairs, if it cuts either way. Say which of these a defender would dismiss as assuming what is in dispute. And tell me honestly whether my position risks anything at all: does it predict something no rival predicts — for instance that a subject can know p, know that p entails q, and know that she does not know q — or have I qualified it into unfalsifiability, and if so is that itself fatal for a view of this kind?
