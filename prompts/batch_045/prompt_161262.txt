Our open-source foundation has about 400 voting members, and we're about to move to one single rule for everything: electing the chair from 3–6 candidates, picking one of 4–8 mutually exclusive roadmap proposals, and producing a ranked shortlist of three for the grants committee. Right now every committee invents its own procedure and it's a mess. I want the spec written down before I look at any actual rule: what does a rule have to get right to count as adequate *for us*, with the case behind each requirement, and which of the things people demand are genuine requirements and which are just what one camp happens to like.

Roughly what's on my list so far:

- if everyone ranks X above Y and nobody changes their mind about anything else, X and Y shouldn't swap places (the spoiler thing — I keep seeing a faction win by getting an extra similar-looking option onto the ballot, which maybe belongs here too)
- if every single member prefers X to Y, Y doesn't win
- a candidate who would lose head-to-head against every other candidate never wins (does this one have a name?)
- nobody should get a better result by staying home, or by ranking there favourite lower
- it has to actually produce an answer, or at least a tie-break a member can carry out by hand
- three things I made up and have never seen written down anywhere: (a) if ~2% of ballots get garbled — misclicks, duplicates, someone filling in only two of the options — the outcome should usually be unchanged, and piling more irrelevant options onto the ballot shouldn't make it more fragile; (b) shortlisting three and then running the same rule on the shortlist should give the same winner as running it once on the full field; (c) it shouldn't matter whether we ask members to rank every option or only the ones they have an opinion about; (d) we need to publish a few lines explaining why X beat Y that a losing member can check without seeing the whole ballot set.

I suspect (b) and (c) are secretly the same requirement as the spoiler one, or the spoiler one in a costume — if so, say so plainly rather than scoring rules against my list politely.

The part I most want is the joint-unsatisfiability part: I know Arrow's theorem says some cluster of these can't all hold at once, and I think there's something similar about strategic voting (Gibbard–whatsit?). State precisely which subsets are impossible together and where exactly I have to choose — and don't just gesture at the theorem, tell me which of *my* items are in the impossible cluster and which are safe.

Two smaller asks. Mark each requirement non-negotiable-for-us vs. nice-to-have, and tell me which single requirement does the most work in ruling rules out — the one where dropping it suddenly makes everything available. A short scorecard at the end is fine: plurality, instant runoff, Borda, approval/score, and the pairwise-Condorcet family, a line or two each, no more.

And be honest about confidence: flag which claims are theorems you'd stake money on, which are standard-but-informal, and which are your own judgement call. If the whole framing is misguided — if one rule for chair elections and shortlists is a bad idea, or a couple of my requirements are just confused — tell me that instead of filling in the table.
