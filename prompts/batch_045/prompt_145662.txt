I am preparing a graduate seminar session on whether large language models are agents, simulators, or something else again, and I have written out an argument that I have come to distrust precisely because it seems to establish considerably more than I am willing to accept. I would be grateful for a diagnosis of the argument rather than a verdict on the underlying question.

The argument turns on a device I shall call the persistence probe. Take a deployed language model whose weights are frozen at release, give it a long-horizon task and a writable memory store that survives across sessions, and stipulate the following outcome: the system forms a plan, records it, and over many weeks — across thousands of sessions, with the staff supervising it rotating, and under repeated attempts to redirect it both by prompt injection and by rewriting its standing instructions — it declines to abandon the plan, gives reasons for declining that appear in no single prompt it was ever given, and revises its subsidiary steps while keeping the stated end fixed. No parameter changes after deployment.

(1) For any system whose outputs at a time are computed by a fixed function of parameters that do not change plus a context presented to it, whatever looks like a persistent end is a pattern in the context rather than a state of the system.

(2) In any such system, scaffolding of every kind — accumulated transcripts, long-horizon memory, tool outputs, retrieved documents, standing instructions, scheduled re-prompting — affects behaviour only by being written into the context.

(3) So in the persistence probe, the end the system appears to pursue across weeks is a pattern carried by the context, not a state of the system.

(4) A pattern carried by a context is no one's end in the sense that matters for agency: it is like the aim of a character in a serial written instalment by instalment, which survives across chapters and resists revision only because the writer keeps it consistent. The character is not an agent; the writer is.

(5) Hence the probe gives no reason at all to ascribe agency to the model, nor to any model-based system. It gives reason to ascribe agency only to whatever sustains the pattern — the surrounding apparatus of people, schedules, and training objectives.

(6) Nor could the probe have come out otherwise in a way that mattered: had the system abandoned its plan at the first injection, that too would have been a pattern in a context. So no behaviour of any such system, now or after any amount of further engineering, could count as evidence of its agency.

(7) A claim about what is and is not an agent that no possible behaviour could count against is not a substantive metaphysical claim; it is a redescription that one may accept or decline at no cost.

(8) So the simulator framing is either empty or it generalises: a human being's outputs are equally a function of parameters fixed before the episode in question and of what is currently presented to her, and her stated ends are equally held in place by diaries, contracts, colleagues, and institutions; by the same reasoning she is a character sustained by an apparatus rather than an agent.

(9) No one should accept that. So something among (1) to (6) has to go.

What I want from you: identify the step or steps you would reject; explain why that step looked acceptable when I wrote it down, and why the inference from it seemed to go through; then price the alternatives — what each of the other candidate premises costs to give up, and whether any of them can be retained in a weakened form. Set this against the four positions I want the session to distinguish: that the model itself is an agent; that the model is a simulator and agency belongs, if anywhere, to the simulated role; that agency belongs to the scaffolded composite of model, memory, and scheduler but not to either part alone; and that agency talk about these systems is simply mistaken. Say what each of the four has to pay given your diagnosis. Finally, tell me whether, once the argument is repaired, the persistence probe retains any evidential role, or whether it was doing no work from the start.

Please write in British English, and do not supply a bibliography or any citations: I will add references myself. Around nine hundred words.
