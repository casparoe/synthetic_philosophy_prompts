Hi — I'm a journalist doing a piece on lifeboat ethics, by which I mean the old Hardin metaphor and the way it keeps coming back in arguments about migration, disaster relief, ICU rationing and research budgets. For the online version I want a "which lifeboat ethic are you actually carrying around?" widget, and I don't want it to just reprint the same four positions every article lists.

What I'm after: seven to nine questions, each a concrete case with two or three plain-language answers, plus a scoring key that sorts readers into named positions. Build it from the logical space first — figure out which questions actually split the views — and only then pin the names on the leaves. My readers are sharp but not philosophy-trained, so no question may just ask someone to pick a school.

The cases I want used, because they're the ones my reporting keeps hitting: a boat at capacity with swimmers who ended up in the water because the crew earlier overloaded a different boat; a swimmer who, once aboard, is the only one who can steer to a second, emptier vessel; a swimmer who refuses the life jacket you are allowed to throw; overcrowding that isn't a one-off emergency but the permenant condition on board; and swimmers you can't turn away without breaking the rule the boat itself is run on.

Leaves I need covered: Hardin-style no-boarding (helping makes things worse downstream), straight triage (most lives saved), a fair lottery among candidates, repair-first (the crew caused this and owes), and the view that the whole setup is corrupt so there is no allocation question left.

Where a combination of answers has nobody living in it, name it and say whether that's because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unwritten. If two of the leaves can't really be separated by case answers, flag that instead of inventing a question to fake a difference. And please commit: tell me which branch cuts you think are genuinely doing the work, and which one you'd throw out if an editor made you lose a question.
