I am a PhD student in decision science, and nearly all of my working life is spent with multi-criteria problems: alternatives described by several attribute scores, pairwise comparison relations that are deliberately allowed to be incomplete and intransitive, concordance–discordance outranking with veto and discrimination thresholds, and the fact that a definite recommendation has to be extracted from a finite menu without any cardinal utility representation being available. I can read the value-theory literature but I do not think in its vocabulary, and I want a translation rather than a survey.

So: restate the debate about whether the all-things-considered better-than relation is transitive in the vocabulary I have just sketched. Take, one at a time, the continuum or spectrum arguments that generate a chain of tiny improvements whose endpoints are intuitively ordered the other way round; the essentially comparative view, on which a betterness claim is true or false only relative to the set of alternatives under consideration; and the pragmatic argument that someone whose betterness relation contains a cycle is thereby exploitable and so rationally criticisable. For each, give the closest object in my framework, and then give me the residue report: what maps cleanly onto machinery I already have, what survives only after distortion, and what cannot be expressed in my terms at all.

The part I actually care about is a hypothesis I cannot shake, and I want it tested rather than flattered: that the spectrum arguments get their force entirely from a kind of menu-relativity, so that once betterness is formalised as ternary, with the comparison set as an explicit argument, the supposed cycles are no longer cycles in a single binary relation, and the exploitability argument — which needs one stable pairwise relation that a bookmaker can walk around — loses its grip. Run that hypothesis against the arguments as they are in fact made, tell me where it overreaches, and tell me whether whatever survives is a defect of my framework or evidence that the philosophical claim about intransitivity was always doing less work than it looks.
