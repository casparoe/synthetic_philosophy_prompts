our lab is building what the funder insists on calling a "consciousness meter" — a bedside box that eats neural data and gives back a number. It's supposed to do three jobs and I've started to suspect they aren't the same job: (1) track anaesthesia depth minute by minute in the OR, (2) for one unresponsive ICU patient at a time, say whether there is anyone home at all, knowing that feeds into a decision about continuing organ support, and (3) our funder also wants it to say something about a large AI system running on a GPU cluster, which I'd of thought was where this whole thing goes off the rails.

Before we pick a theory to build on I want the spec, not the theory. Write me the desiderata: what a theory of consciousness has to deliver for it to be fit to underwrite that box, with the case, state, dissociation or empirical result behind each one, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of a particular camp. Commit to positions and overrule me where I'm wrong — I don't want a menu of options I have to adjudicate myself.

Argue with me about these in particular:

- Is "the quantity has to be computable from data you can actually collect in an ICU, in under an hour, on a parcellation two engineers would disagree about" a genuine desideratum, or philistine engineering pressure that shouldn't be allowed near a metaphysical question? Integrated-information-style measures blow up combinatorially, and I've been told the newer versions made that worse.
- "Must not rank a big regular lattice of simple units above a human cortex." I keep hearing this objection and I genuinely can't tell whether it's a real problem for the theory or just people disliking where the theory is going.
- Can anything count as calibrating the meter, given that in exactly the cases we care about — non-reporters, infants, maybe the AI system — there is no ground truth? Is "comes with a validation strategy for the no-ground-truth cases" one desideratum or a confusion dressed up as one?
- The distinctions I don't want collapsed: creature vs state, level vs content, wakefulness vs awareness, having any vs how much, and the boundary question (why isn't the whole ward one subject).
- Results it can't contradict: the cerebellum has several times more neurons than the cortex and people lose large parts of it without obvious loss of consciousness; anaesthesia and slow-wave sleep knock out awareness while plenty of cortex stays active; dreaming in REM; split-brain.

Then, the two things I most want: which single desideratum excludes the largest number of the popular theories, and whether some subset of your own list can't be jointly satisfied — say, "derived from the intrinsic character of experience" plus "tractable at the bedside" plus "picks out a unique boundary." If they can't all hold, say definitely which one you'd drop and why, not "it depends." Skip the full scorecard across IIT, global workspace and higher-order theories; I can do that, and just note in a clause where a desideratum kills a whole family.
