I've been asked to circulate a one-page statement of my position before the first meeting of a graduate reading group on formal epistemology, and I'd rather find out now whether the pieces hold together than find out in front of eleven people. The group contains at least one convinced halfer and one convinced double-halfer, so I know I'll be pressed on details. Here, as flatly as I can state it, is where I've landed on self-locating credence:

(1) In the ordinary Sleeping Beauty setup — fair coin on Sunday night, one awakening if Heads, two if Tails, memory erased in between — I think Beauty's credence in Heads on waking is one third.
(2) I don't think that verdict is special to that setup. The mechanism I want is: when several candidate experiences are subjectively indistinguishable from the one you are having now, spread your credence evenly over them, so that a hypothesis which gives rise to more such experiences is favoured in proportion. This is also why I reject the Doomsday Argument: I don't think my temporal position among all the people who will ever exist is evidence about how many of them there will be, and I take that rejection and my one third to stand or fall together.
(3) But I want no part of the presumptuous-philosopher style of conclusion: I don't think that one cosmological theory's positing a trillion times more observers than a rival gives me trillion-to-one confidence in it, or that my merely finding myself existing is strong evidence for a very large universe. I've been told that (2) is exactly the move that generates that conclusion. My reply so far has been that the cosmological case differs because there is no actual randomisation device there — but I'm not confident that reply is doing any work.
(4) I also hold a methodological constraint: the answer to a self-locating problem should not depend on how we carve conscious experience into units. Whether we count awakenings, or minutes of consciousness, or instantaneous experience-slices, should not change the rational credence, because that choice looks like a stipulation rather than something the world settles.
(5) Matching case verdict: suppose the coin is fair and Beauty is awakened exactly once either way, the Tails awakening lasting twenty-four hours and the Heads awakening lasting one minute, and suppose she is asked for her credence at the very start of the session, before she can tell how long it will run. I want to say one half. I can't see how the duration of the session she is about to have could be evidence about the coin — and yet (2), applied to moments rather than to awakenings, seems to push me towards something in the neighbourhood of one over fourteen hundred and forty-one.
(6) I think Beauty should assign a half on Sunday, and I don't think there is anything irrational in her knowing in advance that she will later move to a third and planning on that basis. Several people in the group say this is incoherent, on the ground that she is certain from the outset that she will be awakened.
(7) I also think that if, once awake, she is told that this is her first awakening, she should go back to a half — and one member insists that this is inconsistent with (1), which I don't follow, since I'd have thought it dropped out of (1) rather than conflicting with it.
(8) Finally, I'm unmoved by the objection that (1) can't be reconciled with her knowing all along that the coin's chance of Heads is a half. I take that principle to constrain credences conditional on known chances, and not to settle what to think once you have also located yourself within the world.

What I want is an audit, not a survey of the literature. Go through the list and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and how badly; which apparent tensions dissolve once the right distinction is drawn; and, for each real tension, what the cheapest repair would be and what it would cost me — including whether abandoning (2) forces me to abandon (1) as well, or whether there is a narrower formulation of (2) that still delivers one third. Rank the tensions by severity rather than flagging everything at the same level, and say whether there is a single revision that would clean up more than one of them. I have no settled view about which item I would sacrifice, and if the real problem is that one of these is badly stated rather than false, I'd rather be told that than told to drop it.
