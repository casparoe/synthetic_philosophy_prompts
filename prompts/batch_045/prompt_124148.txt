Now take the circumstances away. The interpreter is behind a one-way mirror — no shared objects in view, no visible goings-on, no way of telling what the speaker is reacting to. All they get is a bare time-series of which sentences the speaker assents to. Wave your hands and grant them the logical connectives and quantifier structure of the language, worked out from the pattern of assents alone.

In that stripped-down version, rank these five resources by how much they still do to narrow down which truth theory the interpreter should assign — i.e. how much indeterminacy each one genuinely removes once c is off the table. Most to least, with a line on why each lands where it does:

- charity: construe the speaker as mostly agreeing with you, hence mostly right
- holism: beliefs and desires only get fixed together, so you can trade one against the other
- triangulation with a shared object in a shared world
- the passing-theory move, where you're allowed to read a speaker's words as meaning something idiosyncratic when they get them wrong
- the formal shape of the truth theory itself — T-sentences and whatever constraints on the truth predicate ride along with them
