Hey — I'm writing a dissertation chapter on consistency in sentencing and there's a hypo I keep going round in circles on, so I thought I'd get an outside read. I've been going through the sentencing remarks of one Crown Court judge (I'll call her Judge M.) and this is roughly her track record. Burglary of a occupied house at night, long record: four years, and she refused to shave anything off even though he'd lose his job and his kids would go into care — "the sentence answers the offence, not the fallout". Benefit fraud, about £180k over six years, first offender, no realistic risk of reoffending: 18 months suspended, on the basis that locking her up "would add nothing but harm". A scaffolding firm director whose safety failures killed a worker: two years plus a fine and disqualification, with the remark that prison was needed "to mark the gravity, not because the length deters anybody". A defendant who, after the offence but before sentence, pulled a kid out of a burning car: full credit for the plea and the remorse, but no reduction for the rescue — "good deeds afterwards don't pay off the wrong". Attempted robbery where the wallet turned out to be empty: roughly 60% of the tariff for the completed offence, because "what matters is what he tried and risked". During a local spike in one offence type she sentenced above her own earlier tariff for it, within the maximum, saying "the message has to land right now". And she cut a 19-year-old first-timer's term by about a third, on the grounds that two years isn't the same two years for him as for a man of forty with a record.

Here's the case I'm stuck on. A pile-on: over one weekend roughly 3,500 accounts each sent a single abusive message to a 15-year-old, and she tried to kill herself. Any one message on its own is the sort of thing that normally draws a trivial disposal; the catastrophic harm is entirely a product of the aggregate. The CPS can realistically charge only about a hundred of them, picked more or less at random, and everyone in the pool knew there was something like a 3% chance of ever being charged. Two of the hundred are before Judge M. D1 is 34, has two previous convictions for similar online harassment, was one of the first five senders, knew a handful of others were piling in and could see where it was heading. D2 is 17, no record, was about the 2,900th sender, joined when it was already viral, and his message made no difference to anything — he knew harm was happening but reasonably assumed his own contribution was nil. Guilt isn't in dispute for either; the only question is the sentence. My supervisor keeps banging on about what he calls the discounting problem — if only 3% are ever charged, doesn't deterrence mean the penalty needs to be roughly thirty times what desert would otherwise hand out, capped only by whatever ceiling desert sets? — and about whether desert here tracks what each person actually brought about, or the collective harm they knew they were joining.

So: what do you think Judge M. would give D1 and D2? More usefully for me, can you lay out the two or three rules that fit her track record about equally well, say what each of them predicts for these two, which one you'd actually bet on and why, and name the one extra case that would best tell those rules apart — e.g. whether it's a marginal late joiner, a lone sender whose message did the damage by itself, or something else. Please flag where you're really just guessing, and tell me which single argument or hypothetical would most change your prediction.
