The warm-up has one specific job to do: show that SIA isn't, despite the way it usually gets introduced, the "big worlds are more likely" assumption as such — that the whole SSA/SIA disagreement lives in a single factor (whether the total number of observers in your reference class is itself evidence), and that once you hold that number fixed across the hypotheses the two assumptions collapse into each other and into ordinary "I was sampled from this population" reasoning.

So: build me a case to that spec. Constraints:

- Since neither assumption comes out badly here, tell me what each side would claim the case shows about the other one, and who's actually right about that.

What's the strongest version of that objection, and is it fair to this particular case or only to the literature around it?
