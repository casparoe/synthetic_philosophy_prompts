I'm a first-year PhD student and my supervisor runs a fortnightly reading group on realization and multiple realizability. Over the last two terms she's handed down verdicts on a pile of cases — either "genuine realizer of pain" or "that's a model of a mind, not a mind." I'm writing up the notes for the group and I want to know what she's going to say about the case she's promised for next week.

Her track record:

- silicon-based alien, totally different chemistry, internal states play the same causal role: realizer
- a creature whose damage-detector state causes it to burst into song rather than to avoid the damage: not pain (she added it might still be some other state of theirs)
- someone whose neurons are swapped out one at a time for functionally identical silicon units: a realizer at every stage, and at the end, when there's no biology left at all
- an alien whose pain state has no behavioral effects whatsoever — no wincing, no avoidance, no reports, it only causes further internal states: still a realizer
- a creature that only plays the pain role because a team of technicians is continously correcting its internal states from outside: not a realizer, "the system has to do the work itself"
- the entire population of a large country wired together by radio, one person per neuron, running the same functional organization: not a realizer — though she admitted she doesn't have a principled story about why
- a warehouse of filing cabinets plus a clerk that reproduces the exact input-output profile of a pain-feeler: not a realizer
- a functionally identical twin whose color experiences are supposed to be inverted: same mental states, no mental difference without a functional difference
- a system functionally identical to a pain-feeler with nothing that it's like to be it: she says it does feel pain, the case is empty

Next week's case: engineers build from scratch a causally integrated system that runs in real time, is embodied in a robot, and ends up with the pain role occupied by internal states nobody designed for that role — they fell out of training. No biology anywhere, no scan of any particular brain, no gradual replacement, nothing descended from an actual pain-feeler.

What will she say, and what's the rule she's running? I dont need the true answer, I need to walk in knowing the two or three rules that fit her verdicts, what each predicts about this case, and which one you'd bet on and why. And since several of those rules will agree here: what further case should I put to her in the session to separate the ones that agree?
