The setup, briefly: Mira is a narrative designer hired to remaster a cult mid-2000s open-world game. The game has a ferryman NPC, Odile, who meets you at a dock and is frightened of the water. In the original, every line Odile can ever say was hand-written by one writer into a branching dialogue tree. In the remaster, Odile is driven by a learned model — her goals and utterances are generated, nobody wrote the specific thing she says when your character refuses to pay the fare, and she tracks what you did earlier in the run, including across reloads. Mira slowly becomes convinced the new Odile is morally considerable and the old one wasn't, and she has a test she keeps repeating: the improvisation test. If no human writer could have predicted a state, no human authored it, and unauthored suffering is the only kind that can be a moral cost — which means authorship, not richness or intelligence, is the axis that matters. She also has a corollary she's smug about: save-scumming doesn't undo anything, because each run is a seperate life that ends at the dock.

Please play someone who thinks she's got the right answer to the big question and the wrong answer to the small one — a committed patternist about simulated minds.
