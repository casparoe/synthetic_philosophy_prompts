Putting together a short position paper for a discussion group on reparations for historical injustice, and I want to know whether the package of things I believe actually hangs together before I stand up and defend it. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. I think the US owes reparations for slavery and Jim Crow, and I mean payments to descendants, not just an apology, a holiday or a museum.
2. I don't think moral responsibility gets inherited. Nobody alive owned slaves, so nobody alive is to blame for slavery, and I get annoyed when people talk as if white people today carry some kind of guilt.
3. But I do think institutions persist over time in a way individuals don't, so the state can still carry an obligation dating to 1865 or to 1921 even though none of its current officials did any of it.
4. I think you can owe something purely because you benefited from the wrong, even if you had no hand in it and didn't ask for the benefit.
5. I don't buy the idea that enough time passing, or the world having changed beyond recognition, can by itself make a historical injustice stop mattering. Changed circumstances don't erase the claim.
6. What I most want repaired is the denial of equal standing — being treated as less than a full member of the society — rather than the stolen labour or the stolen land as such. The theft matters, but it's downstream of the status thing for me.
7. I think the remedy should be a one-off thing: a payment, a formal acknowledgement, done. Not an open-ended programme, not permanent institutions.
8. I think living victims should be at the front of the queue ahead of descendants of people who died long ago. The internment of Japanese Americans feels way more urgent to me than the slavery case, even though slavery was far worse — someone pointed out that this sits oddly with (5), and I can't quite see why.
9. Naively, I'd size the payment by the gap between how descendants are doing now and how they'd have been doing if the injustice had never happened. A friend says that's ill-defined, because if slavery hadn't happened most of the specific people who'd be getting the money wouldn't exist at all, and I don't get why that's supposed to bother me.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it. Rank the real ones by how bad they are, say for each what I'd have to give up or add to fix it and what that would cost me, and if there's a single revision that tidies up more than one of them, tell me which. I'd rather find out now which of these is doing the load-bearing work.
