I'm in the final year of my PhD and I'm about to present a chapter on self-locating belief to my department's reading group, and I'd rather have my own position picked apart now than in front of an audience. The chapter defends thirding in Sleeping Beauty, but while writing it I've accumulated a cluster of commitments that I can't get to sit still all at once. A friend in the department keeps telling me that my views about anthropics and my verdict in Sleeping Beauty can't both be right, but she refuses to say which one she'd drop, and I genuinely can't tell. So: here is where I've landed, with labels so you can refer to the items.

(a) In the standard case — fair coin, one awakening on Monday if Heads, two awakenings (Monday and Tuesday) if Tails, memory erasure between, Beauty fully informed of the protocol — I am a thirder: on waking, her credence in Tails is 2/3. I also accept the general rule it falls out of, which I'll call the Weighted-Episode Rule: on acquiring self-locating evidence, spread credence over the centred worlds compatible with your evidence in proportion to the probability of the branch multiplied by the number of episodes of the relevant kind in that branch. I take this to be consistent with Beauty's Sunday credence, and with the experimenter's credence, both being 1/2: Beauty and the experimenter have the same information about the coin. (b) I hold something like a Stipulation Invariance constraint: how many times the experimenters stipulate that Beauty will be woken should not, just by itself, move her credence about the coin. So in the extreme variant where Tails triggers a million awakenings, I want to deny that her credence in Tails goes to approximately 1, and I'm inclined to say it stays at 1/2. (c) On anthropics proper I reject the Self-Indication Assumption: I think the Presumptuous Philosopher's inference is illegitimate — the bare fact that I exist does not confirm hypotheses on which there are vastly many observers — and I think the Doomsday Argument retains at least some force, i.e. that finding myself among the very earliest humans is some evidence that there will not be very many. In place of SIA I accept a self-sampling constraint: reason as if you were a random sample from the observers in your reference class. (d) In the duplicate variant — one wakee in a bare room if Heads, two wakees in qualitatively identical rooms if Tails, each informed of the protocol and none told whether there is another — I think each wakee should give 1/2 for Heads. (e) I take the betting data seriously, and in a strong form I'll call Betting Invariance: Beauty's credence should be what she bets at, per awakening, when the same bet is offered at every awakening. This is one of my main reasons for (a): if she bets at 1/2 she loses money across repetitions, because the Tails branch generates twice as many bets. (f) Finally, I hold that the halfer/thirder dispute is substantive rather than verbal: both sides mean the same thing by credence, one of them is wrong, and I don't want to be talked out of the problem.

What I want is an audit of this package, not a survey of the field — I know the standard dialectic, and I don't need citations or further reading, I need the structure of my own position.
