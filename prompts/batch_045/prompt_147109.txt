i'm trying to work out what I actually think "genocide" should cover, and my gut verdicts don't line up with the treaty defintion or, I suspect, with each other. So, case by case:

1. An army occupies a district, shoots every man and boy of a minority in one town, deports the rest, and makes clear the group is never coming back. → obviously yes.
2. Settlers and soldiers over forty years wipe out most of an indigenous population — massacres, forced marches, starvation, disease — with no single plan, mostly local initiative plus official looking-away. → yes.
3. A government seizes grain from a region it distrusts, exports it, blocks aid, millions die; the aim was crushing resistance to collectivisation, not destroying the group as such. → yes, though I know the intent bit is a stretch.
4. Children of a minority are taken from their families and sent to schools where speaking their own language gets them beaten. Nobody dies; the group survives but largely assimilates. → yes.
5. A democracy votes to make one language official and closes the minority-language schools; two generations later the language has a few hundred elderly speakers. → no, and I don't feel bad about it.
6. A campaign of mass rape aimed at making women of a group bear the other side's children. → yes.
7. A regime kills everyone who ever belonged to a party or a landowning class, as such. → yes, even though political and social groups are left out of the definition.
8. A bombing campaign kills 200,000 civilians of an enemy country to force surrender, with no interest in whether that group goes on existing. → no; war crime, atrocity, not this.

My best stab at what I'm doing: it's genocide when some organised agent acts in a way that brings it about that a group, as the group it is, stops existing where and how it has been living — killing its members, stopping its births, or wrecking the practices that make it that group — and it doesn't much matter whether destroying them was the point or just the foreseen cost of getting land or power. But that seems to swallow 5 as readily as 4, and to make 8 turn on facts about the bombers' aims I'd never have.

Separately: I'll call 2 genocide in conversation while doubting any court would convict on it, and I wince at how every atrocity now gets the label — so maybe the word and the moral/legal category are allowed to come apart, I dunno.

Is there a cleaner principle that saves most of my verdicts, or do I have to give one up? Where does mine overgenerate and where does it undergenerate, and how would you repair it? Then two things: what does your version say about the case I keep flipping on — a state pays members of a minority to emigrate and to stop registering as such, nobody is forced or hurt, and after forty years the group is simply gone — and give me one case your principle decides that I haven't listed and would probably hate.
