Write the joint statement that a defender of non-empirical theory assessment in fundamental cosmology and an empirical-primacy critic could both sign after two years of good-faith adversarial collaboration. Call them, for short, the assessment theorist and the testability critic.

The subject is whether — and if so, in exactly what sense — a research framework can gain support from considerations that are not direct empirical tests. Take the framework to be a candidate quantum theory of gravity possessing a very large landscape of low-energy solutions which, combined with eternal inflation, implies that the observable universe is one member of a far larger ensemble. The non-empirical considerations on the table are three: the no-alternatives argument (that after decades of concerted effort by many independent groups no empirically adequate rival of comparable scope has been constructed); the meta-inductive argument (that the historical record contains theories which were rationally pursued on structural grounds long before decisive tests arrived); and the argument from unexpected explanatory interconnections (that work inside the framework has repeatedly produced results not built into it, unifying phenomena it was not designed to unify).

Both parties accept the following stipulations, so the document need not re-litigate them. No direct empirical signature of the other members of the ensemble is currently available, and the characteristic scales of the framework are inaccessible to any experiment either side can now specify. There is a genuine, so far unsolved problem about how to define a measure over the ensemble, so that some putative anthropic predictions are not yet well-defined. The framework has so far produced no novel, precise, confirmed empirical prediction that a rival could not also accommodate. Neither side claims the other is irrational, dishonest, or scientifically incompetent.

The document should have five parts.

1. Agreed claims, numbered. Include what both accept about the formalism and the empirical situation; about what the measure problem does and does not rule out; about the distinction between the landscape's being a mathematical feature of the theory and the ensemble's being physically realized; about the distinction between the degree of confidence warranted in a claim and the pursuit-worthiness of a research program; and about what each side grants to be the strongest point on the other side. Every claim should be substantive: none that a side accepts only because it is vacuous, and none phrased so as to smuggle in that side's own conclusion.

2. A disagreements table, with these columns: the crux; the assessment theorist's position in wording she would sign; the testability critic's position in wording he would sign; the test, case, or argument both agree bears on that crux; the assessment theorist's prediction about how that test would come out; the testability critic's prediction, likewise recorded in advance. Give at least five cruxes. Cruxes worth including, unless you find better ones: whether the absence of alternatives is evidence about the world or only about the imagination, attention, and incentive structures of the community looking; whether pursuit-worthiness can be converted into confidence at all, or whether the Bayesian framing requires a prior over an unsurveyable space of possible theories and therefore purchases nothing; whether the no-alternatives argument is dialectically symmetric — whether someone who treats no alternatives found as evidence for must treat no signatures found as evidence against, and if not, what exactly breaks the symmetry; whether the measure problem is a fatal defect or a surmountable technical gap, and how much it matters that some ensemble predictions appear measure-robust while others do not; whether the historical meta-induction is a genuine argument or a selection artifact; and whether the ensemble is best regarded as a prediction of the framework or as a symptom of the framework's failure to supply a selection principle.

3. Three stipulated probes. Treat each as hypothetical, and answer for each in terms of three quantities: confidence in the framework, confidence that the ensemble is physically realized, and recommended funding posture. Give explicit numbers before and after.

Probe one, the registry probe. Suppose an international funding agency runs a deliberately pluralist alternatives registry for thirty years. Each attempt to construct an empirically adequate rival of comparable scope is logged at the outset together with the researcher-years devoted to it, and the groups attempting it are sociologically independent — no shared doctoral lineages, no shared funding source, no shared prize committees. At the end the registry records four hundred logged attempts and twelve researcher-centuries, and no rival passes a jointly agreed adequacy checklist — a checklist that is not impossible to satisfy, since the framework itself passes it. What does this do to the three quantities, and what alternative registry shape (for instance the same number of attempts but two researcher-centuries, or attempts concentrated in one lineage) would render the result evidentially inert?

Probe two, the orphan prediction probe. Suppose the framework yields a new, precise, quantitative relation between two independently measurable cosmological parameters — say between the tensor-to-scalar ratio of primordial gravitational waves and the running of the scalar spectral index — where the relation was not used in constructing the framework, has no anthropic selection explanation, is confirmed to within one percent, and is not derivable from any logged rival. Say separately what this does to confidence in the framework and to confidence that the ensemble is physically realized, and say explicitly whether the testability critic should now concede that the framework has been empirically confirmed while continuing to withhold confidence from the ensemble — and if not, why not.

Probe three, the measure catastrophe probe. Suppose a theorem is proved: for every admissible measure over the ensemble, the predicted distribution of some observable X is well defined, but the class of admissible measures yields predictions spanning the entire observationally allowed range of X, while the prediction for the vacuum energy density is measure-robust. Does the framework retain testable status in virtue of the single robust prediction, does it lose it, or does the question decompose? Record each side's advance prediction about what the community's reaction would in fact be.

4. A short section identifying which parts of the dispute are verbal rather than substantive — naming each, restating it in neutral terms, and saying why the restatement does or does not dissolve it — together with a statement, from each side, of what would count as the debate having dissolved rather than having been resolved.

5. A closing conditions-of-signature paragraph that both can sign: what each side has given up over the two years, and what each still refuses to sign.

Constraints: no citations, no bibliography, no appeal to what particular named individuals have argued, and no request for information either party could only obtain by looking something up. Draw only on the stipulations and probes above. Where a claim is quantitative, supply the number. Where one side suspects the other's formulation is question-begging, flag it and provide a neutral rewording. Where a probe would in fact leave both sides unchanged, say so plainly rather than manufacturing a movement in credence.
