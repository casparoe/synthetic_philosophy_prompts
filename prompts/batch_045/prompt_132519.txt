hi — I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training worth the name, and I've definately drifted into a position on consciousness that I want stress-tested before I inflict it on my team at the pub. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Illusionism — I think that's what the view is called — is true. There are no phenomenal properties. The way I put it to myself: introspection is an API that hands back a struct with a field that has no counterpart in the implementation. It reports its own states as having this ineffable, intrinsic "what-it's-like-ness", and nothing in the system actually has that character.

2. It's an empirical thesis, not an armchair one. The zombie intuition and that lingering feeling that something's left over are data to be explained away by cognitive science, not constraints any theory has to respect. Illusionism stands or falls on whether the research programme can actually produce the account of how the misrepresentation gets generated.

3. Introspection is otherwise reliable and I don't want to give that up. If it seems to me that I'm in pain, that's pretty much the end of the matter — no amount of theory gets to tell me I'm not.

4. Suffering is intrinsically bad: not bad because of what it causes, not bad because we don't desire it, not bad because it derails our plans. Verdict: causing agony in something that will never remember it, never form any attitude towards it, and where nothing downstream changes at all, is still a bad thing to do.

5. Neuron-by-neuron silicon replacement preserves everything that matters — same person, same moral status, nothing lost. And there's a determinate fact about whether a given system has anything going on, even in cases where we could never tell from the outside, whcih is why I don't think spending real money on "digital welfare" questions is silly.

6. Why I ended up here rather than as a realist: I'm convinced that no amount of functional or neural description could ever make it unsurprising that there's something it's like. The gap is unbridgeable, so the honest move is to say there's nothing to bridge.

Where does this not hang together? Please audit rather than lecture — I don't need the 101, don't bother with citations (I'll look things up myself), and I'd rather you didn't flag every pair as equally problematic. Rank whatever tensions there are by how bad they actually are; for each, say what would have to give and what giving it up costs me; and tell me if any of these only look like they clash. Then: which single revision would clean up the most? And be honest about what would change your mind — which consideration would make you re-rank, and where is your own reasoning most likely to be wrong? I'd rather have something with the uncertainty showing than a tidy, confident answer.
