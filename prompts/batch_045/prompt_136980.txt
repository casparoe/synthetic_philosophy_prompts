I work in the metaphysics of time, and I want a diagnosis, not a survey of the literature and not a verdict.

The presentism/eternalism dispute has now run for more than a century with, as far as I can tell, almost no conversions in either direction. Both sides accept the same physics. Both accept that our best spacetime theories assign no distinguished global simultaneity relation to the light-cone structure, and everyone concedes these days that the relativity of simultaneity by itself doesn't settle anything, since a presentist is free to posit a privileged slicing that no local experiment could detect. Both sides also agree on the phenomenology of temporal experience, on the fact that tensed discourse is cognitively ineliminable, and on the standard repertoire of arguments: the truthmaker problem for the presentist, the argument from the explanatory idleness of the past and future, the appeal to the passage of time, the charge that positing an unobservable global "now" is ad hoc. Each side has heard the other's arguments many times, has a reply, has a reply to the reply, and nothing moves. That pattern is what I want explained.

My own hypothesis is that the two sides aren't disputing what there is at all, but what an ontological claim is supposed to do: the eternalist is specifying the domain over which a complete tenseless description of the world quantifies, while the presentist is making a claim about what reality amounts to "at bottom" — about fundamentality or about what grounds what — and the word "exists" is doing different work in the two mouths. If that's right, the debate is a collision of questions under one label rather than a substantive disagreement.

Here is the case that makes me doubt my own hypothesis. Our actual universe isn't the featureless Minkowski or de Sitter spacetime the textbook arguments trade in. On the largest scales it is approximately homogeneous and isotropic, and that structure picks out a physically salient foliation: the cosmic-time slicing of the comoving frame, the frame in which the microwave background is isotropic. It is not a mere coordinate convenience; cosmology is framed in it, and it is the frame in which the large-scale laws take their simplest form. Suppose, in addition, that a completed fundamental theory turned out to be one of those formulations that postulate a dynamically determined preferred slicing, empirically equivalent to current physics on every observation we can perform. The presentist would then have exactly the structure she was said to lack, and the eternalist's reply — that a preferred foliation is just a choice of variables, or that it is unobservable and so not the sort of thing metaphysical privilege can attach to — looks like a claim about the standards for physical reality rather than about time.

So: what kind of stalemate is this? A verbal dispute about "exists," a substantive dispute about what counts as real structure, a bedrock methodological difference about what metaphysics must answer to, or several of these stacked? Does the cosmic-time case support my hypothesis or refute it? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, an empirical discovery, a change of question — or should we expect none, and why?

Please structure your answer in three labelled parts and keep the whole thing under 700 words: (i) your diagnosis with the reasons that carry it; (ii) the strongest objection to my hypothesis, stated as you would state it if you were arguing against me; (iii) what would count as progress on your diagnosis, or an argument that nothing would. I don't want a bibliography or a map of who has said what — I want the structural question, and I want you to say where your own diagnosis is weakest.
