I keep arguing at dinner that the switch to farming was a disaster: that foragers had it better (more leisure, better health, less grinding work), that nobody in their right mind would have picked agriculture if they could see where it led, and that farming is basically where private property, hierarchy and inherited inequality come from. I want you to attack that.

Not with the obvious stuff I've already heard. I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want you to say which ones a defender of my view should genuinely concede rather than brush off. Concretely:

- What would have to be true about forager life for the comparison to collapse? I have my own worry here: the foragers we can actually observe mostly live on land nobody else wanted — deserts, arctic, scrub — so using them as a stand-in for how people lived before farming feels like judging the whole thing from the worst cases. Does that sink my claim, or does it cut both ways?
- The population thing. Way more humans existed after farming than before it. If you care about total wellbeing rather than average, or you think being alive at all is a good, then farming looks like a win even if it made each individual life worse. Is that a real defeater or a change of subject?
- Is "mistake" even coherent here? Nobody voted for it, it took thousands of years, and by the time it looked bad whole populations depended on it. Is there a sense in which no one could have done otherwise, and does that excuse it or make it worse?
- Which of these might already have happened? I gather skeletons from early farming villages show worse health than forager ones. Is that the decisive evidence already in hand, or is it more ambiguous than people say?

And tell me honestly whether I've stated my position so loosely that nothing could count against it. If so, say how Id have to sharpen it to be taking a real risk, and what the sharp version predicts that the other side doesn't.

At the end, tell me which defeater youd actually bet on, where your own reasoning is shakiest, and what single argument would change your mind.
