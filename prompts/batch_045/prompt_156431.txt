I am a novelist, and for the past four years I have been writing a trilogy set among a dynasty of Lancashire cotton families between 1826 and 1926 — one book per generation, each narrated as far as I can manage in the moral vocabulary that generation would actually have reached for, so that what counts as a duty, an insult, or a reasonable expectation shifts under the reader without ever being announced. Working on it has forced me to be explicit about how history moves, and I have noticed that I have quietly assembled a package of commitments that I now want audited before I commit them to a long essay about the trilogy. Something in the package feels to me as though it is doing less work than I think it is, but I cannot locate the slack, and I would rather have the tensions ranked and priced than have the whole thing quietly patched. Here is the set, roughly as I would defend it:

(a) I accept the base/superstructure picture, but only in what I call the menu version, not the blueprint version: the relations of production determine which legal, religious, and domestic arrangements can stably persist across a generation or more, not which ones actually show up, and not their internal detail. I take the observation that very different welfare states, family law, and religious practice coexist with what looks like the same capitalist base to be entirely compatible with (a), and I would defend it that way.

(b) I distinguish origin-determination from tenure-determination. The base explains why a form comes into being; it does not explain why the form keeps going. Forms get a second life, carried by their own institutional momentum, professional interests, and aesthetic appeal, and they routinely outlast the mode of production that needed them — the culture of respectability in my second book, I would argue, survived its material basis by something like two generations.

(c) Causation runs upward too, and not decoratively: a legal form, a religious movement, or a widely read novel can change what people will put up with, and so can change accumulation, discipline, and the terms of a bargain. I take seriously the remark in the Eighteenth Brumaire about people making their own history under circumstances not of their choosing, and I read it as allowing real, difference-making causal power to ideas.

(d) The categories through which my characters experience themselves — conscience, respectability, their own interest, even what strikes them as a reason — are produced by the practices of their mode of production rather than being discoveries about a timeless human nature.

(e) Literary form has enough autonomy that a novel can be formally advanced and perceptually truthful in ways that exceed, and sometimes indict, the class whose world produced it; this is not a comforting add-on for me, it is what makes the trilogy worth writing.

(f) Capitalism is unjust, in the specific sense that it is exploitative, and I want that judgment to be more than a report on where I stand — I do not want to have to hear it as the noise my class position makes.

(g) History at scale is not made by great individuals or by ideas arriving on time; material conditions select which large-scale outcomes are available and stable, and individuals and ideas matter only where those conditions have already opened a space.

(h) Capitalism will be superseded, on mechanistic grounds about crisis tendencies and the capacities it creates — not because it is unjust.

Please audit this rather than explain historical materialism to me: go through the commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, ordered by severity rather than listed as if all faults were equal; for each real tension say precisely what would have to give and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the set; and separately identify any pair that only looks like a tension and dissolves once one commitment is read carefully, since I would rather not throw away something that was never inconsistent. Argue from the structure of the commitments themselves — in particular, tell me whether the version of determination left standing after (b) and (c) can still assert anything testable, and whether (d) leaves any standpoint from which (e) and (f) could be true rather than merely caused. Finally, if one single revision would clean up more than the others, say which it is, even if it is the one I would least like to make.
