You are a philosopher of science with a working knowledge of population genetics, and I want a number from you rather than a survey of positions.

Background I'm granting: in the mid-1970s Popper argued that Darwinism is not a testable scientific theory but a metaphysical research programme, on the grounds that the principle of natural selection, stated at full generality, is close to tautological and cannot be falsified; by the late 1970s he had publicly retreated from the strong version, conceding that selection hypotheses can be tested against concrete cases.

Now the proposition I want your credence on. Grant everything defensible in the original charge: on its own, the general organizing principle of selection is unfalsifiable, and the theory is best called a metaphysical research programme. Even so, that verdict does no demoting work — a science whose general organizing principle is unfalsifiable in isolation is not thereby less scientific than physics, and evolutionary biology as practised is not worse off than the physical sciences on Popper's own demarcation criterion. Count this as resolved true if the most defensible reading of the criterion makes an unfalsifiable-in-isolation organizing principle compatible with full scientific status, so that the charge cannot by itself place evolutionary biology below the physical sciences.

Give me your credence in that proposition, decomposed. The terms I'd like priced, with a number and one line of justification each:

1. That the general principle really is unfalsifiable in isolation once it is formalized properly, via the covariance form of selection rather than the slogan version. Note the awkward case of a population sitting at a stable internal equilibrium under heterozygote advantage: genotypes differ in fitness and heritable variance in fitness is present, yet allele frequencies do not move. If the naive slogan were the theory, that case would refute it rather than confirm it, which complicates the tautology story in a way most discussions skip — say what it does to term 1.
2. That the central laws of a paradigm physical science are unfalsifiable in isolation in the same sense, or, if you think the analogy fails, why.
3. That testability of specific models plus auxiliary hypotheses is enough to satisfy Popper's criterion for the whole enterprise.
4. That metaphysical research programme is, inside Popper's own scheme, a demotion at all, rather than a tolerable or even honourable status for a framework that guides research.
5. That his late retreat surrendered the substance of the charge and not merely its wording.

Before you combine them, list any further sub-question you think belongs in the decomposition that I have left out. Then combine, show the arithmetic or say why these terms don't multiply, and flag which single term your final number is most sensitive to.

I sit at about 0.7. A friend sits at 0.25 and thinks the entire question turns on whether the tautology charge sticks. Tell me which sub-question most likely carries our disagreement, and what one piece of evidence or one argument would move your own number the furthest.
