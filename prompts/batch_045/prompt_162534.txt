In our lab the PI settles disputes about model choice with what he calls the Solomonoff line: preferring the simpler hypothesis is just Bayesian inference with the universal prior, and because the invariance theorem says the reference machine shifts description lengths only by an additive constant, simplicity is an objective, machine-independent quantity — so Occam's razor needs no further epistemic justification, and residual disagreements about priors are artifacts of not having computed the lengths properly. Yesterday someone re-ran our standing compression bake-off, where candidate theories are scored by the length of the shortest program on one fixed reference machine that reproduces the dataset, on a machine with built-in numerical and physics libraries, and the winner flipped; the PI replied that this is only the constant, and constants wash out asymptotically.

Please audit the analogy doing the work here — scientific theory choice as Bayesian inference under a universal prior, with description length playing the role of the log-prior.
