I keep hearing that FTX vindicated the critics of effective altruism and I think that is too fast in both directions, so I want the careful version of it. Take Sam Bankman-Fried as an earning-to-give case: physics and maths at MIT, a few years at Jane Street, then Alameda, then FTX, huge pledged giving to EA-adjacent causes, and then the collapse in November 2022, when it came out that customer deposits had been covering Alameda's trading losses, and the fraud conviction the year after. Those are the newspaper facts as I remember them and you can corect me if I have one wrong.

Now run three critiques at that case, and I mean really run them, not survey them. First, the marginal-impact worry about earning to give: if you do not take the trading seat, someone nearly as good takes it and gives away far less, so the donation is not counterfactually yours in the way the naive arithmetic assumes, and if the seat is in a mostly rent-seeking or zero-sum corner of finance then your real contribution may be negative rather than merely small. Second, the institutional critique: EA let its judgement get concentrated in a handful of grantmaking intermediaries, so when one very large new funder showed up, those intermediaries acquired a dependence that made them structurally unable to ask about him the questions they existed to ask. Third, measurability bias: ranking causes by clean, trial-sized effect estimates made the giving side legible and the earning side invisible, so the variance of the machine funding everything never got priced.

Give me exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, no intro and no outro: 1) the mapping, who the actors were at each step and what their actual options looked like rather than a morality tale; 2) what each of the three critiques says the right play was, for him and for the grantmakers around him; 3) which features of how it actually went the critiques explain well; 4) where they go silent or actively misdescribe it, including the awkward bit that they were critiques of an epistemology and not of fraud, which is a failure mode none of them anticipated; 5) what the episode does to the critiques themselves, real confirmation, or an off-distribution case that only looks like confirmation, right verdict reached for the wrong reason. Somwhere in there say how confident you are overall and single out the thing you are least sure of.
