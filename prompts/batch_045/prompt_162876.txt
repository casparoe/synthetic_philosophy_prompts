ok so I keep flip-flopping between the two big rules for self-locating credence — treat yourself as a random draw from the observers your world actually contains, vs. treat yourself as a random draw from all the observers that might have existed — and I've realised I dont actually have a spec for what such a rule is for. So before I pick a side: write me the desiderata.

What I want is the list of things any rule for "given that I exist and am here, how confident should I be about which world I'm in" has to deliver, with the case behind each, and each marked either non-negotiable or preference-of-one-camp. Start from the cases you'd expect — sleeping beauty with the memory wipe, the presumptuous philosopher and his coin flip between a one-observer and a trillion-observer universe, the doomsday-flavoured update from your birth rank, an Adam-and-Eve style case where you're the first two people and there is nobody else to sample from, fission/teleportation where there are two continuations of you and neither is the real one — but the ones I actually want are the ones I dont see discussed:

- carving-invariance: the rule shouldn't let you move your credence about how big the universe is just by deciding to count time-slices, or cerebral hemispheres, or a person and their later self, as separate observers. Is that even achievable, or does every sampling rule secretly smuggle in a way of counting?
- no armchair cosmology: the bare fact that you exist shouldn't do enormous work, like promoting a speculative cosmological hypothesis from a prior of 0.001 to near-certainty. Is this a real requirement or just a prejudice of people who already dislike the presumptuous philosopher? Tell me which.
- staggered arrival: heads, one observer is created and dies; tails, a thousand observers are created a thousand years later, none of them ever overlapping in time with the first. You find yourself existing. Should temporal coexistence matter to the rule, and does it?
- the duplication lottery: you'll be scanned, and a fair coin decides whether you wake up as one continuation or two. Beforehand you're offered a bet that pays out to each continuation. What credence does the rule have to give you for the bet to come out right — and is "number of continuations is what matters" a desideratum or a downstream consequence of something else?
- big and messy worlds: it has to say something (even 'this question is ill-posed') when the candidate worlds contain infinitely many observers or where the total isn't well defined, rather than returning 1 or NaN.

For each, why its a requirement and what breaks if you drop it. Then tell me which subset you think is jointly unsatisfiable — that's the bit I care about, if the whole list can't be had at once I'd rather know up front — and which single desideratum does the most work in ruling out the popular candidate rules. A short scorecard for the two sampling rules (plus a sleeping-beauty halfer-ish view if it deserves its own column) is fine, but keep it brief, the criteria are the point. Purpose: I want a rule good enough for an agent inside the world making decisions, not for bookkeeping from outside it.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — whether the full list is jointly satisfiable and, if not, what has to give — then the reasoning. No preamble, no restating the question.
