I run a fortnightly reading group for people who came to Stoicism through the modern revival — the self-help books, the 'control what you can control' fridge magnets, Stoic Week — and they've split into two camps: half think the whole thing collapses into 'don't worry about stuff', and half have drifted towards the older Epictetus material where the only thing genuinely up to you is your own assent, and everything else, your body, your reputation, your job, is on the other side of the line. I want a flow chart that sorts them by what they actually think about cases rather than by which version they'd sign up to.

What I need: 8 to 10 questions, each a concrete case with two or three plain answer options — you've rehearsed a talk and it goes badly; you're trying to get a friend to stop drinking; you're campaigning for something that probably won't pass; you're waiting on a medical result; you lose your temper and later think the anger wasn't worth it; you could have prevented something bad and didn't. No question may use the words 'up to us', 'in your control' or 'dichotomy', and none may ask straight out whether emotions are voluntary — someone who'd never heard of Stoicism should be able to answer every one.

Then build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of names: start with the distinctions the answers actually turn on (is 'control' here metaphysical or just therapeutic; do outcomes you can only influence count; can another person's action ever be partly yours; is it the voluntariness of judgement or of attention and effort doing the work; is failing to prevent something you could have prevented a failure of yours at all), and only then drop in the positions as leaves — strict Epictetan, the modern influence-sphere version, the indirect-influence reading where you can partly steer outcomes, the therapeutic reading where it's about where you put your anxiety rather than about what causes what, the quietist-leaning one that makes political action look pointless, and the flat rejection ('wrong question — ask what's worth caring about, not whats in your power').

Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really seperate, especially if the therapeutic reading and the self-help one turn out to be the same leaf wearing two hats, and for each empty region of the space say in a line whether nobody's there because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Give me the questions first, then the answer-pattern-to-leaf key, then the notes on the gaps.
