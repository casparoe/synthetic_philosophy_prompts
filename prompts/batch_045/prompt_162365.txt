I'm an economist by training, and for the past few years I've been working on the design of a retail central bank digital currency. The vocabulary I actually operate in is that of modern monetary economics and double-entry accounting: unit of account, medium of exchange, store of value; the nominal anchor and the price level; seigniorage and the consolidated central-bank-and-treasury balance sheet; one sector's asset is another sector's liability; credit as an intertemporal price and the natural rate of interest; commodity money versus fiat money as a legal artifact. I am fluent in that apparatus and I think in it most days.

On the philosophical side I'm a tourist. I read Book I of the Politics and the part of Book V of the Nicomachean Ethics that deals with exchange and reciprocal proportion, in English translation, once, some years ago. I remember the rough shape: a contrast between managing a household and the getting of wealth, the latter having both a bounded and an unbounded form; the claim that money exists by convention rather than by nature and serves as a measure that makes unlike goods commensurable; and the remark that lending at interest is the most contrary-to-nature form of wealth-getting, because it makes money breed money. I don't know the scholarship and I can't tell which parts of my memory are later scholastic accretions rather than Aristotle.

What I want is a translation, and I want it to be honest about where it breaks. Take Aristotle's account of money and exchange and restate it, claim by claim, in the vocabulary I listed above. For each claim, tell me what translates cleanly, what translates only if it is bent, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. Four claims in particular:

First, that money exists by convention and not by nature. Does this map onto the fiat or chartalist view of money as a legal artifact, onto the arbitrariness of the numéraire, onto the social fact that a currency is worth what others will accept it for, or onto something else — and are those even the same claim? My suspicion is that he is running together several theses my vocabulary keeps separate, and that prying them apart is part of the answer rather than a preliminary to it.

Second, that money is a measure which makes goods commensurable and thereby permits proportionate reciprocity in exchange. I take it this has a clean counterpart in the unit of account and in representing an exchange as a price vector. What I cannot see is the work his notion of need is doing. In my vocabulary relative prices come out of preferences and endowments; there is no single thing called need that does the commensurating. Is that a genuine difference, or is there something in a general-equilibrium vocabulary that I'm failing to reach for?

Third, the distinction between wealth-getting bounded by the requirements of the household and wealth-getting that is unlimited, together with the apparent claim that the unbounded form is a corruption of the bounded one. Can this be said at all in a framework without teleology? I can imagine it recast as an externality argument, as a claim about satiation, or as a claim about the point of an institution, but I can't tell whether any of those is the same claim wearing different clothes or a different claim that happens to land nearby.

Fourth, the hostility to interest as money breeding money. In my vocabulary this looks straightforwardly false: the intertemporal price of resources is not generally zero, and lending is not a barren activity. Still, I'd like you to try to state the strongest version of it that survives translation — perhaps as a claim about what the natural rate can be, or about unproductive as opposed to productive credit — and then say what is left over.

The part I actually care about is the residue report. Where the translation fails, give me a verdict with reasons: is the failure a defect of my vocabulary — something monetary economics as I use it cannot say, and arguably ought to be able to say — or is it a sign that Aristotle's claim was doing less work than it looks like it was doing? Please separate two kinds of residue: claims that are substantive but inexpressible in my terms, and claims that merely look substantive because my terms don't let me see them sharply. And if there is some part of his account that my vocabulary handles better than his own did — something my framework can state precisely that he could only gesture at — say that too.

Please answer in American English.
