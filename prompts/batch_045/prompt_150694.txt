Here is the case my tutor set us — a nonce quatrain of his own in the manner of Marot:

    Nul n'est venu ; nul n'a rien su
    Le jour s'achève sans espoir
    Le feu s'éteint, le ciel est noir
    Moi, je ne suis pas revenue

Four octosyllables, rhyme scheme ABBA. A crib: no one has come, no one has learned anything; the day ends without hope; the fire goes out, the sky is black; as for me, I have not come back. Two things are doing the work. First, nothing in the first three lines marks the sex of the speaker, and the feminine agreement on the final participle does — so the disclosure lands on the last word of the poem. Second, that marker is a mute *-e*: in most pronunciations *revenue* and *revenu* are homophones, so the disclosure is written, not heard, and the same silent letter is what turns the A-rhyme from a masculine rhyme into a feminine one.

My proposal, which I'll call channel-neutral equivalence: two renderings are equivalent with respect to a formal feature if they disclose the same thing at the same point in the reading sequence and thereby force the same retrospective re-reading of what came before, regardless of which linguistic channel carries the disclosure — inflection, word choice, syntax, typography. My candidate last line is "And I — a woman — don't return" (never mind that *learn*/*return* is a slack rhyme; I'd fix it later), with the first three lines as "No one has come; no word I learn / The day dies out; the fire burns low / The sky turns black where embers glow."

So here is my opening, and I'd like your reply to it. Your position has a hole in it that I think is fatal. If what you care about is the *music* of language, then a feature no one hears should be the last thing you insist on preserving — yet you are the one insisting on the silent *-e*, and I am the one saying the channel can be swapped. So you already accept channel-neutrality somewhere; the only question is how far it extends. What I need from you is a non-circular reason for drawing the line where you draw it: a reason for excluding the lexical channel that doesn't simply assume the very thing in dispute.
