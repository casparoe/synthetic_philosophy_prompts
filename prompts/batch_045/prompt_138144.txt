Last night a friend and I argued late into the evening about whether the Condorcet jury theorem gives any genuine epistemic support to majority rule, and I came away unable to locate what I take to be his mistake. Here is his argument, as carefully as I can reconstruct it.

(1) The theorem: take an odd number n of voters who, independently of one another, each vote for the true one of two options with the same probability p > 1/2. Then the probability that the majority votes for the true option is strictly increasing in n and tends to 1 as n grows.

(2) The mathematics is indifferent to how the votes are produced. What it requires is that, conditional on which option is in fact true, the votes be independent draws from a common reliability above one half. 'Voter' is only a slot in the proof.

(3) So one person can fill every slot. Suppose she arrives at three judgments on the same yes/no question in ways that are, given the truth, independent draws from her own reliability — differently framed versions of the question, different orders in which she reviews the evidence she already has, sessions separated by enough time that she cannot recall her earlier answer — and then goes with the majority of the three.

(4) By (1) she has raised her own accuracy. And the construction iterates: three such judgments in place of each judgment, and so on. She can push her probability of error as low as she likes while acquiring no evidence she did not already have.

(5) That is absurd. Whatever makes a large electorate wiser than a small one, it is not something a single person can manufacture alone in a room by putting the same question to herself repeatedly.

(6) So the theorem, as stated, is either vacuous or is being misapplied, and majority rule gets no epistemic support from it.

Of the steps, I would most like to keep (1) and (5). Which one would you give up, why does it look harmless in the way he has stated it, and what does giving it up commit me to? My friend also claimed that once (4) is granted, a single well-informed person who clones herself in this way outperforms an electorate of thousands of barely competent voters, which he takes to turn the theorem against democracy rather than for it — is that a further mistake, or the same one wearing different clothes?
