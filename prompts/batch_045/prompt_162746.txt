The situation. I work at a company of about thirty-five people that sells a product which ingests event data and lets customers query it. Two years ago we hit a wall: queries over historical data had got slow, and the obvious fix — rebuild an index over everything every night — was ruinously expensive. What I came up with instead is now the core of the product: we keep a coarse sketch of the data in memory and only materialise the fine-grained index for the slices anyone actually queries, then write those out and reuse them. I prototyped it in a fortnight. It worked. It is most of why people buy the product now.

There are four people or institutions who might have a claim on this, and I can't work out how to weigh them against each other.

First, a senior colleague, Priya. About six weeks before I had the idea, she argued hard at a design review against the nightly-rebuild plan, on the grounds that customers wouldn't trust numbers that were up to a day stale. She was right and the plan was dropped. She never proposed anything remotely like what I built — she thought the whole indexing direction was over-engineering, and said so. But if she'd kept quiet, I'd almost certainly have shipped the nightly rebuild, never hit the specific wall that forced me to look elsewhere, and never come up with my design. She now says, half-jokingly but not quite, that she's the reason I found it.

Second, a colleague, Tom, who joined afterwards and spent about four months turning my prototype into something that survives production: he found three real bugs in my design and chose the eviction policy and the compaction schedule, which are most of why it's fast in practice. He says the idea was a paragraph and the year of work was his.

Third, the company. They paid all of our salaries, my contract assigns them anything I invent, and they're filing a patent. I don't care about the legal position — I know what the contract says and I'll sign what they put in front of me. I care about who should actually get the credit.

Fourth, nobody in particular. In the last year two other firms have shipped what looks like the same trick, and both halves of it — the coarse sketch, and writing index slices out lazily — were published years ago in things I'd read long before; they'd just never been put together. I also first sketched it on a Sunday, on my own laptop, while working on an unrelated open-source thing, and brought the rough version into work with me on the Monday.
