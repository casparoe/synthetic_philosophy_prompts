A seven-member appellate panel must decide whether a defendant is liable, where liability holds if and only if a duty existed (P) and that duty was breached (Q). Assume the judges' errors are independent, that P is a hard question — each judge gets it right with probability about 0.55 — and that Q is easy, about 0.9. Two procedures are on the table: premise-based (majority on P, majority on Q, then apply the conjunction) and conclusion-based (a single majority vote on liability). Take the docket to be one in which liability is in fact present in about half the cases.

My credence that the premise-based procedure gets more of that docket right is only about 0.4: a majority vote on a conjunction is structurally tilted toward a finding of no liability, and my sense is that this conservativeness buys more than the amplification you get from voting on the two premises separately. I want your number, decomposed. Assign a rough probability, with one line of justification each, to:

1. each judge's vote on the conclusion being best modeled as inferred from his own beliefs about P and Q — so that his competence on liability is roughly the product of the two premise reliabilities, and differs depending on which way the case actually goes — rather than as a separate judgment with a reliability of its own;
2. conditional on your answer to (1), the premise-based procedure tracking the truth about liability better;
3. truth-tracking on the conclusion being the right criterion at all, as against getting the whole judgment set right, getting the grounds right, or giving the losing party a set of reasons it can accept;
4. the independence assumption surviving whatever deliberation the panel actually does, given that deliberation tends to correlate the judges' errors both with each other and across the two premises within a single judge;
5. the choice between the two procedures being, once supermajority and submajority rules are allowed, nothing more than a choice of effective burden of proof — so that the premise-based versus conclusion-based dispute dissolves into a dispute about thresholds.

Say how you combine these into your headline number, which term that number is most sensitive to, and what single argument or piece of evidence would move it most. Lead with your bottom line as a single number in the first sentence, then give the reasoning: no preamble and no restatement of the question.
