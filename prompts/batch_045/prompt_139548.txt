I'm writing up a short position statement on money pumps and diachronic rationality for a decision-theory reading group, and I want it checked for coherence before I present it. Roughly, here is where I've landed.

(1) The argument I actually trust for the standard coherence requirements on preferences is the exploitability argument: if there is a sequence of offers, each of which an agent with those preferences would accept as it came up, that leaves her strictly poorer and otherwise no better off than the sequence in which she declined them all, then there is something wrong with her preferences. That, rather than any intuition about the axioms themselves, is why I accept transitivity and, more tentatively, the independence-style constraints.

(2) Cyclic preferences are not irrational. The familiar three-option cycle catches only the myopic agent: an agent who foresees the whole sequence declines the first trade and is never pumped. I mean this to cover fine-grained cycles as well, ones whose steps are individually negligible, like adjacent settings on a dimmer, since foresight is still available in principle. What is wrong with the agent who gets pumped is a defect in her choice procedure, not in what she prefers.

(3) Present bias is a genuine diachronic irrationality, and sophistication does not excuse it. An agent who knowingly adopts a plan she will later abandon without acquiring any new information is criticizable; if no commitment device happens to be available to her, she is not thereby off the hook, she is just an irrational agent who could not buy her way out.

(4) Changing your preferences over time is not irrational. Someone whose tastes or values genuinely shift, a convert or a person who acquires a taste for olives, is not criticizable merely for the change, even if her earlier self would have rejected the later preferences.

(5) The autopilot case does not tell against intransitive preferences either. Suppose a platform runs a standing rule, on by default and never chosen by me, that automatically swaps my holding for a strictly preferred alternative whenever a one-cent fee covers it, and a counterparty able to generate such alternatives cycles me until I am drained. I never accepted anything, so what is defective here is the mechanism, not my preferences.

Go through these pairwise and tell me which are in genuine tension, which only look like they are, and in each case what would have to give. I have a vague feeling that (2) and (3) cannot both survive, but I am not confident the trouble is there. Rank the tensions by severity, give the cheapest repair for each, and say which single revision would clean up the most.
