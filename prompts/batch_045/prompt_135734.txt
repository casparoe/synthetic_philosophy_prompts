I run a 75-minute session for a grad reading group on social choice and decision theory — 12 people, whiteboard plus index cards, no laptops, no real money. I want an activity where they feel the force of Harsanyi's aggregation theorem instead of watching me derive it. The version I have in mind: if each person's preferences over lotteries satisfy the vNM axioms, the social ranking satisfies them too, and society is indifferent whenever everyone is indifferent between two lotteries, then social welfare has to be a fixed weighted sum of the individual vNM utility functions — additive across people, with weights that track how you scale each person's utility but that nothing in the result itself pins down.

Two things I want them to run into on their own. First, that additive separability shuts out any priority for the worst off that ramps up with how badly off they actually end up: that move has to break one of the three assumptions, and I want them to discover which one by trying to make it work. Second, that the weights are completely undetermined, so all the moral work is being done by whatever device fixes them.

My idea for that second bit is a mechanic where participants spend points to shift the probabilities in the veil-of-ignorance lottery they choose behind, and then find out theyve just chosen the weights. Give each of them an index card with their own utility-over-money function at a different curvature. I need rules, materials, a payoff scheme in points worth nothing, a timeline, and a debrief that maps moments in the game onto each assumption, tells me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and is honest about which feature of the result I have to just state in words because the game cant produce it. Dont say Harsanyi's name until afterwards.
