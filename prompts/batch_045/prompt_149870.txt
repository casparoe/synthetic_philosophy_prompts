I've been roped into doing a five-minute reading to close a one-day workshop on comparative ethics next month, and I really don't want to end on a summary. Half the room will be philosophers who think "role ethics" is just virtue ethics with the individual taken back out again, half will be people who write poetry and couldn't care less either way. I want to end on a sestina, and I want the form to do the arguing rather than the decorate it.

Six end-words: FATHER, RULER, HUSBAND, BROTHER, FRIEND, STRANGER. The first five are the poles of the five relations — parent and child, ruler and minister, husband and wife, elder and younger sibling, friend and friend. The sixth isn't a relation at all. The thing I want the rotation to show: on this reading there isn't a person standing behind the roles who then enters them, so FATHER keeps meaning something slightly different depending on which slot it lands in, and STRANGER — which has no slot — keeps turning up anyway in every stanza, and each time it either gets absorbed (recast as guest, host, neighbour, customer, patient) or is just left standing there as the case the theory can't place. I've been calling that the empty-slot objection in my notes. Don't resolve it; I'd rather the poem leave the stranger unabsorbed and let the tornada be the only place where STRANGER and FRIEND turn up in the same line, since friendship is the one relation you choose rather than inherit or get appointed to, and it's definately the one everyone reaches for when two roles collide.

It needs to be right enough that someone who's read the Mencius wouldn't wince — no cosmic harmony business, no fortune-cookie lines — and it still has to work as a poem for someone who's never heard of any of this. Plain concrete diction, one image or scene running through it rather than a pile of abstract nouns. Get the rotation right (ABCDEF, then FAEBDC, and so on), six stanzas of six, then a three-line tornada with three of the words ending lines and three hidden inside them. No footnotes, no afterword explaining the form, and no endnotes — I'll sort any acknowledgements myself.
