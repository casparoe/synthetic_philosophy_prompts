I'm giving a short paper to our reading group next month — working title something like "is lawhood extrinsic?" — and the claim I want to defend is that on a Lewis-style best-system (Humean) account, whether a perfectly respectable regularity counts as a law of nature can depend on what's happening in a part of the mosaic that has no causal or nomic connection to it at all, whilst on an anti-Humean picture it can't. Use whichever anti-Humean you can make the case work with — Armstrong-style necessitation between universals, or dispositional essentialism — but say which one you're using. My mate Dave said over a pint afterwards that this is just the "Humeanism can't handle uninstantiated laws" complaint dressed up, and it definately isn't, so the case has to be one where that isn't doing the work.

What I need you to build me is a single small world, described as a Humean mosaic — finite, no actual infinities, small enough that a determined reader could in principle write the whole thing out — containing a regularity R such that: (a) the best-system Humean has to say R is not a law of nature (if it matters, say whether you're counting laws as all theorems of the best system or only its axioms, and make the case work for your choice); (b) the anti-Humean has to say R is a genuine, exceptionless law; and (c) the only thing driving the two verdicts apart is the globality of the Humean criterion versus the locality of the anti-Humean one. To pin (c) down: give me a comparison world W* that differs from yours only by deleting or altering a region B which is causally and nomically isolated from the region A where R lives, and in W* everyone, Humeans included, says R is a law. So the Humean's verdict flips on the strength of goings-on in a region that by hypothesis can't touch A.

Cheating, for my purposes, would be any of these: (1) R uninstantiated, barely instantiated, or sitting in a degenerate one- or two-object world — the standard uninstantiated-laws move; (2) R accidental in the all-gold-spheres-are-under-a-mile-wide sense — it needs to be projectible, support the ordinary counterfactuals, be the thing you'd cite in an explanation, and range over perfectly natural properties, with Lewis's natural-property restriction on the best system's vocabulary firmly in force, so no grue-ish or otherwise gerrymandered predicate gets to help; (3) R a higher-level or special-science generalisation — I know the Mendel's-law / ideal-gas-law dialectic and it isn't what I'm after, so R should be a candidate fundamental regularity; (4) anything in B making a difference to A by causation, shared initial conditions, or both regions answering to one and the same system of laws. If your two regions share natural properties, tell me why that doesn't itself hand the anti-Humean the connection I'm claiming is absent.

Where I've got stuck, so you don't repeat it: my first attempt was a region A of a million particles tracing a clean inverse-square pattern plus a region B of a billion particles of pure noise, the idea being that the best system blows its whole simplicity budget coping with B and leaves R out. Dave pointed out, correctly and annoyingly, that one more axiom for R costs next to nothing and buys a million instances, so R stays in. So drowning R out won't do it; I suspect what's needed is something in B that actively competes with R — makes some rival system beat the one containing R — but I can't untill now get that to work without sneaking in one of the cheats above.

Deliverables if you can manage them: (i) the world in one paragraph, with the toy ontology stated up front; (ii) the verdict for each theory with the reasoning, including the rough shape of the simplicity/strength/fit trade-off — no hand-waving, I want to be told roughly what the competing systems look like and why the one that leaves R out wins, and I'd like the win to have some margin rather than being knife-edge (tell me if you can't get margin); (iii) the check against W*, i.e. does the Humean verdict actually flip; (iv) one line on which single feature of the construction is doing the work and what happens if you remove it; (v) the strongest Humean reply you can construct — I'm expecting something like biting the bullet on holism, or arguing the cross-world comparison is illegitimate, or retreating to a local or dynamic version of the best-system account — plus whether that reply costs them anything elsewhere; and (vi) an honest note on where the case is weakest, because Dave will go straight for it.
