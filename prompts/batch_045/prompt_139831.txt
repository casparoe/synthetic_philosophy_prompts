Give me an actual number for this one: that whole-brain death — irreversible loss of all function in the entire brain, brainstem included — is genuinely the death of the human organism, i.e. the brain criterion tracks a real biological event rather than being a well-motivated legal fiction that mostly exists to make organ procurement and withdrawal of care possible. I'm sitting around 0.6. A transplant coordinator I know is at 0.95, an ICU nurse I know is at 0.25, and I want to know where that spread actually lives.

Decompose it for me, one number and one line of justification each, then say how you're combining them (and flag where the terms aren't independent):

- how much rides on whether "death" is even the kind of concept that has a determinate answer here, versus a cluster concept we stipulate our way through;
- how much on the "organism as a whole" / integration story being the right account of when an organism dies;
- how much on the brain being the only thing that could possibly play the integrator role — and stipulate for this one that the clinical tests are perfectly reliable, so don't spend your answer on empirical quibbles about lingering hypothalamic function in diagnosed patients;
- how much on whether it matters if it is a fiction: whether the practical case (transplants, futile care, families) makes the criterion correct in the only sense that counts.

Then the bit I actually care about. Suppose that in twenty years we have an implantable device that restores somatic integration — hormonal regulation, temperature control, coordinated responses to injury — in a body whose entire brain has been destroyed, and does it indefinitely. Does your number on the second or third term collapse? My hunch is that if a machine integrator would make that body an organism again, then "the brain is the integrator" was doing all the work and brain death was only ever death by contingency; but if you'd still call that body dead, then something else — irreversibility of the person, or brains mattering in themselves, not just for what they do — was carrying the weight the whole time. Say which, say where your number moves, and tell me which single term you're least confident in and what would move it most. American English, and keep it reasonably tight.
