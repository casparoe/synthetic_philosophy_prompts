So this has become weirdly practical for me. I've spent nine years doing taxonomy and metadata work for a big public library consortium — authority records, controlled vocabularies, URI redirects when we merge subject headings, the whole deprecate-don't-delete dance — and because of that someone decided I should be on my town's street-name advisory committee. Three meetings in, we have one street: Whitcomb Street, named in 1923 for a mayor who spent his career blocking integration of the schools and the fire department. My grandmother lived on that street for forty years, so I have feelings, which is exactly why I want the abstract version handed to me.

People keep sending me stuff from the philosophy literature on commemoration and I can't tell if the disagreements are substantive or if everyone is making the same three points in different registers. The four options in front of us: (1) keep the name and bolt on a big interpretive plaque; (2) rename to something with no commemmorative content at all, like a number or a landform; (3) dual signage, both names on the blade, new one on top; (4) leave the sign literally reading "Whitcomb Street" but have the town resolve, by ordinance, that the name now commemmorates a different Whitcomb — his cousin, who ran a stop on the Underground Railroad and who basically nobody has heard of. Option 4 is a colleague's idea and I genuinely can't tell whether it's clever or insane.

What I want: I'm fluent in identifiers, referants, dereferencing, deprecation versus deletion, redirects, provenance metadata, namespaces, link rot. I have essentially no philosophy. Restate the main positions in the commemoration literature in that vocabulary, as faithfully and as unsympathetically-to-either-side as you can, and then run our four options through it. Don't smuggle in my framework's assumptions and call it a translation — if the identifier framing quietly presupposes something (like that commemoration is basically a referring relation when maybe it isn't), say so in the process.

Then the part I actually care about, the residue: which claims come across cleanly, which only come across if you fudge them, and which can't be expressed in my vocabulary at all. For each bit of residue, your read on whether that's my framework being missing something or the philosophical claim being less determinate than it sounds.

Format: exactly five numbered items, a sentence or two each, no intro and no summing-up at the end.
1. The central claim in the literature about what a monument or street name is doing, and what that claim is in identifier-and-referant terms.
2. The claim about whose speech a monument is — who the "we" is that's supposedly honoring someone — in my terms.
3. All four of our options translated, a clause or two each, and what the translation makes each of them look like (option 4 most of all).
4. What you had to distort to make it fit, named precisely.
5. What has no counterpart at all in metadata vocabulary, and whether that's my framework's blind spot or their fuzziness.
