I'm the eng lead on a small team shipping an LLM product and we keep going in circles in standup about whether the thing we shipped is "actually intelligent" or just really good at pattern matching. I want to settle it with a quiz: 8 questions, each a concrete case with 2-3 answer options, plus a scoring key that sorts whoever takes it into a position on what intelligence even is. The point is that it should sort people by what they say about cases, not by which label they like — so no question that just asks "do you think today's models are intelligent?". Takers are all engineers with no philosophy background.

The angle I care about: build the tree from the logical space first, then locate named views in it, don't just start from a list of -isms. The distinctions I want doing the work: (a) one general capacity vs. a bundle of mostly independent ones; (b) where the system boundary goes — the trained weights, or the whole harness (prompt scaffolding, tools, retrieval, the fine-tuning loop); (c) whether transfer under adversarial rewording or under deletion of test-time compute is required, or whether raw benchmark score is enough; (d) whether something has to be going on inside (understanding, a world model, consciousness) or whether outward behavior is all there is; (e) whether intelligence can belong to a group — a swarm of very simple agents, or a model plus the humans using it — rather than to an individual.

Cases I want used: a chess engine that is superhuman at chess and useless at everything else; a model that gets 90% on a benchmark and 20% when the questions are reworded; a system that solves the hard problems only if you give it 10,000x more inference compute for search — same answers, way more time; a swarm of dumb agents that collectively do something none of them can do alone; a model that looks brilliant only while an expert human is prompting it in a loop; and a hypothetical "slow alien" that takes a month to answer but is never wrong.

Give me 6-8 leaves, named where a name exists and "unnamed" where it doesn't, one line each on the leaf's most awkward commitment. Then tell me which pairs of leaves this tree can't actually seperate, and give me at least two answer-combinations that nobody occupies, with your guess why — incoherent, unatractive, or just unexplored.
