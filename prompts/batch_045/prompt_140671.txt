Please argue for the following position, as a single continuous prose argument of roughly eight hundred words. Do not cite, name-check, or point to any literature; the case should stand on its own reasoning. Bold or italicize coined terms on first use, but do not use block structure or headings.

Assume for the sake of argument that panpsychism is true: at least some fundamental physical entities have phenomenal properties in virtue of their intrinsic nature, and there is no sharp ontological line in nature separating systems that have experiences from systems that do not. Now consider the argument from expected micro-suffering, which runs as follows. We have no way of knowing whether the experiences of fundamental or near-fundamental systems are on balance pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral. Symmetric treatment of this ignorance therefore requires assigning some non-negligible probability to their being unpleasant. Ordinary human activity, and high-energy physics especially, creates and rearranges enormous numbers of such systems: stipulate that a proposed next-generation particle collider would bring something on the order of a hundred billion short-lived particles per second into existence, and that some of its collisions produce a genuinely thermalized, strongly integrated droplet of quark-gluon plasma whose entire career is over in a few times ten to the minus twenty-three seconds. An expected disvalue is the product of a probability, an intensity, and a head count; here the head count is astronomical, so the expected disvalue is astronomical too. Hence physical quietism: minimize the creation, division, and recombination of physical systems, and in particular do not build the collider.

The thesis you are to defend is that this argument is unsound in a way that generalizes beyond the collider: once the combinatorial and measure-theoretic structure of panpsychist axiology is made explicit, the argument cannot even in principle yield a differential reason to prefer any physically available option over any other, and the collider decision should accordingly be settled on ordinary scientific and social grounds with no sentience-based adjustment either for or against.

Your argument must contain at least three moves.

First, the subset explosion. If every collection of fundamental entities composes a system and composition generates candidate subjects, then a universe of N fundamental entities contains on the order of two to the N candidate subjects, the overwhelming majority of which are scattered, causally disjoint aggregates that no one would count as experiencers, together with nested systems that overlap the subjects we do want to count. Show what this does to the expected-value calculation: the totals before and after any human-scale intervention are both astronomically large and differ from each other by an amount that is either undefined or dwarfed by the experiences of scattered systems whose valence we have even less reason to assess. Conclude that the argument as stated cannot support quietism about this decision rather than quietism about everything, which is self-defeating, since staying alive also rearranges matter.

Second, the conservation point. At terrestrial energies the net count of fundamental entities is, to an extremely good approximation, fixed: what a collider does is rearrange, excite, and briefly transmute, not populate the world with new enduring subjects. Explain why this forces the micro-worry to be reconstrued as a claim about which arrangements host subjects rather than about how many subjects exist, and why that reconstrual hands the panpsychist a structural criterion of subjecthood that does all the real work.

Third, the comparability point. The expected-disvalue product needs a number for the hedonic intensity of a micro-experience expressed on a scale continuous with, and tradeable against, the scale on which we measure human welfare. Argue that no such scale is available even in principle, that the difficulty is the problem of comparing intensities across subjects in its most severe form, and hence that the product is not a quantity at all.

You must then defeat the strongest reply available to the quietist: that she need not count every mereological sum, and can instead adopt a continuous, degree-theoretic account on which moral weight scales with causal integration and temporal thickness, so that scattered sums contribute negligibly while the many brief, integrated micro-systems contribute a very small but real expected disvalue that, multiplied across the collider's output, still swamps ordinary considerations. Steelman this reply, including its most favorable version, and show where it fails. Close by stating explicitly what attitude toward the collider proposal follows from your argument, and by naming the one consideration on the losing side that you find hardest to dismiss.
