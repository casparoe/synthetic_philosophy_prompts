For our final-year reading group on functionalism I want to build a "which multiple-realizability position are you actually in?" decision tree, and I'd rather it be built out of the logical space than out of a list of isms. Everyone starts from the same commitment, stated as neutrally as possible: a mental state is whatever plays a certain causal role, and what plays it is some physical state of the organism. Then branch on cases only — a Martian whose pain is a hydraulic mechanism hooked up to different behaviour; me with one neuron swapped for a silicon chip doing exactly the same local job; two creatures made of the same stuff in the same quantities but wired differently; the same brain state of mine playing one role in me and a different role in a twin with a different learning history (the reverse of the usual case); a creature whose pain-state also plays what we'd count as the belief role; a pair of systems with identical internal organisation but different surroundings, where you want to say the contents come apart; a case where nobody has found the reduction but nothing rules one out; and one where the realisers differ at the level of neurons but not at the level of fundamental physics, or the other way round.

Give me roughly eight branch questions and eight to ten leaves, with the named positions at the leaves — type identity, token identity, machine/psychofunctionalism, analytic functionalism, role- versus realiser-functionalism, the view that each realiser kind gets identified separately so reduction can go through locally, the reading on which multiple realizability is only an epistemic or practical thesis, and the anti-functionalist line that the mental kinds are just much narrower than folk psychology says. But no node may be phrased as "is the identity theory true?" or anything else that just asks for a label: every question should be one a smart person who's never heard these names could answer from the case. Note the combinations of answers that turn out to be empty and say whether that's because they're incoherent, unattractive, or just unoccupied, and flag any two leaves your tree can't really pull apart — I suspect role- and realiser-functionalism collapse once you say how finely the role is specified, but tell me where you actually find problems.
