About twenty-five students, mostly non-majors taking it to fill a requirement; they've had one session on Gettier-style cases and one on the Moorean hand argument, so they've already met closure under known entailment in its single-premise form and found it hard to doubt, which I think is why I've been asked to do the multi-premise version. Please don't explain the paradoxes to me — I've read enough to be dangerous and I'd rather have a plan than a mini-lecture. What I want is the plan.

Concretely, here is what I want from you, and what I already believe about each piece so you can tell me where I'm wrong. On ordering: my instinct is to open with the lottery, because the mechanics are impersonal and easy to get on the board (a fixed number of tickets, exactly one winner, a tiny chance for any given ticket, so accept of each that it loses, and the conjunction contradicts something you know), and then move to the preface as the case where a response has to do real work; but I worry that order teaches the wrong lesson, namely that the problem is really about small probabilities and big numbers, when the thing I want them to feel is a problem about what follows from what for an all-or-nothing attitude like belief or acceptance.
