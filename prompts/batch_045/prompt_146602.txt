I build and deploy ML models in a mental health service, and I've been asked to give a short talk to our clinical safety group about why the feedback loop in our risk model is a philosophical problem and not just a data hygiene problem. Someone told me to read Hacking on looping effects and I sort of get the gist — you classify people, they come to know about the classification, they change, so the kind itself moves — but I need it in my own vocabulary before I can say anything useful to a room of psychiatrists who mostly think I'm there to sell them a dashboard.

Here's the actual setup. We score patients at intake for risk of self-harm. The score goes to the clinician. Since last year it is also visible to the patient in their own portal. High scores trigger outreach, which changes the outcome, and the outcome is the label we retrain on every quarter. On top of that, patients compare scores in a peer support forum and trade stories about what gets you flagged. So I think I have two different loops tangled together: a strategic one, where people learn to answer intake questions differently to get the score they want, and something more like identification, where people take up the label and its expected course and start inhabiting it. Internally we've started calling the first one gaming and the second one drift, but I don't trust either name.

Do this for me. Restate the looping-effects argument about psychiatric kinds as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use — training distribution versus deployment distribution, distribution shift, performativity in the sense of predictions changing the thing predicted, retraining on labels the model itself influenced, interventions versus observations, causal graphs, bandits if it helps. I'm fluent in all of that, and causal inference especially. Then give me the residue report, and be honest rather than diplomatic:

- Does the looping claim just collapse into performativity plus label shift, or is there something in it my vocabulary literally cannot express? My guess is that the part about people reorganising their own self-understanding around a kind, so that the kind acquires a different causal structure, is the part that won't fit. Tell me if that's wrong and it's just a slow feedback loop I'm being sentimental about.
- What happens to the distinction between the kind and the classifier in my setting, where the kind is just whatever the retrained model carves out and there is no clean ground truth to check it against? Does Hacking's story quietly presuppose something my setup does not have?
- Is there any of it that goes false or empty when the people classified do not know they are being classified? Our portal change is recent, so for years the loop ran only through clinicians.
- The thing I most want your read on: is the residue evidence that my framework is missing something real — agency, self-conception, the fact that the target of prediction is an agent that reacts to the prediction — or evidence that the philosophical version is vaguer than it sounds and would partly dissolve if somebody made it precise enough to implement?

Finally, tell me what I should not claim in front of that room. If there's a strong version of the looping claim that my ML framing genuinely supports and a stronger one it doesn't, say which is which and where the line falls, because I'd definately rather not overclaim to people who already suspect I think a risk score is the same thing as a diagnosis.
