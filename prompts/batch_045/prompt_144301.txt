Background I shall assume we share: Putnam's Twin Earth case and the arguments that grew out of it; Burge's arthritis case; the standard contrast between content externalism and its internalist rivals (descriptivist, conceptual-role, or narrow-content accounts); and the familiar two-factor picture on which a speaker and their microphysical duplicate share a use-profile — a character, an inferential role — while differing in the contents of their thoughts.

The position I hold, stated as precisely as I can manage. Call it causal-historical two-factor externalism:

(i) Necessarily, intrinsic duplicates — indeed full functional duplicates, agreeing in every counterfactual dispositional profile — can differ in the contents of their token thoughts; content does not supervene on the intrinsic.

(ii) For a broad and theoretically important class of concepts — natural-kind concepts, artefact concepts, perceptual-demonstrative contents, the contents of basic observational beliefs — the difference-maker is neither the agent's occurrent or dispositional use nor any description or cluster of descriptions the agent could produce, but a causal-historical relation between the agent's tokenings and the item itself, typically mediated by a linguistic and investigative division of labour.

(iii) The use-profile or character is nevertheless internal, shared by duplicates, and it is what secures first-personal authority.

(iv) Content so fixed is genuinely explanatory: it is what distinguishes my judging truly that I have arthritis in the thigh from my twin's judging falsely, and it is what a complete psychology of an agent must mention.

(v) The thesis is not merely negative. There is a substantive, discoverable story about which causal relations do the fixing, and that story generalises well beyond the paradigm cases.

(a) Continuous switching. (b) The orphaned corpus. A future artificial system is trained exclusively on a corpus produced by a Twin-Earthian civilisation, every one of whose water tokens is causally downstream of XYZ. (c) The idle difference.
