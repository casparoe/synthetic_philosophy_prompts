For a graduate seminar on individual contributions to collective harms, I need a case built to a specification I keep failing to meet, and I would like you to construct it.

Setting: realistic and small-scale — a workplace ballot, a condo board vote, a food co-op, a local union vote. Small enough that a single member's contribution has a genuinely non-negligible chance of being pivotal, and arranged so that the agent knows the numbers: she is not guessing about whether she matters. One paragraph. No electorates of millions, no climate-scale aggregations, no exotic devices.

The specification: the standard reply to the inefficacy objection — that one may turn out to be pivotal, and that even a small chance of tipping a high-stakes outcome outweighs the cost of contributing — should be available and correct on its own terms, and yet a properly done expected-value calculation should fail to support contributing. The gap has to be generated by the case rather than stipulated. What I am reaching for is the thought that being pivotal is itself evidence: the situation in which her contribution tips the balance is also a situation about which she learns something — about what the others want, about whether the collective effort would actually work once undertaken, or about whether she is even right that the outcome she is aiming at is the better one. I want that made concrete in the scenario, not asserted as a slogan.

After the scenario, give me: the naive pivotalness calculation and its verdict; the revised calculation conditional on what pivotalness reveals, and whether it favors contributing, opposing, or abstaining; the single structural feature that does the work; the same case with that feature removed, showing that it collapses back into the ordinary argument; and the strongest reply available to someone who wants to keep the naive calculation.

Excluded in advance: universalization, expressive or symbolic considerations, complicity, and appeals to character or integrity. Whatever reason survives must be forward-looking about outcomes.
