I am writing a paper on whether the Ulysses-contract analogy earns its keep in arguments about constitutional precommitment — entrenchment clauses, supermajority requirements, provisions that remove a question from the ordinary legislative agenda — and I need a case that isolates the feature which decides the question. Please construct one to the following specification.

The case. One realistic scenario, in a single paragraph of at most 200 words, with two structurally parallel halves. In the first, an individual binds her own future action by a device she sets up herself. In the second, a political community does the same kind of thing to itself by constitutional entrenchment. Both halves must guard against the same general kind of predictable failure of future judgment, be authored by the party they bind, be entered into on the same information, and be enforced exactly as written by a faithful enforcer. State explicitly, for each half, what the constraint is, what condition triggers it, and who applies it. Constraints: no exotic technology, no infallible or disinterested enforcers, no addiction, incompetence, or mental illness, and nothing may be made to turn on the founders being dead or the later citizens being different people.

The accounts. The case must be built so that the following three accounts deliver the following verdicts, and the verdicts must fall out of the accounts together with the facts of the case — not from a character asserting them or from the narration telling me so.

1. Design fidelity. A precommitment is legitimate when the same agent authored it, it targets a failure mode she had good evidence she was prone to, and it is applied as written. This must validate both halves.
2. The Ulysses condition. A precommitment is legitimate only if the condition that switches the constraint on can be identified by the enforcer without the enforcer having to make the very substantive judgment the constraint removes from the bound agent. This must validate the individual half and reject the constitutional half.
3. Constitutive authority. A precommitment is legitimate when it is partly constitutive of the agent whose later choice it constrains — absent it, there is no such agent with a will to be thwarted — and illegitimate when it constrains a will that would exist anyway. This must reject the individual half and validate the constitutional half.

Then show the work. Name the single structural feature of the case that makes (2) and (3) come apart, and show what happens to each of the six verdicts if that feature is removed or reversed. Give the strongest attack on the case from the side that loses — someone convinced the scenario is rigged rather than merely unflattering — and say whether it lands. Finally, commit: state whether, in your view, the analogy between individual self-binding and constitutional entrenchment survives your case, and give the reason that actually moves you.

Length: 800 words at most, scenario included.
