Two philosophers in my department have agreed to stop shouting at each other and do an adversarial collaboration: one holds the Taurek-style view that the numbers don't count (save the one, or if you can, give everyone an equal shot, but don't add up losses across people), the other thinks you save the greater number, full stop. Can you draft the short joint statement they could both put there names to?

Some stipulations I want built in: no lotteries are available — you know exactly what happens under each option and can't hand out chances instead — and the many each lose much less than the one does (10,000 people get a mild one-second headache; the one loses a hand). I also want them forced to deal with what I think is the actual crux: a variant where those 10,000 headaches all happen to a single person spread over a long life, so there's no 'each' left to appeal to.

Format: numbered agreed-claims section first — including whatever the numbers-don't-count side concedes in the case where the many each lose exactly as much as the one — then a disagreements table with the cruxes (I'd guess: whether there's some seriousness threshold below which numbers start counting again, whether pairwise comparison or total loss is the right frame, and whether it's the separateness of persons or something about the size of a complaint doing the real work). For each crux, give a case or argument both would accept as bearing on it, plus what each predicts it will show.
