Been chewing on Sen's liberal paradox for about two weeks now — the one with the prude and the lewd and the single copy of Lady Chatterley's Lover — and I think I've accidentally souped it up into an argument that proves way too much. I can't find the joint and I'd rather not walk around believing the conclusion, so: where does this break, and what does fixing it cost?

Two people, Ann and Bob. Ann's protected sphere is whether Ann reads the book; Bob's is whether Bob reads it. Four states: 0 = neither reads, A = Ann only, B = Bob only, AB = both read.

(1) Minimal liberalism, in Sen's form: if two states differ only in whether Ann reads, and Ann prefers one of them, then society should prefer that one too. Same for Bob. I want to keep this — a right just is your ranking winning on your own sphere. If you tell me to drop (1), you also owe me an account of what a right is instead.
(2) Pareto: if Ann and Bob both strictly prefer x to y, society should prefer x to y.
(3) "Society should prefer" is a single relation and it has to be acyclic. If it cycles, no state is undefeated and we aren't ranking anything.
(4) Unrestricted domain: any profile of coherent individual rankings can show up.

(5) Sen's own cycle first, as a sanity check: Ann prefers 0 to A, Bob prefers B to 0, and both prefer A to B (the prude would sooner read it himself than let the lewd have it; the lewd would rather the prude squirm). Rights give 0 ≻ A and B ≻ 0; Pareto gives A ≻ B; cycle. Fine. That's the famous result.

(6) Now the part that worries me: you don't need (2) at all. Take Ann's preferences over her own sphere to be 0 ≻ A and AB ≻ B, and Bob's to be A ≻ AB and B ≻ 0. Ann's sphere covers the pairs {0,A} and {B,AB}, Bob's covers {0,B} and {A,AB}. Reading off (1) alone: 0 ≻ A ≻ AB ≻ B ≻ 0. A four-cycle out of rights, no Pareto step anywhere.

(7) And nobody here is being nosey. Ann is indifferent between A and AB, and between 0 and B — those pairs differ only in what Bob does, and she has no view about that. Likewise Bob is indifferent between A and 0 and between AB and B. All each of them has is a preference about their own action that happens to be conditional on the other's: Ann wants to do the opposite of Bob, Bob wants to match Ann. Wanting to do the opposite of someone is not having a preference about what they do. At least, that's how it seems to me.

(8) So generalize: hand out spheres to any number of people however you like. Whenever at least two people's preferences over their own sphere are conditional on someone else's sphere — coordination, anti-coordination, congestion, I'll go if you go, wanting to be the odd one out, all completely ordinary — the same construction runs, and rights alone cycle. Minimal liberalism plus acyclicity are then inconsistent in most of the preference configurations that actually occur.

(9) Which means the idea of a protected personal sphere is formally broken, and my libertarian friend's claim that the state may never override her about her own body has no coherent content even in a world where nobody cares what anybody else does. That is definately not something I believe.

Some things I've already tried. A guy at my meetup said isn't this just Condorcet in a trenchcoat, and I don't think so: there's no aggregation rule being fed preferences here, the social ranking is read straight off the rights. Someone else said step (7) can't be right, because if Ann is indifferent across Bob's sphere then transitivity forces her ranking of her own reading to be the same whether he reads or not. I don't see the grip: her indifferences are between states where her own action is already fixed, and the conditional preference is about her action given his, and those look like different claims to me. I'd rather give up (4) than (1), (2) or (3), so I'm happy to restrict the domain, but I can't see which restriction saves me if (7) is right and the bad profiles are the ordinary non-nosey ones.

So: which step do you kill? If it's (7), taboo "nosy" and tell me exactly what has to be true of someone's ranking for Sen's machinery to count them as meddling, and whether the thing I wanted in (7) — a preference about your own action that shifts with someone else's — can be represented at all inside a single ordering over joint outcomes. And price the alternatives: what do I pay for dropping (1)'s decisiveness reading, for dropping (3), or for restricting the domain?
