I work on the team shipping an AI companion app, and we are deciding whether to ship an erotic mode for verified adult users. Several jurisdictions now require age assurance for adult content, so we are in scope, and the internal debate has stalled on the ethics rather than the compliance: everyone grants that there is no identifiable victim in a session between a consenting adult and a model, and our critics still call the product degrading. I have no philosophy training, and the framework that keeps being invoked in these threads is Benthamite, so I want to know what Bentham himself would have said, and what he would have told us to ship, refuse to ship, or leave to users.

What I think I understand of his position: he judged sexual conduct by the harm it did rather than by its kind or by the disgust it provoked; he argued, in an essay he did not publish in his lifetime, that same-sex acts harmed no one and so should not be punished; he counted pleasures by quantity rather than by kind; and he held that conduct harming only the person who engages in it is not the law's business, though informal disapproval of it may still be a real and legitimate cost.

Apply that, if it is right, to three features of our case with no eighteenth-century analogue: the objection that synthetic gratification is degrading or corrupting even with no victim at all; the empirical question whether it substitutes for real-world sexual harm or feeds demand for it; and the fact that any harm in our system probably sits upstream, in how the models and the material they were trained on were sourced, rather than in the user's act. Tell me whether his refusal to rank pleasures by kind leaves him with any resource for treating synthetic pleasure as a lesser thing. Mark clearly which parts of your answer follow from what Bentham actually committed to and which are you extrapolating on his behalf, and say so plainly if the question is underdetermined or if my summary of him is wrong — I would rather be corrected than handed a confident answer.
