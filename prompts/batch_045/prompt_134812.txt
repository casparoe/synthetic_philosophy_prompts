For a graduate seminar in political theory I am building a diagnostic decision tree, not a survey: given what a student says about concrete cases, it should sort them into a conception of liberty, and no node may ask them which label they prefer or presuppose the standard threefold taxonomy.

Build the tree from logical space rather than from a list of isms. Start from a small set of yes/no questions (at most six), each phrased as a case a student can answer without knowing where it leads, and treat each as isolating one axis: whether unfreedom requires an agent or can be produced by a jammed lock; whether an unexercised but standing power suffices; whether the obstacle must be external; whether interference by an authority you endorse and can contest reduces freedom at all; whether unfreedom is a property of whole persons or of specific options. Use at least these cases as branch nodes: the prisoner whose guard holds the key and will never turn it, versus the prisoner behind a lock no one can open; the reliably benevolent master who has never once interfered; the law you fully endorse, which tracks your own reasons but closes off options you would otherwise have; the addict who wants to stop and cannot, with no external obstacle; the official who may override you only on conditions you helped write and can appeal; and one case of my own, the anticipatory scheduler, an adaptive workplace system that learns your plans and quietly rearranges the environment so that you never attempt anything that would have been blocked, so that no one ever prevents you from doing anything and no one ever threatens you.

Then locate the live positions among the resulting answer combinations (no more than eight leaves), including at minimum: non-interference understood as a purely causal, act-based and option-level matter; a moralized version on which only illegitimate or interest-flouting interference counts; non-domination understood as the absence of an unexercised standing power, with the internal split between arbitrariness as failure to track the subject's interests and arbitrariness as unaccountability; self-mastery or autonomy views, in both a procedural and a substantively perfectionist form; and one view on which freedom is a status sustained by social and material conditions rather than by the absence of obstacles. Note which combinations stay empty and diagnose each: incoherent, unattractive, or merely unoccupied. Flag explicitly any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate, say whether the tie is a genuine collapse of the distinction or a fault in your questions, and, if some further case would separate them, supply it. Finish with a scoring key that lets a reader who answers the cases in a different order still land on a leaf.

Please write the answer in American English.
