So about six months ago I had a full-on panic attack in the middle of a supermarket — heart going like a drum, couldn't get a full breath, walked out and left the cart in the aisle. Nothing bad happened, nothing was ever going to happen. Since then I get these episodes in supermarkets where my body does the whole thing but I'm not thinking anything is wrong and I'm not, like, judging supermarkets to be dangerous. I know they're safe. My therapist keeps saying "you're afraid of supermarkets" and I keep saying no I'm not, and its become this weirdly load-bearing disagreement: if I am afraid, then the exposure stuff she wants me to do makes sense, and if I'm not, I'm being asked to treat something I don't have.

My friend Jules — hospice nurse, not a philospher, but extremely opinionated about what does and doesn't count as an emotion — has been ruling on cases for me for weeks and I've written them all down. Her verdicts so far. Not an emotion: jumping when a car backfires; being nasty to everyone because you skipped lunch; the warm pleasure of a hot bath on a cold day; nausea from a bad oyster; tip-of-your-tongue; the sense that a room is off before you work out that someone moved the couch. Emotion: delight at a friend's good news; outrage at a company poisoning a river; being creeped out by a guy at the bus stop who hasn't actually done anything; disgust at the idea of eating a chocolate shaped like dog shit; sadness at the end of a movie you know is fiction; a three-day gloom with no cause, which she calls a "mood with a hook"; schadenfreude at a rival's small misfortune while thinking it's wrong to feel that — she says that one is the real thing, definitely.

Here's what I want from you. Predict what she'll say about my supermarket episodes, and tell me what rule she's actually running, because I can see more than one that fits all of the above: something like emotions-are-evaluations, something like emotions-are-perceptions-of-value-that-don't-have-to-agree-with-what-you-believe, and something like emotions-are-feelings-that-are-about-something-and-shove-you-around. Say what each of those predicts about my case and which you'd bet on and why. I have a candidate rule of my own, the stubbornness test: it's an emotion if it survives your telling yourself I know better. Mine survives that. Also name the further case that would actually split the rules apart — I was thinking of the flat version, someone who after a stroke says her husband died and she is sad and feels absolutely nothing at all, but if there's a better one, use it. End by telling me what would change your mind, or where you think your reasoning here is most likely to break. And please, no headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables — just write it out like an email.
