Bit of a weird request. I help run a Sunday-afternoon moral-psychology discussion group that meets in the back of a coffee shop (about half the regulars are therapists, so every other week someone drags in the clinical literature and we lose forty minutes), and we've spent three weeks going in circles about guilt versus shame without getting anywhere. Part of why it matters: two of our members are advising a small alternative school that is rewriting its discipline policy, and the staff keep using a phrase like "repair-without-stigma" and want to know whether they should try to cultivate guilt and extinguish shame, or whether shame can be rehabilitated and aimed at the right things, or whether the school should get out of the emotion business altogether and lean on clear rules, predictable repair and restitution instead. So the handout I want isn't neutral — the point of sorting people is to see which of those three things their view commits them to telling a school to do. Right now everyone can only name two options (the old anthropological guilt-culture/shame-culture contrast, and the big philosophical rehabilitation of shame that's on every syllabus), which is why they talk past each other.

Could you build me a one-page decision tree that sorts people by what they say about cases rather than by which emotion they're "for"? Roughly six to nine branch nodes, eight to ten leaves, built from the logical space first and only then labelled. Every node should be a yes/no or two-or-three-way question a thoughtful 19-year-old who's read nothing could answer, phrased about a concrete case, and no node or leaf should carry a theorist's name or the words "better" or "worse" on their own. The splits I care about are the ones that decide whether shame is reformable: whether shame needs an actual witnessing audience or only an internalized one; whether shame's target is essentially the whole self or can be a local trait; whether the action-tendency people attribute to shame (hiding, lashing out) is essential to it or just what happens in a bad environment; whether guilt really requires an act of one's own that one could have avoided; and whether an emotional repertoire is something a small institution like a school can deliberately shift over a few years or is just downstream of how mobile, surveilled and interdependent people's lives are. Please have the tree use these four cases: (1) a school where every hallway is recorded and misbehaviour is known within minutes, so exposure is guaranteed without a crowd — I call it the fishbowl; (2) what one of our members calls "lone shame," where someone feels degraded and exposed in their own eyes over something no one witnessed, that no one will ever witness, and that isn't clearly a wrong they committed or a norm they violated — does this show shame needs no audience at all, or is it a different animal; (3) inherited money that came from a historical wrong, where the heir feels a strong pull to repair even though she did nothing and could not have acted otherwise; (4) the repeat offender who feels loads of guilt, feels no shame at all, apologises efficiently every time and does it again next month. The leaves must include, at minimum: shame-is-just-worse; shame-rehabilitated-as-identity-concern; shame-is-only-as-good-as-its-audience; pick-per-case pragmatism; the distinction-between-the-two-is-misdrawn; shame-is-tied-to-rank-and-hierarchy-so-equals-should-want-less-of-it; both-are-disciplinary-technologies-and-the-real-question-is-what-kind-of-subject-you're-making; nobody-can-choose-because-repertoires-track-social-structure; and stop-engineering-emotions-and-build-procedures-instead. Add any leaf you think we're missing.

Give it back as a single JSON object with exactly these keys: "branch_nodes" (each with "node_id", "question", "case_used" naming which of my four cases or a new one, "options" as a list of {"text", "next_id"}, and "why_it_splits" in one sentence naming which two or three leaves come apart here); "leaves" (each with "leaf_id", "label", "signature" describing the pattern of case-answers that lands here, "awkward_commitment" in one sentence, and "school_advice" in one sentence: cultivate-guilt, rehabilitate-shame, or get-out-of-the-emotion-business); "empty_regions" (each with "path" describing a combination of answers nobody occupies, "verdict" as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and "note"); "flag_pairs" (each with "leaves" as a pair of ids, "why_the_tree_cant_separate_them", and "extra_question" that would separate them); and "notes" (anything you'd warn me about before I photocopy this). No bibliography, no citations, no further reading — we're not writing anything up, we just need something to argue with on Sunday.
