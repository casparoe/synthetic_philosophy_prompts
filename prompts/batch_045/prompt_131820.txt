The setup. I host a moderately technical philosophy podcast. My next guest is a composite figure — please treat her as invented rather than as any real individual, and call her "Dr. Renard" — who defends the KK principle (roughly: if S knows that p, then S is in a position to know that S knows that p) while *accepting* the anti-luminosity argument associated with Timothy Williamson's *Knowledge and Its Limits* (2000). As I understand the dialectic, and I may have it slightly wrong so correct me in passing if so: the anti-luminosity argument runs through a gradual-change case — a subject's condition shifts by tiny steps across a long sequence of neighbouring cases, the subject must be right about some case in the sequence and must be able to discriminate it from its immediate predecessor, and a margin for error is then needed at each step; the upshot is supposed to be that no non-trivial condition is luminous, and that result is standardly used to undercut KK, on the thought that knowledge would have to be luminous for higher-order knowledge to be guaranteed. Dr. Renard's way out is what she calls the constitutive reading: KK is not a claim about a psychological detection mechanism by which a subject scans her own condition and infers that she knows; it is a constitutive norm on knowledge itself — one knows only if one is also in a position to know that one knows. Her stock move when cornered is to say that the objection is really a theory of introspection, not of knowledge, and I want that move made progressively harder to reach for, in the way a good interviewer arranges.

What I want from you: eight to ten questions, in the order I should ask them, written as I would actually say them aloud to a mildly technical audience (short, no jargon dumps, no "what drew you to this topic"). For each question give me (a) the question itself, (b) two or three sentences on the answer Dr. Renard is most likely to give, given the constitutive reading, and (c) one follow-up written specifically against that answer, not against a straw man. I must get at least one question on each of the following, and I want the sequencing to build:

- The luminosity argument itself: whether the constitutive reading simply relocates the luminosity assumption one level up, and what exactly it means for a norm to require being in a position to know something.
- A case I made up for this episode, the Two-Dial case: a radiologist whose first-order discrimination is intact while a second, independently adjustable dial governs her metacognitive calibration; over a shift the second dial drifts downward, so that she is reliably right about the images and her confidence-track record becomes noise. Fix the details yourself if they need fixing; I want a question that forces her either to deny knowledge to someone intuition says knows, or to admit that knowledge is hostage to a dial with no connection to truth.
- The iteration worry: KK plus closure looks like it generates an unending tower of higher-order knowledge, and I want a question about what that costs a view that was supposed to be about finite subjects.
- Knowledge without metaconcepts: reliable belief-forming processes in subjects who arguably have no concept of knowledge — a clairvoyant with a reliable faculty and no supporting evidence, small children, non-human animals.
- Whether the constitutive reading does more work than advertised: if it silently settles Gettier-style cases, is that a bonus or a symptom?

Give the set some arc: open where she is comfortable, compress later, and close with the question she cannot answer with the introspection move — something about what evidence could in principle count against the constitutive reading, and what would actually make her give up KK. Don't include questions that merely ask her to restate the view, and don't name-drop papers or ask for reading: I want questions, not a bibliography.
