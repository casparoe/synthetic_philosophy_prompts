Question for anyone who works on this stuff — please answer in British English, it's going into a seminar handout.

I teach a grad seminar on naturalizing mental content, and I keep hitting the same frustration in the readings and in seminar, and I want a word for it. I'm going to describe it without the jargon on purpose: every time I use the jargon someone says "oh, that's just the disjunction problem" or "that's the generality problem again", the conversation stops, and I don't think either of those is quite what I've got.

Here's the pattern. Take a fairly simple signalling system — a vervet alarm call, or whatever state of a frog's visual system triggers the tongue flick. To say what that state is *about* you apparently need two things: a story about what the mechanism was built or selected to do, and a story about what the creature is currently using it to track. What I keep noticing is that these two stories get adjusted to fit each other, and adjusted until the answer comes out as "leopard" or "fly" — i.e. until it matches what we were going to say anyway. Press on the history story ("why not: selected for tracking leopard-shaped things, or aerial hazards?") and the answer comes back in terms of what the downstream system needs. Press on the downstream story ("why not: it causes climbing behaviour, so why isn't the content *climb now*?") and the answer comes back in terms of the history. Neither story seems to do independant work; each is calibrated against the other, and both are calibrated against the content we antecedently wanted. The machinery looks like it's deriving a content when it's really being tuned until it outputs one.

Two more places I see it, which is why I think it's one thing and not two:

(a) Co-opted mechanisms — a trait that was shaped for one job and is now used for another. Here the history story and the current-use story genuinely come apart, and people either go quiet or quietly pick whichever one gives the intuitive answer for the case in front of them.

(b) The grain question — "leopard" vs "predator" vs "danger from above" vs "get into a tree". Nothing in the naturalistic machinery seems to fix the grain; what fixes it is what makes the creature's behaviour come out successful in the cases we've already picked as the paradigm ones.

So: does this have a name? I've got "the calibration loop" in my notes, which I'm sure its not. But I want more than a label anyway — I've had enough of labels ending discussions. Specifically:

1. What are the nearest established things, and how does mine differ from each? I keep being told the disjunction problem and the generality problem; I want to know exactly why mine isn't just one of them, or if it is, which one and in what respect.
2. Is this best thought of as a circularity objection, an indeterminacy objection, an underdetermination objection, or something else? Those feel like different objections with different cures, and I'm not sure which one I actually have.
3. Is it fatal, or just a constraint on how a theory of this kind has to be stated? I have a suspicion the answer turns on whether the machinery was supposed to *generate* content ascriptions from scratch as opposed to systematising ones we already make, but I can't say why.
4. What would the boundary cases look like: a case where the mutual adjustment is legitimate (the two stories genuinely converge for reasons that don't presuppose the answer) versus a case where it's just curve-fitting? If I can't tell those apart I don't have an objection at all, and I'd rather know that than go on waving my hands.

If there's genuinely no established term, coin one, define it, give me the boundary cases, and tell me what I'd have to find to convince myself it had been noticed before. And please write it in British English, and go light on the bibliography — I can find the reading myself, I just want the carving.
