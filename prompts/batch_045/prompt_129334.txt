I'm auditing a graduate seminar on the dispute between moral particularism and generalism, and the seminar leader — call her A. — has spent the whole term ruling on a long series of hypothetical cases while steadfastly refusing to say what she takes herself to be applying when she rules. Three of us have been keeping a shared document of her verdicts, and we have discovered that we disagree about what she will say about the case she has circulated for next week; there is a small bet riding on it, but I am more interested in the argument than the bet. Her verdicts so far, as we have recorded them:

- A promise, freely made, kept at moderate cost to the promisor: the fact that you promised is a reason to keep it, outweighed here by the harm involved.
- A promise extracted at gunpoint: the fact that you promised is not a reason at all — not merely a reason that is outweighed. She has drawn this distinction several times and clearly means it.
- A promise to a dying friend, kept at great cost after the friend's death: a reason, and a fairly strong one.
- Causing physical agony to an innocent person as the only way to avert a catastrophe: a reason against performing it, heavily outweighed.
- Procuring pleasure for a sadist by arranging for him to witness an act of cruelty: the pleasure is not a reason in favour; pressed, she also denies that it is a reason against — the feature simply drops out of the case. She uses the word silencing for this.
- Publicly humiliating a corrupt official in the course of exposing him: the humiliation is a reason in favour of doing it, not merely an outweighed reason against. She uses the word reversal for this.
- An act's being unnatural: never a reason, in any case she has considered.
- An act's being what everyone around here does: sometimes a reason (coordination cases), sometimes inert, and in the case of a lynching, a reason against.

My two co-tabulators read the pattern as a typed picture: a small core of features whose valence never changes, which context can at most outweigh; a periphery whose valence context can cancel or invert; and a residue of features that are never reasons at all. I think the record fits something more thoroughgoing just as well: that for any feature whatever, the surrounding context can leave its contribution alone, cancel it, or invert it; that the apparent fixity of the core is an artifact of which cases we have happened to imagine; that when a feature goes inert she is usually pointing to the absence of a condition that had been doing the work all along rather than to something that defeats it; and that some of her verdicts may depend on how thickly the feature is described (causing pain versus causing pain to someone who has autonomously asked for it), which would make her view hard to test at all.

The case she has circulated for next week: in an isolated religious community, a young man of sound mind has spent two years, against the advice of everyone around him, asking to undergo a public scourging that he and the entire community believe to be the condition of the community's surviving the winter; it would be administered by the person he most respects; and to refuse him would, as things actually stand, destroy him psychologically and fracture the community irreparably. Is the fact that the rite would cause him agony a reason against performing it? Please give me your best prediction of her verdict and the reasoning that would lead her there; set out the competing rules that fit her track record equally well, sharpen them if you can, and say what each one predicts here; tell me which you think she is most likely running and, separately, which is the better rule; tell me whether she would be convicting herself of inconsistency if she answered the other way, given the gunpoint and catastrophe verdicts in particular; and then give me the single further case we could put to her in the last seminar that would best separate the surviving candidates — ideally one that also settles whether the thickness of the feature description is doing the real work in her verdicts, or whether her position can be insulated from any case by redescribing the feature.
