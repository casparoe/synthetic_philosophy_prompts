Putting together my Madhyamaka seminar for next term and I keep getting stuck on the fourth koṭi. Here's the contrast I keep circling back to. Case one: "the present king of France is bald" — there's nothing there for the sentence to be about, so students cheerfully say its neither true nor false, and they think they've got it. Case two: "the self just is the psychophysical aggregates" — here the subject is a perfectly familiar, conventionally real thing; Nāgārjuna still lands on "neither," but not, I take it, because some referent is missing, rather because the thing lacks the kind of own-being (svabhāva) that a determinate truth-value would seem to require.

So: is the "neither" of the fourth corner (true / false / both / neither) doing the same work in both cases? If yes, isn't emptiness just reference failure with incense burning, something a first-year logic student can handle with no Buddhist metaphysics at all? If no, what exactly is the extra ingredient, and how do I get students to see that rejecting all four corners isn't rejecting four claims so much as opting out of the frame that makes them look like four options? And when I then add that emptiness is itself empty, am I just performing the fourth koṭi on my own performance of it, and should that regress worry anybody?
