hey — I need a case built to order, and the important thing is that the two accounts actually come apart in it, rather than me just stipulating that they do.

What it's for: a friend keeps telling me that Yudkowsky's coherent extrapolated volition is just the standard idealized-preference story with extra clauses bolted on that never bind. You know the four: knowing more, thinking faster, being more the person we wish to be, and growing up farther together. On his reading the last two are decoration and CEV collapses into something like a Railton/Sobelish package — full relevant information, perfect instrumental rationality, desires made consistent and non-self-defeating, then respect whatever is left. I think that's wrong and I want one case that shows it, for a short blog post.

The two views to separate:

- the vanilla one: give the person all relevant information, let her reason perfectly and imagine outcomes as vividly as possible, iron out incoherence and self-defeat among her desires, hold what results fixed, and deliver that.
- CEV: the target isn't her informed current self but the limit of running her forward — more the person she wishes to be, converging, and (if you use it) the growing-up-farther-together clause.

The case I want: one ordinary person, nothing science-fictional — no uploads, no sims, no prediction tech, no therapy that doesn't exist — a situation a real adult could be in this year. The vanilla account says: don't push the change, give her what she informedly and coherently wants. CEV says: move her along the trajectory even though she presently declines it. And the disagreement has to be about her own will and her own good, not about what she owes anyone: no dependents, no victim, no kid in the story, because as soon as there's someone she's harming the answer goes moral instead of structural.

Constraints, and these are the ones I'll check:

1. Not information. Both views get the same full information. She has no factual error that matters, so knowing more can't be what does the work.
2. Not coherence. Her actual desires should come out of the info + rationality + consistency pass more or less unchanged. If the only reason the vanilla view gets it wrong is that her desires were self-defeating, the case is cheating.
3. Not pathology. No addiction, nothing clinical, nothing where we'd independently say she's broken — I want the leave-her-alone answer to be genuinely respectable, the kind of thing a reasonable person would defend.

The engine I have in mind (use it or beat it): path dependence. The person she'd become is reachable only by actually going through something — months of practice, an apprenticeship, a stretch of living somewhere, a discipline — and the person she is now, even with full information and with the endpoint imagined as vividly as you please, has a real and non-ignorant aversion to starting. From the far side she's glad it happened and would not undo it, and that gladness isn't sunk-cost coping or a shrug. So one view takes a fixed point of her present concerns and the other takes the limit of a path she won't volunteer for. Keep it to a single person unless you think CEV is irreducibly multi-agent — in which case say so and argue it, and then you may lean on the growing-up-farther-together clause.

What I want back:

- the scenario, under 250 words, in plain prose rather than bullet points, and concrete enough that I can picture this person's kitchen. If I can't, the case has failed.
- the check: what each account delivers and why, step by step, including a short argument that constraints 1 and 2 really are inactive in your case, which CEV clause is carrying the weight, and whether the extrapolation in your case actually converges or whether you had to add something to make it converge.
- the ablation: delete the path dependence — make the endpoint reachable by information, reasoning and vivid imagination alone — and show the two views collapse back into agreement. That's the proof the feature is doing the work.
- the counterattack: the strongest thing my friend says back. My guess is something like: the person she wishes to be is just one more of her preferences, my machinery runs over it too, so your extra clause is either redundant or it's you overriding her with your own idea of who she ought to be. Tell me how to build the case so that reply hurts, and where it still lands even so.

Thanks — and sorry if this is over-specified, I'd rather have something that survives him than something elegant.
