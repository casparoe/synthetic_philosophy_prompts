There's a podcast I listen to where three hosts rule on listener cases. The setup is always the same: your past self made some plan or promise, your present self wants out, who wins. From memory, the rulings have gone like this:

- 5.30am alarm for the gym, morning comes and you want to sleep: past self wins, and they were totally fine with tricks like automatically donating money if you dont go
- you book the nice place across town, halfway through the drive you'd rather eat at the chain restaurant you're passing: past self wins, keep driving
- you said two drinks / one more episode / one more level, and when the moment comes you want another: past self wins, set the limit first and hold it
- you trained as an accountant because it made sense at 19, at 31 you find out you're good at something else and like it far more: present self wins
- you signed up for something at 3am during a really bad patch: present self wins
- you promised your sister you'd go on a hike with her, you can't be bothered on the morning, and she's actually texted to say she's happy to go alone: past self wins anyway

My brother says the rule is obviously "whoever was thinking more clearly wins". I think it's more like "the plan stands unless something in the world actually changed or you weren't in your right mind when you made it". As far as I can tell both of those fit all six episodes. I want to know which one they're really running, and my case splits them:

Not drunk, not at 3am, I thought about it on and off for months and talked it over with people. Part of why I wanted it was that I wanted to be the sort of person who wanted that instead of always chasing the next thing. Since then nothing new has happened — no new facts, no diagnosis, no breakup, nothing. I've just slowly, over about a year, stopped wanting it. I've gone over it a lot, I'm not depressed, there's nothing shinier I'm chasing, and I still think it was a good plan when I made it. The move would be in three months. Right now, asked calmly, I want to stay in the city and keep doing what I'm doing.

Here's the bit I think actually matters and that I've never heard them talk about. This has happened to me twice before and the pattern is: in about five years I'll want the workshop again, five years after that I'll want the city again, and so on. I can feel it coming. Every switch costs real money and burns a few bridges. So on my brother's rule I'll just keep paying to swap forever and end up nowhere, which is that money-pump idea I read about somewhere, except the pump is my own thirties and forties.

So what I want isn't really "what should I do".
