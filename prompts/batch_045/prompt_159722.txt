Me and my brother have got ourselves into a proper arguement about what we're even saying when we say something is, say, 70% likely. It started over a weather forecast and it's got way out of hand. I looked some of it up online and saw words like "frequentist", "Bayesian" and "propensity" flying about, but I'm not sure I've got them straight, and honestly I'd rather just start from the cases where I feel I know what I think. Here are my verdicts, such as they are.

1. A coin flicked by a machine, same machine, thousands of times, nothing changes: I'm happy to say the probability of heads just *is* the share of flips that come up heads over the long run. Nothing more to it.

2. A single atom of the radioactive stuff, and I say there's a 50% chance it decays in the next hour. That atom gets one go and then it's gone. I do NOT think this one is about how much I know — I think the world itself could have gone either way, even though there's no long run of atoms-in-an-hour for this particular atom.

3. My washing machine, which is on its last legs and gets a bit worse every wash: "there's about a 30% chance it floods next time I use it." I want to say that's a real thing about the machine that goes up each wash, and not just about what I know, but there's no stable long run to measure it against, because every wash changes the setup.

4. Me and my brother found out the letter definitely went out, we just don't know which day. "There's about a 60% chance it went out on the Tuesday." I think that's a perfectly fine, meaningful thing to say, and that it's about how strong our evidence is — there's nothing chancy about it any more, it's settled.

5. My nephew's battery coin flipper. You press the button, it flicks a coin; if it comes up tails the machine stops, if it comes up heads it flicks once more and then stops. So the runs come out as T, HT or HH. I sat down and worked out that over lots and lots of runs, the share of all flips that are heads works out at exactly a half — but if you take the share-of-heads *within* each run and average those, you get three-eighths. My verdict: the chance of heads on any particular flick is a half, and the three-eighths thing is some kind of artefact and doesn't show the chance is 3/8. I can't properly say why though.

6. My sister's planning application: "about 70% it gets approved." One meeting, one decision, never to be repeated, and whoever decides it is a person, not a coin. But I've read the planner's report and I know how the committee has gone on similar ones, and I want to say there's a fact of the matter here — that somebody who said 20% would just be wrong, not merely differently calibrated.

7. "There's a 50% chance there was never any life in the rocks under Mars." Here I flip completely: I don't think there's a right number at all. My brother says 60%, I say 50%, and I don't think either of us is wrong, I think we're just saying how surprised we'd be.

8. The one I go back and forth on: my team's match on Saturday. Part of me says there's a real fact about how likely they are to win given the injuries and the form; part of me says it's just the odds I'd take.

The rule I've been toying with is: if there's some physical setup that could genuinely go either way, the number is out in the world; if the thing is settled already or isn't chancy, the number is about how much I know. But case 3 (chancy, but never repeats) and case 7 (settled, and I don't think there's a number) make me think it needs another clause or two, or maybe I've got the whole shape wrong.
