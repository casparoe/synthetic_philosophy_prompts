I teach a final-year seminar on Marx's social and economic theory and I am setting the in-class final. Students will have read only three things: the machinery and relative-surplus-value chapters of the first volume of *Capital*, the section of the 1857–58 notebooks usually called the *Fragment on Machines*, and the part of the third volume of *Capital* that argues for a tendency of the rate of profit to fall. They have no secondary literature, and the exam is closed-book. I need one essay question on Marx's view of technology and automation, together with a model answer and a marking scheme.

The question has to be unanswerable by summarising either text, and it must not let a student rest on the observation that Marx says two apparently different things. The crux I want forced is this: in the notebooks, the threat automation poses to capitalism is that the measure itself fails — as machinery and socially accumulated knowledge become the direct force of production, living labour time ceases to be the measure of the wealth produced, and a system organised around exchange value and the appropriation of living labour loses its basis. In the third volume, the threat is a squeeze internal to the value framework — the growing mass of constant capital relative to variable capital depresses the rate of profit even while exploitation continues. These are not the same diagnosis, and a good answer must say what each predicts about the fate of the wage relation, of exploitation, and of the political agent of transformation when direct production labour approaches zero.

So build the question around a case that separates them. Describe a fully automated production chain: machines are built by machines in plants that themselves employ no direct production workers; the only living labour is a small design and software-maintenance workforce, remote fault diagnosis, and the education and reproduction of that workforce; and because automation has spread into the machine-producing sectors themselves, the value of the fixed capital stock does not rise even as its technical mass and physical productivity do. Ask what each reading implies for value, surplus value and the rate of profit in that economy, which reading handles the cheapening of constant capital better, and whether the familiar reply — that the relevant unit is the collective labourer, so the total social labour does not fall but only migrates into research, education, logistics and care — rescues the labour theory of value or merely relocates the difficulty, and where it relocates it to. The question should also make students answer the deflationary move that the notebooks were only ever an unpublished rough draft and that the mature view is the one in *Capital*, rather than allowing them to ignore it. Finally, it should require a commitment: which diagnosis does more of the explanatory work, and what observable pattern in such an economy would tell against the one you pick?

Deliverables, please:

1. The essay question itself, self-contained in about 180 words, with the case stated precisely enough that students cannot supply their own version of it. It must call for an argument, not for quotation or citation.
2. A model answer of roughly 900 words, written so that a marker could see which claims are load-bearing.
3. A marking scheme of five must-hit moves with marks adding to 25, each followed by one line on how candidates typically get that move wrong. The scheme should name the single move whose presence separates an A from a B, and should state what makes an answer *wrong* rather than merely thin — in particular, what it would mean to conflate value with price or with material wealth, or to mistake the falling-rate argument for an empirical forecast about money profits.

Assume an answer length of about 1,000 words in 90 minutes, and keep the whole instrument pitched so that it tests whether students can work inside Marx's categories rather than whether they can name them.
