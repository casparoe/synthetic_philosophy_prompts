I am a high school student working on an independent project about Wittgenstein's later remarks on religious belief, and I keep running into the same objection: if a religious belief is not a hypothesis — if it predicts nothing, is not answerable to evidence, and cannot be shaken by it — then it seems to have no content at all, and calling it a belief is just politeness. What I need is not another general discussion but a case built to order, one that separates the two readings rather than stipulating which of them wins.

Please build me a case, and grade it against these two views:

Propositionalism: to believe is to assent to a proposition; what someone believes is fixed by the proposition assented to, together with their behavioral and dispositional profile; whether the assent is in good shape is a question about evidence.

Grammaticalism (the Wittgensteinian reading): a religious belief is partly constituted by the role it plays in a form of life — by what it lets you count as a reason, which analogies strike you as apt, what you are prepared to treat as settled and what as still open. Two people can sincerely assent to the same sentence and be doing quite different things with it, and those are different beliefs.

Specifications:

1. Two characters who both sincerely assent to one and the same religious sentence and both describe themselves as believers.
2. No difference in what they expect to happen: no afterlife, no miracles, no answered prayers, no hidden intervention. No possible observation, and no body of evidence, could tell them apart.
3. No difference in degree: not in how often they pray, attend services, give money, or how strongly or vividly they feel anything. The easy version of this case, where one person just takes the belief more seriously, is the one I already have and it does not work.
4. The difference must be purely grammatical, in the sense above — a difference in what would count as a consideration for or against what — and, so that the propositionalist cannot simply cash it out as a dispositional difference, it must never be actualized: the situation in which the two would diverge is one neither of them ever encounters, seeks out, or avoids, and neither would act differently if they did.
5. Ordinary realistic setting, one paragraph for the scenario, no exotic apparatus, and the grammaticalist verdict must fall out of the case rather than be announced.

After the scenario, give me the verdict each view returns and the reasoning behind it; identify the single feature of the case doing the work; say what becomes of the case if that feature is removed; and give the strongest thing a propositionalist could say to defuse it. If the specifications cannot be jointly satisfied, say so instead and tell me which one makes them unsatisfiable.

Length: the scenario in one paragraph of at most 120 words, and the whole response under 500 words.
