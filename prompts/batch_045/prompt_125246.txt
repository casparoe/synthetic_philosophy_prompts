I've been reading LessWrong-adjacent blogs since the Sequences were still being posted, and I run a small monthly meetup where, instead of arguing a topic, we try to build a map of it. Next month is the fine-tuning argument, and I want a decision tree / flowchart, not an essay. (I've watched this exact argument happen maybe a dozen times and it always goes the same way: someone brings up the cosmological constant being tuned to something like one part in 10^120, someone says "anthropic selection effect, obviously", someone says multiverse, someone says design, and it turns out the two people shouting at each other were stuck three branch points upstream of where they thought they were.)

What I want: a tree that takes someone from "yes, I accept that the constants look sensitive" down to a named leaf. Rough spec:

- Build it from the logical space, not from a list of names. Give me the diagnostic questions first, and the combinations of answers they allow, then locate the actual positions in that space, and tell me which cells are empty and why (incoherent? just unattractive? merely unexplored?). If a cell is occupied by one guy's pet theory, say so.
- Where it's natural, the questions should be phrased so someone can answer without knowing which leaf they're heading for. No node should just be "do you believe in God" in disguise.
- Required cut #1, near the top: is the datum itself in good standing? I mean the worry that "fine-tuning" is partly an artifact of varying one constant at a time while holding the rest of the laws fixed, of there being no well-defined measure over theory space to say what fraction of it is life-permitting, and of "life-permitting" possibly being the wrong target anyway. I want that to be a real branch and not a footnote.
- Required cut #2: does an observer-selection effect already account for the datum, so that no further explanation is owed? People at my meetup use SIA-flavoured and SSA-flavoured reasoning without noticing they're doing it, and the reference-class / "are we a random sample at all" question should be on the tree.
- Required cut #3, and the one I care most about: separate explanations that work by making our universe *typical* from ones that work by making it *aimed at*. Then within typical-making, split the same-laws-different-constants multiverse from genuine law-variation from a filtering-or-reproduction mechanism among universes from "everything possible exists". Within aimed-at, split an agent with intentions from an impersonal principle (value, or logical necessity of the constants).
- Required node somewhere on the typical-making side: if your explanation works by making us typical, why aren't we in the cheapest universe that supports one observer -- a small fluctuation, a single disembodied moment of experience -- rather than the big orderly one we're in? I want that used as a diagnostic because I think it's where a lot of the multiverse talk quietly gets expensive, and I want leaves to be marked by whether they have to swallow it.
- Also: flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely can't seperate, rather than inventing a question to force them apart. My guess is that "everything mathematically possible exists" and some versions of design are going to collapse into each other, and that the brute-fact-plus-selection leaf and the multiverse leaf are hard to tell apart by what they predict about anything we can see.
- Size: 8 to 12 branch questions, no more than 8 leaves. Each leaf gets a short code made of its answers, the name of the position if it has a standard one (call it "unnamed" otherwise), and one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

Audience is people who've read the Sequences but would roll their eyes at anything that sounds like philosophy of religion, and I want to print it as a one-pager, so keep each node to a sentence. If you think one of my required cuts is the wrong cut, say so and substitute your own -- just tell me what you changed and why.
