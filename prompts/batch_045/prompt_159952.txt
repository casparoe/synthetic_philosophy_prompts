I host a small interview series that grew out of a reading group most of us met through in the comments of the same rationalist blog — which will tell you the house style: steelman before you swing, treat unfalsifiable posturing as a vice, and don't let anyone get away with 'that's just a map-territory confusion' as if it settled something. Next month's guest is a philosopher of mind who defends a variant of eliminative materialism about belief and desire that I'll call non-substitutive eliminativism. Unlike the Churchland-style version, which says the attitudes get replaced by a better taxonomy in mature neuroscience, her claim is that there is nothing for the successor science to replace them with: the phenomenon folk psychology posited the attitudes to explain — an agent's being guided by how it takes the world to be, acting for reasons — is not a real phenomenon that a later theory will redescribe, and the success of belief-desire talk is a fact about a useful compression of a high-dimensional system, not about a structure the system actually contains. Two further commitments make her position distinctive and, I think, vulnerable. First, she wants to keep attitude-talk at what she calls the stance level while denying the ontology: belief ascriptions are a load-bearing fiction in our self-model, on a par with the compatibilist reconstruction of free will, and she thinks this is a stable resting point rather than a contradiction. Second, she bites what she calls the upstream bullet: if there are no beliefs, there is no inference, no evidence, no justification, and no knowledge in the ordinary normative senses, so she proposes to rebuild epistemology as a branch of control theory — calibrated, world-tracking dynamics instead of justified true belief — and she is explicitly a realist about conscious experience while eliminating only the propositional attitudes.

I want eight to ten questions, plus for each one a short note on the reply she is most likely to give and a follow-up keyed to that reply, so the sequence has some spine rather than being a list of independent objections. Please cover at least these pressure points, in whatever order serves the arc of the episode: the reflexive worry that the view is stated in a form that presupposes what it denies, and what her assertion that there are no beliefs is even doing if it has no truth-conditions; the predictive and explanatory track record of folk psychology in everyday life and in the human sciences, and whether a compression that successful can be a mere instrument when the compressions we trust elsewhere are backed by the microstructure; the awkward fact that working cognitive neuroscience already posits things that look a lot like attitudes — prediction and update quantities in Bayesian and predictive-coding models, expected-value and reward-prediction-error signals — and whether refusing to call those beliefs and desires is a substantive claim or a terminological one; the asymmetry she accepts, where experience stays and content goes, and what becomes of intentionality if there is nothing a conscious state is about; and her control-theory epistemology, specifically whether calibrated degrees of confidence have really been purged of everything that made beliefs suspicious, or whether calibration just sneaks the truth-norm back in, and whether a view with no room for inference can coherently be presented as supported by arguments.

Two more constraints. She has two stock definitional moves — 'that's stance-level talk, not ontology' and 'you're treating the model as if it were the thing modeled' — and I want at least two questions built so that using either move visibly costs her something, rather than dissolving the question. And I'd like the last question to be about what evidence could count against the view: she frames eliminativism as an empirical bet about what mature psychology will posit, so press on whether that bet has any real risk, given that she seems able to reinterpret any candidate successor state as merely mathematically defined and hence not content-bearing. No biographical or how-did-you-get-into-this questions, and please keep the tone of an interviewer who respects the position enough to attack its strongest form.
