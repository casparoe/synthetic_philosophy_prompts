i've been writing down my applied ethics professor's verdicts all term because they dont line up the way i expected, and i want to know what she'll say about a case i made up. her track record (all of it about whether the act is permissible, not about blame):

1. standard switch case — five on the main line, one on a dead-end spur, the spur stops the trolley by itself: permissible. she added "and dont tell me it's letting die, you kill him, and its still fine."
2. footbridge, the fat man, his body is what stops the trolley: impermissible.
3. loop variant — the spur rejoins the main line, so the one guy's body is *needed* to stop the trolley before it reaches the five: permissible. "yes, really yes, he has to be what stops it, i still say yes."
4. trapdoor variant — the guy is standing on a trapdoor over the spur, you pull a lever and he drops into the trolley's path, you never touch him: impermissible. "you're dropping him into it. that's a new thing you start, not a threat you redirect."
5. surgeon kills the healthy visitor for organs to save five: impermissible.
6. icu, the ventilator can only support five, a sixth patient arrives, you leave him off it and he dies of his condition: permissible. "here it really is letting die, and here i think it matters, even though i said it doesn't matter in the switch case."
7. same icu, except the sixth got onto the ventilator first and is stable, and disconnecting him is the only way to keep the five alive: impermissible. someone asked whether it'd be different if it were his own ventilator he'd brought with him, and she said no — whose machine it is changes nothing.
8. and in discussion: someone asked whether it matters that the person at the switch is the one whose carelessness started the trolley, and she said no. "it doesn't matter whose fault the threat is, only what your options are now."

new case: same as 1 — five on the main line, one on a dead-end spur, the spur stops the trolley on its own, so the one guy's body isn't doing any causal work, he'd just be run over. but he didn't wander onto the spur. someone who wants the five dead chained him there last night specifically so that whoever found the switch would be reluctant to divert onto a person who'd been put there as a shield. if you don't divert, the five die and he gets what he wanted; if you do divert, he gets one death instead of five. he's done this on two other lines before and both times the operator froze and didn't divert, so he'll keep doing it unless somebody calls it. the chained guy is a stranger to everyone, unconscious, no special ties, and the villain is unstoppable and uncatchable.
