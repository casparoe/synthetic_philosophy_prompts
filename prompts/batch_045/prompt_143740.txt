I am putting together a graduate seminar handout on the paradox of tragedy, and I want the available answers presented not as a list of named views but as a decision tree: four or five branch questions, each phrased so that a student could answer it before knowing which theory it leads to, and seven to nine leaves.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from the labels. The splits I want doing the work are: whether the audience's negative response is a full emotion involving the belief that someone is really suffering or only a quasi- or imagined response; whether the enjoyment is merely added to the distress or in some way depends on and is shaped by it; whether what is gained is hedonic or cognitive and moral; and whether the distress is only a price paid for something separate or is itself partly what is valued.

Two constraints, because the puzzle is not only about drama: the tree must also locate sad instrumental music, where there is no fiction, no character and no plot; and for each family it should say what is predicted about a viewer who already knows the ending and chooses to return, and about a documentary of real suffering that no one would want to be caught enjoying.

Give each leaf its recognized name where one exists — the Humean conversion account, quasi-emotion, compensation, catharsis under both its purgation and its clarification readings, meta-response, the learning or simulation option, the perverse-pleasure option, or simply unnamed — plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Then flag any pair of leaves your questions cannot separate, and any combination of answers the questions allow that no actual position occupies, noting briefly whether it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored.
