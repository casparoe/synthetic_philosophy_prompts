I'm in the final year of my philosophy degree and am currently taking a seminar on the philosophy of statistical mechanics; we have just come out of three weeks on Maxwell's demon and I am building a revision aid for our study group of six (all of us have had one term of thermal physics, none of us has had any information theory). I would like a decision tree, not an essay.

The tree should take a reader from a single starting commitment to a named position. The starting commitment is roughly this: a sealed vessel holds a gas at uniform temperature; a device inside it opens a shutter selectively, so that over many cycles one side ends up hot and the other cold; the device itself appears otherwise unchanged, and no work was put in. Everyone in my group agrees that the second law survives and that the appearance has to be explained, so please do not waste a branch on whether the second law holds or on whether the demon is a clever engineer. The interesting disagreement, for us, is about where the compensation lives and about whether there is a compensation to be found at all.

What I want you to do first is lay out the logical space rather than the list of names. Give me four to six branch questions, each a yes/no or a forced three-way choice about a concrete setup or claim, phrased so that a classmate who has never heard the labels can answer it from physical intuition alone. No node may contain a person's name, a paper, or an ism, and no node may simply ask whether the reader thinks information is physical. Show me the grid of answer combinations the questions generate before you place anything in it.

Then populate six to nine leaves. Each leaf should carry a short structural name of your own (erasure-ledger, correlation-ledger, reservoir-ledger, and so on), the conventional label in parentheses only if there clearly is one, and I do not care about precise attribution or dates my instructor is not testing us on who said what. Each leaf should also state in one line the exact inequality or conservation claim it rests on, and in one further line its most awkward commitment the thing it has to swallow that the others do not. Where a combination of answers is unoccupied, mark the cell and say briefly whether that is because the resulting view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Where two leaves cannot be separated by your questions, say so plainly rather than inventing a distinction.

Three further constraints on the content.

First, I do not want the branches to be the historical order with questions bolted on. If your tree ends up tracking the standard chapter order one-molecule engine, then the cost of seeing the molecules, then the cost of resetting the memory, then the algorithmic version tell me which branch is doing no real work and could be collapsed.

Second, these setups must each appear at least once as the material of a branch question, because they are the ones our group kept arguing about: the one-molecule box-and-partition engine; the purely mechanical one-way trapdoor that does no recording and no information processing at all; the demon that never erases and simply appends records until its memory is full, after which it is either replaced by a fresh demon or simply stops; the demon whose measurement is unreliable and misclassifies some fraction of the molecules, where the question is whether the extractable work degrades continuously with the reliability of the record or falls off a cliff; the demon that acquires its correlation through a reversible interaction and never resets, so that the record and the gas end up correlated and nothing anywhere is erased or dumped; and the demon that does no measuring of its own because it is handed a record someone else made, in a gas someone else prepared.

Third, one axis I want made explicit: for each leaf, say whether the entropy it is balancing the books in is the fine-grained entropy of the joint system or a coarse-grained thermodynamic entropy, and whether the leaf has to claim that the coarse-grained entropy of an isolated system never increases. Our tutor kept pushing on this and I could not tell which positions it separates.

Please render the tree as an indented outline with path codes, so that each leaf is reachable by a string like 1Y-2N-3b that I can check against a classmate's answers, and keep it to something we can work through in about ninety minutes. If any information-theoretic quantity appears in a node or a leaf, define it in one line for people who have never met it.

At the end, add a short confidence note. Tell me how confident you are that your branch questions are jointly exhaustive of the serious options, distinguish the places where the physics itself is genuinely unsettled from the places where you are simply unsure how to summarise it, and single out the one branch or leaf attribution you are least confident about and why.
