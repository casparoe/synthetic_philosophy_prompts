I work on developer tooling, and most of what I ship is external memory for other people's thinking: language-model agents with retrieval and scratchpads, IDEs that hold half a codebase's invariants in autocomplete, note systems people query instead of remembering. In design reviews we keep having the same argument in engineering vocabulary — is this artifact part of the cognitive system, or an instrument the agent consults? I want a specification, not a slogan.

Please write out what a theory of cognitive extension would have to deliver in order to be usable for that purpose: as a decision procedure for someone who has to say, of a given artifact embedded in a given practice, whether it is (or is becoming, or could be trained into being) part of the agent's cognitive machinery rather than a tool the agent uses. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable for my purpose or merely a preference of one camp. Where a requirement is really a prejudice of a research programme dressed up as a constraint, say so.

Here are the cases I want any candidate theory forced to adjudicate — add the ones I'm missing and discard any you think are bogus:

- A retrieval-augmented agent whose retrieved passages are causally upstream of every output, are not in its weights, are fetched by a mechanism the system itself invokes, and are intermittently wrong without the agent noticing. Contrast it with an agent that ingested the same corpus during training. Both are causally dependent on the same text; only one is intuitively "remembering."
- A senior engineer who cannot state the invariants of the system she maintains unless the IDE is open with autocomplete and inline docs. Her dependence looks structurally identical to the agent's, but no one calls her prosthetically augmented — they call her competent.
- The diarist who writes everything down and never rereads it: the externalization does its work at write time, with no read-back loop at all.
- Resources the agent modifies and whose modification does the work — a scratchpad, a sketch, a reordered pile — where nothing like storage-and-retrieval is happening.
- Resources the agent built itself versus ones designed by someone else: your own notebook versus your colleague's, your own retrieval corpus versus a vendor's.
- Dependence that fades with practice: the newcomer who must look up every API signature and the veteran who no longer does. Does the theory have to handle degrees and trajectories, or can it defend a binary verdict?
- The pair-programming and on-call-rotation cases, where the functionally relevant loop runs through other people. If the cut isn't at the skull and the skin, what stops it from running out to the whole organization, and is that stopping problem a defect of the theory or of my question?
- The gap between extending cognition and extending a mind: whether my design question even needs the phenomenal-consciousness half of the claim, and whether a theory may collapse that distinction or must respect it.

Beyond the cases, tell me which desiderata you think cannot be jointly satisfied — if some subset is unsatisfiable, that itself is the answer I most want — and which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidates. I'd also like a short scorecard, but score moves rather than authors: the parity-style move (treat an external process that plays the same functional role as an internal one as cognitive), the proprietary-marker move (cognition requires a kind of content or process artifacts cannot have), the integration-and-entrenchment move (availability, ease of access, trust, and a history of incorporation into practice), and the interventionist move (constitution as mutual manipulability). No names, dates, or citations — I want the moves, not the bibliography. A table is fine if it stays readable.

Two last things. First, I have no philosophy background: flag any term of art when you first use it, but don't slow down to explain the basics of the debate. Second, calibrate and push back: tell me which claims in your answer you hold confidently and which are contestable or speculative, and if the request as posed rests on a confused premise — if "part of the cognitive system" has no fact of the matter to be right about, or if my design question turns out to be a different question wearing this one's clothes — say that plainly instead of handing me a tidy list.
