I've been a Bayesian for something like fifteen years — long enough that the position has hardened into something I defend rather than examine. Stated as carefully as I can, it is credence-first monism about probability: degrees of belief are the primary notion; talk of objective chance is legitimate but derivative, a chance being roughly what an ideally informed agent's credence would settle on; and the reason long-run frequencies matter is that they are exceptionally good evidence about chances, not that they are what "probability" means. Probabilistic coherence plus conditioning on new evidence is, on this view, the whole normative story, and propensities, if they exist at all, do no work that chances-as-ideal-credences do not already do.

I want a map of what this position actually risks. Please skip or compress the objections I have already absorbed — the arbitrariness of priors, the problem of old evidence, the fact that ordinary people violate the axioms in laboratory ways — and give me the ones that should move me, ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Do not cite papers or name authors or point me at a literature; I want the arguments and their structure, not references.

Two cases I have been turning over should be treated as the sharp end of the question rather than as items on a checklist:

1. Non-monetizable frequency drift. Suppose a repeatable physical setup whose microdynamics is symmetric enough that any agent, however well informed about the trial-by-trial physics, would set credence one-half on each outcome — and suppose additionally that the laws of this world nomically guarantee that across the large but finite run of trials that actually occurs, the frequency is stably 0.7, with the divergence so arranged that no agent inside the world can tell which trials are which: any betting scheme keyed to the divergence does exactly as well as chance. Frequencies and well-informed credences then come apart permanently and unprofitably. What I cannot work out is whether this refutes my view or merely forces an unpalatable specification of it: if the ideally informed credence is allowed to take in the global, nomic fact about the run as a whole, then exchangeability pushes the per-trial credence toward 0.7 anyway, and my view seemingly collapses into frequentism in exactly the cases where the two were supposed to differ. Is that collapse a defeater, a benign special case, or a sign that "ideal information" was never a well-defined notion in my account? Note also that a propensity theorist sails through this case, which is part of why it bothers me.

2. Indexical underdetermination. In fission, duplication, or unbounded-cosmology setups, coherence plus conditioning plus all available evidence leaves the required credence underdetermined: the answer depends on a measure over observer-copies, and no possible evidence settles the choice of measure. My view says rationality is coherence plus updating; here coherence permits a continuum of answers, and the extra input is neither evidence nor a prior over hypotheses in any ordinary sense. Is the honest conclusion that subjectivism is incomplete rather than false — and if so, is that a third kind of probability input, one that is neither credence nor chance nor frequency, or just an unremarkable extra premise?

Please also address the two structural worries I take most seriously. First, the Dutch-book and accuracy-dominance arguments both presuppose that credences already track logical and mathematical entailment; no bounded agent satisfies that, so the normativity either fails to apply to any real agent or is satisfied only vacuously — and there are rival frameworks built precisely for agents facing unknown-composition urns, adversarial environments, and hypotheses they cannot assign sharp probabilities to. Second, the physics: quantum mechanics hands us numbers that look like irreducible single-case chances with values fixed independently of anyone's information state, and my view has to reinterpret them rather than take them at face value.

For each defeater, tell me whether a committed credence-first monist would themselves concede it as counting against the view, or would dismiss it as confused or as attacking a strawman; whether the evidence is decisive or merely awkward; and whether any of it should change how I actually forecast, as opposed to only changing what I say about the metaphysics of chance. Then give me an honest verdict on exposure: coherence-only normativity looks close to unfalsifiable by construction, since if chances are explicated as ideal credences, no observation could ever show them coming apart. Say plainly whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if it has, whether that is a genuine problem for a position of this kind or just the appropriate ambition level for a normative thesis. Finally, state what my view predicts that frequentism and propensity theories do not, so I can see what I would be giving up.

For what it is worth about my own priors: the physics argument currently feels most corrosive to me, and the indexical argument barely moves me at all. Tell me if I have that backwards.
