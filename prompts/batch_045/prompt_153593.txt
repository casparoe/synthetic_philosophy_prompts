I've been lurking here for years and I've finally got something I can't put a name to, so I'm asking rather than going off to search — partly because I don't want links or a reading list, I want the shape of the thing and its edges.

Two instances. I work through a derivation with the model's help, and afterwards I can explain it, field the follow-ups it pre-empted, and reject a garbled version of it. But a question that perturbs the setup in a direction neither of us considered — change a boundary condition, drop an assumption — leaves me with nothing at all. My confidence, meanwhile, was calibrated on the questions I could answer. Same pattern with arguments in a comment thread: I can reliably review what comes back and reject what is wrong, but I can't produce it, and my sense of how well I understand the subject has quietly come to track review-ability rather than generation-ability.

What makes this feel like more than ordinary forgetting or ordinary skill decay is a selection effect: the cases where the borrowed state fails are systematically cases the model would also have failed on, so the evidence my own experience hands me is drawn from the region where it holds up. My competence and my fragility have the same outline and I only ever sample the competence.

The nearest things in the vocabulary I've picked up around here — map and territory, cached thoughts, fake understanding — each catch part of it and none catches that; and the nearest academic-sounding ones I half-remember, the illusion of explanatory depth, transactive memory, and the thought that a notebook or calculator can be part of your cognitive system, all seem off in their own ways: the first is about missing mechanism and gets punctured by being asked to explain, the second is about knowing where to find something rather than thinking you already have it, and the third seems to say the state is genuinely mine and there was never a problem. What I'm describing is a state that is mine, that behaves like knowledge inside one channel and like nothing outside it, and whose calibration is corrupted in a direction I cannot see from inside.

Does it have a name — a failure mode, a distinction, an argument I'm groping at? A label alone won't satisfy me. I want to know what is supposed to be degrading here: knowledge, understanding, calibration, or something like robustness under counterfactual perturbation, and how one would even tell from the inside.
