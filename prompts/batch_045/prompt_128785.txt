In my philosophy of science seminar we have been arguing about whether a long-run prediction that fails inside its stated window can be "right but early" rather than simply false. The test case is Malthus's population theory in the Essay on the Principle of Population: population tends to press against subsistence, and positive and preventive checks pull living standards back toward it — yet English real wages rose substantially over the following century instead of reverting, because of productivity growth and cheap food from the Americas. My credence that Malthus was right-but-early rather than wrong is about 0.35; my tutor puts it near 0.7, and I want to know where the disagreement lives.

Please give me your own number, decomposed. I have been using a rough test of my own: a failed prediction is right-but-early only if the corrected date is derived from the theory itself rather than fitted to the failure — call it the retiming test. Decompose into at least: (1) whether the Malthusian mechanism was in fact operating in England after publication, even if swamped; (2) whether the escape is better described as an exogenous shock the theory did not anticipate or as a refutation of the mechanism; (3) how far the apparent later confirmations — pre-industrial stagnation, the modern demographic transition — are genuine instances of the mechanism rather than post hoc selections; and (4) whether "right but early" passes the retiming test here at all, given that its mirror image, the hypothesis that population pressure itself stimulates the innovation that raises subsistence, can be called right-but-early on much the same evidence. Add any further term you think belongs, give a number and one line of support for each, say how they combine into your headline figure, and tell me which term you are least confident about and which single piece of evidence — a long real-wage series, a natural experiment, a counterfactual with no coal or no Americas — would move you most. Finally, separate what historians and philosophers of science actually agree on here from what is still contested among them, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus.
