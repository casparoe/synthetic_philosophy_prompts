I'm putting together a closing reading for a reading group that has spent a few weeks on whether 'better than' is transitive — the quality-versus-duration spectrum (the kind that walks you from a short, blazing life to a very long mild one, every step looking like an improvement) and the intransitivity reply to it. I've read the usual takes on the Repugnant Conclusion and I don't want another one; I want to end with a pantoum instead.

Six quatrains, and the poem should *be* the spectrum: lines 2 and 4 of each stanza reappear as lines 1 and 3 of the next, and the last line of the poem is its first line again. Each stanza stages one adjacent comparison among six lives, A through F — A being roughly thirty years of extreme intensity and achievement, F being something like a millennium of contentment barely above neutral — where every step trades a small drop in peak intensity for a large gain in duration, so that each pairwise verdict is locally compelling. The two lines a stanza hands on to the next should be the dimension its neighbours share, the aspect that makes *this* comparison look easy. My one addition to the standard setup: somewhere around the midpoint the dimension doing the work reverses — the early steps are won on duration, the later ones on intensity — so that when the closing line returns the opening line, the chain has closed, and we have improved our way back to where we started. Make the reversal legible in the verse, and let the return land as a result rather than a wink; I don't want the poem to collapse into the thought that 'better than' is just vague, which is the dodge I'd rather not hear. No technical vocabulary inside the poem — it should read as a poem to someone who hasn't done the reading.

Then, in prose afterwards, commit yourself: name the one link in the chain you would actually deny and say why, and add a short note on where the verse takes liberties — which contested reading of the spectrum or of pairwise comparison it leans on. I'd rather have one defended choice than a survey of the options.
