I have been defending a particular reading of Marx on exploitation in a reading group, and it has now survived every objection I know how to raise against it — which makes me suspicious of it rather than confident in it. I would like a map of its exposure: what would have to be true, or what would have to be shown, for this reading to be mistaken, and how badly mistaken.

The reading has three parts.

First, exploitation is a structural property of a relation of production, not a verdict on a transaction. A wage-labourer is exploited because their surplus labour is appropriated by whoever controls the means of production, and this holds even when the wage is the full value of labour-power, even when no one is coerced or deceived in any ordinary sense, and even when the arrangement leaves the worker better off than any alternative actually available to them. It follows that the charge is not answered by showing that the exchange was voluntary or mutually advantageous; on this reading, those facts are beside the point.

Second, and this is the part I take to be load-bearing: what does the normative work is not the transfer of surplus as such but what that transfer reveals — namely that the worker's productive activity is organised by, and directed towards, someone else's accumulation. Exploitation and alienation are, on this reading, the same fact seen from two sides, one from the side of the product and one from the side of the activity, and the appropriation of surplus matters chiefly because it is the measurable trace of a relation of command over another person's life-activity.

Third, so understood, the exploitation of the wage-labourer would not be abolished by raising wages, by share-ownership that leaves effective control with a distinct managerial stratum, or by a tax-and-transfer system that returns to workers more than the value of what they produce.

What I want is the case against this, sorted by how much damage it does. Please distinguish defeaters that would be fatal from ones that would merely be awkward, and — this matters to me — say which of them a thoughtful defender of the reading should concede as counting against it, and which they would be entitled to dismiss as merely assuming a rival framework.

Some cases I have been turning over, and which I would like treated rather than bypassed:

A limit case. Imagine an economy in which production is almost entirely automated and the aggregate surplus labour performed by human beings approaches zero, because the mass of capital is vast relative to the remaining human labour-time. If the machinery of the first part is taken literally, exploitation is vanishing here. But nothing intuitively important about command over life-activity has vanished: the remaining workers are, if anything, more thoroughly subordinate to decisions made elsewhere. Does this show that the second part is doing all the real work and the surplus-labour apparatus is an inessential historical scaffolding — or does the reading have a principled reply?

A control case. Consider a federation of worker co-operatives that formally owns its capital but delegates all investment and siting decisions to a professional planning board it cannot realistically overrule, and contrast it with a conventional firm whose workers exercise substantial informal control through a strong workplace organisation. Which way should the reading go, and can it distinguish formal from effective control without helping itself to the very notion of command it was supposed to be analysing? I worry about circularity here.

A measurement case. Suppose we had a working surplus-labour accounting, using input-output data and some convention for imputing the productivity of heterogeneous capital and of skills acquired through publicly funded education, and suppose it turned out that for the median worker in a contemporary service economy the imputed surplus is negative: the worker receives more than the value attributed to their labour. Would the reading have to say that such workers are exploiters? And if its defenders reply that any imputation convention already presupposes a distribution and is therefore ideological rather than neutral, I want to know whether that is a legitimate methodological point about the limits of the accounting or simply the move by which the first part is protected from ever being tested.

A motivational case. The second part seems to presuppose that it is bad for a person to have their productive activity directed by another. Suppose we encounter workers who genuinely do not want self-direction, who are content to be told what to do and would find wider control burdensome. Is the reading then committed to a perfectionist claim about human flourishing that it has not argued for, and if so how damaging is that?

Finally, two structural worries I cannot settle myself. The first is insulation: has the first part been stated in such a way that nothing could count against it, so that it becomes a stipulation about how the word is to be used? Please say whether that is in fact fatal here — my own view is that it would be fatal if the reading were offering a moral verdict, but perhaps less so if it is offering the descriptive core of an account of accumulation and class conflict, and I would like that distinction examined rather than assumed. The second is collapse: what would this reading lead one to expect — about the behaviour of co-operatives, about the trajectory of profits under automation, about why workers do or do not resist — that a plain distributive-justice view, or a view organised around non-domination, would not equally predict? If the honest answer is nothing, I take that to be a serious problem, and I would like to be told if it is.

I should say what would move me most. I am unmoved by the objection that the labour theory of value is false as a theory of prices; I think the reading can concede that. What would move me is a case in which the reading's verdicts come apart from anything we have independent reason to think bad about the arrangement, and an argument that the second part makes the first redundant.
