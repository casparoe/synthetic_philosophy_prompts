Context, which is dumb but is what started it: we're rebuilding our incident tooling, and someone asked whether a service that's been running for six years, been rewritten twice, and whose owners, on-call rotation and runtime have completely turned over is still the same service when we attribute repeat incidents and SLO burn to it. My coworker says yes, obviously, and explains it like this: persistence through time is exactly like extension through space — an object is spread out in time the way a road is spread out across a field, what we call 'the object right now' is just one temporal slice like one mile of the road, and the real object is the whole four-dimensional thing, the entire history taken at once. On his picture there's no puzzle about how one and the same thing can be running on three nodes in one year and five nodes in another, any more than there's a puzzle about a road being straight in one stretch and curved in another: different parts, different properties, done. He's using this to license two conclusions: that our talk of 'the same service' is a convenient projection onto a slice rather than a fact about a persisting thing, and that the classic identity puzzles just dissolve instead of needing an answer — the ship whose planks get replaced one at a time, or a statue and the lump of clay it's made of, which look like two different things sitting in exactly the same place at the same time. He also says the engineering version is obvious to anyone who's built an event-sourced system: the aggregate is the append-only log, current state is a fold over it, nothing really changes, there are just later events.

What I want is an audit of the analogy, not a lecture on the metaphysics, and I have specific things I want worked through. First, the mapping: what plays the role of the road, the field, the mile marker, the bend, and of me standing on the road looking at it. Second, where it breaks: spatial parts are all there at once and I can point at any of them, whereas the later slice of my service isn't something I can point at yet — so is the analogy quietly helping itself to the claim that other times are just as real as the present, which is half of what's actually in dispute? And doesn't the log itself grow, with its length being a property it has at one time and not another, so that the event-sourcing version just pushes persistence up a level rather than explaining it — and if so, where does that stop? Third, I made up an alternative and he says it's his view with extra steps; I want to know if he's right. My idea was that the object is like the head of an immutable linked list, or the tip of a git branch: there's a current stage that is the object, and when we say what it was or will be, those claims are really about earlier or later versions of it rather than about the very same thing. He says that's either his worm-thing in disguise or incoherent; I say it at least doesn't force me to say that next year's outage already exists somewhere. Is that a genuinely different position, or does it inherit the objections to both pictures? Fourth, tell me which disanalogies actually matter for the two conclusions he wants and which are noise, and whether either conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table — including whether those puzzle cases really dissolve or whether they just get relocated into questions about which slices count as stages of the same object.
