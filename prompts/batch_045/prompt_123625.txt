hey — I'm the student rep on this committee our town put together about the statue by the courthouse and whether the high school should change its name, and I had to write down what I actually think so people could argue with it. Here it is:

1. you can't really blame someone for believing stuff that basically everyone around them believed — blame needs that they could have known better.
2. not blaming someone isn't the same as honoring them. you can decline to celebrate a person without calling them evil.
3. what matters about a statue is what it says now, not what it meant when it went up. a statue in a public place is the town vouching for the guy, today.
4. so contextualizing is almost always better than removal — keep it up, put the bad stuff on a plaque next to it.
5. names are different from statues. a school or street name is just a label, it doesn't really honor anyone, so renaming things is kind of a waste of time.
6. the museum is the right answer for people who were genuinely awful: out of the square, still around to learn from.
7. one more thing I basically made up and kind of like: honor can split. a monument to the men who served only honors each of them a little, so a bad guy or two inside a big group shouldn't sink the whole thing, while a statue of one man has to carry all of it.

people keep telling me different pairs of these clash and I can't tell which ones actually do. also, for what its worth: I think the statue should come down but I'm fine leaving his name on the library he paid for, idk if that's allowed by my own list.

go through the seven and tell me which ones really pull against each other and which only look like they do, ranked worst first, and for each one say what I'd have to give up and what that costs me. don't just tell me everything is in tension with everything.
