ok so my undergraduate seminar on consciousness has its last session next week and instead of a summary handout i want to close with a poem. weve spent the last few weeks on higher-order thought theory — the view that a mental state is conscious only when a further thought takes that state as its object — and on the two objections that i think actually bite. first the regress worry: if the higher-order thought has to be conscious in order to do its work, then it needs a third-order thought, and so on up. the standard move is to let the higher-order thought be unconscious, which blocks the regress but invites the question of how a thought you are not aware of can make anything feel like anything. second the rock problem: a thought can attribute experience to something that has no experience whatsoever, and the theory seems short of resources for saying why it isnt doing that all the time. we also read the same-order or self-representational alternative, where the higher-order bit is a proper part of the very state it is about, so there is no regress and no question of the two ever having to find each other.

what i want is a sestina — six stanzas of six lines plus a three-line envoi, so 39 lines — with these as the rotating end-words in the usual pattern: ache, thought, unconscious, itself, after, stone. swap one if it really fights you, but keep all six slots, and keep a word for the rock case and a word for the self-representational reply. dont worry that stone shows up once in every stanza; the objection turning up where the theory doesnt want it is sort of the point.

the structural bit that matters most: the two words ache and thought must never appear in the same line anywhere in the six stanzas. that distance is the no-contact problem — nothing in the theory explains why this thought lands on that state rather than on some other one, or on nothing. then let them finally meet in the envoi, and let that meeting be genuinely ambiguous: either it is the same-order answer, the state being aware of itself as part of what it is, or it is the collapse, the worry that once you let the higher-order state be unconscious, non-inferential and a proper part of the first-order one you have quietly become the rival view. dont tell the reader which. leave it open, because that is honestly where the seminar ended up.

there is one more wrinkle i keep getting stuck on and id like the poem to reach, using the after word. call it the delayed thought: someone walks home with a tooth aching the whole way, and the thought that she is in pain only arrives once she is through the door and the ache has stopped. so did the ache become conscious retrospectively, when the thought finally came — or was it conscious the whole walk and merely unattended, which would mean attention is doing work the theory said it wasnt? either answer costs something. have the poem arrive at that moment without adjudicating it, and without turning it into a little moral about paying attention to your life.

it should be right enough that someone who knows this literature finds it accurate rather than name-droppy — no proper names, no citations, and dont stick technical phrases like transitive consciousness into a line — and it should still just be a decent poem for the students who are only half following. and please no formatting at all: no heading, no bold, no bullets, nothing before it like here is your sestina and no explanation or notes after it. just the lines and the stanza breaks, im pasting it straight onto the handout.
