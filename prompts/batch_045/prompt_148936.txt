I spent a career in the physical sciences and have only recently begun reading philosophy of law, so I should say at the outset that I can find the literature myself — I want substance, not reading suggestions.

The view I find myself holding is this: paradoxes of legal self-reference are artifacts of careless drafting rather than genuine formal pathologies. A well-designed order can always avoid them, and in consequence a legal system can never actually get stuck — some residual rule, hierarchy of norms, or official practice will always break the circle. To make the question concrete, suppose a constitution contains what I will call a mirror clause: an amendment is valid only if the procedure used to adopt it would itself have been a valid procedure under the constitution as it stands once the amendment has taken effect. My view predicts that such a clause is either trivially satisfiable — whatever is done becomes valid, since after the fact the constitution says so — or is silently repaired in application, but never genuinely paralyzes the order.

What would count against this? I would like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I would like you to separate the two halves of my position and tell me which is more exposed. Please include, and assess honestly, the analogy I keep reaching for from mathematical logic: that any system able to encode claims about its own rules admits fixed-point constructions that break it — undefinability, incompleteness, and the paradoxes that turn on self-referential conditionals. Does that carry over to a system of norms, or is it a category mistake on my part? Say which defeaters a defender of my view should concede as genuine and which she may dismiss as changing the subject. Finally, give me an honest verdict on the view's exposure: if courts never in fact stall on such a clause, is that evidence for me, or only evidence that courts decline to notice? Does my view make any prediction its rivals do not, does that prediction have teeth, and has it arguably already failed? Please answer in American English.
