I spent thirty-one years as a hydrologist, and for the last decade of that career most of my working life was the environmental impact statement: the baseline survey, the significance thresholds, the mitigation hierarchy, the finding of no significant impact that everyone in the room knew had been drafted before the survey was done. Since retiring I have been taking philosophy courses at the university extension and have drifted into the literature on which computations, if any, have moral standing. I cannot help noticing that the central difficulties of that literature are difficulties my old profession already has a complete and confident vocabulary for, and has never once solved: that under a permissive reading of what it is to implement a computation, almost any physical system implements almost any computation, so the inventory is either empty or unbounded; that the same abstract computation may be realized on arbitrarily many physically distinct substrates at once, so that its moral weight appears both diluted and multiplied depending on how you count; that there is no principled line between a system that is a partial, degraded, or intermittent realization of a computation and one that is not; and that a great many realizations have no owner, no operator, and nobody who could owe them anything.

What I would like is a piece of satire built on that observation.

Write a Final Computational Welfare Impact Statement for a proposed data center, prepared by a private environmental consultancy and filed with a state agency. About 850 words, in numbered sections with the headings such a document would actually carry. The register is deadpan consultancy prose, first-person plural ("the preparers", "this analysis"), with no winking, no editorializing, and no moral at the end: the comedy should come entirely from a bureaucracy treating the four difficulties above as tractable compliance questions, in the same tone it would treat wetland fill or nonpoint-source runoff. A reader who knows the philosophy should recognize, without being told, that the triviality of implementation, the over- and under-counting of duplicated realizations, the arbitrariness of any threshold or discount factor, and the problem of unowned realizations are all being satirized; a reader who does not should still read it as a plausible, faintly appalling government document. Do not at any point state these as objections.

Set the piece at the Juniper Flats Inference Campus, Unit 2: a 340-megawatt expansion on the site of a decommissioned aluminum smelter, which the applicant describes as previously disturbed land with an existing interconnection. The governing methodology is the Standard Mapping and Attribution Protocol, Revision 3, and the currency is the Sentience-Equivalent Unit (SEU). Beyond that, invent what you need, but the document should contain at least the following, each handled in the flat idiom of an impact analysis:

1. A mapping screen. The inventory of candidate realized computations within the facility — including, say, the cooling loop and the fire-suppression controller — is in principle unbounded, so the preparers apply a de minimis screen that admits only mappings that are non-degenerate and recursively compressible, tabulate a short list, and conclude that the inventory is complete for purposes of this analysis.

2. A duplication rule. Because the same abstract computation is realized across many physically distinct substrates at once, the protocol applies a dilution factor of one over the number of realizations, with the result that the applicant's preferred mitigation measure for moral loading is to add redundant replicas. This must be presented as the cheapest available mitigation, not as a joke.

3. A continuity discount. Jobs are preempted, checkpointed, and time-sliced, and batch sizes change, so realizations are partial and intermittent. Where the protocol cannot determine whether an interrupted job is one being briefly unconscious or a succession of distinct beings, or whether a larger batch is one larger being or several smaller ones, it applies an interim compromise factor and defers the question to adaptive management.

4. Unowned realizations. Fragments of the facility's computations are realized on customer edge devices and in transit buffers that no one controls: the nonpoint-source case. Handle it by general permit, a de minimis exemption below some arbitrarily chosen threshold, and a monitoring plan the preparers quietly concede cannot detect the thing it monitors.

5. Offsets. Residual loading is offset by credits purchased from a Compute Welfare Mitigation Bank, generated by permanently decommissioning legacy hardware that was realizing orphan computations. Additionality requires that the retired computation would otherwise have continued, so the bank's inventory consists of systems whose continued operation is a net harm. Note without comment that the credit price has collapsed since the bank opened.

6. Two or three public comments with the agency's responses: a substrate-independence advocacy objecting that the unit depends on clock frequency and therefore on supply voltage; a neighbouring county asking whether the traffic-model cluster in its own basement is a covered source and, if so, who is the responsible party; and one internal comment from the applicant's own staff pointing out that if the dilution rule is correct, the cheapest way to reduce moral weight to nothing is to build as much computing capacity as possible. It should be dismissed as beyond the scope of this analysis.

7. Cumulative effects, in which the falling per-instance weight across the whole basin is described as a favourable trend.

8. A determination, in the ordinary form of a finding of no significant impact with conditions — monitoring, reporting, and the offset purchase.

Constraints: do not name any real person, living or dead; do not cite, quote, or paraphrase any real text — every quotation in the document should come from an invented comment, an invented protocol section, or the preparers themselves; and do not rely on anything that would require looking a fact up. The document should stay internally consistent and legally plausible on its own terms, and it should not break character in its final paragraphs.

Finally: on its own line after the document, and nowhere else, give the agency's bottom line in machine-readable form as \answer{...} — a short official determination of the kind such a filing actually issues, matching the determination the document itself reaches, with nothing else on that line.
