You are a philosopher of science who works on realism and theory change. Think the diagnosis through carefully before answering, but give me only the finished five items.

The scientific realism debate has been deadlocked for decades, and I have stopped believing another round of examples will move it. The pessimistic meta-induction runs on the familiar roster of once-empirically-successful theories whose central posits we now reject — crystalline spheres, humoral medicine, phlogiston, caloric, the luminiferous ether — and concludes that our best current theories are probably false in the same way. Realists reply that the roster is cherry-picked, that what made those theories work was retained in their successors, usually as a limiting case, and that the miraculousness of novel predictive success still needs explaining. Both replies are on the table, everyone knows them, and nobody converts.

My hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the impasse is really two unmarked questions plus one unmeasured quantity. The induction needs a base rate over a properly specified reference class of theories that were successful for a sustained period, and no one has fixed that class. The retained-as-limiting-case reply quietly changes the question from whether a posit survived to whether something recognizably descended from it survived in some attenuated form, and no one has fixed a standard of survival either. Supporting this: I tried a crude audit of my own, coding posits from a couple of dozen abandoned programs as dynamical, where the posit was doing quantitative work inside the equations, versus classificatory, where it was mainly sorting stuff into kinds. Roughly two-thirds of the dynamical posits reappeared in the successor theory as a limiting case or an effective description; the classificatory ones almost never did. If that pattern is real, the dispute has been hiding an empirical question about base rates and a verbal question about survival under one heading.

Diagnose the standoff: is it verbal, evidential, a clash of bedrock intuitions about what demands an explanation, or several questions sharing a name? Test my hypothesis against what each side actually argues, including the possibility that my audit is an artifact of how I coded it. Then say what would count as progress — a fixed reference class, a coding standard, a formal result, a change of question — or argue that no progress should be expected and why.

Take a definite position and give your reasons instead of surveying the options, and mark where you are guessing.

Answer in exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, with no introduction and no conclusion: (1) what kind of disagreement this is, stated flatly; (2) the strongest evidence for that verdict from what each side in fact says; (3) whether my coding distinction and reference-class hypothesis survive scrutiny; (4) what would concretely count as progress; (5) where your own diagnosis is weakest or most speculative.
