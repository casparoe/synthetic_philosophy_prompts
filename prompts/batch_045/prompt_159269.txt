So I've been reading about this thing people online call superrationality and arguing about it in comments sections, and I think I've backed myself into a corner I can't see out of. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) In a one-shot prisoner's dilemma against someone I have good reason to think reasons more or less exactly the way I do — no communication, no repeat rounds, no reputation effects — I cooperate.

(2) Against a stranger I have no reason to think reasons like me, I defect. The ordinary game-theory answer is basically right there, and the superrational reasoning just doesn't get a grip without some shared-reasoning link.

(3) The way I justify (1) is purely evidential: my cooperating is evidence that my twin cooperates, in the same way one-boxing in Newcomb's problem is right. Nothing spooky, no causation running backwards.

(4) But I won't pay in the counterfactual mugging. The coin has already landed, nothing I do now makes anybody better off, and handing over the money is just throwing it away.

(5) I do think the reasoning changes the case for voting though. I vote, and not because my vote might swing the election, but because my voting is evidence about whether people like me vote.

I cant tell which of these is load-bearing. Is (4) actually the odd one out, or does it follow fine once you accept (3)? Does my (2) quietly pull the rug out from under my (1)? Does (5) commit me to something (2) rules out, or is the size of the group doing real work there?

Please be specific about which commitment has to go and what giving it up would cost me — and don't just tell me all five are in tension with each other, I can already do that myself. If a pair only looks like it clashes, say so and say why.
