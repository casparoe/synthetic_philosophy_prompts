hi — I'm taking a comparative constitutional theory seminar this term (joint-listed between politics and philosophy) and we've spent the last few weeks on the arrangement where a hereditary head of state genuinely holds legal powers — assent, appointment, dissolution — that convention forbids them from ever actually using, and on why that setup isn't just a silly leftover but arguably does real work. For the final we're allowed to hand in one creative piece instead of an essay, and I've decided I want a parody document, but I'm not a funny writer, so here's the ask: write me an internal HR packet — think "Role Profile & Annual Performance Review, Office of the Sovereign" — for the position of hereditary head of state in a modern parliamentary democracy. Invent the country and the job family; don't name or describe any real monarch and don't make any real politician a character. Aim for 700–900 words, formatted like a genuine corporate HR artifact: job title and band, key responsibilities, KPIs with targets and stretch targets, a competency rating scale with written justification, a risk register, "areas for development", and a slightly passive-aggressive comment from the Private Secretary as line manager.

The joke has to come from the actual constitutional weirdness rather than from added silliness — no secret-tyrant stuff, no conspiracies. The bits I'd love to see: (1) KPIs where every single measure of success is a non-event — reserve powers exercised: 0, target 0, stretch target 0 — and where one actual exercise is logged as a category-1 incident that triggers an abolition review, so that doing the job perfectly is indistinguishable from abolishing it; (2) a "Neutrality" competency that is impossible to satisfy, because the incumbent is supposed to have frank private views (there's a whole duty to advise and warn in confidence) but any leak of a view is an instant fail, while "no evidence of any views held" also scores badly; (3) a pay-band justification arguing the office is worth an enormous amount of money precisely because its output is unmeasurable, with any attempt to produce measurable outputs (influence, cohesion, tourism) scored as underperformance; (4) a harmonization section where HR is trying to merge three member-states into one job family — the one with uncodified reserve powers held only under convention, the northern one whose 1970s rewrite formally stripped the holder of political power but kept the ceremonial role, and the east Asian one whose post-war constitution defines the holder as the symbol of the state with no powers of government — and the document keeps discovering that the same "power you must never use" puzzle just reappears one level down in each variant. Keep it deadpan: never use the word paradox, never wink at the reader, but a first-year politics student should still be able to tell what's being made fun of, and someone who's actually done the reading should think the satire is fair rather than a strawman.

One more thing, which my flatmate (who does UK constitutional law) talked me into. Our tutor floated a deliberately made-up proposal from an invented think tank: since a legal power that is never exercised might in principle lapse through sheer disuse, the Sovereign should perform an annual "continuity drill" — formally signing an instrument refusing assent to a bill that the government has pre-declared void, so that everyone can verify the power is still there. Please build the packet around this, and let HR tie itself in knots over it: the drill either proves nothing (everyone knows it's a drill) or is indistinguishable from the real thing and therefore ends the arrangement it was meant to certify, and the document should respond to that by adding more procedure — a drill governance framework, a drill impact assessment, maybe a drill for the drill. My tutor's one real grading criterion is that the piece should make the constrained-monarchy setup feel more rationally uncomfortable than the readings did, so please resist the urge to resolve it at the end: end on "areas for development" with something like a note that the incumbent needs to work on demonstrating authority without ever demonstrating it. Recieve nothing less then your best deadpan.
