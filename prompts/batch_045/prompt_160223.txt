Suppose a patient with a complete forebrain commissurotomy performed years earlier for intractable epilepsy is run through a protocol I will call divided betting. On each trial the left half-field (right hemisphere) is shown a rapid stream of draws from an urn that is 80% red, and the right half-field (left hemisphere) an independent stream from an urn that is 30% red: too many draws to count, no draw ever crossing the vertical meridian. The patient is then asked aloud for a single fair price, in cents, on a ticket paying one dollar if the next draw from a randomly selected one of the two urns is red. Only the left hemisphere can answer. Suppose that with repeated sessions the spoken price settles reliably near 55 rather than near the 30 that the answering hemisphere's own evidence supports, that it drifts somewhat within a session, that the patient supplies confident justifications citing evidence neither hemisphere was shown, and that on control trials where the two half-fields display the same stream the patient performs no better than one hemisphere alone would.

Give me your credence, as a number, in this proposition: at the moment the price is spoken, that patient has two numerically distinct simultaneous conscious streams, one per hemisphere, rather than a single stream whose contents are partly inaccessible to verbal report. Decompose it. Assign rough probabilities to at least these: that the spoken price reflects a genuine integration of both hemispheres' evidence rather than one hemisphere's judgment shaped by information that enters its output without entering its experience; that a hemisphere lacking language can be the substrate of a conscious stream at all; that "number of streams" is a determinate quantity rather than a way of speaking about degrees of integration or of global access; and that the same question is well posed for this patient before the surgery. Say approximately how the terms combine into the final number, and mark the one your number is least robust to. Mine sits near 0.35 and a colleague's near 0.8, and I would like your view on which sub-question that gap is really about.

Last: tell me what would settle it, given that I do not think the patient's own report can, since any report is itself the product of one hemisphere. Specify a result — from divided betting or from any study you can describe concretely — that would move your credence by more than 0.2, and say which way.
