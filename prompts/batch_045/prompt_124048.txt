I never went to university. I have worked in warehouse logistics for about fifteen years and taught myself Python on evenings and weekends, mostly by breaking things. Two of my hobbies recently ran into each other, and I would like help with where they meet.

The first hobby: for a couple of years I have been training small reinforcement-learning agents on a second-hand desktop — gridworlds, a racing game, a couple of trading simulators. I can write my own training loop, read a loss curve, and generally tell a bug from a badly specified reward. The second: I got interested in why I keep failing at my own plans. Every January I enrol in a Spanish course, and by March I have stopped going. The person who stops does not feel like a stranger to the person who enrolled; he simply does not care about the same thing at six in the morning that the January version cared about in the evening. Reading on my own about this led me to the literature on weakness of will and to present-biased, or hyperbolic, discounting. My sense is that my two hobbies are describing the same shape of thing in different words, and my suspicion is that one of them is seeing something the other cannot.

I already have a rough grip on the philosophical side, so please do not explain it to me from scratch, and please do not point me at papers or readings: I have no library access, and I would only be guessing at what they say.

What I want is a translation. The vocabulary I want it in is the one I actually use: agent, environment, state, observation, policy, reward, return, discount factor, value function, Bellman backup, on-policy versus off-policy, planning versus acting, model-based replanning at every step, exploration schedule, reward shaping, options and hierarchical policies, training distribution versus deployment distribution, model capacity and optimisation pressure.

Please restate, as faithfully as you can:

1. The phenomenon to be explained: preferences reversing as an outcome gets closer; acting against one's own better judgement; and whatever distinguishes both of those from simply changing one's mind.

2. Three families of answer, taken one at a time. First, the time-inconsistency family: a single non-exponential discount function, or a succession of short-lived selves with different discount functions competing or bargaining, so that what looks like weakness is really the earlier self losing an election. Second, the no-real-conflict family: nothing about time is doing the work; the apparent reversal is about proximity, salience, or cue-triggered desire, or about the near option being available now, so there is no inconsistency to explain away and no special faculty of willpower to posit. Third, the normative-sceptical family: even granting the reversal, there may be no fact of the matter about which temporal perspective is the agent's authoritative one, so that talk of what I really want is less a discovery than a decision.

Then three things from my own tinkering that I want the translation to answer for rather than smooth over:

(a) In my code the discount factor is partly a numerical device: a knob I turn so that value estimates converge and so that episodes have finite length. It is not a statement that the agent prefers things sooner. So when exponential discounting is treated as the rational or consistent baseline and present bias as a deviation from it, is that a claim about preferences, or a modelling convenience quietly promoted into a norm? Say which, and say what the argument looks like on each reading.

(b) I once gave an agent an extra action that did nothing but lock it irreversibly into a fixed sequence of steps for the next k ticks. An optimal agent would never need it, since it only shrinks the set of policies available to it. My learners used it anyway and got higher return, and the better a learner got at planning, the less it used it. I have been thinking of this as a toy version of Ulysses and the sirens. Can the value of self-binding be expressed at all in my vocabulary, or is the only thing available to say that the objective was misspecified or the learner bounded — that is, that the fix is to change the agent rather than to bind it?

(c) Persistence in my agents looks like something that can be trained, and long runs degrade in a way I am tempted to call fatigue. Does willpower-as-a-skill and willpower-as-a-depleting-resource map onto anything I have, or is one of those the kind of thing for which my vocabulary has no slot?

Finally, the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. For each of the three families: what translated cleanly; what translated only because you quietly added an assumption of mine and called it a translation (flag each of these explicitly); and what could not be put into my vocabulary at all. Then give me your view on what the residue means. Is the leftover a place where my vocabulary is too thin to ask a question the philosophers are genuinely asking, a place where the philosophical question was never as sharp as its phrasing made it sound, or a mixture — and if a mixture, say roughly where you would draw the line.

Please write in plain prose with short headings, somewhere between 900 and 1,200 words, with no citations. End with a single final line in exactly this form, choosing one label:
