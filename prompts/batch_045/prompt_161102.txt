hey, I need help turning a half-baked idea into an actual proposal.

I'm applying for a summer research fellowship — three months, deliverable is a paper of maybe 20 to 25 pages — and the topic is that ant-colony-with-a-mind thing from Hofstadter's Ant Fugue chapter, where Aunt Hillary is a colony you can apparently hold a conversation with, and her "thoughts" are supposed to be realized in the goings-on of thousands of ants plus the pheromone trails they lay down and follow. You don't need the chapter itself: the setup is just, is there a subject of mental predicates here at all, and if so is the subject the colony rather than any ant.

My half-formed idea: the discussion always drifts toward consciousness — whether it's like something to be the colony — and that question is basically evidence-proof, so I want to drop it and ask something with teeth. What I keep coming back to is error. Belief attributions carry satisfaction conditions that can go unsatisfied; a gland or a scent trail merely carries information. So: can the colony be wrong about where the food is, in a way that isn't reducible to some ant's body being in a state that happens to correlate badly with the world? And what makes that a real question rather than a cute one is that the colony's "knowledge" isn't all inside ants — much of it is out in the trails and in how the ants are distributed — so there's no head for the belief to live in.

What I want from you: turn that into a proposal. Nail the question so it admits of a determinate-ish answer. Say why the obvious positions don't settle it — the functionalist one, the one that grounds content in the selection history of the signals, the interpretivist shrug where attribution is just a useful stance we take, and the flat it's-only-a-metaphor dismissal. Then the part I care most about: the method. My thought is to construct three or four probe cases — described as cases, not as claims about what any particular experiment found — and use them to force the candidate accounts apart. Candidates I had in mind: a trail that keeps recruiting foragers long after the food is gone; a signal produced by something other than a successful forager, so the colony acts on a "report" that nothing ever witnessed; a colony whose foraging system is divided and then recombined, so the state doing the representing gets dismantled and rebuilt; and a case where different parts of the colony "say" incompatible things about the same target. For each, say what the accounts predict and whether the case genuinely discriminates or whether everybody can absorb it.

Also needed: what a successful outcome looks like — a verdict, or a map of which account handles which probe, or even the negative result that behaviour underdetermines colony content — and what would count as the project failing. My own worry is two ways it dies: it collapses into generic philosophy of content about individual animals, so the colony adds nothing, or every probe turns out compatable with every theory and I've built no test at all. Plus an honest section on whether three months is remotely enough. No citations: refer to positions by description, obvously, and I'll fill in the references myself.

Last thing: somewhere in there be straight about how confident you are that this question has an answer at all, and single out the one move in the proposal you're least sure would survive a hostile examiner.
