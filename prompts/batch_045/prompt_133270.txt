Course conventions (taken from the handout, so that the question is self-contained). "Grounds" denotes the metaphysical in-virtue-of relation among facts. It is factive (if A grounds B, both obtain), hyperintensional (not reducible to necessary covariation), and entails necessitation (if A grounds B, then necessarily, if A obtains, B obtains). We assume that grounding is irreflexive (nothing grounds itself) and transitive, and we treat asymmetry (if A grounds B, then B does not ground A) as a further, contestable assumption. No fact is grounded in the empty plurality. A fact is ungrounded iff nothing grounds it. Two notions of fundamentality are in play:

I-fundamental: x is I-fundamental iff x is ungrounded.
B-fundamental: x is B-fundamental iff x is a member of some minimal complete basis, where a set C of facts is a complete basis iff every fact not in C is fully grounded in facts in C, and C is minimal iff no proper subset of C is a complete basis.

Two theses about a world: (T1) every fact that is not fundamental has a full ground; (T2) grounding is well-founded — there are no grounding loops and no infinitely descending grounding chains. Foundationalism about a world is the conjunction of T1, T2, and the claim that the world contains at least one fundamental fact.

The question (25 marks).

Case I ("the Coil"): a world containing exactly two facts, P and Q, such that P fully grounds Q and Q fully grounds P, with no grounding relations beyond those stipulated and those entailed by the background conventions.

Case II ("the Ladder"): a world containing exactly the facts F1, F2, F3, …, such that for each i, Fi is fully grounded in Fi+1, with no grounding relations beyond those stipulated and those entailed by transitivity.

(a) [8 marks] In the Coil, which facts are I-fundamental and which are B-fundamental? What does the case show about the relation between the two notions?
(b) [9 marks] In the Ladder, which facts are I-fundamental? Is there a minimal complete basis? Argue for your answer to the second question, stating any further assumption you rely on.
(c) [8 marks] Assess the following remark: "The Coil and the Ladder are both counterexamples to Foundationalism, and for the same reason: in neither world is there a fact in which everything else is grounded, so in neither world does grounding bottom out."

The student's answer.

(a) Neither P nor Q is I-fundamental, since each is grounded: P is grounded in Q and Q is grounded in P. Both are B-fundamental. The set {P, Q} is a complete basis trivially, since nothing lies outside it, but it is not minimal: {P} is also a complete basis, because the only fact outside it, Q, is fully grounded in P; and {P} is minimal, because the empty set is not a complete basis, given that nothing is grounded in the empty plurality. Exactly parallel reasoning shows that {Q} is a minimal complete basis as well. So P and Q are both B-fundamental while neither is I-fundamental, and the two notions therefore come apart: membership in a minimal complete basis does not require being ungrounded. One further point is worth making. Given transitivity, the stipulations that P grounds Q and Q grounds P entail that P grounds P, which contradicts irreflexivity. So anyone who wants to keep the Coil as a genuine possibility must surrender transitivity or irreflexivity, and of course asymmetry goes as well. Finally, since grounding entails necessitation, P and Q are necessarily equivalent, and that necessary equivalence is really all that the Coil asserts.

(b) Every fact in the Ladder is grounded — F1 in F2, F2 in F3, and so on — so no fact is ungrounded and no fact is I-fundamental. There is also no minimal complete basis. A complete basis must consist of facts that are themselves ungrounded, since if a member of the basis were grounded, that member would still stand in need of an account, and the basis would not have done its job. But the Ladder contains no ungrounded facts. Hence no set of facts can serve as a minimal complete basis: the two notions of fundamentality collapse here, and the Ladder contains no fundamental facts in either sense. Note that this world also fails to be well-founded, since F1 is grounded in F2, which is grounded in F3, and so on without end.

(c) I agree with the remark, both about the conclusion and about the reason. Foundationalism requires that every non-fundamental fact have a full ground and that grounding terminate in facts that ground everything else. In the Coil, grounding never terminates: P depends on Q, which depends on P, in a circle. In the Ladder, each fact depends on a further fact, again without end. In both worlds, then, we have an unending regress of grounds, and so the requirement that every fact have a full ground cannot be met — there is no bottom level at which the regress comes to rest. Both worlds therefore violate T1 as well as T2, and Foundationalism fails in both for the same reason: grounding never bottoms out.
