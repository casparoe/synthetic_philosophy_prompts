I am applying for a three-year doctoral studentship and need the research-proposal section of the application — roughly 900 to 1,000 words — plus a working title.

My half-formed idea is this. The covering-law versus narrative dispute strikes me as framed around the wrong question. Both sides ask what explains a historical event, as though there were one thing there to be explained. I suspect they are answering two different questions: the Hempelian model answers a forward-looking one — given these initial conditions and this regularity, why should we expect an outcome of this kind — whereas a narrative answers a backward-looking one, making an outcome intelligible by exhibiting the sequence of earlier happenings it hung upon. And the items in that sequence often acquire their significance only retrospectively: Danto's notion of narrative sentences is supposed to capture something like this, since an event can only be described as the opening of a war once that war has come about. If that is right, the temporal asymmetry of narrative is not a defect to be apologised for but the very feature that distinguishes it from subsumption under a law, and the live questions become: first, whether any notion of causal significance can be irreducibly retrospective rather than merely a reflection of where the historian happens to stand; and second, whether a narrative can be assessed as correct or incorrect rather than merely as plausible, coherent or illuminating.

There is a second puzzle I want folded in, because I think it discriminates sharply between accounts. In a deductive-nomological explanation, irrelevant detail is a vice: the ideal derivation strips away everything the law does not need. In a narrative the opposite seems true — thickening the story, adding local texture, hesitations and near-misses, characteristically makes it more explanatory rather than less. Anyone who wants to say that narratives explain owes an account of what the accreted detail is doing, and my suspicion is that the answer connects to the first question: the detail is what stabilises the counterfactual dependencies the narrative asserts.
