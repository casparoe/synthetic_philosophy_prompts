hey — i need a poem to close a seminar session on lexical threshold negative utilitarianism, and i want the form itself to carry the argument instead of the poem just announcing the view.

The version of the view to write from (please stick to it, it matters): mild suffering is commensurable with pleasure — a two-hour headache is worth a cold drink and a nap, the trade is real and fine — but there is a threshold of intensity above which suffering stops being tradeable at all. No number of headaches adds up to one night of cluster-headache pain, and no quantity of pleasure can pay that one down either. That's the move that's supposed to get the view out of the pinpricks-versus-torture aggregation mess while staying far more pessimistic than classical utilitarianism.

Write a Shakespearean sonnet in the voice of a clerk in a ministry of aggregation: two ledger columns, a daily exchange rate posted on the wall. Lines 1-8 stay entirely below the threshold — mild discomforts only, small pleasures, and they should actually trade against each other, the rate works. The volta at line 9 is the arrival of a case above the threshold, and what should happen after it is not that the agony wins the comparison but that the comparison stops being available: both columns go dead, the mild pains and the pleasures alike. Nothing in the octave may be allowed to sum to it. The closing couplet should hold both the appeal of the view — the relief of never having to do that arithmetic — and the standard discomfort with it, that pain intensity runs on a continuum and somebody has to pick which rung is the threshold, and that choice isn't written anywhere in the ledger.

Then one titled section, "What the form is doing", about 150-200 words, on where exactly the threshold falls in the structure and why the two columns had to die at line 9 rather than just loose.
