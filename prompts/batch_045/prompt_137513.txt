The question was:

In this module we've distinguished two readings of the claim that the economic base determines the superstructure.

(1) The selection reading: the base explains which superstructural forms get reproduced and stabilised over time — roughly the way a selective environment explains the persistence of a trait — without claiming to explain why each individual item arose in the first place.
(2) The explanatory reading: the base is the (or a) cause of the particular content of particular superstructural items — this statute, that doctrine, this form of religious legitimation.

We also distinguished relations of production (property and power relations governing access to and control of the productive forces) from relations in production (the technical, task-based cooperation of the labour process, e.g. the relation between a supervisor and a machine operator).

(a) Explain the difference between the two readings, and say which of them is more vulnerable to the objection that historical materialism makes law, politics and religion mere epiphenomena. [5]
(b) A critic argues as follows: on the selection reading, anything that persists in the superstructure must in fact be serving the base, so the theory predicts that there will be no stable, long-lived legal forms that frustrate the reproduction of the existing relations of production. Yet there plainly are such forms. So the selection reading is empirically refuted. Assess this argument. [7]
(c) A common defence of historical materialism is that the superstructure reacts back on the base. Explain why this defence, even if true, may not answer the epiphenomenality objection, and say what it would cost the theory to accept it. [8]

Student's answer:

(a) On the selection reading the base is a filter: forms of law and politics that fit the existing relations of production survive, and the ones that don't get weeded out over time. On the explanatory reading the content of any given law is caused by the base, so the base is the efficient cause of superstructural items. The explanatory reading is more vulnerable to the epiphenomenality objection, because if every law is produced by the base then law and politics are just effects with no life of their own, and nothing political ever really makes a difference. The selection reading avoids this, since it only says why forms stick around and not where they come from, so it can allow that ideas arise independently and are then selected.

(b) The critic is right, and this is the strongest objection available. If the base selects, then persistence is evidence of fit: a legal form that damaged the reproduction of the dominant property relations would be selected against and would disappear, in just the way a harmful biological trait gets selected out. But plenty of legal forms persist for centuries while making the dominant property relations harder rather than easier to reproduce — rules that block the enclosure or alienation of land, for instance, or liability rules that make a profitable technique uninsurable. Since the theory predicts that we will not see these, and we do, the selection reading is refuted. A Marxist should therefore retreat to the weaker explanatory reading, or give up the idea that the base determines anything at all.

(c) The defence is that although the base determines the superstructure, the superstructure also acts back on the base, so there is two-way causation. But this does not answer the epiphenomenality objection, because determination is by definition one-directional: if the base determines the superstructure and the superstructure also determines the base, then neither of them is the base and the distinction collapses. So the reacts-back defence quietly abandons the claim that the base is what determines things. Worse, if the superstructure can affect the base, then we cannot pick out the base independently of it, because the relations of production are themselves legal property relations, which makes the whole framework circular. What this shows is that the theory can only be saved by dropping the base/superstructure distinction and locating the base instead in relations in production, since those are purely technical and can be described without any reference to law.
