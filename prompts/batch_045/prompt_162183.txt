Write a satirical onboarding packet, roughly 700 words, issued by the Office of the Sortition Authority of a country that has replaced its elected legislature with a chamber chosen by lot. Format it as three short sections: a summons-and-congratulations letter to a newly selected member, a numbered FAQ, and a bulleted code of conduct. The voice should be deadpan bureaucratic cheerfulness — the tone of a jury-duty notice crossed with a corporate HR welcome guide.

The packet should register the real objections to sortition by embodying them rather than stating them, and two invented institutional features should carry most of the weight. First: because members serve one short term and then rotate out, the Authority forbids them from acquiring subject-matter expertise or hiring their own staff — a member who researches an issue on their own is said to have "de-mirrored," and all briefing is supplied by a permanent civil service that also drafts the motions the chamber may vote on. Second: because non-response and dropout would otherwise ruin the sample's descriptiveness, the Authority reweights votes to restore the original demographic proportions, so the packet must explain cheerfully that a given member's vote may count 1.37 or 0.62 depending on how many people of their kind declined to serve, quit, or were excused.

Work in as well: who sets the agenda and what the chamber is not permitted to vote on at all; the thought that selection by lot is the democratic method while election is aristocratic; descriptive representation as the whole point of the exercise, together with the awkwardness that a member is chosen for being typical and yet may not claim to represent anyone; the handling of experts as witnesses who must always be heard in adversarial pairs; and the residual rule that any motion on which the chamber deadlocks is settled by a fresh lottery, so that judgment is never really eliminated, only relocated.

No character should wink at the reader or observe that any of this is undemocratic; the reader should arrive there unaided. Do not name or cite real theorists, living or dead. Make the piece intelligible to someone who has never heard the word "lottocracy," while keeping it fair enough that a defender of sortition would concede the packet describes a recognizable version of their own view rather than a caricature of it.
