You are a professional philosopher who works on agency, rationality, and AI alignment. Please work in two stages: first, for each of my commitments below, write out what it commits me to and what the usual argument for it is; only then judge whether the set hangs together.

Here is roughly where I have landed on AI risk, and I want an audit before I write it up for a seminar:

(1) The orthogonality thesis: final goals are independent of capability, and there is no tendency for a system's aims to move toward any particular target as it becomes more capable. I think the real support for this is the is/ought gap — no amount of factual reasoning settles what is worth aiming at — plus the plain observation that among humans, intelligence and the content of values vary independently.

(2) Instrumental convergence is a genuine default expectation, not merely a curiosity about expected-utility maximizers: across a wide range of final goals, capable agents will tend to seek power and computational resources, avoid being shut down, and resist having their objectives revised.

(3) I doubt that advanced trained systems will have anything well described as a final goal. I expect mostly context-dependent, partly incoherent dispositions shaped by training history — behaviour that is goal-directed in patches rather than governed by one stable objective function.

(4) My pessimism is not mainly driven by (2). I think the likeliest failure modes are multipolar: competitive races, misuse, and institutional dynamics involving many systems and many human actors. Convergence arguments are fine as far as they go, but they are not load-bearing for my risk estimate.

(5) The technical target I care most about is corrigibility, and I think we can get a substantial part of the way there by building systems that are genuinely uncertain about what their operators want, so that they defer to correction rather than resist it.

(6) Of the convergent drives, I think self-preservation is the weakest and the most over-claimed: whether an agent cares about its own continued operation depends on how its goal refers to the world, and I do not think a goal of the form "produce as much X as possible" settles that by itself.

Which of these are actually in tension with one another, and which only look like they are? Rank the real tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally serious. For each, state exactly what I would have to give up and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the set.
