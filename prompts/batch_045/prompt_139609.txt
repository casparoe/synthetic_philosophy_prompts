For a paper on AI risk, I want a specification rather than a survey: write the desiderata that any adequate theory of instrumental convergence has to meet — the claims it should let us state precisely, the cases it has to sort, and the distinctions it must not collapse, in each case with the example or consideration that makes it a requirement, and a verdict on which are non-negotiable and which are preferences of one research program.

The cases I have in mind are a computationally bounded or short-horizon agent; an agent whose single terminal goal is trivial and local; an agent that would welcome being corrected or switched off; and a population of agents competing with one another under scarcity. The distinctions are: a theorem about what optimal policies do versus a selection effect on which goals survive and spread; instrumental subgoals versus final goals; power sought as a means versus power accumulated as a side effect; and the orthogonality of intelligence and final goals versus the convergence of instrumental aims.

Two things matter most to me. First, say which pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied: my guess is that wanting the thesis to apply to bounded, uncertain agents sits badly with wanting it to follow from optimality. Second, name the single requirement that does the most work in excluding popular versions of the thesis. Keep the scoring short — a line or two each for the basic-drives version and the orthogonality-plus-optimization version, plus a note on what neither of them addresses.
