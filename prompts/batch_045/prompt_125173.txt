I work in deep RL — most of my week is POMDPs, belief states and aliased observations, occupancy measures, policy-gradient objectives, off-policy policy evaluation with importance ratios, shared-parameter multi-agent setups, exploration schedules and the grief they cause — and I only know the absent-minded driver problem from one seminar a friend in formal epistemology dragged me to, so I want it rebuilt in my own vocabulary before I decide whether it is deep or confused. The setup as I remember it: an episodic, two-step problem in which the agent must choose CONTINUE or EXIT at each of two intersections that are observationally identical, so the policy is a single mapping from one aliased observation to a distribution over two actions; exiting at the first intersection is worst, exiting at the second is best, and continuing past both is middling; and the striking result is that the continuation probability you would select at planning time is not the action you would select if you re-deliberated at an intersection using the posterior, derived from that very plan, over which intersection you are at. Before I write it off as a curiosity about self-locating belief, I want to know what it looks like from inside a framework where plans are parameterized objects and beliefs are posterior distributions.
