I teach a fourth-year seminar on prediction and its failures, and I'm writing the final exam. The last unit was on Malthus, and I need one essay question — answerable in about 45 minutes, worth 25 marks — that a student cannot clear by reciting the familiar sequence: population grows geometrically, subsistence only arithmetically, and then the green revolution, Boserupian induced innovation, and the demographic transition arrive to explain why it never happened.

What I want tested is whether students can adjudicate the three moves by which a long-run prediction gets rescued once its apparent deadline has passed: re-dating (the mechanism stands, only the timing was off), re-scaling (the unit or the window was wrong — not England but the planet, not one century but several), and re-describing the variables (not mouths but consumption-weighted demand, not grain but whatever resource happens to bind). In lectures I set these against a deadline test I sketched rather than borrowed from anyone: a claim advertised as predictive about the long run retains content only if it commits to some date, threshold, or observable difference that could in principle be missed, so that some finite run of prosperity would refute it.

Please write the question, a model answer of roughly 600 words, and a marking scheme out of 25 built around five must-hit moves, with the marks for each and a line on how otherwise strong candidates typically botch it. The question should put a concrete hypothetical in front of them — a twentieth-century Malthusian who reformulates the original claim so that no finite run of prosperity can count against it — and ask whether that reformulation is a degenerating research programme, an unfalsifiable tautology, or a genuine constraint that behaves more like an accounting identity or a budget constraint than a forecast. An A answer should state what would have to be specified for 'right but early' to be more than a face-saving formula; an answer that merely sorts the three moves into Popperian or Lakatosian boxes should be capped at a B.
