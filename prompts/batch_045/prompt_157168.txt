I work as an auditor for a mid-sized seafood certification scheme — three of us, roughly 120 farm audits a year between us, mostly tropical shrimp and finfish operations in Southeast Asia and Latin America. My training is in fish health, not philosophy, but animal welfare has become the section of the audit that our retailer and NGO board members fight over, and I am the one who has to turn whatever we decide into something a field auditor can actually execute. The constraints are unglamorous: half a day per site, usually with a translator and a farm manager who decides which ponds I see; a bucket, a thermometer, a phone camera and no lab; about forty minutes of the visit for welfare; and a written finding that has to survive both a retailer's lawyer and an NGO campaigner reading the same paragraph. One of my two junior auditors has no training in animal-based indicators at all, and since the UK's Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022 brought vertebrates and some invertebrates — octopuses and decapods among them — within the scope of sentience law, the board has started asking why our standard treats shrimp so differently from salmon, and I have no principled answer to give them.

What I need is a decision procedure, not a position paper: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure terminates in an automatic no, and where it stops and hands the decision to a person because the disagreement is really about what welfare is — felt experience, or health and normal function, or the chance to live a reasonably natural life. I have a draft I want you to stress-test, rewrite and reorder rather than accept: (1) a capacity-gap default, saying that where we cannot establish whether a taxon has the affective capacity a practice would engage, we score the practice against the more demanding plausible capacity rather than the one the producer assumes absent; (2) a detectability constraint, saying no requirement is admissible unless an auditor can verify compliance and detect non-compliance within a forty-minute visit, and that anything failing this must be labelled as paperwork or self-declaration rather than dressed up as an inspection; (3) a two-account rule, keeping lifetime welfare (roughly, time spent in aversive states) separate from the death itself, with the two non-compensable in both directions, plus whatever aggregation rule makes partial credit possible; (4) a rule for slaughter that immobility is not insensibility, specifying what field-observable evidence of insensibility would count and what to do when no such evidence exists for the taxon in front of me. Tell me which of these survive translation into an audit, which should be deleted, and what is missing.

Then run the procedure on three real-shaped cases and give a committed verdict on each. First: a whiteleg shrimp hatchery that eyestalk-ablates its broodstock to induce spawning and now wants credit because it has started applying a topical anaesthetic first, with no data on whether the anaesthetic works in that tissue or how long it lasts. Second — the one where I expect to dislike the answer: a grow-out shrimp farm that harvests into an ice slurry and argues that thermal shock is a stun, and which our current standard gives full credit; the neighbouring farm installed an electrical stunner and gets the same credit, and I need to know how an auditor with no instruments distinguishes an animal that has stopped moving from one that cannot feel, and whether either farm passes. Third: a tilapia operation with excellent water quality and very low mortality but chronic crowding and a harvest sequence in which fish are crowded, lifted, and left in air for several minutes before killing. Be committed throughout: tell me which draft rules to keep and which to throw out, take a definite position on all three cases and on whether the chill-kill credit survives, and mark explicitly which steps of the procedure are genuine evidence and which are value judgements wearing a clipboard — along with an honest account of the cases where this procedure will misfire and what it simply cannot do.
