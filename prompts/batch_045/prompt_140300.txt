I work as a research engineer on embedding and retrieval models, and the vocabulary I actually think in is: a training corpus, a vector space with a nearest-neighbour structure, a downstream benchmark, distribution shift between training and deployment data, fine-tuning, and a probe classifier trained on internal activations to recover what the system "knows." I'm fluent in that vocabulary and have only a summary-level grasp of the philosophy. I've been reading about semantic externalism and want it rewritten in my terms, case by case. The cases are: Putnam's Twin Earth setup, where a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me, on a planet whose lakes and rivers hold some other microstructure, says "water" and is said to mean something different by it; the social variant, where a speaker who applies "arthritis" to an inflammation in his thigh is corrected by his doctor and is said to have thereby acquired a false or different thought in virtue of his community's usage rather than in virtue of any change in him; and the slow-switching variants, where the transport between environments is gradual and undisclosed, so that no amount of introspection would reveal which environment the speaker is in. For each, say what it would be for the content of a term to be fixed by something outside the head, in a framework where everything internal is a function of the data the system was trained on.

Two specifics. Second, consider two deployments of one model with byte-identical weights, serving two communities that apply "water" to different stuff: my vocabulary has exactly one thing to say about those two systems, and I suspect externalism owes us two.
