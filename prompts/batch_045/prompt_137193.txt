Right, I'm doing a five-minute set at the philosophy soc social in a fortnight (yes, really) and I want one bit that actually lands with the people who've read the stuff, rather than a "it's both on and off, like Schrödinger's cat" gag with the serial numbers filed off.

Could you write me a joke built round Thomson's lamp — the one from his 1954 paper "Tasks and Super-Tasks": lamp on, flick at the end of a minute, again at the end of another half minute, again at another quarter, each wait half the previous one, so the whole lot is over in two minutes, and the question is whether it's on or off at two minutes. Thomson reasoned that the set-up generates a contradiction and so the supertask can't be carried out; I gather the reply (Benacerraf's, I think, 1962) is that there's no last flick, so the story simply doesn't determine the state at the limit, and the "contradiction" is a descriptive gap rather than a proof of impossibility. Either of those is fair game, but I'd rather the joke hung on one of them than on "on and off at the same time".

Here's the thing I actually want you to build it round, and it's the whole reason I'm asking rather than just googling gags:

I've been calling it the ln-2 lamp. Same two minutes, same infinite schedule of flicks, but it's wired to a dimmer instead of a toggle. It starts off, and the nth flick doesn't toggle it — it nudges the brightness by 1/n, alternating: +1, −1/2, +1/3, −1/4 and so on. In that order the brightness converges on ln 2, about 0.69. Thomson has that line about how "we attach no sense here to saying that the lamp is half-on", and the nice bit is that this lamp is perfectly content being 69% on. But — and this is what I want load-bearing in the punchline, not just decoration — those nudges are only conditionally convergent, so if you perform the exact same nudges in a different order the lamp settles at whatever brightness you fancy. Two electricians, same box of parts, different order, one lamp ends at 0.69 and the other at 0.2, and neither of them has done anything wrong.

So: write the joke. The punchline has to turn on the order-dependence (or on the contrast with the toggle lamp, where the sequence just alternates and never settles at all) — something someone who knows the puzzle would genuinely laugh at, and something that would die in a normal comedy club. Don't worry about whether intermediate brightnesses clip at full or dip below zero, that's not the gag. Under 150 words if you can manage it, and British English please.
