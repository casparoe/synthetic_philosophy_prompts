hey so i want a piece of satire, and i want it properly in the weeds — weedsy enough that someone who actually knows the futarchy literature reads it and winces in recognition rather than rolling their eyes at a cartoon version.

Form: the minutes of a meeting. Specifically, the minutes of the eleventh sitting of the Welfare Measure Standing Committee in a country that adopted decision markets about a decade ago — the setup where the electorate settles the welfare index once and then conditional prediction markets pick the policy, so values are supposed to be voted on and only beliefs get bet on. Keep it deadpan bureaucratic throughout: apologies for absence, numbered agenda items, a recorded vote, action items, date of next sitting. 700 words max, hard cap — and if you need to cut, drop a whole agenda item rather than trimming them all down.

Here's the angle. The joke has to be that every real value decision which was supposed to have been settled by the voters has quietly migrated into the fine print of the index, while the Committee sincerely thinks of itself as a technical body that only fixes measurement problems. So each agenda item should be a genuine, recognizable technical fix of exactly the sort people actually worry about — and each one should also quietly shrink the set of people whose welfare gets counted, or otherwise load the dice, in a way the minutes present as an improvement in measurability.

Things i want in there, most of them at least:

- the index is only measured with a long lag and gets revised later, so traders can't be paid off for years; the Committee discusses settling early against a forecast of the revised figure, and someone notes helpfully that a trader can now profit by making the revision unnecessary
- the self-reference problem: these markets price the index conditional on the policy being adopted, but adoption is triggered by the price, so the number is a fixed point of itself. The Committee's answer should be a procedure rather than a confession — e.g. they run the market for a fixed window and then treat wherever it happened to stop as what the market judged all along
- sabotage: a trader with a position can act to bring about the outcome they bet on. The Committee's remedy should be a disclosure threshold that mostly catches small traders
- discounting people who don't exist yet, and whether people who will exist only if the policy is not adopted get counted at all. Make this one actually uncomfortable instead of glib
- thin markets. I want one member to object on liquidity grounds to something that is screamingly objectionable on human grounds, and to go into the record as the meeting's lone dissenter on a technicality

Invent a chair and two or three members with plausible official titles. Don't name any real person, living or dead, and don't let anyone in the room notice what they're doing — no winking, no aside to the reader, no character suddenly realizing the horror. The reader should be able to tell what's being ironized without your help, and it should still land for someone who has never heard of the proposal, carried by the bureaucratic voice alone. No footnotes, no citations. End on an action item that takes the logic one step further than anything else in the document has gone.
