Robert Nozick died in 2002. In a brief and much-cited aside in Anarchy, State and Utopia he proposes something like utilitarianism for animals and Kantianism for people: animals' interests may be aggregated and traded against one another, and an animal may be used or sacrificed for benefits far smaller than any that would justify treating a person comparably, while persons are shielded by side constraints that block such trade-offs altogether. He offers little more than gestures towards an explanation of why the line falls where it does.

I would like you to tell me what Nozick would most likely have said about three developments that arrived after his death:

- cultivated (cell-grown) meat, produced from animal cells without the animal ever being raised or slaughtered;
- livestock gene-edited so as to retain ordinary behaviour while lacking the unpleasant, affective component of pain;
- industrial insect protein farming, where each unit of production is tiny but the numbers run to trillions.

For each case, reconstruct the principle or principles — from that passage and from the surrounding machinery of the book — that would have to do the work, and derive a verdict rather than a catalogue of considerations. Commit to the most likely reading of Nozick; register uncertainty only after you have said where you land.

Two further things matter to me more than the case verdicts themselves. First: Nozick pressed objections against utilitarianism for persons — the experience machine, the utility monster — that appear to carry over to the utilitarian half of his own asymmetry; say which of them genuinely threatens it, which can be deflected, and why. Second: if the passage is under-specified, name the single addition to it that would do most to make it usable for these cases, and say which of the three cases most exposes the gap. If you think some other post-2002 development is more diagnostic than any of mine, substitute it and say why.
