I run a tiny grad-student philosophy podcast, and next week's guest is someone who defends an ideal-advisor account of reasons: roughly, what there's most reason for you to do is what a version of you with full information, unlimited time to think, no mood effects and no deliberative blind spots would advise you to do. The account is meant to be indirect — not a claim about how anyone should actually deliberate or about what should move them.

I want eight to ten questions that get past her just restating the view again. Things I want covered:

(1) The order-of-idealization worry. Give her a concrete case where informing first and then making the agent fully coherent yields different advice than making her coherent first and then informing her — and build in the follow-up to her likeliest move, which is to say she idealizes "all at once" and so there's no order.

(2) The handoff problem: the advisor that exists now and the advisor who'd exist after I'd taken her advice pick different options, so which one supplies my reasons now? Follow up if she says reasons are time-indexed.

(3) The insulated agent: someone whose actual motivational set screens off counterevidence (a true believer, say, whose commitment is defined so that no experience counts against it), so the "fully informed" version never gets going. Follow up on the likely reply that insulation is itself a deliberative defect to be idealized away.

(4) Whether an advice-relation can really carry the weight here without the view collapsing either into objectivism with extra steps or into plain subjectivism — plus a closing question about what she'd count as evidence against the view.

Keep the questions in her vocabulary rather than mine, and skip anything biographical.
