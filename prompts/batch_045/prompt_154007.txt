In a talk I attended last week, the speaker used program equilibrium — the construction in which each player in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma submits a program that can inspect the other player's source and condition its move on it, so that a program cooperating with copies of itself becomes an equilibrium — to argue that cooperation among powerful artificial agents needs neither enforcement nor repetition. The analogy offered was that such an equilibrium is like a contract with no court behind it: each party can read the other's terms in full before committing, the terms themselves deter defection, and the Hobbesian sovereign, or the shadow of the future, turns out to be unnecessary. The conclusion drawn was that worries about conflict between advanced AI systems are overblown, because transparency supplies whatever contracts get from enforcement.

I want an audit of the analogy rather than a verdict on the conclusion. Please lay out, in running prose, what is supposed to map onto what: what plays the role of the parties, the terms, the reading of them, the signature, the court, and enforcement in the program game, and what in the program game has no counterpart in the contract picture. Then separate the disanalogies that matter for the use the speaker made of the analogy from the ones that are beside the point. I am especially interested in what the contract picture quietly imports that the program game lacks — a shared language for the terms, an interpreter of them, partial performance, damages, renegotiation — and in whether the mechanism doing the real work is contractual at all, or closer to a mechanical interlock or a doomsday device. I would also like to know whether the existence of cooperative equilibria settles anything, given how many other equilibria there are and how brittle the cooperate-with-your-twin program is when comparison has to be semantic rather than syntactic. Close by saying whether the conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and what a better analogy, if there is one, would be.

Please answer in American English, a page or so, in continuous prose as you would in an email: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables.
