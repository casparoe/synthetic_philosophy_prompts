I run a small reading group — six or seven of us, mostly ex-maths and ex-CS people who found each other through the usual rationalist-adjacent corners of the internet — and we've spent the last month or so on the philosophy of the infinite: Cantor's diagonal argument, Hilbert's Hotel, Thomson's lamp, and the finitist replies to all three. What keeps irritating us is how the Hotel gets deployed as a conversation-stopper. One side says, in effect, look, the manager can always fit one more in, therefore an actual infinite is perfectly coherent; the other side says, yes, but nobody could ever actually do the moves, therefore it's absurd; and both sides treat a piece of set-theoretic bookkeeping as though it settled something about the world. I want to make fun of that move from the inside, and I'd rather do it as a sustained piece than as a joke.

So: write me a leaked internal document from the Hilbert Grand Hotel — I'm imagining a Standard Operating Procedure manual or an "Occupancy Event Playbook" for front-desk staff, in flat corporate voice, roughly 800 words, arranged as numbered procedures with one or two short sidebars or incident reports, signed off by some invented Director of Occupancy Solutions. The comedy should come entirely from taking the mathematics completely seriously and then treating it as an operations problem: every set-theoretic fact in the document is true, and the joke is that being true turns out to be almost no help to a hotel that actually has to run. Target the technocratic slide from "the formalism permits it" to "we can do it on Tuesday," and the way the Hotel is used as an intuition pump by both camps — never the mathematics itself. No winking, no footnotes explaining the jokes, no "infinity is weird!" lines; a clever reader with no set theory should still be able to tell what's being sent up.

A few things I'd like the manual to get into, since these are the bits we argued about: (1) State both standard manoeuvres correctly — shift the guest in room n to room n+1 to absorb one late arrival, move the guest in room n to room 2n to absorb a countably infinite coach party — and then handle the hotel's new wing, whose rooms were unfortunately indexed by the integers rather than the naturals, so there is no room 1 and no "next room up" and the shift procedure has nowhere to start; the manual should propose a fix that is genuinely correct (an explicit bijection from the integers back onto the naturals) and note the operational cost of telling every guest in the wing their new number and reprinting the signage. (2) Finance: rooms are billed at a tariff of 1/n² dollars per night for room n, so a fully occupied infinite hotel's nightly revenue converges to π²/6, about $1.64, which does not cover the night porter; let the CFO propose repricing at 1/n, which does diverge, and let the manual deadpan about the resulting minimum-billable-amount policy when every guest in the tail owes a positive but indefinitely small sum. (3) A coach party defecting from the rival Continuum Hotel, whose rooms are indexed by the reals: the manual should state correctly that no countable reindexing can absorb them, and then observe quietly that the Continuum's own register can only ever hold countably many names, since a name is a finite string over a finite alphabet — so the rival runs at countable occupancy indefinitely while advertising itself as full. (4) Labour relations: the moves have to be completed before midnight within a supertask schedule, and there should be a union grievance about being asked to perform infinitely many acts in a finite hour, answered by the observation that each guest is moved exactly once and each porter performs only finitely many tasks, with the infinitude distributed rather than borne by anyone. And (5) a maintenance ticket about the lobby light, toggled once per move on the supertask schedule, asking whether it is on or off at midnight; the manual should route that to Legal without resolving it.
