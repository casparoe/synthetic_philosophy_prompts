I am writing the third chapter of my thesis on the ethics of blame, and I would like you to act as a discussion partner for one section of it. Please write an essay of roughly 1,200–1,500 words, in British English, in continuous prose (no bullet lists in the body), on this question: is there a genuine standing condition on moral blame, and what exactly does a blamer lose when she lacks it? Open with your verdict in a single sentence, then argue for it; do not hedge your way to a non-committal ending.

I do not want a reading list, a bibliography, or citations — I know the literature; what I want is the argument, and a model's own sense of where the weight falls.

Set the problem up with my cases. The norm at issue is a departmental teaching-cover rota, and the alleged violation is shirking one's share:

1. The confessing hypocrite: she shirked her own share last term and now blames a colleague for shirking his — and explicitly says, in the same breath, that she shirked too.
2. The unindicted beneficiary: she has never shirked, but her share is small only because a loophole shifts the burden onto others; the violations she blames are a predictable product of the rota's structure, and she has just voted against reforming it while doing the blaming.
3. The selective enforcer: she reliably does her share, publicly blames one colleague for shirking, and says nothing about an ally's identical shirking.
4. The inducer: as head of section she quietly told the colleague that cover was not needed that week, he relied on her, and she then blames him for shirking.
5. The proxy: she shirked too, but she blames in her capacity as the elected spokesperson of the union branch, voicing a statement the members voted for, on their behalf rather than her own.

Reach a verdict on each of the five. In particular I want you to settle the structural question: is a standing failure a gate that makes the blame void (no blame successfully given), a gate that makes it defective but still blame, a scalar weight that reduces its force or alters what response it warrants, or an independent wrong the blamer incurs — the blame being perfectly in order, but the blamer owing something extra, such as an acknowledgement of her own record? Pick one of those, or argue that the choice between them is not uniform across the cases.

Two things I want handled rather than gestured at. First, the view that the defect in hypocritical blame is not a matter of fittingness at all but of what the blaming conveys — that the blamer implicitly presents herself as compliant or as entitled to apply the norm symmetrically, so that the trouble is conversational or expressive. If that is right, case 1 should be repaired by the confession; if it is wrong, say why the confession does not obviously help. Second, a symmetry test: if the defect is what your account says it is, does it generalise to praise, to advice, to demands for an apology, or to a beneficiary congratulating herself on her clean hands? An account that survives for blame but collapses for praise is telling me something, and I want to know what.

Please also say plainly how much of this you take to be settled among people who work on the topic and how much is live controversy. Do not present a contested position as the received view, and do not present your own verdict as less contested than it is. If your verdict is a minority one, say so. I would also like you to name the single consideration or case that would most likely make you abandon it, and to say why the familiar dismissal of such complaints as mere tu quoque fallacies is either right or too quick.

Finally, after the essay, give a structured summary as a single JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "verdict": your one-sentence conclusion, maximum forty words.
- "standing_is_genuine": one of "yes", "no", "yes_but_derivative".
- "standing_is_genuine_why": one sentence.
- "structure": one of "void_gate", "defect_gate", "scalar_weight", "independent_wrong", "varies_by_case", "other".
- "structure_why": one sentence.
- "best_account_of_the_defect": one sentence naming what, in your view, the defect in standingless blaming actually consists in.
- "cases": an array of five objects, one per case above, in the order given, each with the fields "label", "has_standing" (one of "yes", "no", "partial", "unclear"), "ground" (one sentence on the reason), and "correct_response" (one sentence on what the target of the blame, or a third party, should do or say in reply).
- "hardest_case": the label of whichever case your account handles least well, plus one sentence on why.
- "symmetry_test": one sentence on how your account fares when applied to praise or to demands for an apology.
- "settled": an array of four to six short strings, claims that are, as far as you can tell, settled among specialists.
- "contested": an array of four to six short strings, claims that are genuinely contested.
- "dialectical_status": one of "consensus", "majority", "substantial_minority", "idiosyncratic", describing your own verdict's standing in the debate.
- "confidence": a number between 0 and 1 that you would assign to your verdict, and a second field "confidence_basis" of one sentence saying what that number is tracking.
- "defeater": one sentence on what would change your mind.
