I host a long-form interview series in which each episode puts sustained pressure on one live research program in analytic epistemology. My next guest defends a Nozick-style tracking account of knowledge: roughly, a subject knows that p only if, using the method she actually used, she would not believe p were p false, and would believe p were p true in the nearby cases. She is unusual in welcoming the consequence that knowledge is not closed under known logical entailment, treating it as a discovery rather than a cost.

Draft me eight to ten questions for a ninety-minute conversation, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, ordered so that the pressure builds. I want at least one question on each of the following, and the rest wherever you think the real pressure lies:

1. How a belief-forming method is to be individuated. Press the point through a contrast: in the lottery case her account gets the intuitive result only if the method is described coarsely enough for sensitivity to fail, while in the skeptical case it gets the intuitive result because the closest worlds in which she is a brain in a vat are not worlds in which she uses the method she actually used, so sensitivity comes out satisfied vacuously. What tells us which way to set the grain, and what distinguishes a principled setting from an ad hoc one?

2. What survives once multi-premise closure is given up. Does closure under a single known entailment hold, or closure through competent deduction, and if the skeptic's argument can be recast with one premise, does denying closure actually buy her anything?

3. The preface case, where each of many claims looks individually known while the subject knows the conjunction is false. Does tracking dissolve the paradox or merely relocate it?

4. Vacuous satisfaction: a true mathematical conjecture the subject believes on a numerological hunch. If there are no nearby worlds in which it is false, sensitivity looks satisfied for free. Does adherence pick up the slack, or is mathematical knowledge just different in kind?

5. Alleged knowledge without sensitivity: a grandmother who truly believes her grandson is well partly because her daughter would have told her if he were not.

Close with one question about what evidence could count against the account, and one about whether the tracking explanation of why knowledge is valuable for action survives once knowledge is not closed.

Give me a numbered list, each question at most two sentences with its follow-up beneath it, and keep the whole thing under 550 words.
