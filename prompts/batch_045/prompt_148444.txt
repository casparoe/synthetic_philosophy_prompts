I write a column on science and ideas, and we're building one of those click-through "find out what you actually think" interactives for a piece on whole-brain gradual replacement — the slow neuron-by-neuron swap with synthetic parts, the version everyone in the field treats as the *safe* route to uploading because there's no single moment where the lights go off. I don't want an explainer and I don't want a FAQ. I want a sorting instrument.

What I want from you: ten to twelve questions, each a concrete case, each with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key that maps patterns of answers onto leaves.

Rules:

- The reader is the one on the table. Write every question to them in the second person, about what they should do or what they'd expect — who signs the next consent form, who gets the house, who remembers the operation, who has the next experience. Stakes, not metaphysics.
- No question may contain the words soul, identity, self, essence, or consciousness. If you need to pick out who's who, say "the one who wakes up" or "the one who signed." A short glossary after the key is fine.
- The questions have to do the sorting work. Nobody should be able to tell from a question which leaf it leads to, and there should be nothing like "do you think you're your body."
- The key has to be rules over patterns of answers, not a list of pre-written personality profiles.
- Leaves I need covered: substrate-independent functionalism; the same with a no-branching clause; the view that you're the living organism and this is just a very fancy transplant; psychological continuity in the memory-and-character sense; the four-dimensional "you're a stretch of a longer thing and the question was badly posed" view; the deflationary there's-no-deep-fact option; and one for "close enough, stop asking" pragmatism. Six to eight leaves total — your call which pair to merge, but say why. Each leaf gets one line on it's most awkward commitment.
- Cases I want used somewhere, in whatever order you think works: (1) the swap done in one session under deep cooling with nothing experienced during it, versus over thirty years awake; (2) the synthetic parts installed inert and all switched on at the end; (3) each synthetic part running a hair slower, so by the finish the tempo of your whole mental life has drifted; (4) the biological parts that come out kept alive and grown into a second brain in another body; (5) the procedure then run in reverse, synthetic parts swapped back for lab-grown ones; (6) at the halfway point the pattern copied into two brains and both woken up. Cut one if it's doing no work, add one of your own if a distinction is missing — but tell me which and why.
- Where the tree genuinely can't separate two leaves, say so plainly and name the pair. Don't paper over it with a fourth answer option.
- At the end, three or four unoccupied combinations — boxes in the logical space nobody seems to live in — with one line each on whether it's incoherent, unattractive, or just untried.

And commit. Pick the cases, the order, the branch logic, the leaf labels, and give me your reasons for the two or three calls you think a philosopher would most want to argue with. Then one paragraph at the end, no weaseling: which leaf you'd land on if you took the thing yourself, and which of your own questions you trust least. I can get a menu of options out of a textbook — I'm asking you to make the decisions and defend them.
