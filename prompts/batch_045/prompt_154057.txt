The practice: my university's research ethics board reviews any study involving identifiable people, and it gets extremely jumpy about sensitive content (drug use, sexual behaviour, immigration status): locked cabinets, encryption, sometimes even legal protection against subpoenas. Identical sentences about someone's heroin use; one version is a closely guarded ethical risk, the other is just a spreadsheet. What theory of privacy does that pattern presuppose? Is one coherent theory doing all the work, or is the practice just underdetermined?
