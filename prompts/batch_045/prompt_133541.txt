I'm writing a paper in which constitutive panpsychism is supposed to do real work rather than sit there as a promissory note about the intrinsic nature of the physical. Specifically, I need it to deliver an extension thesis: for any arbitrary collection of micro-subjects there is a fact of the matter about whether they compose a further subject, and about what it is like for that subject if they do. I need the criterion rather than a list of paradigm cases, because I eventually want to apply it where I have no reliable antecedent intuition — a beehive, a firm, a split-brain patient, a data centre running a large model — and a theory that only tells me about organisms like me is no use to me.

Before I commit to a version, I want the specification. Please write the desiderata that an account of phenomenal combination would have to meet to count as adequate for that purpose. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark it as non-negotiable or as a preference of one camp.

My current candidate, so the list can be tested against something concrete: there is a primitive phenomenal-unity relation; a further subject exists over a collection just in case the micro-experiences form a maximal connected plurality under that relation, and what it is like for the resulting subject is fixed by the micro-characters together with the pattern of the relation among them. I want the desiderata to tell me what would have to be true of the relation for this to have a chance — whether it must be transitive and otherwise well behaved enough to partition experiences into non-overlapping subjects, what that commits me to about near-ties and borderline degrees of unity, whether the relation may be brute in the way bonding strategies are accused of being or whether the list should demand that it be explained, and whether it is a problem that the relation is stipulated rather than something I could ever observe from the inside.

Among the questions I want you to take a position on rather than survey: whether any genuine criterion collapses either into every collection composing a subject or into only the most basic ones doing so; whether combination is meant to iterate upwards, and what stops it if not; whether the demand that the criterion cohere with what our best story about causal integration says about brains is a desideratum or a surrender of the whole motivation; whether the summing-of-subjects question and the summing-of-character question can be separated at all; and whether some subset of your own list is jointly unsatisfiable — I would rather be told that my project's requirements contradict one another than be handed a tidy list that conceals it.

Take a definite line. Tell me which single requirement excludes the most candidate theories, which one you would abandon if forced to abandon one, and commit to a verdict on whether the list as a whole is satisfiable. A short scorecard — three or four lines each — for a bonding-style micropsychism, a cosmopsychism that derives subjects by decomposition, and a non-constitutive emergence view can close the piece; the list itself is what I care about.
