I've ended up believing a package something like this: corrigibility isn't a goal you can hand an agent, it's a structural feature of a particular design, and the classic design — the one where the agent's preferences switch over to a shutdown utility the moment the button is pressed, arranged so it's exactly indifferent between being switched off and not — is the best thing anyone has actually produced. And precisely because it's a design feature and not a value, it's the kind of thing that gets selected away the moment the agent starts modifying itself, so corrigibility is a race we lose eventually and the only question is how long the track is. Call it the fragility view.

What would count against it? I don't want the standard objections I can already recite; I want the ones you'd rank highest, and I want them ranked from fatal to merely awkward.

I've been kicking around a variant I can't evaluate, and it's mostly what I want you to take seriously: suppose the off-switch isn't a lever in the world at all, but a channel of evidence about the agent's own goals — the press is something the agent conditions on to learn which value function is the true one, the way you'd update on a diagnostic result, rather than an event it can cause. Then there's nothing to prevent, because preventing isn't a category the agent has. My suspicion is that this just relocates the pressure: the agent now has reason to manage the evidence, e.g. to prefer operators who won't press, to avoid having a channel installed, or to arrange for presses to be uninformative. But I genuinely don't know how much of the original problem that reproduces versus dissolves.

A second wrinkle in the same family, which I like better: if a press is evidence that the agent's model or objective is wrong, then an agent that wants accurate beliefs about its own goals wants the channel to exist and to be informative — corrigibility starts looking self-reinforcing instead of self-undermining. Does that escape the self-modification objection, or does a self-modifier just stop caring whether its beliefs about its goals are accurate?

Also: what would the fragility view's own defenders concede as counting against it, as opposed to what its critics say? Does it predict anything its rivals don't — about how corrigibility should degrade with capability, say? And be honest about whether it's been qualified into something unfalsifiable, where every proposed design gets reclassified as just another fragile hack, and whether for a claim like this that's actually a defect.
