For a decision-theory reading group I want the spec before the theories: write the desiderata that any rule for choosing between prospects with undefined or order-dependent expected value has to meet, with the case behind each, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are only reflexes of one camp (dominance-first, repair-the-expectation, or drop-expectation-entirely).

Scope: one agent, one-off choice, the question being whats the most you should pay for a ticket. Not social choice over infinite populations.

Cases it has to handle:

- the Pasadena game: fair coin tossed till it lands heads; if the first heads falls on toss n you get (-1)^{n-1}·2^n/n. Summed in that order the terms give 1 - 1/2 + 1/3 - ... , but the positive and negative parts are each infinite, so rearranging the terms gives you whatever you like.
- the Relabeler: for a penny he permutes which prize goes with which first-heads position. The payoff distribution is untouched; only which event carries which prize changes. Is the penny worth paying? And if relabeling is free and reversible, can your rule be cycled — pay to go from A to B, then pay to go from B back to A?
- the split ticket: the same game sold as two tickets on the same coin, one paying only on odd n and one only on even n. Each alone is a St. Petersburg (infinite value, infinite disvalue); together they are the Pasadena game. Does buying both have to be worth the sum of buying each?
- two ways to cut it off: stop the coin after N tosses, versus cap the payouts at ±M. These give different finite games. Must their limits agree, and may the verdict depend on which cutoff you used?
- the long run: someone plays it daily for a year. I'm told the running average never settles down, because the largest single payout in a batch swamps the sum. If that's right, does "agrees with long-run frequencies" belong on the list at all, or is it a prejudice?

Close by saying which pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied, and which single requirement kills the most candidate rules. One or two lines per desideratum, bullets are fine, under 700 words total.
