The early-1990s effort to design permanent warning markers for the Waste Isolation Pilot Plant is one of very few real institutional exercises I know of in which people had to act under an explicitly ten-thousand-year horizon, and I want it run through infinitarian ethics rather than through the usual nuclear-semiotics or "hubris of the present" framing. I've been reading this material in the longtermist corner of the internet long enough to have absorbed the standard moves, so please skip the scene-setting and go straight to the mapping. No citations, no bibliography: I want the argument, not the literature.

Take these as given, since I am not asking you to look anything up. WIPP is the Department of Energy's repository for defense-generated transuranic waste, in a stable salt formation roughly two thousand feet below ground near Carlsbad, New Mexico; it accepted its first waste in 1999 and had a containment incident in 2014 that shut the place down for years without any significant off-site exposure. The 10,000-year figure in the design brief came from the regulatory standard for the disposal system, not from any ethical argument about how long our obligations run. The marker studies, carried out under a human-interference task force and written up by Sandia in the early 1990s, proposed a graded message — first that something artificial is here, then that it is dangerous, then what and where and how, then full written records — realized through hostile architecture (fields of spikes, lightning-bolt earthworks, a black slab making the land unfarmable, a granite information centre engineered to produce a mournful sound in the wind), through pictograms, and through parallel texts in a spread of living languages including Navajo. Alongside these sat two proposals on a different channel: maintain the warning as folklore, carried forward by a self-perpetuating priesthood that transmits a taboo through myth and periodic ritual, and breed animals that visibly change appearance in the presence of radiation, so that stories about shunning them survive the death of every language and script. I am not certain what, if anything, was eventually built on the surface, and I do not want the analysis to rest on that.

The lens is aggregative consequentialism under an unbounded future: the package in which the expected value of a course of action can diverge, in which expected-utility maximization then either fails to discriminate between options (infinitarian paralysis) or makes every finite cost negligible against any nonzero probability of an infinite or astronomically large difference (infinitarian fanaticism), and in which the standard repairs are extended or hyperreal decision rules, isolated-state comparisons, bounded utility functions, discounting, and the cluelessness objection about sign-flipping uncertainty in far-future effects.

What I want is a disciplined mapping and then a verdict in both directions. Say who the decision-makers are for the purposes of the theory, what their option set actually was, what the payoff-relevant outcomes were, and what would have counted as evidence — and be explicit where the theory is silent rather than forcing it to pronounce.

Four things in particular.

First, the fork: does this case have finite stakes that the theory reduces to ordinary cost-benefit analysis with a discount rate, or does it have open-ended stakes, because a surviving message, a surviving priesthood, or a surviving taboo could in principle influence an indefinitely long future? Tell me which horn the case is really on, and whether the answer depends on a factual question nobody has settled.

Second, the structure I find most interesting and have not seen addressed. Model a family of designs indexed by the number of centuries N the warning is intended to last, with cost rising in N as some increasing function and the probability that the message still deters at year t decaying according to some survival curve. Then ask what infinitarian ethics says about the choice of N. My suspicion is that the verdict is entirely controlled by the tail of that survival curve: exponential decay gives a finite, small expected benefit and a boring interior optimum, while a heavy-tailed or power-law survival curve gives a divergent integral and the paralysis result, so that the theory's recommendation flips on an empirical parameter that the design studies could not possibly have estimated and that no amount of additional funding would have pinned down. Work this out properly, tell me whether the suspicion survives, and give me the arbitrariness audit I actually want: which parameters set the answer (intrusion hazard, message-decay rate, future population, discount rate, institutional persistence), what happens to the verdict as each is varied over its plausible range, and whether an interior optimum exists at all without importing an arbitrary choice.

Third, the folklore proposals. These look like high-variance, low-probability, unbounded-upside strategies, which is exactly the shape fanaticism likes: finite cost now, small chance of deflecting action across an indefinite future. Does the theory rank them strictly above the granite and the spikes? If it does, does that vindicate the theory or discredit it — and what is the strongest internal repair (timid or noise-tolerant aggregation, bounded value, variance penalties at cosmological scale) that stops the ranking, and what does that repair cost elsewhere?

Fourth, the return path. My own sense is that the episode exposes three things about infinitarian ethics at once: that it has no register for bounded, institutionally legible, low-variance stewardship, which is what actual long-term responsibility consists of; that it is arbitrarily sensitive to whether the future is modeled as unbounded in duration, in population, or in value-density; and that the practitioners were operating with an implicit modest longtermism the theory cannot reconstruct from the axioms. Tell me which of these you think is really shown, whether any of them is an anomaly rather than an unanticipated case, and whether the episode is overall evidence for, against, or orthogonal to taking infinite stakes seriously.
