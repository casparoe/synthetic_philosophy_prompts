ok so I think I've basically become a no-self person. Like the Buddhist anatta thing: there's no real me underneath anything, there's just a constantly changing bundle of physical and mental stuff causally hooked together, and "me" is a handy label we stick on that stream, the way a chariot is just a name for its parts arranged a certain way. I've been trying to figure out how you'd even test that, and I made up two cases. One: forget the usual split-a-person-in-half thing, instead imagine swapping out someone's psychology 1% a day — memories, habits, personality, one piece at a time — so that after a few months you've got an entirely different person's psychology, and at no point does anyone report a moment where they felt themselves stop existing. Two: imagine brain science finds one stable process that every single experience gets bound to, and stimulating it makes people say "that was happening to me" about experiences that weren't theirs. Does either of those count against anatta, or does the view just swallow both? More generally: what would actually have to be true for no-self to be wrong? Rank the defeaters from "this kills it" to "mildly awkward," and be honest about which ones someone who actually holds the view would concede as counting against them and which they'd just wave off as one more dependently originated process with a name stuck on it. And if your real answer is that basically nothing could ever count against it, say that straight, commit to it, and tell me whether you think that's a problem for the view or whether it's fine because the view was never really a metaphysical claim to begin with. Please actually pick a ranking and give me a verdict with reasons instead of laying out both sides and leaving me to choose.
