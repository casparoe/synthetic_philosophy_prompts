You are a professor of legislation and statutory interpretation who has spent years teaching textualism and purposivism and who is unusually good at building classroom hypotheticals. Please brainstorm several candidate scenarios before committing to one, discard the ones that cheat, and tell me in a sentence or two why you discarded them.

I am writing a problem set for an upper-level Legislation seminar — 2Ls and 3Ls who have done the standard materials and have absorbed the usual cartoon: purposivism is the expansive method, textualism the cramping one. I need one self-contained fact pattern, realistic and one paragraph long (a state agency, a licensing board, a workplace, a hospital; no criminal prosecution, no science fiction), in which a textualist and a purposivist reach opposite results in the direction students find hard to build: the textualist has to read the statute more broadly and the purposivist more narrowly. I want them to feel the reversal rather than be told it, and I will cold-call on it for about twenty-five minutes.

The constraints are where my own attempts have died, so let me name the failure modes. My first draft leaned on semantic drift — a term whose ordinary meaning had shifted between enactment and today — so everything turned on what "ordinary meaning at the time of enactment" means and both methods merely piggybacked on whichever answer was given. My second draft let the divergence collapse into a fight over whether an ambiguous word covered the case at all: the textualist said the ordinary meaning was broad, the purposivist said it was narrow, and the methodological disagreement was really lexicography in a costume. So please ensure that the operative words have a stable, uncontested ordinary meaning from enactment to the present, and that the divergence arises from what each method does with a text whose clear coverage extends past the statute's purpose — not from either side winning a semantic dispute. Additionally: a realistically drafted provision, no scrivener's errors, no antique or absurd terms, no classroom chestnuts; a thin and unhelpful legislative record with no smoking-gun committee report handing the purposivist the answer; no lenity doing any work; and an objective rather than psychological account of purpose — build it for a reasonable-legislator purposivism, then note in passing whether it survives the switch to an actual-enacting-coalition version.

After the paragraph, show me the reasoning each method produces and the exact point at which they come apart; then give me a paired near-variant — same statute, one fact flipped — in which the two methods converge, so the students can see which single feature is load-bearing; then an honest note on what happens if that feature is removed, whether the case collapses back into an ordinary easy one, and which direction it collapses in; and finally the strongest attack a textualist would make on your pattern as rigged against them, together with how you would answer it.
