I teach intro metaphysics and I'm doing a film night next week with Blade Runner, the idea being to get students to feel the sorites problem instead of me writing the heap argument on the board for the fourth year running. I want a reading that takes the vagueness seriously, filosophically, and not just as atmosphere.

The film is stuffed with borderline cases: the Voight-Kampff test returns a verdict, but only by measuring a bundle of continuous emotional responses against some threshold; Rachael is a special model running on someone else's memories; the fugitives are stronger, quicker and more emotionally articulate than the people hunting them and still get retired like animals; and Deckard's own status is left dangling in a way I've never been able to make up my mind about.

So here's what I want: is the world of the film one where there's a sharp cut-off between human and replicant that nobody can locate — the ignorance view, where the fuzziness is in us — or one where the category is genuinely vague and there's no fact of the matter about the borderline cases? Which scenes push which way, and does the film itself ever notice the difference between the two?

Second thing, which I haven't seen anyone do: a tolerance principle for moral status. Roughly, if two beings differ only imperceptibly in the respects that are supposed to matter, they can't differ in whether they're owed anything. Deckard's whole job is killing things that talk, plead and grieve, and the film seems to lean on that tolerance intuition and to deny it at the same time. Does it have the resources to hold both, and what would consistency actually require of it?

And is the unresolved business with Deckard better read as higher-order vagueness — no fact about whether there's a fact — rather than as an open question a sequel settles?

Be honest too about where the reading breaks down: where the film is just being sentimental, or needs the ambiguity for plot reasons, and where a student whos read a bit of metaphysics would come away with a misleading picture.
