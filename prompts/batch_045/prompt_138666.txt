Hey — one sanity check before I send this to our vendor.

We're a county agency buying a pretrial risk score (predicts new arrest or failure to appear within 18 months), and our legal team wants the contract to require, separately for each racial group: (1) the score is calibrated within each group, and (2) false positive and false negative rates are equal across groups at whatever threshold we end up using. Our data folks pushed back and said theres a well-known impossibility result here — you can't have calibration plus balance on both the positive and negative class at once, except in degenerate cases: perfect prediction, or when the two groups' base rates happen to be equal.

So here's my workaround. That exception is just a fact about whatever joint distribution of (score, outcome, group) you evaluate the conditions on, right? So I want to define an official audit pool: we resample our own historical cases so the two groups have identical base rates (and identical score distributions too, if that helps), and we require the vendor to hit calibration and equal FPR/FNR measured on that pool. The score is one fixed function, so if it satisfies all three conditions on the pool it satisfies them in the field too, and we've sidestepped the trade-off. If anyone says we're cherry-picking the sample, I figure we say the pool is the neutral benchmark — and arguably equalizing base rates corrects for the fact that they differ mostly because of differential arrest practices, which is label bias we shouldn't bake in anyway.

Is there a hole in this? The DS team keeps muttering about the deployment distribution but nobody can give me a crisp reason the workaround fails, and I need something I can put in an email. And if it does fail, what are we actually supposed to do — just pick which of the two criteria to drop, and on what basis?
