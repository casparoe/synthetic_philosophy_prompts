I am a novelist applying for a six-month writing fellowship attached to a philosophy department, and I need a two-page research proposal. My novel-in-progress turns on a woman who, after her brother's death, replays an old role-playing game in which a companion character is fated to die, and who has come to believe that by never reaching that chapter — by keeping him alive in her save file — she is discharging a duty to him. I need to know, in terms my narrator could actually think rather than merely gesture at, whether she is deluded and if so in exactly what respect.

The question I want the proposal to state precisely is whether the object of whatever duties we might have towards a video-game character is the character as encountered within a single playthrough, or the character as the whole branching tree of possibilities the game makes available — call these the run-relative and the modal character. My half-formed suspicion is that both stock answers, the dismissive one (nothing is owed to the character; only the player's own dispositions and their effects on other people are at stake) and the sentience-conditional one (nothing is owed yet, but it would be if the characters were sophisticated enough), quietly presuppose the run-relative character, and that taking the modal character seriously opens a third answer: that some playthroughs are worse for a character than others in a way with nothing to do with causing it suffering, because they are the ones in which what the character could have been is most thoroughly wasted. Set out why the existing positions leave this open, and give me a method — I imagine four or five worked case studies of distinct game structures: a fixed scripted death, a branching romance, a permadeath run, a procedurally generated companion, a fate the player can only delay — rather than a survey of the simulation-and-consciousness literature, which I do not think bears on it. Say what a positive and a negative result would each look like, what would count as the project having failed, and where the scope must realistically stop given six months alongside a draft novel. No bibliography, please; I will supply references myself. Please write in British English.
