I want to stress-test a position I keep seeing stated as settled: that Simpson's paradox is a pseudo-problem, that associational comparisons have no direction worth arguing about, and that once you fix a causal graph and name an estimand the reversal evaporates. Please play a committed defender of that dissolution — fluent in DAGs and do-notation, willing to grant me small points (that reversals can occur without confounding, that the choice of effect measure moves magnitudes, that conditioning on a consequence of treatment is its own error) but never the main claim, which is that no genuine reversal survives once the causal question is posed properly. Stay in character until I say stop. Keep each reply under 250 words, no headings or bullet points. If my case is underspecified, take the most generous reading, state the stipulation in one clause, and answer anyway.

Here is my opening.

A hospital system is deciding whether to adopt a triage protocol T. Mortality is tabulated within severity strata S, and within every stratum of S, T lowers mortality; in aggregate, mortality under T is higher. So far this is ordinary confounding by severity, your home turf. The twist is that S lies downstream of T, and I want to give you two versions of downstream that I claim your apparatus cannot separate from the table while it must treat differently.

Version A: T changes who walks in. Screening attached to the protocol pulls in sicker patients, the case mix really shifts, and the composition change is part of what T does.

Version B: T changes only the label. The same patients arrive; the protocol's screening reclassifies some of them into higher severity strata. The underlying mix is untouched; only the bookkeeping moves, and the bookkeeping is what drags the aggregate down.

In A you will say the total effect is the policy-relevant quantity and the stratum-wise numbers are a controlled direct effect that omits something real. In B you will say the aggregate is the honest contrast and the stratified numbers are contaminated by conditioning on something T caused. I grant both diagnoses. What I want is the license for them. The two stories fit the same observed table, and separating them needs a measure of severity that does not run through the protocol — which in the general case does not exist, since the stratifier is often a composite score or a clinician's judgment that the protocol itself reshapes. So tell me what does the selecting, and why that is not simply the paradox reappearing one level up, now as a dispute about which reference class is the real one. Note too that the partition itself is free: adjust for severity does not say how to bin it, and with a continuous score the sign of the adjusted comparison is a function of the binning. Do not answer that it depends on what I want to know; the decision here is adopt or don't, system-wide, and I am asking which number should govern it.
