Rewatched Bruce Almighty the other week and it struck me as a near-textbook Goodhart case, but I can't quite get it to sort. He builds an email system for the prayers, gets overwhelmed, and flips it to auto-answer yes to everything — result: Buffalo riots, because everyone who prayed to win the lottery now splits the jackpot into about seventeen bucks. And separately, he tries to make Grace stay / love him and can't, because he isn't allowed to touch free will. Those feel like different failure modes, and i don't think either is cleanly regressional, extremal, causal or adversarial.

The category I want to propose: constitutive Goodhart, where the proxy isn't just evidence for the target but partly what the target is made of, so manufacturing the proxy can't get you the target even in principle — love, trust, consent, maybe "a real conversation". The target carries an etiological condition: it has to come from the right source. My worry is this is just causal Goodhart with extra steps.

So: read the film. Which scenes are which flavor? Does the Grace plot actually support my category, or is the free-will limit an arbitrary external rule that makes it irrelevant? Does Bruce's own arc (chasing the anchor job, then chasing being loved, and getting exactly the proxies) dramatize the same point about himself that the prayer subplot dramatizes about everyone else? And is the ending — Grace's last prayer is to stop loving him, and he ends up letting go of the outcome — a genuine take on how you aim at an etiological target, or does it punt to "be a good person and hope"? Tell me plainly if some scene is just a joke; I'd rather have that than a tidy allegory.
