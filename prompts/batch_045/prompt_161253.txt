Hey, I'm putting together a reading-group handout and I need a closing poem for our session on Zhuangzi's veiws on death. Could you write a villanelle in the voice of Zhuangzi talking to his disciples right before he dies? Heres the background so you dont have to recall the text: when his wife died, his friend came to mourn and found him drumming on a pot and singing — he explained that her death was just another turn of the seasons, a move from one resting place to another, so wailing would be failing to understand the natural transformation. Later, when Zhuangzi himself was dying, his disciples wanted to give him a fancy burial, and he told them heaven and earth were already his coffin, and that it made no difference whether he was eaten by crows and kites above ground or by ants and grubs below ground — why play favorites and rob one to feed the other? I want the poem to lean on that second scene, but the form has to do the philosophical work. Use a villanelle with two refrains: the first refrain should be a line about the crows and kites taking him, and the second a line about the ants and grubs taking him. As the refrains come back, they should slowly lose their difference — maybe the first time they sound like horror, later like two ways of being handed back to the world, and in the final quatrain they should land side by side as the last two lines, and the speaker should make it clear theyre the same mouth, not two rival fates. That collision of the refrains is the point: the villanelles return is supposed to show how a distinction the mourners thought was huge just dissolves through repetition. No quoting the actual Zhuangzi, please, and dont bring in souls, immortality, or an afterlife — it should be transformation, not reward or punishment. Its fine if its a little rough, 19 lines, and maybe leave a typo or two so it feels like a live handout rather than a polished anthology piece. Someone who knows the text should be able to tell which refrain is which and why they end up together; someone who dosen't should just feel the shift. Thanks!
