I'm a TA for an intro course on democracy and collective decision-making — well, "teaching fellow," whatever — and I've got one 75-minute slot with about 20 undergrads coming up and the sylabus just says "majority rule and beyond," which is useless. What I want is for them to run into the problem rather than me putting it on a slide. The thing I keep half-noticing is this: if a group votes separately on each of several connected questions, the group can end up committed to a set of answers that doesn't hang together even though every individual person voted consistently, and whether you get one bottom-line answer or the opposite one seems to depend on whether you ask people to vote on the reasons or on the final verdict. I'd like them to feel both of those before anybody says the name, so ideally the name comes out of the debrief, and not out of some kid's mouth in minute three.

Rough constraints: movable chairs, a projector, index cards, phones if needed but I'd rather most of it be on paper — voting on a google form feels like a survey, and I want it to feel like a decision. The scenario I was toying with is the class acting as some university committee that has to rule on a case (cancel a visiting speaker? deny a grant? find a student responsible for a conduct violation?) where the verdict depends on two or three sub-questions that are logically connected. I can't decide on the stakes. Part of me wants the outcome to be real, e.g. it determines whether the last session is a review session or whether they get to pick between two essay prompts, but I worry real stakes make everyone strategic in a way that washes out the effect; part of me thinks a fake department with real voting is cleaner. I did a dry run with two friends over coffee and it sort of worked, but they both already knew what I was fishing for, so I learned nothing.

What I actually need is the runnable thing. Give me the scenario and the exact propositions to be voted on — and I'd like the connections between them to not all be the same shape: at least one place where the verdict needs all the sub-questions to pass, one where there are two independent routes to the same verdict, maybe one sub-question that's required but not on its own enough. I want them to have to look at the structure each time rather than pattern-match after the first round. Then phases with timings for 75 minutes, and roles if they earn their place: I was thinking of handing one person the power to decide which questions get asked and in what order, and privately telling them which outcome to aim for, to see whether they can steer it — but I don't know whether that's a great idea or whether it just tells the room the answer in advance.

Two specific things I'm stuck on. First, abstentions: I'd like one of the propositions to be something students genuinely have no firm view on, and I don't know whether a blank ballot should count against, count as going along with the majority, or remove that person from that particular vote — and I'd like to know what each choice does to the outcome, because I suspect the choice isn't neutral. Second, the debrief: I want it to map moments from the session onto the structure, especially the moment when somebody says "wait, nobody actually voted for that." Also, relatedly: is there anything real to say about whether the issue-by-issue procedure or the bottom-line procedure is more likely to land on the truth? I have a vague memory that it depends on how reliable the individual voters are, but I don't trust the memory and I'm not going to tell 20 undergrads something I can't defend — so tell me what's settled there and what's genuinely contested.

And be honest about what will go wrong. My two fears are that one sharp student names the whole trick in the first five minutes and deflates it, and that the votes just won't split the way the setup needs and I'll have to quietly fudge the tally, which I really don't want to do. If there's a way to rig the setup so the interesting pattern shows up without me having to interfere in the counting, that's the part I most want.
