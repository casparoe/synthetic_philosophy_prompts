A guy I work with — call him Dan — does a kind of in-house should-we-enlarge-the-decision-body consulting for our clients, and I've been trying to reconstruct the rule he's actually running. His calls so far:

- 100 raters, each right 51% of the time, independent, two options, plain majority: yes, enlarging helps a lot.
- Same but each rater is at 49%: no — he said adding more just makes the majority more confidently wrong.
- 500 people who all watched the same misleading explainer before deciding, each about 60% accurate otherwise: no. His line was that the shared bad input is a common cause and the crowd can't correct for it.
- 12 independent raters at 65%: yes.
- Sequential voice votes where each person hears the earlier ones and updates honestly rather than strategically: no. He called it a cascade and said the later votes add almost nothing.
- 200 independent raters, half at 70% and half at 47%: yes, though he grumbled about it and said equal weighting was leaving something on the table.
- Tacking one extra 50%-accurate rater onto an existing panel: no, basically a no-op that can hurt through ties.

Now the case on his desk. A client runs a 7-person case-review panel and wants to go to 31. All 31 would come out of the same training pipeline and use the same scoring protocol. On held-out data each reader is about 67% accurate, and the correlation between whether two randomly picked readers get the same case right is about 0.25. That's all anyone knows: nobody has checked whether the shared component of their errors points in some particular direction or is mostly just case difficulty (easy cases everyone nails, hard cases everyone struggles with), and the client has never ran the panel at more than 7.

Does Dan say enlarging helps? I care less about the yes or no than about the different rules that fit his track record about equally well and what each of them predicts here, which one you think he's really running and why, and what measurement or follow-up case would separate them. Also tell me where your own answer is most likely to break and what would change your mind.
