I've been reading rationalist blogs and forum threads since the original sequences days, and there's one argument I keep circling back to that I've literally never seen resolved. I'm starting to suspect it's the kind of thing where everyone is pattern-matching on the word 'experience' and nobody has actually tabooed it.

Setup: flash a red dot at one location, then a fraction of a second later a green dot a bit to the right. Subjects report a single dot travelling across, and — this is the bit that does the work — it looks like it changes colour partway along the path, at a moment *before* the green dot was ever presented. Since the second dot's colour can't have exerted any influence before it arrived, something postdictive is going on. The standard framing is Orwellian vs Stalinesque revision: either (O) there genuinely was an experience with some content at that time and the memory of it then gets quietly rewritten, so you now seem to recall a colour change you didn't see; or (S) there was no experience with that content at that time at all, and the whole thing — including your current sense of having seen the change — gets fabricated downstream of the second dot. Dennett's move was that there's no fact of the matter which happens, that no conceivable evidence could tell them apart, and that this shows we were wrong to assume contents get assigned to times in experience the way we naively do. Others dug in: the distinction is perfectly coherent, it's just hard to test, and introspection (or, later, neural timing data) is *some* evidence for O.

That was decades ago, and as near as I can tell nothing has moved. Every time it comes up in a comment section the same three moves get replayed and noone updates.

My two-part hunch, offered as a hunch: (i) it's substantially a fight about which thing counts as a *datum* — one side treats the subject's report as authoritative about the timing of experience, the other treats the report as just more behaviour in need of explaining; and (ii) O and S share a presupposition, namely that there's a determinate time at which a given content is conscious, and maybe that presupposition is the actual mistake, which would make the question ill-posed rather than hard.

Two probes I've never seen anyone run or even discuss — tell me if either would break the tie or whether they just re-import the assumption in a costume:

1. Speeded forced choice inside the gap. Subject presses one key if the moving dot is currently red, another if green, with a hard deadline that forces the response to land inside the interval before the second dot appears. If responses go green-typed well before dot 2, you have an externally time-stamped commitment with no introspective report in the loop. Or does this only show the *brain* had settled on green by then, which is the boring question both sides already agree on?

2. A colour-congruency probe during the gap. Mid-interval, flash a task-irrelevant coloured distractor or colour word and measure interference as a function of the dot's 'current' colour. A flip to green-congruent interference mid-gap looks like a time-stamped signature of the constructed content, again without asking anyone anything.

What I want is a diagnosis, not a survey of positions and not pointers to literature: verbal dispute, substantive dispute, two different questions sharing a heading, bedrock intuition nobody can argue for, or a hidden empirical question doing the work — which, and why? And then: given that diagnosis, what would actually count as progress (a bridge principle from neural timing to conscious timing, an argument about the authority of introspection, a dissociation experiment, a change of question), or should we expect no progress and why?
