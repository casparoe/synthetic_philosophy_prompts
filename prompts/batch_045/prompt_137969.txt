Three cases. (1) A dance trend. The version that ends up everywhere is a chopped-down two-move version; the original basically disappears, and the two-move version seemed to show up separately in a bunch of places at once instead of spreading out from one video. (2) A slang word. Starts out meaning something pretty specific, and a month later everyone uses it as an all-purpose filler that means basically nothing, and that emptied-out version is the one that travels to other schools. (3) A life-hack clip. The version that goes around is the one where the trick has been simplified so far that it doesnt actually work anymore, and it totally out-competes the versions where it still works.

The pattern I think I see: nobody is copying faithfully, everyones version is a little off, but the off-ness isnt random — everyone is off in the same direction, and the direction is toward whatever is easiest to hold in your head and redo. So the winning form isnt the one that got reproduced most accurately. Its the one that keeps getting rebuilt the same way over and over, including by people who never saw each others version.

Why I dont think my friends version of it is right: in case (1) the winner wasnt a mutation in one place that then spread, it looks like it got rebuilt independently a bunch of times, and in case (3) the winner is worse at the thing the idea is supposed to be for. Also a lot of the shaping isnt in anyones head at all — the app copies the audio exactly but drops the video, theres a character limit, the sound only plays for fifteen seconds. That stuff is doing as much shaping as what people find memorable.
