I am not looking for a general essay on the old evidence problem; I am looking for a specific diagnosis with a price attached.

The context: I am writing a term paper on whether evidence already in hand can confirm a theory formulated later, and I built the argument below to show that the repair I was taught in the seminar is worse than useless. My supervisor read a draft and said the argument has a hole but would not say where, and after two weeks I still cannot see it. Here it is, with the steps marked:

(1) Confirmation, Bayesian-style: evidence E confirms hypothesis H for agent A at t iff P(H | E) > P(H); given 0 < P(H) < 1, this is equivalent to the comparison P(E | H) > P(E | not-H). Call these the difference and the comparison formulation.
(2) If A is already certain of E, i.e. P(E) = 1, then P(H | E) = P(H) and also P(E | H) = P(E | not-H) = 1, so on either formulation E confirms nothing. Certainty is the obstacle.
(3) The old evidence case: the anomalous advance of Mercury's perihelion had been a measured, repeatedly confirmed quantity for decades before general relativity was proposed; the relevant community assigned it probability 1; and yet it is the textbook example of a phenomenon that confirmed the new theory. So (1) plus (2) misdescribe a case we care about.
(4) The repair I was taught: what changes when the theory is proposed is not A's attitude to E but her attitude to the bridge claim B, that H together with the accepted auxiliaries entails E. B is a logical or mathematical claim, and an agent who is not logically omniscient can be genuinely uncertain of it. So condition on B instead: P(H | B) > P(H) remains possible even with P(E) = 1.
(5) Grant that this can work. For conditioning on B to raise P(H) we need P(B | H) > P(B), and I will grant that this is sometimes satisfied — an agent may reasonably think that if a theory of gravitation is true and complete then it should account for the already-measured motions of the planets, and so be more confident of the entailment under the supposition that H is true.
(6) Now generalize. Nothing in the machinery of the repair ties B to an observed phenomenon. For any hypothesis H and any consequence C of H that A has not yet noticed H has, the derivation of C from H is the same kind of cognitive event, with the same claim to raise P(H), by the same mechanism.
(7) Every consistent H entails every logical truth and entails infinitely many further consequences that nobody has derived. So for any H at all — including one invented yesterday to fit nothing — A can raise P(H), and go on raising it, simply by carrying out derivations, with no new observation anywhere. And since these derivations are a priori, they carry no information about the world.
(8) Worse, the size of the boost grows with how uncertain A was about the derivation, i.e. with how bad she is at mathematics. A logically omniscient agent would have P(B) = 1 for every bridge claim and could never learn one, so the boost goes to zero exactly as the agent approaches ideal rationality. On this repair, the very possibility of confirmation by old evidence is constituted by a cognitive defect.
(9) Confirmation is supposed to hold between a hypothesis and the world, mediated by evidence. On the repair it holds between a hypothesis and the agent's bookkeeping. Therefore the repair fails, and with it the only Bayesian treatment of old evidence I know of.
