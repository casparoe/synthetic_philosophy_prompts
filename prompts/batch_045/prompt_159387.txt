I teach a six-week module on time for second-year philosophy students who have never seen a Minkowski diagram. One session, 75 minutes, 16 students, a room with movable chairs, index cards, string, and a whiteboard. Design a participatory activity in which they feel the force of the Rietdijk–Putnam argument rather than follow its steps on the board.

The situation they should end up in: each of them, applying rules they were given and choices they made themselves, certifies some distant event as happening now, while another student has already certified that same event as over and a third as not yet begun — nobody has made a mistake, and nothing in the rules lets them adjudicate. Two design constraints I care about. First, build in a light-signal delay mechanic, and arrange it so that signal latency is visibly not what is doing the work: participants should be able to see the difference between "real now" and "knowable now" because both appear in play and come apart. Second, include a mechanic I'll call the ratification ledger: an ambassador who walks past the room at a velocity drawn from a lottery each round, and whose simultaneity slice ratifies events for everyone; the ledger accumulates and cannot be un-written. Give one student the role of advocate for a single privileged foliation, required to produce a decision procedure that every frame applies and gets the same answer; the point is that the lottery keeps breaking it, and they should feel that failure rather than be told about it.

Give me the rules, materials, roles, round structure with timings, the payoff or scoring scheme (no real stakes needed), and a debrief that maps specific moments in play onto specific premises: relativity of simultaneity, the step from "simultaneous with something real" to "real", the move that makes everything equally real, and the distinction between that conclusion and the epistemic one about what anyone can know. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be frank about which feature of the argument the activity cannot reproduce and which I will have to supply myself in the last ten minutes.
