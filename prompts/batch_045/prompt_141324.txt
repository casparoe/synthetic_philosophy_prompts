so there's this argument that comes up at basically every meetup I go to and I've never been able to tell how much of it is actually load-bearing versus how much is just vibes with a technical vocabulary. The set-up: a wonder-material called careenium (I think its a Hofstadter thing, one of the Strange Loop chapters, but I might be half-remembering it, and I can never recall whether it's spelled careenium or carenium — anyway) which you can sculpt so that any chunk of it reproduces the causal behaviour of whatever it's standing in for. Swap your neurons out for careenium bits one at a time, each bit behaving exactly like the bit it replaced, and at the end you've got something that acts like you, running on different stuff.

Last week someone in the group said this is obviously fine and that the whole argument is just the old software/hardware one: the mind is to the brain as software is to hardware. Nobody thinks your spreadsheet stops being the same spreadsheet when you buy a new laptop; the program is what matters, the box is incidental; so gradual replacement preserves you, uploading would too, and anyone worried about substrate is doing chemistry-chauvinism. Then someone else pushed back in a way I can't get out of my head: on that picture, the simmball never knocks over any simmpins. In the simulated bowling alley — simmball rolls down the simmlane, hits the simmpins, they fall — all of the actual causation is careenium doing careenium things. The simmball is a description, not a cause. So if mind:brain :: software:hardware, then the mind doesn't push anything around either, and the "pattern" the uploading argument is trying to rescue is preserved only in the way a description is preserved — you kept the pattern and lost everything that was doing work.

So: audit the analogy. I want the correspondence table first — what plays the role of the program, the source code, the compiler, the instruction set architecture, the CPU, the peripheral ports, the port to new hardware, and the user — and then where the mapping breaks. In particular:

- Software has a designed abstraction layer that makes "same program, different hardware" a well-defined question: there's an instruction set, and sameness of program is sameness relative to it. What plays that role in a brain? If the honest answer is "nothing, the levels are something an interpreter or an evolutionary history imposes, and there's no fact of the matter about which coarse-graining is the program," what happens to the substrate-independence claim?
- Does the simmball objection actually land? Is there a reading on which symbols really do push the substrate around, and if there is, does that reading survive being transplanted into the software/hardware frame — or does it only work because in a brain the symbols are made of the substrate, which is precisely what the analogy denies?
- Which disanalogies matter for the conclusion the first guy wanted, and which are pedantry? Brains are analogue and clockless, the fine-grained detail (spike timing, neuromodulation, glial stuff, noise) might be doing real work, there's no clean separation between stored program and processor, and the brain was never written by anyone.

Then the part I actually care about: does the conclusion survive, does some weaker version survive, and is there a better analogy than software/hardware for whatever is true here? If it only partly survives, tell me exactly which extra premise has to be added to carry it and how much that premise costs — I want to know what I'd be committing myself to.

Two more things. Say what would change your mind: which argument or consideration would flip you on the simmball question, or on the substrate-independence verdict, and where your own reasoning is most likely to be broken (my guess is the part where you have to say what makes two physical systems implement the same computation, but you tell me). And lead with the bottom line in the first sentence, verdict up front, then the table and the reasoning — no preamble, no restating my question back at me, and no reading list or citations: I've been reading this stuff since the Sequences were going up one post at a time, I want the arguments and not who to go read.
