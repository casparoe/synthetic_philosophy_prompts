Me and my friend spent like three hours last night arguing about whether bitcoin counts as real money, and she kept dragging Berkeley into it — his book of queries about the Irish economy. Her strongest move was that if money is only a token or counter for reckoning exchange, then a fixed supply is a defect rather than a feature, so the thing hard-money people treat as the whole point is, on Berkeley's view, exactly what you should try to avoid, since the job of a token is that there are enough of them floating around to keep the reckoning going. I half believe her, but I also think something slippery is happening: I suspect the claim that real wealth is the industry and labor of the people is doing all the real work while the token claim gets the credit, and I can't tell which of the two would still be standing if you dropped the other.
