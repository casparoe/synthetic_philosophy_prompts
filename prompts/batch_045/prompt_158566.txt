Two graduate students in a perception lab where I work as a technician got into an argument after a departmental talk on the color phi phenomenon, and they asked me to referee. I don't know the philosophy well enough, so I'd rather have the arguments adjudicated on their merits than have anyone cite authorities at me.

The phenomenon, as described at the talk: a blue dot is flashed at one location, a fraction of a second later a red dot is flashed at a nearby location, and observers report a single dot moving from the first location to the second and changing color abruptly in the middle of its illusory path — even though nothing red has been presented until the dot is already at the second location. The report appears to place a later stimulus inside an earlier moment of experience. The talk used the labels *Orwellian* (the Ministry of Truth edits the record after the fact) and *Stalinesque* (the record is fabricated before it is ever published) for the two candidate explanations.

Maya: The report needs explaining, and two stories explain it. Either you first see a moving blue dot and the memory of that moment is afterwards quietly rewritten to contain the change — Orwellian — or nothing is conscious until the second flash is in, and the first conscious moment already contains the change — Stalinesque. I prefer Orwellian: it keeps perception honest, since what you experience at a time depends only on input up to that time, and only the record is corrupted. Stalinesque makes present experience depend on future input, which turns every perception into a postdiction and leaves the timing of experience arbitrary.

Raj: The window involved is only a couple hundred milliseconds, and we already accept short-latency integration elsewhere, so nothing about my story makes timing arbitrary — the window is fixed by the data. Yours has an unacknowledged cost: it must store the pre-edit content and overwrite it, which is as much of a buffer as mine, and it owes an account of why subjects never catch the edit. Mine also predicts something yours merely stipulates: stretch the delay past the integration window and the illusion collapses, which is what you would expect if experience waits for the later input rather than being retouched afterwards.

Maya: The collapse at long delays is neutral — an edit cannot be made outside the window either — so that datum only fixes a parameter we share. And your why-never-notice objection cuts against you: if the record is the only thing anyone ever has access to, then what you experienced just is the later report, and your story is mine told in a different tense. On your own terms the dispute is verbal.

Raj: Then it should be unsettleable, so let's settle it. Insert a probe at the midpoint of the interval: a cue to make a speeded two-alternative button press for the color being seen right then, with the press logged by the computer. Orwellian predicts blue; Stalinesque predicts red. That is a measurement, not a matter of tense.

Maya: The probe is itself an event inside the window; it can disrupt the very integration it is meant to read out, and the subject's later account of what they pressed is as revisable as anything else. You have just reproduced the original tie one level down.

Raj: The press is not a later account. It is a physical event at a physical time, recorded by the machine.

Please go through this exchange and say who is right about what at each step, not just who wins overall — and say what, if anything, both of them are missing, in particular whether any third-person measurement could in principle settle a dispute pitched at this level.
