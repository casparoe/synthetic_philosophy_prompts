I'm in a small reading group working through Book III of Schopenhauer's The World as Will and Representation, and I've got stuck on the chapter on music. As I understand the claim: painting, poetry and sculpture are copies of the Ideas, the definite grades at which the will objectifies itself; music alone skips the Ideas entirely and is instead an immediate copy of the will itself, which is why Schopenhauer rates it above the other arts and even remarks that music is so independent of the phenomenal world that it could, to some extent, subsist even if there were no world at all. Here is my problem. The will is for him a blind, aimless striving: one, formless, outside space and time, and outside the principle of sufficient reason, so that all the distinctions between individuals, species and grades belong to the world as representation rather than to the will as such. But a copy of a formless, undifferentiated thing should itself be formless and undifferentiated. So I've been defending what our group has started calling the drone test: if music is a copy of the will as such, then the most faithful piece of music would be the one with the fewest determinate features, so that a single sustained tone, or an undifferentiated hum, would be the greatest music ever written, and the more structure a piece has — bass against melody, definite intervals, a key, a development section — the further it has drifted from what it is supposed to copy. Since that conclusion is absurd, I think the music chapter has to collapse into one of two things: either the claim that music has content is an unargued leftover from his more Platonist treatment of the other arts, or Schopenhauer is really a kind of proto-formalist, for whom absolute music means nothing beyond its own sounding shape, with everything we hear in it — grief, longing, resolution — projected onto it by us rather than expressed by it.

My friend, who is a composer, says I have the whole thing upside down. She says it is precisely because the will is not bare striving but a striving that objectifies itself in a graded, structured series that music can copy it at all, and that this is what lets Schopenhauer pair the deepest tones of the harmony with the crudest, unorganised grades of nature and the leading melody with the intellectual life and striving of human beings. She also says this explains why he treats music as self-sufficient and thinks it goes wrong when it is welded too tightly to a text or a story — whereas on my reading it should be the opposite: if music were contentless, words and a programme would be the only things that could give it any content. Which of us, if either, is reading him right? Where exactly does the drone test go wrong, and is there a coherent position here, or does Schopenhauer need one of the two fallbacks I sketched? Please lead with your verdict in the first sentence and then give me the reasoning, with no preamble and without restating my question.
