I've got roughly eighteen months of a postdoc and I've decided to spend them on one question in the metaphysics of consciousness: whether the combination problem for panpsychism is actually fatal, or just hard. I want advice on which part of it to attack first — the reasoning more than the map.

Before you answer, here's a candidate target I keep coming back to. Everyone runs the combination problem in the composing direction: given micro-subjects, how do you get one macro-subject. But conscious unity also seems to come apart in cases where, plausibly, no micro-subject is created or destroyed — split-brain patients, dissociative identity, anaesthesia, deep sleep, maybe just attention narrowing. Call that the dissolution problem. My worry is that it has a different solution profile: a story that handles summing might have nothing to say about how unity diminishes under purely macro-level rearrangement, and vice versa.

So: for one person working alone with philosophical tools and eighteen months, where would you start — subject-summing, the quality/palette mismatch, the grain problem, dissolution, or something else you think is the real crux? Rank them and defend the ordering: how much of the rest of the dialectic each is upstream of, how tractable it is solo, and what result in the next few years would move it up or down. Also tell me whether dissolution is even a distinct problem or just summing run backwards.

Last thing: I keep running into confident claims that the combination problem is only the hard problem restated, or that it refutes panpsychism outright, or that Russellian monism dissolves it. Please be clear about which of your claims are settled among people who work on this and which are one camp's contested position. I'd rather know where agreement stops than get a clean answer.
