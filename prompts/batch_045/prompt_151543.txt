hey, I'm taking a philosophy of technology course this term and we've been doing social ontology and virtual objects, and I keep running into a thing in games that I can't find a word for. sorry if this is rambly, two cases from an MMO I've played for years:

(1) Before every raid everyone goes to a particular shrine and types a specific emote. Everyone I've ever asked says it's a hidden mechanic -- skip it and the boss gets a buff or the loot table gets worse. A couple months ago some dataminer types went through the game files and there is no such flag, nothing tied to that shrine or that emote. The practice didn't stop. People still get removed from groups for not doing it, guilds still schedule around it, and if you ask why, people say because it works.

(2) There's a street in the main city where everyone trades. Everyone treats it as a no-PvP street and tells new players it's an official safe zone. Also not in the code. Someone attacks there maybe once a month and gets yelled at, kicked from their guild, sometimes mass-reported. Then last year the devs actually added a real safe-zone flag to that street and everybody said it had always been that way.

So the thing I want a name for: features of a virtual world that are genuinely there -- they coordinate behaviour, people get punished over them, they show up in schedules and in the in-game economy -- but whose existence depends on each player assuming the devs put them there, while no individual player seems to have ever checked or to believe it on their own authority. Nobody is collectively accepting anything; each person is deferring to an authority they assume everyone else verified. And it's not the usual everyone-knows-that-everyone-knows situation either, because what gets shared is a belief about the devs, not about each other.

Is there an established concept for this, or is it just an urban legend that happens to be load-bearing? I want the carving, not a label: what kind of thing this is, how it differs from its nearest neighbours (I can think of a couple -- a convention people follow because others follow it, and the code-vs-made-up-rule distinction), at what point one of these stops existing (does a patch note saying it was never real kill it? does the devs later coding it make it real retroactively, or make it a different object?), and whether the punishment in case (2) is a real harm in the same way it would be if the safe zone had been coded from the start.

under 400 words please, and if you end up coining something I want the definition in one sentence plus one case that clearly counts and one that clearly doesn't. thanks!
