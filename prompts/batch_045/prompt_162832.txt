The context: I'm a senior backend engineer, about fifteen years in, five companies, no philosophy training. I keep running into the same shape of thing on different teams and I want to know whether anyone has a name for it.

Three instances. (1) At one job, nobody could merge their own pull request — in a four-person team where every PR was read by everyone else anyway, where merging your own PR was strictly faster, and where nobody could point to a single incident the rule had prevented. Privately, every person I asked said the rule was pointless. At a retro, when it was raised, the objection was some version of "that's just how we do code review here." (2) At another place, deploys only happened on Tuesdays. The reason — a database maintenance window — had been gone for two years, and the engineer who set it up had left. (3) Smaller: a codebase where every module had to contain a file re-exporting its own contents. Nobody knew why. People copied it from the neighbouring module. A new hire asked and was told it was in the style guide. It was not in the style guide.

The features I think matter, and which I want tested individually: (a) whatever purpose the practice once had is simply absent — not contested, not outweighed, just unlocatable; (b) it is held up by each person's expectation that the others will keep doing it, not by anyone's approval — asked alone, each person would happily drop it; (c) it is attributed to a standard or authority that does not exist; (d) it is individually rational to keep and collectively better to drop, and the second half of that is out in the open: people say in the meeting that they'd rather drop it, they hear each other say it, and still nothing moves.
