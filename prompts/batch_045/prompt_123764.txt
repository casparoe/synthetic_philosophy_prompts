1. Realism: phenomenal consciousness is real, and illusionism is false.
2. I think the illusionsts have correctly identified the thing most in need of explaining — the bare fact that we make "hard problem" judgments at all — and that the best available explanation of it is that introspection represents phenomenal properties in a way that misleads us about their nature (roughly the attention-schema sort of story), just without the illusionist payoff.
3. I also hold the phenomenal concept strategy: phenomenal concepts are conceptually independent of physical and functional concepts, refer to physical properties, and that independence is what generates the explanatory gap.
4. I think the meta-problem is independent of the hard problem: a complete explanation of why we make problem-judgments would leave the hard problem exactly where it was.
5. I think that if such an explanation needed nothing beyond ordinary cognitive science, that would be strong evidence against realism — maybe enough to make realism unreasonable.
6. I think a non-conscious AI with the same introspective architecture would produce all the same problem-talk, and that this tells us nothing either way about whether it's conscious.
7. Mary learns something new when she leaves the black-and-white room, in the ordinary everyday sense of that — and I'm still a physicalist.
