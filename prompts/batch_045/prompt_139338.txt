I am writing the evaluation specification for one component of an automated-reasoning pipeline. Call it the lemma triage module. It receives a stream of candidate lemmas, a fixed compute budget per lemma, and must decide for each whether to keep searching for a proof, switch to hunting for a counterexample, or stop and let downstream proofs depend on the lemma provisionally; it must also be able to show enough of its work that a human mathematician can contest its verdict. What it needs, in short, is a formal account of degrees of belief in unproved mathematical statements. Before I read any proposals, I want the requirements.

Write the desiderata such an account must meet to be adequate for this job: the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it has to respect, the established limits it has to be consistent with, and why each is a requirement rather than a prejudice of one research program. The cases I already know I want addressed: the same lemma presented in two trivially equivalent formulations, so that credence cannot be allowed to hinge on syntax; a lemma whose shortest proof lies beyond the budget; the discovery that the background axioms are inconsistent; the discovery that the lemma is independent of them; and the run in which the module's own earlier verdict is the only evidence it has.

Two questions matter more to me than the list itself. Which subsets of the desiderata are jointly unsatisfiable — I would rather have a short list with the contradiction marked on it than a long list that conceals one. I suspect that computability within a fixed budget cannot coexist with eventual certainty on every theorem independently of the order in which proofs are found, and that letting the module rely on its own future credences runs into something Löb-shaped, but argue the point rather than granting it. And which single requirement excludes the most candidate accounts, and is that a mark in its favour or a sign that I have over-specified?

Then, a few lines each: how three available styles of account fare against the list — a prior over completions of the background theory, conditioned as proofs come in; a market-style criterion requiring that no computationally bounded trader be able to make unbounded gains against the credences; and a heuristic that reads confidence off search progress, such as the shortest proof found so far or the share of budget spent. Score them only against desiderata you have actually argued for.
