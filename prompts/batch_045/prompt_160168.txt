I do some volunteer search-and-rescue dispatch, and I keep noticing a kind of complaint we make about things people say over the radio, and I dont think it is any of the three things Ive seen philosophers argue about.

Case one: someone says the ridge above the treeline is clear. It is clear. He believed it, and he had looked, sort of, from the truck. But there is a pair of binoculars in the truck and thirty seconds to use them, and he did not. Afterwards everyone says he should not have said it. Nobody says it was false, nobody says he did not believe it, and when you push them they do not quite say he did not know it either. What they say is that he could not have told if it had not been clear.

Case two: another guy gives a position off a single cell ping, gets it right, and then says so -- I was right, wasnt I -- and that makes people angrier, not less. If he had said I got lucky, everyone would of dropped it. And if the position had been wrong, lucky would not have been any excuse at all.

So the complaint does not track how things turned out. It seems to track whether the person would have said the same thing if it had been false, and it seems to get worse when there was a check he could have done cheaply and skipped. In thick fog with no way to check anything, nobody complains, even though the basis is just as thin.

I know there is a big argument about whether you are only allowed to come out with what you know, or what is true, or what you believe, but none of those three sounds like what we are doing. Does this thing have a name? I dont want just a label though -- I want to know whether it is a fourth norm or a reading of one of the three, whether it is really about assertion at all or about what the rest of us may do with what we hear, and whether the cheapness of the skipped check actually matters or only feels like it does.
