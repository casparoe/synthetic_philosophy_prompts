I want a stress test of a position I have ended up holding about what normative reasons are, and I would rather you attack it than summarize it. Assume no one in this conversation can look anything up: argue from the position as I state it. I do not want a literature survey, a bibliography, or the names of debates this resembles — I want the arguments, reconstructed so that someone who had never read the literature could follow and contest them.

The position. For any agent A in circumstances C, A has most normative reason to φ if and only if A*, A's counterpart produced by a specified counterfactual procedure, would choose φ in C. The procedure applies four corrections to A: removal of every relevant false belief, factual and moral; full imaginative acquaintance with the experiences at stake, the sort of vividness normally available only from having lived through them; full coherence among desires and among means-end beliefs; and elimination of akratic and other executional failures. These corrections are the entire story. I impose no further requirement that A*'s ends be a development of, or recognizably continuous with, A's actual ones. If the procedure outputs someone who wants something A has no taste for whatsoever, that is what A has reason to do, and I accept that consequence. I also lean on a path-independence thesis: for any given agent, every admissible order in which the four corrections might be applied yields the same verdict, at least across a central range of cases. I lean on it heavily, because where it fails I need to say which order is canonical, and I have no principled answer. Last, the view is indirectly normative in a strong sense: the fact that A* would choose φ is authoritative for assessing A and for advising A, but it is frequently not what A should aim at in deliberation, and it can itself be what A has most reason not to know.

Deliverable. What would count against this, ranked from fatal through serious to merely awkward. For each defeater, tell me whether a committed defender would concede it as counting against the view or dismiss it as smuggling in a rival premise, and say which. I want the distinctive exposure of the view: what it predicts that its rivals do not, what it cannot describe, and what it collapses into if one of its qualifications fails. In particular I want an honest verdict on whether the counterfactual procedure is insulated — whether any possible observation, intuition, or argument could bear on what A* would choose, given that we can never run the procedure — and whether that insulation is a defect in a view of this kind or merely the price of making a claim at this level of generality.

Two cases I cannot shake, which you should rule on rather than list. The first is laundering: an agent whose aversion to every option but full-time caregiving is traceable to misinformation and to never having been vividly acquainted with the alternatives, and whose corrected counterpart trains as a mathematician. Is the resulting reason genuinely hers, or has the procedure stopped correcting her and started replacing her? If you think that is answerable, say precisely how, and say what a non-answerable version would look like. The second is noncommutativity: an agent whose false beliefs are partly constitutive of an end, so that correcting the beliefs first dissolves the end while correcting incoherence first consolidates it, with different verdicts depending on order. Is this evidence against path-independence, or only evidence that the corrections were misspecified? If neither case is the real defeater, construct the case that is.

Also tell me which actual, everyday phenomena — ways people's ends change under information, ways we in fact advise, the gap between what we recommend and what we expect someone to aim at — already function as evidence here, and in which direction.

Close by saying what would change your mind about the ranking you have given, and where your own critique is most likely to fail: flag any step at which your objection assumes the very premise about reasons, or about path-independence, that it needs to prove.
