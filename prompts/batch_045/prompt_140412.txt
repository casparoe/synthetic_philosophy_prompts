Here's where I think I've landed:

1. Blame isn't punishment and it isn't a sanction. It's holding someone answerable — registering a demand and addressing it to them.

2. Whether someone is blameworthy depends entirely on them: what they did, whether it was wrong, whether they had an excuse. It doesn't shift depending on who's doing the blaming, and it doesn't get diluted just because loads of other people have done the same thing.

3. Even so, hypocrites lack standing. If you blame someone for something you've done yourself, your blame is defective in a way that's more than just bad manners — it's genuinely morally off.

4. And the reason, for me, is fairness. You can't hold someone to a standard you aren't prepared to be held to yourself. That's what makes it objectionable.

5. But relevance has to come in: a murderer can perfectly well blame a pickpocket. The blamer's own thing has to be the same-ish norm. Chain-smoking parent telling their kid not to smoke is the obvious no-standing case.

6. I think I was entitled to say what I said, and to say it to his face rather than only filing something.

7. Standing is a matter of degree, not on/off. Someone who's been at it for years has less of it than someone who slipped once, and a lot of the time the right verdict is "you may say it but he's entitled to resent it" rather than "you may not say it at all."

8. Quietly knocking it off isn't enough to get your standing back — you have to actually own up to what you did. Someone who stopped fudging things and said nothing has no more standing than someone still at it. And I think that holds even if the person you're blaming never finds out about your thing.

9. I don't think "standing laundering" is a real problem. If I lack standing to blame him, it's fine for me to turn to our postdoc, who is clean, and say "you should say something."
