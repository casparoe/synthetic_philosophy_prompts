Two moves I keep leaning on, which you should either build into the decomposition or knock down. First, what I call the screen-off condition: for variation in verdicts to be evidence about variation in moral sensibility at all, rather than about differing non-moral inputs — local ecology, resource scarcity, what people think the world is like — you need those non-moral differences screened off, and I doubt the WEIRD-samples studies screen off anywhere near enough. Second, the you'd-have-found-it-anyway point: it is so easy to find some population that differs from us on some case that finding variation looks close to predicted on realism and on antirealism alike, which pushes the likelihood ratio toward 1 and deflates the argument.
