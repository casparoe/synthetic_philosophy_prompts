Not a philosopher — I write software. Hobby project for the last few months: I've been generating Bongard problems with a script. You know the format, twelve little boxes of shapes, six on the left obey a rule, six on the right break it, you're supposed to find the rule.

Here's the pattern I keep hitting and don't have a word for. On maybe half the problems where I think I've written a clean rule, there's a second rule I never intended that also sorts all twelve pictures correctly, and the annoying part is that I can't kill it by generating more pictures. The pictures that would tell the two rules apart are pictures my generator never produces. Either the sampler can't emit them, or they'd break the other thing a Bongard problem needs: each side has to look varied and messy, otherwise the answer is just "count the dots" and the problem is garbage. So the rival rules disagree only on cases the setup rules out in advance — not merely on cases nobody has gotten around to looking at yet.

Two actual examples. One: my intended rule was "the dots are collinear." The rival that also fits every picture is "there are three dots" — my sampler drops points into a thin strip, so triples come out visually collinear, and three scattered dots is a picture the strip never produces. Two: intended rule "one shape sits inside the other," rival "the two shapes don't overlap," because my placement routine snaps shapes to cells and never produces a partial overlap; so inside and disjoint are the only two things that ever happen and both rules fit.

So: does this have a name? I half remember something from a blog post about a predicate like grue — thing is green if examined before some date and blue after — which sounds adjacent, but that's about cases that are merely unexamined rather than cases my whole setup makes unreachable, and I can't tell if that difference matters or if I'm overthinking a detail. Also tell me whether I've got one phenomenon or two: the rival I can't produce a counterexample for, versus the rival I could produce but only by making the problem look trivially easy. Same failure or not?

Don't hand me a reading list or citations, I can't chase them and don't want them. And don't lay out four candidates and leave me to choose — commit: say which existing concept you think this actually is and why the others are near misses, or, if there isn't one, coin a name, define it, and give me the boundary cases where it stops applying. Say plainly which bit you're least sure about.
