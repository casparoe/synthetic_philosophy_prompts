ok so context: I'm on a small team at work that shipped a companion chatbot for older people last year, and my grandmother is one of the users. Since then I've basically accidently become the family's resident philosopher of "does it understand her," and I've been trying to write down what I actually think so I stop contradicting myself at dinner. Here's the list, roughly:

(1) Physicalism is true — everything that happens is physical happenings, and nothing extra gets added to the world when some system becomes an agent.

(2) The Dennett-style test for whether a pattern is real is the right one: a pattern is real if there's some description of the goings-on that compresses them better than the brute blow-by-blow physical description. I actually run a stricter version: I don't compare against the raw physics, I compare the intentional-stance description against the best design-stance description I could in principle give. Whatever survives that gap is what I'm willing to call real.

(3) But I also think "compresses better" is relative — to the scheme, to what you're tracking, to the grain you care about — so there's no scheme-independent fact about which patterns are real. I've decided I'm fine with that and that it's still a kind of realism, just a not-observer-independent one.

(4) Which stance you take — physical, design, intentional — is purely a matter of what predicts better for you. There's no fact of the matter about which stance is really the correct one.

(5) Our bot is a genuine intentional system in the same sense my grandmother is: its "beliefs" and "desires" are real patterns, not just as-if. My evidence is that we predict what it will say much better by talking to it than by reading the weights, and that gap doesn't close.

(6) We ran a blind thing in-house where users rated the bot's replies as more empathetic than the human volunteers', and I do NOT think that shows the bot cares about anybody. I think it would be deceiving users to market it as caring, and I think something is being taken from my grandmother when it says it's there for her.

(7) The intentional stance is in principle dispensable: for any system, somebody ideal working from the design or physical description would predict everything the intentional stance predicts. We're just not ideal.

(8) Methodological one: for a system I built myself, if I can't predict it from the mechanism, that's a failure in my understanding, not a sign there's an agent in there.

So: which of these actually fight each other, and which only look like they do? I don't care which one goes, I just want to know which one has to. Rank the real conflicts by how bad they are, tell me for each what would have to give and what that would cost me, and then tell me which single change would clean up the most. Also flag any pair that looks seperate-but-inconsistent to me and actually isn't, because I have a feeling at least one of them is fine and I can't tell which. And if the whole set needs some distinction I haven't drawn, say what it is.
