Everybody in the meritocracy fight seems to agree on one thing without noticing that they agree on it: that there is a determinate fact of the matter about how much each individual contributed, and that desert tracks it. The anti-desert people say fine, but nobody deserves their natural endowments or the capacity for effort that flows from them, so the contribution isn't really theirs. The luck people say brute luck leaks in everywhere downstream. The efficiency defenders say just pick the highest-contributing candidate and stop doing metaphysics. And the recent critics who argue that meritocracy has turned into a new hereditary caste mostly fight about which traits get counted as merit, and about what the system does to people's characters and to the losers. What none of them ask, as far as I can tell, is whether the *unit* of contribution is well-defined in the first place — which is a question that comes logically before all of theirs.

Here's the bit I think is actually new. A huge amount of real allocation happens in jointly produced settings: coauthored papers, surgical teams, a product org shipping a release, an orchestra, an open-source codebase, a firm's annual promotion round. In those settings output is frequently non-separable — with strong complementarities between people, the group produces more than the sum of what the members would produce on their own, and no individual's marginal contribution is picked out by the facts. How much someone "added" depends on which ordering of the team you imagine them joining, or on which surplus-sharing rule you reach for: average marginal contribution across all orderings, an equal split, everything to whoever was indispensable, and so on. My hunch is that in these settings the claim that the best person got the job isn't false, it's indeterminate, and that the choice among those rules is not settled by any fact that desert-talk cares about. If that's right, meritocracy in these domains isn't defeated by luck — it never gets off the ground, because there's nothing determinate for desert to track.
