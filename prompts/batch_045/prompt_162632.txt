I've been reading about accuracy-first epistemology on my own for a couple of months now — I'm a self-taught programmer, no philosophy background, so I tend to work everything out numerically rather than by reading — and I've written up a short thing arguing that the framework has a problem I haven't seen anyone deal with. Before I decide whether to keep pushing on it or drop it, I'd really like someone to tear into it. Here's the notes version. The setup: credences are graded by how accurate they are, as measured by a scoring rule; take the log score first, where your inaccuracy is minus the log of whatever you assigned to the true cell of the partition. Two people, Ari and Bea, are thinking about whether it rains tomorrow (R). Ari is at 0.6, Bea at 0.7. Coarse partition {R, not-R}: if it rains, Bea is more accurate, 0.357 vs 0.511. Now split rain into rain-before-noon and rain-after-noon. Ari thinks that, conditional on rain, before-noon is 0.9 likely, so his refined credence in the before-noon cell is 0.54; Bea is indifferent, so hers is 0.35. If it rains before noon, Ari's inaccuracy is 0.616 and Bea's is 1.050 — Ari now wins. Neither changed their mind about whether it rains; they only filled in a detail both had left open, and the ranking flipped. I also ran it with the Brier score (sum of squared errors over the cells) and the flip still happens — Ari 0.375 vs Bea 0.635 — so it isn't an artifact of the log score specifically. What I take this to show: how finely you slice up the space of possibilities is a bookkeeping choice, not an epistemic one, but it changes which credence functions accuracy considerations recommend, and under a log score you can inflate anyone's inaccuracy by as much as you like just by refining evenly into more and more sub-cases (each halving adds a fixed penalty). So accuracy-first either isn't in the business of giving norms, or it needs some further norm that fixes the grain. My proposed fix: score inaccuracy relative to the finest partition your evidence actually discriminates, where ways the world could be that your evidence can't tell apart get lumped into one cell rather than split — so the grain is set by your evidence, not by an arbitrary description, and refinements by merely notational distinctions leave all verdicts unchanged.
