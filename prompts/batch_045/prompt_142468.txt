I'm putting together a one-year dissertation fellowship application and need a two-page proposal, due in ten days. I came to philosophy late, after eight years doing benefits and HR comms, and what I want to work on is nudging — specifically the kind that runs on autopilot. I was the person who wrote the emails when my company switched our 401(k) from opt-in to automatic enrollment and then tacked on auto-escalation, and the entire discussion in the room was "nobody's forcing anyone, they can opt out whenever." Nobody asked what things look like by year five. My dad retired last spring with almost nothing saved because he never got around to signing the form, so I dont find the opt-out line as reassuring as I used to.

My half-formed idea is in two parts. First: the ethics literature seems to evaluate a nudge one choice at a time — is the option still there, how costly is the opt-out — but the interventions that actually get deployed are set once and then run over a long sequence of decisions, and the person who sets them isn't the person who lives inside them. So maybe the autonomy question should be about who controls the trajectory rather than about each individual juncture. Second, and related: defenders say these interventions are fine because they leave people better off by their own lights, but if a default also shapes the preferences it's being measured against, that benchmark stops being independent and the justification turns circular in a way I dont think anyone has spelled out properly.

Turn that into a proposal. I need the question stated sharply enough that it has a determinate answer; why both the pro- and anti-nudge positions as usually stated leave it open (I'll drop in citations myself, so no bibliography or reading list); what I'd actually do month by month across the year, and why that approach rather than the obvious alternatives like running interviews or a survey; what a deliverable would look like for someone sitting in the meeting I used to sit in; and an honest verdict on what would count as the project failing or as it being too big for a year.
