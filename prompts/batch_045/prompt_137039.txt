In a normal hospital, the radiologist reads your scan with the referral note in front of them: the GP's guess, your symptoms, your previous imaging. As far as I can tell everyone in that field treats this as making them *better* at spotting what's actually on the image, not just more confident about it. But then in screening programmes (breast screening, say) and in pretty much every imaging trial, the readers are deliberately kept blind: no history, no old scans, often two readers who don't see each other's reports, with a third person adjudicating disagreements. So the same profession acts one way in the clinic and the opposite way in the trial.

What does the first practice presuppose about perception? Who's closer, and what would we actually have to observe to tell those two stories apart?
