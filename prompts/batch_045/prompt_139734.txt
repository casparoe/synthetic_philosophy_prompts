Hey — I run a small reading group (four of us, crammed into the back room of a café, and one of us is a biologist who derails everything into questions about cephalopods) and we have been stalled for about three weeks on the four-tier tower of kinds of minds: Darwinian creatures, hard-wired so that what they can do is fixed by selection across generations; Skinnerian creatures, who reshape their own behavioural repertoire within a lifetime by reward and punishment; Popperian creatures, who can run candidate actions through an inner simulation and let the bad ones perish before they are ever performed; and Gregorian creatures, who pick up words, number systems, diagrams, checklists and other thinking tools from the culture around them and then use those tools to reorganise the way they search, so that they learn how to learn. Two of us have effectively declared war on each other. My friend Priya holds that each new tier is a genuinely new kind of mind and not just a faster version of the old one: on her view a Gregorian creature does something no amount of extra Popperian search would ever add up to, because it can hold a candidate action up as a bad idea and reject it in advance on the basis of how it is described, and that is the first place in the tower where anything like responding to a reason shows up. I hold the deflationary line: generate and test all the way up, with the tiers differing only in where the candidate moves come from, where the testing happens, and how wide and how quick the search is — so the "upgrade" language is dressed-up infrastructure talk. We have agreed to stop shouting at each other and write a document instead.

So please act as a professional philosopher of mind who knows this terrain properly, has also thought hard about scaffolded and cultural cognition, and is going to take this seriously rather than split the difference. Draft the joint statement the two of us should be able to sign. I want: a numbered section of agreed claims — about what the tiers really differ in, what it takes for a creature to count as occupying one, what the tower is even supposed to explain, and what neither of us is claiming; then a disagreements table giving, for each remaining disagreement, the precise formulation each side would accept for it's own position (Priya's wording for Priya's side, mine for mine, no strawmen); then the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of us, and for each crux a concrete case, hypothetical creature, or experimental result we would both agree bears on it, with each of our predictions written down in advance so we can't fudge it later. Here is my seeding, use it or replace it with better: one crux might be whether having a reason represented is anything over and above having the winning option selected during inner rehearsal; another is whether a creature could skip a tier — imagine a born-Popperian with an enormous innate inner environment but no operant conditioning at all, and ask what that does to the idea of a hierarchy; a third is whether the borrowed thinking tools have selection dynamics of there own or only ever spread because creatures intend to use them, and whether the answer changes how big a Gregorian mind is; a fourth is the feral Gregorian, a single creature with no culture to inherit from, which must invent it's own tools or have none, and whose fate should tell us whether Gregorian-ness is a property of an individual or of a lineage. If some part of our fight is merely verbal, say which part and why, in as many words as it takes.

Be committed rather than balanced for it's own sake: tell us which single crux you think the whole dispute actually turns on, which side you would bet on for each crux and why, and what specific finding would make you switch sides. Also flag anywhere you think both of us are confused, and anywhere you are just guessing. Take your time, think it through carefully, and do a bit of brainstorming about what each of us is really committed to underneath the slogans before you start writing the statement itself.
