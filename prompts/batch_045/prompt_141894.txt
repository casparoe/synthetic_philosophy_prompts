hey, I need a piece of deadpan satire, roughly 650-750 words, written as an internal hospital compliance document — think an SOP memo with a header, numbered clauses, a definitions section, a couple of worked cases, and a footnote or two. It comes from a hospital system that has fully and sincerely implemented something it calls the Equivalence Protocol: the view that there is no moral difference, in itself, between killing a patient and letting a patient die, and that the two should therefore be entered on the same form, reviewed by the same committee, and reported in the same column of the annual statistics.

The joke has to come from the fact that this is a genuinely hard thing to administer consistently. Concretely:

- The distinction they're trying to abolish keeps reappearing the moment somebody asks "would this patient have died anyway?" — so the hospital has to pick a baseline. Have the memo obsess over this without ever naming it as a philosophical problem: they need a rule for what counts as the natural course of events, and every candidate rule is either too high (then every death in the building is a killing) or too low (then nothing is, and the Protocol has no teeth). Make them settle on something absurd but workable — e.g. an officially gazetted Standard Course of Nature, revised annually, which currently includes saline drips but not oxygen, or whatever you prefer.
- Include at least two genuinely difficult worked cases, not easy ones. My favorites: a patient who was going to die in three weeks anyway and is now dying in two because a treatment was withheld (is that a killing of one week? how do you bill it?); a patient whose condition the hospital itself caused and then declines to treat, so the "what would have happened otherwise" counterfactual has to be scrubbed and replaced with something else — call it a grafted baseline, feel free to coin better; and a patient who is never admitted at all because the ambulance was redirected. Pick two and do them properly, in full compliance-speak.
- I want the withholding-vs-withdrawing distinction to show up as pure paperwork. The hospital should have invented a category like provisional non-commencement for care that has been authorized but not yet started, so that turning it off can be logged as a withhold rather than a withdrawal. Be specific about how somebody games it.
- There should be a moment where the Protocol's own logic produces a number nobody wants to look at: once everything is on one ledger, the hospital's homicide count equals its mortality count, or its deaths-by-omission figure exceeds the number of beds, or the death certificate form has a new field reading something like natural causes, declined. Work it into the reporting clause rather than saving it as a punchline.
- One footnote where the compliance officer quietly concedes the whole thing may be unfalsifiable but insists the paperwork is internally consistent, which is what matters.

Rules: stay deadpan the whole way through — no winking, no tells, nobody breaking frame — while still being legible to someone who knows the doing/allowing literature, who should recognise it as an exaggeration of features that are actually there: baseline-dependence, the difference between not starting and stopping, and the question of whether the distinction adds some fixed moral cost on top of everything else or just redescribes stuff you'd already counted. A layperson should also be able to read it and laugh. Don't name any real philosophers or papers, and don't let anyone in the memo argue against the Protocol on ethical grounds — the only objectors should be HR, Legal, and Billing. Casual register is fine, its a memo.
