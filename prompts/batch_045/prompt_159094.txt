my reading group is doing a session on supertasks next week and i want to close it with a short poem, maybe 18-24 lines, no fixed form, that gets at the thing i actually find weird about them instead of just going "oooh infinity."

the weird thing is this: in the balls-and-vase version (add ten balls, take one out, halve the waiting time each round) there *is* a right answer at noon even though the number of balls in the vase grows at every single step — it's empty, because each individual ball gets removed at some finite step and there's no leftover "ball infinity." but with Thomson's lamp, flipping a toggle at 1/2, 3/4, 7/8 ... of a minute, there's no right answer at the minute, even though before every flip the lamp is definately on or definately off. same convergence, and one has a determinate limit while the other doesn't.

i'd like the form to carry that contrast. say: the first half's lines keep getting fuller or longer and then land on a line that's empty or almost empty; the second half alternates two short refrains faster and faster, and the closing line somehow refuses to settle on either one without just announcing "it's indeterminate." and if you can fit in the variant where instead of a toggle it's a dimmer you halve each time, so the limit is boringly determinate (half brightness) — that'd be a great last beat, since it suggests the paradox comes from having only two states rather than from doing infinitely many things.

keep it a poem, not a lecture — someone who doesn't know the literature should still get something out of it.
