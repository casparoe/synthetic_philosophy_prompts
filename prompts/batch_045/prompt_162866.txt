hey — I'm running the last session of a paradoxes seminar tomorrow and we just did Thomson's lamp (switch flipped at 1 min, 1.5, 1.75, and so on, so the thing is on at every even flip and off at every odd one, and the question is what's true at the two-minute mark). I want to close on a poem instead of a summary, and I have a fairly specific thing in mind that I'd like you to actually pull off rather than gesture at.

Write a sestina. Six end words rotating in the usual pattern — pick them yourself, but they need to include one for each candidate end state (bright/dark, on/off, whatever), one for the halving, and one for the hand doing the flipping. The form should be doing the work: the same six words returning in a fixed permutation *is* the sequence, and six stanzas are never going to get you to the end any more than the flips get you to two minutes.

Two more constraints:

- Let the lines get shorter as the stanzas go on, so that by the envoi the poem has almost no room left in which to state the answer. The shrinking should read as the intervals halving, not as you running out of things to say.
- The envoi is the limit. All six end words come back, including both candidate states, and I want the reader to feel the difference between two diagnoses rather than get a mystical shrug: (a) there is no consistent state at the end, so the supertask itself can't be completed, which is roughly how Thomson uses the lamp; (b) the flips are all in order, it's just that the story never settles what's true at the limit, so there's no contradiction here, only a description with a hole in it (I think that second line is associated with Benacerraf but I might have the name wrong). Don't pick a winner — let the two collide in the last three lines.

Also, please don't sneak in the physics dodge. No "a lamp needs a finite switching time" or "no hand can move that fast" — we dealt with that in about five minutes and it isn't the interesting part.

Someone who's never heard of the lamp should come away with an actual poem. Someone who has should be able to tell which reading the envoi is leaning toward and what in the wording does the leaning. Standard 39 lines is fine, no footnotes, and dont add an explanation afterwards.
