I am writing the short "where I stand" section that opens my dissertation on self-knowledge and iterated attitudes, and my committee wants the commitments stated up front so they can be tested at the defense. Before I commit to them in print I would like an audit of whether the set actually hangs together. Here it is:

(a) No non-trivial condition is luminous. I accept the margin-for-error argument for this (the one that runs a fine-grained series of cases from clearly feeling cold to clearly not, together with the requirement that being in a position to know carries a margin for error), and I accept that it covers knowledge itself, so the KK principle fails: there are cases in which a subject knows that p and yet is not in a position to know that she knows that p.

(b) A subject is always in a position to know what her own evidence is.

(c) Evidentialism: a subject's total evidence settles what she knows. Fix the evidence and the support relations, and you fix the knowledge.

(d) By reflection alone, without further empirical inquiry, a subject can tell what her evidence supports.

(e) A verdict on a case. Call it the amplified subject: a subject wired to a reliable, fully trusted readout of her own condition, which she consults before answering. I hold that the luminosity failure is repaired for the composite system of subject plus readout, even though it holds for the bare subject.

(f) Introspection is a generally reliable faculty. Most of my introspective beliefs about my current mental states are true, and most of them count as knowledge.

Each of these looks to me like something a sensible person could hold, and I cannot locate the fault line myself. Please audit them: for every pair that appears to pull against the others, tell me whether the clash is real or only apparent, what the two commitments jointly entail, which commitment has to give, the cheapest revision available, and what that revision costs me elsewhere in the package. I have no settled view about which item is the weak one, and I would rather find out now than at the defense.

Order the rows by severity, genuine clashes first and dissolving ones at the bottom, and include a row for every pair you examine, including pairs you judge to be in order. Below the table, add one line naming the single revision that does the most work across the package as a whole.
