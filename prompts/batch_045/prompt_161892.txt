The agreement results people lean on are small-world results: the state space is on the table, so pooling evidence is in principle just pooling partitions. But in a genuinely large world — Savage's sense, where the space of hypotheses is far bigger than anything your evidence could pin down — no one can transmit everything they've seen, so any real testimony is a lossy compression. And here's the bit I keep circling: in a large world each agent's evidence is mostly *local*, discriminating sharply between a few neighbouring hypotheses and staying nearly silent on the rest. So maybe what you should transmit isn't your posterior on the target question, and isn't your raw data either, but a contrast report: something like "my evidence pushed me against H17 relative to H23 by about this much" — a compressed statement of which pairs of hypotheses your evidence discriminates between and how strongly. My hunch is that a group trading contrast reports tracks the truth better, and maybe converges faster, than a group trading posteriors, even at identical bit-budgets, and even though each individual contrast report says less about any single question. But I'm not sure the hunch is well defined: "discriminates between H17 and H23" looks like it smuggles back exactly the small-world structure I said we don't have. And I suspect there's no agreement theorem for contrast reports — two agents can be fully transparent about them and still disagree — and that the reason why is the interesting part.
