we're wrapping up six weeks on the MMK tonight (chapter 1 mostly, the four-cornered rejection of arising, plus some of the two truths material) and I somehow got volunteered to close the last session with a poem, which I definately should not have agreed to. can you write it for me?

what i'm after, roughly:

five quatrains, so around 20 lines. no fixed rhyme scheme, but keep it tight and speakable, because it's going to be read out loud and half the room are philosophy grad students with no Sanskrit — so no transliterated terms beyond maybe one, and nothing that needs a footnote to land.

here's the structural bit that matters. the first four stanzas are the four corners, and i want all four aimed at the same ordinary event — i keep thinking a rumour going round a building would work, but pick whatever you like. the rule is that the last line of each of those stanzas is the same line with only the pivot changed: from itself / from another / from both / from nowhere, in that order. the point is that the reader should be able to see that it's the negation slot that's moving, not the picture of the world — it isn't four different accounts of where the rumour comes from, it's one account with all four corners knocked out.

the fifth stanza is where this either works or doesn't, so please be careful with it:

- it can't be a fifth corner. no "so it comes from nothing", no "so it never really started".
- it can't be a synthesis either — no "and therefore everything is connected" / "emptiness turns out to be fullness", which is the move our convener calls the recoil.
- and it can't be mysticism. no silence, no beyond words, no the wise do not say. i know that's how this stuff usually gets versified and it's more or less what we spent week three arguing against.
- what it has to do instead is show that the four rejections were themselves empty — that denying arising isn't a position about arising, the way he answers the objection that he must have a thesis of his own by saying he hasn't got one. our group has started calling the move where you clear all four corners and then set up home in the fourth one corner-squatting, and i'd like the poem to be unsquattable.
- the ordinary level has to come back. the rumour is still going round at the end, someone still believes it, and none of that is being denied. what's gone is only its having a source of its own.

one more constraint, and it's the one i care about most: the final line shouldn't be quotable. not in the sense of badly written — in the sense that if someone can lift a line off the end and put it on a poster, that line has become the fifth corner. i want people walking out with the poem in their heads and unable to say what it concluded.

oh, and the whole thing in British English please (realise, colour, etc).

if it's not too much hassle, give me three or four sentences after the poem on which bit is doing which job — which line is which corner, and where exactly the last stanza is refusing to land — mostly so i can check i'm not about to read something false out loud in front of people who actually know this material.
