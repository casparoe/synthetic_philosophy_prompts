hey — I want a piece to drop in the ACX open thread and on my own blog, and I want it to work on two audiences at once: people who've actually read that 700-page book about Gödel, Escher and Bach and take the strange-loop theory of the self seriously, and the ML-adjacent crowd who think the self is obviously an illusion but have never tracked what that commitment costs.

Format: an RFC. A real one — Rust-style, with the numbered sections (Summary, Motivation, Guide-level explanation, Reference-level explanation, Drawbacks, Rationale and alternatives, Prior art, Unresolved questions). 800-1000 words. Filed by an invented working group (something like the Substrate Independence Working Group) to some fictional standards process. The proposal: deprecate the first-person symbol — what the strange-loop people call the self-symbol — and migrate to a third-person, indexical-free way for a system to refer to itself.

I want the jokes to come out of the actual mechanics of the view, not a strawman:

- The self-symbol is a symbol inside the system that denotes the whole system containing it. It lives on a tangled hierarchy, so it can't be flattened into a clean level diagram. It's a pattern rather than a substance, hence substrate-independent, and it comes in degrees — so legacy deployments (infants, dogs, long-married couples, nation-states) retain partial I-ness after the migration. Get that right and a Hofstadter person should laugh instead of wince.
- The document has to identify who's proposing it without ever writing I or we. I want at least four different workarounds to show up and each to fail in its own way: reference by definite description (the entity that instantiates this document), a quoted string, a Gödel-flavored self-referential index (this RFC, section 3.1), and plain this document. Each one should quietly reconstitute the loop it's trying to delete, and the doc should never notice.
- The Prior art section should deadpan through the standard touchstones: the self-referential formal sentence, the Escher lithograph where two hands draw each other, the canon that ends up a whole tone above where it started.
- The straight-faced part, which is where the real reductio lives: the migration is a complete success. Self-model recursion gets bounded, no more I-symbol thrashing, systems keep behaving purposively, and the issue tracker goes silent because there's nobody left to file a bug. The doc should report that silence as its headline metric and call it a promising signal. Nobody in it ever says out loud that this is the problem.
- Drawbacks has to admit, in flat engineering language, the thing that makes you recoil: nothing is at stake for anyone anymore, so the cost-benefit analysis has no beneficiary; and everything that needs a graded or extended I — grief, resentment, love, blame — gets listed as broken by this change, won't fix.

Ground rules: deadpan the whole way, no winking, no closing paragraph that explains the joke, and don't let the document deny that the self exists. The target is the strange-loop view, which says the self is real, just a loop — what should get skewered is what happens when you start treating real but implementational as therefore optional. Should still be readable to someone who's never heard of a strange loop, and definately shouldn't name any real person as the butt of it.
