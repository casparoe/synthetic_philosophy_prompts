Two members of my reading group have spent months on Gödel's rotating-universe solutions, and the usual rationalist-blog treatments of the block universe just restate the disagreement rather than locating it, so I want the document they should have produced instead: a joint statement from an adversarial collaboration between a Gödel-style idealist, who takes those solutions to show that the lapse of time is not an objective feature of the world, and a temporal realist who thinks the solutions at most refute a package deal of a global "now" plus absolute simultaneity and leave temporal structure itself perfectly real.

The deliverable: a numbered section of claims both could sign — about what a global time function is and why closed timelike curves preclude one, about which solutions are even candidate descriptions of a universe like ours, and about what an argument from "this is a solution of the field equations" to a conclusion about time would have to establish — followed by a disagreements table in which each row is worded so that the side holding it would accept the wording as its own position. I suspect part of the fight is verbal and that "time is unreal" is doing at least three separate jobs; if so, say which part is verbal, what the distinct readings are, and which substantive claims survive the disambiguation.

Then give me the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side — and, for each crux, a case both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. For the cases, work with these two, which I have not managed to use to break the tie: (A) a model locally indistinguishable from ours at every event, but in which closed timelike curves pass through every event while every such curve's total proper time exceeds the lifespan of any observer who could traverse it; and (B) a model in which closed timelike curves are confined to a bounded region and a global time function exists everywhere else. For each, say what each side predicts the right treatment to be, and why it does or does not bear on their dispute. Please stay neutral throughout — I want a text both could put their names to, not a verdict on who wins.
