so im in this little alignment reading group and one of the guys keeps saying HCH is basically just a company: there's one human at the top who can't do the work herself, she breaks the question up and hands the pieces to copies of herself, and the magic is supposed to be that judging and stitching is easier than doing. He uses it as an optimistic argument — orgs already outperform any individual on almost everything, we've got centuries of know-how about structuring them, so amplification is a solved-in-practice idea and the rest is engineering.

The more I sit with it the more I think the analogy is carrying the whole conclusion and maybe also describing the wrong kind of thing. HCH is supposed to be an idealization of deliberation — one mind thinking longer and harder and arriving at an answer it endorses — whereas a firm isn't a mind arriving at a belief, it's a production unit that ships an action and gets sorted out later by market feedback. The questions HCH is meant to answer are exactly the ones with no cheap feedback.

Can you audit the analogy properly? I want the correspondence table first: who is the CEO, the middle manager, the leaf worker, what plays the role of headcount, of a meeting, of a promotion, of the handbook, and what distillation corresponds to (my guess is something like writing the org's accumulated know-how into one policy doc, or training a single model to play the whole org). Then the disanalogies, and specifically these three, which are the ones I can't stop poking at:

1. In a firm the nodes are different people with different training, so you get comparative advantage and real specialization. In HCH every node is the same person with the same knowledge, so all you can divide is attention and tree position — there's no talent to exploit.
2. Firms are persistent. They keep institutional memory across years and get better at their division of labour. A HCH tree is assembled for one question and dissolves when it's answered, so it can't accumulate anything.
3. The one I think is actually load-bearing: what does the aligning in a company is liability. Someone's neck is on the line, she can be fired, she eats the cost of being wrong, the workers are paid and monitored, and ex post the org learns who was full of shit. HCH has no necks — it's the same agent all the way down, so there's nobody to be accountable to and no sanction after the fact. Am I onto something there or am I just reinventing the oversight problem in worse words?

Then, the part I actually need: which of the disanalogies matter for his conclusion that amplification obviously scales, and which are cosmetic? Does the optimistic version survive once the necks and the persistence are taken out? Is there a better analogy — I keep trying an idealized peer review process, or a judge with unlimited clerks who are only allowed to read and summarize, or a mathematician with a hundred grad students — or should we drop org metaphors entirely and think of it as something closer to ideal reflection, what you'd believe if you could think for a million years? And given that the root has to endorse the answer rather than just ship it, is HCH more like a firm or more like a committee that has to issue a joint statement?

I'm writing this up as a short piece for the group, so be disciplined about the mapping and just say so where the analogy is silent instead of forcing it to speak.
