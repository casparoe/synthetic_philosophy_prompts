I have been reading about Einstein's religious and philosophical views on my own — a popular biography and a few essays I found online, nothing academic — and I would like to know how much of one summary of them I should trust. The biography puts his position roughly like this: Einstein was really an atheist who used God-language only as a modest way of admitting how little he knew; he thought that because physics describes everything in terms of deterministic laws, free will is an illusion, and our sense of choosing is just ignorance of the causes behind our behaviour; he therefore held that nobody is responsible for anything, so blame and punishment cannot be justified, and this is what carried him into pacifism and socialism; and the same refusal to accept that anything is uncaused explains his dislike of quantum mechanics — if nature were genuinely random at bottom, he apparently thought, science would have nothing left to explain.

Some of this sounds right to me and some of it does not. I am most suspicious of the move to punishment, because Einstein seems to have been quite harsh about people he thought were morally at fault, but I cannot tell whether that is just him failing to live up to his own theory. I also have a distinction of my own that may be doing some work here: "my choice was caused by things outside my control" and "my making the choice made no difference to what happened" feel like two different claims, and if they are, I suspect the book runs them together when it slides from physics to blame.

Could you go through the passage claim by claim and tell me what a careful reader of Einstein would accept, what is a fair simplification for a popular book, and what actually misrepresents him? I especially want to know whether the conclusions about blame, punishment, politics and quantum mechanics follow even from the parts of the summary that are accurate, or whether they need extra premises he did not hold. Please say where my own distinction helps and where it is beside the point, avoid assuming any background in philosophy, and write in British English.
