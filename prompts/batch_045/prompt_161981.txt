Something about how money in politics is regulated has been bugging me, and I can't tell whether the rules are just inconsistent or whether they secretly hang together. I've been going down this rabbit hole on my own, no poltical science background, so correct me if I've got any of this wrong: in the US you can only hand a candidate's campaign a fairly small amount directly — nothing like what a real race costs — but you can spend unlimited money on ads backing that candidate as long as you never coordinate with the campaign; you can pay a lobbyist enormous fees to go argue your case to the same politician; you can hire that politician's staffer shortly after they leave office; and if you're rich you can spend unlimited amounts of your own money getting yourself elected. What's criminal is paying an official to vote a particular way, or paying a voter to vote at all.

What has to be true for that package to make sense? To me it only adds up if the thing wrong with money in politics is a deal — cash traded for an official act — since everything banned is transactional and everything allowed just buys access, influence, or a bigger megaphone. But then there's the bit my brother-in-law threw at me: if the worry is that money distorts an official's judgement, then buying the best research team in the country and genuinely convincing a politician on the merits should count as the worst case, not as the most protected activity there is. That seems backwards, so either the rules were never really about distorted judgement, or there's a line between convincing someone and corrupting them that I'm not seeing.

Can you reconstruct the theory of corruption — or of democracy, or of what speech even is — that this set of rules actually presupposes? Tell me which rival theories it quietly rules out (for instance the one where the problem is that the legislature as a whole ends up answering to donors even though nobody is ever bribed, or the one where wildly unequal spending is itself unfair whatever deals do or don't get made), where the rules are actually underdetermined so that several different theories would license them, and what would need to change in the rules if we swapped in each of those rivals.
