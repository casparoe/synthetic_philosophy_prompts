I write about science and ideas for a living, and the commons keeps coming up in my pieces, so I want to get clear on what I actually think instead of just reaching for the same tired pasture metaphor. Here are my gut verdicts, case by case.

A small fishery I reported on years back where the boats set their own trap limits and policed each other: no blame, actually kind of great. My neighbor who never chips in for the volunteer snow clearing but walks the cleared sidewalk every morning: mildly annoying, mild blame. Me, reading Wikipedia for twenty years and donating twice: I feel fine, honestly. Someone who skips a flu shot: mildly blameworthy, and clearly more so if they work in a nursing home. A drug company that prices a medicine built on publicly funded research at fifty times cost: I feel real anger. A company that builds its product on a widely used libary maintained by one unpaid person, contributes nothing, and files bug reports demanding fixes: clearly wrong. A hobbyist who uses the same library and contributes nothing: fine. A big country that won't cut emissions while smaller ones do: strongly blameworthy.

So: identical behavior, opposite verdicts, depending on who's doing it. Is there one principle that gets all of these — something about rivalry, or scale, or dependence, or whether you're a member of the scheme you're using — or do I have to choose between a tidy principle that overturns one of my verdicts and a messy one that saves them all? Tell me which verdict you'd throw out and why, and what your principle says about the case I keep flip-flopping on: a company that trains a model on everything publicly posted online and then gives the thing away for free.
