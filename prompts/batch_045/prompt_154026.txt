As a journalist who reports most of the year from cities that aren't mine, I keep hitting small local rules I can't get straight, and I'd like a principle rather than a rule of thumb. My verdicts: leaving no tip in a city where restaurant pay is built around tips is a genuine moral failing, not a breach of etiquette, even when the meal was mediocre and I'll never see the server again; leaving no tip in a city where tipping isn't practised and has to be handed back is entirely fine, and leaving money there is generous but not required. Pushing into a queue at a bus stop is wrong; pushing into the same crowd where nobody is queueing is not wrong at all. Crossing against a signal at 2 a.m. on an empty street is not wrong, but crossing in front of a car that has to brake is. Handing over the customary small cash gift to get a vehicle across a border, in a place where that is simply how it is done, is wrong, and the local practice does not excuse it; yet slipping cash to a hotel housekeeper in a non-tipping city, where nothing of the kind is customary, strikes me as good though not required. Those last two sit badly together, and so do these: I think a visitor of five years owes more compliance than one of two days, though the difference can't be a matter of who gets hurt; and I think a visitor should follow what I've started calling a phantom norm, a rule nearly everyone believes is in force and nearly nobody actually follows, such as giving up a tram seat to anyone visibly older in a city where perhaps one passenger in ten does.

My working principle is that I owe compliance to a local convention just when flouting it would set back someone's interests in a way they couldn't reasonably avoid, or would make me a free rider on their own compliance. Test it against the verdicts above and repair it, or replace it if repair won't work. Two cases I haven't settled: in one city, drivers on a ride-hailing app expect five stars by default and a passenger who rates honestly is quietly penalised with longer waits, so is an honest four a moral failing? And a hotel in a non-tipping city installs a tip jar because foreign guests kept pressing cash on the staff, which seems to bring the convention into existence by stages; am I bound by it now, and if so, when did it start binding?

Please answer as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no preamble and no closing line: the repaired or replacement principle stated precisely; the case that motivates each clause; the verdicts of mine it cannot accommodate; its verdicts on the two unsettled cases; and the one distinction it still cannot draw.
