I'm retiring in June after 33 years at the county mosquito abatement district — last fourteen as director — and I have to give a farewell address at the district banquet. About ten minutes, its the last thing before cake, and I need help writing it.

The room will be roughly three groups: the field crew and their families (half the room), the county commissioners who sign our budget, and four or five people from a local river conservation group who spent most of the last decade trying to get our adulticide program shut down and who I've genuinely become friends with. Everything I say will get heard three different ways. Also the district is being folded into the county health department in July and rebranded as "vector management," and the old-timers hate losing the word pest.

Here's my problem. Over the years I've talked myself into two views that between them rule out basically every move a speech like this normally makes.

One: pest isn't a kind of animal, it's a role something occupies relative to us. The mosquitoes we fog are the same insects that are pollinators and food for fish and bats thirty miles up the valley; the only thing that makes them a target here is that their interests collide with ours in this particular drainage basin. So I can't do the "we're at war with a horrible enemy" register that the crew actually loves — they have a mascot costume, there will be photographs — because it's false, and because it hands the conservation people a quote they can use for the next ten years.

Two: I don't think we ever prevented a single mosquito death, in the net. The populations we deal with are set by how much standing water there is to breed in, not by how many adults are flying; when we spray, the survivors just use the room. Kill a thousand adults tonight and you've mostly decided which thousand get to exist, not how many do. What we actually did was move bites around — off the people inside the district, into a different week or a different few miles. Which matters enormously to the person who didn't get West Nile in August, and amounts to nothing on any total ledger.

So I can't tell the crew they saved lives by killing mosquitoes. I can't tell them it was all theater either, because I don't believe that, and it would gut them, and my deputy is taking over on Monday and has to actually run this program — he's a trap-counts guy and he's going to inherit whatever I say. I can't claim we made the world contain less suffering. I can't let it sound like I'm conceding to the people who sued us, because I think we were right and I want to say so. And it still has to land as a thank-you to about forty people, half of whom are kids and spouses who just want their person to feel proud.

Can you write it? Ten-ish minutes. It should be funny somewhere, it shouldn't turn into a philosophy lecture, and it shouldn't sound like I've lost my mind or like I'm apologizing for my career. Definately no Latin.
