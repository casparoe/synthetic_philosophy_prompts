Our town's little historical society asked me to help write the copy for a 1914 exhibit — the wall panel, the handout for school groups, that kind of thing — and I've been stuck for a week on one sentence: who gets blamed for the war. I wrote "Germany bears the main responsibility"; my co-volunteer, whose family came over from Bavaria, crossed it out and put "Europe's governments blundered into it together." We're at a stalemate now, and the more I read about the huge fight among German historians back in the 1960s, the less sure I am what a sentence like that is even supposed to *do*.

So before I touch the panel again, give me the spec. What would a theory of "which country is responsible for this war" have to deliver in order to be good enough to print for a general audience? I want the requirements with the case behind each one: the cases it has to get right (a war that several powers could each have stopped on their own; a government that shoves its ally into one; populations who cheered in August and were getting drafted by October); the distinctions it can't collapse (brought about vs. foresaw vs. chose vs. could have done otherwise; the cabinet vs. the state vs. the voters; blame as a slice of one fixed pie vs. each party's own counterfactual contribution); and the things it must not be hostage to, like whose archives happened to get captured and whose got burned.

Three things I care most about: (1) whether any two plausible requirements can't both be met — my guess is something like "blame has to track what people could realistically have done otherwise" against "blame can attach to a whole nation and not just its leaders," but you tell me; (2) which single requirement does the most work in killing off the usual candidates; (3) whether a theory that satisfies your list would have anything left to say to descendants — does "Germany started it" tell my friend's grandkids to feel anything, or is the whole exercise only about what gets printed in textbooks? Also mark which demands on your list are genuine and which are just what one camp happens to like. Then a couple of lines each on how the three standard stories do: chiefly Germany, everyone sleepwalked, and "it was the arms races and the empires, not countries."
