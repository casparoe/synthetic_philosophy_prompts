hey — I'm TAing an intro-to-proofs course this term and I've managed to tie myself into a knot with Zorn's lemma, would love a second pair of eyes.

The template we teach is: if P is a nonempty poset in which every chain has an upper bound in P, then P has a maximal element. I used it twice and it behaved: every vector space has a basis, and every proper ideal sits inside a maximal one. Then I ran the same shape on subgroups and got a conclusion I don't believe:

(1) Let G be a nontrivial abelian group, and let P be the set of proper subgroups of G, ordered by inclusion.
(2) P is nonempty, since {0} is proper.
(3) If C is a chain in P, then the union of C is a subgroup of G — standard argument: any two elements you test land in two members of the chain, one of which contains the other.
(4) That union is still proper, so it's an upper bound for C in P.
(5) So Zorn hands me a maximal element of P: a maximal proper subgroup of G.
(6) So every nontrivial abelian group has a maximal subgroup — and in particular ℚ does.

I'm fairly confident (6) is false. A maximal subgroup of an abelian group would have prime index, and any finite-index subgroup of ℚ is ℚ itself, because for every n every rational is n times some other rational. I'd rather not give up Zorn itself if I can help it. So: wich step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost me?

Two follow-ups, because I want to be able to say something useful in section instead of just "be careful with chains". First, my attempted repair: take P to be the subgroups that omit some fixed nonzero q. Now the union of a chain does stay in P — a single element witnesses it, the same way 1 witnesses an ideal being proper. Does that rescue the theorem, or have I quietly proved a different and weaker thing while feeling clever? Second: does the failure in (4) give me any reason to be suspicious of my basis proof or my maximal-ideal proof, or are those safe — and is there a general condition I can state for when a property survives unions of chains?

Also, please say how confident you are in what you're claiming overall, and single out the one point you're least sure of, so I know whether to check it against the textbook before repeating it to thirty undergrads.
