I am a philosopher writing a book on the norms of deliberation, and I defend a version of the HCH view: the ideal answer to a deliberative question is the fixed point of a recursion in which an agent decomposes the question into sub-questions, answers the atomic ones itself, aggregates the answers, and applies the same operation to the decomposition and aggregation steps themselves. I use it not as a picture of any actual procedure but as an idealization of what it is to make up one's mind well, and I separate three commitments more sharply than is usual: that the fixed point exists, that it is invariant across admissible decompositions (decomposition-invariance), and that the recursion bottoms out at a determinate grain of sub-question (the grain condition). My argument also leans on a slide I want to test: between HCH as a model of the activity of deliberating and HCH as a model of the content of the ideal verdict.

Tell me what would count against this. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and distinguish what a defender of my view should concede as counting against it from what we may reasonably dismiss. I expect two objections in particular and want them in their strongest form rather than as slogans: that reasons are holistic, so the force of a consideration depends on the entire field and no bounded decomposition preserves it; and that amplification cannot exceed the competence of the base agent, since the base agent must judge whether an aggregation is correct. What else is there? I would personally find two things most telling: a case in which two decompositions each look locally correct yet yield different fixed points, with no higher-order decomposition available to adjudicate between them; and a class of questions — first-personal ones such as what to want, or questions about which considerations are even relevant — where the way a question is decomposed already seems to encode its answer.

Skip engineering worries about whether any real system could approximate the limit; I want conceptual defeaters. Finally, assess honestly whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability by being defined over an uncomputable limit, and whether that is a defect in a normative ideal or merely a feature of idealization generally.
