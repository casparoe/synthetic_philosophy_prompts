I am a PhD student in programming languages. The vocabulary I work in every day is that of modal type theory and proof assistants: necessity as a comonad together with its counit, possibility as the dual, worlds-as-evidence-stages with persistence along an accessibility relation, proof irrelevance, erasure, quoting and reflection, guarded recursion and the Löb rule, and no-go arguments of the Gödel–Löb–normalization family. I have no training in epistemology, and every modality I have used until now has ranged over states of a system rather than over what some creature could in principle come to learn.

What I want translated into that vocabulary is the knowability paradox: Fitch's argument that the verificationist thesis that every truth is knowable collapses into the claim that every truth is known. The source text, as I have reconstructed it from a colleague's notes, runs on four ingredients — the knowability principle, formalized as p → ◇Kp for arbitrary p; the factivity of knowledge, Kp → p; the distribution of knowledge over conjunction, K(p ∧ q) → Kp ∧ Kq; and the platitude that some truths are in fact unknown, ∃p(p ∧ ¬Kp). One instantiates the knowability principle at the Moorean proposition p ∧ ¬Kp, notes that if that proposition were known at some accessible stage then both Kp and K¬Kp would hold there, the latter giving ¬Kp by factivity, and concludes that the Moorean proposition is unknowable, hence (by contraposition on the knowability principle) never true, hence that every truth is known after all.

Please do the following.

First, translate the argument piece by piece: the operators K and ◇, each of the four ingredients, the formation of the Moorean proposition, the reductio, and the final generalization. I want to know which of these become natural citizens of the type theory — my guess is that factivity is exactly the counit of the necessity comonad and that distribution over conjunction is nothing more than functoriality plus projections, but I would like that confirmed or corrected — and which of them have to be forced into a shape they do not want. In particular, say what the Moorean proposition is as a type. It mentions the very operator that is applied to it; under a predicative stratification, does it even typecheck, and if it does not, is that an artifact of my framework or the real content of the paradox?

Second, translate the standard escape routes, which I will describe structurally so that you need not track who proposed what: (a) restricting the knowability schema to a class R of propositions, where the difficulty is that R has to contain the ordinary empirical propositions the verificationist cares about while also being closed under the operation p ↦ p ∧ ¬Kp if the argument is to be re-run inside R; (b) denying that knowledge distributes over conjunction, in the way that tracking-style accounts deny closure; (c) reinterpreting the possibility operator so that knowability does not require an accessible stage at which anyone relevantly like us does the knowing, distinguishing idealized from actual cognitive reach; (d) letting the Moorean proposition be a gap or a glut rather than a contradiction. For each, tell me the counterpart in my vocabulary — I suspect (a) is something like restricting an eliminator to a stratified universe and (b) is something like refusing to make □ a functor, but I want to know whether those suspicions are right, and I especially want to know whether any of these moves is available for free in the type theory in a way that would make it look more principled than it is.

Third, give me the residue report. Which claims survive translation exactly, which survive only after being bent, and which have no expression at all? I can name two candidates I expect to be in the third category, and I want your verdict on them and on any others you find: the fact that a proof assistant's notion of verification presupposes a single shared, decidable checker, so that nothing in my framework corresponds to an epistemic subject with finite reach; and the fact that identifying truth with inhabitation would hand the verificationist their conclusion at the start. More generally, mark every place where the translation would smuggle in a target-framework assumption and present it as translation.

Fourth, three checks I care about, because I have a use for the answer if it is positive. My own derivation, done informally last night, seems to deliver only p → ¬¬Kp when the metatheory is constructive, so that the collapse to p → Kp depends on double-negation elimination somewhere; is that right, and if so, exactly where does classical reasoning enter? Does the argument belong to the same family as Löb's theorem — a fixed-point or diagonal phenomenon — or is that a false friend, and what is the minimal structural package (counit, functoriality, normality, monotonicity of the stage ordering, classical metatheory) under which a Fitch-style collapse is provable, stated tightly enough that I could formalize it as a general no-go theorem about a comonad on a category of evidence states rather than about knowledge? And does the restriction strategy (a) amount to universe stratification, in which case does the paradox simply reappear one level up if the universe is impredicative?

Please state explicitly any assumption you make about the normality of K, the duality of ◇, and the classical or constructive character of the metatheory, rather than assuming I know the house conventions; and please do not spend anything on references or a literature survey. What I need is the mapping, its failures, and a candid verdict on whether the residue indicts my framework or the original argument.
