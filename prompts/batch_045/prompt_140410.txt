I chair the design-review board at a mid-sized software company. Roughly once a week we route a proposed LLM-based feature down one of two paths: the tool path (every irreversible action authorized by a named human, full logging, no autonomy) or the agent path (red-team evaluation, staged rollout, kill switch, quarterly audit). The routing is currently done by intuition and then argued about, and the stakes are real in both directions: the agent path costs us about six weeks of engineering time, while under-routing is how we end up in the incident channel. I want a written procedure a reviewer can run in under an hour from the spec alone, with no access to model internals and no ability to execute the system.

The axes I have been trying to use are *borrowed initiative* — delete the user's instruction and is there anything the system is still in the middle of? — and *carried purpose* — across steps it takes unprompted, does it maintain a representation of its own unfinished business, such that interrupting it would frustrate something it was trying to do that nobody asked it to do on that turn? I do not fully trust either one: a fixed-goal planner with no reach outside a sandbox looks like it has carried purpose yet can do nothing unsanctioned, while a single function call that emails a thousand customers looks like a tool and carries all of the irreversible consequences. Drop them if they are the wrong cuts.

What I want: (1) the procedure itself — questions in order, what counts as an affirmative answer, where it terminates in a no, and where it hands the decision to a person because what remains is a question of values rather than fact; (2) which of my distinctions I am wrong to insist on and which I am wrong to discard; (3) the procedure applied to three proposals: a coding assistant that edits a repository, runs the test suite in a sandbox, and loops up to ten times before presenting a diff; a support bot with retrieval over our policy corpus and authority to issue refunds under fifty dollars with no human in the loop; and a "deep research" feature that browses, writes a memo with citations, and can otherwise only write to a scratch directory. Give me at least one case where the procedure returns an answer my team will argue with, and state plainly what the procedure cannot settle.
