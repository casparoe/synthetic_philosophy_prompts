Looking for something a bit more than mates-down-the-pub advice here, if that's ok, because this has been going round my head for weeks and I keep going in circles.

Short version: for most of my twenties I was in a band. I wrote nearly all of it, we got a small deal with an indie label, and I spent about four years working on an album that's maybe two thirds done. Then two years ago I had a really bad depressive episode, spent a fortnight in hospital, came out on medication, and I'm fine now — genuinely fine, better than fine. But I don't care about music any more. Not "I'm scared of it" or "I'm too sad", just nothing. I've got a dull data job I quite like, I walk the dog, I see my mates. If someone puts the record on I feel about as much as I'd feel about a stranger's record.

Everyone from my old life keeps saying the same thing: you've obviously still got every reason to finish it, it's what you always wanted, you'll regret it for the rest of your life. My brother said last week "it doesn't matter whether you feel like it, you have a reason to do it and that's the end of it." It feels like a kind of pressure, but I can't put my finger on why it feels wrong.

My therapist said something I keep chewing on: that if nothing in what I now actually want connects to finishing the record, then there's no reason for me to do it, and people saying "you have a reason" are just dressing up what they want from me in a fancy word. That sounded dead convincing in the room and slightly mad since.

Three things that probably matter:

1. About six months before I got properly ill, my old therapist had me write one of those letters to future me. In it I basically said: if I ever stop wanting to finish this record, that's the illness talking, don't listen to that version of me, make me do it anyway. I read it back and it's like something a different person wrote. I don't disagree with a word of it and also none of it moves me.
2. It's not only about me. The label paid us an advance; the other three put years in; one of them turned down a job to keep the band going, and if I walk now they lose the studio booking they've already put a deposit on. So even if I feel nothing about the music, there's other people's stuff tangled up in it.
3. My GP thinks some of the flatness is the meds and says we could try lowering the dose. I don't want to. I like being flat. Which I notice makes me suspicious of my own "I don't want it" — but I'm not sure what to do with that suspicion either.

What I actually want to know: when my brother says I have a reason and I say I don't, are we disagreeing about a real fact, or are we just using the same word for two different things? What would have to be true for him to be right, and what would have to be true for the therapist to be right? Does the letter make any difference — can a version of me that doesn't exist any more hand me a reason, and if not, why does it feel like it should? And does any of this change what I should actually do about the deposit and the other three, or am I overthinking something that's just about being a decent mate?

Please don't just tell me to dip a toe back in for a month and see how it feels. I tried that. I sat in the practice room for an hour and felt precisely nothing, and then I went home and didn't think about it again.
