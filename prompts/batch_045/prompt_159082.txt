I'm a political theorist in the later stages of a dissertation on the ethics of memory, and I'm using Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind (Gondry/Kaufman, 2004) as the opening case in a chapter that also serves as a graduate seminar session. I'd like a close reading of the film through two lenses that are rarely put together: Avishai Margalit's The Ethics of Memory and Philip Rieff's The Triumph of the Therapeutic. I have the bibliography; I don't need citations, further reading, or plot summary. I need the argument.

Roughly the frame I'm working from: Margalit grounds the duty to remember in care rather than in truth or in the intrinsic claims of the past — what is owed is owed to persons with whom one stands in a thick relation, and his emblematic case is the officer who cannot recall the name of a soldier under his command, where the failure is a failure of caring rather than of record-keeping. Rieff describes the reverse cultural movement: a therapeutic culture in which the vocabulary of good and evil is displaced by that of health and sickness, and memory becomes an instrument for release from the past rather than a bond to it. On that pairing the film should be a Rieffian artefact — Lacuna, Inc. literally sells therapy by erasure — but I don't think it settles there, and I want to know where it does and doesn't.

Two distinctions I'm considering introducing, which I'd like you to test rather than adopt:

First, mnemonic euthanasia versus mnemonic excommunication. Ending a memory for the sake of the person who suffers from it is one thing; removing someone from the community of memory one carries is another. My suspicion is that on Margalit's care-based account the wrong-making feature of forgetting is closer to excommunication than to falsification: what Joel and Clementine lose is not a true record but a standing — each is dropped out of the other's ongoing "having in mind." If that is right, then the therapeutic defence of erasure (it relieves suffering) answers the wrong charge, and the film's willingness to treat the procedure as a consumer service is itself the moral datum.

Second, what I call the exographic residue. Nothing in the film is forgotten in the strict sense: Lacuna keeps files, the tapes circulate, Joel and Clementine re-encounter each other and their own dispositions survive the erasure, and Mary's earlier procedure is effaced only from her. So the procedure may be better described as a relocation of memory into artefacts and other people than as its destruction. That raises a question I can't settle: if the record is preserved in the community while the person is internally emptied, has a Margalitian duty been met in some thinner sense while being violated second-personally? And does the asymmetry matter — Clementine's consent is given in acute distress, Mary's erasure is imposed by the man who has authority over her — so that the duty falls hardest where the relation of dependency is greatest?

I want you to anchor the reading in particular scenes: the erasure nights, Patrick's reuse of Joel's memories as seduction material, Mary's collapse and the tape she is handed, and the ending, where the couple listen to their own damning recordings and begin again anyway. On that ending I want a verdict, not a hedge: does it vindicate the care-based view (what matters is not the accurate record but continuing care), or the therapeutic one (a second chance is worth a repeat of the injury)? Where does the film resist the reading — as romance, as farce, as a story about self-narration rather than memory — and where would an honest critic say the philosophical frame is being imposed?

Finally, and importantly for a chapter that will be read by people who work on this: separate what is settled in the literature from what is genuinely contested. I take it to be contested whether Margalit's care-based account can generate duties to remember events or strangers at all, and how much of Rieff's contrast between cultures of commitment and therapeutic culture is a defensible description rather than a decline narrative with thin empirical footing. Tell me which moves in my reading rest on those contested claims, and which of my distinctions are my own coinage rather than the field's machinery — I would rather cut a clever section than present a homemade concept as received doctrine.
