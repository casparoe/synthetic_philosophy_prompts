But devs switch it on mostly for the ugly tickets — cross-service refactors, legacy modules with no tests — and once you cut the data by task difficulty (rated at triage, before anyone starts work), assistant-on beats assistant-off inside every one of the five difficulty bands. Textbook reversal: difficulty is correlated both with whether the assistant gets used and with whether the PR merges.

(3) Priya: that's a different question, and its a real one, but 'merged rate' is the wrong statistic for it. The denominator grows when people attempt more stuff, so the rate can fall while merged PRs per dev rises. On counts, assistant-heavy months come out ahead.

Also the triage rating is coarse, two of the bands have like forty PRs in them, and you are reading a lot into five noisy subgroup estimates. Direction-consistent, sure, but I don't trust any magnitude that comes out of it.

(6) Marco: the mechanism also predicts people reach for it on tickets where a bad merge is expensive, and your outcome variable collapses 'reviewer asked for changes', 'author abandoned it' and 'reverted in prod' into one failure. Those aren't the same size of bad.
