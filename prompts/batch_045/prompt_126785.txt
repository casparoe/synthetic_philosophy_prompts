So I've been reading the rationalist blogs since basically forever and I still can't referee an argument two friends of mine got into after a board game night, so I'm outsourcing it.

Setup: my friend Dev is quitting his job to build a niche B2B SaaS thing solo. He figures 8 months to first paying customers and says it's 60% it works out. My other friend Mira is a hard outside-view person and the two of them went at it for like forty minutes. Roughly:

(1) Mira: don't use your own model, use the reference class "solo first-time founder software projects" — median time to anything shippable is way more then 8 months and most of them never get a paying customer at all. Your 60% is a planning-fallacy number with a confidence interval welded onto it.

(2) Dev: that class is doing no work, it lumps me in with people who are teaching themselves to code. I've got twelve years in the industry, I've shipped in this exact domain before, and I have a 9k-person mailing list from a newsletter I've run for years. The relevant class is "senior engineers with distribution and domain experience building in a market they've already worked in," and that class looks a lot better. Also, I said 60%, not 100 — I already took a haircut.

(3) Mira: that's reference class gerrymandering. You picked the narrowing features after looking at your own case, and every founder can do exactly this, so the class always collapses into "people circumstanced like me," which is the inside view with extra steps. If narrowing is free, the outside view has zero bite.

(4) Dev: it isn't free, but if it's banned then the outside view is just conservatism with a spreadsheet attached. Every successful thing was, ex ante, a member of a class with a bad base rate. And worse: the outside view isn't closed under universal adoption. If everyone actually deferred to the broad class, the population of attempted projects would change — the ones that still get attempted would be a different set, selected for ignoring exactly the number you measured — so the base rate you're quoting wouldn't describe the world you'd be acting in. The number eats itself.

(5) Mira: fine, but you're conflating two things people mean by "outside view." One is correcting for a corrupted estimator — your optimism, your availability, the fact that you've already sunk two months into building. The other is a claim about which evidence to use. Those have different remedies, and the first one says debias your own model, not replace it with a class average. But on the second: the burden is on the narrow class to show the narrowing feature actually tracks outcomes, and "I feel like my experience is relevant" isn't that. Show me that newsletters predict shipping.

(6) Dev: I can't, and that's structural, not laziness. The classes anybody has numbers for are the ones some institution already bothered to count — broad, old, legible ones. Call it the census effect. So the outside view doesn't drag you toward the truth, it drags you toward whatever was cheap to tabulate, and it gets to look modest and rigorous the whole time it's doing it.

(7) Mira: okay, but then what, we go back to vibes? And honestly this whole argument is beside the point, because the outside view is about your probability, not your decision. Fat-tailed payoff — you don't need 60% for quitting to be right. What the outside view changes is the shape of the attempt: keep the job two days a week, precommit to a kill criterion at month twelve, sell before you build. Dev: but if it doesn't change whether I go, and my inside number was already above what I needed, then what exactly did it do?

I want a verdict on each of (1) through (7), somewhere on a scale from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with a sentence or two on why, then who is more right overall and what both of them are missing. Please actually pick — I've watched this same fight play out on the forums a dozen times and it always dissolves into "both sides have a point, reference classes are hard," which is useless to me. If one of them is equivocating somewhere, say exactly where. And don't tell me to go read somebody, I've read the blog posts; I want the object-level argument.

Bonus if you want it: is there any principled way to choose a reference class, or is the honest answer that you have to use your inside view to pick the class and then your outside view to set the number — in which case Mira's whole thing is just the inside view wearing a lab coat?
