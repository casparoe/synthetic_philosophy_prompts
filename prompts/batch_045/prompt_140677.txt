Hey — I'm a PhD student in religion here (I work on Jain and Buddhist epistemology; my advisor is basically the department's Jain person), and every August our grad association puts out a satirical Survival Handbook for the incoming cohort. Faculty read it, which is either the point or the problem. I want the centerpiece this year to be a parody document and I've been staring at a blank page for a week, so: write it for me.

The setup, which is unfortunately almost real. Last spring, after a genuinely ugly fight over a departmental statement, our Faculty Senate passed a Non-Absolutism in Official Communications policy (NOC-3, drafted with a consultant), and the new Office of Institutional Pluralism has to issue guidance for implementing it. The policy says all official assertions must be non-one-sided, and it applies to letters of recommendation, search-committee reports, program-review self-studies, the annual climate-survey summary, and — this is the part I care about — anything sent to the external auditor.

So: write the Office's internal guidance document, maybe 700-800 words, deadpan admin voice, numbered sections, with worked examples. Include at minimum a sevenfold-compliant letter of recommendation for a job candidate, a search committee's attempt to rank three candidates, and a reply to an auditor's yes/no question about whether the budget is balanced.

The jokes have to come from getting the doctrine right rather than from a cartoon version of it. So: syāt as a standpoint-indexed qualifier — in some respect, from this viewpoint — not maybe; a naya as a partial but not thereby false take, where the actual error is treating one standpoint as the whole (ekānta), not the having of a standpoint; the seven modes of predication including the one where the thing has to be called inexpressible; the point that the full view isn't sayable in language at all; and the fact that the pluralism / intellectual-ahimsā reading the consultants are selling is itself a modern revision rather than the core claim.

Two things I care about. First, the target is the administrators, not the doctrine — the Office's guidance should be a recognizable misreading (qualified turned into hedged, standpoint-relativity turned into a liability shield that lets you launder a claim you know is false), and the piece should leave room for the fact that the actual view does license determinate assertion within a standpoint and isn't a skepticism. My advisor will read this and I'd like her to laugh rather than wince. Please don't have anyone say that Jains think contradictions can be true, or that everything is true — that's precisely the misreading. Second, let the reductio build: an institution that takes many-sidedness as a communications policy can't rank, can't certify, can't fire anyone, and the guidance should keep patching that gap and failing, ending on some compliance certification that cannot be signed. Let the self-reference problem show up — is the guidance itself only relatively true, and is mandating non-absolutism itself one-sided — but don't resolve it and don't have a character explain it.

No winking, no one saying wow this is absurd. It should still be legible to a first-year who's never heard of any of this, so gloss the terms once in passing and move on.
