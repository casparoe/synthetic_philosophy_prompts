hey — I'm putting together a nomic variant to run on a small discord server, maybe 12 people, one round a day for a month. If you havent met nomic: it's the game where the rules are just a numbered list, players take turns proposing changes (add / repeal / amend), changes pass by majority vote, and the twist is that the rules about how to change the rules are themselves ordinary changeable rules. A couple of them start out immutable. I want the interesting part to be the rule layer itself, so assume there's some boring fixed win condition bolted on and nobody's trying to just end the game early.

I've sketched six candidate amendment architectures and I can't decide between them. Assume players are competitive, read carefully, and two of them are the kind of person who enjoys finding loopholes.

1. Ratify-under-successor: every rule-change is voted on twice — once under the current rules, then again under the rules as they would be after the change takes effect. Repealing the double-voting rule is itself subject to double voting.

2. Quorum echo: quorum for a rule-change vote equals the number of people who voted on the previous rule-change, plus one. Abstaining counts as not voting. This rule is changeable like any other.

3. Self-mention void: any rule whose text contains its own rule number is void on enactment. This rule is rule 4.

4. Entrenchment ladder: rules can be tagged entrenched (needs two-thirds) or double-entrenched (needs unanimity). The rule that says how tagging works is itself untagged and changeable by plain majority.

5. Precedent lock: the first time a kind of rule-change passes, the procedure used to pass it (notice period, majority, who may propose) becomes binding for all future changes of that kind. What counts as "the same kind" is asserted by whoever proposes the change and approved by majority at the time.

6. Imported authority: by majority vote the game can import some outside document — e.g. a real parliamentary-procedure manual, or the server's existing mod policy — as binding on procedure. Once imported, amending it requires that document's own amendment procedure, which no player is in a position to run.

What I want: sort these into tiers by roughly how many rounds of play you'd expect before the game degenerates, where degenerating means either it locks up (no legal rule-change is possible anymore, or every proposal fails for structural reasons) or it goes molten (anything can be made legal, so the rule layer stops constraining anyone). Give rough round numbers per tier. Then rate each one 0–10 on two separate things: how much genuine strategy the rule layer creates, and how resistant it is to a three-person faction quietly seizing control without ever breaking a rule. One sentence of justification each. Finally, if you had to ship two of them together, which two, and what do they do to each other — I have a feeling some pairs are way worse than either alone.
