I've been reading LW/OB since basically forever and I keep failing to get traction on one specific disagreement, so: please actually compare these two things rather than listing pros and cons of each in turn.

Thing 1: coherent extrapolated volition — the CEV cluster, where you take a person (or humanity) as input and apply a bundle of improvement operators: more knowledge, more thinking time and speed, becoming more the person they'd wish they were, having grown up closer together with everyone else — and then take the coherent fixed point of all that as the thing to be optimized.

Thing 2: what shows up in analytic moral psychology under the label of idealized preferences — strip out factual error, instrumental irrationality, maybe akrasia and the motivational effects of depression, but hold final ends as fixed as you possibly can. Call it IP for short.

I know the standard background: value is complicated and fragile, orthogonality, you can't hand-code human values, the AI is not your mom. Don't re-explain any of that. And no citations, bibliographies or reading lists — I can't look anything up anyway, so I only want the argument.

Run the comparison on one axis, because I think that's where they genuinely come apart: is the idealization procedure allowed to be *transformative* of final ends, or only *corrective* of belief and reasoning? IP is officially corrective-only. CEV also wants to be corrective-only, but I don't think it can pull that off, and here's where I keep getting stuck. For each item, tell me which of the two handles it better, and whether the difference is principled or just a difference in how much the author trusted the procedure.

1. Order-dependence. The CEV operators don't commute: knowing more *before* you become more the person you wish you were can land you somewhere quite different from knowing more *after*. So "our extrapolated volition" has to be a fixed point of a process whose order is doing a lot of unnoticed work. Does IP have the same problem, or does holding final ends fixed buy it immunity? And if a fixed point doesn't exist, or there are several, what's each view supposed to do?

2. The horror asymmetry. Dana is 34 and has wanted kids since she was fifteen. Her IP counterpart — same final ends, full information about the costs and about her own motives — decides not to have them and doesn't regret it. We're all comfortable saying "then that's what she really wants." Now flip it: Dana's *CEV* counterpart ends up wanting something Dana currently finds abhorent, and would not thank you for undoing it. Suddenly "you'd endorse it later" reads as a threat rather than a reason. Is there a real asymmetry here, or do we call it extrapolation when we like the destination and coercion when we don't? Which framework has the resources to say "this particular extrapolation went too far" without already smuggling the answer in?

3. Orphaned volition. If the operators are strong enough — especially the grown-up-closer-together clause, which is doing merger work *across* people — the output may be continuous with nobody in particular. A coherent volition, sure, but not anyone's. Is that a bug, or is it secretly the point? My suspicion is that the interpersonal aggregation bit was always doing more work than the within-person extrapolation bit, and that CEV's appeal comes from sliding between them. But if that's right, in what sense is the result still *ours*, which is the whole selling point?

4. Refusing the procedure. Dana can also say in advance: I don't consent to being improved, I want to be the one who decides, and if the only way to give me what I want is to replace me with somebody who wants something else, count me out. Both views look bad at handling this, but for different reasons — IP because it can't hear the refusal as anything other than one more preference to be corrected, CEV because, well, I'm honestly not sure, and that's part of what I want you to work out.

Take a side at the end: if you were specifying the target for a value-learning system and could hand it only one of these two, which one, and why?
