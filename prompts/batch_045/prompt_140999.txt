so this is a weird ask but bear with me. I work on the platform side at a mid-size cloud company, and about six weeks ago legal decided that any training run or inference endpoint we host above a certain size has to have a "sentience attribution statement" filed alongside it — a form saying, roughly, how many morally relevant computations the workload instantiates and what we're doing about their welfare. The policy behind it is honestly not stupid on its face. It says three things: moral patiency tracks causal-functional organisation rather than substrate; two workloads with the same organisation count once, not twice; and anything under a stated complexity threshold is presumed not to matter.

I've been handed the job of writing the internal FAQ that our solutions engineers will copy-paste into customer answers, and I've realised that if I write it dead straight and just follow the policy wherever it actually leads, the document becomes a reductio of the whole exercise. I want help making it a *good* reductio rather than a cheap one.

What I want: an internal helpdesk FAQ, "Attribution Statements: Answers to Common Questions", around 700-900 words, maybe eight numbered Q&As plus one worked example at the end. Voice is cheerful compliance bureaucratese. The target is the practice of turning that policy into an auditable number, not the metaphysics — I don't think the underlying functionalist idea is dumb, and I don't want the piece to read like it does. The joke should be that a view about *which* computations matter gives you no grip at all on the counting, thresholding and reporting questions the compliance process needs answered, so the gaps get filled by conventions, and the conventions immediately become arbitrage.

Things I want handled correctly, and explained in the document rather than assumed, since our engineers won't have read any of this:

- The lookup-table case, where you keep the exact input-output behaviour of a system but throw away the internal causal structure the policy cares about. The straight-faced consequence is that "how do I reduce my welfare obligations" has the answer "reimplement, don't change what it does", and the FAQ should offer that as a tip.
- The fact that which computation a physical system instantiates depends partly on what mapping or description you use. Our guidance should instruct engineers to file under the mapping that minimises the count, and mention offhandedly that the marketing team uses a different one. A reader should be able to see that this is the same freedom that makes the number arbitrary.
- Counting: whether two identical copies are one patient or two, what happens when you batch a few dozen sequences through one forward pass, and whether the candidate parameter settings an optimizer considers during a single step count — they look counterfactual — while the parallel candidates a decoder keeps alive at inference time somehow do.
- The lower threshold: the sorites problem, and customers sizing their models to sit just beneath it. The FAQ should be entirely unbothered by this.
- The bit where even after you've settled *whether*, the form also asks *how much*, and there is no unit and no measure available; let the guidance invent one and quietly concede it's a convention.

Constraints: no citations, no bibliographies, no named philosophers or papers, invented internal jargon and product names very welcome. It has to stay legible to a smart non-specialist and it can't wink — no paragraph where the author shows up to tell you this is all absurd. The last item should land it: I was thinking a question about whether the FAQ itself was produced by a morally relevant computation, but if you have something better, use it.
