ok so I've just spent two hours in the kitchen arguing with my flatmate about vagueness and I need to get it out of my system. She's a supervaluationist, I was making the epistemicist noises — there's got to be *some* exact number of grains where a pile goes from not-a-heap to a heap, we just can never find out which — and it ended with us agreeing that whichever of us is right, some poor civil servant is eventually going to have to write the form.

So: write the form.

Guidance Note 17 (Third Revision): Designation of Heaps, Baldness, and Other Statutory Predicates. A leaked internal guidance note from the Office of Admissible Sharpenings, a bureau in a country that has officially adopted supervaluationism as its doctrine of vague language, while a small heretical faction of epistemicists passes notes to each other in the margins. Around 650–750 words, numbered clauses, with a short Definitions section at the top, in deadpan civil-service register. Don't name any real philosophers — keep it in-universe.

The jokes have to come from actually getting the theory right, so that someone who knows the debate laughs instead of winces. Things I want smuggled in as bureaucratic procedure:

- supertruth means truth under every admissible sharpening, so a claim can be true without being supertrue, and a penumbral claim gets no truth value at all — I want a clause where an officer is told a pile is neither a heap nor not a heap, and then disciplined for writing either one down
- the lovely bit where the Office can happily affirm that a threshold exists while being unable to name it: it's supertrue that some number is the cut-off, but for every candidate number n, "n is the cut-off" falls in a gap. So there's a clause confirming the existence of the threshold, and immediately after it a clause forbidding officers from ever introducing a name for it in reasoning — no existential instantiation, on pain of dismissal
- higher-order vagueness: borderline cases of borderline cases, an Office of Second-Order Admissibility, a sub-clause on sharpenings of sharpenings, and the hint of a regress that the note keeps deferring to a future revision
- the practical payoff of all this: since almost nothing is heaped on *every* admissible sharpening, the Bureau can only certify absurdly huge piles as determinately heaps; small piles are uncertifiable; and the Bureau's own operating budget is listed as "not determinately appropriated" or fines are levied in penumbral currency — whatever deadpan version you like

Then give me at least three leaked marginal notes from an epistemicist clerk: she believes the true number is written on a card in the Deputy Director's safe, that looking at a pile one grain either side of it would give you the same belief while it was false (so no amount of looking could ever make that belief safe), and that you can never tell whether you're in the penumbra. Keep her consistent — she thinks there's a sharp fact, she just thinks it's necessarily unknowable, and she should never accidentally talk as if the fact were fuzzy.

The whole thing should stay internally consistent with official supervaluationist doctrine: no clause should quietly sneak in a sharp boundary the Office denies. And it should still be legible to my flatmate's housemate who's never heard of any of this — the nightmare should come through as a nightmare about forms, not as jargon.
