I'm working on something and I need a satirical document for it: about 700 words, laid out as numbered sections of an internal compliance memo, from the Office of Experiential Ethics at a big particle physics lab, set a few years after pan-sentience has become regulatory orthodoxy — the view that the intrinsic nature of physical stuff includes felt experience that comes with a valence — and got written into the grant conditions. The memo itself is a routine pre-approval review of the next collider run. Voice is deadpan throughout: nobody in it should ever act surprised. The funny part has to come from following the metaphysics into paperwork, not from the paperwork alone.

Its fine to set it up however you like, but I want it to land these four things without ever naming them as objections:

- The combination problem should show up as a jurisdictional dispute: does the experience belonging to a proton count separately from that of the composite system it's part of, or does it get absorbed into the bigger subject? The board needs a rule, doesn't have one, and so invents an administrative one. The arbitrariness of that rule is the joke, and it should be the kind of arbitrariness someone who actually knows the literature would recognize as tracking the real debate.
- The measurement problem should be visibly unfixable. The usual motivation for the view is that physics only ever hands us relational/structural properties, so there's no instrument here, no behavioural or functional signature, nothing to calibrate a valence reading against. The board wants numbers. It cannot have numbers. Have it cope.
- Causal closure: if micro-experiences do no causal work, then nothing the lab does or doesn't do makes anyone's suffering worse or better, and the whole review is purely expressive. Let the board nearly notice this, file it under a subheading, and move on to item 4.
- The hedonic arithmetic going wrong in both directions at once — an enormous number of extremely simple experiences versus a small number of very intense ones, with no way to add them up and no agreed unit. The committee should get stuck on whether a vast faint valence sums to more than one sharp one.

End it where a modest proposal should end: the board can't recommend not doing physics, so it recommends the run proceed, reclassifies something banal (the beam dump, the cooling system) as the site of a mass-casualty event, and routes the actual question into a funded research program. Also I'd like one clause, filed and unremarked, making clear the board's remit has no principled stopping point.

Then a short P.S., 100 words max: tell me where the memo leans on a contested reading of pan-sentience and where it just exaggerates, and commit to whether each liberty is fair or a cheap shot — I'd rather it be a fair piece that a defender of the view would half-agree with than an easy one, but if you think a bit is a strawman, say so and say what you'd do instead.
