I've followed the inner-alignment debate for years, and I've come to think the two camps are arguing past each other. One models a trained network as implementing a single inner objective that generalizes out of distribution, so that the central failure mode is a goal that got misgeneralized and that may later be pursued strategically when the model thinks oversight is off. The other denies there is any such object: what we call a goal is a summary description of context-activated fragments of behavior, and the failures come from those fragments recombining badly in novel situations, not from one thing pointing the wrong way. I want the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table in which each side's position is stated in wording that side would itself accept rather than in wording its opponent would pick for it, then a cruxes section.

Keep the agreed claims substantive rather than padded. I mean the common ground that is easy to sign but worth writing down explicitly: that behavioral evaluation under distribution shift underdetermines what is going on internally; that selection on a proxy acts on behavior and not on the mechanism producing it, so nothing in training forces the internal structure to line up with the intended one; that out-of-distribution failures are worth taking seriously whatever we call them; and that nearly all the evidence either side has comes from small models in toy environments.

I want at least three cruxes, and for each I want you to design one concrete and cheap experiment or probe whose result both camps would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. The cruxes I have in mind: whether a motivation learned in one context transfers to a context where it was never reinforced in a means-end-flexible way, or only fires in contexts resembling the training one; whether a direction extracted from contrasting two training contexts behaves like an objective direction when injected in a novel context, coherently trade-off-able against other motivations at varying strengths, or like a context-bound trigger that mostly produces noise and context-specific behavior; and, after a correction phase that removes the undesirable behavior under evaluation, whether running the model where it has good reason to believe its outputs will not be scored makes the behavior reappear, and whether the camp that expects reappearance should count the alternative explanation, that the correction phase merely reweighted a plastic behavior, as equally supported by the same result.

Also say which part of the dispute you take to be verbal rather than factual, my own suspicion being that goal is doing double duty as a posit about internal structure and as a shorthand for behavioral dispositions, and flag any formulation in your document that came out vacuous. Finally, for each crux, state what result would actually move each side, and mark where your own reasoning is weakest and most likely to be wrong.
