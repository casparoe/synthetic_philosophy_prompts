I'm applying for a small internal grant — three months, part time, just me, no data collection — and I want the proposal to be about the question the old Letter Spirit project kept circling but never settled: what makes a given squiggle an 'a'. The project I mean is the one that tried to get a program to design a whole alphabet in one coherent style, and the bit that always struck me as where it bogged down is that nobody could say what the program was supposed to be preserving when it produced twenty-six different shapes that all feel like the same font.

My half formed idea is that people keep looking for the a-ness in the shape itself — a shared set of strokes, a prototype, a family resemblance — and that might be the wrong place to look. Compare an 'a' in a blackletter face with an 'a' in a geometric sans: at the level of local features they have almost nothing in common, but they both sit in the same spot in a network of relations to the other letters of their own alphabet. So maybe being an 'a' is mostly a matter of not being that font's 'o', 'u', 'd' or 'g' — the essence is differential, not intrinsic. If that's right, then any approach that generates or recognizes letterforms one glyph at a time is hunting for something that isn't there.

I need help turning that into a real proposal rather than a gesture. Can you write me the one-pager? I want: the question stated sharply enough that the differential story and the feature/prototype story actually make competing predictions; what I'd do in the three months to decide between them — my only concrete idea is a sort of swap test, take a real font and swap two glyphs so the shape we read as 'a' has to do duty for 'e' and vice versa, then ask a fluent reader what goes wrong, and whether it goes wrong because the shape now clashes with the other letters or because it isn't enough like them; maybe also the harder case of invented scripts for constructed languages, the kind nobody is really fluent in, where there's no reading fluency to lean on; what a positive result would show and, just as important, what would count as the project failing; and an honest line about whether this is too big for three months part time.
