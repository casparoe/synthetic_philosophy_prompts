ok so I'm trying to work out what I actually think counts as art, and my plan is to just go with my gut on a pile of cases and see whether anything consistent comes out. Could you write your answer in British English please.

Where I land, case by case:

- The sand pattern a male pufferfish builds on the sea floor — the ridges, the shells he arranges, weeks of work — to get a female to mate with him: not art. I'll happily call it beautiful, but not art.
- A diver's photograph of that pattern. Uncropped, natural light, no editing, she just found it and shot it: art.
- A chess *problem* composed for a magazine — a position that could never come up in a real game, made so that the solution is elegant: art, definitively.
- A game from a real tournament that chess writers describe in exactly the same words (a beautiful game, the ending is lovely), where neither player was trying to make anything beautiful: not art. This is the pair I keep coming back to, because I can't point to a single difference in the objects.
- A corporate logo I think is gorgeous, by a designer who cared enormously how it looked: not art, and it isn't close.
- My grandmother's dumpling recipe, adjusted over forty years partly for how the finished plate looks: not art.
- A plate of food at a tasting-menu restaurant, arranged with tweezers, photographed by every customer before they eat: I want to say not art, but I'm much less confident here than I am about the logo and I dont know why.
- A 40-minute recording of rain on a patio that a man made to help him sleep, never played for anyone, and deleted: not art.
- The identical file, recovered by his sister, submitted to a sound-art festival after he died and played to an audience in a dark room with a programme note: art.
- A script someone wrote that sets off the car alarms in a multi-storey car park in a sequence. He thinks of it as a piece, he's never performed it, nobody has heard it: art, I think — which seems to flatly contradict the patio recording.
- A nonsense rhyme I made up on the spot to make my niece laugh, never written down, forgotten by tomorrow: not art.
- The same kind of rhyme if I'd written it in a notebook alongside other poems and shown it to people: art.
- A spice jar of beach sand my kid put on the mantelpiece and announced was "a sculpture of the sea": I want to say no, but I said yes to the pufferfish photo and I can't see what separates them.

Two I genuinely go back and forth on: my uncle's model railway in his attic — thirty years, insanely detailed, has never once shown it to anyone and gets irritable if anyone asks about it; and 4,000 photos of the same doorway taken daily for eleven years by a security guard who just liked doing it and never told a soul.

My own rough guess at a principle: something is art if it was made by somebody with the intention that it be attended to in the way things belonging to some existing art kind are attended to, whether or not anyone actually does attend to it. That seems to handle the car alarms and the notebook poem but it looks like it also makes the logo art and the pufferfish photo not art, so I suspect it's wrong or at least badly worded.

What I want from you: a principle, or two or three candidates if no single one does the job, stated precisely enough that I could apply it myself — with the case that forces each clause to be in there. Then: what it says about cases I haven't decided (give me at least three of your own: a suspension bridge, a slowed-down birdsong recording, a church service, whatever you like), what it says about the model railway and the doorway photos, and an honest list of which of my verdicts above it cannot accommodate and which one you'd overturn. Also: do I have to give up on "art" being yes-or-no? I have clearly graded reactions — definitely, barely, not close — and I'd like to know whether a good theory should output degrees, and if it does, whether that collapses the chess pair or makes it worse. If your principle has to treat one of my "not art" cases as art in order to be simple, say so plainly rather than quietly reinterpreting it.

thanks!
