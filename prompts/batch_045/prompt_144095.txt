Hey, so I'm stuck on this genuinely ridiculous committee at my university — we're choosing what to name the new interdisciplinary building, and we've now spent three meetings going in circles about how to aggregate everybody's ranked preferences without anyone being able to complain afterward. Last week someone finally said "isn't this just Arrow's theorem" and I've been volunteered to write something up for the committee newsletter explaining why we're deadlocked. I'd much rather do it as a joke than as a memo.

So: write me the minutes of the meeting — say 700 to 900 words, deadpan bureaucratic register, full solemnity, standard headers (Present, Apologies for Absence, Old Business, New Business, Any Other Business, Adjournment). Setting: the Ad Hoc Committee on Naming, seven members, trying to adopt a voting rule that satisfies the four conditions they drafted at the previous meeting, which I want stated properly in the minutes: every member's ranking of the shortlist has to be admissible; if everyone ranks A above B then the committee ranking does too; the committee's ranking of A versus B may depend only on how members ranked A against B; and there is no single member who always gets their way. Include one footnote getting the last of those right — a dictator in the theorem's sense is someone whose strict preference over any pair becomes the group's, not merely someone influential, and not someone with a veto. Then have the work through the real escape routes and find a bureaucratic reason to reject each one: restrict the domain to single-peaked preferences along some axis (someone should propose a subcommittee charged with making the faculty single-peaked, and it should be referred, not laughed off), abandon independence of irrelevant alternatives for a Borda-style point system, move to cardinal utilities with interpersonal comparisons, or output just a winner instead of a full ranking (the newsletter needs an ordered shortlist of three, so that's out; if anybody Points out that it wouldn't save them anyway, note it and table it for lack of time). Somebody proposes instant-runoff and somebody else notes, with visible irritation, that it fails independence of irrelevant alternatives and can also make a name lose by becoming more popular. At least one member should state independence in the loose "no spoilers" way and get corrected in a footnote by the Secretary. Shortlist can include one or two joke names.

The ending has to be the reductio, played completely straight: the committee notes that the only procedure meeting all four conditions is for a single member to decide, and then unanimously votes, using whatever procedure is nearest to hand, to appoint the Chair as dictator pending review at the next meeting, with the minutes recording the general satisfaction that this was arrived at democratically. Nobody in the room should find any of this strange — no winking, nobody saying "this is absurd", no character realization. Real theorem and condition names are fine, but please don't cite or quote any actual published source, and don't name any living person.
