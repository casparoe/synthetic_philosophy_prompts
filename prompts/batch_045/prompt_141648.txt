I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I help maintain a small open-source library that runs a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma tournament: each entrant submits a program, each program is handed the other's source code as input, and it returns cooperate or defect. The bots people have heard of are the Löbian ones — cooperate if and only if you can produce a proof, in your own formal system, that your opponent cooperates — and our README claims results of this kind "dissolve" the dilemma.

I'd like a parody CHANGELOG.md for the library, roughly 700 words: versioned release entries from 0.1 to the present, then a Known Issues list and a short FAQ, in the deadpan voice of a maintainer who has quietly stopped believing the README. Invent a maintainer handle and bot names, and target the practice — treating proof-theoretic cooperation between mutually transparent programs as a solution to cooperation — rather than any real person. No citations, references, or footnotes.

The jokes have to come from getting the mechanics right rather than from invented quirks: cooperation resting on a proof search that never terminates, with each bot needing the other's proof to complete first; the way every hardening patch — defecting against unconditional cooperators, refusing to trust proof systems you cannot audit — leaves a bot cooperating with strictly fewer opponents; and the fact that all of it works only because each side is handed the other's actual source and runs on a deterministic, verifiable machine, which is the one condition a real strategic situation never satisfies.

One invented thread I want in the release notes: from 2.0 onward, cooperation becomes visibly a function of the proof-search budget, so agents that can afford to verify cooperate with each other and defect against agents that cannot, and the library quietly starts sorting entrants by how much compute they can spend while the landing page still says defection has been refuted. Stay deadpan enough that a reader can tell what is being ironized, and let the real worry land: that the cooperation was imported by the setup rather than derived from the payoffs.
