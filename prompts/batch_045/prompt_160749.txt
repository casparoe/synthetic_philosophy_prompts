Two people in my board game group got into it over the Sleeping Beauty problem on Sunday night, and I got voluntold to referee, which I am not remotely qualified to do. Setup, so we're all on the same page: on Sunday Beauty is told the protocol and put to sleep; a fair coin is tossed; heads means she's woken once (Monday), tails means she's woken twice (Monday and Tuesday), with her memory of the first waking erased so she can't tell which waking she's in. Question: on waking, what should her credence be that the coin landed heads?

(1) Sasha: a third. From where she is on waking there are three candidate nows she can't distinguish — heads-and-Monday, tails-and-Monday, tails-and-Tuesday — so a third each.

(2) Dev: a half. She knew in advance she'd be woken either way, so waking up tells her nothing she didn't already expect, the coin is fair, and there's nothing to update on.

(3) Sasha: your credences lose money. Beauty is offered, at every waking, a bet that costs 55 cents and pays a dollar if the coin landed tails. You refuse it, since by your lights its expected value is minus five cents. But if she takes it, tails gets her 90 cents over two wakings and heads costs her 55 cents, which is plus 17.5 cents per experiment — a bet even you would have wanted her to accept on Sunday.

(4) Dev: that's about how many times she's asked, not about what's likely. The bet pays because tails gets her to the betting window twice, not because tails is more probable. I'd accept it ex ante and I'd accept it in the waking too, if I evaluate the policy rather than the single act. Nothing here puts pressure on the one-half.

(5) Sasha: that move quietly concedes the practical question and leaves only a verbal one, except that my version keeps the ordinary link between credence and action while yours needs a special decision rule for self-locating cases. And your half isn't as clean as it sounds. Put a display on the wall showing which waking this is, and let tails mean a thousand wakings. You say a half before she reads it. But she can predict that whatever number comes up will swing her hard: if it says 1 she drops to about a thousandth on tails, if it says 500 she goes to certainty. So your half is an average over self-locating possibilities — you aren't avoiding self-locating uncertainty, you're weighting it by chance of the branch instead of by number of wakings, and that weighting is the whole dispute.

(6) Dev: then here's a variant that breaks your weighting. Toss the coin Monday night, after the first waking, instead of on Sunday. Nothing about your reasoning cares when the toss happened, so you still say a third — but now you're putting a third on a fair coin that hasn't been tossed yet, and you'd drop to a half the moment someone tells you the toss is still to come. That shows the third was never really about the coin.

(7) Sasha: no violation. Conditional on the toss not having happened yet I say a half. My third is my credence that the coin lands heads, and it's below a half partly because I might be in the second waking, where it already came up tails. Knowing the chance is a half while having credence a third is fine when you're not sure whether the toss has happened.

(8) Dev: last thing, and you haven't answered it. On Sunday Beauty can foresee with complete certainty that she will end up in exactly the evidential situation she's in when she wakes. You can't get a real update out of evidence you were already certain you'd have. So your third isn't the product of learning anything, and I still don't know what kind of thing it's supposed to be.
