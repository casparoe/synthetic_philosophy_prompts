so I'm TAing an intro philosophy discussion section next week and we're doing the free will unit, and I keep trying to build a session around the sphex wasp and it keeps collapsing into "look how dumb the wasp is, aren't you glad you're not a wasp." I want to get underneath that.

You know the story: the digger wasp paralyzes a cricket, drags it to her burrow, parks it on the threshold, goes inside to check the burrow out, comes back up and hauls the cricket in. If you nudge the cricket a few inches while she's down there, she comes up, drags it back to the exact same spot, and goes in to check again — and you can supposedly run that loop on her over and over. (I've also heard the classic version is tidier than the actual field observations, but let's just grant it.) The standard move is to say: there's your mechanical rigidity, and if we're ever sphexish, that's the shape of unfreedom.

Here's where I get stuck. On the one hand, rigidity all by itself can't be the mark of the mechanical, because flexible behavior is just as mechanizable as rigid behavior — a machine that recalculates is still a machine, and nobody thinks adding a lookup table to the wasp would make her free. On the other hand, we are definately sphexish in patches: the door-lock check, re-reading the same paragraph three times after an interruption, the way you keep re-opening the fridge. If rigidity is the tell, then either we're not free either, or the tell has to be more specific than "does the same fixed thing again."

So I want help making the property more specific. My own attempt, which I don't trust, is that there are two different things going on in the wasp and only one of them might matter:

1. re-triggering: the sub-routine fires again from the top when its own completion conditions are re-satisfied from outside, instead of the agent treating the sub-goal as already achieved
2. re-specification: whether the agent can revise what counts as the sub-goal being done at all — change the standard, drop the check, substitute something else that would do the job

The wasp is obviously rigid on (1). I have no idea whether she's rigid on (2), and I don't think the story tells us. So: which of these is doing the free-will-relevant work, if either is? Is there a third axis I'm missing that's actually the one that matters? And does the wasp case have any argumentative force on its own, or does it only do work once it's bolted onto some independent claim about what mechanisms can and can't do?

Two more things I'd like you to wrestle with. First, the trap I think I've built for myself: imagine a variant wasp whose re-checking gets a little faster each time you fool her — one flexible parameter, habituation or whatever. She still re-does the whole thing. Is she no longer sphexish? If yes, the concept has become so weak that almost nothing is sphexish; if no, then no amount of added flexibility could ever help, and the concept has become useless in the other direction. I'd like to see you actually thread that rather than pick a horn and shrug. Second, levels: the wasp presumably does fine navigating back to a burrow after detours, so she's flexible up there and rigid down in the action sequence. Does freedom require flexibility at every level, or at some privileged one, and how would we tell which level is privileged?

Write it for someone who knows the example but hasn't read the literature — explain, don't cite, and skip anything you'd need to look up.
