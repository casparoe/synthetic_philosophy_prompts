I sit on the animal-welfare committee of a farmed-seafood company, and my verdicts case by case don't seem to add up to anything I can state. Here they are as I actually have them.

1. Stunning farmed salmon electrically, or with a percussive blow, immediately before they are killed and bled: fine, and I would make it mandatory. Killing the same fish by chilling them in an ice slurry while they are still conscious: not fine.
2. Ablating one eyestalk of breeding female shrimp, with no anaesthetic, to induce them to spawn: not fine, even though the gain in hatchery output is large.
3. Holding salmon at a density that leaves most of a cage with split fins and chronically elevated cortisol, while food, oxygen and disease control are all in order: not fine, but I would cap density rather than ban the practice. My discomfort: on my own arithmetic the total harm in 3 dwarfs the harm in 2, since it touches millions of fish rather than thousands of broodstock, and yet I want 2 banned outright and 3 merely capped.
4. A feed additive that blocks the stress axis, given to fish in the very cages of case 3: they eat, grow and survive as well as ever, show no cortisol response to crowding, and no longer shoal or try to avoid the net. Not fine. It looks like it solves 3, but it strikes me as fixing the fish instead of the cage.
5. Fixing the cage instead: low-density, structurally enriched ponds with predators, parasites and real social hierarchies, ending in the same stun and bleed as case 1. Fine — and I would bet the fish in 5 get hurt far more often, and some eaten, than the treated fish in 4.
6. The one I keep reversing on: a gene-edited shrimp line in which the affective component of pain has been removed. They still withdraw and tail-flip at a noxious stimulus, but show no avoidance learning, no grooming of wounded parts and no physiological signature of distress, and they feed, mate and spawn normally. Ablating the eyestalk of such an animal, or crowding it, seems to stop being wrong; the whole programme nevertheless makes me uneasy in a way I cannot cash out.

What principle, if any, returns my verdicts as its output — stated precisely enough to apply to cases I haven't listed? I would rather have a principle that overturns one of my verdicts than one with a separate clause shaped around each case, so say which verdict you would give up.

Answer in exactly six numbered items, one or two sentences each, with no preamble and no summing-up: (i) your candidate principle, stated as precisely as you can; (ii) the clause you are least confident in, and which of my verdicts forces it; (iii) how the principle handles the pair of cases you think is doing the real work, if you disagree that it is 4 and 5; (iv) your verdict on case 6 and why; (v) which of my verdicts you would overturn; (vi) one case of your own, not on my list, that discriminates between your principle and its nearest rival, with your verdict on it.
