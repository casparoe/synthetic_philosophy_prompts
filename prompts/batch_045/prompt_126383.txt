I could use some help settling an argument I keep having with two friends — and with myself — about how large a stake to place in a situation I'll describe as concretely as I can. I'm 34, I have £60,000 in savings, and I would like to buy a flat in about two years, which means a deposit of roughly £120,000. I don't especially need more than that: anything above it would just go into a pension I can't touch for decades, and it wouldn't change which flat I buy. If my savings fell below about £30,000 I'd have to abandon the plan, and that would be a real blow rather than a mild disappointment. Separately, over the past couple of years I've built a model for a small, illiquid betting market where the odds are even money; it has been right on roughly 60% of about 200 bets, though I know 200 isn't many and the true rate might be nearer 55%. My plan is to use it once a month for the next two years, staking some fraction of whatever I have at the time, and I can skip a month or stop early if I want. I'm also putting about £500 a month of salary into a separate account, which I intend to keep out of all this. I work in data science, so please don't spare me the mathematics.

One friend says I should stake a fixed 20% of my pot every month, because that is the fraction that makes money compound as fast as possible. He talks about *geometric* growth and about the difference between averaging across many possible futures for one person and averaging across many people at one time, and he says anyone who maximises expected value is making a basic error. The other friend says this is superstition: the bet is favourable, so every pound left unstaked is expected return thrown away, and I should stake as much as I'm allowed. I simulated both. With 20%, I reached £120,000 in something like half the runs and the middle run finished near £83,000; with 50%, I reached the deposit noticeably less often but sometimes ended up with several hundred thousand. Neither piece of advice seems aimed at what I actually want, which isn't wealth in the abstract but a particular sum by a particular date, with a level below which I have to walk away.

So my questions are these. What fraction should I be staking, and should it be the same every month? It seems to me that the compounding rule must be assuming something like an endless run of bets and indifference to when the money arrives, neither of which holds for me, but I can't see what the right standard is in its place, or how the fraction ought to change when I'm ahead of schedule versus behind, or when only three months remain. I also half-remember a result saying that when the odds are against you and you need a fixed sum, staking as much as possible without overshooting is actually optimal — which, if I've got it right, makes me unsure whether having a favourable bet flips the advice in the opposite direction, and whether that's why a constant fraction feels wrong to me. And how much should the uncertainty about whether my edge is really 60% or 55% change the answer? I'm not asking whether the scheme is sensible at all; my partner has already made that case at length. I'm asking how to choose the stake given that I'm doing it, and what exactly the fastest-compounding argument assumes when it says the other approach is an error.
