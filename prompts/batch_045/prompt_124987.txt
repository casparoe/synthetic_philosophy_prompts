Draft it. Section one: numbered claims both of us can sign — about the formalism (what's actually provable where, that nobody's derived a contradiction from ZFC, that the diagonal argument really is a valid proof that no map from the naturals onto their powerset exists once you grant classical logic and the powerset axiom), about what the hotel story is and isn't doing, and about what would need defending either way. Be careful with the hotel: I want it pinned down whether the full-occupancy version adds anything over the plain fact that an infinite set can be put in bijection with a proper subset of itself, and separately whether the labour version adds anything again — the bit where fitting in one new guest requires infinitely many existing guests each to move once, i.e. infinitely many completed actions, which feels to me like a distinct supertask worry but Sam says it's the same worry in a costume. If it's a distinct cost, say so; if it collapses, say why.

Section two: a table of the disagreements. My side I'd rather you call the view that infinite totalities are only ever potential — I dont beleive "all subsets of N" picks out a finished collection, I think statements about it are really statements about what's provable or constructible. Every row needs the disagreement phrased so that I would accept the wording of my own view and Sam would accept his — no strawmanning, and anything that can't be phrased to both our satisfactions goes in the table rather than being smoothed over in section one.

Then the cruxes, and this is the part I care about most. For each crux, a case or result both of us agree would bear on it, and a prediction from each of us written down in advance. The ones I think are load-bearing: whether impredicative definitions are legitimate, given that the powerset step is impredicative in a way the step from finite to countable isn't; whether "no contradiction in a century plus it systematises everything we do" is a kind of warrant, or whether warrant needs a finitary consistency proof of the sort we know can't be given from inside; and the one I keep coming back to, which is whether the transfinite machinery earns its keep for purely finitary questions. For that last one use this hypothetical, it's the cleanest version I can think of: someone proves a simple for-all-n-there-is-m claim about finite strings — checkable by hand for any given n, nothing infinitary in its statement — and the only known proofs detour through uncountable totalities or large cardinals, every instance up to n = 12 has been verified, and nobody has found a finitary proof in forty years. Both of us agree that case is evidence about something. Set out what each of us should predict about how it goes. My guess is there's a finitary proof that's just very hard and we haven't been clever enough; Sam's guess is wierdly the opposite.
