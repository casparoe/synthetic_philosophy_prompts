I've got a 75-minute seminar with second-years next week on Harsanyi's aggregation theorem, and I want a plan, not an explanation — I know the result, I just don't know how to get it into their heads.

Where they are: they did vNM expected utility three weeks ago and can tell you that a utility function is fixed only up to positive affine transformations (sevaral can even say what that means), they've read nothing on social choice, and the room is split between people who arrive thinking utilitarianism is obviously right and people who think it's obviously vile — both of whom will pounce on anything that looks like a proof of utilitarianism.

Rough statement I'd put on the board: give each person i a vNM utility function u_i over lotteries; suppose society's ranking of lotteries also satisfies the EU axioms; suppose Pareto indifference, i.e. if every individual is indifferent between two lotteries then society is indifferent too. Then there are constants a_i, not all zero, such that society ranks lotteries by the expected value of W = sum of a_i u_i.

What I want from you: (1) the order the pieces have to go in, and what breaks if I swap any two. I have a feeling the rescaling point — each u_i is fixed only up to affine transformation, so the a_i soak up the units, and "changing the weights" can be the very same social ordering written in different units — has to land before the weighted-sum formula rather than after, but I can't say why, so tell me whether I'm right and what it depends on. (2) A carrying example. I've been toying with a currency-exchange frame: read a_i as the exchange rate at which person i's utility converts into the social currency, so rescaling someone's u_i is just redenominating (dollars into cents) and changes nothing, while changing the rate changes their social weight. I like that it makes the units point vivid. I worry that exchange rates are prices you read off a market and there is no market here, so they'll walk out thinking the a_i are measurable magnitudes rather than a free parameter of the representation. Run with it, fix it, or bin it — and if fix, tell me exactly what to bolt on. (3) Where in the sequence to defuse each of these: "so Harsanyi proved utilitarianism"; "so all the real moral work is in the weights"; "the u_i already carry interpersonal comparisons"; and the one I always get — we're applying Pareto to expected utilities, i.e. ex ante, so what if the coin lands badly and after the lottery everyone is worse off.
