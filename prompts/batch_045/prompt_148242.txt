I teach an upper-level seminar on free will, and the final session is on the question that gives this literature its name: not whether we have free will, but which variety of it is worth wanting. My students leave able to run the arguments and still wanting the wrong thing — the contracausal power they think they need in order to deserve blame, or the minimal compatibilist elbow room they have concluded is all anyone could get. I would like to close with a sestina (six stanzas of six lines, then a three-line envoy, unrhymed) whose six end-words are six candidate varieties, each named so that a specialist would recognize the position: leeway or alternative possibilities; sourcehood, being the origin of the action; reasons-responsiveness; having a will of one's's own in the sense of not being governed by another's will; diachronic self-creation; and the deliberative variety, the openness the future has from the inside while one is still making up one's mind. Let the rotation do the conceptual work: because the form compels each word to end a different line in every stanza, no variety ever occupies the same position twice, and that restlessness should come to feel like the thesis that none of them stably holds the role we keep trying to assign it — with the envoy gathering the words together without crowning one of them.

As the poem moves through these varieties, it should show each at the job it is wanted for: underwriting blame or praise, licensing deliberation, making an achievement genuinely one's own rather than a matter of luck, sustaining a friendship or a promise. It should also be honest that some varieties are wanted only derivatively — wanted because they are thought necessary for something else — and that the question which variety is worth wanting for its own sake may have a different answer from the question which variety we need. Include at least one variety the poem intimates we want but could not coherently have, or that we would not want if we saw it clearly. It should be a poem a roomful of students can hear once and be moved by, and that someone who works in this debate would not find wrong: no verdict handed down for compatibilism, and no closing shrug about mystery.
