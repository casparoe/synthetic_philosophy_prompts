I'm a PhD student in philosophy, working on a chapter on the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, and for the past few months I've been collecting the verdicts of a friend in the department — a philosopher of physics — on a long list of "is it already real, as of here-now?" cases, because I can't work out what rule she is actually running. Her record so far: an event whose light reached Earth in 1987 — already real, settled, no question; my department's vote next month — not yet real, "just the ordinary open future"; a collision between two Kuiper-belt rocks that happens, in the Earth's frame, at this very moment — already real, and she added that I shouldn't lean on the Earth's frame, that I should pick any frame I like and the verdict has the same standing; the two walkers passing each other on the street who disagree about whether the Andromedan council has already voted to launch the fleet — already real, and their disagreement is precisely the point, since there is nothing for "what is happening now on Andromeda" to be. When I pressed her on whether "already real" in those spacelike cases means "real, full stop" or only "already past relative to such-and-such observer's simultaneity slice," she said it doesn't matter, because no one frame's verdict has a better claim than any other's and so there is nothing left for a privileged present to be. That answer has been running in my head ever since, because it seems to me to slide between two quite different positions, and she has never been asked the case I actually care about.

Here it is. Take my reading group's meeting one week from now: timelike-future from here-now, squarely inside my future light cone. Now take an observer ten light-years away who, in my rest frame, is moving toward Earth at about one percent of the speed of light — the threshold for an event a week in my future to fall into their past is only about 0.002c (and the reason the textbook version can get away with pedestrians is that Andromeda is 2.5 million light-years off, so a walking pace buys you a few days at that distance); in their rest frame, as far as I can tell, my meeting happened roughly a month before their present moment. Please check that Lorentz transform. Then the variant I really want: the same set-up, except that the observer sits behind a horizon — put them in a galaxy receding from us so fast that no signal from them will ever reach us, or in whatever other idealization makes permanent causal disconnection possible — so that no comparison of notes, no later confirmation, no eventual "told you so" is available even in principle. If that variant can't be stated cleanly, because global simultaneity in an expanding spacetime isn't just a Lorentz boost and "their slice" has no univocal sense, then say so and say what the failure reveals; if it can be stated, tell me whether it changes anything.

What I want from you is not a verdict on the block universe — I've read enough of that literature. I want: her verdict in each variant; the list of candidate rules that fit her earlier verdicts, with at least two of them diverging on this case; what each rule predicts here and which reading of her earlier "already real" answers each one corresponds to, including whether the position that stops at the complement of my future light cone is really available to her given what she said about frames, and whether the perspectival reply (that "already past" is always indexed to an observer and never transfers into a claim about what is real, so that her earlier verdicts were indexical all along) is one she has already ruled out or one she has been covertly relying on; your best guess with the reasons for preferring it; and the single further case I should put to her that would best separate the rules that survive. I also have a nagging suspicion that, despite the "pick any frame you like" line, she is quietly helping herself to the comoving frame's cosmic-now slice — if that is a fourth rule that fits her record just as well, say so and tell me what it predicts here and what would expose it.
