I have recently been made the clinical member of the committee that decides which decision-support tools our hospital may deploy, and under what conditions of checking their output may be acted on. Every meeting turns out to turn on the same old epistemological question: which sources of knowledge are basic, how many kinds there are, and what must be true of a source before acting on what it tells you is permissible. I have been reading work on the pramāṇas in the Nyāya tradition and its interlocutors, and I have concluded that what I actually need is not another enumeration of knowledge-sources but a specification: what any such enumeration would have to be able to do before it could underwrite a real protocol.

So please write me the desiderata. The purpose I am fixing: a theory of the knowledge-sources adequate for underwriting an institutional protocol in a high-stakes domain, where acting on an output that does not warrant action harms someone, and where the person acting often cannot audit the source herself. Adequacy is relative to that purpose, so say which requirements follow from it and which would only be requirements for a theory meant for something else. For each desideratum give the case or consideration behind it, mark it non-negotiable, negotiable, or a factional preference, and note what it rules out.

The cases I need handled, roughly in the order they arose for me:

A risk score for sepsis produced by a model trained on last year's admissions at a different hospital. It is not a speaker; it has no awareness of this patient; it offers no reason; its only warrant is a statistical relation that holds for a population and may not hold here.
A waveform on a continuous monitor, and the same number read off a different device I do not understand.
A counterfactual output: the patient would have arrested without the bolus. Nobody observed anything of the kind.
A colleague's note in the chart, and a guideline issued by a committee I cannot interview.
My recollection of the morning vital signs, contrasted with re-reading the chart and learning the same thing again.
Looking at the medication list and seeing that no anticoagulant is on it: I come to know an absence.
A potassium level that is normal on a regimen that should not permit it, from which I conclude something unobserved.
An alarm I was told sounds like the old one, which I then hear and recognize for the first time.
A source whose reliability is now known to have drifted downward since it was last certified, and a source I have no way of checking at all.

Alongside these, the distinctions I do not want collapsed: being a source of knowledge versus being a merely reliable indicator; a source being reliable versus my having warrant for taking it to be reliable; first acquisition of something versus confirmation or re-identification of what one already knows; the failure of a source versus the mere absence of knowledge; and the claim that there are n basic sources versus the claim that the others reduce to those n. I am also unsure how to treat the demand that a pramāṇa be an instrument that brings about a veridical cognition rather than simply a reliable process that happens to issue in one: is that a genuine requirement or a commitment of one school? Tell me, too, which of the things people demand of such a theory are real requirements and which are inherited prejudices: that the list be short, that perception be non-conceptual, that no source certify itself, that the list be closed under some fixed number.

The part I most want is the tension structure. I suspect at least two subsets cannot be jointly satisfied, and I want them stated as claims about the list itself: that no source may certify its own reliability, together with the demand that every certification proceed by some knowledge-source, together with the rejection of circularity and regress; and the treatment of a pramāṇa as an infallible instrument, together with the requirement that the theory explain systematic error and the cases in which a source looks exactly like a working one and is not. Add any further pair you think is jointly unsatisfiable, including pairs involving a desideratum that a source's reliability hold for this case and not merely on average, or that a theory say what happens when a source degrades. Then tell me which single desideratum does the most exclusionary work, in the sense of knocking out the largest share of otherwise attractive theories.

Only after that, and briefly: how the familiar options fare against the list. Perception alone; perception and inference alone; the Nyāya set of four; the larger set of six that adds postulation and non-cognition; reductionist treatments of testimony and analogy; and a broadly reliabilist or probabilistic treatment on which warrant is a matter of calibrated process rather than of source-type. Two or three lines each is plenty; I will do my own scoring in committee.
