I'm on a modelling team at a public-health institute — we build transmission models that get handed to people who have to decide things weeks or months before anyone can check whether we were right — and I've been asked to give our group's methods seminar a short talk on the difference between an assumption that is merely idealizing and one that is just wrong. I assumed this would be easy and it isn't. A reviewer recently rejected a paper of ours on the grounds that our treatment of contact structure isn't merely false but "the wrong kind of false," and when we argued about it none of us could say what the kinds are, or why the taxonomy should track anything a modeller cares about. I've since read a little of the philosophical literature on idealization — the F-twist and the reactions to it, the de-idealization picture on which a benign idealization is one you can walk back toward the truth, the view of models as imagined systems we study in their own right before asking how similar they are to the target, the "isolate a real capacity or causal factor" view, and the adequacy-for-purpose view — and what struck me is that each of these gets scored against its author's favourite examples: frictionless planes and the ideal gas law on the friendly side, as-if rationality and unfalsifiable optimality stories in biology on the hostile side. Nobody starts from the standard and then audits the examples.

So I want the specification rather than the scorecard. Write me the desiderata an account of when an unrealistic assumption is legitimate would have to meet to be worth anything to a working modeller: someone who has to rank which assumptions to worry about first, before the model is used, often with no validation data anywhere near the regime that matters. For each requirement give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark it as non-negotiable or as a preference of one camp. My own pile of hard cases, which I'd like either handled or explicitly placed out of scope: (i) an assumption that is de-idealizable in principle but not in practice, because the honest version has more unknown parameters than we have data — so that whether it counts as benign appears to drift with available compute and technique; (ii) an assumption that is benign for interpolation and malignant for extrapolation, with the model unchanged, because the neglected term is small in the observed regime and dominant in the one the decision concerns; (iii) a prediction that holds only in a limit no real system approaches and that provably has no finite analogue, so there is no de-idealization to walk back to; (iv) a model whose recommendation, once implemented, falsifies its own assumption about fixed behaviour — the intervention works by breaking the thing the model held constant; (v) a model that gets the right headline number because two errors compensate, where fixing one of them makes the number worse; (vi) an assumption nobody on the team can justify, inherited from a parent model where it once had a robustness argument — does an account have to be genealogical, or may it judge assumptions only on their present relation to the model, the question and the target; (vii) exploratory models that deliberately have no target system at all, where "false" seems to lose its anchor.

Two further things I especially want. First, flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — I have two candidates in mind, one pairing the demand to grade assumptions prospectively and without track record against any criterion that makes predictive success the test, the other pairing robustness-under-de-idealization with the milder demand that legitimately fixing an error shouldn't make the model worse; tell me whether those are real conflicts or whether I've confused two senses of something, and whether there are deeper ones I haven't seen. Second, tell me which single requirement excludes the most candidate accounts, and whether the way I've framed my purpose — a triage tool for modellers — has already begged the question in favour of one of the views I gestured at. A short table scoring three or four of those views against the final list is fine, but keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases.
