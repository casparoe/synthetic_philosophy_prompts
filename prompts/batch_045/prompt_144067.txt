ok so I've been going back and fourth on this and I want a consistency check before I present it to my formal epistemology reading group. Roughly where I've landed on confirmation and Bayesian updating:

(1) Conditionalization is the only legitimate way to revise credences, and the Dutch book / Dutch strategy arguments are the best reason to think so.

(2) Confirmation just is credence-raising: E confirms H for you iff P(H|E) > P(H). I don't want a separate support relation bolted on next to this.

(3) The problem of old evidence is a real problem and not a pseudo-problem, and the benchmark any solution has to hit is the usual one: the anomalous advance of Mercury's perihelion really did confirm general relativity, and it still does — it's part of the total case for GR for us now, not just a thing that briefly bumped up Einstein's own confidence back when he first got the derivation to come out.

(4) The right fix is the one where what you newly learn isn't E but the connection: you weren't certain that GR plus the auxiliaries entails the perihelion result, and finding the derivation is the update. I'm happy to pay for the denial of logical omniscience that this needs.

(5) But I also want how strongly the evidence supports the theory to be intersubjective. It shouldn't be a function of how good a mathematician you are or how recently you checked the derivation, and it should come out roughly the same number for Einstein and for me.

(6) No special-purpose machinery for old evidence. Whatever handles Mercury should be the same apparatus that handles brand new evidence; if the only way to keep (3) is a rule that fires just when the evidence happens to already be known, I'd rather drop (3).

(7) Subjectivism about priors: any coherent prior is rationally permissible, no prior is rationally mandated, and residual disagreement in priors is fine.

(8) On method, I don't think pre-theoretic verdicts about cases are evidence. The verdicts should fall out of the formalism, not be fed into it.

Which of these actually conflict? I have a vague feeling (4) and (5) are in worse shape than they look to me, and that (8) might be doing something I can't have given (3), but I genuinely can't locate the fault line and I don't have a favourite I'd give up. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, tell me which pairs only look like they clash and why they dissolve, and for each real tension say what exactly would have to give and what giving it up would cost me. Then tell me whether there's one revision that cleans up the most, and whether the package I actually want — old evidence confirms, no ad hoc machinery, intersubjective degrees of support — is available at all or whether I'm asking for three things that can't be had together.
