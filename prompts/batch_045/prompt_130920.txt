I keep noticing a pattern in how people behave when they are genuinely indifferent between two options, and I have no word for it. Offer such a person a fair coin and they will often refuse it — not because they fear regret, or doubt the coin is fair, but because flipping strikes them as a way of making the decision happen without making it. Yet give the same person a tiebreaker that is connected to one of the options in some narratable way — the one that took more effort to arrange, the one a friend would have preferred, the one they noticed first — and they will use it without discomfort, and afterwards report having chosen, citing the tiebreaker as their reason. Both tiebreakers are equally irrelevant to the comparative value of the options, and the person can usually see that. Three features seem worth marking: the refusal gets stronger the more the decision matters, even though on the agent's own assessment it does not matter which option is picked; it disappears entirely when the randomization has a strategic point, as in a game where being unpredictable is the whole object, so it is not a bare aversion to leaving things to chance; and people will often expend real effort to find a narratable tiebreaker they know to be as arbitrary as the coin, and then rest content.

Buridan's ass is the nearest thing I know of, but that is a puzzle about whether indifference makes action impossible, not about which ways of breaking indifference are tolerable to the agent, and it says nothing about why some arbitrary tiebreakers are experienced as exercises of will and others as surrenders of it. I have also heard the suggestion that in a hard choice the agent's will confers value on whichever option is picked, but that is a claim about where reasons come from, not about the difference between the coin and the story.

Does this pattern have an established name? If several established concepts each capture part of it, I would like them set against one another and against their neighbours — I am not after a label, I want the thing carved: what precisely the coin does that the narratable tiebreaker does not, whether that difference survives the observation that both are arbitrary, and where the edges lie (does it depend on how much is at stake, on whether the agent can identify with the tiebreaker, on whether anyone else is watching?). If nothing established fits, coin a term, define it, and give the boundary cases that would test the definition.
