Here is the position I hold, stated tightly enough that you can attack the seams. Call it Reconstructionism, about the class of philosophical devices usually called intuition pumps.

Thesis: a thought experiment carries evidential weight for a thinker exactly to the extent that it can be reconstructed, for that thinker, as an argument whose premises she already accepts or would accept on due reflection, and whose conclusion she can see to follow. The narrative machinery — the characters, the order of disclosure, the sensory concreteness, the emotional charge, the metaphor — does no epistemic work at all. It does three other things: it speeds retrieval of a modal or classificatory judgment the thinker was already disposed to make; it fixes attention on a feature she would otherwise have overlooked; and, very often, it smuggles in a premise she would reject if it were stated flatly. Those are psychological and rhetorical functions, and their size is unrelated to their epistemic value.

I commit myself to four separable claims:

(E) Extractability. For any pump that has genuinely moved anyone, there is an argument that does the same work, and the pump is epistemically idle relative to it. There is no such thing as residual force that belongs to the story and not to the argument.

(N) Narrative neutrality. If framing a case one way rather than another changes verdicts, and the change is not traceable to a difference in the propositions being evaluated, the extra verdict-shifting power is noise, not evidence. Order effects, helping-versus-harming asymmetries in attributions of intentionality, and the vividness of incidental detail are all in this category.

(I) Invariance (an empirical prediction). Present the extracted argument alone, cold, to subjects who have never encountered the story, and the distribution of verdicts will match the distribution you get from presenting the story, controlling for comprehension. My view predicts invariance and its rivals — views on which imagination or simulation is itself a source of modal knowledge — predict a residual story advantage.

(P) The privilege clause. The premises in the reconstruction need not be ones the thinker accepts today; they may be ones she would accept on reflection, where reflection is not allowed to include re-running the pump.

2. Three kinds of case worry me and I want to know which of them is a genuine cost. First, argument-silent pumps: cases that work by making you fail to imagine something, where the failure is the evidence and no proposition is delivered for reconstruction to get a grip on. Second, therapeutic pumps: cases whose function is not to teach you that such-and-such is possible or that a concept applies, but to reveal that your concept has no determinate application here, so that the right response is to stop asking. Third, calibration pumps: cases that teach you something about the shape of your own thresholds and dispositions rather than about the target domain, and that seem to do it in a way no explicit argument about your dispositions could. Which of these can Reconstructionism describe, which can it only redescribe, and which does it have to declare bad pumps?
