Me and about a dozen others run a tiny giving circle — we each put in £20 a month, and once a year we argue about where the few thousand pounds goes. I've definately started to think we're not being consistent with ourselves, so I'd like to know what rule we're actually running and what it says about this year's decision.

What we've decided so far:
- bednets against malaria, in a country none of us has any connection to — funded
- a hospice in our own town that two of our members have personal ties to — turned down, because they hit their fundraising target every year and our money wouldn't have added anything
- a summer holiday version of a reading programme in our own city, in a cause that's already well funded, because nobody else was going to pay for that particular bit — funded
- research into a rare disease almost nobody funds, tiny number of people affected, no real track record — funded, but only £500 and after a fight about it being a lottery ticket
- work on making dangerous pathogen labs safer, because it might matter a lot in thirty years — turned down, we couldn't see how to tell if it had done anything
- a small group whose entire output is research telling other funders where to give — funded, which three of us thought was mad
- a campaign to get a supermarket chain to stop using one particularly nasty method of killing farmed fish — we couldn't agree, put it off, and it's come back round

That last one is the decision in front of us now. The numbers of fish involved are enormous, nobody else is funding anything on fish here, and the campaign is cheap. But we're not sure fish feel much of anything, the evidence that campaigns like this actually change practice is thin, and one of us says that if it works the supermarket will just buy from a farm still using the old method, so we'd have bought nothing at all.

I know the importance / tractability / neglectedness thing, so don't just explain that at me — I want to know which version of it we're running, or whether we're really doing something else, like only caring what our particular money causes. Given our record, what should we decide, which old decision is the closest thing to it, and where our record genuinely doesn't settle it, what would we be signing up to either way? And what's the one application we'd have to see next year to tell the difference?
