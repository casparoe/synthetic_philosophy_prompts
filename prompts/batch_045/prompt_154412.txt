Here's my profile, more or less as I'd defend it at a seminar:

1. I accept the language of thought hypothesis as a substantive empirical claim, not a metaphors: having a propositional attitude is standing in a relation to a mental representation with combinatorial syntactic structure, and cognitive processes are computations defined over that structure -- the syntax is causally relevant to what the process does, not just a description we happen to like.

2. The reason I'm a LOT person rather than a just-build-a-net-and-see person is the systematicity/productivity line of argument: the abilities come in packages (if you can think a is bigger than b you can think b is bigger than a), and the best explanation is constituency. And I think the classic argument that associative nets don't get systematic generalization for free is basically right -- that reintroducing structure into a net is cheating if it's constituency all the way down.

3. But I'm a connectionist at the level of implementation. I don't think anyone is ever going to point at the sentence in the head, or if they do it'll turn out to be about as arbitrary as pointing at a register in a CPU, and I don't think that threatens (1) at all.

4. I think non-conceptual content is real: plenty of perception, and plenty of infant and non-human animal cognition, is iconic or map-like rather than discursive. And I also want to say that perceptual experience stands in genuinely rational, justificatory relations to belief -- not merely causal ones. My seeing the mug is my reason for thinking there's a mug there, in the full norm-y sense, not just the event that caused me to think it.

5. On content: I want a naturalistic story, and the one I like is nomic covariance / teleosemantics -- a symbol means what it does because of what it tracks or what it was selected for.

6. And I think the argument that you can't learn a representational system richer than the one you already have is basically sound -- while also thinking it's obvious that I acquired the concept of a baryon, or of a filibuster, at some identifiable point in my life, and I don't want to settle that by saying it was in there all along.
