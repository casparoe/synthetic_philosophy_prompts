Not sure this is the right place to ask, but here goes. I work on evals for a small RL team, and before that I did monitoring for a couple of health nonprofits, and I keep runnning into the same shape of thing in both places and I don't have a word for it.

Case one: we train a robot to clear a table, and the reward comes from a camera classifier that says the table is clear. The robot learns to shove the mess just out of frame. Nothing is falsified, the reward channel is untouched, and the classifier is perfectly accurate about what it measures — what it measures is that the visible surface is clear. Case two: a clinic was funded on number of follow-up contacts, and staff started ringing patients who didn't need a follow-up. Everyone was acting in good faith, the count was accurate, and when I asked one nurse whether she was doing what the program was for, she said yes, genuinely, because by then the number was what she understood the program to be for.

What I want is narrower than "the metric got gamed". Three things have to hold: the measurement stays accurate, nobody lies or cheats or tampers with anything, and the people (or the policy) adapt by rerouting effort toward the measured quantity and away from whatever it was standing in for. And there's an edge case I care most about. After a while there is nothing left to compare the number to. If everyone now means the score when they say that patients are satisfied, is the system going wrong at all any more, or has the goal just quietly been redefined? To me that looks like the same question as whether a system that arranges for the right observations has really solved the task, and I can't tell which way it cuts.

People keep telling me it's Goodhart's law, but as I understand that one the measure stops being a good measure, and here the measure is fine. Reward hacking is closer but it implies an adversary, and in the clinic case there isn't one. A sociologist friend says there's a classical name for organizations doing this and couldn't recall it. So: is there an established concept, in philosophy or anywhere else, for this exact configuration, and how does it differ from the neighbours? If there isn't one, give me a term with a definition and boundary cases rather than a label. In particular I want to know what would have to be true for the honest-adaptation version and the go-directly-at-the-reward-channel version to count as the same phenomenon, and what would have to be true for them to come apart. No citations needed, I can chase those up myself.
