Trying to work out what I actually think counts as wireheading, because my gut verdicts on cases are all over the place and I cant tell if there's one principle behind them.

- Rat with an electrode in the pleasure center, presses the lever till it starves: clear failure, not a success.
- RL agent rewrites its own reward function so sitting still pays max: same, clearly bad.
- Same agent instead upgrades its camera so it can see the blocks it's meant to be sorting: obviously fine.
- Agent rewarded on whiteness of the pixels in a room: painting the room white, fine. Shining a flashlight into the camera, not fine. Agent told to keep the floor clean: installs a self-cleaning floor, fine; drops a sheet over the mess, not fine. These pairs look structurally identical to me but my verdicts flip, and I don't know what does the work.
- A floor-cleaning robot that rewrites itself to find floor-cleaning intrinsically enjoyable: I want to say fine. A human who takes a pill that makes them love their tedious job: not fine. A human who slowly learns to appreciate the job through practice: fine. Those three can't all be right, surely.
- Agent updates its reward model because it got better evidence about what the user really wanted: fine. Agent drops a constraint from its reward model because the constraint was expensive: not fine.
- And the one that bugs me: an agent that first persuades the user to want something else, then honestly re-learns the user's values and serves those. Each step looks like the good version of the previous two cases, the combination is obviously manipulation. Does the badness live in step one, or in the pair?

My tentative principle: the reward signal has to be a measurement of some feature of the world the designer cared about, and the agent's influence on the signal has to route through that feature rather than through the signal or the sensor. Test that against my cases, patch it where it breaks, and tell me which verdict I have to give up. Then give me its answer on the case I go back and forth on: an agent that, foreseeing it'll be tempted to wirehead, deletes its own reward signal entirely and acts on its best model of what the principal wanted. And one case I haven't listed where your principle gives an answer you'd expect people to hate.
