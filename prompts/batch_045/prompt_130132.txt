I'm running a seminar on the 'equality of what?' question for final-years next term, and instead of opening with a lecture I want to hand them a sorting quiz: ten concrete cases, each with two or three options, and then a key that tells them which currency of equality they've just committed themselves to. Most of them will have read the obvious stuff, but I want the quiz to work for someone who's read nothing — so no question may name a theorist, a currency, or an -ism, and nothing like 'do you think we should equalise welfare or resources?'. Every question has to be a case a real person could have a gut reaction to.

Cases I want in there (bin any you think are duds, but keep at least six): the person with expensive tastes who gets much less out of the same money; the cheerful person whose preferences have quietly adapted downwards; the wheelchair user and, separately, someone whose body just costs more to keep running; someone fasting for religious reasons next to someone with no food at all — I want that one in, it's the cleanest way at the capability-versus-functioning split; someone who took a gamble and lost versus someone hit by something they couldn't have insured against; someone with a superb set of options who can't act on any of them because of depression; someone whose prospects are modest but almost impossible to knock down versus someone whose prospects are great but collapse entirely if one thing goes wrong; and one whole-life-versus-time-slice case — brilliant sixty years then a miserable decade, or the reverse.

What I need from the key:

- Six to eight leaves, built from the logical space rather than from a list of names: start from the questions, show which combinations of answers are actually available, then drop the familiar views into the slots and tell me which slots are empty. For the empty ones say whether nobody lives there because the combination is unstable, merely unattractive, or just unclaimed.
- The leaves have to include: an actual-welfare currency; an opportunity-for-welfare one (Arneson? don't sweat the attribution); a resources-or-means currency; the social-primary-goods sort of currency; a capability currency with an open or deliberately unspecified list; a capability currency with a fixed list; and something in the 'access to advantage' / 'midfare' region Cohen gestures at.
- Two currencies I've been toying with that I want as leaves too, because without them people with perfectly coherent views get misfiled. One where the unit is your fallback position — the value of the worst place you could end up in, through no fault of your own, or through your own fault, say which you'd pick. One where the unit is recoverability: how fast and how fully you get back to your baseline after a setback, rather than the level of your prospects. Tell me honestly if either collapses into a leaf already on the tree, and if so which.
- Flag any pair of leaves my tree genuinely can't separate. I suspect primary goods versus resources is going to be one, and I'd like to know whether the expensive-tastes and disability cases are enough to prise them apart or whether I need a question about talents and brute luck.
- There'll be two or three sufficientarians and prioritarians in the room who'llanswer every case with 'who cares whether it's equal, what matters is that it's bad'. Give me one question near the start that catches them and routes them out of the currency tree, so they don't end up filed as welfarists by accident.

Don't bother with paper titles, dates or exact attributions — I don't need them and I'd rather you spent the words on the cases. If you want to name a view in the key, use a rough label like 'the resources view'. And no quotations.
