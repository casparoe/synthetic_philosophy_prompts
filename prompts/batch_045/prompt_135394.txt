I spent thirty-odd years in industrial chemistry, most of them on flame-retardant formulations, and since retiring I have been sitting on my county's fire-safe council and auditing an evening seminar in metaphysics at the local university. Last week we covered dispositions, and I have not been able to look at our own mitigation budget since without the new vocabulary running through my head. As I understood it, the distinctions were these: a disposition has a stimulus and a manifestation; a fink is a case where the stimulus itself destroys or removes the disposition; a mask is a case where something blocks the manifestation while leaving the disposition entirely intact; and there was a third category, mimic I think, where the manifestation occurs without the disposition ever having been there. The seminar left me unsure how much metaphysical weight these categories can actually bear.

So consider what my county actually does. Almost all our wildfire money goes to four things: clearing brush and enforcing defensible-space rules around houses; hardening the houses themselves, replacing shake roofs and screening vents against embers; response capacity, meaning engines, aerial retardant, and home sprinkler and gel kits applied during a fire; and prescribed burning with shaded fuel breaks. The pattern puzzles me in several places at once. Our official risk maps score a parcel largely by predicted fire behavior and by response time, so a shake-roofed house in heavy chaparral with a station five minutes away and a cleared perimeter is scored close to a hardened house on bare ground, even though in the first case we have left the disposition to burn completely untouched and merely arranged for the manifestation to be interrupted. Meanwhile we fight hard against any suggestion that we stop protecting houses in the wildland-urban interface, we describe prescribed burning as restoring something even though some of these drainages have not burned in a century, and after a fire we treat the burn as safe for a couple of years and then watch grass come back and carry fire through it again.

I would like you to reconstruct the implicit theory of fire risk, or of the disposition to burn, that this package of practices presupposes. Does it strictly require a real metaphysical difference between removing a disposition, removing its stimulus, and merely masking it, or is that difference only a matter of timescale and reliability that a practice like this could do without? Could someone who thought the only genuine properties here were the present categorical ones, the fuel load and the moisture content and the roof material, license exactly the same spending, or does the practice need a disposition that can survive unmanifested for a hundred years? Is prescribed burning best described as a fink, as an ordinary manifestation, or as neither? Where is the practice philosophically underdetermined, so that several rival accounts of dispositions would all license it? And what would have to change if we replaced the implicit view with a purely actuarial one, on which there are no masks and no finks, only expected loss per year? Please answer in three short sections, theory, presupposition versus mere fit, and consequences, in under 600 words total.
