Two friends of mine have been going at it for about a year over whether the quiet sky means we're alone. They at least agree that the silence only pins down a product: how often lineages like ours get going × how long a technosphere stays detectable × how visible it is while it does. One says nearly the whole update should land on the first factor, since the other two would have to be huge for us to have noticed anything by now — a galaxy-crossing expansion at even a hundredth of light speed is over in a blink next to the age of the galaxy, so anyone who gets that far and stays loud should already be obvious; the other says it should mostly land on the last factor, because we've barely looked, and because we're reasoning from inside the very sample we're drawing inferences about, which makes our own earliness the datum to be most careful with. Write the joint statement they should have produced: a numbered section of claims both can sign (including about what it even comes to for the silence to be evidence of rarity, given that it constrains a product rather than any single factor), then a table of their remaining disagreements, each in wording it's own side would accept. I suspect one crux is how much of the update each factor is allowed to absorb and another is whether a single confirmed atmospheric biosignature on a nearby exoplanet in the next twenty years would of settled it; for each crux name a concrete observation or argument both would accept as bearing on it, and write down in advance what each of them predicts it'll show. If some of the fight is really just about words, say which part and why.
