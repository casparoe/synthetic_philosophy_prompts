hey — I'm building a first-year seminar session on history and power and I wanted to use Nuremberg as the case, but I'm honestly not sure the slogan I'm building the session around survives contact with it. So: take the main International Military Tribunal (1945–46, four-power court, Jackson as chief US prosecutor, the works) and analyze it through the lens of "history is written by the victors."

Here's the taxonomy I've been using to break that slogan apart — tell me if its junk:
- the archival thesis: victory decides which paperwork survives and gets catalogued at all
- the framing thesis: victory decides the later vocabulary and categories (aggressive war, crimes against humanity) — the grid, not the data
- the uptake thesis: victory decides what gets believed regardless of the evidence, because schools, money and prestige follow power
- plus two things the slogan as usually stated has no slot for: (a) losers' counter-histories, where defeat is itself a rhetorical resource — the clean-Wehrmacht story was mostly built by the Germans who lost, and it held for decades, same shape as the Lost Cause; and (b) what I've started calling the evidentiary boomerang — a victor that legitimates its victory by playing by evidence rules produces an archive that outlives the narrative it was built to serve.

What I want is the mapping run in both directions, and honestly run. Concretely: who even is "the victor" here, given it was a four-power coalition already coming apart, with the Soviets wanting Katyn charged against the Germans and everyone wanting a slightly different story? Which of my theses does the tribunal confirm, which does it break, and what would a full-strength version of the slogan have predicted that just didn't happen? (I think three of the defendants were acquitted, which seems like awkward evidence for any pure show-trial reading — correct me if I'm misremembering, and tell me whether acquittals are evidence for anything at all here.)

And then the bit I actually care about: does this case support the slide from "power shapes which account circulates" to the relativist conclusion that there's no fact of the matter about what happened, or does Nuremberg show something closer to the opposite — that a victor's court which commits itself to evidence produces a record that later constrains its own authors? Say where the theory is silent instead of forcing it to speak, mark how confident you are in each verdict and which ones you'd retract if a fact went the other way, and if the whole "written by the victors" framing is too vague to be applied to a case like this, just say so plainly rather than politely working inside my taxonomy.
