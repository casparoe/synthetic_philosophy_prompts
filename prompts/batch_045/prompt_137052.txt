I've been asked to put together a short "position file" on personal identity and survival for a reading group I'm in, and I'd like someone to check it over for coherence before I stand up and defend it. Here's where I've landed, more or less. Some of it feels like a package deal that doesn't quite fit together, and I'd rather find that out now than halfway through a seminar.

(1) Reductionism. There's no deep further fact about whether I survive — no ego, no soul, nothing over and above the more particular facts about brains, bodies, psychology and causal chains.

(2) What matters in survival is psychological continuity and connectedness, however it's caused — call it R. Bodily or organismic continuity matters only as the usual cause of R. If R holds, the cause is beside the point.

(3) Standard destructive teletransporter: scan me here, destroy the body, rebuild from the scan on Mars. I press the button. As good as ordinary survival, no discount.

(4) Fission, both hemispheres into two bodies. Strictly I don't survive, since identity can't be one-many. But I don't care about that: what matters is preserved, and I'd press the button just as readily as in (3). I also want to say it isn't *twice* as good as ordinary survival — two lives aren't better than one.

(5) Branch-line style variant: non-destructive scan, replica on Mars, and the Mars branch dies of a heart defect two days later. Barely worse than ordinary survival, as I see it — the Earth branch carries on, the extra bit of life was short, no real loss.

(6) Ordinary duplication where nobody dies: non-destructive scan, and both the Earth continuer and the Mars one go on for decades. I think the Earth continuer has a genuine and serious complaint here, and that something has gone badly wrong for them. I'd pay a lot to avoid this, and nowhere near as much to avoid (5). Which is odd, given that less dying happens in (6).

(7) Swampman by fluke: a quantum accident assembles something molecule-for-molecule identical to me on Mars, with all my apparent memories, while I carry on here. Not me, not my survival — and (2) shouldn't be read as covering it.

(8) Survival comes in degrees. If the scan is degraded and the replica only overlaps with me about 60% psychologically, I'm happy saying I partly survive, and I think the combined-spectrum style argument shows there's often no sharp yes or no answer.

(9) Delayed reconstitution: scan, I die, and the replica is built from the archived scan forty years later with no memories of the gap. Survival with a forty-year hole in it. I'd sign up.

(10) Asymmetric division: one branch comes out with maybe 90% of the connectedness and the other with about 30%. I want to say the first is nearly as good as ordinary survival and the second is a bad outcome, but not as bad as death.

So: which pairs of these are actually in tension, and how badly? Rank the tensions by severity rather than flagging all of them as equally bad, and for each one say exactly what would have to give and what that revision would cost me somewhere else in the set. There's got to be at least one verdict in there that my own principle can't deliver — (6) is my suspicion, but I'd like you to work out which one it really is rather than just agreeing with me. Tell me too which of the apparent tensions dissolve on inspection and why, because I don't want to go around thinking I'm being bold when I'm just being confused. Then, if you had to fix the whole thing with one revision, which would clean up the most and what would it leave me committed to?

Two more things. Please seperate what's settled among people who actually work on this from what's a contested reading — don't smooth a contested reading into "the standard view", and if you're unsure whether something is contested, say so instead of quietly picking a side. And flag anywhere my own wording is doing load-bearing work, i.e. where the tension is really about how I've phrased a thesis rather than about the cases. No need to cite anything or point me at papers, I've read the standard stuff; I just want the audit.
