I'm a journalist working on a piece about why economics keeps building models out of assumptions everyone involved agrees are false. Nearly every economist I put the question to comes back with the same picture: a model is a map. The Tube map is not drawn to scale, and no one takes that to stop it getting you from Holborn to Bank; a model's false assumptions are its not being to scale. The picture then does real work: it is used to dismiss worries about unrealistic assumptions as pedantry about scale, and, in the stronger form Friedman gave the point in his 1953 essay on the methodology of positive economics (the one Samuelson called the F-twist), to suggest that unrealistic assumptions are a mark of a theory's significance rather than a cost.

I would like to audit it before I let it into print. Three things worry me. First, I have started sorting false assumptions into three kinds: those that shrink a magnitude the modeller takes to be irrelevant (no friction, no transport costs); those that fix or restrict the domain (a continuum of traders, zero transaction costs); and as-if surrogates, where the mechanism is granted to be false but the behaviour is claimed to match (firms acting as if they equated marginal revenue with marginal cost when no one is doing any such calculation). I suspect the map has resources for the first two and none for the third. Second, the map seems to cover only distortions you could in principle pay to remove, not falsehoods without which the model derives nothing at all. Third, a map states its scale and projection on its face and can be checked point by point against a territory that exists independently of it; a model does neither.

Give me the correspondence table — what plays the territory, the scale, the projection, the route — then where the mapping holds, where it breaks for the use I have described, which of my three kinds each breakage bears on, and whether the stronger conclusion survives once the breakages are in, or whether the analogy was carrying it.

Sections in this order, please: "Correspondences" (a table, or about 100 words); "Where it holds" (about 100 words); "Where it breaks" (about 250 words, each breakage tagged to one of my three kinds); "What the analogy was carrying" (about 100 words); "A better analogy, if there is one" (about 75 words).
