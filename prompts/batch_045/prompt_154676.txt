Here's where I've landed on intellectual property. I dont think ideas can be owned. Not "they can be owned but the rights should be limited" — I mean there's nothing there to be owned in the first place. Whenever it looks like an idea is owned, something else is doing all the work: a physical token (the notebook, the prototype, the drive), an expression (this exact wording, this drawing), a confidence (I told you in private), or a commercial position (being first to market, having the leverage). The idea/expression line is doing the whole ballgame, and it isn't really about ideas at all. My diagnostic for this is what I call the subtraction test: build a case where an idea passes from me to you with no token changing hands, no expression copied, no confidence broken, no market position taken, and nothing lost to me — then ask whether anyone has been wronged. My go-to version uses tech that doesn't exist yet, call it a concept link: you strap it on, it copies a fully formed idea straight out of my head into yours, and I still have mine afterwards. Nothing of mine went missing, so on my view nothing was taken, so there was nothing there to own. There might be a real wrong in that case — you got into my head without permission — but it's a privacy or bodily-integrity wrong, not a property one.
