I want the specification, not the survey. Suppose someone accepts a lexical-threshold form of negative utilitarianism: there is a grade of suffering such that any quantity of suffering above that grade lexically outweighs any quantity of enjoyment, or of milder suffering, below it. What would a theory of hedonic commensurability have to deliver before such a view could be plugged into a decision procedure — say, one used by a regulator ranking animal-welfare or public-health interventions, where every available option mixes a little severe suffering with a great deal of mild enjoyment?

Give me the requirements, each with the case or consideration behind it, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are preferences of a particular camp. The ones I can see so far, which you should feel free to correct, split, or add to:

- whether pleasure and pain are ends of a single axis or two axes that can come apart, and if two, what supplies the bridge that lets a claim of lexical priority across them mean anything;
- what fixes the threshold — peak intensity, duration-integrated load, predictability, something about the subject — and what makes the cut fall there rather than next door, given that the underlying quantity looks continuous;
- whether the threshold applies to episodes or to aggregates, i.e. whether enough mild suffering in enough individuals eventually crosses it;
- what the theory says when the probability of crossing is small but non-zero, since lexical priority threatens to leave every ordinary option unrankable;
- which transformations of the hedonic scale the resulting ordering is invariant under, and whether the threshold floats when the reporting scale is recalibrated;
- which temporal profile is the currency, given that moment-by-moment and retrospective assessments of the same episode diverge;
- the distinction between a pain's badness for the one who has it and the badness of the state of affairs containing it, so that consensual, deserved, or partly constitutive pains are not misdescribed.

Then tell me which pair of these you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, which single requirement excludes the most candidate theories, and whether anything is left standing. A two-line scorecard for the main contenders is optional; the criteria are the deliverable. I would rather have a list whose joint unsatisfiability is itself the result than one that quietly stacks the deck for a view I already hold.
