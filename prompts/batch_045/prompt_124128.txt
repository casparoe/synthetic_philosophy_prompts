I built the following argument for a reading group on metaphysical grounding, and I am now fairly sure it is unsound — but I cannot locate the defect, and every exit I can see costs me something I wanted. I would like a diagnosis.

Grant me the following background. Grounding is the metaphysical "in virtue of" relation: the bowl's brittleness obtains in virtue of the ionic bonding among its constituents; the strike obtains in virtue of the picketing. A fact is fundamental iff nothing grounds it; a full ground of a fact is a collection of facts that together suffice, non-causally, for it. I also grant the usual structural package: grounding is asymmetric, transitive and well-founded, and full grounds necessitate what they ground. Write ⟨G ground F⟩ for the fact that the facts G ground the fact F.

(1) Grounding is productive of facts: if the facts G ground the fact F, then the grounding fact ⟨G ground F⟩ obtains.
(2) Grounding necessitates: if G ground F, then necessarily, if G obtain then F obtains.
(3) Grounding is itself necessary: if G ground F, then necessarily G ground F. The relation does not hold in some worlds and fail in others, the way a causal relation does; if brittleness is grounded in ionic bonding here, it is so in every world in which both are present.
(4) So every grounding fact is a necessary fact: ⟨G ground F⟩ obtains at every world.
(5) Every fact is either fundamental or fully grounded, ultimately, in fundamental facts.
(6) Grounding facts come free: ⟨G ground F⟩ is itself grounded in G. The alternative is that each grounding fact is fundamental in its own right, and then the fundamental basis of the world contains a distinct fundamental item shadowing every derivative fact — fundamentality stops being sparse or elite, and the distinction between the fundamental and the derivative stops doing the work it was introduced to do.
(7) Modal status cannot increase across a grounding link: if a fact's full grounds all obtain contingently, then it obtains contingently. Equivalently, a necessary fact has no wholly contingent grounds.
(8) Take any grounding link with contingent grounds: let G be contingent and suppose G ground F. By (4) ⟨G ground F⟩ is necessary; by (6) it is grounded in G; by (7) it is contingent. Contradiction. So no contingent fact grounds anything.
(9) Now let F be any contingent grounded fact — the brittleness, say. By (5) it is ultimately grounded in fundamental facts. If all of those were necessary, then by (2), transitivity and well-foundedness, F would be necessary too. So at least one of F's ultimate grounds is contingent. But by (8) no contingent fact grounds anything. Contradiction.
(10) Hence no contingent fact is grounded: every contingent fact is fundamental.

That conclusion is unacceptable, and it gets worse. My friend points out that nothing in the reasoning is specific to grounding. Substitute any metaphysically necessary constitutive relation — composition, constitution, realization, set formation, truthmaking — and the same eleven steps yield the same result: no contingent fact stands in any constitutive dependence relation to any other, so there is no hierarchical structure in contingent reality at all. Every cup, every strike, every mind would be fundamental, and the whole research programme of asking what grounds what would be empty for exactly the subject matter it was designed for. I take that to be a reductio of the argument rather than a discovery.

The two claims I most want to keep are (2) and the denial of (10): I want grounding to necessitate, and I want the brittleness to be grounded in the bonding. Beyond that I am open.

Please tell me: which step do you reject, why did that step look like a safe one to help myself to, and what does rejecting it commit me to? I would also like the alternatives priced, not just listed — what does each of the other plausible rejections cost, and is there any way to keep (2) and still block (10)? If you think the chain of reasoning breaks somewhere rather than resting on a false premise, say exactly where and what the missing premise would have to be.
