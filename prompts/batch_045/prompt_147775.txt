For close to forty years I earned my living as a systems neuroscientist, first working on recurrent processing in the visual system and later on the relation between perceptual confidence and choice behaviour. In retirement I have begun reading philosophy of mind seriously, and I keep coming to grief over higher-order theories of consciousness: the family of views on which what makes a mental state conscious is that the state is the target of a suitable higher-order representation of it — in a thought-like format on the best-known version, in a perception-like format on a variant I have also encountered, and in either case apparently doing something more than merely accompanying the first-order state.

What I want is a translation, not an exposition. Please restate the theory, as faithfully as you can manage, in the vocabulary I actually think in: feedforward and feedback pathways, reentrant cortico-thalamic loops, hierarchical predictive coding, population codes and their downstream readout, the distinction between first-order sensitivity and metacognitive sensitivity in signal detection theory, lesion and stimulation evidence, decoding analyses, and above all the distinction in my field between a signal being present in a circuit and that signal being used by anything. Please also translate, if you can, the distinctions the theory draws between a state being conscious and a creature being conscious. Where you have to substitute a claim my vocabulary can express for the claim the theory actually makes, say so explicitly rather than letting the substitution pass as translation.

Then I would like the translation exercised on three cases, which are where I suspect the vocabulary either bites or fails to:

1. A system in which the first-order state is present and a higher-order state carrying accurate information about it is also present, but the higher-order state drives nothing at all — no report, no confidence-guided behaviour, no downstream control; a readout that is never read. Is the first-order state conscious according to the theory?

2. The timing difficulty, put in my terms: the feedback signal that would carry the higher-order state arrives some tens of milliseconds after the feedforward sweep, and the first-order state it would have to be about has already been replaced. What is conscious, and when?

3. A higher-order state that targets a first-order state that is not there: a monitoring stage continuing to issue confident verdicts after the stage it monitors has been silenced.

For each case, tell me what the theory is committed to, what you had to add in order to extract any verdict at all, and whether the addition is one a defender of the theory would grant.

Finally, and this is the part I care about most, I would like a residue report. Having done the translation, what is left over — what does the theory assert that cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all? My own candidates are three: the claim that the higher-order state does not merely cause or correlate with consciousness but constitutes it; the theory's need for some condition distinguishing a genuine higher-order state from a stray or confabulated one, something in the neighbourhood of warrant, non-inferentiality, or being formed for the right reason; and phenomenal character itself, the what-it-is-like. Tell me which of these are genuinely inexpressible in my terms and which only seemed so. And then give me your candid answer to the question I cannot settle for myself: once rendered in neural vocabulary, is the higher-order theory still distinguishable from the first-order recurrent-processing and global-broadcasting views it is meant to be competing with? If it is not, I want to know whether that is a limitation of my vocabulary or an indication that the disagreement was never, in the first place, a disagreement about mechanism.
