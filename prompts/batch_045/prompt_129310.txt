me and my mate were up too late last night arguing about functionalism and multiple realizability. his line was that the multiple realizability argument is circular: we only think an octopus or a silicon chip could be in pain because we've allready signed on to the functionalist account of what pain is, so it can't be an independent reason to dump the identity theory. i think he's wrong but i kept restating myself instead of answering him.

can you write me a sestina that does something with the argument instead of just naming it? six end words — pain, role, silicon, octopus, weather, qualia — rotated in the usual pattern, plus a three-line envoi where two of them share each line. give each stanza a different candidate realizer: a human nervous system first, then the octopus, then silicon, then a crowd with walkie-talkies if you want one more, and then one i want you to invent — a big distributed system like a weather front that only instantiates the machine table approximately, so no single part of it is ever in the state the table names, it's smeared across the whole thing and only averages out over time. the form should be doing the work the way the form in a villanelle does: the words keep coming back in different company, so the role stays put while the stuff that occupies it keeps changing seats.

I want the closing to register his circularity objection honestly rather than pretend the poem has settled it — make the reader feel how "same role, different stuff" might already contain the conclusion it's supposed to support. and it has to be an actual poem: someone who's never heard of the debate should still get something out of reading it, while someone who has should find it right rather than just decorated.
