I've recently become a trustee of a tiny local charity — I got pulled into it after a couple of years of my own chronic pain nonsense — and we've got a one-off bequest of about £20k to split between two things: a peer support group for people living with chronic pain, and a summer playscheme/befriending thing for kids who'd otherwise be stuck indoors the whole holidays.

One of the other trustees, who's very into the effective altruism stuff, keeps ending the discussion with some version of 'you can't weigh pain and pleasure on the same scales' — suffering and happiness just aren't symmetric, therefore the pain group should get the lot. He also throws around the phrase "lexical threshold", which I've half-gathered means something like: past a certain level of suffering, no quantity of the good stuff compensates. Maybe I'm being thick, but that sounds like a different claim from the first one, or else the same claim in jargon, and I genuinely can't tell which.

What I'd like is a menu rather than a verdict. What are the distinct precise things his slogan could be asserting — about whether pain and pleasure are commensurable at all, about their relative weight, about duties to prevent bad stuff vs duties to bring about good stuff, about how the numbers behave out in the tails, whatever else belongs on the list — and for each one: is it a platitude, is it a real substantive position someone actually defends, or is it just false? And the bit I really need: which of those readings, if any, would actually get him the conclusion that the pain group gets everything?

Two things I keep tripping over. First, if they genuinely aren't comparable, don't you just get paralysis rather than a verdict in favour of the pain group? Second, the health economics people do trade these off numerically all the time — QALY-ish scales where a year in bad pain scores low and states worse than death go negative — so doesn't that settle it that they are comparable, or is there a real gap between "we can put numbers on both" and "one unit of pain is genuinely exchangeable for n units of pleasure"?

Also, even on his own terms: is a playscheme for isolated kids obviously an *adding happiness* project rather than a *removing misery* one? Because if it's the second, his line seems to point the other way, or at least stop helping him.

Not after links or a reading list, just the breakdown — I need to be able to say something at the next meeting that isn't just vibes.
