The immediate thing: my department gives out a small six-month "topic development" grant for PhD students — money to turn a half-baked idea into a dissertation prospectus. I need a ~1000-word proposal for it. Here's the half-baked idea I want turned into something sharp.

The Darwin/Turing move — call it the strange inversion: you explain the appearance of understanding as the product of processes that have none of it, competence first and comprehension only later, if ever — normally gets applied *once*, to whatever capacity is in question: seeing, designing, reasoning, whatever. But it seems to me it has to be applied twice or it never gets going, because comprehension is itself a capacity. Producing reasons on demand, answering "why" questions, catching your own mistakes, teaching someone else — those are all things an organism *does*. So either the account of where comprehension comes from is itself comprehension-free, in which case it's unclear what has been explained, or there's some floor where the inverting has to stop, and nobody has said in a non-question-begging way where that floor is or what's special about it.

Second thing I can't get straight, possibly the same point: I suspect people run together two different things under "comprehension" — (a) generating accounts at all, and (b) those reports mattering, i.e. being the kind of output that can be wrong in a way that has consequences for whatever produced it, being *accountable* for them. My hunch is that only (b) is doing the real work in our intuitions, and that (b) is a social, second-personal property rather than something inside the head — which would explain both why we withhold comprehension from the models and why we withhold it from my dad's piano playing, but for completely different reasons. I genuinely can't tell whether that's a real distinction or just me inventing a difference to save the phenomenon.
