I run the discussion section for a sophomore cogsci/CS seminar, and next week we're on the GEB chapter about where meaning is located -- the one with the phonograph record and the ancient inscription examples. The students arrive thinking the answer is obvious, half of them convinced it's in the artifact and half that it's in whoever's doing the reading, and I want one case where both of them are clearly half right at the same time, in the same example.

Quick vocab fix so we're on the same page for the rest of this: frame message = whatever tells you that there is a message here at all and what kind of object you're looking at; outer message = the code or format that lets you turn the marks into candidate content; inner message = the content you end up with once the decoding is done.

Build me one case. What it has to do:

- ordinary and present-day -- no aliens, no lost languages, no enemy codes, no scifi gadgets -- and short enough to put on one slide, a paragraph or two
- two interpreters, both fully competent, who agree on the frame message and who have the whole outer message. The disagreement has to survive me granting them unlimited time, complete knowledge of the code, and as much computing power as they want. Nothing in the scenario should be fixable by going off and looking something up.
- they end up with two incompatible inner messages from the same artifact: not the same content described at two levels of detail, but two that can't both be right
- the disagreement can't be lexical or semantic ambiguity, vagueness, indexicality, or implicature, and it can't be missing context. "They just need more context" and "that word is ambiguous" both count as you dodging.
- and the location of meaning has to split inside the one case: for one part of the content the "it's in the object, just look at the marks" answer should feel obviously correct, and for another part the "it's the frame / it's what the interpreter is doing with it" answer should feel obviously correct

After the scenario: walk through frame, outer, and inner for each of the two readings; tell me which single feature of the case is load-bearing and show me that pulling that feature out collapses it into something boring (plain ambiguity, or a case where the frame message is just missing, or a case where there's no real disagreement); then give me the strongest version of the objection from a student who insists the inner message is fully determined by the artifact plus the code, and what I say back without just asserting my own reading.

Last two things: flag what the case can't show -- I don't want to overclaim in front of them -- and tell me what to do if someone shrugs and says "so meaning is in the whole chain, done." Is that the right answer or is it a cop-out, and how do I tell the difference in the room? Also give me the one question I should be ready for that would eat the whole 20 minutes.
