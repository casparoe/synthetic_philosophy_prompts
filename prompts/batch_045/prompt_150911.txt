hey — so a few of us in my reading group have been arguing about insecticides for something like eight months now and I'm fairly sure we've been talking past each other the whole time. Two rough camps: one thinks the sheer number of invertebrates killed by agricultural and household insecticides is a moral emergency (and that the mode of death matters — neurotoxic poisoning plausibly involves a long stretch of distress, versus starvation or predation), the other thinks insecticides are one of the load-bearing tools of crop protection and disease control, that they plausibly spare land and wild habitat, and that we're way too uncertain about invertebrate sentience to be doing anything dramatic on the strength of it. What I want is the document they should have produced instead of all those arguments: a joint statement from an adversarial collaboration between them. Numbered section of claims both can sign (about the scale of the numbers, about how uncertain the sentience evidence is in both directions, about knock-on effects on non-target invertebrates and on the birds/bats/fish that eat them, about resistance and resurgence, and about the fact that the real comparison is insecticides versus the actual alternatives — tillage, host-plant resistance, biological control, habitat manipulation — not versus doing nothing). Then a disagreements table where each row is phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own view (not the other side's caricature of it). Then the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling them would move at least one camp — and for each one, the concrete case or measurement both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance, before anyone sees the result. Feel free to invent the cases and just state the assumptions inside them; I don't want anything here that needs fact-checking, so a clearly hypothetical cotton or rice system with made-up numbers is better than a real one.

Two things I'd especially like handled. First: I've been using a made-up unit in our meetings that I call a sentience-weighted insect-day (number of insects alive times some credentialed probability that they have valenced states, integrated over time) and it keeps generating weird results. Please either use it, fix it, or say why it's a bad unit — but the population-level accounting has to be there, because I think a lot of our argument is secretly about whether spraying raises or lowers the total number of insects alive in the medium run, once you count the acute kill, the suppression of later generations, resurgence and secondary pest outbreaks, resistance, and whatever the land-sparing effect does. Second: I suspect each camp has a concession it really doesn't want to make — if insect lives are on average net negative, then some spraying might be welfare-improving even by the welfare-first camp's own lights, and the crop-protection camp might have to admit that a poisoning death is worse than the death it replaces. Put those in as a short 'uncomfortable concessions' section at the end, and also flag any part of the dispute that's just verbal (I have a feeling 'counts morally' is doing about four different jobs in our conversations). somewhere between 900 and 1400 words, tables where tables are warranted, and please don't pad it with citations or a reading list — we've read enough, we need the argument mapped.
