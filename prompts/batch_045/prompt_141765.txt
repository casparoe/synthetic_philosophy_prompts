Hey — no academic background here, I came at this from meditation and from reading bits of Buddhist stuff online, so sorry if I'm reinventing something that already has a well-known name.

I keep noticing a move and I dont have a word for it. Whenever a "who?" question comes up, the answer comes back as a description of a process with no one in it. Who is thinking? No one — there are just thoughts arising and passing away. What carries over between lives, if there's no self? Nothing passes over, it's like one candle lighting another, and yet nothing is lost. Over and over, the same shape: the who gets replaced by a structure of events, and that replacement is offered as the answer rather than as an admission that there isn't one.

What makes me want a name for it is that I hear the exact same shape in places where it doesn't feel like an insight at all. When my dad died, someone told me he lives on in everyone he affected and in everything he set in motion — and part of me thought, that's just a polite way of not saying he's gone. When a company does something harmful and everyone says no one decided it, it just came out of how the place was run, we hear that as a dodge, not as metaphysics. So the same move is treated as the deepest thing there is in one setting and as evasion in another, and I cant tell what the difference is supposed to be.

And maybe this is the same point: I can't come up with anything that would count as evidence either way. No observation would show me that there is a self, and no observation would show me that there isn't one. That makes me suspect the disagreement is about what we're willing to count as a self rather than about what there is.

Is there an established concept for this move — answering a question about who by dissolving the who into events — and for the two ways it can land? And how close is it to the things it resembles but isn't? I'd rather have the carving than the label: what exactly is this, what is it not, and where are its edges. If nothing established fits, say so and give me a name and a definition that separates it from its neighbours.
