I've been chewing on this since a reading group on Thursday and I can't locate the mistake, though I'm sure there's one somewhere.

(1) Thomson's lamp: it starts off and gets toggled at 1/2, 3/4, 7/8, ... of a minute in, so all the toggling is over by 1 minute.
(2) Every toggle is followed by another toggle, so there's no last toggle.
(3) The lamp is on at 1 minute only if there was a last toggle and it was a toggle to on (and likewise, mutatis mutandis, for off).
(4) So neither state fits: there's no consistent state for the lamp at 1 minute.
(5) A lamp that's still there at 1 minute is either on or off.
(6) So the sequence can't be completed — the supertask is impossible. I'm happy with everything up to here.
(7) But none of this can really be about lamps, or about toggling in particular. By (2), what breaks the case is just that there's no last step, and every infinite sequence of tasks lacks a last step.
(8) So no infinite sequence of tasks can ever be completed.
(9) Which is mad. A dimmer turned down to half its previous brightness at those same moments has a perfectly determinate state at 1 minute — off, or as good as — and it has no last step either. And a lamp switched at noon every day from now until forever has no last switching too, and there's nothing remotely impossible about that.
(10) So (6) is false: the toggling can be completed, and the lamp is on or off at 1 minute, the story just doesn't tell us which.

I'd much rather keep (6) than (10), and I'd rather give up (7) than (5), but I can't see what's slippng between (2) and (8). Which step do I reject, why did it look safe, and what does rejecting it cost me — especially if I want to go on saying both that the dimmer case is fine and that Thomson's lamp isn't?
