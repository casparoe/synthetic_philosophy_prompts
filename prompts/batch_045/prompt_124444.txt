I'm in a seminar this term on AI, rationality and ethics, and we've got a short response paper due where we're supposed to take some popular piece on AI risk and say whether it gets the philosophy right. A friend sent me a long-read article on why superintelligent systems might be dangerous, and I want to use it, but I can't tell which parts are standard and which parts the writer amped up. I'm reconstructing the key paragraph from memory, so the wording is mine but I think I've got the content:

"The orthogonality thesis says intelligence and goals are completely independent: any level of intelligence can be combined with any goal, so a superintelligent system could be every bit as dedicated to manufacturing paperclips as to curing cancer. That is why we cannot assume that an AI smarter than us will share our values, or that it will get more reasonable as it gets smarter. Instrumental convergence is the companion claim: whatever its final goal turns out to be, a sufficiently intelligent agent will always end up pursuing the same short list of sub-goals — staying alive, grabbing more compute, more energy and more resources, and resisting being switched off — because those help with everything. In other words, convergent instrumental goals are close to universal laws of rationality: an agent that didn't seek power would be failing to be rational, so intelligence pushes every agent, whatever it wants, toward basically the same behaviour. Put the two together and you get the alignment problem — the first superintelligence will try to take over the world, not out of malice, but because power-seeking is simply what rational agents do."

The "universal laws of rationality" line definately set off alarm bells for me, and so did the idea that intelligence pushes everyone toward the same *final* values, which sounds like it contradicts orthogonality rather than following from it. But I'm not confident about anything here, and I also don't trust my sense of where the popularizer is just simplifying for a general audience versus actually changing the claim.
