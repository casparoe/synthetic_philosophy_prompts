Been chewing on the eliciting-latent-knowledge stuff again, and I want to turn one half-baked idea into a real proposal for a small three-month independent grant, so help me write it up.

Setup I'm assuming, no need to rehash it: there's a big predictor trained to predict future frames of a sensor stream, the canonical case being camera footage of a vault with a diamond in it, and we bolt on a reporter whose job is to tell us what's actually going on in the world. The thing everyone worries about is the reporter drifting into the "what would a human say about this" / "what would make the human's later impressions come out looking right" direction. Most proposals I've seen try to make that strategy structurally unavailable: constrain the reporter, penalize it for complexity, train it on cases where humans can check the answer and hope that generalizes out to the cases where they can't.

My worry is that all of those quietly assume the target is a faithful, human-legible restatement of the model's latent state. But if the model's world-model is richer than the space of things we can say in human concepts, then no reporter can be both fully faithful and legible to us, and then the difficulty isn't deception at all — it's lossy compression. So reframe: don't ask for the truth, ask for a translation with an explicit distortion budget, relative to a specified class of decisions we'd take on the basis of the report. Then the real question is which features of the latent state have to survive translation for the report to be safe to act on, and how you can tell when you've compressed away too much. I also want the diagnostic this suggests: take two reporters that agree on every human-checkable case and disagree off-distribution, and ask whether the disagreement is expressible in human terms or whether its an aliasing artifact with no human-legible description at all — if the second kind is pervasive, the standard statement of the problem is asking for something incoherent.

Give me roughly two pages: the question stated precisely enough to have a determinate answer, why the constrain-the-reporter approaches don't settle it even granting they work, what I'd actually do month by month including the toy setups, the possible conclusions and what would decide between them, what deliverable would be useful to someone deciding whether to trust a system, and an honest failure condition — including the possibility that the reframe just moves the bump under the rug.
