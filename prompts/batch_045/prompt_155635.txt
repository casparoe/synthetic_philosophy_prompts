Next Thursday I'm giving a seminar to the Cognition and Consciousness group in our department — about twenty-five minutes of talk and twenty of questions — and I'd like a prep sheet for the question period rather than help with the talk itself. My thesis is that Dennett's Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction is not, as he takes it to be, a distinction without a difference, and that the timing of experience is a perfectly good empirical question even though it is not the question the Cartesian theater picture was asking. The route in is postdictive phenomena: in the color phi setup a red spot is flashed at one position and, a fraction of a second later, a green spot at a nearby position, and subjects report a single spot travelling between the two positions that changes color partway along its trajectory — that is, they report green at a place and time at which the green stimulus had not yet been shown. There are related cases (the cutaneous rabbit, the flash-lag and Fröhlich effects) where later input seems to determine what was experienced earlier. Dennett's move is that the choice between a Stalinesque story, on which the content is manufactured late and no earlier draft survives, and an Orwellian story, on which there was an earlier experience that memory then rewrites, makes no first-personal difference and therefore leaves nothing for a theory of consciousness to care about. My counter is to replace the phenomenal-finish-line picture with what I call an availability criterion: a content is conscious at a time just in case, at that time, it is available in parallel — without any further inference or reconstruction — to voluntary report, to working-memory-dependent choice, and to motor preparation. On that reading the Orwellian story is testable rather than empty, and I'll be presenting a probe design for testing it: interrupt the trial at variable delays straddling the second flash with a speeded, task-irrelevant forced choice about the travelling spot's current color, and look for whether the choice tracks pre-revision or post-revision content, and whether the reaction-time distribution shows a discrete transition or only a smooth drift. I have no data yet; the talk is a proposal, and I'd rather hear now what will sink it.

The room matters a lot for this, so let me describe it. Roughly half the audience are vision scientists and psychophysicists who know the postdiction literature properly and will object in methodological vocabulary — masking and SOA jitter, criterion shifts and response bias, whether a forced choice measures perception or a post-hoc inference the subject draws under time pressure, whether my probe is itself a second stimulus that changes the very trajectory I'm sampling. The other half are philosophers: two of them are committed multiple-drafts theorists in Dennett's lineage, who will say that my availability criterion merely re-describes whatever the brain's editorial processes happen to have produced, that the clause about no further inference smuggles in exactly the privileged finish line I claim to have abandoned, and that if several partially-processed drafts coexist then asking which one is conscious is stipulation rather than discovery. There is also one neuroscientist working on conscious access in the global-workspace tradition, who will want to know whether my transition point corresponds to any measurable neural event and what my account predicts if it does not; and two philosophers of a more old-school phenomenal-realist stripe who are friendly to the idea that there is something it is like before the revision but who think the availability criterion changes the subject — that I have made consciousness a dispositional, functional property and then congratulated myself for answering a question about experience. Several graduate students will try to drag the discussion toward Libet and conscious intention.

Finally, include the objection I'm hoping nobody asks — I have a feeling it's the one where my three availability channels (report, working memory, action) turn out to yield three different transition times, which would leave me with exactly the multiplicity I was trying to argue against — but say it in the strongest form you can, and tell me what my best available answer would be if it does come.
