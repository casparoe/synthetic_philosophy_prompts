I've been reading the rationalist blogs and forums for something like fifteen years, so my native vocabulary for anything involving information is Bayesian: priors and posteriors, evidence measured in log-likelihood-ratio bits, log-scoring and calibration, expected value of information, Solomonoff-style complexity penalties, the map/territory distinction. I can follow algebra in the log domain; I cannot follow a partition function. My thermodynamics is at the level of a well-read blogger.

Here is the physics as I understand it, so you don't have to teach it to me — correct me if I have it wrong, briefly, but don't spend the answer on physics. Take the one-molecule engine version of Maxwell's demon. The demon's measurement of which side the molecule is on can in principle be performed reversibly, at no thermodynamic cost; the bill comes due not at acquisition but at disposal, because to run another cycle the demon must reset its one-bit memory to a standard state, and resetting one bit against a bath at temperature T dissipates at least kT ln 2 into that bath. The generalization I have seen floating around: the work extractable per cycle is bounded by kT ln 2 times the mutual information between the demon's memory and the molecule's state, while the erasure cost is kT ln 2 times the full entropy of the memory — so a demon with a redundant or noisy memory, where H of the memory exceeds its mutual information with the molecule, pays more to forget than it earned by noticing.

What I want is a translation, not a metaphor. Restate the whole shape of that resolution — noticing is free, holding on is not, and the books balance because disposal costs at least what acquisition earned — in the vocabulary I actually think in, with the physics removed from the final statement, as faithfully as you can. Then give me the residue report: which parts translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion (and say explicitly what you had to bend to make them fit), and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. If the only way you can make a piece come out is to hand my Bayesian agent a finite memory, a body, or an energy budget, that is import rather than translation: do it if you must, but flag it as smuggling.

Three places where I suspect the translation leaks, and which I want you to press on specifically:

First, the gap between the two quantities. In my vocabulary information is pure gain: value of information is never negative in expectation, and as I understand it the expected improvement in my log score from an observation is exactly the mutual information between that observation and the thing I care about. So the mutual information looks like a genuine bridge — the same number bounds the demon's winnings and mine. But the demon's cost is the other number, the entropy of its memory, and I have no term for that at all. Nothing in Bayesian updating charges me for carrying a belief, however fine-grained it is. Where do the bits my demon wrote down that were not about the molecule go in my framework? Does my vocabulary contain anything that plays that role, or is the gap simply invisible to an agent with unbounded memory?

Second, agent-relativity. Mutual information is a property of a joint distribution and does not care whether anyone can read it. My epistemic winnings are relative to my prior and my likelihood function, and if my likelihood is misspecified I can be made worse off by updating. So consider a demon holding a record that is strongly correlated with the molecule but that it cannot decode — a scrambled or encrypted bit, high mutual information, no usable coupling between what it holds and what it can do. Does the physics bound care whether the demon can act on the correlation? Is information in the demon argument correlation, or correlation-available-to-an-agent? And if it is the former, what happens to my bridge when the agent on my side is miscalibrated?

Third, timing. Consider a demon that never erases: either it is used once and thrown away, or it writes each result to fresh tape and never reuses a bit. As I understand it the cost reappears somewhere else — in manufacturing a blank demon, or in the accumulating tape. In my vocabulary there is no difference between paying at acquisition, at retention, and at manufacture, because an update is a single event with no duration and no cost. Does the time-shape of the payment survive translation at all, or is it purely a fact about physical implementation?

Finally, the verdict. I keep hearing from people in my circles that the real moral here is that information is physical, and I want to know whether that is substantive or a pun on two senses of information once you have run the translation. Is the residue a defect of my framework — a missing term for the cost of keeping a map — and if so what is the smallest honest addition that would supply it? Or is it a sign that the physics result was doing less epistemic work than it looked like it was doing, and that the thermodynamic cost was always a fact about memory as a physical degree of freedom rather than about information as evidence? Tell me how I would go about deciding which, rather than just picking one.
