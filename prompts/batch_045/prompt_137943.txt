I'm working on a peice about moral trade — the idea that people who disagree about what's worth doing can each do better by their own lights if they do some of what the other side actually wants done. Concretely: two big philanthropic blocs, one that thinks the money is best spent on near-term global health, one that thinks it's best spent on cutting risks of astronomical future suffering. Fixed pots. Each thinks the other's priority is worth about a third as much, pound for pound, as its own. If they specialise and trade — each putting its whole pot into the other's cause — both end up better off by their own lights than if each had split its pot. Fine so far.

Here's the bit I can't get straight. The size of that gain is a function of how far apart the two views are; if they agreed completely, there'd be nothing to trade. So moral trade seems to hand each side a reason to want the other to go on being wrong — and, pushed a bit further, a reason to quietly fund the spread of a view you think is false, purely to keep a useful trading partner in business. I've started calling this the disagreement dividend, though maybe someones already got a name for it.

What I want is an essay on whether that's a genuine problem for moral trade or a cute artefact of the setup. Is wanting others to be wrong really what's going on, or is it only wanting them to care about something you don't, which seems harmless? Does it matter that a view you spread in order to create a trading partner leaks — to people who never trade with you, or to your own successors, whose values then shift? And does anything change when the trade is one-shot and unenforceable, say with a future generation or a civilisation you'll never meet?

Reach a verdict and defend it — I can't publish "it depends". And tell me how confident you are, and what would actually move you off it. If I've misdescribed how moral trade works, or the dividend collapses into something boring once you set it up properly, say so plainly instead of humoring me. Continuous prose only, please: no headings, bullets, bold or tables, because I need to be able to paste this into an email to my editor.
