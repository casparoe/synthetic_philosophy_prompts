Write a dialogue between two analysts at a hospital system who must make a recommendation about a new sepsis protocol and who disagree about which number to act on.

Take the case as given. A trial compared the new protocol N with the old protocol O. Randomization happened inside each of seventeen sites, so within any site the two arms are exchangeable and there is no confounding by indication anywhere. What differs is how many patients each kind of site contributed to each arm: the five tertiary hospitals enrolled 900 of the 1,000 patients on N and only 100 of the 1,000 on O, while the twelve community hospitals enrolled the reverse, because N requires monitoring only the tertiary sites could staff. Baseline mortality is far higher at tertiary hospitals. The result is a textbook reversal. In the tertiary stratum mortality is 40% on N against 45% on O; in the community stratum it is 10% on N against 12% on O; N is better in both. Pooled across all sites it is 37.0% on N against 15.3% on O, so N looks far worse overall.

One analyst, call her Reyes, holds that the pooled contrast is the number to act on: it is the intention-to-treat comparison the randomization actually licenses, it is the pre-specified primary analysis, and it answers the question the system is really asking, what happens if we roll N out the way the trial used it. The other, call him Farouk, holds that the pooled figure is an artifact of who happened to be enrolled where, that only the stratum-specific effects travel, and that the system should standardize to its own case mix; on a realistic 30/70 tertiary/community split that gives roughly 19.0% on N against 21.9% on O, reversing the verdict a second time.

Neither should be a caricature. Reyes knows about standardization and effect modification; Farouk knows that subgroup analyses are fragile, that seventeen sites may not share one effect, and that the pooled estimate has precision the stratum-specific ones lack. Have them answer each other's actual points, and have them press on the following:

First, whether standardize to your own target population is a real answer or merely a license to choose your weights and get any verdict you like, particularly if adopting N would itself change the mix through referral patterns, transfers, and which site a given patient ends up in.

Second, whether anything of the paradox survives once the design asymmetry is removed. Suppose both arms had identical site mixes, so that no reversal is possible on the risk-difference scale, and yet the pooled odds ratio still fails to match the stratum odds ratios because odds ratios are non-collapsible. Is that the same phenomenon or a different one, and what does the answer imply about the common practice of reporting adjusted odds ratios from a logistic regression as if the aggregate and the stratified estimates were competing estimates of one quantity?

Third, whether the puzzle is partly an artifact of a policy space that was never justified. The system is being asked to choose between N everywhere and O everywhere, when it could also choose N at tertiary sites and O at community sites; once that option is on the table the reversal stops looking like a paradox and starts looking like an assignment problem. Say what, if anything, makes that move unavailable or illegitimate: equity between sites, site type as a proxy for something invisible at the bedside, the cost and error of running two protocols, or something else.

Do not let either of them retreat to the claim that it depends on the causal graph. They agree on the graph; that is stipulated. The disagreement is about which quantity the graph tells you to estimate and about what the decision problem is.

After the dialogue, return a JSON object with exactly these fields, each holding one or two sentences unless a controlled value is requested:

reversal_mechanism: what generates the reversal in this design, given that no confounding is present.
pooled_side_best_argument: the strongest version of Reyes's position that a competent opponent still has to answer.
stratified_side_best_argument: the strongest version of Farouk's position.
standardization_verdict: one of principled, arbitrary, principled but underdetermined, or other, followed by the reasoning.
non_collapsibility: one of same phenomenon, different phenomenon, or different but easily confused, followed by the reasoning.
policy_space: what admitting site-specific adoption does to the paradox, and the strongest reason to refuse it.
estimand: which quantity the hospital system should in fact be estimating, stated in one sentence.
graph_sufficiency: one of yes, no, or partly, on whether knowing the causal graph settles the dispute, followed by the reasoning.
bottom_line: whether this instance of Simpson's paradox is best understood as a statistical artifact, a causal one, or a decision-theoretic one, and why.
unresolved: what the dialogue leaves genuinely open.
