For the last fifteen years I have run a small volunteer project at our county historical society, recording older residents talking about the place they grew up in and typing up the transcripts. I have no academic background in history or philosophy; I just do the typing. But transcribing the same area over and over has made me keep noticing one shape of situation, and I don't have a word for it, and I'd like to know whether someone else has given it one.

Here are three instances. In the first, everyone in a mill town tells the story of a fire in which the owners locked the stairway doors on the night shift. Twelve years ago a retired insurance assessor went through the inspection files and found that the doors were not locked at all — they opened inward and jammed when the floor above came down. The story is wrong on the fact. But the assessor still tells the story the old way at the annual commemoration, and so does everyone else, and when a university historian published the correction the town did not dispute a word of it; they simply carried on, and treated her intervention as though she had misunderstood what a commemoration is. The owners were still negligent. Nobody denies that.

In the second, one family in the county has sent money to another family every Christmas for sixty-odd years because, as the story goes, the second family's grandfather hid the first family's grandfather for a winter and was ruined for it. A researcher checking enlistment and employment records found the two men were almost certainly in different regions that winter and met for the first time in 1953. The current generation knows this, has stopped telling the story as history, and has not stopped sending the money. The story is the only account anyone has of why the payments began, and when I asked one of them whether it now felt like a false reason for a real obligation, she said something I've been chewing on: that the story was never really a claim about 1943.

In the third, a hamlet kept the memory that men from the village were taken to a quarry and shot in the last year of the war. The regional archive had nothing, and the prefecture's papers for that year survived intact, so the silence counted as evidence against the story, and outside researchers said so plainly. Then in the nineties the remains turned up in the quarry. Here the same refusal to let the record adjudicate — the same shrug at the archive — turned out to be the right stance, which is what makes me think the pattern is not just stubbornness or denial with a nicer face.

In my own notes I've started calling these anchor stories and distinguishing two kinds: ones where the inaccurate detail carries a live commitment between people, and ones where the whole stance is a protest against who gets to say what happened. I don't know if that is one phenomenon or two, or whether the third case is the same thing at all.

So my question: is there an established name for a memory that a community goes on using after it has been shown, by evidence the community itself accepts, to be inaccurate in a specific detail — where the point is not that anyone doubts the evidence, and not that anyone is being prevented from saying so, but that the memory is doing something a corrected version couldn't do? If there is, I want more than the label: I want to know what exactly the phenomenon is, which nearby things it gets confused with and where the line falls between them, and in particular whether there is a principled difference between the case where the refusal is vindicated and the case where it isn't, or only a difference in how things turned out. If there is no name, I'd be grateful for a coined one with a definition and a statement of the boundary cases: what would have to be true for a case to stop being an instance — a state imposing the story from above, say, or the inaccurate detail costing a named living person something real.

Please write in continuous prose, as you would in a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables. And please don't give me a reading list or authors to look up; I have no way to chase them, and what I'm after is the shape of the idea rather than sources for it.
