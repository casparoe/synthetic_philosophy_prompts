After a long argument last night I still cannot tell what my friend thinks he is committed to about civil disobedience, and I would rather have a prediction about the next case than another round of general theory.

Here are his verdicts so far. Citizens who sit in at a segregated lunch counter, openly, nonviolently, and who let themselves be arrested: justified, and his paradigm case of civil disobedience. The same ordinance broken at night by people who leave no trace and deny it afterwards: unjustified, and the state is entitled to punish them. A taxpayer who withholds only the military share of her taxes, writes to explain why, and pays everything else: justified, but he insists this is conscientious refusal and not civil disobedience. Sabotage of machinery with no one injured, for a cause he agrees is grave: unjustified, even when done openly and followed by surrender to the police. A legal permanent resident, twenty years in the country, no vote, pays taxes, children are citizens, who joins the first sit-in and is arrested: justified.

The new case: a woman brought to the country as a child, fifteen years here, working and filing taxes, with no legal status and no route to obtaining one, who sits in against a law that lets police demand papers from anyone they stop. She acts openly, under her own name, does not flee and does not resist, and says in advance that she will accept the penalty. But the penalty is not a fine or a night in a cell; it is removal from the only country she has lived in as an adult, by a power she is protesting.

What will he say, and what is he actually running? I want the rule his five verdicts suggest, at least two rival rules that fit them equally well, what each predicts here both about justification and about whether this counts as civil disobedience at all, which prediction you think is best and why, and what further case would most cleanly separate the rules. If his precedents genuinely underdetermine the case, tell me which principle he and I would each be committing to with each answer.
