Next month I am giving a seminar to a quantum information theory group and I want a Q&A prep sheet, not a defence of the position. The room is two device-independent cryptography theorists, three experimentalists who run loophole-free Bell tests, one quantum-optics theorist, and one philosopher of physics who is sympathetic to my conclusion but thinks my way of stating it is obscurantist. I grant the experimental results and the closing of the detection, locality and freedom-of-choice loopholes; I am not claiming the data are wrong.

The thesis: the observed violations establish only what I call *predictive non-separability* — that no model in which a complete specification of the state in the common past makes the two outcomes independent of one another can reproduce the correlations — and this is not non-locality in the sense that would clash with relativistic causal structure. I argue that Bell's factorizability condition is a conjunction of two independent requirements: *parameter independence*, that the setting chosen on one wing does not alter the probability of the outcome on the other, which relativistic causal structure does require and which no experiment has violated; and *outcome independence*, the screening-off of each outcome from the other given the shared state and both settings, which I claim is a demand about explanation — that the shared state be a complete explanatory intermediary — rather than a claim about causal influence. Factorizability fails because the second fails. Giving up the second costs no causal claim anyone should care about; it costs a kind of explanation. A secondary and more tentative claim I want to test: the assumptions in device-independent security proofs are usually described as free or independent settings, when what they actually need is weaker, and my distinction lets one say precisely how much weaker.

Give me roughly eight objections in the order they will actually arrive, phrased the way this audience would phrase them — in their vocabulary of resources, no-signalling, measurement dependence, self-testing and security assumptions, not in the vocabulary of interpretation — and ordered by how much damage they would do rather than by how deep they are. For each: the concession I should make before I am asked, a two-minute reply, and the point at which the reply stops working if they push once. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks, and say which single unanswered objection should make me abandon the thesis rather than refine the answer. Finally, tell me where you think my own argument is most likely to be wrong as opposed to merely unfashionable, and what result or argument would lead you to reverse your advice.
