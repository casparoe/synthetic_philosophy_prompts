Take the 2008 crash and read it as a case study in factored cognition — the HCH-flavoured idea that you can beat a hard question by breaking it into sub-questions that one human (or one copy of yourself) can actually answer, then recombining the answers. I care less about the macro story then about the failure mode. Take as given: mortgages got chopped into tranches, each tranche was rated by asking narrow questions about default probabilities conditional on a model, the models leaned on defaults being roughly uncorrelated, and nobody near the top could really sanity-check the ratings. So where exactly did the decomposition break — bad questions, bad answers at the leaves, or bad recombination? And does the episode suggest a tree of sub-agents is structurally blind to correlations that only exist at the level of the whole system, given that the people who called it were mostly looking at housing as a whole rather than tranche by tranche? Then turn it round: what does it mean for HCH as an idealisation of deliberation — is there a fix (nodes forced to declare their background assumptions, sibling nodes cross-checking each other, something price-like), and does the fix still hold at depth, or does it just shove the bottleneck further up the tree? British English please.
