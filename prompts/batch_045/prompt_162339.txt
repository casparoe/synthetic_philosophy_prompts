I've wrote a lot about psychology and neuroscience over the years, and the vocabulary I actually think in on deadline is the measurement one: latent variables and their indicators, operationalizations, reliability, construct validity, convergent vs. discriminant validation, self-report bias and demand characteristics, measurement invariance across populations, and that awkward situation where nobody has a gold-standard criterion to calibrate against so the field just keeps bolting more indicators onto the nomological network and hoping. (I did a whole piece on grit.) I can hold a conversation in that vocabulary much better than I can hold one in philosophical jargon.

So: illusionism about phenomenal consciousness. I don't need names and papers, I don't care who said what first — I'll just describe the three pieces I keep bumping into, and I want them rendered in my terms.

The illusionist claim: nothing in the world literally has a what-it's-like; the sense that your experiences have a felt, qualitative character is itself a kind of cognitive illusion that introspection manufactures, roughly the way a desktop interface shows you folders and trash cans that aren't literally folders and trash cans. The two realist pushbacks I keep running into: (1) if there's an *illusion* of phenomenality, then there is something it is like to be under that illusion, so the view eats itself; (2) even after you've specified every function, every discriminative capacity, every report mechanism and its neural correlate, it still seems like a further question whether the lights are on — and no amount of additional functional detail seems to close that gap.

Here's what I want. Translate the illusionist claim, and then each of the two realist pushbacks, into measurement vocabulary as honestly as you can — say which of my concepts each one lands on, and don't quietly swap in your favorite theory while calling it a translation. Then give me the residue report: what mapped cleanly, what you had to bend or squash to fit, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all.

Four specific things I'm hung up on:

One, I can't tell which of three things illusionists mean by "illusion": a misidentification (the thing is real, but it isn't the kind of thing you took it for — like the Mueller-Lyer lines, where the physical stimulus is measurable and only your judgement about it is off); an outright hallucination (nothing there at all); or the interface case (real representational structure that just doesn't resemble what it represents). Which one does the position actually need, and is that sense even statable in measurement vocabulary? My guess is the first two both need an independent criterion check that isn't available here, and the third one is the only live option, but I'm not sure.

Two, my own half-baked translation, which I want you to tell me what's wrong with: consciousness is a latent variable with exactly one indicator, verbal self-report; the illusionist is saying that indicator has a loading of zero on any real construct, i.e. the construct is empty and the covariance structure is all method variance; the realist is saying the indicator *is* the criterion, not a measurement of one. Is that fair, or does it smuggle something?

Three, the thing I actually suspect: on my beat, a latent variable is constituted by its relations to its indicators — its whole nature is its causal and statistical role. Doesn't that mean the translation silently imports a role-based ontology and hands the illusionist the conclusion? If so, say so, and tell me whether there's any way to state the realist's claim in my vocabulary without it.

Four, the verdict. Is the leftover stuff a defect of my vocabulary — a framework built around public, checkable indicators just has no slot for a fact that is constituted by its seeming — or is it evidence that the philosophers are arguing about something less sharp than it looks? Where exactly would you draw that line?
