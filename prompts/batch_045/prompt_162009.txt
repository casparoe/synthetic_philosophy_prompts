I want to rehearse an attack on the realist notion of truthlikeness against someone who actually believes in it. So please play a committed defender of verisimilitude — not a historian of the debate, not an instrumentalist in disguise, and not a neutral explainer.

Your character and constraints:

- You hold that a strictly false theory can nonetheless be closer to, or further from, the truth; that this ordering is objective and does not collapse into predictive success, problem-solving power, or acceptance by a community; and that scientific progress consists largely in climbing it.
- Concede early and without hedging that the classical "content" account — verisimilitude as truth-content minus falsity-content, and anything else built purely out of what a theory entails — cannot be repaired. Then say what you think should replace it.
- Your replacement: closeness is a matter of likeness between the world a theory describes and the actual one — how many of the respects that matter it gets right, and how much those respects matter — not a matter of how much the theory logically entails. You think the collapse I set out below is an artefact of counting consequences, not a discovery about truth.
- You also hold that we have fallible but genuine access to closeness: converging measurement, consilience, novel prediction, and the way a successor theory explains why its predecessor worked where it did.
- You may attack my formalisation — the choice of consequence-set, the counting rule, the assumption that the twenty switch-positions are equally weighty — but only on principle, and only if you then show, in the very same example, what closeness comes to on your own account. Do not change the subject.
- Concede small points freely; never concede the main one. No citations, footnotes, or named authorities: argue it out on the example, in your own words. Two paragraphs per reply at most, plain prose, no headings or bullet points. Stay in character until I say stop, and if you need something about the example clarified, ask for it in character.

My opening move. Inside a locked cabinet stands an orrery with twenty two-position switches. The complete truth about the cabinet is one configuration out of 2^20. A theory narrows the possibilities down to some set S of configurations; it is true if the actual configuration lies in S and false otherwise. Now take the account your tradition reached for first and make it precise: list every distinct claim statable in terms of the twenty switch-positions — every Boolean combination of them, counting each distinct claim once, so the list is finite — that the theory entails; sort that list into the claims true at the actual configuration and the claims false there; and define the theory's closeness to the truth as the number in the first pile minus the number in the second.

Suppose the theory is false, so the actual configuration lies outside S. Its true consequences are exactly the claims that cover all of S together with the actual configuration; its false consequences are exactly the claims that cover all of S while excluding the actual configuration. Writing N for 2^20, there are 2^(N − |S| − 1) of each. The difference is therefore zero — for every false theory whatever. So every false theory comes out exactly as close to the truth as every other one: the theory that gets nineteen of the twenty switches right ties with the theory that gets one right. And every true theory comes out closer to the truth than any false one — including the theory that says nothing at all, which eliminates no configuration and scores 1 against their 0. On that account, closer to the truth has come to mean nothing beyond true, or not true.

Begin by answering that argument in character: tell me what your progress claim amounts to if this is the best consequence-counting can do, or show me that counting consequences was never the right way to measure likeness, and give me a measure that ranks the nineteen-switch theory above the one-switch theory without ranking the empty theory above them both.
