Hi — I'm giving a job talk next Friday (25 minutes plus 15 of questions) defending a chapter from my dissertation, and I need Q&A prep more than I need feedback on the paper at this point. The chapter tries to rescue a Menkiti-style view of personhood — the one where personhood isn't something you automatically have but something attained through being incorporated into a web of relations, the view Gyekye was pushing back against with his moderate communitarianism — by adding a move of my own. Roughly: I want to say the community doesn't *confer* personhood on anyone, it *custodies* it. I've been calling it escrow personhood, maybe badly. Personhood exists independently of whether anyone recognises it, but it can only be held, exercised, and kept warm by a community: for those who can't yet or can't any longer exercise it themselves (newborns, someone in a coma, late-stage dementia), the community holds the standing in escrow, with real duties — to ratify, to maintain, to hand it back — and if a community withholds or withdraws recognition, the community is simply in breach, and if the breach is bad enough the community forfeits the escrow and it passes to whoever will actually hold it. So "we never recognised them as a person" can never do justificatory work, which is the payoff I care about: I get relational constitution without making moral status hostage to whoever happens to be holding the clipboard. I've already got answers down for the two obvious ones in section 4 — the "so you've made infants non-persons" bit and the "this is just communitarianism with extra machinery" bit — so don't spend time on those.

The room: two bioethicists who work on moral status and think in terms of psychological capacities and think the whole relational tradition is sociologising a metaphysical question; a Kantian who'll insist personhood is a normative status you can't lose by being forgotten and will use words like second-personal and standing; a care theorist with disability-studies leanings who will want to know who exactly counts as the community doing the holding, who audits the custodian, and what this means for people whose dependencies are permanent; and one scholar of African philosophy who is sympathetic to Menkiti but thinks I've moralised what is really an ontological and developmental claim, and that Gyekye already gets me everything I need without the escrow apparatus. Plus two generalists on the committee who mostly want to know if the view hangs together.
