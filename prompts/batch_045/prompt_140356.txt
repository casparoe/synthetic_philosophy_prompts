I'm in a cog sci / philosophy of science seminar this term and we just spent two weeks on Dennett's cranes and skyhooks and his complaint about greedy reductionism, and the thing that annoyed everyone is how easy the distinction sounds in the seminar room and how hard it is to actually apply to a real explanation. The department puts out an end-of-term zine and I want to contribute a fake administrative document to it.

Please write it: a memo from our university's newly created Office of Explanatory Compliance announcing a Crane Certification Policy that all grant proposals must now comply with, followed by the certification form itself as numbered clauses. Around 700 words total. Deadpan university bureaucratese throughout, memo header with To/From/Subject/Date, plus a footer directing questions to a compliance address. No winking, no nudging, no "as if" — the voice should be completely sincere about something absurd.

The clauses should each be an exaggerated version of a real move in this debate, not invented ones:

- a definitions section that tries to make "skyhook" precise and quietly fails, e.g. via a load-bearing test: does the supposed crane actually do explanatory work or does it just relabel the thing to be explained
- a prohibition on "subcontracted skyhooks": a crane that is described in detail but whose own assembly is never explained (its parts are just stipulated to be there)
- a clause on "crane laundering": explanations previously retired as skyhooks being reintroduced at a lower level under new names (emergence, downward causation, "information", the hard problem of whatever)
- a regress clause: every crane must be supported by a further crane, unless it is licensed as a self-certifying basal mechanism — and the licensing procedure is of course never itself certified
- a clause that punishes greedy compliance, i.e. the Office's own overreach: researchers barred from using words like meaning, function, representation, selection or norm until they have produced a reduction, with the memo obviously not noticing that this would stop all research

Then three very short worked adjudications at the end: a filing on the irreducible normativity of reasons (rejected), a neuroscientist's "binding by synchrony" (flagged as a label rather than a crane), and the physics department's fundamental constants and laws (granted basal-crane status on condition that no one inquire what holds them up). That last one should be the biggest skyhook in the building and should be processed as the most routine item on the page.

Constraints: keep it fair — someone in my seminar who likes Dennett should feel got rather than misrepresented. The target is the discourse of compliance culture and the position, not any real person, so invent the Office and sign the memo with a made-up administrator. Don't break character to explain the jokes, and don't resolve anything at the end. It should also still read fine to a first-year who hasn't done the reading.
