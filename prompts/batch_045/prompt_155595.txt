I help run a small housing co-op, about 30 adults and no manager, and we're trying to write a house constitution: one written set of rules meant to settle any dispute that comes up (noise, guests, chore rotas, who pays when someones kid breaks the boiler) so that decisions don't just go to the loudest person in the meeting. The idea is that the rules plus a short list of ways to reason from them (majority of the mediation committee, precedent from past rulings, and so on) should in principle yield an answer for every case.

A housemate says Godel's incompleteness theorems show this is doomed: any rule system strong enough to do even basic arithmetic will have cases it can't settle, and if it's inconsistent it settles everything, which is worse. I half believe him, but I also suspect the analogy smuggles something in, because our rules are vague and we can always just vote to add a new one when we hit a gap. I've been reading about this on my own and there are two wrinkles I cant get clear on.

1. We were going to add a closure clause along the lines of: if the rules don't cover a case, do whatever the founding intent document says. I worry this just moves the bump in the carpet, the way adding the unprovable sentence as a new axiom is supposed to just give you a new system with its own unprovable sentence. Is that right, and does it make the clause pointless, or only mean it isn't a magic fix?

2. We also thought about adding a clause flatly stating that the rulebook contains no contradictions. My housemate says that's like a system asserting its own consistency and buys you nothing. I dont get that: surely adding it rules out the cases where the rules contradict each other?

One more thing: does any of this argue for giving up on written rules and just trusting the mediation committee's judgement? Someone told me Godel shows human minds can't be machines, and also that it shows there are truths we can never know, and I can't see how both could be right.

What are the most important implications of the incompleteness theorems for a project like ours? I care much less about the general metaphysics than about which parts of my housemate's argument actually carry over from formal systems to a rulebook and which parts break. Take a definite position on each of my two wrinkle questions and on the give-up-on-rules question, give your reasons, tell me where I'm going wrong, and say plainly which bits you're unsure about rather than hedging everything.
