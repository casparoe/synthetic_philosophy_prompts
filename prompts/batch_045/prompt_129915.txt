You are helping me draft the closing document of a two-year adversarial collaboration on the nature and moral role of personhood. The two parties have published against each other and have now agreed to stop talking past each other.

Party RC (the relational-constitutive position) holds that personhood is not a property an individual has in isolation but is constituted by standing in the right kind of relation to others, on the Ubuntu-style thought that a person is a person through other persons; relations are not merely how persons develop or flourish but part of what makes something one.

Party IC (the individualist-capacities position) holds that personhood is a status fixed by features of the individual, such as psychological capacities, agency, or species membership, and that although relations are causally indispensable for a human being to develop or to fare well, they are not partly constitutive of the status itself.

Write the joint statement both could sign. Do not appeal to scholarly authority, texts, or traditions: no citations are wanted, and every claim should be defended from the cases and distinctions themselves. Structure the document as follows.

1. A numbered section of agreed claims, eight to ten in number. Each must be non-vacuous: it should be a claim that a reasonable partisan of one side might well have denied before the collaboration, such as a claim about what the counterfactual history of a relationally deprived human being shows, or about what must be true for withdrawal of recognition to be a wrong rather than merely a loss.

2. A disagreements table: for each remaining disagreement, give the formulation of the relational side's view that RC would accept as its own, the formulation of the individualist side's view that IC would accept as its own, and a judgment of whether the disagreement is substantive or merely about words.

3. A cruxes table. For each crux, state the case or argument both parties agree would bear on it, and record each side's prediction in advance, together with what each side would count as evidence against its own position. The cruxes should include at least the following, using these jointly accepted cases:

- Adaeze, the last surviving member of her community, who retains full psychological capacities and her own communal self-understanding but now lives among strangers who refuse her any recognition whatsoever.
- The Unreached: a human being with full adult capacities who has been raised and still lives in total social isolation, with no relations in either direction.
- The Well-Related Artifact: an artificial agent, long since abandoned by its makers, that for two decades has been fully embedded in a human family and neighbourhood, is treated as one of us by them, and reciprocates.
- The Apostate: someone who continues to live among and be recognized by her community while knowingly repudiating its constitutive values; and, alongside her, someone whose only community is a criminal gang.
- The Distant Stranger: a person with whom one has no relation and never will have, who is in dire need.
- Cases at the margins of the human life cycle, and the question whether personhood admits of degrees and, if so, what follows for moral status.

The cruxes must include: whether the relational claim is constitutive or merely developmental; whether RC can rescue the Unreached by appealing to the relations she could have stood in, and whether that rescue collapses RC into IC and makes part of the dispute verbal; whether making standing depend on others' recognition leaves it hostage to their wrongdoing; whether RC must read relation normatively rather than as mere causal-social entanglement, and what that costs it; whether the two views must decide the Unreached and the Well-Related Artifact symmetrically, and what an asymmetry would show; and whether either view can generate duties to the Distant Stranger of a stringency comparable to its duties to intimates.

4. A short closing section recording which disputes turned out to be verbal, exactly which formulations had masked them, and any formulation either party withdrew during the collaboration.

Aim for 900 to 1,300 words, use the labels RC and IC throughout, and keep the register of a document both parties would be willing to put their names to: precise, unevasive, and free of diplomatic padding.
