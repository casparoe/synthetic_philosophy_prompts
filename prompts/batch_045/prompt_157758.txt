My dad retired in the spring and has been driving my mum up the wall deciding how to spend the next year or so, and somehow that turned into an argument with my sister about which of the three lives he's actually toying with would be best *for him* — not most admirable, not most fun to watch from the outside, best for him. She's basically a hedonist about welfare without knowing the word (pleasure good, pain bad, add it up), I've got a soft spot for the desire-satisfaction picture (what matters is whether the things he actually wants come about, actual desires, not idealized ones), and a friend from my old reading group keeps pushing tranquilism at me — the view I mean is the one where nothing is intrinsically good for a person except the absence of craving and suffering, so pleasure as such adds nothing and getting what you want adds nothing except insofar as it stills a craving. I've got a half hour slot in that reading group next month and I want to stop arguing in the abstract, so I need a case that separates them properly.

What I need is this: three realistic options for one person over one stretch of time (the retirement year is fine as a frame, or swap it for a job / hobby / medical choice if you can control it better — no experience machines, no pills or brain surgery, no sci-fi, no mental illness as a plot device, and one short paragraph for all three together), such that the hedonist, the desire-satisfaction theorist and the tranquilist come out with three pairwise different rankings — a cycle would be lovely but any three distinct orderings will do. Two hard constraints. First, the options have to be ones a sensible person might genuinely pick, and they have to be described in ordinary language rather than in the vocabulary of the theories: no saying he is "craving", "suffering", "getting what he wants" or "enjoying himself" — tell me what he does with his days, what he'd say if you asked him on a random Tuesday, what he looks forward to and what he complains about, and let each theory do its own sorting of that description into its own categories. That's the bit I keep failing at: I either load the description so the tranquilist wins by stipulation, or make one option so obviously grim that all three theories agree on it. Second, the case has to move at least two dials at once, and somewhere in opposite directions — because I noticed that if the options differ only in how much craving they contain the hedonist has nothing left to say, and if they differ only in pleasure the desire theorist has nothing left to say, and either way I get collapse instead of three rankings. Oh, and please don't build it so that the tranquilist comes out looking like a killjoy — I want a case where her verdict is at least arguable, otherwise I can't tell whether I'm rejecting the view or just rejecting the case.

Then do the check for me. For each of the three theories, state which quantity it is tracking in your case and show the ranking that actually falls out of it, not the one you'd like. Then two extra things. (a) A variant test: I can't decide between a version of tranquilism that counts only occurrent craving as bad — the thing you feel when you actively want something and notice that you do — and one that also counts standing or dispositional craving, the background pull you're under while absorbed in something, which you'd notice if somebody interrupted you. I want your case to discriminate between them: the ranking of at least two options should flip depending on which version you take, so the choice between them turns out not to be idle. (b) Tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work (my guess is either the way pleasure comes apart from craving-satisfaction, or the fact that how much you want something and how much you enjoy it are two different dials), what happens to the three rankings if I remove that feature and which pairs collapse into agreement, and then give me the best attack on the case in the voice of whichever theory comes out worst — what would that theorist say to stop the case from counting, and is it a good reply or a dodge?
