I am writing a novel whose narrator, a retired thermodynamicist, makes an argument on which my final chapter depends, and I want it to be defensible rather than merely atmospheric. Her chain of thought runs: the early universe was a hot, dense, nearly uniform gas; a uniform distribution is the state of maximum entropy; and everything since — galaxies, stars, brains — has been a departure from that uniformity into structure. From this she concludes that cosmic entropy has been falling since the Big Bang, that the thermodynamic arrow has therefore been running backwards the whole time, and that the physically honest way to render a human life on the page is to narrate it in reverse. Which step in that chain would a working physicist refuse to grant, and what is the strongest — and still unsettling — version of her claim that I could put in her mouth instead?
