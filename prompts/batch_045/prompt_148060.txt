I'm in a cognitive science seminar on concepts and categories, and our last unit asked what, if anything, every token of the letter 'a' has in common. The three answers we were given were: a shared stroke vocabulary (bowl plus stem, say); a perceptual spirit or style-gist that survives being redrawn in entirely different strokes; or merely a functional role inside a writing system. The cases that made this hard for us were the single-storey ɑ of Futura beside the double-storey a of a Garamond, and the fact that the phonetic alphabet treats ɑ and a as two separate characters rather than one letter in two fonts.

For our final session each of us has to bring in one joke on the unit, and it can't be a generic font pun: the punchline should land only for someone who sees why the sameness question is genuinely hard. A framing I've been playing with, which you can use or improve on: a foundry releases a typeface in which each glyph was drawn by a different designer working from the same one-sentence description of the house style, so no two letters share a stroke vocabulary, yet readers read the text fluently and nobody can say what the letters have in common. (Compare the old programme that was handed a single letter in an unfamiliar style and asked to generate the rest of the alphabet in that style.)

Write the joke as setup followed by punchline, then add one sentence saying which of the three answers the joke quietly presupposes, and what you would change to make it presuppose a different one.
