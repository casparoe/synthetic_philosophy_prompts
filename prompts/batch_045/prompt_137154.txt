I am circulating a paper to my department's work-in-progress session next month and, before I do, I would like an audit of the position I have ended up holding about reasons. I set out to write a fairly ordinary defense of internalism and have finished with a package of commitments that I cannot get to sit still. Several colleagues have told me the package is inconsistent, but they have not pointed at the same joint, which makes me suspect at least some of the accusations are wrong. I would like to know which are.

Here is the set, stated as flatly as I can manage.

1. Internalism in the route formulation: a consideration counts as a reason for an agent to act only if she could arrive at the conclusion that she should so act by a sound deliberative route from her subjective motivational set as it actually is. I reject the externalist claim that there are considerations that are reasons for an agent simply in virtue of her being a rational agent, whatever she happens to care about.

2. Route-possibilism: the route in (1) need not be one this agent could in fact follow. It is enough that a sound route exists from her set as it stands. This is what lets me say that the host who has no way of discovering that her guest is allergic still has a reason not to serve the dish, and that the man who cannot read the label still has a reason not to drink what is in front of him.

3. No ex nihilo: sound deliberation may correct factual error, add true belief, trace means-end chains, and expose elements of the set to rational pressure from other elements. It may not install a wholly new end that nothing already in the set gestures at. Without this constraint I do not see what distinguishes internalism from a disguised externalism.

4. Reasons for non-deliberators: I want to say that a two-year-old and a dog can have reasons, and not merely that their caretakers have reasons concerning them. This is a verdict I am unwilling to give up; it is doing real work in my other writing.

5. Global defect: I hold that some motivational sets are defective as wholes, not merely element by element. That is, for some agents, every element of the set is such that the agent has reason not to be moved by it, and there is an intelligible critical judgement to that effect which is not just a report of what the critic happens to care about.

6. An access condition on blame: an agent is open to blame for failing to act only if she could have discerned, by deliberation of the kind available to her, that she had reason so to act. I hold this because I think blame without any such access is unfair, and I do not want to give it up.

7. Two case verdicts. The comfortable indifferent, who is fully informed, free of reasoning error, and simply unmoved by the suffering of others, has no reason to aid them. But the severely depressed patient does have a reason to accept treatment even when she sincerely and lucidly reports that nothing matters to her any more.

8. A methodological constraint: the point of tying reasons to the motivational set is that reasons should be able to figure in the explanation of action. A consideration that could not figure in the explanation of any action by anyone, even in principle, is not a reason at all. This is why I became an internalist rather than a moral realist of the externalist kind, and I would not treat dropping it as a free move.

What I want from you is the audit, not a survey of the literature. Which pairs of these are genuinely in tension, and which only look as though they are? Order the real tensions by severity, and for each say precisely what would have to give: which commitment would have to be weakened, restated, or qualified, and what the restatement would cost me elsewhere in the set. Where an apparent tension dissolves, tell me what generates the appearance. If one revision cleans up more than one joint at once, say so, and say whether it is a revision an internalist could accept or only a way of leaving internalism behind. Assume I would rather pay almost any cost inside the set than be told simply to abandon internalism, but do not spare me if the cost accounting really does point there.
