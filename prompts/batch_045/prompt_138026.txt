I sit on the four-person program committee of a private, non-profit public-lecture series: about thirty events a year, a decision needed inside a week, no counsel on call, and every refusal published with a one-paragraph justification. Our stated commitment is to open inquiry. I want a written procedure we can actually run on an individual invitation, not a statement of principles.

Build it out of the paradox of tolerance, but I think the usual framing is too coarse to be usable, so please keep the following four distinctions apart unless you can argue they should be collapsed.

1. A ladder of responses rather than a binary: legal or administrative suppression by the state; protest that physically prevents the event; declining to invite; disinviting after a pressure campaign; hosting but changing the format or adding a respondent. The classic Popperian argument is about the first of these; our actual powers are mostly the third, fourth, and fifth, and I suspect the argument does not transfer cleanly down the ladder.

2. Content harm, which counter-speech in principle answers, versus platform harm: credentialing, audience aggregation, and the signal to sympathetic listeners that they are numerous. Counter-speech does not answer the second, because the injury is that the person was given the podium rather than anything he said from it.

3. The predicate intolerant is itself vague and higher-order vague, with real borderline cases and no bright line. Say what the committee does in the penumbra, and whether it is pretending to discover a pre-existing fact there or openly exercising an authority to close a gap.

4. Who bears the cost of a tolerant decision is usually not who makes it. Tell me what standing, if any, that asymmetry gives the cost-bearers in the procedure.

Deliver: the ordered questions; what counts as a sufficient answer to each; the points where the procedure returns host, the points where it returns decline-and-publish-this-reason, and the points where it stops and hands a values question to the full committee; plus at least one check that stops us laundering viewpoint discrimination as safety or as community standards, ideally a test that forces any rule we invoke to be applied to the mirror-image speaker.

Run it on three cases. First, an epidemiologist with genuine peer-reviewed work on vaccine safety who now claims, to a large following, that health agencies ran a deliberate cover-up, invited to speak on his published topic. Second, an already-invited evolutionary biologist whose research program concerns population differences in cognitive traits and who has made no political claims, where faculty in two departments have asked us to cancel. Third, as a check on vacuity, an avowed neo-Nazi who wants to speak about immigration. I expect the procedure to give me an answer on the second case that I will not like; say so plainly rather than softening it. Then say what the procedure cannot do.

Write in American English, and end with one final line, set off on its own, in exactly this form: \answer{one sentence naming the single question the whole procedure turns on}.
