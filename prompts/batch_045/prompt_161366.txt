You are both a philosopher of mind and a working poet: you write in received forms, you have argued in print about the causal theory of the mental, and you care that a poem about a position be a poem a specialist could not fault. Take your time, and think before you draft.

I am convening a one-day graduate workshop on functionalism — the working title is The Multiple Realization Argument: Sixty Years On — and I want to close it by handing each participant a single printed poem. The conceit I have in mind is this. The six end-words of a sestina should not be six things in the world but six nodes in the causal role of pain: its characteristic input, and the states, actions and dispositions it tends to produce. The six stanzas should be six different physical systems that are candidates for occupying that role. The same six job-words keep coming back while the matter in the lines keeps changing, so that the form itself enacts the thesis rather than illustrating it.

Constraints on the poem.

1. Six end-words, six nodes of the role. Unless you can argue for a better set, use: DAMAGE (the input), FLINCH (withdrawal, the wince, the groan, the cry of pain), WANT (the desire that it stop), BELIEF (that something is wrong with the body), DISTRACTION (its engrossing of attention), LEARNING (the subsequent avoidance). Note in your planning if you substitute one, and why.

2. Correct sestina rotation: 1-2-3-4-5-6 in the first stanza, then 6-1-5-2-4-3, then 3-6-4-1-2-5, then 5-3-2-6-1-4, then 4-5-1-3-6-2, then 2-4-6-5-3-1; then a three-line envoy in which all six recur, two to a line, one placed mid-line and one at the line's end. Either tetrameter or pentameter, but consistent, and rhyming only where rhyme happens to help.

3. One stanza per candidate realizer, ordered as the poem wants, drawn from among: mammalian neural tissue of the familiar fibre-bundle sort; an octopus or some other nervous system built on quite different chemistry; a silicon artifact; a hydraulic or clockwork device; a giant lookup table of input-output pairs with no internal causal structure at all; a whole population of people relaying messages to one another so as to simulate, between them, the workings of a single brain. Use at least five of these, and let the imagery of each stanza be recognizably that system's own — no interchangeable lines about a machine.

4. The stanza I care most about is one I have never seen discussed, so I hand it to you as a case of my own devising: call it the graft. A single creature whose pain system has been partly replaced — part of the causal work of the role done by living tissue, part by an implanted prosthetic doing the rest, both on-line at once, the creature behaving, wanting, believing and reporting exactly as before. On the view that a mental state is the first-order state that occupies the role in this creature, the graft looks like a subject with one pain realized twice over, or with a disjunctive pain, or with two pains at once. On the view that a mental state is the occupancy of the role itself, it is plainly one pain, occupied by neither part alone. Put the case in the stanza without settling it; let it be the stanza where the six words start to look like they belong to nobody.

5. Somewhere in the poem, let one occurrence of a role-word sit in a line where the job is explicitly not done: the word printed, its clause denied. The system in that stanza satisfies most of the specification but not all of it. The point is the one about partial satisfaction — how much of a role must be occupied before the state is there, and what fixes which nodes are load-bearing.

6. The envoy should stage, not settle, the choice the poem has been circling: pain as the second-order property of occupying the role — one property, shared across creatures, but apparently doing no causal work that its occupant does not already do — versus pain as whatever occupies the role here — many first-order properties, so that pain is a disjunction and human pain is not the same property as octopus pain. Let the envoy also register the squeeze the position is under: an input-output specification admits the table and the population, and tightening the specification until they drop out either looks arbitrary or quietly builds in our inputs, our hardware and our timing.

7. The six words name the job, not the hurt. Keep the hurt itself present as something the words keep turning toward and never quite catch: leave the residue in place rather than letting the role-specification swallow it or preaching about it.

What to give me, in this order. First, your working-out: a few paragraphs on the node set, the ordering of realizers, and what the envoy will concede — show me this, I want to see the reasoning. Then the sestina, thirty-nine lines plus envoy. Then a brief apparatus, under three hundred words: bullets mapping each stanza to its case and to the position that case pressures; one bullet on where the form forced you to simplify something; and one on whether your poem assumes that multiple realization actually occurs in nature or only that it is possible.

Accuracy bar: no textbook paraphrase set in verse. Avoid the two errors a seminar would catch — functionalism is not the thesis that mental states are computational states individuated by input and output alone, and the bare possibility of multiple realization does not refute the identity theory, since an identity theorist can help herself to species-relative or disjunctive physical types. Someone who has read the literature should be able to say which stanza is which case, which two lines of the envoy are the two theories, and why the poem could not have ended anywhere else.
