I need advice about a decision I have to make within two days, and the people I have talked to have stopped being useful, because they have sorted themselves into two camps and now only repeat slogans at each other.

My father is dying in Kraków and probably has a day or so. I am in Vancouver with my eight-year-old daughter. Flying is roughly twenty hours with connections, so I would most likely arrive after the funeral.

A company called Meridian operates a passenger service that avoids this. You enter a booth; a machine records the state of your body, they say down to every cell, although the consent form discloses that roughly three tenths of one percent of your synaptic fine structure is reconstructed statistically rather than measured; the body in the booth is then destroyed; and an identical body is assembled in Kraków from feedstock held there, waking with everything I remember up to the moment of the scan. The whole thing takes about forty minutes. Under the arrangements recognised here, the arrival is the same legal person I am: my daughter's custody, my teaching post, my lease, my father's estate.

There are four complications.

Meridian also sells a cheaper variant in which the scan is identical but the body in the departure booth is not destroyed, so both of us walk out. Under the same legal rule, the arrival abroad is still the continuing person, and the one who remains is registered as a second instance, given a resettlement grant, and given no claim on my job, my flat, or my daughter. A friend says this variant is plainly better because then nobody dies. A colleague who has used the standard booth more than forty times says the difference between the two is meaningless, because either way the person who arrives is me.

Meridian's safety record, which they publish, notes that in something like one trip in fifty thousand the scan is transmitted twice and two arrivals wake up. Their protocol is to deactivate the second arrival at once, and the consent form describes this as recovering surplus feedstock rather than as anyone dying. I have to sign this form to buy the ticket.

Three years ago, in the pilot programme, I took the twin variant once. There is accordingly a man in Abbotsford who is, as far as any test can tell, exactly as much me as I am: my memories up to the morning of the scan, my temperament, my habits. He works as a graphic designer, goes by a shortened version of my name, and we have had lunch twice and got on well. Everyone I mention this to disagrees about whether his existence should make me more comfortable or less comfortable about what I am about to do, and I cannot tell either.

Finally, quite separately, my employer will fund one long-term arrangement for me and not two, and the enrollment closes this week, so I have to choose now between Meridian's own continuity plan, under which a body is rebuilt from my most recent annual scan if I die, and a competitor's plan involving no scan and no copy at all: over about three years of monthly outpatient visits, my neurons are replaced a fraction of a percent at a time with synthetic units that behave identically, so that, in their brochure's wording, nothing in me is ever interrupted and nothing is ever copied, and what my daughter eventually gets is me rather than a facsimile. Whatever I decide about the trip, I suspect I will decide this the same way, but I cannot see whether that is a real insight on my part or just stiffness of mind.

What I would like from you is not a survey of what people generally think, and not an account of what the law currently says. I would like you to identify which consideration is actually doing the work in each of these four complications, and to tell me where my reasons for one answer commit me to something I may not want about another. Specifically: is there a difference between the two booth trips that I should care about, and if there is, which way does it cut? Can I consistently buy the ticket and sign the consent form with the surplus clause in it? Does the gradual plan deserve the claim its brochure makes for it, or does whatever makes the booth acceptable also make the gradual plan unnecessary, or the reverse? And what is the honest answer to my daughter, who asked me whether it would really be me who came back?

Please also tell me if two of these are the same question wearing different clothes, and tell me if some distinction I am leaning on is not bearing the weight I am putting on it.
