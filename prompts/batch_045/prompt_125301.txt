I've been reading about hedonism on my own and I think I've cracked the thing people call the heterogeneity problem — you know, where they say the pleasure of a cold beer, of finally getting a puzzle, and of running into an old friend have nothing in common, so hedonism can't be about one thing. But surely pleasure just means "the kind of experience you'd rather be having than not having," i.e. the ones you want to keep going. Every case fits, so they obviously do share something, and the objection dissolves.

So why is this still treated as a serious problem? My guess is that philosophers are mixing up "these feel different from each other" with "there's no single thing they all are," and that they set the bar weirdly high — demanding some shared tingle or sensation, when the only thing needed is a shared role. I also don't get why it matters for the bigger question: if I want to know whether my life is going well, can't I just list whatever experiences are good for me, weight them by how long and how strong they are, add them up, and be done? I don't buy that old crack about pushpin being as good as poetry either, which makes me think I'm missing something about what the worry was supposed to be. Where exactly does my definition go wrong — or is it actually fine and the whole debate is overblown?
