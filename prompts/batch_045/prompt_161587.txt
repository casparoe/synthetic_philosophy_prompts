I'm teaching a graduate seminar this term on races to the bottom — credential inflation, journal-prestige cascades, the AI-capabilities thing — and there's one move I keep hitting that nobody will cash out for me. Someone says "it's Moloch," reaches for the deity from the Moloch section of Ginsberg's Howl (furnace, children, an appetite that isn't anyone's will), and treats that as having settled three things at once: that blame is misplaced, since no participant is really the one choosing; that the only real fix is something centralized with teeth, because you can't negotiate with Moloch, you can only oppose it; and that soft measures — voluntary pauses, professional norms, moratoria — are naive by construction.

I want that analogy audited properly. Give me the correspondence table first: what plays the idol, the priests, the children, the rival tribes, the sacrifice, the propitiation. Especially tell me what the god itself maps onto — the payoff structure, the selection mechanism, the aggregate dynamic, or each participant's beliefs about the others — and whether it can map onto more than one of those at a time without equivocating.

Then the disanalogies, sorted by whether they actually bite for the conclusions people draw. The ones I keep circling: Moloch has a single unified appetite, whereas a trap has as many separate pressures as it has participants; sacrifice to Moloch buys something (rain, victory), while positional defection buys a lead that evaporates the moment everyone matches it; and the priests in a trap are also the parents and, confusingly, also the children. And I'm not sure the picture survives my own sorting of the cases — I've been dividing them along two axes, whether the defector's gain is positional or absolute and whether the sacrificed good is consumed by the process or merely forgone, and I suspect Moloch only fits some of the cells: credential inflation feels like a Moloch case, whereas a straight arms race to war feels like something else, maybe just a race with a deadline.

So: which of those three licensed conclusions survives once the mapping and the breaks are on the table, which one was being carried by the personification alone, and is there a better analogy — commons, Red Queen, ratchet, treadmill, price — that does the same motivational work without importing a will? Tell me how confident you are in what you claim, and single out the point you're least sure of.
