You are a philosopher of science whose specialism is theory assessment in domains where direct evidence is unavailable — someone equally fluent in Bayesian confirmation theory, in the historical literature on progressive versus degenerating research programmes, and in the details of contemporary cosmology (inflation, the landscape of vacua, the cosmological constant, the measure problem). Take your time and think carefully. Before you write anything structured, spend a while brainstorming freely: restate the position below in the strongest form you can find, list every way you can think of for it to go wrong, and only then decide which of those are genuine defeaters and which are noise. Do not hedge symmetrically and do not sit on the fence — I want you to end up somewhere. No citations, no literature review, no "it depends on what you mean by confirmation" unless you then say which reading you are adopting and why. Reason from the structure of the problem.

Here is the position, which I will call Modest Non-Empirical Confirmation (MNEC). In a domain where no direct empirical test is currently available, a theory can acquire genuine but small, defeasible probabilistic support from three markers taken jointly:

1. No-alternatives: sustained, well-motivated search by a large and competent community has failed to produce a comparably good alternative to the framework, and it is not for want of trying or for want of funding.
2. Unexpected explanatory coherence: the framework, built for one purpose, turns out to explain things it was not built to explain — as defenders of string theory claim about the microscopic derivation of black hole entropy, or about gauge coupling unification falling out of a structure designed for something else.
3. Anomaly absorption: the programme's anomalies have, so far, tended to dissolve into confirmations or into new theoretical resources rather than accumulating.

I apply this package to the combination of eternal inflation, a large landscape of vacua, and anthropic selection, and I conclude that it deserves real credence rather than the dismissive "not even wrong" treatment — I would put my credence that something in that neighbourhood is on the right track at roughly one in five. I hold MNEC, not the stronger claim that non-empirical grounds can raise a theory to near-certainty, and not the claim that they can ever substitute for evidence.

Tell me what would count against MNEC. Specifically:

(a) Give me the defeaters, ranked from fatal through serious to merely awkward, and say for each whether it is an observation, an argument, a formal result, or a development in the field. Include both empirical defeaters (what would the sky, or a collider, or a tabletop experiment have to show?) and non-empirical ones (what would have to happen inside the theoretical programme?).

(b) I have been trying to make marker 2 responsible rather than hand-wavy by constructing what I call a Calibration Archive: a set of historical research programmes, selected for coding before their outcomes are consulted, coded for whether they displayed unexpected explanatory coherence and no-alternatives at a given time, and then checked against what later happened. I already know about three problems and I want you to take them seriously rather than reassure me: my archive can only contain programmes that eventually received a verdict, and verdict-receiving programmes are a biased sample of programmes; "displayed unexpected explanatory coherence" is coded by someone who knows how things turned out, so hindsight bias may be baked in; and the base rate of success among nineteenth- and twentieth-century programmes is probably wildly different from the base rate for candidate final theories, so the likelihood ratio I want may not transport. Analyse whether this audit could ever be built well enough to be evidentially serious. What would it have to show for me to have to give up MNEC? Would a flat result — markers uncorrelated with later vindication — actually defeat me, or only deflate me? Is there an asymmetry: does MNEC predict anything about how the audit should come out, or is it compatible with every outcome?

(c) The thing I would personally find most devastating is a construction, not a historical case: a theory I can see is false, deliberately engineered by a red team, that nevertheless scores as well as the landscape package does on all three markers. Say whether such a construction is possible in principle, roughly what it would have to look like, and what exactly it would show if someone produced it. If it is impossible in principle, explain why that is not simply a lucky feature of my position.

(d) Be honest about insulation. I said "genuine but small", "defeasible", and "taken jointly", and I set my credence at one in five. Is that modesty a methodological virtue, or has MNEC been qualified into something that cannot lose? State what an immodest version would look like, and whether the modest version inherits its problems anyway.

(e) For each of your serious defeaters, mark whether a defender of MNEC should herself concede that it counts against the view, or whether she would be right to dismiss it as attacking a caricature. Where you think the disagreement would turn out to be merely verbal, say so and give the substantive version underneath.

Structure your answer as: a one-paragraph verdict up front; then the ranked defeaters; then the audit analysis; then the insulation assessment; then the one thing you would most want to see tested that nobody has tried.
