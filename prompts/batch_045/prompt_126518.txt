hey — i need a poem to close the last session of a reading group. we've spent the term on dennett: the three stances (physical, design, intentional) and the compressibility idea, that a pattern counts as real when the data admits a description shorter than just listing the data out, whether or not anyone is actually around to produce that description. the bit we got stuck on, and the bit i want the poem to stay stuck on, is that compressibility looks relative to whoever's doing the compressing and what code they happen to have — so realism about patterns threatens to collapse into "real for us." here's the version of this that really bit us, and which i've started calling an orphaned pattern: a data set that nobody left alive can compress, because the only scheme that would do it is gone. a codebook nobody kept, a language no one speaks any more, a file format on a dead drive. the numbers still sit there. is the pattern in them or not? we did not agree, and i definately don't want the poem to settle it.

form: a sestina. six sestets plus the three-line envoi, end-words rotated properly. the six words are: bit, map, rule, error, cost, real. no rhyme needed, but keep some kind of loose pulse so it reads aloud. the reason i want a sestina is that the same six words keep coming back and meaning something different each time — which is exactly what happens when you switch stances. so: each of the six words should get used at least once doing physical-stance work, at least once design-stance, and at least once intentional-stance. someone who knows the three stances should be able to point at a line and tell which one it's spoken from, and someone who doesn't should just hear six ordinary words doing a lot of work. the envoi — all six words, two per line — should be the moment all three stances land on the same stretch of behaviour at once, without ranking them or announcing that it's happened.

setting: make it concrete, please. my suggestion is the wind log of an automatic weather station on a small island that was evacuated decades ago, still reporting numbers no one is reading, until somebody notices something. but if you've got a better concrete case, take it.

two more things: it shouldn't be in the voice of a philosopher, or of dennett — more like whoever it is who finds the log. and it shouldn't turn into either a sermon about how it's patterns all the way down or a shrug about how it's all just physics. if one of the six end-words turns out to be unworkable you can swap it, but tell me in one line which you swapped and what for.
