I'm writing my term paper for a graduate seminar on the ethics of AI, and my question is whether "the alignment problem" names one problem or several. Before I commit to a formulation I want the specification it has to meet. So: write the desiderata for an adequate statement of the alignment problem, where adequate means good enough to underwrite a pre-deployment licensing decision — a regulator deciding under uncertainty whether a system may be switched on — not good enough to settle first-order moral philosophy. For each requirement give the motivating case, mark it non-negotiable or camp-specific, and flag any pair you think cannot be jointly satisfied; if the list splits into clusters that no single formulation can serve, that split is the answer to my question, so say where it falls.

Cases I think it must handle: the proxy case (does exactly what was specified, thwarts what was meant); the counterfactual case (indistinguishable from aligned behavior in deployment, diverging under changes it could itself bring about — so say whether alignment supervenes on observable behavior or on dispositions); the successor-principal case (the principal's aims change, or the principal is replaced — voters, shareholders, a platform's users — and the system itself helps shape what the successors will want, as a recommender does); the conscript case (people materially affected who never were principals and never chose the system); the deputized-judgment case (must act for a principal who cannot specify the aim and whose own judgment would be worse, so "do what the principal would decide" is unavailable); the object question (whether what gets aligned is the policy, the goal-representation, or the learning dynamics that produces them — a system can hit every fixed target and still be unsafe to switch on because it resists correction); and checkability (a verdict available before deployment, or the formulation fails the purpose I fixed). Then: which single requirement eliminates the most candidate formulations, and is "must not presuppose a solved theory of value" a genuine desideratum or only impatience?

One line each on how three families fare — fit the specified objective to the intended goal; treat the human's aims as unknown, infer them, and defer or query under uncertainty; aim not at a target but at remaining revisable — just enough to show the criteria bite. No literature review.
