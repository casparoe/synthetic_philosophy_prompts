I've been reading the rationalist blogs and forums since the mid-2000s, and once a month I run a ninety-minute session in a pub back room for a mixed crowd: mostly working programmers, two economists, one attendee who has read the Sequences closely and will correct me if I'm sloppy, and three or four newcomers who know the prisoner's dilemma and nothing else. Next month's session is on Hofstadter's superrationality cases — the Platonia dilemma, in which twenty players who cannot communicate may each send one telegram and the prize goes to the sole sender and to nobody if two or more send or if none do, and the Luring Lottery, the open one where a single prize is divided evenly among everyone who enters.

I don't need the ideas explained to me; I need a plan for explaining them, one that leaves the room clear on why the superrational answer is neither evidential decision theory wearing a hat nor causal decision theory plus common knowledge of rationality.

Specifically, I want:

1. The order in which the components have to arrive, and what depends on what. My sense is that nothing lands until the room sees that what is shared between players is a decision procedure rather than a decision, but I want your read on which idea has to be in place before the Platonia case is even intelligible, and which pieces can be moved later without cost.
2. Which single case should carry the whole session, and why not the other candidates. The candidates are the original Platonia, the Luring Lottery, and a variant I've been using: the same twenty-player telegram game, except that each telegram is independently lost in transit with probability one in ten so that what matters is delivered rather than sent telegrams, and the twenty players have been drawn from a much larger pool by a short quiz, so nobody can be certain the others are running anything like the same reasoning. Tell me what the carrying example must have and must lack.
3. The misconceptions to expect, and the exact point in the sequence at which to head each off. My guesses: superrationality just means cooperate; if we are all rational we all do the same thing, therefore all send or all abstain, therefore abstain; common knowledge of rationality is doing the work that correlation between copies is really doing; and the one I expect from the person who has read the Sequences, that this is just one-boxing again. Name the ones I've missed, tell me which to defuse early, and tell me which are worth leaving standing until the end because they are doing useful work meanwhile. Note that someone will produce the calculation in which all twenty randomise with probability one in twenty; say where in the sequence that belongs and what it cements if it arrives too early.
4. One question I can put to the room at the end that would show me they have the idea rather than that they have agreed with me.
5. What I should openly decline to explain at this level, and how to say so. I already intend to refuse two things: what actually happened when the real lottery was run, since I won't rest anything on figures I can't check, and the full functional decision theory apparatus, which would eat the hour. Tell me what else belongs on that list.

Lead with the spine of the session in one sentence, then give me the plan; no preamble and no restatement of what I have asked for. British English throughout, and no further reading for me or for them.
