I maintain a small agent-based simulation of iterated prisoner's dilemma tournaments — a few hundred agents per generation, each pair plays a few hundred moves with a small chance that any given move is misreported as the opposite one, and a reproduction rule in which a strategy's share of the next generation is proportional to the average score it earned. I've been running it on and off for a while, and there is a pattern in the output I have no vocabulary for. That matters because I want to write it up for readers who will take it as a claim about cooperation rather than as a claim about my code, and I would rather be using the right word than inventing one by accident.

Two observations. First, the strategies that end up dominating fall into groups that don't line up with each other. Some cannot get established at all when I inject them at 2% into a settled population, but if I seed the same strategy at 30% it drives the resident type out completely and then holds off every mutant I throw at it. Others do the opposite: they spread from 2%, climb, and then collapse once they are common — their own spread destroys whatever made them spread in the first place. As far as I can make out, the phrase "evolutionarily stable strategy" as it gets used in popular accounts covers only the first of these properties, and covers it only for a population that is already almost entirely that strategy. I have not found a term for the ability to spread from rarity, or for the fact that the two abilities can come apart in the same strategy.

Second, and this is the case I actually want named: one family of strategies has an average score that peaks at an intermediate share of the population — around 40% in my runs — and falls off on both sides. Below roughly 15 or 20% they lose to the resident type and die out; above that they climb toward the peak; past the peak they get pulled back down, so the population settles near the peak instead of going to fixation. Stuck when rare, stable at a middling frequency, self-limiting when common, all at once.

Is there an established concept for that last configuration: a type whose success is maximal at some intermediate share of the population, with a threshold below which it cannot establish itself at all? I'm after the carving more than the label. What are the nearest established concepts, presumably somewhere in the area of frequency-dependent selection; how does my case differ from each; does the answer change between the one-shot game and the iterated game, where what evolves is a program with memory rather than a single move; and how much of what I'm seeing is an artifact of my reproduction rule rather than a property of the game? I've seen people reach for "tipping point" and "critical mass," but those describe the shape of the curve rather than the mechanism behind it, which isn't what I need. If nothing established fits, I'd want a term with a definition and its boundary cases. I'm not asking for citations or a reading list — a bare label wouldn't settle anything for me either way.
