I write distributed systems, and most of the last three years has gone into a deterministic simulation harness: the whole cluster runs in one process, every source of nondeterminism is routed through a seeded generator, and any run can be replayed bit for bit from a seed. I also run incident reviews, where the job is to say which component's behaviour caused an outage and whether to file a bug against it. I have no philosophy training, and I would rather not be handed any as a package.

Please restate the compatibilism-versus-incompatibilism debate about free will and moral responsibility in that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. The vocabulary I actually reach for: deterministic replay, seeds and pseudo-randomness, event traces, provenance and data lineage, module boundaries and encapsulation, control-flow attribution, root-cause analysis, fault injection, fail-safes and kill switches, counterfactual branches, code coverage and unexercised paths, and models whose policy was shaped by a loss function someone else wrote. Use it to render at least these five pieces: (a) the argument that if the past together with the laws fixes everything that follows, then no one could ever have done otherwise; (b) the reply that could-have-done-otherwise is a claim about which branches are reachable under a perturbation of the agent's own state, not a claim that one replay of a fixed trace could emit a different output; (c) the view that what matters is not alternate possibilities but whether the causal path to the action runs through the agent's own decision procedure; (d) the well-known kind of case in which a fail-safe would have forced the outcome had the agent wavered, but in fact never fired; (e) the objection that injecting randomness into a decision procedure adds luck and nothing else.

Then give me the residue report. Which claims map cleanly onto something I already know how to talk about, which survive only after you have quietly bent them, and which have no expression in my vocabulary at all? I specifically want you to tell me what happens to the following, none of which I can see how to say natively: the feeling, from inside deliberation, that more than one outcome is genuinely still open; the difference between blaming something in order to change its future behaviour and blaming it because it deserves it; the idea that a person can be the ultimate source of their own values; and any question about what blame is for, as opposed to what it does.

Two constraints on the translation. First, flag every place where the vocabulary begs the question in favour of one side: root cause, ownership, and bug all seem to me to presuppose that causal attribution survives determinism, and I want to know what the same sentence looks like when that presupposition is removed. Second, do not let the translation quietly decide the debate.

My own suspicion is that compatibilism is mostly the observation that causal attribution is layer-relative: the load-shedding logic caused the 503 even though the transistor voltages did too, and nothing about that is threatened by the run being deterministic. Tell me whether that is the real content of the position or a cheap parody of it, and what the position has to add once the layer-relativity point is granted.

Then test your translation on three cases I care about more than the textbook ones. (i) An agent whose entire policy was shaped by a loss function written by somebody else, so that every value it has is a product of that training, but whose decision on this occasion does run through its own reasoning and would have come out differently had it reasoned better. Is it the source of the action in the sense that matters? (ii) A decision module that computes a verdict, logs it, and has it discarded in favour of a value written by an upstream system, next to an otherwise identical module whose verdict actually routes to the action. Does the difference between these two have any counterpart in the free will literature, and why does it matter to responsibility? (iii) A harness that can replay the exact trace up to the decision point and then let me edit one register before resuming. Is could-have-done-otherwise in that setting the genuine article, or is my editing a register just me stepping outside the system, and does it matter if it is?

Close by telling me which of your translations you are least confident in, and where you suspect the dispute turns on something that neither my vocabulary nor the philosophers' can settle.
