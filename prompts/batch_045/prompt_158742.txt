Hey — I'm a stats PhD student and I run a kind of informal reading group with a bunch of applied people in my department (ML, biostat, one econometrics guy) who are all convinced that philosophy of probability is terminological hygeine. I don't want to hand them a paper. I want to ambush them with a decision tree: start from answers they'd actually give about concrete cases, and land them on a named interpretation, so they find out they've been committed to hypothetical-limit frequentism this whole time without ever having chosen a label.

What I need:

- Max 7 branch questions. Each one has to be a concrete case or a plain claim someone can agree or disagree with, phrased without jargon and without already pointing at a leaf. So no "are probabilities in the head or in the world," no "are you a Bayesian?", no question that's just 'do you think a single event can have a probability' said louder.
- Please use at least four of these cases, since they're the ones my group actually fights about:
  1. A model I've calibrated: across ~10k past cases, 70% of the ones it scored 0.70 came out positive. Tomorrow's case scores 0.70. Is there a fact of the matter about "the probability" for that one case, and is it 0.70?
  2. A fair coin flipped five minutes ago, still covered, nobody looked. Probability of heads? And: would your answer have been any different before the flip?
  3. One spin of a coin I machined slightly bent, drawn from a batch whose biases run roughly uniformly between 0.45 and 0.55. No repeat of this exact setup; I'm melting the coin afterwards.
  4. Gas in a box, the usual statistical-mechanics probabilities — you pick whether to ask about the deterministic or the indeterministic version, whichever makes the tree sharper.
  5. A pure one-off with no reference class and no model: "probability that clause 4 of the pending bill gets amended before the vote." One expert's gut says 0.3.
- Leaves I want to be able to reach: actual-sequence (finite) frequentism, hypothetical-limit frequentism, classical/indifference, logical probability, subjective Bayesian (including the exchangeability story about where single-case talk gets its content), objective-Bayesian/maxent, best-system (Humean) chance, single-case propensity, and — if it earns its place — the hardline operationalist line some of my group takes ("probability is just whatever makes my scoring rule stop hurting"). If operationalism is really one of the others with the metaphysics deleted, say so and put it where it belongs rather than giving it a leaf.
- Build it from the logical space, not from the list of names: lay out the answer combinations the questions allow first, then locate the real positions in that space. For at least two combinations nobody occupies, tell me why — incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored?
- Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't separate. My hunch is that single-case propensity and best-system chance collapse on every question I can think of; if that's right, say so, and say whether some case could seperate them anyway (something where the chance is irreducibly indeterministic vs. derived from a symmetric microstructure?).
- One short note at the end: which question is doing most of the work, and which one I can cut without the tree losing anything.

Keep the whole thing under 900 words, with the tree in a compact nested or indented form — I'm pasting it onto a slide. And please no reading list or citations, I already have more PDFs than I'll ever read.
