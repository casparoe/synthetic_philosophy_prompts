I write about how institutions measure themselves, and I've arrived at a position I keep restating in drafts, so I want it stress-tested rather than applauded. The familiar thought behind Goodhart's law is that a measure used for control stops tracking whatever it was chosen to track. My stronger version is that the failure lives in the channel rather than at either end: any proxy that must be recomputed at scale has to be cheap, and cheapness means the people being measured can find and exploit the gap between the proxy and the good it stands in for — which is why I think the search for a gaming-resistant metric is a dead end, and why I've started describing reward hacking in machine-learning systems as the same phenomenon under a different name. I also lean on a distinction I haven't seen drawn cleanly: between proxies whose degradation announces itself, where the number visibly detaches from the thing it names and provokes a fight about the definition, and proxies whose degradation is silent, where the figure keeps looking healthy while the activity it describes has quietly changed shape. My claim is that almost all the durable damage is in the second class, because a visible failure at least recruits someone to renegotiate the target.

What I want from you is a map of my exposure, not a survey of the topic. What would actually count against this, and against which part specifically: the channel story, the dead-end claim about metric design, or the announced-versus-silent distinction? Rank the defeaters by how much they should move me, and be explicit about which ones a sympathetic defender of the view would concede as counting against it, as opposed to objections only a critic would raise. The claim I care most about is the silent-proxy one, and the single thing I'd find most persuasive is a description of a case where an extremely cheap, obviously gameable indicator kept tracking the thing it stood for for a decade or more, under real stakes, with nobody renegotiating it — I'd like to know whether such cases are merely possible or whether you can point to the sort of setting that reliably produces them. I also want the uncomfortable question answered straight: has the channel story been made general enough to survive any outcome at all, and if so, is that a vice in a thesis of this kind, or just what a structural claim looks like when it is stated honestly?
