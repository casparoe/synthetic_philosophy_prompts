I help run a casual math-and-philosophy night at my local library: about 12 adults, one 90-minute session, most of them haven't written a proof since they were 16, and no homework between sessions. I've been reading about constructivism on my own (no classes, so please skip the jargon dump) and I want an activity where people hit the wall themselves instead of me announcing it. The two things I want them to feel: the gap between "I can't see how this could be false" and "I can actually show it", and the point where helping yourself to "either it happens or it doesn't" stops being free.

The hook I keep coming back to: the claim that there's an even number above 4 that isn't the sum of two primes. Nobody's found one, nobody's proved there can't be one. I definately want that to become something that happens in the room — somebody asserts it, somebody attacks it, neither side can cash thier claim, and it somehow doesn't get settled by "well, one of you has to be right."

My rough mechanic: a two-role game, one player asserting and one attacking/defending, where you may play "either X or not X" as a move but it puts you in debt — you score for playing it, and you lose more than you scored if you later get forced to say which half holds and can't. I also want "I can't find a counterexample" and "I can prove there isn't one" to cost different amounts, because I think that's where the real action is. Throw the mechanic out if it doesn't work.

Design it: setup and materials (nothing I can't get at a drugstore), roles, the move or card types, a scoring scheme with no real money, a minute-by-minute timeline, and a debrief that maps what actually happened onto the formal points. Nobody should explain the view up front, and don't give it its name until afterwards. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be straight about which part of the position the game just can't stage and I'd have to say in words.

2. A table with columns: Phase | Minutes | What participants do | What I do | What should start to feel wrong.
