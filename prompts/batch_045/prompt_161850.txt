I am in the second year of a PhD in philosophy of physics, and our reading group has spent most of this term on the hole argument; we have ended up somewhere I did not expect — not at a verdict, but at a stalemate nobody seems able to break. To fix terms briefly: take a globally hyperbolic vacuum model of general relativity, pick a Cauchy surface, choose a bounded hole region to its future, and apply an active diffeomorphism that is the identity outside the hole. The result is another solution of the field equations, one that agrees with the first everywhere up to the surface and outside the hole and disagrees with it inside. If the points of the manifold are genuine individuals and if diffeomorphism-related models count as distinct physical possibilities, general relativity looks indeterministic: the state on a slice fails to fix the state in the hole. The responses — abandon the manifold as an object in the ontology (relationism, eliminativism), keep the manifold but deny that the dragged and undragged models represent distinct possibilities, the move usually called *sophisticated substantivalism*, sometimes motivated by the interpretive principle that Leibniz-equivalent models represent one and the same situation and sometimes by a memory of Einstein's own retreat to point-coincidences, or else simply bite the indeterminism bullet, with a fourth option pressed by the mathematical physicist in our group, namely that the whole episode is a fact about the formulation of the initial-value problem and nothing about the world — have all been on the table for decades, and what strikes me is how little movement there is. Each side knows in advance what will be said to it: the relationist, that she has quietly stopped doing physics on a manifold at all; the sophisticated substantivalist, that the collapse of the models is either relationism under a new name or a thesis about representation smuggled in as a thesis about the world; the bullet-biter, that the indeterminism is idle, in that no possible observation could reveal which hole-model one is in; the mathematical physicist, that a reformulation is not an answer to a question about what exists.

My own hypothesis, which I want tested rather than flattered, is that the argument's apparent force comes from sliding between two readings of the claim that the models differ: a semantic reading, on which our formalism supplies more representations than there are situations and nothing in the world corresponds to the surplus, and a metaphysical reading, on which there are surplus situations because the points are individuals over and above their relations. On the semantic reading the argument is close to a platitude about redundancy in a coordinate-free formalism, and it is unclear why a substantivalist should care. On the metaphysical reading it turns on whether spacetime points have primitive identity — haecceitism about points — and that is close to what substantivalism was supposed to consist in, so the argument looks question-begging against the substantivalist and trivial against everyone else; the debate then survives only because each side helps itself to whichever reading is convenient. Two things I tried out on my supervisor push me toward this, though neither settles it for me. First, a shrinking hole: take a nested sequence of holes converging on a single point, so that the dragged and undragged models differ only within an arbitrarily small neighbourhood, and in the limit at a point. Does the substantivalist now owe us a continuum of possibilities that differ at a point, and does the anti-haecceitist's collapse of the models leave any residue at all, or does the disagreement evaporate into the question whether there can be a difference that makes no qualitative difference? Second, a background-structure contrast: in a theory with a fixed, non-dynamical background metric, active diffeomorphisms are not symmetries of the theory and the hole argument cannot get off the ground. If we were learning something about the metaphysics of spacetime points, why should the lesson be available only in theories that happen to have that much symmetry? That dependence on the symmetry group looks to me like evidence that what is at issue is representation rather than ontology — but my supervisor replies that it shows only that substantivalism is endangered where the physics is rich enough to endanger it, which I cannot refute and do not fully understand.

So what I want is a diagnosis, not a verdict: what kind of stalemate is this, and what would count as progress given that diagnosis? Please give the diagnosis in prose first — a few paragraphs that engage each position in its own terms rather than in my paraphrase, and that say whether the disagreement is verbal, a fight over what substantivalism is now taken to mean; substantive but metaphysical, resting on bedrock intuitions about primitive identity that neither side can argue for; a disguised methodological disagreement about how much metaphysical cost to incur in order to protect determinism; or several of these at once — and then follow it with a brief structured summary. Please do not cite or name papers; I have the literature in front of me and I want your reasoning, not references. The summary should be a single JSON object with exactly these fields: `diagnosis_label`, a short label for the kind of stalemate plus one sentence of justification; `axes`, an object with the keys factual, evidence_standards, which_cases_count_as_data, starting_intuitions, semantic_vs_metaphysical and verbal_slippage, each mapped to an object with a boolean `sides_differ` and a `detail` string of one to three sentences stating what each side actually commits to on that axis; `hidden_question`, the single question doing the real work, or null together with an explanation of why there is none; `hypothesis_verdict`, an object with `verdict` drawn from confirmed, partly_confirmed or rejected, and a `reason` string; `probe_upshots`, an array of two objects, one per probe above, each with `probe` equal to shrinking_hole or fixed_background, a `helps` string naming which position it helps or else the word neither, and a `why` string of two to four sentences; `progress_conditions`, an array of objects each with `item`, a `kind` drawn from distinction, formal_result, empirical_finding, change_of_question or methodological_decision, a `would_it_end_the_stalemate` drawn from yes, partly or no, and a `note`; `no_progress_argument`, the strongest case that no progress should be expected and why; `confidence`, a number between 0 and 1; and `likely_error`, a string saying where you think your own diagnosis is most likely to be wrong.
