ok so me and my friend just spent like two hours arguing about creativity at a bar and I need somebody to settle it. He's really into that old Hofstadter chapter — Variations on a Theme as the Crux of Creativity, from Metamagical Themas, which he tells me was originally a Scientific American column around 1982 (there was another one the same year called Can inspiration be mechanized? that he keeps bringing up too). His reading is that the upshot is dead simple: to be creative is just to take some theme and twiddle the knobs, i.e. wander around the space of possible variations on it. And he says Hofstadter's whole "the mind is a machine" thing means mechanism all the way down, no spooky stuff left to defend.

Here's my problem. I've been messing with a dumb little script that takes a short musical motif and grinds out variations: transposition, inversion, retrograde, stretching each interval by some percentage, changing the meter, with a slider for each one. I can crank through tens of thousands of knob settings in a couple of minutes, and I even made up a metric I call variation coverage — the fraction of the knob space it actually visits — and I've got it near 100 percent. But everything it spits out sounds like the same motif wearing a hat. Nothing is the jump I'd actually call creative, and it's never the jump *I* would have found.

So I said the knob model must be wrong. He says no, it just means I need more knobs: add a meta-knob that chooses which knobs exist and what ranges they have, crank coverage on that too, and eventually you enumerate your way to something genuinely new. And then he said that's exactly why Hofstadter should be thrilled about the current crop of image and text models, which churn through vastly more variations than any person ever will — but instead Hofstadter spent decades being dismissive of machine creativity, which my friend says is flatly inconsistent with his own mechanicst view of mind.

Who's right here? Is the "just add more knobs" move an actual fix or does it dodge the point? And is Hofstadter contradicting himself — can you think mind is mechanical and still deny that a machine cranking through huge numbers of variations is the creative one? Also, is my variation coverage number measuring anything real, or is it just a fancy way of counting?
