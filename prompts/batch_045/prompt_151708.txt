The four floors, in my terms:
- Darwinian: the behavioral repertoire is fixed by selection acting over generations; within a lifetime the individual can only mature and be triggered, not redesigned by experience.
- Skinnerian: lifetime learning by real trial and error, where bad candidate actions have to fail out in the world for the creature to learn from them.
- Popperian: lifetime learning in which candidate actions are prescreened against an internal model, so hypotheses can die in the head rather than the body.
- Gregorian: the creature is furnished with culturally transmitted mind-tools — words, numerals, diagrams, categories, trained habits of attention — and acquiring them reorganizes what its brain can subsequently do.
