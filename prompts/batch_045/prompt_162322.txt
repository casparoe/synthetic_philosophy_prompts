please build it from the logical space instead of from a list of isms: first the questions (the ones I care about: are there non-actual concrete worlds, or only surrogates, or no world-quantification at all; can one and the same individual be located at more than one world or are de re claims handled by counterparts; is individuation qualitative or does it lean on haecceities; is the counterpart relation fixed by similarity, primitive, or sensitive to context/contrast; does the view have to smuggle a modal primitive in somewhere in the base), then drop Lewis's modal realism, actualist transworld identity with haecceities, ersatz modal realism, modal fictionalism and modal primitivism into the cells they actually occupy, and mark the empty cells with a one-line guess about why nobody sits there — incoherent, unattractive, or just never tried?

three cases should do the diagnostic work: (1) a world where I have no counterpart at all, since some views turn 'I might not have existed' into a necessary-existence claim or make it vacuously true, and I want that to be a genuine fork; (2) two qualitatively indistinguishable candidates for which one at some world is me, as the haecceitism test; (3) a world where I fission into two successors, which should force the question of whether the counterpart relation can be one-many or whether neither is me. also flag any pair of leaves your tree can't separate — especially if it can't tell similarity-based from primitive counterpart relations from the outside.

about six branch nodes max, phrased so a first-year who has never heard the names can still answer them.
