I help moderate a very argumentative group chat (six of us, none of us philosophy people, we've been at it since lockdown) and we keep having the same fight: when two people disagree about something like "that curry is too hot", "she knew the deadline moved", "that investment is too risky", "the film was scary" — is there one answer that's right for everybody once you've fixed what you're talking about, or can it be true for me and false for you with neither of us being wrong?

I want to turn that into a little decision tree we can each walk through and then see which box we land in. Please build it from the questions rather than from the names: work out which yes/no questions actually separate the options, then hang the named views off the ends. Concretely:

- Every branch has to be a question one of my friends could answer without knowing any jargon — a concrete case, or a plain claim about what's going on in the disagreement. No "are you a relativist?", no "is truth relative?". Labels only on the leaves, each with one line on what's awkward about holding it.
- Around 5–7 branch questions and 6–8 leaves.
- These have to appear as leaves: there's one right answer whoever's asking; the meaning shifts with the person speaking so the two aren't really contradicting each other; same claim and a genuine disagreement, but it can be true for one of us and false for the other depending on who's judging; and whatever the view is that says sentences like these aren't really stating facts at all.
- Do the bookkeeping too: which combinations of answers my questions allow but nobody actually holds (and whether that's because it's incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored), and flag any two leaves your tree can't really separate instead of pretending it can.

One case I want handled specially, because its the one I actually care about and I've never seen it discussed anywhere: sometimes one of us isn't merely different from the other, they're stricter in every single way — they want more evidence (or more heat, or more risk) on every part of the question, not just on one bit of it. Can both people still be faultless there, or does that stricter-in-every-way setup squash some of the options together? I feel like it breaks something but I can't say what. Give it its own branch if that's what it needs; if it means the tree has to be rebuilt, say so.

And be straight with me about confidence: mark the leaves where you're least sure the view is something anyone really defends, or where you've had to invent an occupant for an empty slot. If my setup is muddled — if "true for me but not for you" is really two ideas I've run together, or if my stricter-in-every-way case doesn't do the work I think it does — tell me that plainly at the top rather than quietly producing a tidy tree anyway. I'd genuinely rather find out the question was bad.
