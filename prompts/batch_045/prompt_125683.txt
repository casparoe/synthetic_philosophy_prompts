Could you build me a decision tree that takes a student from a fixed starting commitment — "here is a training run: one policy, updated by gradient descent on a scalar reward, thousands of parallel environments, discounting, reward clipped to a fixed range" — to a named position on whether anything in that setup is a candidate sufferer. Aim for something like ten to fourteen branch nodes and six to nine leaves.

Rules I care about:

Every branch node has to be a claim or a concrete case someone could answer without already knowing which view it leads to, and none of them can just be "is reward the same thing as valence?". The sort of splits I want used are things like: whether valence has to be "for" a subject that persists across weight updates, or whether a single rollout could be the whole subject; whether the reward being written by an external designer (so it's a gift the agent never needed, rather than something it lacks) disqualifies it; whether a negative reward-prediction error is already enough for something disappointment-shaped, or whether suffering needs the state to actually grip the agent; whether intensity has to be encodable, so that clipping rewards to a narrow fixed range does something like analgesia; whether the negative signal has to be unavoidable for the policy to count as trapped; whether an architecturally installed "pain" term in a collision-avoidance robot differs from an ordinary shaping term; whether the multiplicity of parallel copies changes the moral arithmetic or only the headcount; whether the signal has to be globally broadcast and integrated or a local error in a value head is fine; whether a frozen deployed checkpoint with a low value estimate but no learning and no incoming reward is still a candidate; and whether an agent trained only on intrinsic curiosity, with no negative reward anywhere, is the control case that makes the whole debate collapse.

Then locate the actual positions in that space — the view that reward is just a hyperparameter with no valence anywhere, the view that reward literally is valence, the conditional architecturalist who says current systems no but successors yes, the "gradients of suffering"-style scale view, the process-level view that treats the optimisation run rather than the policy as the candidate subject, the organicist restriction, and whoever else belongs there — each with one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

Two extras: flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely can't separate rather than pretending the last question sorts them, and note the unoccupied combinations you get from your questions, with a sentence on whether nobody is there because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.
