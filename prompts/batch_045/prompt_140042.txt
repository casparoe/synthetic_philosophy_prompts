Before I commit to a relation I want the spec.

Write the desiderata a theory of 'better than' has to meet to be fit for that purpose — the cases it has to get right (chains of tiny improvements over many steps, small trade-offs along several dimensions at once, cases where neither option seems better though they clearly differ), the distinctions it can't collapse, the formal properties it can't contradict — with the case or consideration behind each, and mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of a particular camp (transitivity, acyclicity, completeness, menu-independence, sensitivity to small improvements, whatever else belongs on the list).

If that's a distinction people in this literature already draw under some name, or if it's just confusion on my part, say which.

Then, a line or two each on which single requirement excludes the most contenders — the aggregative/total-welfare view, the lexical-priority view, the vagueness-or-imprecision view, the essentially-comparative view. No table needed, no bibliography.
