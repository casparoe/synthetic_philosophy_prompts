Some background before the ask: I'm recently retired — spent the better part of thirty-five years doing taxonomic work on freshwater diatoms, so my instinct in any messy domain is to built a dichotomous key for it rather than argue about words — and about two years ago I started sitting with a Buddhist meditation group, which got me into a reading group on personal identity. The thing that keeps irritating me there is that people use talk of anatta to mean maybe five different things and then congratulate each other for agreeing. So I want a key, not an essay.

Please take the role of a professional philosopher who knows the Buddhist material properly, and think carefully before you write: start by brainstorming which cases actually discriminate between the views (i.e. which cases people who hold different views would answer differently), and only then commit to the final tree. What I want is a decision tree that sorts somebody from their answers about concrete cases to a named position on what a person is, with the no-self options as real leaves rather than as slogans. Roughly ten questions, eight to twelve leaves, audience is my reading group — a couple of ex-scientists like me and a bunch of long-time meditators — so no jargon without a one-line gloss. Cases I'd like used, or better ones if you have them: the fission/branching case; slow neuron-by-neuron replacement; total amnesia with dispositions and intentions intact; a dreamless-sleep or anaesthesia gap in a stream of consciousness; and a case where the mental stream carries on while every psychological feature changes gradually, so that "same stream" and "same psychology" come apart. Two things I specifically want in the tree because our group always collapses them: first, a node separating synchronic unity (is there a single subject of experience right now?) from diachronic unity (is there one persisting across time?) — I think there's an unoccupied box where you have a stream over time but no subject at a time, and another where you have a subject at a time and nothing that persists, and I want you to say whether those boxes are empty because they're incoherent, just unattractive, or merely untried; second, a node on whether the unity in question is a matter of degree or all-or-nothing. Also include a node on whether continuity is supplied by memory or by bare causal continuity of the mental stream, one on whether momentariness is a separate commitment or falls out of the rest, one on whether the conventional person is picked out by karmic/causal role or by psychological continuity, and one on whether a view has to make prudential self-concern (or anything like karmic consequence) intelligible to count as adequate. No question may just ask "do you believe in a self?" — sort people by what they say about the cases, not by which label they like. Build the tree from the logical space: give me the questions, say which answer-combinations they permit, then place the actual named views in that space and mark the empty combinations with a clause on why nobody lives there. Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really separate instead of pretending it can — I suspect the reductionist view and the Buddhist conventionalist one may collapse into each other, and I want to know if that's your fault or theirs. Finish with a short scoring key: which answer patterns land on which leaf, plus which patterns are ambiguous and what that ambiguity tells us. Don't bother with sutta references or a bibiography — half the group won't look them up and I can't check them anyway.
