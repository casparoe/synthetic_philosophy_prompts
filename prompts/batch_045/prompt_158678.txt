hey — I've got a rough essay-in-notes form on psychiatric classification and Hacking's looping effects, and I want some sharp comments on it before I decide whether it's worth writing up properly. Notes first, then the specific things I want poked at.

LOOP-PROOFING (working title)

1. The setup. Hacking's idea as I understand it: with a natural kind the thing classified doesn't care what we call it; with human kinds it does. Put people in a category — the mad travellers of the 1890s he wrote a whole book about, people with multiple personality, kids with ADHD — and some of them reorganise their self-understanding, their memories, their sense of what's expected of them around the category. That changes what there is to classify, so the kind moves under the very description that was supposed to track it, which is part of why the human sciences don't accumulate the way the natural ones do. He labels his version of this transcendental or dynamic nominalism. (I'm working partly from memory here so flag it if I've got him wrong.)

2. Three orders, not one. I want to claim the standard retelling stops one step early, and that there are three distinct feedback channels, which get attention in roughly the reverse order of how much they matter:
 - first-order: the classified person changes. This is Hacking's case and everyone knows it.
 - second-order: the classifiers change. The clinicians who sit on revision committees formed their clinical intuitions under the previous edition of the manual; their threshold for what counts as clinically significant impairment drifts with prevalence; and for some categories the people now revising the criteria include people who themselves have the diagnosis. The kind moves because the observers are inside the loop too.
 - third-order: the instrument changes meaning. Screening instruments are public now. People read the item list before they're ever assessed, online, in their own communities, and the questionnaire stops being a probe and becomes a script. That's a different failure from ordinary unreliability — the items are being trained on.

3. Loop-proofing. If looping is the problem, you could design a category to resist it: (a) criteria stated in terms of behaviour or measured performance rather than self-report; (b) thresholds anchored to a fixed historical baseline population instead of to distress-or-impairment, since distress and impairment are exactly the variables the first-order loop moves; (c) withholding the label from the patient, the way a drug's identity is withheld from a patient in a trial, so the self-understanding channel never opens.

4. The turn. Loop-proofing would probably work, technically. But I want to argue it's self-defeating, and that this tells us something: the loop isn't a defect in psychiatric kinds, it's the mechanism by which they take hold. A category that loops badly doesn't stay stable and useful, it just doesn't catch on, or it gets dropped at the next revision. Making up people is what the classification is for. If that's right, the instability isn't evidence that the kind isn't real — it's the sign that the kind has acquired causal grip. And it means the authority of a nosology is always partly a political fact about who wants the label, not a discovery about what's out there.

5. The bit I'm least sure about. Call it the uptake thesis: how fast a psychiatric kind churns should track how much cultural uptake the label gets, not how much we learn about the underlying mechanism. Prediction: psychiatric nosologies won't converge over time even given perfect neuroscience, because uptake keeps moving the target, and churn will be fastest for categories that are easy to self-apply, that explain a lot of ordinary failure, and that have an accommodation industry downstream of them.

6. Test case I invented, so the prediction isn't vacuous. Acquired depth-attention deficit (ADAD): persistent inability, onset after age twelve, to sustain attention on a single text or task beyond roughly fifteen minutes, not better explained by another condition, causing impairment in work or study. Suppose it's proposed for inclusion. My prediction: fast loop. Prevalence explodes, criteria broaden within two revisions, then a backlash splits it into a narrow severe medical form and a broad cultural identity, and by then the screening instrument is worthless because everyone has practised the items. Under loop-proofing — a timed sustained-reading measure on a fixed corpus — the first-order channel slows but doesn't close, because the test itself becomes something you train for.
