Design a case with the structure of Parfit's hitchhiker — one party must supply a benefit first, the other must pay afterwards, and at the later node paying is contrary to what the payer most wants — but relocate it: no roads, deserts, or cars. Put it somewhere ordinary and present-day, a hospital, a workplace, a tenancy, a lab, where the stakes are real for a normal person and the benefit genuinely has to be delivered before any payment can be extracted.

Now engineer the case so that the payer's credibility can be secured only by rational irrationality, in the strict sense: the payer does something in advance that leads them, at the moment of action, to do what they then have no self-interested reason to do. Make each of the three standard alternatives fail for a structural reason that you state explicitly: external commitment (escrow, deposit, contract, any third party with enforcement power); repeat play and reputation; and the familiar contractarian solution, a transparent disposition to comply of the constrained-maximizer sort. Add these constraints: the encounter is genuinely one-shot and effectively anonymous; there is no enforcement technology and no reliable intention- or lie-detection, only what one human being can read off another in a conversation; the payer cannot simply choose to hold a belief, so any self-modification has to run through something a person could actually do in an afternoon; at the moment of paying the payer knows both that paying buys them nothing further and that no one who matters will ever learn which way they chose; and the change they undergo must be costly to fake, so that a pretender cannot emit the same signal without undergoing the same change — say what the signal is and why it cannot be mimicked cheaply. It follows that the disposition must also be selective: it must not turn the payer into a general sucker, or adoption would not be worth it.

Then do the demonstration. Give the sequence of choices, say what each of the four accounts prescribes or predicts at each node and why, and mark where the credibility problem — making the disposition legible to the counterparty — is doing work that the rationality problem cannot do on its own. Then remove the unfakeability condition and tell me whether the case collapses back into a plain assurance problem or whether something of the puzzle survives. Finally, take a position on the nub: on a present-aim theory of rationality the cultivated disposition is not irrational to act on, since at the moment of acting it is what the agent most wants, whereas on a reward-based or reasons-based theory it is; say which verdict your case actually supports, say which of the four accounts comes out worst, and give the strongest objection a defender of that account would make against your case. I want to use this in a seminar to argue that the hard part of the hitchhiker is credibility rather than rationality, so flag any feature of your scenario that would let a student reply that the verdicts were stipulated rather than generated from the accounts.
