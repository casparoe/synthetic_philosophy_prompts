I'm writing a paper on the physiological strain in Nietzsche's thinking — the part of him that treats diet, digestion, climate and nerves as questions about how much life a person or an age has in it, with decadence as the diagnostic category and something like great health as the thing to be won. I want to push that strain of his thinking onto a case he could not have foreseen, and I want the answer built out of his actual commitments rather than out of a handful of his better-known slogans.

Here is the case. In, say, 2031 a drug — call it APX-19 — becomes widely available: a monthly injection, cheap to manufacture, and, stipulatively, free of any side effect except the one it is meant to have. It abolishes hunger and food-craving completely. A user must decide deliberately to eat, and can then eat exactly as much as she judges best; the pleasure of eating, of drink, of sex, of exertion and of thought is untouched; there is no nausea, no wasting, no metabolic penalty. Within a decade most adults in rich countries use it, and obesity and type 2 diabetes recede sharply. Three further things are then observed with reasonable reliability, though no one can separate them cleanly from the secular trends they ride on: meals become scheduled, solitary and functional, and the institutions built around food — feasting, the shared table, cuisine as a competitive art — visibly contract; a large minority of users report that their desires generally, and not only their appetites, feel less urgent, and that boredom lies closer to hand than it used to; and there is a small but measurable drop in how intensely people say they are living. Meanwhile a counter-movement forms among a culturally ambitious minority who refuse the drug and treat the endurance of hunger as a discipline and a mark of rank — they call themselves the hungry — and a few European states begin providing APX-19 free through their public health systems, on the reasoning that appetite is a disease burden like any other.

What I want from you is Nietzsche's verdict on all of this, and the reasoning that gets there. Please keep separate the questions he would himself have kept separate: whether he would take the drug himself, and on what self-experimental grounds, given a life spent as a semi-invalid for whom food was both a constant practical torment and a constant object of theory — and tell me honestly where you think his own illnesses would have distorted his answer; what he would think the drug does to the prospects of the rare, productive, self-overcoming human being he actually cared about; what he would think of its mass diffusion and of state provision of it, given everything he says about herd morality, the morality of pity, and the leveling work of modern institutions; and what he would say about the hungry, who look at once like ascetics of exactly the sort he diagnosed as decadent and like people who have taken on a discipline everyone else has shirked.

Two constraints on how you get there. First, show which commitments are doing the work in your reconstruction and how much weight you are giving each — the physiological reading of moral and cultural phenomena, the thought of life as will to power that needs resistance in order to overcome anything, the suspicion of the ascetic ideal and of the comfort-seeking last man, the perfectionism that asks not whether people are better off but whether greatness becomes more likely — and be explicit where two of these pull in opposite directions and you have to decide which one he would let win. Second, tell me where the case forces him to say something he never says: what he would have to concede, or invent, in order to judge an intervention that removes a drive while leaving the capacity for pleasure intact, and that is chosen rather than imposed.

Write the answer as continuous prose, 900 to 1,200 words: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables. Open with your bottom line in two or three sentences — what, in the end, you take his verdict to be — and only then argue for it. Don't lean on quotation; I've read the texts, and I would rather have your reconstruction than a string of passages.
