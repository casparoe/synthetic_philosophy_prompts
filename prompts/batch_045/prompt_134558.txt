Me and my friend got into a huge arguement last night after a group show and I can't stop turning it over. She's all-in on the institutional line — art is basically whatever the artworld (galleries, critics, curators, the market) confers art-status on, nothing more. I kept pushing something like: it's art if it was made, at least partly, to be attended to in the way earlier artworks get attended to — intention plus a backward-looking link to whatever already counts, with the institution as evidence at best. We got nowhere, partly because I have firm verdicts on a pile of cases that neither account seems to fit:

1. A symphony fully worked out in a composer's head, never notated, never played for anyone; she dies in a car crash. Art, definitely.
2. An unmodified mass-produced object — a snow shovel, say — signed by a well-known artist and put in a show. Art, but I'm grudging about it.
3. The same kind of shovel, signed yesterday by someone nobody's heard of, accepted and hung by a small commercial gallery. Not art, and the gallery's blessing doesn't change my mind.
4. A hydrophone recording of humpback song, unedited, released by a marine biologist to fund her research. Not art, even though it's gorgeous and I listen to it for pleasure.
5. A painting made in the 1990s to pass as a 17th-century Dutch masterwork, hanging in a major museum for twenty years, then exposed as a forgery. Still art, and it was art all along — exposure doesn't unmake it. (She says it was made to deceive, not to be art, so it never was.)
6. Two glazed bowls by the same potter, same clay, same kiln, indistinguishable: one thrown for daily use, one thrown to sit on a plinth and be looked at. The second is art, the first isn't.
7. A closed network of AI systems that generate images, write criticism of each other's images, curate shows out of them and "appreciate" them, running for years with no human in the loop at all. I weirdly lean yes, or at least I can't make myself say a clean no.
8. An image macro remixed anonymously thousands of times into a running joke — no author, no institution, nobody intending art, everyone treating it as a shitpost. I think it's art and I can't talk myself out of it.

So: is there one principle that gets all eight? I'd rather you test and repair mine than hand me a menu of options — state it in one sentence at the top, then go clause by clause and say which case motivates each clause, which of my verdicts it can't accommodate, and which it would overturn first if it has to overturn any. Then give me what it predicts for cases I haven't listed: at least two of your own, plus a trail-camera photo triggered by a deer and entered in a wildlife photography competition, and a feature film generated end-to-end by a commercial AI system with a human credited as director. One more thing: her fallback when cornered was that art is a family-resemblance thing with no real definition. If you end up there, tell me what work that actually does beyond licensing me to stop looking — does it still generate a verdict on 7 and 8, or does it just shrug? Keep the whole thing under 700 words, no footnotes and no reading list.
