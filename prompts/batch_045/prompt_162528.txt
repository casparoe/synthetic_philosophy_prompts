I'm in a comp-ling phd program and theres an analogy my supervisor keeps leaning on in group meetings, and I cant tell whether it's doing real work or just carrying the conclusion.

His version: a language model is like a dictionary — every entry is defined in terms of other entries, and nothing ever points outside the language. Sometimes he swaps in a person who's spent their whole life in a library and has read everything ever written about the world without ever seeing it. He got it, as far as I can tell, from the Harnad-style framing of the symbol grounding problem plus the Chinese room setup, and from it he concludes: text-only training can't ground anything, so no LLM means anything by "red" or "squishy", and you need a layer of nonsymbolic sensorimotor representations — the capacity to actually pick out referents — before meaning talk is even licensed.

My labmate's counter, which I can't shake: grounding is like backing currency with gold. Gold is supposed to be the thing outside the money system that anchors it, but gold is itself just another commodity that trades inside the economy, so the "outside" is fake. Either the demand is trivially satisfiable — any non-textual signal counts as gold, so any multimodal model trained on images is grounded — or it's impossible, because the visual pathway gets "read" by downstream layers too, so nothing is outside either way.

Then there's a third thing I keep chewing on because it's my actual project: an LLM emmiting action plans for a robot arm, where contact-mic readings get turned into text and fed back in before the next step. Nothing there looks like a dictionary entry and nothing looks like gold; it looks more like money being accepted in exchange. Is that a third kind of grounding, or just more currency?

Can you audit this for me? What I want is the correspondence tables: for the dictionary/library analogy, what plays the headword, the definition, the reader, the referent, and understanding; for the money one, what plays gold, the currency, the exchange rate, backing, and inflation. Then: which features of the source does the target genuinely lack, and which ones does the analogy quietly import — e.g. that grounding is a property of individual symbol tokens rather than of a whole causally embedded system, that perception is itself analog and nonsymbolic, that "outside the system" is even well-defined for something physically implemented? Where does each mapping break for the specific inference he's making, from "trained only on text" to "no meaning", and does that conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table? And is there a better analogy for whatever is actually going on in the robot-loop case? Please don't hand me a reading list — just the analysis.
