I moderate a large hobbyist forum, and over some years I have noticed a recurring shape in the claims and practices that spread through it, for which I can find no word. It is not that they are false, or vivid, or useful. It is something about how they handle being checked.

Three instances, invented but typical. A warning circulates about a faulty batch of a component, and it travels from the start with a rider: that anyone who asks to see the measurements is advertising that he does not care whether people get hurt. The warning may well be true; that is not the point. What travels with it is a clause that makes the act of checking expensive, and the cost falls on the checker rather than on the believer. Second: a practice spreads through a volunteer organisation of never writing decisions down, carried along by the remark that wanting them written down is itself the trouble. Third: a conversation-stopper — only someone who has never had to do this would ask that. Nothing is said about the question. The questioner is simply priced out.

I have read a little of the popular Darwinian literature about ideas spreading the way genes do, and I do not think the mechanisms usually listed are doing the work here. The parasite or mind-virus picture does not fit either: nothing is being hijacked, the ordinary channels of transmission work perfectly well, and what distinguishes these variants concerns scrutiny rather than copying.

Two further observations, which may or may not mark real distinctions. In the cases I care about the shield is rarely part of the variant's own content; it is borrowed from a norm that was already in place, so the variant is protected by something it did not have to carry. And the protected element is usually attached to a package that is independently worth passing on, so it may be riding along rather than being selected for.

My questions. Is there an established concept for selection on this property — the property of making oneself costly or impossible to check — as a mechanism distinguishable from the familiar biases of content, prestige and conformity? Is Darwinian machinery the right machinery here at all, or is selection the wrong word for what is going on? And where does the boundary lie between a clause that shields a claim from scrutiny and one that legitimately specifies what checking a claim of that kind would involve — a specification that any serious framework of inquiry has to make? The distinction is not that the claim is unfalsifiable in principle: the measurements might exist. It is that the route to them has been socially closed.

If there is no established term, coin one, define it tightly, and give me cases that fall in and out. If there is one, tell me where my description fits and where it does not. A name by itself is of no use to me. I have to write moderation policy, and a rule against unverified claims would catch whistleblowing along with everything else, so I need the carving rather than the label — and I would rather you flagged the places where the classification is genuinely contested than smoothed them over.
