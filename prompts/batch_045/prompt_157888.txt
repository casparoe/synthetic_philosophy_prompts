ok so I need advice and Im not even sure what *kind* of advice it is, which is maybe the first clue.

I write novels. Suspense-ish literary stuff, six books out, loyal readers, not remotely rich. And I narrate my own audiobooks — always have, since book two. It's about five or six weeks in a booth per book, plus the pickups, and I hate maybe a third of it and love the rest.

My publisher has proposed this: they've got a system that can build a voice model from the recordings I've already done (theres ~40 hours of me reading my own prose, apparently that's plenty). They'd use it to narrate the new books, re-do the early ones, and do the "dramatized" edition they've been wanting. I'd get a one-time fee and a smaller royalty per copy, no booth time. I keep the writing entirely — nobody's asking to touch the prose, and I want to be clear about that because it's the part people assume.

Here's what's messing me up. Like five things.

One: readers write to me about the *reading*. Not the writing. People say they listen at 1x specifically because of how I do a certain character, or that they "hear" the books in my voice now. If the model is good enough that they can't tell, is anything actually lost? And if they *can* tell and prefer it, is that just snobbery I shouldn't indulge?

Two, and this is the one I keep coming back to: reading aloud is where I find out if a sentence works. Around book three I started reading drafts out loud to myself before I turned them in, and my editor says my later drafts got noticeably tighter. I think the booth is part of how I write now. My fear is that if I give up the reading I don't just lose five weeks of unpleasantness, I lose a thing that was quietly training me, and the writing degrades three years later and I never connect the two. Is that a real worry or am I inventing a noble reason to keep doing the fun part?

Three: I narrate the female POV chapters and there's a guy — Dan — who does the male POV ones, and he's built a chunk of his career on my series. Cloning me probably means cloning him or just cutting the second voice. So my "five weeks back" is partly his rent.

Four: the obvious math. Six weeks returned per book, smaller cut per copy. I could plausibly write another book a year with that time. If my actual goal is "more novels exist," the deal is clearly good. If my goal is something about the shape of my weeks, maybe it isn't. I genuinely dont know which of those is the right thing to want.

Five: I took one philosophy class in college and I remember the word alienation, so I'm suspicious of my own vocabulary here. I can't tell if my gut reaction is a real insight about work or just craft-prestige plus fear of being replaceable plus status quo bias in a trenchcoat.

So: is there a version of this where refusing is principled rather than precious? Is there a version where signing and then narrating anyway — into a microphone at home, releasing nothing, purely as practice — is coherent, or is that just having it both ways? And when you tell me what to do, please also tell me which part of my discomfort is load-bearing and which part I should get over.
