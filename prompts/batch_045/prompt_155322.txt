My epistemology study group has been chewing on the Judy Benjamin case for a week and I've gone and built an argument out of it that I'm now fairly sure is sick, but I can't put my finger on where. Simplified setup: Judy is dropped into a grid of four equally likely cells — Red or Blue territory crossed with Headquarters or Second Company — so before the radio message she's at 1/4 on each cell, and territory and company are independent. The message has exactly one content: conditional on being in Second Company territory, the chance she's in Red is 3 out of 4. That's it. It's a constraint on a conditional, not a new probability for any cell of either partition. My argument goes:

(1) So the whole of what she learns is: her posterior has to satisfy P(Red | Second) = 3/4. Nothing about the marginal of {Red, Blue}, nothing about the marginal of {HQ, Second}.
(2) The message is about territory. It says nothing at all about which company she's in.
(3) When evidence bears on one partition, you hold fixed the conditional probabilities of everything else given the cells of that partition. That's the rigidity principle, it's what makes Jeffrey conditioning the standard story for uncertain evidence, and I don't have any other story.
(4) With a uniform prior, rigidity on {Red, Blue} pins P(Second | Red) = P(Second | Blue) = 1/2, and then a couple of lines of algebra gives P(Red | Second) = P(Red) no matter what. So meeting the constraint forces P(Red) = 3/4.
(5) Posterior: Red&Second = 3/8, Blue&Second = 1/8, Red&HQ = 3/8, Blue&HQ = 1/8. Note what happened: P(Red | HQ) climbed from 1/2 to 3/4, even though the message was explicitly restricted to the Second Company cell and told her nothing whatsoever about HQ.
(6) And it generalizes into something I definitely don't want. Take a disease with a 1% base rate, a variant carried by 5% of people, and P(carries | diseased) = 0.9, so P(carries | healthy) is about 0.041. Now learn "if you carry the variant, your chance of the disease is 0.8." Run the same rigidity on the partition {diseased, healthy}, hold P(carries | diseased) and P(carries | healthy) fixed, solve for the new P(diseased), and you land somewhere around 15%. I get roughly 8% if instead I hold the carrier rate fixed and only touch the carrier cell — which is what any clinician would say — so my argument seems to be instructing base-rate neglect out of a message that never told you whether you carry the variant.

One person in the group says the right move is to minimize relative information to the prior, which gives Red&Second = 3/8, Blue&Second = 1/8, Red&HQ = Blue&HQ = 1/4 — a genuinely different posterior, with P(Red) only 5/8 — but nobody could tell me why that beats rigidity rather than just being a different temperment. If it matters, I'd rather keep (1) than (3). Which step do you throw out, what does throwing it out cost you, and is there any principled way to pick the partition you hold rigid when the thing you learned is conditional — or does the whole question secretly depend on how the message was produced, which the problem never says?
