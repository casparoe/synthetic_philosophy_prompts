I'm a retired geophysicist — most of my career was spent with sediment cores, so I've got some feel for very long timescales — and lately I've been going to a philosophy reading group with a few other old people. Last month we did the non-identity argument and it split the room right down the middle: half of them think its a genuine proof that our energy policy can't harm whoever comes after us, and half of us think it's a conjuring trick we should be embarrassed to have fallen for. I'm in the second half, and I'd like to write something for the group that makes my side of the case without ever arguing it.

So: could you write me a document that's funny because the argument is stated correctly?

What I have in mind is an extract from the official guidance issued by a made-up Office for Intergenerational Equity — the standard form civil servants have to fill in when they assess whether a policy harms future generations — together with a short covering memo from the Office's director. Numbered clauses, deadpan bureacratic register, around 700–900 words.

The Office has fully internalised the person-affecting view together with the non-identity argument, and its guidance should read as the administrative working-out of that. I'd like the following things to be load-bearing in the piece rather than explained anywhere:

- the thought that who someone is depends on the circumstances and timing of their conception, so a policy that changes those doesn't make anyone worse off — it just means a different person turns up. Please invent at least two worked examples of your own along those lines rather than recycling the famous ones.
- the cases the argument doesn't reach, where the very same people would exist either way, and the Office's evident discomfort with them — I'd like it to treat those, quietly, as a loophole to be closed rather than as the ordinary case.
- a discount rate. Make up plausible-looking numbers and let the compound interest do part of the joke, with damages two centuries out coming out as pocket change.
- one place where the guidance is forced to concede, in exactly the same flat voice, that on its own rules a policy making every future life considerably worse than it would otherwise have been still scores as harming nobody — and where the Office plainly does not regard that as a difficulty.

Nobody in the document should ever use the phrases "non-identity problem", "person-affecting" or "repugnant conclusion", and please don't add an afterword explaining the joke or a note saying what's being satirised — I want the group to get there on their own. If you can smuggle the repugnant conclusion in as a single clause the Office waves away as out of scope, so much the better.
