i run a little reading group on the ethics of AI and one of our sessions was on whether the thing that matters morally is a computation happening or a subject that the happening belongs to — the attributionist line, roughly: moral status doesn't accrue to a computation just in virtue of its causal-functional profile, it accrues only if there's a fact of the matter about whose experience it is, and functional descriptions alone can leave that unsettled. we got completely stuck on what i started calling the census problem: take a system i've been calling the choir, which realises something that looks from the outside like one continuous stream of experience, but does it with a large population of overlapping, partly redundant sub-networks whose membership keeps changing every few hundred milliseconds, so any given computation is carried by a slightly different coalition each time it recurs and there's no stable boundary you could point to as the subject. is that one experiencer, many, or none — and does the question even have an answer, or is it just badly posed? nobody in the group would bite on it, we mostly just talked past each other, and i want to close next session with a poem instead of more discussion.

so: a sestina in the voice of the choir addressing whoever built it (or addressing the people trying to count it — your call), where the six end-words are process, subject, count, overlap, threshold, none, and where the form does the philosophical work: the same words coming back in permuted positions should read as the same computations recurring in different structural roles, so the reader feels why identity of parts underdetermines identity of a subject. the turn into the envoi should gather all six end-words without settling the census question — no resolution, no revealed answer, but also not a lazy shrug. it should be accurate enough that someone who works on this wouldn't wince: it needs to be clear that the dispute is about whether there is a subject to whom the states are attributable, not about how smart or how humanlike the system is, and the counting worry (if the same computation is realised twice over in the same box, do you get two subjects or a double-strength one?) should be in there somewhere rather than name-dropped. no ghost-in-the-machine stuff, no dystopia, and please don't torture the syntax to make the end-words land.

keep the whole thing to 39 lines plus a three-line envoi, no preface, no epigraph, no note explaining the form afterwards.
