I help run a small reading group that works through popular books on math and AI, and I keep tripping over the same argument shape. Someone wants to show that a thing can't be captured by rules — human mathematical reasoning, or what an AI system should be aiming at, or a final theory in physics — and they reach for incompleteness: any rulebook rich enough will have truths it can't get to, therefore whatever it is we're doing can't be bottled, therefore either the thing doing it must be able to step outside any rules, or no one should expect to ever finish writing them down. I've seen the version about minds and I think that one has a name (Lucas-Penrose?), but the version I keep meeting isn't about minds or consciousness at all. In one case the conclusion was completely flat: you'll never finish the spec, so build in ongoing correction instead of pretending the spec is complete. That sounded reasonable to me and not at all mystical, and I can't tell weather it's the same move being careful or a different move wearing the same clothes.

The other thing that bugs me is that the instances don't feel alike underneath. In the physics one the gap seems to come from the rules being able to talk about themselves. In the rules-for-a-system one it seems to come from the world throwing up situations nobody imagined when the rules were written, wich I gather already has a name of its own (something about open texture? I'm fuzzy). And there's a third version where people say no system can prove its own consistency, so you can never really trust your own reasoning, and I feel like a step is missing there but I can't say which.

So: is there an established name for the slide from "this fixed rulebook has blind spots" to "no artifact could do this / no rules will ever be finished"? I don't want to just file the whole family under "people misuse Gödel," because in at least one of my cases I think the conclusion survives. I'd rather have the map than the label: which of my cases are really the same thing, which are different and how, and if nothing established fits, give me a name and tell me what would and what wouldn't count as an instance. Don't bother with references — I just want to be able to say, next time this comes up, which one somebody is actually doing.
