Hi — I'm on a small volunteer working group drafting a proposal for a standing randomly selected chamber (a sortition-y second chamber, rotating half its members every year) for our regional government, and I keep running into one line.

I suggested we don't use a flat lottery but a stratified one that over-samples the people with the most at stake in whatever the chamber is deciding — so for a housing bill, renters and people in temporary accommodation get something like double the weight in the draw — and someone killed it with "it should be a mirror of the people". Wich I get, sort of, but I don't think it settles anything and I can't say why.

So: what are the different precise things that slogan could be claiming? Mirror which traits, and measured how? Mirror at every moment, or only across the sequence of rotating panels over time? Mirror people, or mirror opinions, or mirror something else entirely? Is it a claim about what makes the body legitimate, or about what makes it produce good decisions, or about both with the two coming apart? And is it a claim about how the members are picked, or about what they're allowed to become once they're in the room? Give me the whole menu, trivial to radical.

Then verdicts. Which readings are platitudes nobody would deny, which are actually false (my guess: a 150-person panel can't literally mirror a population on more than a handful of traits at once, and the really strong version where the assembly somehow just *is* the people has to be incoherent — but tell me if I'm wrong), and which ones would actually justify shooting down my stake-weighted draw.

One thing I've been chewing on that I want you to engage with rather than just nod at: if mirroring means mirroring opinions, then the whole deliberative point of these bodies is in trouble, because a panel that has genuinely deliberated has changed its mind and doesn't mirror anything anymore. So maybe what we should want to mirror isn't persons or opinions but the spread of reasons and considerations that exist out there in the public — or the spread of stakes — and whether that twist helps or hurts my proposal is exactly what I can't work out.

Please commit. Tell me which one or two readings you'd actually design a chamber around, whether my over-sampling idea survives on that reading, and what would change your mind about it. Don't just lay out the options and wish me luck.
