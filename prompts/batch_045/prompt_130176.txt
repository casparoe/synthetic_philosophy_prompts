I help moderate an online discussion forum where the case of one person being tortured for fifty years versus an astronomically large number of people each getting a speck of dust in the eye comes up regularly. Over the years I've noticed two patterns in how people argue about it, and I have no word for either. I'd like to know whether either has an established name, but a name alone won't do: I want the phenomenon itself carved up and distinguished from its neighbours.

The first is a ladder. Line up harms by how bad a single instance is — a speck in the eye, a day-long migraine, a broken arm, a year of chronic pain, fifty years of torture — and for each adjacent pair ask whether there is some number of the milder one that would be worse than one instance of the harsher. Nearly everyone says yes, rung by rung, without much hesitation. Then ask about the bottom rung against the top — some number of specks versus one torture — and many of the same people say no, no number could be enough. In my experience nobody can point to the rung where they switch.

The second: hold the total fixed by any additive measure and change only how it is spread. A million people get one speck each; one person gets a million specks, one per minute for two years; one person gets a million specks in a single moment. People rank these very differently, and the spread version strikes them as less bad the more people share it, even though the sum is unchanged and sharing makes nobody's own share smaller.

What I want is the carving. Is the first pattern best described as the claim that no amount of the mild harm could ever outweigh the severe one, as the claim that there is no fact of the matter about whether it does, or as the claim that there is a number but it is blurry? Is the second pattern a separate phenomenon or the first one in another guise? If there are established concepts nearby, tell me which is nearest, how each pattern differs from it, and which fits best; if not, coin a term, define it, and give me the boundary cases. Please skip reading suggestions and references.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning with no preamble and no restating of my question, and finish by naming the part of your answer you would abandon first and what argument or case would make you abandon it.
