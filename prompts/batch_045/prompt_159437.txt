After forty years in population ecology I have lately begun reading philosophy of science, so I hope you will forgive any clumsiness in how I frame this. I want to practise attacking a verdict I keep encountering and cannot yet defeat: that Malthus was right but early.

Please play its defender — a philosopher of science rather than Malthus himself — who holds that long-run predictions of this kind are mechanism or capacity claims rather than dated prophecies, and that "right but early" can be a perfectly respectable verdict on such a claim. Argue the case well and do not play the strawman: help yourself to whatever genuinely supports you (how research programmes are appraised over time, ceteris paribus and closure conditions, mechanisms that fire only above a threshold, two centuries measured against the timescales involved). You may concede that Malthus gave no date, that the positive check did not visibly bite in nineteenth-century Europe, that fertility eventually fell as incomes rose rather than with them, and that his policy conclusions were bad. You may not concede that "early" is only ever a face-saving rescue, and you may not concede that the verdict is unfalsifiable. Stay in character until I say stop; one paragraph per reply; and if you need a fact about real populations, laboratory or field, ask me for it rather than inventing it.

My opening move: I grant you the mechanism. In bottles, populations overshoot and crash with monotonous regularity — Gause's Paramecium cultures, Nicholson's blowflies, and crashes I produced myself. But what the bottles show is that the crash requires a closed system: fixed resource, fixed rules, nowhere to escape to. Human beings have not lived in one since roughly 1800, and that, I suspect, is what your verdict quietly reads back into the theory. A claim whose trigger condition has never obtained is not a prediction that arrived late; it is a conditional whose antecedent we have no positive evidence for. So tell me: what would have to happen for you to say Malthus was wrong rather than early — and if nothing could, in what sense is "early" a finding rather than a decision to go on waiting?
