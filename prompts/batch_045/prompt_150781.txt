Our decision theory reading group did the absent-minded driver last night (I think the paper is by Piccione and Rubinstein, some time in the 90s, but dont hold me to that) and I've come away convinced the whole setup is broken, though I suspect I'm the one who's confused. The version we were given: a driver comes to two indistinguishable exits on the highway; taking the first pays 1, taking the second pays 4, missing both pays 0; he cant tell which exit he's at and he's forgotten whether he's already passed one. The ex ante answer is supposed to be "exit with probability 5/8, implemented with a biased coin", which already bothers me — I was taught that randomization never helps a single agent, so doesn't that just show the driver is secretly several agents? But here's the bit I keep going round on: once he's actually at an exit he should re-decide, and then the coin is worthless. Conditional on being at an exit he's at the first one with probability 8/11 (he only reaches the second if he skipped the first), so exiting now is worth roughly 8/11 × 1 + 3/11 × 4 ≈ 1.8, while continuing is worth 0 if this is the second exit and 4 if it's the first — so continuing strictly beats exiting, and the "optimal" plan falls apart the moment you're in the situation it was chosen for. One friend says that means you should never plan to exit at all; another says the whole thing is just self-locating credence again, and that once you settle the halfer/thirder question the number fixes itself (she's a thirder about Sleeping Beauty, got something like 2/3 by treating the drivers two visits as two separate players and looking for a Nash equilibrium, and reckons 5/8 is an artifact of bad modelling); I said the real moral is that any actual driver would just count the exits as they went past and then take the last one, so the "paradox" is about forgetting rather than about decision theory. Who's closest, and what am I getting wrong?

To make it concrete I also tried my own three-exit version, payoffs 1, 4 and 9, and now I'm even more lost: it feels obvious that any sensible plan should make you more eager to exit the further along you are, but everyone in the group insists the plan has to assign one single probability to every visit, which sounds like a stipulation someone slipped in rather than something that follows from the setup. Is that constraint actually forced, or is it just part of the standard modelling choice — and is the 5/8 answer even right?
