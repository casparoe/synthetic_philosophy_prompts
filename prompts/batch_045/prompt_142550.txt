I design model-based fault detection and isolation systems for safety-critical process plants, and I have for about fifteen years: observer and Kalman filter design, residual generation, the eternal trade between sensitivity to faults and robustness to disturbance and model uncertainty, minimal detectable fault sizes, unknown-input observers, isolability as opposed to mere detectability, and more recently the false-data-injection literature, where an adversary corrupts the measurement channel along a direction that lies inside the residual's null space and is therefore invisible to the monitor no matter how well tuned it is. I have no philosophy beyond a first-year survey course, and I've fallen into an argument with a philosopher friend who claims that Nozick's tracking analysis of knowledge, and his denial that knowledge is closed under known logical entailment, are in some sense the same problem I work on, with the same structure and the same difficulties. I don't want to lose this argument on a pun about the word tracking, so I want the translation done properly and then taken apart.

Here is what I want translated, and the vocabulary to translate it into. The material is: the tracking conditions themselves, both of them, the one requiring that if the target proposition were false the believer would not believe it and the one requiring that if it were true the believer would, with both subjunctive conditionals evaluated while the believer's method of forming the belief is held fixed across the counterfactual situations; the use of that analysis to dissolve Gettier-style cases; and then the anti-closure argument, according to which I can know that I have hands, and know that having hands entails not being a brain in a vat, and competently perform that deduction, and still not know that I am not a brain in a vat, since in the nearest situations in which I am envatted my method delivers the same belief. My friend insists the point survives even for the weakest, single-premise version of closure, which is why she thinks it bites. There is also a counterexample family she mentioned that I want included: cases where the belief is insensitive only because, were the proposition false, something else would have intervened to produce the very same belief, so that a fault would be masked by an active compensator rather than by a blind sensor. Please recast all of this in the terms I actually use: plant and measurement models, an estimator whose structure and gain are frozen, residuals, the distinction between faults, disturbances and channel corruption, the subspace of perturbations the monitor cannot see, detectability versus isolability, some ordering on perturbations that makes talk of the nearest failure well-defined, and the requirement that a test be both non-degenerate and powerful. My own first pass, which I suspect is too glib, is that tracking just means the residual responds to the fault consisting in the proposition's being false while the estimator is held fixed, and that closure fails because detectability doesn't propagate along logical implication: taking a consequence re-partitions which situations count as failures and re-ranks them by distance, so a residual tuned to one hypothesis can be blind to one it entails. I want that checked rather than flattered.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Tell me what came across cleanly, what came across only after you quietly changed the subject, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all, and in each case give me your honest view on whether the gap is a defect of the engineering framework or a sign that the philosophical claim was less determinate than it sounded. Four things I specifically want adjudicated. First, what in my vocabulary corresponds to holding the method fixed: the estimator structure, the gain, the measurement model, the disturbances I have declared exogenous? In my own field I can make a fault detectable or undetectable purely by deciding which parts of the model are uncertain and which are given, so I want to know whether the philosophers' dispute about the grain of the method is that same move, and whether that makes the tracking conditions interest-relative in a harmless way or empty in a fatal one. Second, what takes the place of the ordering over counterfactual situations: I only have norms on signals and bounds on disturbance sets, whereas I gather the philosophical machinery is willing to evaluate counterfactuals at situations where the laws themselves are violated, which for me is not a perturbation of the plant at all but a change of model, so I want to know what breaks when a signal norm is substituted for a similarity ordering. Third, whether the anti-closure result is the banal structural point I took it for or has a further content my framework cannot reach, since my framework will happily tell me that a good detector of one condition need not be a good detector of a condition it entails and sees nothing puzzling in that. And fourth, what to do with propositions that cannot be false, where the sensitivity condition looks vacuously satisfied in the way an alarm condition that can never occur has a perfect false-alarm rate: does my vocabulary offer anything here, some analogue of demanding that a test have power against alternatives, or does it simply fall silent? Finish by telling me whether the residue, taken as a whole, points to something real about knowledge that a signal-level model of estimation and fault detection structurally cannot represent, or whether part of what my friend is defending is an artefact of a formalism that lets you choose too much after the fact.
