I've been running a free will reading group for a couple of years now — four of us, mostly grad students, plus an emeritus who turns up mainly to tell us we've all misread his father — and we've spent two years on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument without anyone shifting position by a millimetre. I want a diagnosis, not a verdict and not a survey of the literature.

Here's the shape of it as we've been arguing it. Strawson's three steps: you do what you do because of the way you are; to be truly responsible for what you do you'd have to be truly responsible for the way you are, at least in the crucial mental respects; but to be responsible for the way you are you'd have to have made yourself that way, which already requires a nature in light of which you did the making — regress, self-creation is impossible, done. And he insists the conclusion comes out the same whether determinism is true or not, so indeterminism isn't an escape hatch. Then the optimist half of the room: Fischer and Ravizza on guidance control plus the idea that you can come to be responsible for your character by taking responsibility for it; the identification/attribution line people run through Watson and Korsgaard, where what matters is whether the action expresses who you are rather than whether you authored who you are; the complaint that the second premise is asking for something not even coherent; Wolf conceding the desert-based thing and re-describing freedom as the ability to do the right thing for the right reasons; Dennett treating the demand as mystery-mongering. And then Pereboom, who basically agreed with Strawson and spent an hour arguing that nothing changes in practice, which the semicompatibilists took as proof he'd given away the store.

Hypothesis I've been nursing, and I want you to tell me if it's a dud: this isn't a dispute about free will or even about responsibility at all, it's an Agrippan regress dispute in a costume. Strawson is the sceptic; reasons-responsiveness and taking-responsibility are the reliabilist-style stopping points; the identification/attribution accounts are the coherentist ones, where who you are is held in place by the network of your commitments instead of grounded in anything; and nobody has ever seriously defended the infinitist option, which on this reading is the one that might actually work. If that's right, nothing about agency or the mind is going to settle it, because the real disagreement is about what would count as a regress stopping. Second, smaller hypothesis: some of the standoff is just verbal — Strawson means basic-desert-entailing responsibility and a lot of his critics defend something thinner and then declare victory.

So: is either of those right? Where exactly is the fault line — a hidden factual question about how agency works, a normative one about what makes desert claims appropriate, a verbal one about the word 'responsibility', bedrock intuitions about whether self-creation is even intelligible that neither side can argue for, or people answering different questions under one heading? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a formal result about regresses, a distinction nobody has drawn, dropping the desert-entailing reading, actual data about folk concepts, or should we just expect the standoff and be able to say why?

Also please be explicit about which bits of what you say are settled among people who actually work on this and which are contested or your own speculation, and flag anywhere my framing above is probably just wrong. Sorry this got long, hopefully its useful.
