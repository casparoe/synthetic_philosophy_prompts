I want you to restate the Aunt Hillary debate in that vocabulary. Aunt Hillary is Hofstadter's ant colony from the Ant Fugue: the idea that a colony can be a single subject that has beliefs and desires and can be conversed with, while individual ants relate to it the way neurons relate to you.

There are four positions I want translated, one at a time, and I want each rendered as faithfully as you can manage without quietly substituting your own framework's assumptions and calling it translation:

1. The enthusiast: colony-level belief and desire attributions are literally true, not merely useful; the colony is a genuine subject, and the ant's point of view is as irrelevant to the colony's mind as a neuron's is to yours.
2. The debunker: only the ants have minds; colony-level talk is bookkeeping; there is no fact of the matter how many minds occupy the nest, and any decomposition you pick is gerrymandered.
3. The instrumentalist: colony-level states are real patterns — incompressible away, predictive, not eliminable — but treating them as beliefs is a modeling stance; the colony is an agent in a way a thermostat is not and a person is.
4. The consciousness objector: grant the whole functional and information-processing story and there is still an unanswered further question about whether there is anything it is like to be the colony, and a correct functional description does not settle it.

Test each translation against four cases I care about, because they are where my intuition is unstable:

- The mirrored colony. Researchers invert the trail-gradient across part of the foraging terrain, so the colony's collective state becomes reliably anti-correlated with where food actually is, while every individual ant's local rule is unchanged. In my terms the colony's state is now systematically wrong. Is that a false belief, or merely a sensor inversion for which no one is in error?
- The replayed ant. A forager is isolated from the nest before it ever walks a trail, then given the complete trail-chemical history of a route and thereafter behaves exactly as a veteran of that route does. In my terms this is log replay into a fresh replica. Does a replayed replica remember, or only behave as if it does?
- Partition and heal. The nest is split in half for two weeks; each half develops its own coherent foraging pattern; then they are rejoined. Was that one mind temporarily partitioned, two minds that merged, or one death and one survivor — and does my vocabulary have the resources to say which, or only the resources to describe the traffic?
- Nested crews. The sub-colony that tends the fungus garden looks exactly like a smaller version of the same architecture. Is it also a subject, and is Aunt Hillary then a component inside a larger one? This is my version of asking whether a Raft group inside a service is an agent.
