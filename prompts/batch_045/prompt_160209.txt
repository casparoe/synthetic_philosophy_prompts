Hey — I'm the trainee rep on my hospital's Courage and Conscience Award committee (I'm a PhD student in bioethics; I got put on it because someone had to be). We have to decide this year's award by Thursday and we are completely deadlocked, so I want to circulate a short written opinion beforehand that actually recommends something.

The award is one per year, a plaque plus $5k from a donor whose deed says it's for 'an act no one could have been required to do.' Three finalists:

- A radiography tech who flagged a shielding defect in the interventional suite, was repeatedly told to drop it, kept going, and eventually left for another job after being frozen out by his own department. The defect was real and got fixed. No one was harmed yet.
- A nurse who stayed through the evacuation during last year's flooding, hand-bagging ventilated patients for about eleven hours in the dark after the generators failed, against an explicit order to leave. Two of the four patients she was bagging died anyway.
- An intern who donated a kidney to a patient she had met twice on consults. No prior relationship, no family pressure.

The split is roughly: one half says the tech was just doing his job, reporting a safety problem is exactly what you're supposed to do, and rewarding him insults the people who quietly report things every year and get nothing. The other half says that if we skip him we are telling everyone that the cost of doing the right thing falls entirely on them.

Three more things I need handled. First, a committee member found out the intern is going through a divorce and has said, off the record, that the donation was 'about guilt, not generosity.' I think that should be irrelevant but I can't really say why. Second, someone has argued that honouring this kind of thing tells everyone else the behaviour is optional — that the award itself lowers the bar. That objection keeps sticking with me and I don't know what to do with it. Third, we screened out a fourth candidate earlier because covering for a colleague in rehab meant lying to the scheduling system — good outcome, dodgy means — and I'd like to know whether that screen was right.

So: draft the opinion I send round. It should name who gets the award, or give a defensible reason for giving none, and it should say what 'beyond what anyone could have been required to do' ought to mean for us next year, since we'll hit this again. Please don't settle for 'all three are praiseworthy' — the whole point is that we have to pick. And the strongest thing on the losing side has to appear in it properly, not as a straw man. Its the bit I keep going in circles on.
