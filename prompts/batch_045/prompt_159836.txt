Here is the impasse. Van Fraassen's Reflection principle says, roughly: your current credence in A, conditional on your later credence in A being x, should itself be x. I have been watching this get argued about for years, in two places that barely talk to each other. In Bayesian/rationalist blog and forum culture it is treated as close to a theorem: the diachronic Dutch book or Dutch strategy argument is taken to settle it, violations are treated as free money for a bookie, and anyone who dissents is treated as not having internalized conditionalization. In academic epistemology the same principle is treated as one consideration among many, and a small fixed set of counterexamples is treated as refuting it: cases where you know you will forget things you now know; cases where you know you will be drugged, deceived, or otherwise distorted; cases where you know you will be given misleading evidence; cases where all that changes is your self-location; and the permissivist thought that there may be no unique rational credence for your later self to land on, so "your later credence" has no determinate reference.

The dialectical pattern I keep seeing, and want explained, is this. A defender restates the principle more carefully — restrict it to a partition of propositions you might learn, or to cases where the later self has strictly more information, or read it as a constraint on plans and policies rather than on credences held at a time. A critic produces another case in which the later self is epistemically worse off than the earlier one rather than better. The defender says that case was already excluded by the formulation. The critic says the exclusion is ad hoc and question-begging. Repeat. The same handful of cases, no movement, for a very long time, among people who all understand conditionalization.

I have a hypothesis, which I offer as a hypothesis. My thought is that Reflection is best read not as a diachronic norm at all, but as an information-theoretic claim in disguise: it says that your later credence is a sufficient statistic for your total evidence — that between now and then you lose nothing and add nothing except truth-relevant signal. On that reading every standard counterexample is the same counterexample wearing a different costume. Forgetting deletes information the earlier self had. Drugging or misleading evidence adds noise that is not a signal. A pure self-locating change adds a coordinate the agent cannot interpret. Permissivism means the compression from evidence to credence is not even a function. And then the real disagreement is not about diachronic rationality but about whether rationality ever permits an update that is lossy or noisy in those ways — with defenders quietly building the no-loss assumption into the statement of the principle and critics quietly producing cases where rationality plainly permits loss. If that is right, much of the apparent disagreement is verbal, about what "your later credence" ranges over — idealized or actual, as seen from the agent's information or the bookie's — and that verbal part is doing most of the work.
