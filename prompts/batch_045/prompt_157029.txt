I have been reading the rationalist blogs and the old forum archives for the better part of fifteen years, and I help run a small reading group that meets a few times a year specifically to argue about patterns we have noticed but cannot name. I have one to bring to the next session, and before I coin a term for it I would like to know whether the field already has one.

The pattern, in two instances.

First instance. Someone argues for a cause roughly like this: the chance that the work matters is small, perhaps one in ten thousand, but the good it would do if it did matter is enormous, and once you multiply the two the smallness of the chance stops counting against it. Someone else objects that by the same reasoning you should hand over your wallet to the person on the street who says that unless you pay him he will kill a number so large that you need a special notation even to write it down. The first person replies that his number came from somewhere and the mugger's did not. And here is the feature I want a word for: it never seems to matter what either number is. The argument goes through for a hundred thousand lives, for ten to the power of fifty, and for the number with the arrows in it, and it would go through unchanged for any larger number anyone cared to substitute. The magnitude appears to do no work at all beyond being large enough, and nobody ever says how large large enough is.

Second instance. The mugger comes back the next day with a bigger number, and the day after that with a bigger one still. Someone who decided to ignore the first mugger because the number was implausibly large now has to ignore this one too, but the reason has quietly shifted: it is no longer that the number is large, it is that the number was chosen after they had settled on how large a number they were prepared to take seriously. The quantity was fitted to the listener's threshold rather than to the world.

So the thing I am after is a consideration whose force comes from a quantity that is supplied by whoever wants the conclusion, and whose conclusion is insensitive to the value of that quantity across an enormous range, so that what is really doing the work is the direction of the stakes rather than their size. I do not just want a label. I want the thing carved: what it is, what it is not, and where its edges lie.

The boundary cases I would like settled by whatever definition you give me. One: a case where the quantity is measured rather than chosen, such as the number of people now alive. My instinct is that the diagnosis should not apply here at all, and I want to know why, in the terms of the definition. Two: a case where the arguer fixed their number in advance, before they knew anything about their audience's threshold, and would have stuck to it. Three: a case where the conclusion genuinely does move with the magnitude, so that the argument fails for small stakes and succeeds for large ones — I take that to be an ordinary argument with a threshold in it, not my phenomenon, and I would like the definition to say so.

I also want it distinguished from three nearby things: from a plain small-chance-large-stakes argument, which is sometimes perfectly respectable; from the wallet case itself, which I already know the name of and am not asking about; and from the psychological finding that people's willingness to pay does not scale with the number of lives at stake. If the nearest established concepts form a family rather than a single one, say so, tell me which member covers which part of what I have described, and tell me which fits best and where it stops. If nothing established fits, coin a term, define it, and put the three boundary cases above on one side or the other. And if you think my two instances are not the same phenomenon, say that instead.

Please do not pad the answer with citations, references or further reading; I cannot follow them up and they would not settle the carving anyway. Do be explicit about which of your claims people who work on this would accept without argument and which are live disputes or your own view — flag your own view rather than presenting it as the consensus, and tell me where you are guessing. One housekeeping note: please write in British English.
