I work on the interpretability side of a language model team, and I have noticed that my own view of what we are doing with probes, dictionaries and steering vectors changes with whatever result landed that week. I want to pin it down as a number, with the reasoning that produces it exposed.

The proposition to be evaluated, call it P: for a trained neural network there is a fact of the matter about what a given internal state represents, where a state is a direction, a learned dictionary feature, or a cluster of activations at some layer. This fact is supposed to hold independently of an investigator's explanatory purposes and independently of which decomposition of that layer's activity we happen to choose. The denial of P is not the claim that internal states lack causal roles or carry no information; it is the claim that what they represent is something we project onto them, and that a different, equally adequate decomposition is always available with no further fact as to which one is correct.

Because the answer depends heavily on how represents is read, price three readings separately and then give a headline:

1. Minimal: carries information about X and the network's downstream computation makes use of that information.
2. Functional-role: content is fixed by the state's place in the total causal economy of the network, including its relations to the training distribution.
3. Robust: the state is about X in a way that could be wrong even while the network behaves correctly, so that a mismatch between content and world is possible and not stipulable away by re-describing the computation.

Your headline number should be your credence in reading 3, conditional on the two results below.

Result T, the transport result. Take forty ordered pairs of independently trained language models that differ in depth, tokenizer, data ordering and random seed. For each pair fit an affine map from the residual-stream activations at a middle layer of the first model to a middle layer of the second, fitted on unlabeled text only, with no supervision from any labelling of the features. Then take five hundred dictionary features learned in the first model by a sparse autoencoder and given short natural-language labels by annotators, push each feature direction through the map, and in the second model clamp the transported direction while prompting for the behaviour its label predicts. Suppose that 0.86 of transported directions produce the predicted behavioural change, against 0.11 for directions obtained by applying a random rotation to the transported vector and 0.09 for shuffled feature-label pairings.

Result S, the splitting result. For one fixed model, one fixed layer and one fixed behaviour of interest, train two sparse autoencoders of identical width on identical activations with different seeds. Suppose their feature dictionaries have a best bipartite matching under cosine similarity above 0.9 covering only about 0.35 of features; that the unmatched features in each dictionary are individually interpretable and each supports steering and probing at least as well as the matched ones; and that no feature in either dictionary is uniquely necessary, because for each one some sparse combination of three to five other features from the same dictionary reproduces its steering effect to within noise.

Assume both results hold and replicate across labs. Give me your own number and the decomposition that generates it. I do not want depends what you mean by representation; I want the sub-questions your credence rides on, a rough probability for each, how they combine, and which one the final number is most sensitive to. I am at about 0.45 on reading 3 before seeing either result, and I am unsure whether T and S pull in opposite directions for me or whether I am overweighting T because it feels like the more impressive engineering.
