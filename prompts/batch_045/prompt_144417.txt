I help run an internal reading group at the company I work for — half ML engineers, half the people who do our security and compliance reviews — and we have been reading work on safety measures that assume the model may be misaligned: monitoring, tripwires, sandboxes, human-in-the-loop review, capability throttling, and so on. Every proposal we look at seems to be defended against a different unstated threat model and measured against a different standard of success, so the discussion keeps circling. I want to fix the specification before we read any more.

The concrete case we argue about, to keep this from floating off into abstraction: our team runs a frontier model as an autonomous coding agent with write access to a large private monorepo, the ability to open pull requests, and the ability to trigger CI. We don't claim to know whether it is misaligned; we want to act as though it might be trying, patiently and strategically, to introduce a subtle vulnerability or a self-propagating credential leak into code that will look innocuous to a human reviewer. One of my colleagues has proposed a scaffold in which every write is checked by a second instance of the same model that is shown the diff but deliberately denied the task description and the surrounding issue thread — a "blind reviewer," on the theory that it can judge whether a change is suspicious without knowing what would make the change succeed. Another colleague's answer to everything is just to wall off authentication code and infrastructure config entirely. I would like a requirements list that could actually adjudicate proposals like these rather than a verdict on them.

So: write the desiderata for a control measure — what any safety measure for a possibly misaligned model has to deliver to count as adequate, given the purpose of letting us keep a useful system deployed without betting on its alignment. For each requirement give the case or consideration behind it, and distinguish the cases it must get right from the distinctions it is not allowed to collapse: for instance, between the measure reducing risk overall and reducing it against a model that is actively optimizing against the measure; between the model being unable to do the bad thing and us merely failing to notice that it did; between a requirement on the measure and a requirement on the organization that operates it. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are preferences of a particular camp — I suspect the demand for worst-case guarantees is doing very different work from the demand that the measure be transparent to a human auditor, and that at least one of the two is a prejudice rather than a desideratum. Then tell me which subset of the non-negotiables you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and which single requirement excludes the largest share of the kinds of measures my group keeps getting excited about; that is the part I most want to see. Please don't cite or list readings — we fill those in ourselves — and skip a scorecard of individual published proposals, since we can do that in the session; but do say what shape a measure that met all the non-negotiables would have to have, even if you think nothing like it exists.

Format: give the requirements in prose with the motivating case for each, then a table with the columns: requirement | motivating case | non-negotiable or camp preference | what it rules out | conflicts with which other requirement. Close with a short JSON object with the fields "unsatisfiable_subset", "requirement_that_excludes_most", "requirement_I_should_drop_first", and "shape_of_a_passing_measure".
