Imagine your advising a small working group -- four metaphysicians and one sociologist of sciece -- that has one year to write a framework paper on the social construction of kinds, focused on race, gender, and money. They can't run new empirical studies, so the deliverable has to be conceptual: a defended agenda, not a lit review or reading list, and I don't want any bibliographies or citations. Here's a rough distinction to work with: call a kind anchoring-constructed when it exists because our practices pick out or stabilize some underlying pattern, and looping-constructed when classifying things under the kind changes the things themselves or the people classified, so the kind and the classifications co-evolve. Some candidate lines: whether construction is really a claim about causal dependence on our practices, or about this looping instability, or about concepts fixing reference; whether money is a cleaner case than race or gender; and whether one unified account can cover all three. Which two or three questions should the group prioritize, and in what order? Defend the ranking: say which criteria you're using (how upstream a question is, how tractable it is with armchair tools, how neglected it is, and whether its answer would change what they say downstream), what would move a question up or down over the next year, and which candidate you'd tell them to drop because answering it wouldn't matter much. Keep it reasonably casual.
