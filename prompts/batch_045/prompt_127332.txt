Hey — I'm an RL researcher, mostly offline RL and receding-horizon control for assistive robots. My lab's house framework is something we call horizon splicing: instead of one policy trained against a single discount factor, we keep a bank of sub-policies each trained at a different effective horizon (a few steps, a few minutes, a day) plus a scheduler that mixes them at execution time. Commitment devices in our system are just hard constraints on the scheduler's action space, and what looks like temptation is usually distributional shift between the states a short-horizon sub-policy saw in training and the ones it sees at deployment.

I keep hearing philosophers talk about weakness of will and I want to know whether there's anything in it for me. Take the main positions — the Davidson-style puzzle where you act against your own all-things-considered judgment and that looks like it shouldn't be possible, the hyperbolic discounting / preference reversal story where the ranking of two options flips as the smaller-sooner reward gets close, and the planning-style view where the real issue is the stability of intentions over time — and restate each as faithfully as you can in my actual vocabulary: policies, discount factors, value functions, TD error, action-space constraints, distributional shift, off-policy evaluation, reward misspecification. I don't want vibes-charity; I want to see where each one cashes out.

Then give me the residue report: which translate cleanly, which only fit if you smuggle in something my setup doesn't have (a single unified value function, a persisting self, a privileged 'long-run' horizon), and which can't be said at all. Three things I care about especially.
