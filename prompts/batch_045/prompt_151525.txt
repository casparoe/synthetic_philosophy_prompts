For a column I'm working on, I want to run the "flatten the curve" chart of March 2020 past Otto Neurath's theory of picture languages, and see which of the two survives the collision. Let me set out what I take his commitments to be, and do correct me where I have him wrong: that a quantity must be shown by repeating identical pictograms rather than enlarging them, so that the figure can always be counted; that a chart be readable without training by a schoolchild or a factory worker; that a picture is never a neutral transcription but a "transformation", an edited simplification whose omissions are as deliberate as its inclusions, the point being captured by his museum's catch-phrase, "To remember simplified pictures is better than to forget accurate figures"; that "words divide, pictures unite"; and that for all this a picture language remains only a helping language, unable on its own to say no, to say if, or to say is. The chart itself needs little description: two epidemic curves on one axis, a tall peak and a low broad hump, crossed by a single horizontal line for hospital capacity, captioned with an instruction to flatten it. It has no countable units and, in most versions, no scale at all; it depicts not a quantity but a counterfactual and a policy; and it was redrawn in hundreds of jurisdictions, each with its own capacity line, succeeding as visual education in a way nothing else that year did. So, on Neurath's own criteria, is this chart a failure, a violation, or the vindication he was waiting for? Where precisely does it break his rules, and does each break actually cost him something, or is some rule of his merely parochial — a rule for stocks and censuses that has nothing to say about rates, thresholds and feedback? And in the other direction: does the episode confirm his claim that pictures cannot assert conditionals or imperatives, because the caption and the campaign around it were doing that work, or does it refute it, because millions read an obligation straight off a curve? I also want to know whether his anti-foundationalism — the boat rebuilt plank by plank at sea, the refusal of any privileged protocol language, the insistence that every representation is an edited one — is what really explains the chart's success, which would leave the pictogram rules as decoration. Keep the mapping strict, tell me where his theory is silent rather than pressing it to speak, and give me a verdict on whether the case is a confirmation, an anomaly, or something his picture language was never built to handle. Please write in British English, and as continuous prose — no headings, bullet points, bold text or tables, but as you would write an email.
