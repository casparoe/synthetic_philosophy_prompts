I write about machine learning for a general readership and I am fluent in the vocabulary practitioners actually use: training and held-out loss, distribution shift, prompt sensitivity, fine-tuning and RLHF, reward hacking, calibration, linear probes and activation patching, benchmark scores and the ways they get gamed. I am not fluent in philosophy of mind.

I keep having to tell readers whether it is correct to say that a language model believes something — that it believes the capital of France is Paris, or that it has a false belief about a customer's account balance — and I gather that the philosophical literature on the intentional stance contains an established version of exactly this argument: whether treating a system as a believer is merely a predictive instrument or a discovery about what is really going on inside it. Please restate both sides in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report: what translates cleanly into talk of held-out generalization, causal intervention and probe faithfulness; what translates only after being bent; and what cannot be expressed at all.

Two things I want you to handle along the way. First, I seem to have a clean external anchor for the desire side of the attribution — the system's objective function — and nothing comparable for the belief side, and I would like to know whether that asymmetry is an artefact of my vocabulary or a real feature of the dispute. Second, I have been using a criterion of my own in my reporting and cannot tell whether it is an advance or a relabelling: call a belief attribution earned only if the attributed content is recoverable by an intervention that also changes downstream behaviour in the way the attribution predicts, on inputs and tasks that were not used to discover it, and is inert where the attribution says it should be inert. My worry is that this merely redescribes predictive success in causal language, and that a realist and an instrumentalist could both accept it while going on disagreeing somewhere my vocabulary cannot see. My hope is that a distinction my vocabulary draws sharply — between predicting a held-out result in advance and fitting an explanation to behaviour after the fact — does work the philosophers' dispute needs and does not have.

Give me the translation, the residue, and a verdict on the criterion; and where the residue is real, say whether it indicts my vocabulary or the philosophers' question.
