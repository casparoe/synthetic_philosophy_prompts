My friend Mo did a PhD in semantics before she bailed for a data job, and for the past few weeks she's let me read her little scenarios over text and tell her which "if" sentences I think work in them — except she's the one giving the verdicts, and I've definately started writing them down because I want to figure out what rule she's actually running. I don't know any of the technical vocabulary for this stuff, so sorry if I describe it clumsily. The thing I keep noticing is that she treats the plain "if X did/didn't, then Y" sentences and the "if X had/hadn't, then Y would have" ones as different animals, and I can't tell exactly where the difference bites.

Here's the track record so far.

1. Camp stove, never used, dry night, igniter fine.
- "If I had pressed the igniter, it would have lit." True.
- "If I had pressed the igniter and the fuel line had been blocked, it wouldn't have lit." Also true.
- "If I had pressed it, the line had been blocked, and I had primed it first, it would have lit." Also true.

2. Same stove: "If I had pressed the igniter, it might not have lit." True. When I pointed out that this seems to clash with the first sentence in 1, she said it doesn't, because "would have" means what happens in the normal run of things, not in literally every possibility you can cook up.

3. The lab freezer got unplugged overnight. Only Priya and Sam had keycard access that night, and the temperature log shows a steady climb from 2am, so we know for certain somebody unplugged it — just not who.
- "If Priya didn't unplug it, Sam did." Fine, she says — true, even.
- "If Priya hadn't unplugged it, Sam would have." No, she says, definitely not. Priya's not doing it wouldn't have made Sam do it.

4. The freezer's temperature crossing a threshold pings the night guard's phone. Automatic, reliable, never fails.
- "If the temperature hadn't crossed the threshold, the guard's phone wouldn't have pinged." True.
- "If the guard's phone pinged, the temperature crossed the threshold." True — and she had no problem at all with this one running backwards, from the later thing to the thing that caused it.

5. The new one. A greenhouse vent: purely mechanical, a wax cylinder that expands and pops the vent open once the air inside hits 30°C. No electricity, no handle, can't be forced open by hand, hasn't failed in twelve years, and the gardener has never once seen the hinge stick — though he admits it could in principle. Right now I can see from the path that the vent is standing open. The temperature logger's battery died yesterday so nobody knows how hot it actually got, but it was sunny and the gardener says it hits 30 on most sunny afternoons.

Two sentences about it:
- "If the vent opened, the temperature hit 30°C."
- "If the vent hadn't opened, the temperature wouldn't have hit 30°C."

My own guess is that she takes the first one but that the second one is a real fork: she either accepts it too, rejects it, or says it doesn't have a proper answer. What I actually want to know is what rule she's running that fits 1 through 4 — I suspect more than one rule fits those, and that they come apart on the second sentence in 5. So: what are the candidate rules, what does each one say about that sentence, which is your best guess for her and why, and what single extra scenario should I text her next week to separate the rules that survive?
