I'm a journalist writing a feature on whether moral foundations theory can say anything about who is right, rather than only about why left and right talk past each other. The analogy I keep running into is the one Haidt's popular book leans on: morality is a palate. The foundations — care/harm, fairness/cheating, loyalty/betrayal, authority/subversion, sanctity/degradation, liberty/oppression — are taste receptors; cultures are cuisines that differ in how much they cook with each; and on the theory's own telling American liberals respond to roughly two of them while conservatives respond to all six, which is why each side finds the other morally tasteless.

In my reporting the analogy is being made to do opposite jobs. One philosopher I interviewed says it settles the question: if morality is taste, there is no fact of the matter, so the psychology is purely descriptive, the question of who is right is idle, and toleration is the only honest upshot. A political psychologist I interviewed says the reverse: a palate that registers six dimensions simply picks up more of the moral environment than one that registers two, so the wider palate is the more reliable detector.

Sharpen it with two stipulations (hypotheticals, not real studies — assume them for the sake of argument). First: a population that shows no response whatsoever to the liberty/oppression foundation, the way some people cannot taste certain bitter compounds. Second: a small group of moral supertasters who register sanctity violations with the vividness of a spoiled smell, who stop condemning those acts when given a compound that dulls the response, and who insist throughout that the acts really are polluting.

Please audit the analogy: what maps onto what, which disanalogies matter for the two uses above and which don't, what the source domain quietly imports into the target, and whether either conclusion survives. Structure your reply in these sections, with roughly these lengths: Mapping (150 words), Where it holds (150), Where it breaks (250), What the analogy smuggles in (150), A better analogy, if there is one (100), Verdict on the two conclusions (150). Reason from the structure of the theory and the analogy; no citations, references, or reading lists. Flag where your verdict depends on my two stipulations and where it doesn't.
