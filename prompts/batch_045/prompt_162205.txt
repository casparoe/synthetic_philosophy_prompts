I've got a 45-minute slot in the onboarding week for new policy analysts at our think tank — mostly lawyers and a couple of data people, nobody with grad econ, but they've all skimmed the cost-benefit guidance and can use the words Pareto improvement and Kaldor-Hicks, and one of them will have opinions about compesation being hypothetical. I want to teach them safe Pareto improvements. My gloss is: you bundle a policy change with transfers in the numeraire (money) big enough to make whoever loses whole, and the package is then a Pareto improvement even though you never found out how much anyone actually valued the thing — so it's safe against your own ignorance of their preferences, not safe in the ordinary risk sense.

Don't explain it to me; plan how I should explain it. What has to be in place before the punchline lands, and in what order: the Pareto-improvement vs potential-compensation distinction, money as the numeraire with everyone's marginal utility of it normalized to one, the small-changes caveat that makes that normalization OK, then the bundling move itself. Does the small-changes caveat belong up front as a scope marker or at the end as a confession, and why? Which single example should carry the whole thing, and what does it need to have — a numeraire, a local public good with unknown heterogeneous valuations, small stakes, losers you can identify and pay — and what must it lack (I keep reaching for a bike lane and then realizing the financing is where it snags)? Then the misconceptions, in the order they'll actually arise and where I head each off: that this is just cost-benefit analysis with extra steps, that it needs interpersonal utility comparisons, that safe means robust to uncertainty about outcomes, that then every project with positive net benefits is a Pareto improvement so the concept is empty, and that it's bribery. Which of those is worth letting them sit with for a minute before I kill it. Is my opening gloss a good thing to lead with or does it bake in a mistake I'll spend the rest of the hour undoing? One question at the end that shows me they got it rather than just nodding — I was going to hand them a case where total willingness to pay exceeds cost but the move fails and ask why. And what should I flatly refuse to get into at this level and say so out loud.
