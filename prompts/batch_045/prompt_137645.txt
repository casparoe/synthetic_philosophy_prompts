Me and my mate Sam had a proper long argument last night, fuelled by a couple of pints, about whether the weather proves the universe isn't deterministic. His position was basically: the forecasters themselves admit that tiny differences in the starting conditions — the butterfly thing, that Lorenz bloke re-running his model with rounded-off numbers and getting a completely different forecast — so the future isn't fixed and determinism is dead. My position was that sensitive dependence is a claim about our ability to predict, not about whether the laws settle a unique future, and that he'd conflated the two. Neither of us budged, and then we started arguing about something neither of us could actually explain, which is the real reason I'm writing.

What puzzles me is the bundle of things forecasting institutions actually do:

1. They run the same model dozens of times over with the starting conditions nudged slightly differently each time, then publish something like a 30% chance of rain for Thursday.
2. They'll happily do a day-by-day forecast for the next few days, but as far as I can tell they more or less give up on specific forecasts after somewhere around a week or a fortnight; past that it's vague longer-range stuff about whether a month will be wetter or drier than average.
3. At the same time they publish climate projections out to the end of the century and seem fairly confident about them — confident about averages and ranges, while insisting they can't tell you anything about the weather on a particular day in 2070.
4. And after some specific flood or heatwave, scientists go on the news and say both that you can't say climate change caused that particular storm, and that it made storms of that kind several times more likely.
5. Insurers, meanwhile, just price flood risk off historical frequencies, as though the frequency were a stable quantity even though every individual flood is chaotic.

So: what does that way of doing things presuppose? I don't mean what individual scientists say in interviews — I mean what the practice itself only makes sense under. Is the probability in "30% chance of rain" a measure of our ignorance or a real feature of the world, and does the practice secretly require one of those answers? Does running an ensemble and averaging over it commit you to there being something real — the distribution, the attractor, the envelope — that's fixed even when the individual path isn't? Sam and I invented a name for the distinction: "trajectory determinism" versus "envelope determinism", i.e. whether the laws pin down the exact path or only the shape of the range it wanders around inside. Is that a distinction anyone actually makes, does it do any work, or is it just two blokes being drunk? And are the five practices even consistent with each other — does the way they talk about a single storm commit them to a different view of causation from the one they commit to when they publish a hundred-year projection?

Mostly I want to know whether any of this requires determinism to be false, requires it to be true, or is philosophically underdetermined — several quite different theories of chance and causation licensing exactly the same forecasting practice. And if you swapped in a rival view (genuine objective chance in nature because of quantum stuff, versus everything fixed and probability only ever being ignorance), what would concretely have to change in what forecasters do from day to day? I honeslty can't tell whether any of it would definately change anything.

After the prose bit, give me a table summarising it, one row per practice (the five above, or split them up more if that's the honest way to do it), with these columns:

- practice: the concrete thing, in a phrase
- commitment: the single philosophical claim it seems to presuppose, one sentence, plain language
- status: which of "strictly presupposed", "compatible but not required", or "just habit" it is
- rival: a different theory that would license the very same practice
- if_dropped: what would concretely have to change in the practice if that commitment were abandoned
