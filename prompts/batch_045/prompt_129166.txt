I teach philosophy (mostly language and logic) and over the summer I've been messing around with a simulation of Hofstadter's Typogenetics from Gödel, Escher, Bach, largely as a way to procrastinate. Reminder of the setup in case you dont remember it: strands are strings of four bases; a strand gets read three bases at a time, each triple calls up one of a fixed set of enzymes (copy, cut, switch, delete, insert, move), the enzymes' behaviour is given by a lookup table, they chew on strands and emit new strands, and the fun is that some strands, run through the system, bring about a copy of themselves. The point Hofstadter is after is the analogy with DNA and with Gödel numbering: a system that can encode descriptions of its own operations.

Here's what I keep running into, and I have no word for it. In my version I let the enzyme lookup table drift, much more slowly than the strands, and I also let a strand carry a region that specifies a table rather than an enzyme sequence. Whenever I find a strand that genuinely self-replicates, I notice I can shove the work around. I can shrink the strand to almost nothing by hard-coding most of the copying into the table — at the limit the table does the copying and the strand contributes nearly nothing. Or I can make the table maximally dumb and generic and then the strand has to spell out every step. Both setups yield the same daughter strand, and there seems to be no fact of the matter about how much of the specification of the offspring lives in the strand and how much in the machinery.

A second thing, which may or may not be the same thing: numerically identical strands, contributing exactly the same causal work in the same order, are faithful self-replicators under one table and produce junk under another. So self-replication looks like a property of the pair, not of the strand.

My instinct was that the total amount of specification is conserved and just gets redistributed. But I'm not sure there's anything to conserve — it feels more like the split is a choice of description, or like asking how much of a theorem is in the axioms and how much in the rules.

Is there an established concept here? Everything I've poked at is near it and not it: the replicator/vehicle literature, von Neumann's bit about the description being used twice (once executed, once copied), and something about Kolmogorov complexity being machine-relative — though that last one looks like it says the opposite of what I'm seeing, because I thought there was a theorem that the complexity comes out the same up to a constant. What I actually want is to know whether I've got one phenomenon or two or three, what the nearest established notions are and precisely where each stops being apt, and if there's no name, a coined name with a definition and the boundary cases pinned down. Please don't just hand me a label.
