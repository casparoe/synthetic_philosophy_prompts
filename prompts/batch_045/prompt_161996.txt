I'm finishing a PhD in philosophy, working at the metaphysics / philosophy of physics border, and the seminar paper I'm currently writing concerns how much Thomson's lamp argument actually establishes. As I read the literature, it has settled into two diagnoses: either the task-description is logically inconsistent, so no supertask of that shape can be performed at all; or the description is consistent but simply incomplete at the limit instant — it fixes the state at every time before the limit and nothing at the limit — so the argument shows only that a certain way of telling the story fails to settle the outcome.

I want to pry those apart by constructing a supertask in which the limit state is *determined* — determined by the physics of the set-up, not by a stipulation — but is not readable off the sequence of states that accumulates at the limit instant. That combination is what I can't find built anywhere: the underdetermination reply requires that the story leave the limit state open, and Thomson's argument requires that the limit state be settled by the alternation pattern. I want a case where both of those fail at once, so that the argument can be shown to rest on a premise it never states.

Specifications for the scenario:

1. Ordinary mechanics. Newtonian point mechanics or rigid-body mechanics with gravity, macroscopic objects, finite total mass and finite total energy, everything confined to a finite spatial region, and all velocities bounded by a finite bound. No exotic spacetimes, no accelerating or infinite-time computers, no singularities, no agents performing infinitely many deliberate acts. Standard idealizations (perfectly rigid walls, instantaneous collisions with a coefficient of restitution, ideal switches or relays) are fine; anything stronger is not.

2. Supertask structure. There is a binary indicator — a lamp, a gate, a relay — whose state changes infinitely many times within a finite interval, with switching times accumulating at the limit instant from below: no last switching before it, no first switching after it.

3. The switching schedule must be *generated* by the system's own evolution from its initial conditions, force law and coupling rule, not imposed by an external timetable or by a sequence of free actions. This is what should make the underdetermination reply unavailable.

4. The coupling between the indicator and the mechanical system must be local in time: the indicator's state at a time depends only on the mechanical state at that time, not on the elapsed history, not on the time variable directly, and not on how many switchings have occurred so far.

5. The pattern of indicator states must have no limit as the switching times accumulate — otherwise your case is trivial, since the answer would just be the limit of the pattern, and Thomson's argument would never have got a grip.

6. Determinacy. The indicator's state at the limit instant must nonetheless be uniquely determined, and you must derive it from the dynamics together with the coupling rule and the pre-limit evolution, and show that the alternative state is inconsistent with the laws rather than merely unattractive.

7. A check I care about: confirm that the time-reverse of your scenario is not also a solution of the same laws. If it is, your set-up is indeterministic at the limit instant and the determinacy claim in (6) collapses into a bare choice between two lawful continuations. Tell me which way your case goes.

After the scenario (one paragraph of prose, under 150 words, then the demonstration):

- Run both halves of Thomson's argument on it — the "it cannot be on" half and the "it cannot be off" half — and tell me where each breaks, which premise each was silently relying on, and why they are not independent moves.
- State which state the indicator is in at the limit instant and derive it.
- Say explicitly why the underdetermination reply is not available here.
- Name the single feature of the set-up that is doing the work, and remove it: describe what the case degenerates into and which diagnosis then becomes correct.
- Give the strongest objection a defender of Thomson would make — I expect something like: your verdict is smuggled in by the coupling rule, or your case is a convergent physical process rather than a supertask in the relevant sense, since nothing is *done* infinitely many times — and say whether it lands.

What counts as cheating for my purposes: stipulating the apparatus is fine (initial conditions, coefficients, coupling rule); stipulating the outcome is not. If the best case you can build needs a separate clause fixing the indicator's state at the limit instant, say so plainly and explain why no clause-free version works — for the paper, that failure itself would be a usable result.
