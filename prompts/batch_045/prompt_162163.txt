Write a villanelle about the panopticon as it appears in Foucault's Discipline and Punish, in which the form does the work that exposition usually does badly. Between them the two refrains should carry the two halves of the mechanism: the asymmetry of visibility (the inmate is exposed to a gaze he can never verify, and the uncertainty is what does the work) and the internalization that lets the apparatus run with no one in the tower. In the opening stanza both lines should read as describing an external watcher; by the final stanza the same two lines should read as describing the inmate watching himself. The poem must never announce that this shift has happened, and a reader should not be able to point to the stanza in which it does — the claim about the economy of disciplinary power is precisely that there is no such moment, and that power grows more efficient as its exercise becomes less visible to those it addresses.

Set the poem anywhere but a prison: an open-plan office, a school, a hospital ward, a factory floor. What matters for Foucault is that the panoptic arrangement is a generalizable diagram rather than a building type, so the setting should show the schema travelling. Please also steer clear of the two reflexes this material usually triggers: the surveillance-society complaint about cameras and platforms, and the slogan that power is everywhere. Power here is exercised rather than possessed, productive rather than merely prohibitive, and it operates through a timetable and a norm rather than through force.

Close with three or four sentences of prose: which reading of the text the poem commits you to, where the poem strains or flattens that reading, what would make you write it differently, and one consideration that would change your mind about whether the villanelle is the right form for this at all.
