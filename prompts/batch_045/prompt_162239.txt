I'm a backend dev with no philosophy background, and me and two of the guys from my team have been stuck in the same arguement about the dark forest hypothesis for about a month now. I don't want you to tell me who's right, I want to know why it isn't going anywhere.

Roughly where we're at. One of them says the Great Silence is a genuine puzzle and the dark forest story is the best explanation on offer: listening is cheap, a pre-emptive strike is cheaper than indefinite coexistence, there's no cheap way to credibly signal that you're harmless across interstellar distances, and so the equilibrium is shut up and shoot anything that makes noise. The other one says the silence is barely a datum at all — we've sampled a laughable fraction of the search space for a laughable amount of time, and everyone is hiding is an untestable story about alien motivations dressed up as game theory.

The bit that actually stalls us, and where I want your judgement: I tried to push the first guy into making a real prediction, and he came up with something I think is his own invention. Hiding means staying under the noise floor in every band you radiate in, but a strike is by definition a huge, brief release of energy — you can't shoot without lighting yourself up. So the dark forest needs an asymmetry (stealthy ears, loud guns), and whether physics actually gives you that asymmetry is a real question rather than a story. And separately: any civilisation whose energy use compounds at even a percent or two a year ends up unable to hide its waste heat without dismantling its own infrastructure, so the dark forest shouldn't predict permanent silence — it predicts a danger window, which means the thing to look for isn't beacons but short transients, civilisations getting hit right around the point where they become findable.

My mate's reply is that this is exactly the move that makes the whole thing unfalsifiable: whatever the sky turns out to look like, you can retcon the story to fit it, so it's not a theory, it's a genre. He's not obviously wrong, and the first guy isn't either.

My own guess, which I'm offering as a guess: we're not disagreeing about aliens at all, we're disagreeing about what an explanation has to do here — whether a good explanation has to make the silence robust across a wide range of parameters, or whether one contingent fact that happens to be true is enough — plus a second disagreement about whether predicting what we would see if we looked harder counts as content when nothing has been seen yet.

Is that right, or is there a real empirical or formal question hiding in there doing the work? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of paradox solutions. And given whatever you think is going on, is there anything that would actually move this — a particular kind of observation, a result about the game, a distinction we're missing — or should we just expect to be stuck? British English please.
