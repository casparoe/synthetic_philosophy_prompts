I have spent three years on a novel whose protagonist, Anton Brandt, is a thirty-nine-year-old former trade-union librarian from Bremen, a Social Democrat until 1933, conscripted in 1942 into a German reserve police battalion in occupied Poland. The book's spine is a short sequence. At his first massacre he shoots. Twice in the weeks that follow he asks to be excused from the firing detail, and twice he is excused without punishment. Then he stops asking. By autumn he is one of the men the others can be relied upon. He never initiates anything; he never refuses again. Investigated in the 1960s, he says of himself that he had not been the man who wanted it and that he had become the man who did it.

At dinner after a reading, two guests argued about what a book of this shape actually claims about men like him, and I could not settle it. Tobias is a novelist; Ines works on the period. Their exchange, as nearly as I can reconstruct it:

(1) Tobias: the left-wing backstory is decoration, and the book is secretly on Goldhagen's side. Willingness is not produced by the situation; it is revealed by it. If Anton can square the killings with himself inside a few weeks, it is because he already carried the template — some settled conviction that Jews were a mortal threat to the nation. Converts are converts. The Social Democrat of 1933 is what makes the story saleable, not what makes it true. Browning's ordinary men are ordinary only in the sense that ordinary Germans already carried the thing.

(2) Ines: first, the record — these reserve units were not selected for fervour, but made up of middling and often older men, many of them untouched by the party before the war; their commander announced that anyone who did not feel up to it could step out; a dozen or so did; none was punished. If willingness were already present and uniform, stepping out would be inexplicable. Second, and more importantly, willing in Goldhagen's sense is a disposition with content: a specific belief about Jews. What Anton acquires is only a local willingness — he no longer wants to be the man who refuses again, the man the others have to cover for. That has no ideological content whatever. Same conduct, different inner history, and the difference matters.

(3) Tobias: then give me the mechanism, because you owe one. Either the situation supplies incentives — standing, ease, not being thought weak — in which case Anton acts for reasons, and reasons are his reasons, which just is willingness under a flattering description. Or the situation degrades his capacities: exhaustion, drink, terror, the diffusion of a deed among many hands — and then you are describing something much nearer to excuse than to agency. The vocabulary gives the game away: if the situation made him willing, either he wanted to, in which case Goldhagen's conclusion stands though reached by another road, or he did not act at all. And there is the exemption test. The unit itself told him he could step out. He had the alternative, knew he had it, and stopped taking it. What more could engagement of the will require?

(4) Ines: on the test — stepping out in front of your comrades marks you as the man who thinks he is better than the others, and it costs you your place in the only group that is holding you intact. He could have is not the same as he could have and remained who he was among these men. An alternative can be available without being live, and that is not the same as anyone's capacity being impaired. On the dilemma — it is false. A situation can reorganise what is salient, permissible and normal, what a man can so much as see as an option, while leaving his agency entirely intact. He reasons; he simply reasons inside a world the situation has redrawn. And your reading is unfalsifiable: no conduct whatever distinguishes a produced willingness with content from a produced willingness without it, so your interpretation survives everything and is supported by nothing.

(5) Tobias: I will grant the redrawn world. But then watch where your own account drifts. By the tenth action the pressure is internal: consistency, the refusal to repudiate the self who has already done this, the small standing he now has in the group. The causal centre of gravity migrates from the situation into the man as the career goes on. A situational story may explain the first massacre; something much closer to a character story explains the fortieth. So your account hands him back his willingness — only later, and by instalments. Separately: your version is the convenient one. It lets Anton be a murderer and a casualty at once, and lets a reader feel pity and horror in the same breath. That is a novelistic effect, which is why a novelist would want it, and calling it an explanation does not make it one.

(6) Ines: the last point is rhetoric, and the first concedes what I need. If the willingness is acquired by the tenth massacre, then it was produced and not revealed, and the fact that he later owns it — owns it fully — does not show that it was there at the beginning. I pay the price your account wants me to pay: once acquired it is his, and nothing about its origin reduces what he owes.

Please adjudicate the exchange move by move rather than declaring a winner. Rate each of the six moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right; give a sentence or two of justification for each rating; and for each move name the single premise doing the most work and say whether it is a conceptual premise or an empirical one about the historical record. Then tell me what, if anything, both of them are missing — the consideration that reframes the disagreement rather than awarding it to one side. I should add that I do not want to be sent to the literature; I have read the obvious books, and what I need to know is which of these moves survives. A revision is riding on it: whether the second request to be excused stays in the book.
