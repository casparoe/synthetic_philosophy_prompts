Me and three friends have been playing a home-brewed version of Nomic — the self-amending rules game — over email for about two years, and we've run into an argument that has totally stalled, to the point where I'm not even sure what kind of argument it is anymore. Our variant starts from a ruleset where any rule change needs a unanimous vote, and it also contains what we call the mirror rule: a change counts as valid only if the resulting ruleset, once the change has taken effect, would itself permit that change. We added the mirror rule ages ago to stop anybody from repealing the unanimity requirement by a unanimous vote, and of course that's exactly where things broke. Two of us (call us the bootstrappers) say an amendment that repeals unanimity and replaces it with majority rule is perfectly valid: it was proposed and passed under the old rules, so it was properly enacted, and by the time it's in force the old voting rule is gone, so the resulting ruleset obviously permits it — the mirror rule, on this reading, applies at the moment of enactment and nothing else. The other two (the continuity camp) read the mirror rule as a filter a change has to pass both going in and coming out, in which case that amendment is not merely invalid but impossible, and you'd have to amend the mirror rule first — which you can't, for exactly the same reason — so our system has an unamendable core that none of us ever deliberately put there. We've also got a live third case now: someone proposed a single amendment that changes the mirror rule and the voting rule simultaneously, plus our tie-breaker is a coin flip, which nobody trusts, so the whole thing has gone around in circles for months with everyone accusing everyone else of just defining "valid" to suit themselves.

My working hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis, is that this isn't a dispute about logic or about the wording at all: I think the two camps disagree about what a rule system is — whether it's a persisting normative order that can somehow authorize its own transformation, or just a sequence of discrete rule-sets each of which has to be legitimate on its own feet, so that "the rules" at any given time is a fresh object that owes nothing to its predecessor. If that's right we're answering two different questions under one word and no amount of squinting at rule text will settle it. But I'm genuinely not confident, and there are other candidate diagnoses floating around: maybe it's purely verbal, a fight about the scope of "valid"; maybe there's a hidden empirical question about what actually makes our little group *treat* a change as binding (we did, after all, play on for three weeks under majority rule before anyone noticed); or maybe our ruleset is simply under-specified and the honest answer is to amend it, if we can.

So: diagnose our stalemate. Use what each side actually says rather than general theory, be specific about where the two readings diverge, and tell me which of these diagnoses — or something better — is right. Then tell me what would count as progress: a distinction we're missing, a reformulation both camps could accept, a test case we could actually run in the game that both would agree bears on the question, or a reason to expect we'll stay stuck. I don't want a survey of positions, and please don't point me at anything to read or tell me what the literature says about self-amendment — I want the diagnosis in your own words, aimed at our actual mess.
