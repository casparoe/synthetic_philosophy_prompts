I have no academic background and I have been reading about consciousness on my own for a couple of years, driven by one practical question: what should I say about an artificial system that has something like a global workspace, where information is integrated, made available system-wide, and reportable in principle, but where nothing is claimed about how any of it feels? Global workspace theory seems to me to answer that question far too quickly, and the access-versus-phenomenal distinction seems to answer it not at all. So, before I read another theory, I want the specification one has to meet.

Write me the list of requirements an adequate account of the relation between access and phenomenal consciousness must satisfy, given that purpose, with the case or consideration behind each requirement. I want the cases it has to get right, including blindsight-style subjects who discriminate without experience, the partial-report results on iconic memory, dreams, and the difference between a creature's level of consciousness and the contents it is conscious of. I want the distinctions it must not collapse, including consciousness of something versus a state's being conscious, report versus access, and attention versus access. And I want your judgment on which of these are genuine requirements and which are merely what one camp happens to want.

The part I care most about is whether some subset of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied. My own suspicion is the evidential one: if phenomenality is roughly what is left over once you subtract availability, then every method we have for detecting it, including the no-report paradigms, which seem to me to relocate rather than remove the dependence on a report, ends up detecting access instead. If that is right, say so and say which single requirement does the most to exclude the popular views. A brief scorecard at the end is fine, but the criteria are what I am after.
