I have been working through Dennett's two-bitser — the coin-sorting mechanism, built to tell genuine quarters from slugs, whose internal states he invites us to treat as representations of quarters — and I have ended up with an argument that seems to me to prove too much. I cannot locate the bad step.

(1) The physics of a device never determines on its own which of its states represents what. For any state you like and any content you like, some scheme of interpretation makes the assignment come out true: computation, like syntax, is not in the physics.

(2) So whenever a state is about something, the aboutness comes to it from outside — from us, who impose a reading on it. That is all that "derived intentionality" can mean.

(3) The two-bitser is no exception: its states are about quarters only because we read them that way. So it has no original intentionality, and in the sense that matters it does not really represent anything.

(4) But (1) and (2) make no exception for brains. A human neural state is as open to reinterpretation as a two-bitser's, so any content it has is imposed from outside as well.

(5) So no human state has original intentionality either.

(6) Derived intentionality is, by definition, intentionality borrowed from something that has it intrinsically. If nothing has it intrinsically, nothing has it at all.

(7) So nothing is about anything — not the two-bitser's states, not mine, not the sentences of this argument — and the reasoning cancels itself.

I would rather keep (1), which looks to me like plain physics, and (3), which is what I set out to establish. I am much less attached to (6). Where does this go wrong? Name the step, explain why it looked compulsory, and price the alternatives I can see: a teleological account on which biological function fixes content with no interpreter in the picture, a Dennett-style refusal of the original/derived distinction altogether, and any third option you think is live. If the fault is a shift inside a single step rather than in a premise, say exactly where.
