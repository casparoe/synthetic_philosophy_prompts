I want a number, and I want the reasoning behind it.

Consider a legal order I'll call L. Its highest-order rule — call it A — says that any rule of L may be added to, amended, or repealed by a two-thirds vote of the assembly followed by a referendum, and that A itself is among the rules this applies to. In year 1 the assembly and the electorate, following A exactly, enact an amendment E with two parts: (i) Article 12 may not be amended or repealed by any later exercise of A; (ii) any purported exercise of A inconsistent with (i) is not merely wrongful but legally void, and every official of L must treat it as never having occurred — including, if need be, the acts by which the officials who participated in it were appointed. In year 5 a two-thirds majority and a referendum, again following A exactly, purport to repeal Article 12. Nothing turns on the content of Article 12; assume it is an ordinary tax provision.

Question: is the year-5 repeal legally valid within L? I mean "valid" in the sense internal to L — what a court of L, applying L's own ultimate criterion of legal validity, should hold — and not in the sense of morally binding, or of what L's officials would in fact do. Please also say what you would count as this question's being resolved: a decision of L's highest court, the later settled practice of L's officials, the retrospective judgment of a successor legal order, or something else.

My credence that the repeal is invalid is about 0.7; a colleague equally at home in this material puts it at about 0.35.

Give me your number, decomposed. Sub-questions you might use, add, or replace: whether a legal power can be exercised so as to narrow its own future exercise, or whether a grant of power to amend is necessarily the grant it was at the moment of grant; whether the validity of a later amendment is tested against the amendment rule as it stood when that amendment was passed or as it stands when the amendment is challenged; whether there is a stable distinction between procedural ("manner and form") entrenchment and substantive limitation, and whether clause (ii) straddles it; whether L after the repeal is the same legal order as L before it or a successor, and whether that identity question is prior to or downstream of the validity question; and whether a court that upholds E reasons in a circle, given that its own jurisdiction rests on rules E purports to protect.

Response format, in this order:

1. "The number" — one sentence: your credence that the year-5 repeal is legally invalid within L.
2. "What would resolve it" — about 60 words on what counts as an answer.
3. "Decomposition" — a table of five to seven rows: sub-question | your probability | one-line justification.
4. "How they combine" — about 60 words: the arithmetic, and any dependence between sub-questions you had to handle informally.
5. "Where the disagreement lives" — about 80 words: which sub-question most likely separates me at 0.7 from my colleague at 0.35.
6. "What would move me most" — about 60 words: the single consideration that would shift your number furthest, and in which direction.
