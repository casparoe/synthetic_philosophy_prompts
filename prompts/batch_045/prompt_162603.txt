I help catalogue amateur metal-detecting finds for our county heritage record, and I keep arguing with a fellow volunteer — call her Diane — about when a find lets you say something about what people *believed*, rather than only what they did. Her rulings so far:

- Dozens of spearheads deliberately bent and snapped before being put into bogs across the region, none ever fished back out: belief, an offering — you may talk about what they thought.
- A worn, clipped coin from ploughsoil: a loss; says nothing about belief.
- Two hundred graves in one cemetery with the same three objects in the same positions and every body aligned the same way: a shared belief about death.
- A stack of unused iron bars in one fort ditch, no parallel anywhere else, about half of them later dug back up and worked: not belief; somebody was storing hardware.
- Burnt hazelnut shells scattered through the floor make-up of a single house: rubbish; nothing to infer.
- A shrine building with no image in it: absence proves nothing, the statue may have been wood.

The new case: one unused, very finely made bronze axe, deliberately smashed, set point-down in the back-filled posthole beneath a longhouse threshold and sealed under the finished floor, so that once the house was up nobody could ever see it or get it back; nothing like it anywhere nearby for centuries.

Please predict how she rules, set out the two or three rules that fit her whole track record equally well (something like: redundant-cost-and-unrecoverable, pattern-across-many-contexts, placement-in-a-set-apart-place), say what each predicts here and which you'd bet on, and name the one further find that would best separate the rules that survive. Don't tell me what the textbooks say — I want what her own record commits her to. One worry of mine she has never addressed: a deposit sealed where no one could see it and no one could retrieve it isn't addressed to anyone, so calling it an offering may be the wrong story — it may be closer to putting something spoiled or dangerous out of reach. I'd like to know whether her earlier rulings already quietly settle that, or whether it's the case that would finally force her to say which she means.
