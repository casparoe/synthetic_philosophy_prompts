hey — I've got a 90 minute slot in a mixed graduate workshop, around 18 people: mostly CS master's students, plus a handful of public policy and philosophy folks. They all roughly know what an objective function is, but almost none of them have thought about *measurement* as the hard part. Room has a projector, I've got one laptop, index cards and printouts for everyone, no laptops for them. I want a game where they run into specification gaming themselves — and I'd rather they not hear the words "Goodhart" or "specification gaming" until after. No lecture up front, ideally no framing beyond the cover story.

The shape I have in mind, which you should feel free to break if you have something better: a funding-agency cover story. There are 12 binary "program features" printed on cards (things like certified teachers, one-on-one sessions, parent workshops, weekend hours, a van...), each with a cost of 1, 2 or 3 points. Quality is a function I hold secretly. One half of the room is the agency: they get 8 historical programs (feature vectors + measured quality scores, with noise) and have to write a funding formula. Crucially they only get a "reporting budget" of 4 fields — the form the agency actually collects has room for 4 of the 12 features, and they have to pick which 4 and what weights. The other half are service providers: sincere people who want to run good programs and need funding to do it, and they build programs out of the features within a budget. Then I reveal true quality.

What I specifically want baked into the machinery:

- My hidden quality function should have structure a linear form can't capture — at least a couple of complementarity terms (two features that are only good together) and one feature that's actively bad in combination with another. That's the engine: maxing the funded formula pushes you into a corner of the space where the true function is bad.
- Three rounds, with the agency allowed one revision (swap at most 2 of their 4 fields) after each reveal, and providers resubmitting. The number on the formula should keep climbing while true quality plateaus or falls — I want them to *see* the number go up and the thing get worse, using their own formula that they wrote in good faith.
- Measured quality should be noisy, so that after round 1 the agency genuinely can't tell whether the drop is noise or decoupling. I want them to feel that you can't diagnose this from inside the metric's own stream.
- Nobody is the villain. The providers should be instructed to serve kids well and to seek funding in order to do that. The good-faith version is the scary version and I don't want it to feel like a security game.

Please give me: the actual rules and materials; the concrete numbers — the 12 features with costs, my secret quality function written out (with the interaction terms), and the 8 seed data points with their noisy scores; a payoff scheme using points, no money; a minute-by-minute timeline; and the reveal protocol (what gets projected when).

Then the debrief, mapped moment by moment onto the thing they just lived through. Four things I want it to land, in whatever order works: that a proxy is a lossy channel with a carrying capacity and gaming is what happens when you demand more bits of optimization than it carries; that a proxy's rank correlation with the truth collapses *exactly* in the top slice where you're actually selecting, so overall validity numbers are misleading; that noise plus optimization pressure makes the failure invisible from inside; and that the gamers were trying to help.
