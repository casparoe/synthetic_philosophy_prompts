ok so i fell down a rabbithole from some blog post about Aumann's agreement theorem and now I'm trying to write down what I actually think about disagreeing with people, and I have this feeling that some of it doesn't fit together but I can't find the seam. (11th grade, debate club, if that matters.)

Where I've landed, roughly:

(1) The theorem as I understand it: if two people start from the same prior, and after each of them sees their own private evidence it's common knowledge between them what each one now believes, then those beliefs have to be identical — so a disagreement that survives that much openness means one of the inputs broke (someone started somewhere else, someone isn't updating right, someone isn't being straight). I think that's basically right and kind of deep.

(2) Priors are personal. There isn't a rational prior, so you can't criticise someone for where they started out; two perfectly rational people can start in totally different places.

(3) In real life though, smart honest people who've read all the same stuff argue about religion or politics or whether our school's phone ban actually did anything, forever, and nobody moves. I think the honest explanation is just: their priors differ. Nothing has gone wrong.

(4) Priors wash out. If two people keep taking in the same evidence, their predictions converge eventually no matter where they started, as long as neither of them assigned probability 0 to something that actually happens. So really long-running disagreement IS a sign something's broken.

(5) When me and my grandpa start in on politics at dinner, the right move is to drop it. Agreeing to disagree is being mature, not being irrational.

(6) At the same time: finding out that someone I respect as much as I respect myself, who has seen everything I've seen, disagrees with me is definately genuine evidence that I should be less confident. Real evidence, not just a social hint to be polite.

(7) I don't think a bare opinion is evidence. If someone just tells me their conclusion and gives no reasons, I shouldn't move at all.

The case that got me stuck: me and my friend Priya both read the same three studies on phone bans in schools. I think the chance it cuts suspensions is about 25%, she says 70%. We've said the numbers out loud to each other, we both know the other one is smart and honest, and we're both just... sitting there. I want to say both "fine, we disagree, moving on" and "her disagreeing should move me," and I want to say both "our priors just differ, that's allowed" and "priors wash out, so this shouldn't last."

Does this hang together? I have a feeling (3) and (4) are fighting and (2) and (6) are fighting, but I honestly don't know which fight is the real one. Can you go through the pairs, tell me which ones actually conflict and how badly, which ones only look like they conflict and fall apart when you look at them properly, and for each real conflict say what would have to give and what it costs me? Rank them instead of treating them all as equally bad, and be concret about the Priya case at the end — I don't want a lecture on rationality, I want to know which of the seven I should drop.
