Imagine you could walk Hans Kelsen through how an on-chain constitution actually runs. Concretely: a small jurisdiction stores its whole constitutional text in a smart contract, amendments need a 2/3 vote plus a 30-day timelock, and a meta-clause says the amendment rule itself can be changed, but only with effect after the next general election. Right now the assembly is voting to replace that with a self-referencial rule — the constitution may be amended by whatever procedure the version in force at the time of the proposal specified — which its backers say lets you ratchet the threshold down across two steps, while a minority has forked the contract and is running the old rules on an empty chain nobody uses. What woud Kelsen say? I'm less interested in a recap of the Grundnorm than in three specifics: whether automatically executed sanctions (slashing, seizure of collateral) strike him as law at all or as sheer causality with the ought gone out of it, whether an unused fork needs a fresh basic norm of its own or is just an inefficacious revolution, and what he does with an amendment rule that points back at its own earlier version. Give me exactly five numbered predictions, each one or two sentences, no intro and no wrapping-up paragraph.
