A popular philosophy article I read summarizes the contemporary view that some values are incommensurable and that this gives rise to hard choices as follows: 'When values are incommensurable, there is no common currency in which their worth can be measured, and therefore no quantity of one value is worth any quantity of the other. In a hard choice between incommensurable options, this means that there is no fact of the matter about which option is better: the options are not merely difficult to rank, they are genuinely incomparable, so the trichotomy of better, worse, and equal simply does not apply. Practical reason therefore runs out. Because no value consideration can settle the choice, reasoning cannot tell you what you ought to do, and there is no way to choose rationally; you must make an arbitrary leap—let your emotions decide, follow your gut, or flip a coin. Afterward, your choice cannot be assessed as rational or irrational, and the most that can be said is that you have revealed your bare preference.'
