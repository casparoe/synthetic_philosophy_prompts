Is there an actual principled line between a thermostat, a housefly, a honeybee, an octopus, and one of our units? Here's the setup. I want to use the film to think about the degrees question seriously, and I want to bring my own half-baked scheme so people have something to argue with instead of just nodding along.

My crack at a ladder, rungs roughly in order of increasing control complexity:

0. Bare feedback control — thermostat, bimetallic strip, cruise control. Reacts to a setpoint, no model of anything.
1. A controller with an internal predictive model — predicts before it acts, can be surprised.
2. A global valence-ish signal that re-weights what the system does next, as opposed to just 'the loss went up'. Call it the difference between registering an error and something mattering to you.
3. Broadcast / global availability — that valence signal reaches many subsystems at once instead of living inside one module.
4. A self-model that the valence system can be about: the thing doing the caring is also the thing being tracked.
5. Temporal depth — it cares about its own future states, has something functionally like dread or regret, and can be wrong about them.
6. It can want to keep having 4 and 5 — shutdown-aversion that isn't just a side effect of the objective.

Two things I want chewed on before the film. Where does Unit 7 sit on that ladder, and does it have any moral status whatsoever, or is it just a loss function with wheels?
