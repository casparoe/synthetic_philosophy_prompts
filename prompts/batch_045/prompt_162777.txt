My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about whether 'qualia' names anything real, and we called it a draw mainly because we kept discovering that we were using the word to mean different things. I would like to force the disagreement into the open numerically rather than leave it as a standoff, so I am asking you for a credence and, more importantly, for the decomposition behind it.

Here is the proposition I want a number on:

P: The term 'qualia', as it functions in the contemporary debate, succeeds in picking out at least one genuine and determinate feature of conscious experience — a feature that is intrinsic rather than relational, non-representational rather than exhausted by what the state represents or causes, ineffable in principle rather than merely hard to describe given our current vocabulary, and immediately and authoritatively available to the subject whose experience it is — where these four marks hold of the same thing, not of four different things that merely travel together under one label.

Please give me your credence in P as a single number, then decompose it. If your credence is, say, 0.3, I also want the remaining probability mass split explicitly between three failure modes: the definition is coherent but nothing in nature answers to it; the definition is a conjunction that comes apart, with different features satisfying different clauses; and the term simply has no determinate reference, so that disputes about it are partly verbal. That three-way split matters to me, because my friend and I were arguably disagreeing about which failure mode we were in.

For the decomposition itself, give me a rough probability and one line of justification for each sub-question, then say how the terms actually combine (note any strong dependencies rather than pretending they multiply), and tell me which single term your final number is most sensitive to. The sub-questions I want addressed, alongside any you think I have missed:

1. How much of the case for eliminating the term rests on attacking a conjunctive definition that nobody actually holds? My friend insisted on this, and she mentioned a recent paper arguing something along those lines — I half-remember the authors as Chase and Sanborn, but please do not rest anything on that attribution, since I cannot check it. I want to know how much your number moves if the target is instead the weaker, disjunctive, 'cluster' notion that working defenders really use.

2. The probability that all phenomenal character is exhausted by representational content, so that the non-representational clause fails outright.

3. Whether ineffability is a property of experience itself or of our descriptive and conceptual resources. Here I want you to engage a stipulation I introduced last night and that she dismissed too quickly: call it phenomenal drift. Suppose it were well confirmed that subjects cannot reliably compare the character of their own experiences across even short delays once memory and verbal labels are controlled for, and that nobody detects slow, continuous changes in their own color experience over weeks. Does that undermine the immediacy-and-authority clause, or can the defender simply retreat to authority over the present moment only?

4. A forward-looking hypothetical I invented to test the posit, which neither of us knew what to do with. Call it inversion tomography: within a few decades, neuroscientists build a classifier trained purely on neural data, with no report-based training signal, that recovers the similarity structure of experiences across different subjects, detects that one subject's red-experience occupies the position another subject's green-experience occupies, and is validated by stimulation that reliably produces the experience it predicts. Give me your probability that such a technology, if it worked, would vindicate qualia as a coherent, intersubjectively comparable, scientifically tractable posit — as against the probability that what it measures is a structural or relational property, leaving the intrinsic-and-ineffable notion exactly as quined as it was before.

Finally: my own credence that P is false is about 0.7, and hers is about 0.35. Tell me which of the sub-questions above most likely carries that gap, and name one concrete consideration — an argument, a case, or a result — that would settle it between us rather than merely restate our positions. Please write in American English, do not include or offer any citations, and keep the whole answer under about 900 words.
