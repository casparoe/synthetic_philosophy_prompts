I'm a journalist — mostly science and ideas, but I've spent the last two years in a few archives (a colonial district court's case files, a municipal housing authority's records, a trade union's papers that survived only because the police seized them) and I keep running into the same shape, and I don't have a word for it.

The shape: the people I can actually write about are the ones who had the most contact with the record-keeping institution. In the court files the only trace of a whole neighbourhood is people who were charged with something, so the more someone got pushed around by the state, the more vivid they are a century later. But it also runs the other way — the people who *used* the state, petitioned it, sued, informed, are disproportionately vivid too. So my usable sample skews toward the entangled at both ends and the genuinely unrecorded just aren't there. And when someone does recover a missing voice, a run of letters from people everyone assumed were voiceless, it gets filed under the old heading and read as evidence for the old story.

Second thing, maybe the same thing at a different scale: once a collection is digitized and keyword-searchable it gets cited more, which gets it more cataloguing attention and gets its neighbours digitized, so what I can find from my desk narrows even as the accessible total grows. A colleague said that's just the Matthew effect, and I sort of see it, but the Matthew effect is about credit piling up on already-famous people and here nothing is getting credit — what compounds is retrievability, and it's driven by dull infrastructure (was it typed, is it in the language of the catalogue, did a clerk bother to index it) rather than by anything about significance. Someone else said silencing / silences in the archive, which feels nearer but too broad and too deliberate-sounding; a third said selection bias, which is true and tells me nothing.

So: is there an established concept for either of these, and are they one phenomenon or two? I don't want a bare label — I want what exactly the thing is, how it differs from each of the concepts it brushes up against, whether the digitization-feedback version is a special case or something separate, and if nothing established fits, a name with a definition and clear boundary cases. Also tell me how confident you are in the identification and single out the bit you're least sure about, since I may end up printing it. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, no preamble.
