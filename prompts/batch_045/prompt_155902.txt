My opening. Imagine a technique — call it affective attenuation — that, applied to a whole population at once, multiplies the felt intensity of every pleasant and unpleasant state by one half, leaving durations and orderings untouched. Our world has ten billion people, each living a life that is net positive on your own intrapersonal scale: a short stretch of suffering well above the lexical threshold (say a hundred times it), then decades of decent happiness. Each of them, asked in advance, would refuse the whole package. Now we attenuate five times. Before every step at least someone's suffering is still above the threshold, so you definately have to say each step is an improvement; yet afterwards every single life is one thirty-second of what it was on the scale you yourself accept, and every person would say they got a worse deal. And if you try to escape by saying we should stop attenuating just below the threshold and then crank the pleasures back up, then below the threshold your view is just aggregative hedonic utilitarianism with a hard ceiling on pain, and the 'negative' has stopped doing any work. Pick that apart.
