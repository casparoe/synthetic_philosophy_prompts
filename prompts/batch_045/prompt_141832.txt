I'm trying to locate a disagreement rather than win it. Here is the proposition precisely: in a forced choice between (A) one person tortured continuously and severely for fifty years and (B) each of an astronomically large number of people — say 10²⁴ — undergoing a single momentary eye irritation of the kind a dust mote causes, felt for a fraction of a second, forgotten immediately, with no effect on anyone's health, plans, or later mood, and nothing else affected either way, outcome B is the morally better one. I sit near 0.7; a colleague sits near 0.25, and I suspect we are not disagreeing where we think we are.

Give me your own number, decomposed. I want these terms separated, each with a probability and one line of justification, plus any further term you think belongs: (i) whether a harm that leaves no trace on a life — no memory, no change in mood or activity — contributes a nonzero amount to a total at all, or contributes nothing until enough such harms accumulate to cross some threshold of difference; (ii) whether badness aggregates Archimedeanly, so that for any finite harm and any nonzero tiny harm some number of the tiny ones outweighs it, or has thresholds, saturation, or lexical priorities that no quantity of specks can cross; (iii) whether the badness of torture is exhausted by its position on a single hedonic scale, or includes components — the destruction of a life's projects and narrative, betrayal, the annihilation of agency — that are not of a kind with an eye irritation and so cannot be offset by any quantity of it; (iv) whether the summation in (ii) is meant to run across persons at all, or whether one irritation in each of vastly many lives and vastly many irritations in one life come apart in a way the argument does not register; (v) whether "better" is well defined here at all, or whether the two outcomes are incomparable rather than ranked.

Then combine the terms into your number, tell me which one the number is most sensitive to, and say where the 0.7 and the 0.25 most likely diverge.
