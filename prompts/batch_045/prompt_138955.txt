Had a blazing argument in the pub last night with my mate about whether blowing thirty grand on a wedding is actually a signal of commitment. He kept saying it's costly so it's honest, end of; I kept saying cost on its own proves nothing — a signal's only honest if it costs a bloke who's going to bail in two years more than it costs one who isn't, and anyone can borrow thirty grand, so the reception is just a reciept. Problem is I'm best man at that wedding in three weeks and I've now got to write the toast. I need a three-minute speech that gets the handicap principle right without turning into a lecture: the real evidence is him turning down the Singapore posting, selling the car, and doing a reading he wrote himself in front of two hundred people, not the price tag. But I can't say "they must love each other, look at the flowers" because that's the exact move I spent all evening arguing against, and I can't insult her parents who paid for the flowers either. Can you write it so it also gets across that all I can really offer the room is the signals and not his feelings, which I've got no access to — and so it still lands when half the room think the whole wedding's a racket and the other half have never heard of any of this?
