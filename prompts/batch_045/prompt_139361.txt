I am putting together a graduate seminar unit on the surprise exam (unexpected hanging) paradox, and I want to know how well the standard families of response travel beyond the canonical case. Score them on how well they handle four announcement scenarios: (a) one exam, five-day week, students will not know the night before which day it is; (b) one exam, five-day week, with the condition made probabilistic, namely that on every evening the students' credence that the exam falls on the next day stays below one half; (c) one exam, five-day week, with surprise replaced by provability, so that the requirement is that the students cannot derive which day it is from the announcement plus the public record, within a fixed sound formal system rich enough for arithmetic; (d) two exams in a five-day week, each required to be surprising on the evening before it.

Rate these five families, characterized structurally rather than by author: (A) the announcement is a blindspot, so the backward induction is sound and shows only that a true announcement of this kind cannot be known by its addressees; (B) the induction is unsound, because each step presupposes that the announcement is still true and known after the eliminations that the reasoning itself performs; (C) there is no genuine contradiction, only an equivocation on knowledge, surprise or truth, with the utterance treated as a prediction or as a context-sensitive claim; (D) knowledge is the wrong category, and the announcement should be read as constraining a credence function rather than triggering closure; (E) knowledge is to be formalized as provability, with the announcement treated as a self-referential sentence of the sort studied in provability logic.

Produce a tier list (S / A / B / C) for overall robustness across all four scenarios, weighting breadth over elegance on (a) alone, and attach a 0–100 robustness score to each family. Then name the family whose placement shifts most between (a) and the other scenarios, name the single most diagnostic scenario and what it separates, and, for whichever family you rank highest, describe a further variant of your own design that would expose its weakest point. Finally, flag where your verdicts depend on the idealization that the students are perfect reasoners who fully trust the announcement.
