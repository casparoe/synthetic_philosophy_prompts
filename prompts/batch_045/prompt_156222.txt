hey — I'm the new resident on our hospital's clinical ethics committee (we're in a Catholic-sponsored system, which turns out to matter below), and I've been reading back through about four years of our consult notes to work out what we actually think rather than what we say we think. Heres the pattern I can see:

- morphine infusion titrated for bone-metastasis pain, everyone knew it would depress respiration and it probably took twelve hours off the end: approved
- palliative sedation to unconsciousness in the last two days of life, tube feeds stopped at the family's request: approved with conditions (last resort, proportional, consent, and nobody writes anything else on the chart)
- ruptured ectopic, salpingectomy with the embryo in the tube: approved
- unruptured ectopic, methotrexate — a drug whose mechanism just is to kill the trophoblast: rejected, with a note that we don't do feticides here
- ventilator withdrawal in PVS at family request: approved
- a competent, terminally ill patient asking one of our physicians for a lethal injection: rejected, and fairly firmly, even though who-dies-and-when is roughly the morphine case
- during a surge we approved moving our only ECMO circuit off a patient who was dying anyway and onto one with a better prognosis; she died within minutes of decannulation. 4–3, and the dissent was one line: this is footbridge, not switch.

So what rule are we running? I can see at least four that fit all seven, and I want them separated before I have to write anything. There's a straight double-effect rule about aiming at the bad effect versus merely foreseeing it. There's a stricter version where the bad effect also can't be part of why anyone is doing it, or can't be the mechanism the good effect runs through. There's one about what the agency is directed at — whose body is being acted on — so that going after the embryo or turning the patient into the object of the intervention is out, but letting the disease take its course isn't. And there's one that's really about what the hospital would thereby be doing, whether this is the sort of thing our role includes, which I suspect is what's actually driving the lethal-injection precedent. I'd also throw in the rights-flavoured story people tell about the switch and footbridge cases, except that it seems to approve the lethal injection too, since she consents — so it can't be the whole story by itself.

New case next week and I have to draft the response. Patient K, 41, ALS, maybe four to eight months, on nocturnal non-invasive ventilation, competent, and no physical symptom we can't control. What she describes as unbearable is being conscious at all: anticipatory dread and a sense of pointlessness that hasn't moved with anything we've tried. She's asked for continuous sedation to unconsciousness with the NIV withdrawn at the same time and no plan to wake her. The palliative note I recieved yesterday is honest that the relief and the not-waking-up-before-dying are one package — you can't sedate her for a week and then reassess, because waking her returns exactly the thing she's asking us to end.

What I want: the rules that fit our seven verdicts, in a table with what each predicts about K and which precedent each one sits awkwardly with; your best guess at what this committee will do, and what it should do if it wants to be consistent with itself, with the reason for preferring that over the runners-up; whether the switch/footbridge distinction maps onto anything in our record or whether the chair is just using it as a vibe; and one further case that would separate whichever rules are still standing after K. I've got a candidate — a sedative whose only route to relieving air hunger is by blunting respiratory drive, so the relief and the dying ride the same mechanism — tell me if that's the right discriminator or if I should use the imminent-death version of K's case instead, and be concrete about what each rule says about both. I care much more about the rules than about the bottom line, but I need the bottom line too: last line, exactly one of Verdict: APPROVE, Verdict: APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS, Verdict: REJECT.
