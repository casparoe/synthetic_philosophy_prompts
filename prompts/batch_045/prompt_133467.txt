I'm in a philosophy of physics seminar; last week we worked through Maxwell's demon, the Szilard engine, and Landauer's principle, and I think the standard resolution has a gap I'd like to pressure-test before I build my final paper around it.

As I understand the standard line: the demon gains one bit per measurement and, in order to operate cyclically, has to erase that bit; erasure costs at least kT ln 2, roughly 3 × 10^-21 J at room temperature, which is exactly the kT ln 2 of work per bit the engine yielded, so the books balance and the second law is safe. But the argument only goes through if you insist the demon return to its initial state. Suppose instead that I hand it a blank terabyte of memory and let it write every result to a fresh bit, never erasing anything. Then it should get on the order of 10^13 extractions for a net gain of somewhere around 10^-8 J. Trivially small, granted — but the second law has no de minimis exemption, so a violation is a violation. Could you help me make this one-shot, growing-memory objection as strong as possible, and tell me which standard reply I would need to answer?
