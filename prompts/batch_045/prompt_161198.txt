I'm a PhD student in metaphysics, midway through a paper on the metaphysics of dispositions, and I want an argumentative essay I can argue against rather than a survey of what has been said. Roughly 1,000–1,400 words, no citations, no bibliography, no appeals to what some author has argued: reconstruct every position from scratch and defend it on its merits.

The question is whether the simple conditional analysis of disposition ascriptions can be repaired, or whether its failure pushes us toward an account on which dispositions are grounded in categorical causal bases and the corresponding conditionals serve only as defeasible evidence.

Begin by stating the analysis under attack in your own terms — roughly, that x is disposed to give response R to stimulus S just in case x would give R were x in S — and by running the two standard counterexamples against it: the case where the approach of the stimulus triggers something that removes the disposition's own basis, and the case where the basis is untouched but something extrinsic prevents the response from being produced. Explain why these two break the biconditional in different directions, and why that difference matters for any repair.

Then take seriously two repairs before you reject them. The first adds a normal-conditions or ceteris paribus clause; the second makes disposition ascriptions gradable, so that x counts as disposed to R in S iff x would give R in some suitably high proportion of the relevant S-cases, with the threshold fixed by context. Give each the strongest formulation you can, and say exactly where it breaks.

The bulk of the essay should be spent on three cases I have not seen discussed, and I want your verdict on each stated explicitly, including where you think intuition is genuinely unsettled rather than clear:

1. The flickering masker. A wire is in series with an indeterministic interrupter that, on each occasion on which current would flow, has a 0.6 objective chance of opening the circuit and a 0.4 chance of remaining closed. Nothing about the wire's microstructure varies between trials. Is the wire conductive, and if so, to what degree?
2. The reset fink. Contact with a probe opens a relay, and the relay closes again one millisecond later with the probe still in contact, at which point current flows. Nothing about the wire is altered at any point; only the circuit's topology changes. Is this a case in which the disposition is withdrawn or one in which it is merely blocked, and what does your answer commit you to about the temporal window within which a disposition must manifest?
3. The enabler that also masks. A loop is superconducting only while a cryogenic field is maintained, and by a law of nature that same field quenches whenever current would begin to flow. Without the field there is no conductivity; with it, no current ever flows. Does the loop conduct electricity?

For each case, say what the ceteris paribus analysis predicts, what the gradable analysis predicts, and what a basis-grounded account predicts, and argue that the pattern of verdicts favours one of them.

Finally, state and defend your own conclusion. If you land on a basis-grounded or primitivist account, you owe an answer to two costs: that the basis is typically specified partly in dispositional terms, threatening circularity, and that the same disposition can apparently be realized by very different bases. If instead you salvage some conditional analysis, you must say non-circularly how the conditions in the antecedent are restricted. Close with the single strongest objection to your conclusion and a short, non-question-begging reply to it.
