I'm the ombudsperson for a network of about a dozen assisted-living and memory-care homes — families, residents and staff bring me the fights nobody else wants to touch: who gets to be in the room when the doctor calls, whose version of "what Dad would have wanted" wins, whether the staff should keep a promise to a resident that the family hates. Its maybe forty cases a year, I've got half an hour to an hour per intake, and whatever I decide goes in a case note that a lawyer or a state inspector might read later, so theres a legal floor I cant go below. I took two philosophy classes in college and recently got into reading about Confucian role ethics on my own after a genuinely ugly fight with my own brother about our mother, and the core idea keeps sticking: you dont start with a free individual who then acquires relationships, you start with the person as a node of roles and the obligations come already built into the role, graded by closeness, with no impartial standpoint to appeal to. Two caveats before you write anything. I do NOT want this to turn into "use the Confucian lens when the family is Chinese or Korean or Vietnamese" — we get plenty of families like that and plenty where nobody has ever heard of Confucius, and it seems both insulting and actually less useful than just running the role lens on everybody, staff included. And I know role ethics gets accused of being unable to criticize the roles themselves: if the role says the eldest son decides, then the eldest son decides, and theres nothing more to say. I want a procedure that has a real answer to that, or that admits it doesnt.

Heres roughly what I think I need, plus a few half-baked tools Ive been using that you should feel free to throw out or fix. I want an ordered set of questions I can run on an intake: what to ask first, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure just stops with a no, and where it has to hand the decision to a person because its a values call and not a factual one. Concretely Ive been messing around with (1) a role-pair map — list the actual pairs in play, note which ones the institution invented (resident, charge nurse, healthcare proxy) and which ones the people actually live inside, and notice when an institutional role is quietly being asked to carry the emotional weight of a family one; (2) a role-distance substitution test — take the demand being made and re-run it with the person one step further out on the relationship map, and if it collapses immediately then the demand was really about that specific relationship rather than about the residents welfare; (3) something ive started calling the creditors complaint, where someone has been performing a role very richly and has started to feel owed for it, and gets resentful when the other person performs the same role thinly — I have no idea whether role ethics has anything to say about that or whether it just says do the role better. Theres also the names thing: the same person gets called "my father," "Mr. Alvarez," "the resident in 214," "Dads friend Carol," and the mismatch feels like evidence but I cant say what its evidence of.

Can you give me the actual procedure — short enough to actually use, steps in order — and then run it on these three, being honest when it gives an answer I wont like:

- Two brothers. One lives ten minutes away, visits daily, handles everything. The other lives across the country, calls on Sundays, insists on being consulted before every decision, and wants to override his brother about a feeding tube. The local brother wants me to tell the staff to stop calling the distant one.
- A daughter whose father has mid-stage dementia asks the staff not to tell him that his other daughter died — she says in our family we shield him, knowing would only cause suffering and hed forget and relearn it over and over. The staff think this is deception and that he has a right to know. Im fairly sure I dont want the answer this one pushes toward.
- A resident with no children who has an unmarried partner of thirty years; the partner isnt the legal proxy, a niece who hasnt visited since 2019 is, and on the forms the partner is down as "friend." Theress no role on offer that fits and the institution has to invent one.

Also: what does the procedure systematically get wrong, and at what point do I have to drop it and switch to rights language because the law or the liability people will eat me otherwise? Ive got a 90-minute slot with new staff in a couple weeks and id rather make them test something than lecture at them.
