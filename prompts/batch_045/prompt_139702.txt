I want you to map the exposure of an argument I have become attached to. The argument is genealogical rather than metaphysical: cross-cultural variation in folk moral judgment is large, it is predictably related to non-moral features of social ecology, and it is concentrated where we would expect variation if no moral facts were doing explanatory work. So the burden falls on the realist to give a non-question-begging reason to treat that variation as error rather than as sensitivity to local circumstances, and the familiar appeals to worse epistemic conditions, to moral progress, or to disagreement about applications rather than principles do no independent work.

Two stipulations, so that the question concerns the argument's structure rather than the size of the effect. First: suppose that once you condition on a small number of social-ecological parameters — pathogen load, market integration, kinship intensity, how anonymous daily interaction is — residual moral differences between any two societies are small, and the moral systems societies actually occupy form a low-dimensional region rather than an anything-goes scatter. Second: suppose the variation sits almost entirely in two places, the weights assigned to a small set of moral considerations that is itself invariant across cultures, and the boundary of the moral community; the considerations themselves do not vary.

Given that, tell me what would count against the argument.

1. Does the stipulated convergence help the debunker or the realist? Independent cultures landing in one low-dimensional region can look either like the genealogy working perfectly or like independent inquirers homing in on a single target. Say which reading is correct and what the decision turns on.
2. Is the weight-versus-content distinction load-bearing? If cultures differ only in how they weight the same considerations and in where they draw the circle of moral patients, does the debunking argument get stronger, weaker, or does it merely relocate — and does the invariant core then do realist work for free?
3. Which defeaters would defenders of this argument themselves concede as counting against it, as opposed to what critics allege counts against it? Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and include the ones a committed defender would dismiss outright, with the reason for dismissing them.
4. Has the position been insulated to the point of unfalsifiability? It can always add that the residue will be explained by ecological variables not yet measured, and it can redescribe any cross-cultural agreement as agreement in non-moral conditions rather than in moral judgment. If that is unfalsifiability, say whether it is a defect in an abductive argument of this kind or an acceptable cost of making one.

Finally: what would the realist have to show, positively, to turn this from a standoff into a win — some observable difference between a world in which the genealogical explanation is the whole story and a world in which it is not? If there is no such difference, say so, and say what follows for someone who holds my view.
