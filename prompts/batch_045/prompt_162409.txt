I'm in a metaethics reading group, and we keep circling the same stalemate on the Frege–Geach problem, so I want to see whether a specific and somewhat unfashionable version of the expressivist reply can actually be made to work rather than just gestured at. The setup everyone already agrees on: expressivism says the semantic job of an atomic moral sentence like "stealing is wrong" is to express a non-belief attitude — disapproval, a plan, a practical commitment — rather than to describe how things are; the Frege–Geach problem is that such sentences embed (in the antecedent of a conditional, under negation, under quantifiers, inside attitude ascriptions), and the theory therefore owes an account of why it is valid to argue "if stealing is wrong then getting your little brother to steal is wrong; stealing is wrong; therefore getting your little brother to steal is wrong," when the ordinary explanation of that validity — preservation of truth across a shared representational content — is precisely what the theory denies itself. Please skip any overview of the debate, any history of who said what, and any literature survey: I have read all of that, and what I want is the argument.

The position I want you to argue for is that the Frege–Geach problem is not, in the end, a semantic problem about embedding at all, but a problem about the normativity of inference, and that the correct expressivist response is to decline the representationalist framing of validity altogether: "valid" should itself be understood as expressing a verdict of normative incoherence on a package of attitudes — roughly, that accepting the premises while refusing the conclusion is something one has no reason-giving standing to do — rather than as describing a relation among truth-evaluable contents. Argue that this is not a cheap stipulation that dissolves the problem by fiat but is forced on the expressivist by three cases that no amount of compositional machinery for embeddings handles on its own: (i) quantifiers binding into moral content inside an attitude ascription, as in "every candidate believes that everything the committee did was wrong," which appears to require contents that can be shared across subjects, bound by a variable, and re-identified; (ii) comparative and degree morphology — "worse than," "much worse," "the worst of the available options" — where the relevant validity turns on transitivity-like constraints on the underlying attitudes rather than on any embedding whatsoever; and (iii) inferences whose conclusions are not declarative at all, as in "stealing is wrong, so don't steal," where nothing truth-evaluable is even in play. For each case, argue that the normative reply does work that the two standard rivals cannot: the strategy of positing higher-order attitudes toward combinations of attitudes, and the strategy of bifurcating moral sentence content into an expressed attitude plus a thin descriptive (naturalistic or minimal) content. Say for each rival whether it fails by smuggling in representational content through the back door or by leaving the validity itself unexplained, and be fair about what each gets right that your view does not.

Then meet the obvious objection head-on, because it is where I expect the argument to live or die: if validity is itself a normative status, the expressivist has purchased a solution to the moral case by generating an exactly parallel problem one level up, since "valid," "incoherent," and "has no reason-giving standing" now form a normative vocabulary whose sentences embed and figure in inferences just as "wrong" did. Argue that this regress is benign rather than vicious, and say what bottoms it out — what the expressivist can claim about the practice of giving and asking for reasons such that the regress stops rather than continuing indefinitely — while conceding honestly what such a position costs (in particular, whether it leaves expressivism distinguishable from a relaxed descriptivism about the normative domain generally, and whether that is a cost worth paying). A friend of mine says the whole move is just relabeling; I want a version of it good enough that she would have to say something more specific than that.
