I am writing up my position on stakes-sensitivity in epistemology for a graduate seminar, and before I commit to it on paper I want an audit of whether the package actually hangs together. Here are the commitments as I currently hold them.

(a) **Contextualism.** The truth-conditions of "S knows that p" vary with features of the attributor's context — which error possibilities are salient, what is at stake for the attributor, what the attributor's conversational purposes are. This is a semantic thesis about "knows," not a recommendation about how careful anyone should be. A consequence I accept: when a low-stakes attributor says "Ada knows the branch is open" and a high-stakes attributor says "Ada does not know," both speak truly, and there is no substantive dispute between them left to adjudicate. I take this to be DeRose-style contextualism rather than the interest-relative view associated with Hawthorne and Stanley, which I read as a rival.

(b) **Pragmatic encroachment, read subject-side.** Whether S knows p depends in part on what is practically at stake for S in acting on p. My reason for wanting this is not the intuitive pull of the bank-style cases but the role of knowledge in deliberation: knowledge is the norm of practical reasoning, so if S knows p, S is in a position to treat p as settled and act on it. An epistemology on which S can know p while being criticizable for acting on p has, I think, detached the concept from its point.

(c) **Stakes are assessed at the time of action, not at the time of belief formation.** If Ada checks the branch hours on Friday for a trivial errand, and on Saturday that same belief becomes load-bearing for something with her savings riding on it, then — with no change in her evidence, in the reliability of her source, or in the world — Ada has lost knowledge that she had on Friday. That is my verdict on the deferred-stakes variant: she does not know on Saturday.

(d) **Testimony transmits upward across a stakes gap.** Consider what I will call the relay case. Ada forms the belief that the branch is open Saturday from the bank's website on Friday, for a trivial errand; the website is reliable but occasionally out of date, and in this instance the hours are as she believes. On Saturday her friend Bo, whose business collapses if the wire does not go out today, texts to ask; Ada answers from her Friday belief without re-checking. My verdicts: Ada knows when she answers, and Bo comes to know on the basis of her say-so. The recipient's much higher stakes neither reach back to undo the speaker's knowledge nor block transmission.

(e) **Fallibilism plus closure.** Knowledge is closed under known entailment, single-premise and multi-premise, and at no level of stakes does knowledge require evidence that entails the proposition: even at the highest stakes the standard remains fallible.

(f) **A usability constraint.** Whatever account of knowledge is correct, whether S knows p must be ascertainable by S, in the ordinary run of cases, from S's own position — without empirical investigation into facts about stakes, attributors, or downstream reliance that S may have no way of knowing. I hold this because of (b): knowledge is what S is supposed to act on, and a status that floats free of S's own epistemic position cannot do that work. I am more attached to (f) than to any of the others.

For what it is worth, my own suspicion is that (d) is the weakest link, but I would rather not have that taken as a hint.
