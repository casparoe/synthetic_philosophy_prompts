I am a senior at a secondary school and a member of our school's philosophy society, and what I need help with is a piece of vocabulary rather than a topic.

At our last meeting we debated whether the experience of deliberating — the felt sense that one could have done otherwise — is real, given that everything a person does is at bottom physics. One speaker argued that agency is nothing but neural computation, and that the feeling of open possibility is simply the brain's way of tracking its own processing. Another replied that this was just greedy reductionism, and the room treated the reply as though a point had been scored. The same speaker had earlier dismissed a theistic argument by saying that in science it is cranes, not skyhooks, taking a skyhook to be an explanation that gets something from nothing. I have seen both phrases credited to Daniel Dennett's writing about evolution and mind.

My difficulty is that I cannot tell what the first reply accused the first speaker of. So I would like a menu: lay out the distinct precise theses that the slogan greedy reductionism could be expressing, arranged from the nearly trivial to the radical. For each, tell me whether it would be true of every reduction worth the name, whether it is a substantive and defensible charge, or whether it collapses into something false or incoherent. Most importantly, tell me which of these readings would actually do the work the reply was doing in our debate — which would show that the agency-as-computation story is defective rather than merely unfashionable — and say plainly if none of them would.

Two things of my own that I would like judged. First, a test I have been toying with: a reduction is greedy, I want to say, only if the reducing story cannot be re-employed in the other places where the reduced notion was successfully doing work — in law, in everyday deliberation, in psychology, in praise and blame. On this test, a reduction that lets you put belief or choice back to use everywhere, while still insisting that the higher-level thing is nothing over and above the lower-level thing, is well-fed rather than greedy. Does that test track anything real, or does it only relabel the dispute? And does it make greed a fault in an explanation, in an ontology, or in something else? Second, a worry about symmetry: if the paired slogans are both always available, is there a mirror-image vice — a greedy anti-reductionism, one that declares the higher-level phenomenon permanently unexplainable and so licenses a skyhook — and does the pair of slogans retain any content at all, or can any position be accused of one or the other? If the vocabulary is doing no real work, say so.

Please commit rather than survey: tell me which reading I should adopt as what the phrase means, whether the accusation landed in our debate, and whether my test is worth keeping. Where the matter is genuinely unsettled, say so too, and tell me explicitly which of your claims are confident and which are judgement calls, and what would change your mind. I am not after books or papers — I cannot get hold of them, and I want the argument rather than the reading list.
