I sit on the committee that decides which preparations at our institute count as systems of moral concern and therefore require heightened review rather than the ordinary animal-protocol route. Our votes so far, with the reasons recorded in the minutes:

1. An intact, behaving octopus — in scope: flexible, cross-modal problem solving under novel conditions.
2. An adult zebrafish — in scope: motivational trade-offs, and responses that analgesics abolish.
3. An adult fruit fly — out of scope: whatever its history, the organization is too fragmentary to constitute a subject, and at the numbers used a precautionary route was thought unworkable.
4. C. elegans — out of scope: the repertoire is close to innately specified. Its full nervous-system simulation, body and environment included, was also ruled out, on the ground that a model inherits whatever standing the modelled animal has and adds none of its own.
5. A large language model that emits elaborate first-person reports of distress — out of scope: the reports are the output of a training process aimed at plausibility, and there is no unified agent, no persistent body-model, and nothing at stake for it.
6. A few hundred thousand living cortical cells in a dish, trained in a closed loop on a simple sensorimotor task — out of scope: tissue, organized activity, and learning each proved insufficient on their own.
7. An intact large-mammal brain kept metabolically active ex vivo for several hours after the animal's death — out of scope, over two dissenters: the relevant organization would have to be running, not merely available in tissue that could run it.

Note the shape of that record. Every digital system we have ruled on was a model of something we had already ruled out, so we have never actually decided whether an artificial controller can stand in for one we cover.

The new proposal, B-114: a larval zebrafish whose own brain is pharmacologically silenced, with a real-time emulation of that same brain's spiking circuit — same cell classes, same connectivity, same membrane dynamics, reconstructed from that individual — receiving activity recorded from the animal's sensory afferents and driving its spinal motor output. It is the fish's own body, in water, with real consequences for that body; only the controller is synthetic. Please take it as stipulated that the larva is at a stage where our reasons in item 2 already apply, so that the substitution is the only live question. There is one animal. The applicants propose to run the markers from item 2, but since no drug can act on silicon they instead propose to perturb a global aversive-gain parameter inside the emulation and see whether the animal's trade-offs shift.

How should we vote if we want to be consistent with ourselves, and which precedent is the closest analog? Three rules seem to fit all seven verdicts: that the real-time causal organization is what matters and the substrate is irrelevant; that the organization must also have been arrived at through the kind of history that makes something a control system — selection, development, learning in a body — so that an exact copy does not inherit what the original had; and that what matters is being the actual controller of a living animal's body in a real world. They come apart here. Which, if any, is the one we have been running, what else fits the record as well as it does, and what does each predict about B-114? One colleague says we have no rule at all, only the marker test plus a threshold for how many animals are affected, and that this case simply exposes it — is that fair? If our precedents genuinely underdetermine the decision, I want the principle we would be adopting with each vote stated plainly enough that a future committee could see what it had signed up to. Finally, what single further case — the same emulation with a simulated body, or a controller trained from scratch in the body rather than copied from a brain, or something else — would best separate the two rules you think most likely, what would each predict there, and why would the result be informative rather than just another vote?
