I run a 40-minute session during internatinal orientation week for incoming exchange students in the UK — mostly non-philosophers, mixed nationalities, jetlagged, and half of them still weirded out by the queueing. I want to use tipping, queueing and jaywalking to get at two questions: whether everyday conventions are ever real moral obligations rather than just things people get huffy about, and whether a short-term visitor has to follow the local version of a convention they didn't grow up with.

The bit I actually want them to get — and please stress-test it rather than just restate it — is something I've been calling the legibility test: whether a convention morally binds a visitor depends less on how arbitrary it is or how many people follow it, and more on whether the locals around them can tell the difference between someone who doesn't know the norm and someone who knows it and can't be bothered. If they can't tell, then not following it doesn't merely fail to coordinate, it reads as contempt, and that's where the obligation comes from; if it's obvious you're a visitor, a lot of that evaporates. I think it also explains why jaywalking at 3am on an empty street feels different from skipping a queue, and why the tip prompt on a card machine feels like a demand instead of a thank you.

Don't write me the explanation, plan it. What order do the distinctions have to arrive in and what depends on what (I assume they have to feel the arbitrariness before they can feel the force, but I'm not sure). Which one example should carry the whole 40 minutes, and what does it need to have and lack — my instinct is a norm with no victim and no written rule, but you tell me. Which misconceptions will this crowd walk in with and form on the way — I'm expecting when in Rome, no-harm-no-foul about the empty street, it's their country so whatever they say goes, and tipping is just guilt — and at exactly which point in the sequence do I head each one off. One question at the end that would show they got the legibility thing rather than just the vocabulary. And what should I openly decline to get into at this level and say so out loud.
