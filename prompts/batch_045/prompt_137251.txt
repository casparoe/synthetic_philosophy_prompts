My friend won't shut up about how Occam's razor is a theorem now — Solomonoff and all that: hand each hypothesis a prior of 2 to the minus its shortest program length, and you beat every computable predictor up to a constant. I tried to build a reductio and now I keep loosing the thread on where it breaks.

(1) The guarantee is supposed to be machine-independent: swap in a different universal machine and every hypothesis's weight shifts by at most a fixed factor, i.e. by some constant number of bits, no matter how much data you've seen.

(2) Call it complexity laundering. I can build a universal machine whose built-in instruction set has my pet theory wired in — say, sunspots drive the stock market — so that theory gets a two-bit name while its boring rival keeps the long one. The laundering budget is bounded only by how much junk I put in the interpreter, and I can put in as much as I like.

(3) So for any pet theory H and any rival R, there's a perfectly universal machine on which H starts out ahead of R by more bits than all the evidence anybody will collect in the next century could make up.

(4) Nothing about the world picks out a reference machine; the choice is pure encoding convention, and (1) is exactly the reason that choice is supposed not to matter.

(5) So for any data set and any conclusion you like, there is a legitimate version of Solomonoff induction that hands you that conclusion and calls it the simple one.

(6) Hence the razor has no content. It doesn't constrain belief; it just relabels whatever you already fancied as the short program.

(7) But that's crazy. Actual cases — fitting one ellipse to the planets instead of adding a twelfth circle — feel like the razor doing real work that isn't just a choice of notation, and if (6) were right my friend's "the razor is a theorem" would really mean "the razor is whatever you like."

Which step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost me? I'd rather keep (1), (4), and my sense that the ellipse case is a genuine win than keep (6). My one thought is that (5) might be cheating — laundering H on one machine and then talking as if the machine were fixed — but I can't say why that matters if the machine is arbitrary anyway, and I also can't see what would stop me from laundering several pet theories at once into the same machine.
