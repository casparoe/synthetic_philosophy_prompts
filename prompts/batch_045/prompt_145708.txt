So I've got a pile of verdicts about pest control and no principle behind them, and I've went back and forth on this long enough that I want someone to tell me which ones have to go.

Squashing the mosquito that's been in my bedroom for three nights: completely fine. Spraying the ant trail in the kitchen: fine, slighty icky. Glue traps for mice: not fine, and I think what bothers me is the days of it rather than the number of animals. Neonic seed coatings, which as I understand it get into the pollen and nectar and take out huge numbers of non-target insects: not fine, and it feels like the worst thing on the list even though each individual death is presumably quick-ish. Releasing sterile male mosquitoes to knock down a local Aedes population: fine, actually feels elegant. A gene drive that would crash Anopheles across its whole range: not fine, and I can't shake it, even though I'll admit that another ten years of spraying kills more individual mosquitoes than the drive does. Classic biocontrol in the cane toad mould: not fine, but for a different reason — something about not knowing what you've started.

Now the made-up ones, which is where it gets interesting. A species-specific RNA spray that just stops the pest feeding, so it starves over about four days: I recoil at this much more than at a contact poison doing the same job. A drive that doesn't crash the population but spreads a trait that stops them carrying the parasite: basically fine, which is strange, because it's the same technology and a bigger intervention in what the species is. A lure-and-vapourise trap that is 100% species-specific and instant: completely fine, which makes me suspect the rest of my verdicts aren't really tracking insect suffering at all.

My best stab so far is something like: killing is fine when the death is fast and the thing dying is the thing that was causing you the problem, and not otherwise. But that seems to get the neonic case and the gene drive case wrong in opposite directions, and it says nothing about why the four-day starvation spray is worse than the weeks-long starvation of a colony dying off naturally.

So: one principle, or a short list of candidates, stated precisely, with which case motivates each clause, what it says about something I've never had a verdict on (bug splat on the windscreen, insects killed by ploughing a field), and an honest account of which of my verdicts it can't accommodate. And the one I want handled explicitly, because me and my partner argue about it every October: the wasps in the shed — instant freeze spray now, or leaving them, where the colony starves out over a few weeks anyway. Please write in British English.
