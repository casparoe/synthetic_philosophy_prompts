Exam question:
State the dispute between endurantism and perdurantism about persistence. Explain the argument from temporary intrinsics and the endurantist's standard reply to it, and say how the debate bears on the case of a poker that is straight at one time and bent at a later one. Does either view have a decisive advantage? Use at least one example of your own in your answer.

Student's answer:
Endurantism holds that a persisting object is wholly present at every time at which it exists: the object has no temporal parts, and to persist is to be wholly there at each moment of one's career. Perdurantism holds that persisting objects have temporal parts, and that an object persists by having distinct temporal parts at distinct times; the object as a whole is a four-dimensional spacetime worm, and the temporal parts are its shorter-than-whole stages. The standard illustration is the analogy with a road: just as a single road can be straight in one place and curved in another without contradiction, a single object can be straight at one time and bent at another without contradiction, because it is straight in virtue of one temporal part and bent in virtue of another. Perdurantism, in short, treats time as relevantly like space.

The main argument for perdurantism is Lewis's argument from temporary intrinsics. A poker that is straight at one time and bent at another appears to have an intrinsic property that it has at one time and lacks at another. If the poker is wholly present at both times, as the endurantist says, then it has incompatible intrinsic properties, which is contradictory. The endurantist's standard reply is to deny that the properties in question are monadic: strictly, the poker has being-straight-at-t1 and being-bent-at-t2. Lewis's rejoinder is that this restores consistency only by turning what looked like an intrinsic property into a relation to a time, which threatens to make every property relational and leaves the endurantist without an account of what the change in the object itself consists in. The perdurantist, by contrast, distributes the properties across different temporal parts, so no contradiction ever arises.

Because perdurantism treats all of an object's temporal parts as equally real, it is the metaphysical counterpart of the eternalist block universe, and it is further supported by special relativity: relativity shows that there is no absolute simultaneity and hence no objective present slice for the endurantist's claim of being wholly present to attach to. The endurantist is therefore committed to a physics that has been disproven, and this alone settles the dispute in favour of perdurantism.

The argument from fission points the same way. If an amoeba divides, the endurantist seems forced to say either that two numerically distinct objects occupied exactly the same matter beforehand or that identity in such a case is indeterminate, while the perdurantist can say that there was one object before division and two after. The same point applies to ourselves: since on perdurantism I am a worm composed of temporal parts, when I say that I will be in pain tomorrow I am not really referring to myself but to a future temporal part of myself. It follows that nothing is strictly identical with itself over time, and that personal identity over time is a useful fiction rather than a real relation. Perdurantists are therefore committed to holding that prudential concern for one's own future and moral responsibility for one's past actions are considerably weaker than common sense supposes.
