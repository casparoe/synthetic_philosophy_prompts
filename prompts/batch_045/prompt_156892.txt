I am putting together the assessment materials for a second-year module on blame for historically situated agents — the "man of his time" defence, and where exactly it breaks down — and I need a pair of cases that forces students to do genuine work with the defence rather than reach for one of the two lazy moves on offer: condemn everyone retrospectively, or excuse everyone retrospectively.

The schema I have been using in the module separates four things that ordinarily run together inside the defence:

- conceptual unavailability (CU): the wrong-making feature of the action cannot be formulated in any concept available to the agent;
- evidential unavailability (EU): nothing in the evidence accessible to the agent flagged the harm, and no inquiry available to them would have;
- peer-saturation (PS): the agent's conduct and attitudes sit at or below the median of their reference class;
- bridging-description availability (BD): some description already present in the agent's own moral vocabulary — a local thick term of the kind that condemns cruelty, faithlessness, or the betrayal of a dependent — picks out the action under a description the agent had evidence for, such that applying it with ordinary care would have led them to refrain.

What I want from you is two scenarios, one paragraph each, set in the same society, the same decade, and the same broad social position, and differing from one another in one respect only. Pre-twentieth-century setting, invented agents: no real historical figures, so that nothing in the exercise turns on disputed facts about what anyone actually did or believed. In Case A the defence should succeed; in Case B it should fail. The specifications:

1. CU, EU and PS must hold in both cases. The failure in B therefore cannot be that the agent could have known better in the anachronistic sense, and the success in A cannot be that everybody was doing it.
2. In both cases a small, socially marginal contemporary minority publicly criticised the practice, and in both cases the agent had heard of them and dismissed them as fanatics. Hold that fixed: I do not want the existence of dissenters doing the work.
3. Hold fixed as well the cost of refraining, the agent's motives, their general decency, and the magnitude of the harm. The only variable is BD: in A there is no description available to the agent under which anyone is being wronged, while in B there is one, and the agent avoids applying it for self-serving reasons.
4. Invert the sympathies. The case in which the defence succeeds should be the one students will instinctively want to condemn — something we now regard as grave — and the case in which it fails should be the one they will instinctively excuse: something mild, decent-seeming, well-intentioned. Ideally the two paragraphs should be difficult to tell apart on their surface facts.
5. Each paragraph must be self-contained, answerable by a student who has been given nothing but the schema above: no narration of the intended verdict inside the scenario itself.

After the two paragraphs, do the following. For each case, state which of the four conditions holds and which fails, and derive from that the verdict the defence yields — both about the action and about what we are licensed to say of the agent as a person on the strength of it. Show explicitly that the reason the defence fails in B is not also a reason it fails in A, and conversely. Identify the single feature doing the work, and say what becomes of the pair if that feature is removed: I want to know whether the contrast collapses or survives.

Finally, the objection I expect from the strongest students: that a local thick concept can at most render an action locally disapproved, not wrong in the terms we are actually judging by, so BD appears to smuggle back in precisely the anachronism that CU was meant to exclude, and the agent in B is being blamed for failing to anticipate a moral framework they had no way of occupying. Give me the strongest version of that objection, tell me whether it sinks Case B, and if it does, tell me how the case would have to be rebuilt in order to survive it.
