Before I take another claim of the form "this legal change was ultimately a product of the economic structure" seriously, I want a specification rather than a verdict. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of the relation between the economic base and the rest of social life — law, religion, science, family forms, political institutions, art — would have to meet, where adequate means adequate for exactly one job: serving as a standard for judging individual causal claims of that form in ordinary historical and political argument, not as a predictive science of long-run social change. So the theory is not required to forecast anything, and not required to rank modes of production by stage.

The distinctions I most want the list to respect:

Origination, selection, and shaping must be kept apart. A base may bring an element into existence, or merely filter variants that arose for unrelated reasons, or leave the element intact while fixing its content or use. A theory that tests only for origination will misclassify most real cases, and I want to know what evidence would separate the three.

The grain of the base must be fixed in advance — how fine a description of the economic structure may be invoked (capitalist versus financialized, export-led capitalism with a large domestic rentier sector), and who bears the burden of proof when a mismatch is explained away by redescribing the base. Without this I cannot tell a confirmed theory from an unfalsifiable one.

The hand-me-down and callback cases must be handled: elements that arrive by diffusion from another society, or are inherited from a predecessor economic structure, rather than generated by the present one; and old forms that a later structure revives and re-functions for its own purposes, such as feudal honor idioms in a modern officer corps or archaic legal categories pressed into service for novel property regimes. A theory must say what it predicts about such elements and how to tell inert residue from reactivated residue.

The boundary problem must be answered: whether the relations of production themselves include the legal forms that constitute property, contract, and the wage relation. If they do, say what is left of the claim that the base explains the law; if they do not, say what makes the base an economic rather than a juridical entity.

Wrong-way cases must be allowed or excluded explicitly — state fiscal capacity, rules on lending at interest and on marriage, religious doctrine plausibly shaping accumulation rather than the reverse.

Content, uptake, and direction must be separable, with natural science as the test case: a practice's internal content, the resources and prestige it attracts, and the uses its results are put to may each relate to the base differently.

And the theory must say, in the only sense I can test, what the familiar last-instance formula means and what would count as evidence against it.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it and mark it non-negotiable, negotiable, or a preference of one camp (predictive power, rational-choice microfoundations, refusal to treat any elite's self-description as data, and so on). Then tell me which two requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied: my own suspicion is that fixing the grain of the base in advance and giving the theory enough latitude to absorb inherited and imported elements pull in opposite directions, and I want to know whether that is right rather than merely plausible. Finally, say which single requirement excludes the most candidates. A table for the requirements and their motivating cases is fine; keep any scorecard of the familiar positions — straight reductionism, functional explanation, relative autonomy with reciprocal causation, and the view that there is no economic essence to be explained — to a few lines each, since the criteria, not the scoring, is what I want. Lead with your bottom line, whether the list is jointly satisfiable and if not which requirement you would drop, in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble and no restatement of my question.
