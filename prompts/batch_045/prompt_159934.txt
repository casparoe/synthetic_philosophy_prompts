I co-run a small film society and spend a lot of time recommending books to friends, and I keep running into the same odd thing about what happens when I take someone else's word for whether a work is good. I dont have a word for it, so let me just describe it.

1. There's a critic I've calibrated against over about ten years. When he says a film is a masterpiece I believe him fully, and I'll put it on my own top-ten list without having seen it. But if someone asks me at the pub "is it actually good?" I hear myself saying "it's meant to be extraordinary" rather than "it's extraordinary", and if they push me for what's good about it I've got nothing except his phrases, which I can repeat but which don't feel like mine. None of this happens with ordinary deference: my mechanic says the alternator is shot, I believe him, and I'll say flatly to anyone that the alternator is shot.

2. Reverse direction. On his recommendation I watch the film and find it dull. I don't much revise my view of the critic, but I also don't feel I was wrong, exactly — I feel I was never really in a position to hold the view. "I hadn't actually seen it" comes out as if it settles the matter. And when a film I judged for myself and loved gets savaged everywhere, I don't budge at all and feel entirely entitled not to.

3. The belief doesn't seem to do anything. I don't rewatch it, don't press it on people, don't get that odd sting when someone mocks it — all of which I do with things I judged myself. It's as if it's a belief only in the "what will I say if asked" sense.

So: is there an established name for this? Deferred conviction about a work's value that you genuinely have, but that behaves differently from a conviction you arrived at yourself in three ways — what it licenses you to assert, what it moves you to do, and how it handles being contradicted.

Some things I've half-heard of that might be near it, so you can tell me which (if any) it really is: there's the general scepticism about judging art you haven't experienced first-hand, but that seems to be about *whether* you can end up with the belief, and I clearly do end up with it. There's the point that second-hand information can give you the fact without giving you the explanation, but that doesn't feel like my case either — I can recite his reasons perfectly well, and it still feels hollow. Maybe it's just low confidence wearing a costume. Maybe it's about a skill of appreciation rather than a belief. Maybe it's four separate things bundled together and I should stop treating them as one.

I'm not after a label to drop. I want to know what the thing is, what it isn't, and where its edges are: does it show up outside the arts (wine, food, whether a joke is funny, moral or political judgement, factual expertise)? Does it happen as strongly with negative verdicts as with positive ones? Does it matter whether the source is a person whose taste I've calibrated against or an anonymous pile of star ratings?
