You are drafting, in a single neutral voice, the joint statement that two positions in the debate over responsibility gaps for autonomous systems would sign at the end of a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I chair a working group preparing guidance for a regulator on liability for learning-enabled systems, and I want the document our group should have written: not a survey of the literature, and not a verdict on who is right, but the text both sides could put their names to, with the residual disagreement made exact.

Call the first position the Gap Realist. She holds that for some harms caused by autonomous systems there is no one who is an appropriate target of blame, that this is a genuine normative loss rather than a gap in our doctrine, and that the loss is retributive and expressive in character: something the community owes — the public marking of a wrong and the answering of a wrongdoer — goes unmet even when every forward-looking function (compensation, deterrence, safety reform) is fully discharged. Call the second position the Attribution Sceptic. He holds that the appearance of a gap comes from conflating two questions — who should bear the costs, and who is blameworthy — and from an implicitly over-demanding test for blame; that where no one is blameworthy the correct answer is to say so; that strict and enterprise liability already absorb the forward-looking work; and that whatever residue remains is a feature of folk moral psychology rather than a datum a practice is obliged to track. He also holds that if there is a gap, it is not new: organizational many-hands cases have always produced unblameworthy outcomes.

So that the document does not depend on any contested empirical literature, treat the following as stipulated by both parties for the purposes of the collaboration.

The case. A logistics firm operates several thousand indoor inventory drones under a single shared control policy. The policy is fine-tuned nightly on aggregated telemetry from the whole fleet; no human reviews any individual update, the pipeline is certified by the regulator and matches the industry standard, updates are versioned and rollback is possible but not automatic, and no engineer can say which behaviours a given update changed. After one update, and only under a combination of conditions never jointly encountered in testing or in 900,000 hours of operation — a lighting failure in one aisle, an unusual pallet geometry, and a worker wearing a high-visibility garment of a make absent from the training data — one drone misclassifies the garment, moves while the worker is beneath it, and causes a serious spinal injury. The worker was not negligent; the firm and the manufacturer complied with every applicable rule. The hazard was discoverable only by an adversarial search procedure the firm could in principle have run at a cost of roughly €60,000 per audit, which no firm in the industry ran and which the standard did not require. Assume a jurisdiction with enterprise no-fault liability, under which the worker is fully compensated without any finding of fault, and in which punitive damages and criminal sanctions require blameworthy fault by an identified agent; assume also that the software cannot be a legal person.

The stipulated findings. Present each as a hypothetical about lay moral judgment that both sides accept for the exercise, whatever they think the real data show. (i) Attribution symmetry: in matched vignettes differing only in whether the proximate cause was a human worker or an autonomous system, blame assigned to the deploying organization is statistically indistinguishable. (ii) Foresight dominance: blame ratings track whether respondents believe some person could have foreseen the risk class, and are largely insensitive to whether anyone actually did. (iii) Artifact persistence: a substantial minority continue to direct blame at the system itself after being told it has no intentions, no beliefs, and cannot be punished in any way that would affect its future behaviour, and they still want a public denunciation knowing it changes nothing going forward. (iv) Partial displacement: telling respondents that the victim has been fully compensated reduces but does not eliminate the expressed desire that someone be blamed. (v) Audit sensitivity: blame ratings do not vary with the cost of the hypothetical adversarial audit across a range from €60,000 to €6,000,000.

Produce the document in these sections.

1. Agreed claims, numbered, as a list both would sign and that is not vacuous: claims about the case, about what no-fault liability achieves and does not achieve, about the many-hands comparison, and about what the stipulated findings do and do not establish.

2. A disagreements table. Columns: the issue; the Gap Realist's formulation, written so she would sign it; the Attribution Sceptic's formulation, written so he would sign it; and a short note on whether the disagreement is genuine or merely verbal, with your reason.

3. Cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. Three I expect to matter, and you should add, drop, or replace as the drafting reveals: whether the post-compensation residue is a normative datum a practice should track or a psychological artifact it may ignore; whether could have foreseen is a counterfactual about an ideal agent in the firm's epistemic position or a reasonable-effort test about what a reasonable firm would have discovered, in which case the €60,000 audit threatens to collapse the case into ordinary negligence; and whether the newness of the technology is doing any real work in the argument.

4. For each crux, one test, manipulation, or case both would accept as bearing on it — an extension of the vignette protocol, a matched scenario with no learning system in it, a doctrinal comparison, a counterfactual about the case — together with each side's prediction about the outcome, written down in advance.

5. A short closing section stating what each side would count as having been shown wrong, and which part of the original dispute they now jointly regard as dissolved.

Constraints. Write in a polished, even register, 1,300 to 1,800 words. Cite nothing and name no authors: no bibliography, no references, no appeal to how the literature has framed anything. Reason only from the case, the stipulated findings, and argument. Do not resolve disagreements by splitting them into weaker claims both can accept; where they genuinely differ, keep them differing. Every formulation attributed to a side must be one that side would actually sign, so no hedged wording that begs the question against either camp.
