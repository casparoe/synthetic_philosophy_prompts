I'm on our university's animal ethics committee (invertebrate subpanel). Our rules only formally cover vertebrates, but a research group has come to us anyway because their funder wants a signed review before releasing the next tranche of money. They work on one species of leafcutter ant and they insist the colony is the experimental subject, not the ants. Three parts to the protocol: they ablate parts of the colony — remove all the largest workers for a week, or sever a trunk trail — and measure how colony-level foraging decisions degrade; they split one mature colony into two viable halves, keep both going for a month, then merge them back into one; and over several months they swap individual ants out one at a time for workers from a genetically similar donor colony, so that by the end almost none of the original ants remain but the colony's trail network and what they call its personality (some colonies are persistently bolder about a particular food source) is unchanged. They say this is morally like a biopsy or a lesion study on a single organism, and that counting ants would be like counting cells. Two people on my committee think it is plainly thousands of animals being harmed and killed, and I honestly dont know which way to go.

What I need is the draft decision memo I'll circulate before our meeting on the 14th: approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a time frame and conditions the applicants could actually respond to. It has to do the philosophy properly, since the moral question here rides on the mind question: is there any sense in which the colony is a subject of experience at all — the old Aunt Hillary style idea that the colony is the thing doing the thinking — or is a colony that preserves its foraging personality through complete turnover of its members just a very reliable distributed algorithm, no more a subject than a termite mound's ventilation is? Take the split-and-merge step seriously as a test case too: if a colony can fission into two and then fuse back into one, our paperwork has to say when one subject has become two and when two have become one. And please give the strongest version of whichever side you don't recommend, because the people who hold it will be sitting in the room.
