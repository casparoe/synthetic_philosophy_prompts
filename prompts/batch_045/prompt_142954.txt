I am designing a variant of AI safety via debate that I will call budgeted cross-examination, and I would like to check my reasoning about it. A weak judge (a human, or a small model) has to settle a hard question that it could not answer on its own — say, whether a very large codebase contains a subtle security flaw, or which of two medical treatments is better supported by a messy literature. Two instances of a much stronger model argue the opposing sides in a long back-and-forth, but the judge's attention is capped: after reading only the two opening statements, it may inspect at most five individual claims from the transcript, where each inspected claim is a single sentence that the debater who made it is then stuck with. The debaters know the cap in advance but not which claims will be inspected. My worry is this: because the judge reads only five sentences however long the exchange runs, the protocol can carry at most five sentences' worth of information from the strong models to the judge, so the debaters' extra capability is wasted and a long debate is no better than a short one. If that is right, wouldn't it be simpler to drop the debate and just have the strong model write the five-sentence summary it expects to be most useful, with the judge reading only that? Where, if anywhere, does this argument go wrong?
