Hey — I'm putting together a reading-group session on promising and I want to stop two people in it from re-litigating the same ground for the fourth week. One of them is basically in the practice camp: the obligation to keep a promise is laid down by a social practice with constitutive rules, so there isn't really anything to promise outside of something like that practice (the Hume-on-conventions / Rawls-on-practices lineage). The other is in the assurance camp: what promising does is hand the promisee a distinctive kind of assurance and control over whether they form an expectation, and the wrong of breaking a promise is a wrong about that, not about a rule. Both of them are sick of me saying they secretly agree, so I want something better than that.

Could you draft the joint statement they'd sign after an actual adversarial collaboration? I'd like:

- a numbered section of claims both can sign. I'd guess they can agree that promissory obligation is pro tanto rather than absolute, that it isn't just a device for producing good consequences, that it survives cases where breaking would be better overall, that some kind of uptake matters, and that it isn't simply the general duty not to harm or disappoint someone — but you decide what actually belongs there, and don't pad the list with things that are true of every norm whatsoever.
- then a table of the remaining disagreements, each stated twice: once in the wording the practice camp would accept for its own view, once in the wording the assurance camp would accept for its own, plus a column marking whether you think the disagreement is genuine or just about words.
- a cruxes section: the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one side. Mine are roughly: (a) whether promising could get off the ground in a one-off exchange between two people with no shared practice behind them; (b) whether the promisee's release extinguishes the obligation or merely waives their complaint; (c) whether a promise nobody could ever detect your breaking — you promise a dying woman you'll burn her letters, then don't, and no one finds out — generates a reason at all; (d) whether the obligation is directed, in the sense that only the promisee can lift it; (e) whether the apparent content-independence of the reason is best explained by the rules of a practice or by the promisor's control over what the promisee expects.
- for each crux, one concrete case or argument both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Please invent the cases rather than reaching for the famous ones — I want setups where neither of them has a stock answer memorized. Two constraints: include at least one case where the two stories come apart because the promisee is maneuvered into an expectation with no promissory act anywhere (I've been calling this the eavesdropper case, but use whatever version is cleaner), and at least one where the practice is real but morally infected, e.g. a promise made inside a group whose promissory practice is tangled up with something rotten.
- if you conclude that some chunk of the dispute is verbal, say which chunk and why, and what the substantive remainder actually is.

At the end add a short drafter's note from you: which single claim in the agreed section you think one of them would in fact refuse to sign, and what argument or kind of case would make you withdraw it; and where the document is most likely to be a fake compromise, a formulation both can sign only because its empty. I'd rather have that flagged than a tidy ending.
