I run a small interdisciplinary reading group — two chemists, a cell biologist, someone from an ML lab, and me (philosophy) — and we keep talking past each other about natural kinds, because each of us has a different case in mind that we treat as the one an account obviously has to get right. So before we argue about which account to read, I want the specification, and I want it written for a stated purpose: an account good enough to underwrite induction and explanation inside a science that is still in motion, rather than one whose job is simply to classify water, gold and tigers and to dispatch Twin Earth. Give me the desiderata — the phenomena the account must explain, the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it must not collapse — with the case or consideration behind each requirement. I have some of the cases in view already and want your judgment on whether each is really a constraint or only a prejudice of one camp: why jade (jadeite or nephrite) is not a kind while gold and water apparently are, and whether that verdict should track anything beyond what chemistry presently cares about; what to do with kinds that are riddled with exceptions and have no single underlying essence, such as species and cell types; how projectibility is supposed to be explained without circularity, i.e. without saying both that kinds are the properties that license induction and that induction over kinds is licensed; whether the joints are uniquely located, given that the same domain often admits several incompatible carvings at different scales or from the perspective of different sciences, each of them predictively fine; how to treat kinds that were empirically successful and then retired (phlogiston, caloric) as opposed to ones that survived theory change (gold); whether there is a distinction between being a kind and being a good one, so that kindhood can come in degrees; and whether the account may posit essences that biology contradicts (species as individuals, horizontal gene transfer, ring species) or microstructural definitions that a chemical kind's multiple realizability would break.

The case I most want handled, though, is one that none of the standard readings seem designed for and that comes up constantly in my group: what I'll call pre-mechanistic kinds. These are clusters identified in high-dimensional data before anyone knows the mechanism — cell types recovered by clustering single-cell transcriptomic data, or candidate phases of matter flagged by an unsupervised model trained on simulation output. They are robust within the distribution they were found in, they carry real predictions there, they often get a name and enter the literature, and then some of them dissolve under a change of preprocessing or laboratory protocol while others go on to receive a mechanistic explanation and a causal profile. Two further wrinkles: the clustering geometry can be partly an artifact of the training objective rather than a feature of the stuff being clustered, and a gerrymandered disjunctive class (the analogue of grue) can be just as stable in-distribution as a genuine kind. So tell me what an account must say about these: whether statistical robustness can ever be the whole story of kindhood, whether a mechanism or causal structure is required, whether a cluster can be a kind provisionally and then be demoted, and whether the account should treat the ML case as continuous with older cases of taxonomy-before-theory or as a genuinely new form of the problem.

Two structural things matter more to me than coverage. First, mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps, and identify any subset of the desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — if the honest answer is that no single account can have everything on the list, say which requirement you would drop and why, since a list that turns out to be inconsistent is itself the interesting result. Second, tell me which single requirement does the most work in excluding otherwise popular candidates. You may then score, briefly and in a short table, the main contenders — microstructural essentialism in the Kripke–Putnam vein, homeostatic property cluster accounts, pluralist or promiscuous views that allow many cross-cutting carvings, and something like a predictive-cluster view suited to the ML cases — but keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scoring. Close by telling me what would change your mind about your own list: which empirical finding or which argument would make you add or drop a requirement, and where your reasoning here is most likely to be going wrong.
