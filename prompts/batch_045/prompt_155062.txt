Every third Tuesday I run a "big ideas" night in the back room of a pub — about 14 regulars, almost none of them philosophers: a vet, two software devs, a nurse, a beekeeper, a retired civil engineer, a couple of teachers, a few who just wander in. I get 90 minutes, a flipchart and some marker pens, no projector, no budget, and they will sit still for maybe ten minutes of me talking across the whole evening, so anything that looks like a lecture dies on contact. This month I want to do the thing I've been chewing on for weeks: my brother-in-law works at a food-ingredients company on yeast they've engineered to pump out some vanilla-ish compound, and he said about the spliced-in pathway, "well, it doesn't have a function for the yeast." That stuck in my head, because the pathway obviously *does* something — it makes the compound, it burns resources, it changes how the cell grows — so what could "has no function" even mean? Reading around on my own, it looks like there are two ways people go: one says a trait's function is whatever it was shaped by selection to do, so no selection history means no function (and my brother-in-law is just speaking carefully); the other says a trait's function is whatever contribution it currently makes to keeping some containing system running, so history is beside the point and, awkwardly, the answer moves around depending on which system and which capacity you decide to analyse. I've seen these labelled something like the selected-effect and the causal-role account — the second one attached to a name I half remember, Cummins? — and I don't want either label, or any of that vocabulary, spoken aloud until the last five minutes. What I want from you is the evening itself: an activity or game where the group runs into the clash by doing something, not by me describing it. The shape I had in mind, which you should fix or replace if it's weak: invented organisms on cards, each card split into a "history" half (what the ancestors faced, which variants out-reproduced which) and a "mechanism" half (what the part does now, what it contributes to), with people paired up and each partner getting only one half before they commit to a written function attribution; then cases arranged so that the two halves agree at first, then diverge (history points one way, current mechanism another), then one where history is simply absent — a mutation that arose in this very individual, or a pathway spliced in by someone in a lab — then one where it's genuinely unclear which system is the containing one at all (a gut microbe whose trait helps its host, or a colony where the benefit shows up at the group level), and finally one where two groups pick different containing capacities for the same trait and come back with different functions, which is the bit where I hope the interest-relativity of the second account becomes slightly embarrassing rather than a technical footnote. There should be small real stakes — chips, coins, something they can lose — so that committing to an attribution costs something, and the rules should make it definately possible to be wrong in a way they can see. Please don't give me a list of examples to discuss and vote on; we already do that and it turns into people asserting. Give me: the setup and materials, the phases with timings, the actual dossier contents for each case, the stakes mechanic, a debrief that maps specific moments in the room onto each of the two ways of thinking (in plain words, and with the lines I should actually say), an honest list of what will go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and which feature of the whole dispute the activity simply cannot produce and that I'll have to state myself at the end, plus the one sentence in which I finally name the two views.

After the prose, give me a single JSON object with exactly these fields: "room_setup" (string), "materials" (array of strings), "phases" (array of objects with keys "name", "minutes", "participant_instructions", "what_they_run_into"), "dossiers" (array of objects with keys "case_id", "organism", "trait", "history_card", "mechanism_card", "clash_type" — one of "baseline", "divergence", "no_history", "contested_system", "interest_relative" — and "predicted_split"), "stakes_mechanism" (string), "debrief_map" (array of objects with keys "moment", "point_in_plain_language", "facilitator_line"), "failure_modes" (array of objects with keys "what_goes_wrong", "why", "fix"), "activity_cannot_deliver" (array of strings), "success_signs" (array of strings, observable things in the room), "jargon_reveal" (string).
