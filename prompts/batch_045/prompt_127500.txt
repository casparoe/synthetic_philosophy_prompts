I'm a fourth-year PhD student in philosophy writing on multiple realizability, and I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Please don't give me a literature survey, a reading list, or citations: I have those. What I need is an account of what kind of disagreement this is, what each side is actually committed to, and what would count as progress given that account.

Here is the stall as I experience it. The anti-reductionist argument — psychological kinds are multiply realized, so they are not identical to neural kinds, so the special sciences are autonomous in a way that resists reduction — and the reductionist replies — that multiple realization is compatible with token identity, that it merely reflects our coarse-grained explanatory interests, that it dissolves once you say precisely what sameness of function and difference of structure are supposed to amount to, or that the cost of taking it seriously is causal exclusion — have both been fully on the table for decades. Every move and its reply are known in advance. People who have read the same papers end up on opposite sides and stay there. What I find most telling is that the recent empirically informed wave of work has not moved anyone either: neural reuse, degenerate implementations of the same capacity across individuals and species, lesion and stimulation data suggesting that the mapping from structure to function is looser than the identity theorist wanted. Data that looks to me like exactly the sort of evidence the debate ought to have been waiting for has had no dial effect on it whatsoever.

My hypothesis, which I'd like you to test rather than ratify, is that "multiple realizability" names at least three claims with different logics, and that the debate persists because the argument needs all three while each side can always retreat to whichever one is currently unthreatened. Call them diversity: an actual, measurable fact about how many distinct physical kinds realize a given psychological kind in the actual world; tolerance: a modal or counterfactual fact about how much the physical story could be perturbed while the psychological state is preserved; and grain: a fact about which physical kind-descriptions we should be using when we count realizers as the same or different. These come apart in principle — a kind could be instantiated by a single actual realizer while being highly tolerant of perturbation, or realized by many actual realizers while being brittle under counterfactual intervention — and I suspect the reductionist happily concedes diversity, treats grain as interest-relative, and yet the anti-reductionist keeps getting dialectical mileage out of the package. Alongside this I keep running into what I privately call the grain ratchet: for any two candidate realizers, one can coarse-grain the description until they are the same kind or fine-grain it until they are different, so unless there is a non-question-begging stopping point the thesis risks being true or false by stipulation.

The case I most want you to engage is artificial neural networks, which I think is the sharpest available test of the "hidden empirical question" diagnosis. Independently trained networks often arrive at approximately the same input–output function through internal solutions that are not isomorphic; but they also differ by mere permutation of units, which is arguably not genuine multiple realization at all, and there is work suggesting that differently initialized networks trained on comparable data converge on more similar internal representations than chance, which pulls the other way. So even the apparently decisive empirical evidence seems to reproduce the same three-way ambiguity — same function by whose criterion, different structure at what grain, and which counterfactuals do we count — rather than settling it.

So: is this a verbal dispute, a substantive one that is well posed but hard, an ill-posed question, or the causal exclusion problem wearing a different hat? Which of the three claims is actually load-bearing in the anti-reductionist argument, and can tolerance survive the standard moves against it (redescribing the realizer as a disjunction, the epiphenomenalism worry, the charge that counterfactual-support is itself just whatever our best taxonomy happens to track)? And given your diagnosis, what would progress look like — a principled grain-fixing criterion, a perturbation-based measure of realization-tolerance applicable to both biological and artificial systems, a formal result about whether tolerance and diversity must co-vary, or an argument that the question should be dissolved rather than answered? If your answer is that no progress should be expected, I want the reason why, stated so that it bites against my own three-way distinction.
