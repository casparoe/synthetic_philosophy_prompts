I write the user-facing emails and in-app messages for a mid-size health insurer — the boring, high-stakes ones: your claim has been approved; your coverage ends on the 15th; were sorry for the delay; someone from our team will call you within two business days. A few dozen of these ship every release, I get maybe ten minutes per message, and anything that could be read as a promise, a waiver, a warning or a refusal has to be signed off by legal, so I need to be able to say in advance which lines are actually doing something and which just report something.

Can you write me a procedure my team can run line by line? Ground it in the speech act stuff — Austins felicity conditions, the difference between a misfire where the act doesnt happen at all and an abuse where it happens but is hollow, illocution versus perlocution, indirect speech acts — but it has to be usable by someone whos never heard any of that. I want: the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, the point where it stops with a no and sends the line to legal, and the point where it has to stop and hand the call to a person because its a values question not a facts question. Also tell me which bits of the theory you had to drop to make it usable and which you refused to drop.

Two things we keep tripping over that the procedure has to handle. First, weve started calling some of these shadow performatives: sentences with the grammar of a plain report that do the work of an act, like your complaint has been logged said at the moment the caseworker logs it, or I hear that youre upset functioning as a brush-off. When is that fine and when is it evasive, and how do I tell the difference without asking the writer what they meant? Second, sentences built to be deniable: Im not saying we wont cover this, just that well need more documentation.

Then run it on three of our actual lines: your information is safe with us; this decision may be appealed within 180 days; weve noted your concern about how the adjuster spoke to you. Tell me which one comes out worst for us and why, and be straight about where a procedure like this is just going to misfire given ten minutes and no training.
