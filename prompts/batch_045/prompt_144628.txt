Hey, I'v been reading about Gödel's modal ontological argument for fun, and I keep getting stuck on the modal collapse objection. As I understand it, the argument defines a Godlike being as one that has every positive property, and then proves that such a being exists necessarily. The objection I ran into goes like this. Take any random contingent proposition, say the proposition that the Cleveland Browns win the 2045 Super Bowl. Let B be the property of being such that the Browns win the 2045 Super Bowl. In the logic, for every property either it or its negation is positive. So either B is positive or not-B is positive. If B is positive, then the necessarily existing God has B in every possible world, so it is necessary that the Browns win; if not-B is positive, it is necessary that they dont. Either way, there's no contingency left. Since its obvously the Browns winning the 2045 Super Bowl is about as contingent as it gets, the argument must be unsound.

Here's where I think the objection equivocates, and I'd like you to tell me if I'm the one who's confused. The whole point of calling a property positive in this context is that it means something like great-making or perfective. The property of the Browns winning the 2045 Super Bowl is not great-making at all—its a dumb fact about sports—and neither is its negation, since the Browns losing is also not a perfective feature of anything. So neither B nor not-B is positive. The axiom that says for every property either it or its negation is positive can't really be meant to cover junk properties like B; it must only be meant to apply to properties that are candidates for perfection. If we just read positive as great-making and restrict the axioms to those, the modal collapse objection never gets off the ground, and the argument is fine. My friends say this is a bad misreading of Gödel's axioms, but I dont see why. Can't the defender just say that positive means great-making, so dumb properties like B are neither positive nor negative in the relevant sense, and the objection fails?
