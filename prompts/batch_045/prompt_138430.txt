hey — I need a satirical internal memo, around 600 words, from the People Operations team of a mid-sized tech company announcing that the company has abolished discipline: no managers, no rules, no performance reviews, no surveillance, nothing prohibiting anything at all.

The catch is that what replaces it is a total apparatus. Everyone's work sits on an open ledger visible to everyone all the time. There's a weekly thing called avowal hour where each person posts a short written self-critique. And every Friday a coherence score ranks everyone against a curve whose formula nobody has ever been shown — though everyone insists the score is purely descriptive, since there is no longer anything to punish anyone for.

The jokes have to come from actually getting the theory right rather than from inventing a cartoon version of it: power as productive rather than repressive, visibility as a trap you walk into on your own, normalising judgment and the exam, the confession as a technique that manufactures the very interiority it claims only to record, knowledge and power being one operation, and the way resistance gets built in (there's a channel for pushing back, people are encouraged to use it) and ends up feeding the apparatus. Someone who has read Discipline and Punish should feel recognised rather than strawmaned; the target is the sincere, empowering, flat-hierarchy version of workplace discipline, not discipline as such.

Keep it deadpan — no winking, and nobody in the memo ever says panopticon or Foucault. It should still be readable and funny for someone who's never heard of any of this. Maybe have the memo mention cheerily that the old central monitoring setup was dismantled last spring and no one now remembers who removed it or who asked them to.
