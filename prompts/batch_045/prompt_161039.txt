I'm taking an undergraduate seminar on animal ethics, and I keep encountering the same maneuver in readings and discussion without having a word for it.

It runs like this. Someone grants up front that species membership as such cannot be what matters morally, and adds that they would extend full consideration to any non-human being that had the right properties. They then defend some practice toward animals by citing a property that is facially species-neutral: being a moral agent, being capable of reciprocity, standing in relations of mutual dependence with us, or occupying a role in our ecological community. Nearly all adult humans have the property; the animals in question do not. Asked about humans who lack it, they add that those humans belong to a kind whose normal members have it. Asked whether a being of another species that genuinely did have it would receive full status, they treat the question as science fiction and set it aside. I have watched exactly this happen in the debate over eating animals and again in a reading on urban pest control, where the property is assumed of the kind rather than checked of the individual animal.

Speciesism seems to be the wrong name. As I have met it, speciesism is differentiating by species, and these people sincerely disavow that, and their marker could in principle be possessed by a non-human. But the marker operates as a bar that animals must clear and only ever as a sufficient, never a necessary, condition for humans, so the inclusive clause and the exclusive clause are never tested against each other.

Is there an established concept for precisely this configuration? If it is speciesism after all, which formulation covers a case where the agent disavows species and the marker is in principle species-neutral? If it is not, I would like a name together with a definition that settles the boundary cases: a being of another species that really has the property; a human who never has and never will; and a property that matters independently but happens to track species for nobody's chosen reason. A label on its own will not help me — I want to know where the phenomenon stops.

Please answer in 400 words or fewer.
