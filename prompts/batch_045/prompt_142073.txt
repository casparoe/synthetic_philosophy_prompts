Sam is what I'd call a content realist: trained networks literally represent things; a direction or a 'feature' has the content it has in virtue of what it tracks and what it's used for downstream, and interpretability is discovery, more or less like vision neuroscience discovering cells that respond to oriented edges. Interpretation can be wrong, not just useless.

Nour is an interpretive instrumentalist: representational talk is a tool. A decomposition earns its keep by letting us predict and control the network, and once you've said that you've said everything — there is no further fact of the matter about what the network 'really' represents, and no sense in which one decomposition is the true one and the others merely convenient. Interpretation can be useless, but not wrong.

Here is the case they keep circling, and I want you to use it as the spine of the document. It's hypothetical but I think its the kind of thing that could actually happen.

A lab trains a few dozen small transformers from different seeds on the same synthetic task: next-token prediction on strings from a bracket language with several bracket types and a cap on nesting depth. In every model they find a direction in the residual stream that behaves exactly like you'd want a real feature to behave: patched from a run where the model got the close-bracket right into a run where it got it wrong, it fixes the behaviour; it survives fine-tuning on unrelated tasks; it shows up in every seed; and it keeps working when you test on strings nested deeper than anything in training. So far so good for Sam.

Then the wrinkle. At higher dictionary resolution the single direction doesn't split into cleaner parts, it dissolves into a low-dimensional subspace: any basis inside that subspace supports the interventions equally well, and the one human-legible axis the researchers first wrote down is just one of infinitely many. Below that, there are two different carvings of the same subspace, each of which a human would happily describe as 'the' natural one, each supported by equally good interventions, and nothing in the architecture, the loss, the data or the training dynamics picks between them. On top of that, the layer that consumes the subspace doesn't read out either human-legible axis — it reads out something skew to both.
