Write a dialogue, 800 words or so, between two metaphysicians arguing over a made-up counterexample, and make sure neither of them is a pushover.

Nell defends a patched-up conditional analysis: roughly, x is disposed to M in conditions S iff, with all interferers removed, x would M were S to obtain. Ravi thinks dispositions are intrinsic properties of the thing that has them, and that no conditional analysis can be salvaged — the conditionals are at best evidence about the dispositions, never what the dispositions consist in.

The case: a museum keeps two vases on Guardians. Vera is genuinely fragile, but her Guardian reads her microstructure, registers the fragility, and hardens her the instant an impact begins, so she never shatters, and after the first near-miss she isnt fragile any more. Vern isnt fragile at all, but his Guardian reads for sturdiness and, on detecting an impact, makes him fragile and shatters him. So the naive conditionals come out exactly backwards: Vera wouldnt break if struck, Vern would.

Have them really fight about it. Nell has to say what counts as an interferer without building the disposition shes analysing into the definition of an interferer, and Ravi should press exactly there, because each Guardian only fires when it detects the very disposition at issue. Ravi in turn has to answer how we ever find out what an objects dispositions are if conditionals are only evidence. Let them also disagree about whether Vera's Guardian is a fink (it takes the disposition away) or a mask (it just blocks the manifestation), and whether you can tell those apart without already knowing she was fragile. They should end up still disagreeing but clearer about where the disagreement actually sits.
