I want you to build me a case where two sides with genuinely conflicting values both end up better off — better off by their own lights, not by mine and not by some neutral standard — *because* they compromised. The structure I'm after is the moral analogue of two kids trading lunch items: you don't want my apple, I don't want your chips, and the swap leaves us both happier than either keeping their own lunch. I want that shape, but where what's being traded is something each side actually cares about morally.

Constraints, and I mean them:

1. One paragraph, realistic setting. A city council, a hospital board, a university department, a family deciding something — no sci-fi, no constitutional conventions, no imaginary island.
2. They have to disagree about more than weights. At least one thing one side counts as a genuine benefit, the other side has to count as worthless or as a cost. If they just disagree about how much things matter, the case is too easy; I want incommensurability in there somewhere.
3. Nobody changes their mind, nobody learns anything, nobody discovers the other side was secretly right. Values stay fixed. The improvement has to come entirely from the structure of the deal.
4. They can't go their separate ways — they're jointly deciding one indivisible thing, so exit isn't on the table. That's what forces compromise instead of separation.
5. The deal has to beat a stated baseline, and you have to say what the baseline is. My suggestion: the realistic status quo is that one side wins and sets policy alone, and it's a coin flip which. So the comparison is compromise vs. lottery, not compromise vs. my side getting everything. A compromise is never going to beat that second thing, and I dont want you quietly swapping in an easy baseline to make the case look better than it is. Also, each side's concession has to be a real cost by their own lights — if it costs them nothing, you haven't built a compromise, just a coordination.
6. And the deal must not be a blend. By that I mean: splitting the difference — half a bike lane and half a parking lot, everyone sort of half-content — is the boring case, and it's usually worse for both sides than a trade would be. I want a *swap*: each side gets its own top priority more or less fully and concedes more or less fully on the axis it cares about less. Say explicitly which axis each side gives up and why that costs them less than what they get.
7. Here's the wrinkle I care most about. One side — your pick — has to have a side-constraint on its own conduct: there's something it won't do *itself*, even to advance its own goals, though it might be willing to let happen. Build the deal so that what that side needs is delivered by the other side's action rather than its own, so the constraint is never breached. Then tell me what happens if we reinterpret the constraint as also covering what that side permits or funds: does the deal collapse, or is there a workaround?

After the scenario:

- give each side a rough ranking of the outcomes in play (their own ideal, the other side's ideal, the coin flip, the deal), so I can see the deal really is preferred by both and not just by someone splitting the difference between them;
- point at the single feature of the case doing the work — the bit that, removed, leaves no gain from trade at all — and show that removing it does collapse things;
- then let a hardliner on one side attack it, and not a strawman. The serious objections are that the deal is a kind of moral blackmail (I'll let you do what's right only if you let me do what I think is wrong) or that accepting it expresses or legitimizes the wrong value even though it produces better outcomes. Give the strongest version, then tell me whether it lands and why, and commit — don't end with "reasonable people can disagree."

Two last things. I'm making up "blend" and "swap" as labels; don't go looking for them in the literature. And don't reach for an issue where the compromise is a famous real-world one — no abortion, no Israel/Palestine, nothing where you'd just be describing a settlement that already exists. I want the mechanism, not the memory.
