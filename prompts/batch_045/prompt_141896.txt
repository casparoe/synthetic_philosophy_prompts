The situation: I'm giving a paper in about ten days at a joint philosophy of biology / philosophy of mind seminar at a UK department. Working title is something like "Turing's inversion is Darwin's inversion, one level up."

Here's the actual thesis, in three moves, so you can aim the objections at something real:

1. Darwin's strange inversion is etiological. It flips the order of explanation: instead of comprehension/design/foresight first and adaptive competence as the result, you get mindless competence first and comprehension afterwards — and the comprehension is ours, the theorists', not the process's. That's the core of Darwin's Dangerous Idea (1995) as I'm reading it: design without a designer, and no skyhooks.

2. Turing's inversion, in the 1950 imitation game paper, is a different kind of move: it's evidential, not etiological. It doesn't tell you how a mechanism came to be a competent interlocutor; it says competent behaviour is our warrant for attributing comprehension at all. And my claim is that this move only has any force against the background of a Darwin-style selectionist story — some story about how something could get to be competent in a domain without anyone putting comprehension in. Strip that out and the test collapses into behaviourism with extra steps. So: Turing's inversion isn't a parallel to Darwin's, it's Darwin's inversion applied to the space of mind-designs. That's the load-bearing bit.

3. The bit I think will actually get me killed: because the inversion is evidential rather than etiological, it's self-consuming in a way Darwin's isn't. Once competent performance is your evidence for comprehension, any process under selection for passing the test — evolution, gradient descent, a cramming student, a bureaucracy — will give you competence-at-the-test without comprehension-of-the-domain, and the inversion gives you no internal way to say no to that. I've been calling the resulting exposure "comprehension debt": the liability you take on when a system's competence rests on regularities that nobody has articulated and nobody is tracking. It's invisible while the niche is stable and it comes due under distribution shift. On this picture comprehension isn't the cause of competence (contra the pre-Darwinian picture) and it isn't just a confabulation we tell afterwards (contra the harsh Dennett-ish line) — it's insurance, priced by how fast the niche is changing. Slogan: competence is cheap, comprehension is a hedge.
