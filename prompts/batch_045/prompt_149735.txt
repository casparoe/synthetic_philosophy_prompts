I've been trying to cash out my position on working time — the 35-hour week, hustle culture, karōshi — before I write it up, and I'd like an audit of whether the package holds together. I take the standard line that hours are a Moloch-ish coordination problem and think it's basically right, but I'm no longer sure it's compatible with the rest of what I believe. Where I've landed:

(1) Hours aren't a taste, they're a coordination problem. Employers read long hours as a signal of commitment, so no individual can cut back unilaterally without being penalised against their peers, and a legal cap on the working week can leave nearly everyone better off. That isn't objectionable paternalism.
(2) The work ethic — effort as a virtue in itself, idleness as vaguely shameful — is moral superstition. Leisure isn't a lower form of life than work.
(3) Adults should be free to trade hours for money. If a colleague genuinely loves the job and would rather do seventy hours than forty, it's none of the state's business to stop them, and I don't think we should sneer at them either.
(4) Karōshi is a genuine moral scandal, and the blame attaches substantially to employers and to the working cultures they build, not simply to the choices of the people who died.
(5) I'm far more bothered by a cleaner working three jobs than by a founder working eighty, even though the founder does more of it. As I see it the difference is voluntariness, not the hours.
(6) Given the choice, I'd rather productivity growth were taken in time than in consumption.

I can't locate what's wrong here, if anything is. It feels as though (1) and (4) might quietly commit me to abandoning (3), as though (3) and (5) might pull against each other, and as though (2) may be doing no real work at all — or may actually be in tension with (6). Which of these are genuine conflicts and which only look like them? For each genuine one, what would have to give, and is there a single revision that would clean up the most? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and don't assume I already know which commitment I'd be willing to drop. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. British English throughout.
