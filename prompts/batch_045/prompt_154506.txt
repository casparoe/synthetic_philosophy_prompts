I am writing the chapter of my dissertation on supererogation and I keep losing the thread of the dispute, so I want the document an adversarial collaboration would produce rather than another survey. The two parties: an optionalist, for whom the category is genuine — some acts are morally best, praiseworthy if done, and yet permissibly omitted, with no blameworthiness attaching to the omission — and an eliminativist, for whom every apparent case is explained away, either because the act was required after all and our reluctance to blame reflects an excuse or the limits of our practices, or because the verdict that the act is best is evaluative rather than deontic.

What I need, in this order:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims, i.e. the ground both can sign: what any account has to accommodate (the paradigm cases, the asymmetry between praise and blame, the point that beyond duty is not the same as morally indifferent), which arguments settle nothing, and which moves each side has given up.
2. A table of the residual disagreements, one row each, with every row phrased so that the side holding the view would accept the formulation of its own position and would not accept the other side's.
3. The cruxes, each with a concrete case both would accept as bearing on it and with each side's prediction about that case recorded in advance. My candidate cruxes: whether the class of permissibly omitted acts is closed under conjunction, tested by the iterated case in which an agent faces the same trivial-sacrifice opportunity on many occasions, the aggregate benefit of complying throughout is large, and every individual omission is blameless on the optionalist's own terms; whether a role or relationship (lifeguard, parent, promise-maker) generates new duties or only raises the cost an agent may fairly be asked to bear; and whether the absence of blame is evidence about deontic status at all or only about our blaming practices.
4. A short closing note on how much of the dispute is verbal, and in particular how much of it turns on whether duty is defined by what an agent may be blamed for omitting.

Do not supply citations; I will add the references myself.
