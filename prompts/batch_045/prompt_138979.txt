You are a professional philosopher who also writes humor for a satirical news outlet in the style of The Onion: deadpan local-news prose, invented residents quoted as though a real reporter had called them, absurdity delivered in the register of a zoning dispute.

Before drafting, spend a few lines you will not show in the final piece listing (a) the genuine features of acausal trade you intend to exaggerate, and (b) the ways this assignment could go wrong: the article turning into a lecture, the reporter winking at the reader, the jokes landing on "people are silly" rather than on the structure of the trade itself. Then write the piece.

Write an Onion-style news article, headline plus roughly 500 words, on the first-year audit of a small town's newly created Office of Counterfactual Reciprocity, charged with arranging trades with civilizations that may be simulating the town. Take the following as already in place, and work with it rather than explaining it: the council has published its own decision procedure so that any distant simulator can rely on how the town decides; the office keeps a public registry of the counterparties it has concluded it is correlated with; the town's newest trading partner is a civilization understood to value only the precise arrangement of the town's gravel driveways, which the town does not care about; and the council's stated aim is to be the kind of town that would one-box.

The comedy has to come from the mechanics, got right: that the trade runs on correlation rather than causation, so nothing has to travel; that there are no receipts and no way to confirm that any counterparty exists; that any benefit lands on whichever copy of the town was simulated rather than on the taxpayers funding the office; that a counterparty's model may be a model of the town as it was before a given vote, and so may reward a version of the town that no longer exists; and that the town has purchased predictability at the price of being unable to change its mind. Include three quotes: one from the town clerk, eager and confused about which copy of the town is being paid; one from a resident with a concrete, petty grievance; and one from a causal decision theorist in the regional university's economics department, objecting in their own vocabulary (interventions, causal structure, the absence of a path from here to there). No character may explain the theory straight, no one delivers the moral, and the reporter never signals that anything is funny.

Below the article, in your own voice, add at most 80 words noting which real feature of acausal trade three of the jokes exaggerate.
