I have recently taken over the job of drafting every probability statement my research group publishes, and my supervisor reads and can veto each one, so I am trying to reverse-engineer what she thinks a probability is before I send her a sentence next week.

Her verdicts so far. She accepts "the probability that this coin lands heads is 0.5" as objectively true, and says our confidence is answerable to it, not the reverse. She accepts "the probability that this particular uranium-238 atom decays within its 4.5-billion-year half-life is 0.5", and says it would still be true in a universe where that atom is the only token of its kind and repetition of the trial type is nomologically impossible. She accepts "the probability that the accused signed the will is 0.7" as a genuine objective probability, and denies that two people sharing all of their evidence can both be fully rational while assigning it 0.7 and 0.3. She accepts "the probability that the die landed six is 1/6" said after the throw and before anyone looks. But when I wrote "the probability that this patient has the disease is 0.02" using the base rate while the file also contained a positive test result, she struck it out: the number, she said, is answerable to the total evidence.

The case I now have to write up: a single unrepeatable run in which an engineered bacterial strain either does or does not acquire one specific point mutation through a spontaneous tautomeric shift during one replication. No comparable runs exist, the strain will be destroyed afterwards, and the relevant local chemistry is unique and unmeasurable in the time available, so I have neither a frequency nor a symmetry to appeal to — only a coarse mechanistic model. I still have to print a number and a sentence saying what it is a number of.

Predict her ruling. Set out the interpretations of probability that fit her five verdicts jointly — a single-case propensity or chance view and an evidence-relative evidential-probability view both look live to me — say what each would let me write about the mutation case and what each would forbid, give me your best guess with the reason for preferring it, name the further case I could put to her that would tell the two apart, and tell me what she would be committing herself to either way.
