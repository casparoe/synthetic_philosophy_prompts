Here's my opening. Every version of the defense I've heard gets tested the same way: you relax the false assumption, the prediction survives, so you conclude the assumption was only ever doing isolating work. But that test scores average error over some broad distribution. The models that matter to me are used to pick a policy, and what matters there is whether the output is right in the narrow band where the decision flips. Take a compartmental epidemic model that assumes homogeneous mixing, used to decide whether to lock down, where underestimating the peak is far worse than overshooting it. The false assumption biases the prediction in a known direction, and that direction is exactly where the cost asymmetry lives. Also, please don't just tell me to go use a better model — the question is what your methodology says about this one. So which is your actual claim: that assumptions don't need to be true, or that they don't need to be checked in the region where the model gets used?
