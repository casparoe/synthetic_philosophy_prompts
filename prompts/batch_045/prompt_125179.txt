I am a retired physical chemist — thirty-odd years, mostly molecular spectroscopy, later molecular dynamics simulation — and since retiring I have been attending a philosophy reading group at the local university, where we have been working through Dennett on the three stances (physical, design, intentional) and on real patterns. I have come to accept the package: that a pattern is real just in case there is a description of the data that compresses it, noise and error tolerated, so that realness is a matter of there being a description-length saving over the bit-map or atomistic description; that the beliefs and desires the intentional stance attributes are real in exactly that sense and in no stronger sense — no more and no less real than centres of gravity, or vortices, or a species; and that the three stances form a nested family of predictive strategies, each higher one buying tractability at the cost of risk, with the intentional stance as the top of the three for us. I like the view because it promises to turn a metaphysical quarrel into an empirical one, and because it fits how I actually worked: I spent a career coarse-graining, and I never thought the coarse-grained objects were thereby less real than the atoms. But the professor who runs the group says the view has been insulated into unfalsifiability, and I want to know what would have to be true for the package to be wrong, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and with a clear distinction between what a defender of this view would themselves concede as counting against it and what they would dismiss as changing the subject or as resting on a pre-theoretical intuition they explicitly reject.

Three things from my own tinkering that I cannot tell are defeaters or just details. Since retiring I have written some little simulations — a gridworld in which a handful of reinforcement learners forage — and (i) I can get a large description-length saving over the trajectory bit-map by attributing beliefs and desires to the learners and predicting by practical reasoning, but I can also get an equal saving from a codebook that is essentially a table of exceptions keyed to trajectory hashes, and I can inflate the saving of either scheme without limit just by loosening the tolerance for error; so I do not see how realness is supposed to come in anything but arbitrary degrees, or how a gerrymandered codebook is to be excluded in a principled rather than ad hoc way. (ii) The intentional-stance model that compresses beautifully in environment E collapses completely under a perturbation I would have called trivial — I move the food sources — while a much cruder design-stance model, which I had taken to be a mere approximation, extrapolates fine; I do not know whether the realist should say the beliefs were never real, or only real-for-E, and if the latter whether that is a retreat to instrumentalism about every pattern. (iii) The learners are systematically and predictably irrational — they discount hyperbolically and I predict them best by dropping rationality and attributing a discount function instead — and I cannot tell whether that is still the intentional stance, a fourth stance, or a sign that the intentional stance is really a family whose members have quite different commitments. Please say where each of these lands on your ranking, and add the defeaters I have not thought of. I am more moved by cases where the machinery delivers a verdict about a clear case (thermodynamics, a chess program, a human being) that we would not want to accept, or where it fails to be well defined, than by arguments from what is really fundamental; and I would also like you to say what the view predicts that its rivals do not, and to give an honest verdict on whether it has in fact been qualified into unfalsifiability — and, if it has, whether that is a defect in a view of this kind or simply the correct price of the metaphysical modesty it advertises.
