hey — im in this philosophy of physics seminar and my final paper is due in like two weeks and im going in circles on the probability problem in the many-worlds interpretation. You know the setup: the dynamics is deterministic and every outcome really happens on some branch, so the honest answer to "how likely am i to see spin-up" looks like it should be "1, some future copy of me sees it", and then probability — and the Born rule — have no work left to do. My professor says there are basically two families of response left once you put the decision-theoretic route aside, and she wants us to actually compare them rather than list them. (We did the Deutsch/Wallace decision-theory stuff in week three, so dont just re-explain that one, use it as background if it helps.)

First one: the self-locating uncertainty picture. Before the measurement you should have credences about which branch your future self is on, treat that exactly like ordinary uncertainty about where you are in a very big world, and then recover the squared-amplitude numbers from symmetry — the "split your credence equally between copies you have no way of telling apart" move, plus the requirement that this splitting doesn't get messed up by what happens in far-away or unmeasured parts of the system.

Second one is a thing my professor sketched in the last session and seems half in love with, she calls it the branch bargain. Roughly: stop trying to derive Born-rule credences at all. On this view the squared amplitudes tell you how much you should care about each future copy of you — the weights are a budget of concern, not a probability. Rationality only constrains that budget twice over: it has to add up to one across all the copies, and it has to be split equally between copies you cant tell apart. Everything else, including why the budget tracks the amplitudes in the first place, is something you negotiate with your own future selves — hence "bargain".

So please compare those two. The things im actually stuck on:

- Unequal amplitudes. Say they're something like the square roots of 0.9 and 0.1. Both stories owe me an account of why I should bet on the fat branch, and I want to know whether either of them explains why the weights are amplitudes squared rather than, say, amplitudes to the fourth. Which one gives the better answer here?

- And what does care have to do with betting anyway. If my caring budget is 0.9 on the fat branch, is there any sense in which it'd still be rational for me to act as if the probability were a half, or does the whole thing quietly collapse back into credences?
