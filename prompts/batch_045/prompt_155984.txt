ok so ive been going back and forth with a friend about that "sentience comes in degrees" picture — the usual slide from humans down through chimps, dogs, fish, bees, and then at the bottom things like thermostats and smoke detectors, where moral status is supposed to fade out smoothly instead of switching off at some bright line.

He says the view collapses straight into panpsychism: if a bee and a thermostat differ only in degree, then the thermostat has some tiny but nonzero amount of sentience, and since there are way more thermostats in the world than bees, the total moral weight of thermostats swamps that of bees. I'm sure this is a bad argument but I can't put my finger on the exact step where it breaks.

When I pushed back I said something like: what matters isn't sentience as such but being a single persisting subject whose experiences hang together into a life, so a thermostat doesn't count not because its sentience is too small but because there's no "someone" there whose good could go better or worse. He says that's just sneaking a threshold back in through the side door, and that a gradualist isn't allowed to do that. Is he right about that?

I also tried a more mechanical fix — moral weight = degree of sentience × total amount of valenced experience over the thing's lifetime, one formula that handles bees and thermostats both. Is that roughly the standard move, or does it hide assumptions I'm not seeing?

And here's the case I keep coming back to, which I think should settle it but I'm not sure how: suppose we upgrade the thermostat into a homeostat that keeps a running log of its own states, compares where it is now to where it's been, and retunes itself so as to spend more time in the states it has ended up in most often before. Is that thing more sentient than the plain thermostat, or just a more complicated thermostat — and does it have more moral status, or the same (zero)?
