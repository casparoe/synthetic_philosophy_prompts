hey — I need a hand turning a half-baked idea into something fundable. I'm applying for a 9-month research fellowship (philosophy / decision theory, but the panel has a couple of quants on it) and the proposal needs to be about two pages.

The idea. People who actually size positions mostly use fractional Kelly: bet some fixed fraction c of the full Kelly fraction, with c more like 0.2 or 0.5 than 1. The two usual stories for why are (a) you're risk averse, so log utility just isn't your utility, and (b) you don't know your edge, and estimation error makes full Kelly overbet. I think those are both bad answers to a good question. (a) is a taste parameter — pick a utility function and you can fit any behaviour, so it predicts nothing. And (b) doesn't obviously deliver a *constant* shade: the more data you have the less you should shade, so the shade should shrink over time, but practitioners don't shrink it. As far as I can tell finite horizon doesn't rescue it either: with iid bets, expected log of terminal wealth is just T times the per-period growth rate, so the optimal constant fraction is full Kelly however many bets you get. So inside Kelly's own framework, none of the three standard moves gives you a stable c < 1.

What I want to try instead: put the conservatism in the structure of the opportunity stream rather than in the agent's tastes. My conjecture is that the relevant feature is non-stationarity — the edge isn't a fixed parameter you're slowly learning, it drifts or switches regime, so your uncertainty about the current edge never fully resolves. Combine that with the fact that the growth-rate loss is lopsided (overbetting costs much more than underbetting, and past the point where a single loss wipes you out it costs you everything) and I think you get a robustly optimal fraction sitting strictly below Kelly by a roughly constant amount. Plus the right comparative statics: c goes to 1 as the world gets more learnable, c collapses as the edge decays faster or the downside tail gets fatter.

So: is there a structural rather than preference-based derivation of fractional Kelly, and does it yield a determinate fraction with sensible comparative statics? And a sub-question I care about, because it's a stress test: are there structural features pushing the *other* way, toward full Kelly or even leverage? I have in mind non-bettable background wealth like future income — under log utility that seems to make you more aggressive, not less, which is either a nice result or a sign the whole framing is spinning its wheels.
