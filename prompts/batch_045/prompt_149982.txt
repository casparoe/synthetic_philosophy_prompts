I got elected to the board of my housing co-op basically by defualt last month, and my punishment is chairing the committee that's meant to simplify our rulebook, which runs to a few hundred rules nobody remembers agreeing to. Every time I propose cutting one, the treasurer says Chesterton's fence — don't take a fence down until you know why it was put up — and that's the end of the discussion.

Concretely: we have a rule that the super has to log every repair by hand in a paper binder kept in the boiler room. I actually did the digging. A super back in the 80s started it because he didn't trust the property manager at the time and wanted his own record; that manager is long gone and so is he. Meanwhile the binder has started doing work nobody intended, which is that it's the only place recurring leaks show up as a pattern.

I want to bring a real menu to the next meeting rather than a vibe, so: what are the distinct precise claims that one-liner could be making — about reasons nobody knows, about reasons that are known and dead, about arrangements that were never put up for a reason at all, about irreversibility and how cheap a fence is to keep versus rebuild, about who carries the burden of proof, about knowledge baked into a practice that no one can state? Which of those are platitudes, which are genuinely substantive, and which are just false? Which readings would actually let the treasurer block me on the binder, and which would let me delete it? And what do I say to the guy who keeps insisting this is really Burke — that inherited arrangements keep a presumption in their favour even after you've fully explained how they came about? My worry is that it's an unfalsifiable ratchet: whatever I dig up, the answer is keep it.
