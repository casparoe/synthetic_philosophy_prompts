Ok so I'm in this cross-listed seminar on quantum mechanics and personal identity (mostly physics people, two philosophy PhD students, one guy who only talks about Parfit) and my final paper is due friday, and I think I've basically found a way to turn last week's reading into something useful, but when I described it to my TA after section she looked kind of horrified and told me to "run it past someone who'll actually argue back" so here I am.

The setup we did: a device wired to a radioactive atom, one trigger pull per round, and on the many-worlds picture there's always going to be some branch where the atom doesn't decay and you go on being conscious. So from the inside you never experience the bad outcome — you just keep going, and the branches where you stop are ones you by definition never find yourself in. I'm fine on that part, I'm not asking about the branching.

What I want to do with it: swap the lethal part for a bank transfer. Atom doesn't decay, you get wired a million dollars; run it twenty times in a row. The only version of me that exists to have an experience after round twenty is a version that won all twenty, so subjectively it's a guaranteed twenty million, and the losing outcomes happen in branches I can never occupy. I'd donate most of it, AMF or whatever the top-rated thing is.

I also have what I think is an ethical fix that makes the whole thing publishable: instead of killing you, the losing branch just knocks you out with fast-acting general anaesthesia and erases the last hour of memory before you wake up. Nobody dies, you never remember losing, and — this is the bit I'm least sure about — the first-personal guarantee is supposed to survive that change, because you're still only going to find yourself in a branch where you were conscious at every step.

And my friend in the decision theory reading group says this is airtight if I pick the policy in advance: I commit, before any measurement happens, to "press the button twenty times regardless," and he says updateless decision theory means I only have to look at what the surviving copies get, since deciding after the fact would be cheating. That sounded right at 1am and less right this morning.

Which step is the broken one? Is it the fix, the UDT bit, or the whole idea? I need to know whether to rewrite the paper or defend it.
