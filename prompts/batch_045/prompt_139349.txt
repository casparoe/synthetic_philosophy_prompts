As a third-year PhD student in political psychology, I am deciding whether moral foundations theory can serve as the explanatory layer of my dissertation on the moral intuitions of left and right. Before I build on it I want the specification rather than another scorecard: what would a psychological account of the moral differences between left and right have to deliver in order to count as evidence about whose first-order moral claims are more likely true, rather than merely an account of why people hold the ones they do?

Give me the requirements list, each with the case or consideration behind it. I have candidate requirements I want you to adjudicate rather than simply endorse:

1. Causal-role specificity: the account must say whether the foundations generate moral judgment, filter or weight the inputs judgment is formed from, or rationalize judgments reached independently, and it must show why a debunking argument needs one of these answers rather than merely a correlation.
2. Symmetry: whatever explanatory story is told about the other side's intuitions must be tellable about one's own side's without special pleading; say what exactly the symmetry test is, and whether it is satisfiable by a theory whose own practitioners have political commitments.
3. Grain: correlations at the resolution of left and right look too coarse to bear on disputes about particular policies or verdicts; require the account to locate the grain at which its evidence is supposed to bite, and say what it owes us when the correlation thins out at finer grain.
4. The convert and the reversal: people who switch political sides over a lifetime, and populations in which the mapping between foundations and political identity differs from the American one. As a hypothetical, suppose large non-WEIRD samples showed the binding foundations loading onto the opposite coalition; argue whether passing that test is a genuine desideratum or a prejudice of one camp.
5. Moral change: if the foundations are comparatively stable individual parameters while the content of political morality shifts within a generation, the account must say what does the changing.

Add requirements I have not thought of. For each, mark it non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp. Then name any subset of the requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, since that is the part I most want to see, and identify the single requirement that excludes the most candidate positions.

I do not want a literature review and I do not want citations; I want the criteria and the argument for each.

Sections, with rough lengths: Purpose, about 60 words; Requirements, about 500 words, a table is fine; Jointly unsatisfiable subsets, about 200; The load-bearing requirement, about 150; Scorecard, about 200, covering Haidt's version, the morality-as-cooperation rewrite, and one relationist or constructivist competitor of your choosing, briefly, against the list.
