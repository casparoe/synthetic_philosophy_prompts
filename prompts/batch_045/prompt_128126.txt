Hi — I sit on the serious incident review panel at my hospital (mid-sized district general, about 600 beds) and I've been landed with drafting the written opinion we circulate before Friday's meeting. I'd really like it to not read like it was ghost-written by the claims team, which is what usually happens. Sorry in advance, this is long.

The case. Mr H, 79, admitted on the Tuesday with community-acquired pneumonia, on 2 litres of oxygen, frailty score 6 but independently mobile at home. Wednesday night the medical ward had 31 patients covered by two registered nurses (establishment is three) and one healthcare assistant; one of the nurses was agency, newly qualified, third shift ever on that ward. Our obs policy: an aggregate score of 7 or more means immediate review by a doctor, within 30 minutes, plus continuous monitoring.

01:40 obs: score 6. 02:20 obs: score 7 — resp rate 28, sats 90 on oxygen, BP 98/54, newly confused. The agency nurse documented it, went to the nurse in charge and asked whether to call the doctor. The nurse in charge said he'd been like that much of the night, the on-call FY2 was stuck in a cardiac arrest on another ward and nobody would come, so recheck in an hour. She never rechecked — two falls and a cannula to site, and by her own account she forgot. At 04:35 a healthcare assistant found him unresponsive. Peri-arrest call, outreach attended, he died at 06:10. Cause of death on the certificate: septic shock.

Numbers we've agreed, so you don't have to guess at the medicine: two of our clinicians put survival at roughly 30–40% if he'd been escalated at 02:20, down to maybe 5–10% by 04:30.

The complications. (1) The FY2 who would have been called was in fact in a cardiac arrest from 01:50 to 03:20 elsewhere, and the night coordinator had been told at 01:55 she'd be unavailable. (2) At 23:10 the on-call consultant, by phone from home, without seeing him, put a ward-level ceiling of care on him — not for HDU or level 3 — on the basis of the frailty score and the admission note. It's in the notes but not on the handover list, not on the board, and never discussed with the family, who say (accurately) he was walking to the toilet and chatting at 21:00 and would have wanted everything done. The FY2 says she'd have deferred to that ceiling anyway. (3) The bedside monitors alarm at a default sats threshold of 85%; a 2021 action to make that configurable was deferred twice by a committee, and no individual owns it.

What our reporting framework makes us fill in: "causal factors" (defined as actions or inactions without which, on the balance of probabilities, the harm would not have occurred) and "contributing factors / latent conditions" (things that made it more likely or worse but don't meet that bar). Then a tick-box: was the harm avoidable — yes / no / cannot determine. Then a duty-of-candour paragraph to the family about what went wrong. And we can recommend referrals.

Where we're split: our clinical lead says the ceiling of care made escalation unavailable, so nothing anyone did or didn't do changed the outcome, and we should file it as unavoidable with no causal factors. Our lay member says that if we write "no causal factor" we're telling the family and the ward that it was fine not to call a doctor, and someone will die of that next winter; she also says the real omission was a consultant deciding a ceiling of care over the phone about a man he never examined. Two of us have got stuck on whether an omission is a cause of anything at all, or whether "cause" in these reports is just a polite word for blame. The claims team has reminded us, twice, that the avoidable tick-box matters for the family's negligence claim.

What I want: the memo itself, under about 1000 words, with a bottom line for each field — what goes in causal factors, what goes in contributing factors, how we tick the box, what we actually say to the family, whether anyone gets referred — plus the reasons, written so the losing side could recognise their own worry in it and might actually be moved by it. If you think the framework's definition of "causal factor" is the thing that's broken here, say so and give me replacement wording we could genuinely use on the form. And please don't just say "but for the failure to escalate he'd probably have died anyway, so it wasn't causal" and leave it there — that's the bit I'm stuck on, not the bit I need explained.
