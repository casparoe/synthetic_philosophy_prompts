I work in survey methodology and quantitative ecology. Most of my professional life is spent on designs where sampling probabilities are unequal: size-biased and length-biased sampling, capture–recapture estimation of population size, frame over- and under-coverage, and the design-based versus model-based (superpopulation) ways of arguing about the same estimator. Inclusion probabilities, Horvitz–Thompson weighting, the inspection paradox, tag loss, and the way a stopping rule changes the sample space are things I use rather than things I need explained.

What I want is a translation of the anthropics debate — the self-sampling assumption, the self-indication assumption, and the presumptuous philosopher case — into that vocabulary, as faithfully as it can be done, followed by a report on what the translation fails to carry.

Here is my understanding, which you should audit as you translate rather than take over. SSA, as I read it, is not a prior but a conditional sampling model: conditional on a hypothesis, my credence that I am any particular observer in the reference class is uniform, one over the number of observers in it, so the anthropic update comes entirely from more specific indexical evidence such as my birth rank. SIA is a constraint on the prior over hypotheses instead: before conditioning on anything indexical, each hypothesis is weighted by how many observers it contains, so that the bare fact that I exist is itself evidence, and in the presumptuous philosopher case the large-population hypothesis wins by the ratio of the populations, which is what makes the conclusion feel presumptuous. I have also seen it claimed that once SIA is combined with SSA, the doomsday-style shift from birth rank cancels and you end where your unanthropic prior put you. If any of that is a misreading, correct it inside the translation.

Please do the following.

1. Give me the systematic mapping. Pair SSA with design-based finite-population inference, where the unknown parameter includes the population size itself, as in estimating N from recaptures, and where the frame and its inclusion probabilities are fixed before the draw; pair SIA with a model-based superpopulation picture in which the number of units functions as a size-biasing weight on the draw. Then tell me whether the size bias in SIA really is the inspection paradox, or whether the resemblance is superficial.

2. Work two cases in the mapped vocabulary, as likelihoods, inclusion probabilities, and estimators: the presumptuous philosopher, and a birth-rank or doomsday-style case. For the second, tell me whether the alleged cancellation is an identity, a consequence of a particular choice of reference class, or an artifact of the mapping.

3. Adjudicate my recurring referee question. In my field the frame is stipulated by the designer, and its defects are called coverage error, which is a practical problem about bias, not a metaphysical question about which frame is correct. So: does the reference class problem have any home in my vocabulary at all? Is it undercoverage, or is it something the vocabulary cannot express?

4. Give me the residue report: what has no counterpart on my side. I would like you to rule specifically on four candidates. First, pure self-locating uncertainty: once a unit is drawn, the sampler reads its label, and the nearest thing I have to a draw whose identity is unknown is uncertain identification in capture–recapture — is that a genuine counterpart to not knowing which observer I am, or does it smuggle in knowledge the anthropic case lacks? Second, the fact that this is an n-of-one design that cannot be repeated or re-randomized, so design-unbiasedness and interval coverage have nothing to be defined over. Third, hypotheses in which no observer exists and hypotheses with infinitely many observers, both of which my vocabulary handles by convention rather than by argument. Fourth, whether de se evidence can move credence at all, given that nothing in a sampling design distinguishes an observation from the inside.

5. Finally, two constructions of my own that I cannot finish. Call the first the over-coverage model: the frame contains unfilled slots as well as occupied ones, and I conditionalize on having landed in an occupied slot. Call the second the renewal reading: in a renewal process, arriving at a random time gives you a length-biased interval, whereas sampling a random passenger from those present gives you a uniform draw, and the paradox vanishes. For each, work out what it collapses into under the mapping — SIA, SSA, both, or neither — and whether the passenger-versus-arrival distinction is decidable inside my vocabulary or is precisely the external protocol fact that my vocabulary takes as given.

Close with a verdict on what the residue indicates: gaps in the sampling vocabulary, or places where the anthropic debate is less determinate than its participants take it to be.
