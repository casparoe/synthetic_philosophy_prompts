Tiny stakes, low four-figure bankroll, nothing I'd be ruined by. A colleague who did some graduate work in this area told me, more or less, the following:

"Look, this is settled. There's a family of theorems — Dutch book arguments and money pump arguments — and what they say is: if an agent's preferences can't be represented as maximizing expected utility, then there is a set of bets, each of which the agent would happily accept on its own, that together guarantee it a loss no matter how the world turns out. That's not a philosophical opinion, it's a mathematical fact, on the level of arithmetic. And the corollary is the part people miss: expected utility maximization isn't a substantive claim about what's worth caring about, or how much risk to run. It's just a consistency requirement. A non-EU-maximizing agent doesn't have unusual values, it's incoherent, and anyone who notices can drain its bankroll. So if you want your bot not to be exploitable, have it pick whatever has highest expected utility under some fixed probability function and some fixed utility function, and you're done."
