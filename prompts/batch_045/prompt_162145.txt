I've got a chapter draft on laws of nature due in a couple weeks and I'd rather the holes in my position get found by you than by my supervisor. Here's roughly the package I've landed on:

1. I'm Humean. Laws are just the regularities that show up as axioms or theorems of the best system of the world's total particular facts. Nothing governs, no necessary connections out there, no primitive nomic relation holding the facts together.
2. Lawhood is objective. There's a fact of the matter about whether something is a law; it isn't just relative to our interests or to what we find handy to write down.
3. Simplicity isn't a feature of the world. It's a feature of creatures like us, and what counts as a simple axiom is partly up to us. Which is awkward, since the "best" in 1 is supposed to be a tradeoff between simplicity and strength.
4. My attempted fix for 3: forget simplicity, rank competing systems by how much they let you compress the total history of the world — how short a description of everything that happens you can get away with, given the system. Crude, but I like it.
5. There are real laws in biology and in economics, not just useful generalizations we keep around for convenience.
6. Laws explain their instances, and they're what makes counterfactuals true. That's partly what I mean by "law".
7. Newtonian gravitation and the ideal gas law are both laws, even though both are strictly speaking false.
8. The future is open. There aren't facts yet about what will happen, and nothing settles in advance which way a genuinely chancy event goes.

So: which of these actually fight each other, and which only look like they do? Rank the real tensions by how bad they are rather than calling all of them fatal, and for each one say what the cheapest repair is and what that repair costs me somewhere else in the list. Tell me whether 4 is doing any real work or just relocates the problem in 3 somewhere less visible. And if there's one revision that cleans up the most, say which — I don't have a commitment here I'm specially attached to giving up, so don't assume I'd rather drop 1.
