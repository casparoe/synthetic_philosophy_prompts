Write me a satirical help-centre page, around 500 words, for a startup app that sells you a private language: you invent a sign — call it S — for some sensation of yours and log it in a diary whenever the sensation comes back. The pitch is that meaning is yours alone, so the app promises that nothing you write can ever be checked, corrected or understood by anyone else, and it markets that as a privacy feature.

The jokes have to come from getting the private language argument right, not from generic startup mockery. So: the onboarding 'ostensive definition' step (stare at the feeling, tap the sign) quietly does no work; the app can only ever confirm that it seemed to you that you were right, never that you were; every premium feature meant to patch this — a sincerity mode, encrypted backup of your inner life, a streak counter — can't, because what's missing is a practice with criteria, not a better feature; and whenever the app tries to make S actually do something — remind you, flag a pattern to your doctor, compare one week to another — it has to smuggle in something public, then upsell that seperately as an integration. Keep the voice deadpan and corporate: nobody in the FAQ ever admits the premise is broken. And don't let it collapse into behaviourism — the failure isn't that we can't see your feelings, it's that a sign with no criterion of correct use isn't a sign at all.

It should land for someone who knows the argument and still just be funny for someone who doesn't.
