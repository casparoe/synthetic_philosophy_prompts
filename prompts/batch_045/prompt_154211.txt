hey — I'm in this seminar on moral status (it's cross-listed between ethics and phil mind, so half the time we're doing animal stuff and half the time we're doing consciousness) and we spent two weeks on the idea that sentience comes in degrees, so moral status should too, all the way down to things like thermostats. I wrote out my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases and they don't add up, and I'd really like to know what principle I'm implicitly using, or which verdict has to go. Cases (all of them stuff my TA brought up or that I made up at 1am):

— Thermostat: zero. Not a tiny bit, literally zero. And I feel no pull whatsoever even though I can't point to where the line between it and a fly is supposed to be.
— Fruit fly: nonzero but tiny. I'd squash a jar of two hundred for a mild convenience and feel basically fine, but I'd feel gross doing it for absolutely no reason, and I do think two hundred is two hundred times worse than one — linear, no dimishing.
— Lobster dropped in boiling water: I feel genuinely bad about this one, like I'd pay real money to avoid it in a way I wouldn't for the flies. Way more than a fly, not just a bit more.
— A single ant: about fly-level, maybe less. But a colony of 50,000 that forages together, defends the nest, and relocates as a unit: I feel worse about destroying the colony than about destroying 50,000 ants that were never going to interact again, and I don't think that's just 50,000 × one ant. The colony feels like it has some status of its own. I also feel kind of dumb saying that.
— A nematode with a few hundred neurons: basically nothing, less than the fly. And a whole-brain computer emulation of that same nematode: I feel *even less* than nothing, like zero, which is embarrasing because I also think I'm some kind of functionalist about minds.
— A machine-learning system that gets a negative number, updates itself over millions of trials so it stops doing the thing, and generalizes to new situations: zero, I feel nothing. But that's more or less the same description as the lobster learning to avoid the side of the tank where it got shocked, and there I feel bad. The only difference I can name is that I believe the lobster's learning is driven by something it *feels*, and I don't believe that about the system — but I have no idea what I'd look for to settle it.
— A made-up "dimmer" creature: we can turn the felt intensity of its pain continuously down from 100% to 1% while its behavior stays identical, because behavior runs on a separate pathway. At some point I want to say its status hits zero, but nothing seems to happen at any particular setting.
— Two made-up creatures: A has full pain-detection and complete avoidance behavior and long-term learning, but nothing is ever *unpleasant* for it; B has a genuinely unpleasant pain experience but is paralyzed and can't learn or change its behavior at all. A: I feel nothing. B: I feel quite a lot.
— Creature C: one dimension of valence, present moment only, no memory, no anticipation, no sense of itself over time. Creature D: same intensity of feeling, but it remembers, anticipates, and has a crude sense of being the same one over time. D matters more to me, and I *want* to say that's because more of D's life is going badly rather than because D's moments are more intense — but I'm not sure I can hold that line.
— A being whose pain is ten times as intense as ours: worse per moment, but not ten times as bad. Meanwhile ten beings at normal intensity is exactly ten times as bad. Diminishing in intensity, linear in number — those two sit badly together.
— Person under general anesthesia, no experience now, wakes up fine: full status, nothing lost. Person in a permanent vegetative state with no cortex and no prospect of another experience: I feel it's *much* less bad for them to die, but not nothing — I'd be upset if someone used the body for target practice. Person in dreamless sleep: full status.
— Someone born unable to feel pain but otherwise totally normal: full status. So whatever I believe, it isn't only about pain.

My own attempt, which I think is broken: status = (how much things can matter *to* the thing, a function of intensity and of how much of its life the experience colors) × (how much of a 'self' there is for it to matter to, i.e. how unified the subject is over time) + a separate term for future-directed interests that has nothing to do with current sentience. Is there one principle that gets all of this, or if not, which verdict would you tell me to drop? My guess is the colony one and the emulation one are the first to go, and maybe the diminishing-intensity one. I'd also like to know what the principle says about cases I haven't settled: a fetus at twenty weeks; a human painlessly killed in their sleep; a hive mind made by linking a thousand human brains into one shared experience; a creature whose feelings are a thousandth as intense as ours but which lives a thousand times longer; and something that is conscious only in five-minute flashes per day. Please don't just tell me to go read something — our reading list is already unmanagable — I want the principle, the clause-by-clause motivation from my cases, and an honest list of the verdicts it can't save.
