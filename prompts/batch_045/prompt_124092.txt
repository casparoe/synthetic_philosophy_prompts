**Truth as a compression device (rough draft)**

Deflationism, as I understand it, is a package deal. There's no substantive property of being true that does explanatory work in semantics, or in explaining why true beliefs help you get around. What the word true actually does is two things: it lets you go from a sentence to a claim about the world and back (that P is true iff P), and it lets you do something you otherwise couldn't — blind ascription and generalization. When I say everything in the audit report is true, or whatever Kim told you yesterday was true, I'm endorsing a whole bunch of claims whose content I can't produce or list. On the standard telling, the trick is that I could in principle unpack that into a giant conjunction; I just can't in practice, because of my limitations. The predicate is a logical device for handling sentences we can't get our hands on individually.

Here's my worry. That in principle is doing an enormous amount of work, and I don't think it's the kind of work a deflationist is entitled to. Unpacking everything the hospital record system says is true into one long conjunction isn't a paraphrase that you or I or any machine we could actually build could ever finish — the list is open-ended and partly unknown to us. So the sentence with true in it and the sentence without it are not equivalent in any sense that's available to creatures like us. But then what is the deflationist claim? As far as I can see there are two options and neither is comfortable.

Option one: the claim is about in-principle eliminability, full stop. But then it's a claim about an idealization, and it's hard to see what it settles. A full-blown correspondence theorist can grant every bit of it: sure, God could unpack all the conjunctions, and it wouldn't touch her view that truth is a real relation to the world. A thesis that everyone can accept has stopped being a thesis about truth and become a thesis about the expressive resources of an ideal language.

Option two: the claim is about what the device does for us — what job true has in the practice of creatures with finite memory, finite time, and heavy reliance on each other. That's the interesting claim. But now the deflationist owes an account of the function, and the function is going to be a substantive one. You can't just say it's a device for generalization; you have to say what kind, under what constraints, and why that device and not some nearby impostor. At that point you're doing functional-role theorizing about a concept that was supposed to have no hidden depths. Deflationism starts to look like substantivism with the metaphysics left out.

So here's the positive proposal, which is the part I most want attacked. I think the right thing to say about truth attributions in the cases that matter — weather models, hospital records, statistical agencies, big automated pipelines that emit claims nobody individually surveyed — is that they work like a transferable note. When I say everything coming out of that pipeline is true, I am not expressing the same belief as some conjunction, and I'm not reporting a property I've inspected. I'm taking on commitment to each member of an open-ended, unsurveyable set of claims, in advance of being able to evaluate any of them, on the understanding that any one of them can be called in later: challenged, checked, and if it fails, retracted, with the retraction propagating backward through everything I built on top of it. Truth talk is the accounting system for a division of epistemic labor that outruns any single head.

That has a payoff I like and a cost I don't. The payoff is that the normativity of truth stops being spooky. The norm isn't a relation to a property; it's the norm of not issuing notes you won't honor and of honoring the ones you did, which is a plain social fact about a practice we're all stuck in. The cost is that this smells like verificationism or pragmatism with extra steps: if the practice is doing all the work, then truth starts looking like whatever survives at the end of inquiry, and the deflationist's own insistence that truth outruns anything we can ever check goes out the window. My attempted fix is that a note can default even if nobody ever audits it — a claim in the pipeline can be false although no one will ever find out — but I'm not sure my setup gives me the right to say that, since on my own account the practice is what makes the predicate what it is.

One last thing, which is either the best part or the worst part. On this account the value of a truth predicate should scale with how much deferred, unsurveyable testimony a community has. In a small band where everything is learned face to face and everyone can in principle check everything, the device earns almost nothing. In a society with bureaucracies, sensors, models, and supply chains, it earns a great deal. Which suggests that the normative grip of truth talk is institutionally mediated and historically recent, not a timeless feature of the concept. I kind of both believe this and think it's obviously false.
