I've got one whiteboard, no projector, and zero chance of anyone reading anything beforehand, so please don't give me quotes to put on the board or a reading list — I need a plan for how to actually say it out loud to a room of freshmen and sophomores who have no econ background, short attention spans, and who arrive split into two camps: half think the robots are coming for every job and half have the smug "technology has always created more jobs than it destroys so stop worrying" line ready to go.

What I mainly need is the order. I keep tripping over what depends on what: can I even make the point that it's not the machine itself but what the machine is *for* until they've got some grip on why an owner buys a machine in the first place, or does that just turn into a boring digression about profit I should skip? Is that the right carrier, or is one continuous story better, and what does it need to leave out so the difference can't be blamed on the owner being mean or generous?
