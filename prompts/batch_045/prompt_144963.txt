I got talked into a reading group on African philosophy this term and now I'm stuck inside an argument I built myself, which usually means I built it wrong. It seems to show that the whole idea of a person being a person through other persons either collapses into something its defenders would hate or does no work at all. That's way too strong, so tell me where I cheated.

(1) On the relational view, what makes someone a person is not a property they have on their own; it's their being adequately embedded in relationships with others. Personhood is achieved and kept up, not just given.

(2) So personhood is the sort of thing other people have a hand in: it can be built, damaged, and in the limit undone by how those around you actually relate to you.

(3) That looked like the view's selling point. It explains why apartheid's refusal to treat Black South Africans as fully human, or a village turning on someone and driving them out, is a distinctive kind of wrong and not just unkindness: it's an attack on the victim's standing itself, not merely on their welfare.

(4) But take a case where the withdrawal sticks. Everyone around X relates to X as a thing: X is used, humiliated, never addressed, and no one else is in reach. Given (1) and (2), X is not a person, or is a sharply diminished one. And now the view seems to hand the persecutors exactly what they wanted: their refusal did not only mistreat X, it partly made it true that X had less standing.

(5) If you refuse that, and say X is still a full person and the community is simply wrong, then you need something about X that the community's attitudes cannot reach. But then that thing, and not the relationships, is what makes X a person, and the relational claim was decorative.

(6) Two escapes came up in the group and neither convinced me. One: the relevant relations are the ones the community ought to have, not the ones it has. That seems to make personhood depend on normative facts that are not themselves relational, which is (5) again. Two: other relations hold X up anyway, from a wider community, or ancestors, or humanity, so no local withdrawal can undo it. But that just relocates the question to how wide the relevant we is, and I cant see a non-arbitrary place to stop, household or village or nation or humanity or the living and the dead together, and the wider you go the less the view says.

(7) So the relational view is either empty or it licenses the oppressor.

Two more things bother me. (2) seems to commit me to personhood coming in degrees, with the well-liked being more of a person than the lonely, and to a permanent hermit or the last human alive not being a person at all. And one person in the group said the case in (4) cannot even be described, since using someone and humiliating someone are themselves ways of relating to them, so the total absence of relation is not something a community can produce. I genuinely cant tell whether that is deep or just wordplay.

I would rather keep (1) and keep the diagnosis in (3) than swallow (7), and if I have to choose I would drop the slogan before I drop the claim that exclusion is its own kind of harm. Which step is the bad one, why did it look fine when I wrote it down, and what does each way out cost me? Is there a version where relationships enable, express, or are the proper form of respect for personhood without constituting it, and would that version still earn (3)? And please don't just tell me the name of somebody to go read, I want the actual move.
