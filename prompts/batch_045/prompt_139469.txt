Vocabulary so you don't have to guess. A deployed perception stack: a fixed function f (frozen weights, fixed preprocessing and calibration) mapping a sensor input x — lidar sweep, camera frames, extrinsics — to a discrete output over a label space, plus sometimes a confidence score. Scene semantics = the ground truth about what's out there. The "world" = the actual scene together with its counterfactual variants. A threat model = a set of perturbation operators we care about (weather, sensor dropout, occlusion, adversarial patch) plus a nearness ordering on them, usually a radius in some norm but really it's just "what we're worried about". Robustness = output invariance under semantics-preserving perturbations. A per-input certified radius. On-distribution vs off-distribution inputs. Conformal prediction giving marginal coverage over a calibration set rather than anything per-input. Calibration curves, ablations.

What I want translated, one by one: the sensitivity condition; the adherence condition; the claim that knowledge just is tracking; closure under known entailment (do the single-premise, multi-premise and deduction-based versions separately if you think they come apart); and the failure of closure. For that last one I want a worked example in my setting, not the brain-in-a-vat one: give me a concrete case where the stack "tracks" some ordinary claim and fails to track something that claim obviously entails, phrased in terms of perturbations, radii and which perturbations are nearer than which — and say explicitly what in my framework is doing the work that similarity-of-worlds does in theirs.

Bonus bit, and this is the part I care about most. I've been toying with a variant I'm calling radius-monotone tracking. Instead of a yes/no sensitivity check at the nearest not-p worlds, you look at how the stack's confidence in p decays as you move along the nearness ordering toward the boundary of the p-region, and you say it "knows" p only if the decay is steep enough relative to how far out the nearest label-flipping perturbation sits. I want to know: does this defintely avoid the closure failure, or does it just relocate the problem into the choice of ordering and the threshold? And tell me whether I've accidentally reinvented something philosophers already call safety — I may have, I've only got second-hand grip on that literature.
