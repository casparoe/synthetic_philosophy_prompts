A state archive holds the surviving files of a colonial district administration: tax rolls, court dockets, correspondence, police reports. Because almost nothing written by the people that administration governed has survived, those same files are also the only substantial source for those people's history. The archive's cataloguing rules require that each collection be described by its creator and kept in the creator's own filing order, so a person surfaces, if at all, under headings such as Tax defaulters, 1912 or Unrest, Ward 4: in the categories through which the administration processed them. The archive defends this as custodial neutrality — it neither destroys, rearranges, nor adjudicates the truth of what it holds, and it will accession the files of an abusive agency precisely because they are evidence of what that agency did. Yet the same archive has recently begun inviting descendants of the people named in these files to contribute oral testimony and their own descriptions, and catalogues what they produce as associated material: consultable alongside the collection, never altering its arrangement, and where a descendant disputes a file title, the title stands with their note attached beside it.

What does this pattern presuppose about what a record is and what an archive is for? Specifically: does the neutrality claim rest on the view that a documentary trace carries its evidential meaning independently of who filed it and for what purpose, or only on the weaker view that a custodian must not be the arbiter of what is true? Does admitting descendants' material as associated but not as record presuppose a hierarchy of evidence, or is it equally compatible with a flat view of sources plus a division of labour? Where exactly is the practice underdetermined — which rival theories of evidence, or of the point of an archive, would license this same combination? And what would concretely have to change if the archive accepted that arrangement and titling are themselves interpretive acts, or, in the other direction, that testimony is simply another record?
