I write novels — four published, one currently in revisions hell — and I keep bumping into the same shape of thing, in my own drafts and in the industry around me, and I don't have a word for it. Wondering whether there is one.

The version in my own work: I'm on draft eleven of a book. Every single revision has been an improvement by the standard I was applying at the time. Not a rationalisation in retrospect — I genuinely can't find a step I'd take back. But the standards moved. Draft one was a quiet book about two sisters and a house; draft eleven is a plotted thriller with those two sisters in it. If draft-eleven me could go back she'd reject draft one. If draft-one me saw draft eleven I think she'd hate it. And I don't experience any of this as loss, which is the part that's bugging me.

Second version, in my genre: nobody sat down and decided that every literary suspense novel should open with a child in danger, or whatever it is this decade. Each writer moved a bit toward what was selling; the centre moved; and now, ten years on, most of the writers I know genuinely prefer the new thing. The wanting was manufactured by the process, not overridden by it. The only people who can see the loss are the ones who left.

Third, possibly a different animal: readers. Each new cohort arrives and takes whatever's on the shelves as normal. Nobody's preferences changed at all; the population just turned over. Same-looking endpoint, different mechanism.

Everything I can find in this general neighbourhood — race to the bottom, Moloch, whatever the rationalist blogs call it — seems to assume the participants are trapped, know they're trapped, and would all sign a binding agreement to stop if only they could. That isn't my case. In my case the participants at each stage are doing exactly what they want and would sign nothing. The only one who thinks something was lost is the person at stage one looking at stage forty, and even she can't claim she was made worse off, since stage-forty her prefers stage forty.

What I want isn't a label, it's the carving:

- Is this one phenomenon with two mechanisms (preferences changing vs. people being replaced), or two?
- Is there a name for the no-regret variant specifically? What are the near misses it gets confused with, and where exactly does mine stop being theirs? Someone said Goodhart's law, but that reads to me as being about measurement, not this.
- If no one is worse off at any point by their own then-current standards, is there a loss here at all — or am I quietly assuming that the early standards are the correct ones and the later ones are corrupted? I've had "adaptive preferences" mentioned to me as being somewhere near this, but I don't know whether it actually applies or whether it's about something else entirely.
- And a boundary case, so you can show me where the edges are: my daughter adored a band at fourteen and can't stand them at twenty. I assume that's not an instance of this, but I can't say why not, and the fact that I can't makes me think I don't understand my own category yet.

Last thing: please mark clearly which parts of your answer are more or less settled among people who actually work on this stuff, and which are live disagreements — and in particular flag anywhere my own framing rests on something contested, including the assumption that there's any loss to explain. Don't smooth it into a tidy consensus; I'm going to put this in a book and I'd rather know which bits a philosopher would throw a shoe at.
