In the graduate seminar I run on the science of consciousness, the analogy we keep leaning on for Dennett's heterophenomenology is fieldwork in anthropology: the experimental subject is an informant, her verbal reports are field notes, the heterophenomenologist is a fieldworker who records everything the informants say about their gods while bracketing — neither affirming nor denying — the gods themselves. My students use it to license something strong: that the method is metaphysically neutral, that it gives first-person reports their full evidential due while begging no question against the reality of qualia, and that any critic who says it explains consciousness away has simply misunderstood what the method is for.

Audit the analogy. Give me the explicit table of correspondences — informant, field notes, gods, the rituals and other behaviour the fieldworker can observe independently of what anyone says, the fieldworker's own ontology, and whatever else does mapping work — and then the disanalogies, sorted into those that actually bear on the neutrality claim and those that are decoration.

Two points in particular need to be settled rather than gestured at. First: in real fieldwork the notes are evidence of a practice whose existence is not itself in question, whereas the object of heterophenomenological study is said to be the subject's heterophenomenological world rather than whatever her reports are about. That looks less like bracketing an ontology than like constituting the object of study out of the reports. Is it a genuine disanalogy, and what does it do to the claim that the method is neutral rather than already a substantive theory of what consciousness is? Second: anthropologists triangulate — reports get checked against behaviour and artefacts, against other informants, and implicitly against the fieldworker's own participation in a shared form of life. Tell me what plays each of those roles here, and what the method is supposed to do when its informants disagree among themselves about whether the gods exist at all.

Close by saying whether the neutrality conclusion survives, survives only in some weakened form, or collapses; and if the fieldwork picture cannot carry it, either supply an analogy that can, or say plainly what the method commits its users to and why no analogy is doing the work.
