hi — I write SF novels (the kind with a lot of argument in them, sorry) and I'm stuck on a chapter. There's a character, Marta, who has to write the expedition report on whether the locals count as thinkers at all, and I want her reasoning to be better than two cliches trading slogans. The creatures in my book are:

the Shoal — a colony of about ten thousand small organisms. There is no single body whose states do anything. The patterns that would play the belief and desire roles only exist across the whole colony, and if you remove any one organism the pattern survives but reorganises.

the mimics — behaviourally indistinguishable from us, internally nothing like us: same inputs, same outputs, different machinery, and on top of that the machinery that does the job on Tuesday isn't the machinery that does it on Thursday.

the sleepers — internally full of the right states, causally wired to each other in all the right ways, but nothing downstream ever reaches behaviour. They never move differently because of what they believe.

Marta's colleagues have two lines and I don't want her to just recite them. One says: same job, so same mind — which as far as I can tell collapses into saying a thermostat or a national economy has beliefs. The other says: no neurons, no mind — which my readers will see through in a paragraph.

So what I actually want, before I write the scene, is the spec. Write me the list of requirements any theory of what-it-is-to-have-a-belief has to meet to be any good at cases like these. Cases it has to give a verdict on. Distinctions it isn't allowed to flatten — having a mind vs. having the same kind of mind we have; being in a state vs. that state being realised by something; the state vs. whatever plays the role; how fine-grained the job description gets to be; occurrent vs. merely dispositional. And the arguments it has to survive: the old one where it turns out every damn system realises some role or other (Putnam's, I think?), and the one about whether the realiser or the role is what does the causal work.

For each requirement give me the case or consideration behind it, and mark which ones are non-negotiable and which are just what one camp happens to want. Then flag any pair of requirements I probably can't have both of — that's the bit I most want, because it gives me something for Marta to genuinely be stuck on rather than something she wins.

If there's room at the end, a short scorecard for the obvious candidates: the it's-the-job-not-the-stuff view, the view that mental states are just brain states, and whatever the current version is where the science tells you what the realiser is and the folk theory only fixes the role. But keep the weight on the requirements, that's the part I can't get from a skim. And no reading list or footnotes — my character is on a planet without a library, and neither of us can look anything up.
