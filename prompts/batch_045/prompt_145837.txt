Here's the critter we're calling the mesoflage: it's a little membrane-bound droplet found in a methane seep on Titan. It does five things: (1) it keeps it's insides chemically different from the outside by pumping ions across its membrane using a natural proton gradient, so it's maintaining itself far from equilibrium; (2) it grows by taking in simple carbon compounds and building more of its own membrane and a messy set of catalytic peptides; (3) when it gets big enough, physical shear in the seep just rips it into two smaller droplets, and those droplets keep doing the same thing—there's no DNA or RNA, and the peptides aren't copied from a template, they just reassemble from scratch using the same statistical biases, so offspring droplets are similar but not identical and there's no faithful heredity of a code; (4) it reacts to chemical gradients by changing its internal pH and sort of creeping toward richer food sources; and (5) over hundreds of generations it barely changes—there's a little selection for peptide sets that make the membrane sturdier, but no real cumulative adaptation, no eyes, no brain, no nothing.

Modern astrobiologists apparently define life as something like a self-sustaining chemical system that can undergo evolution by natural selection (I'm paraphrasing, don't quote me on the exact wording). The mesoflage seems to half-meet that: it self-sustains and it's subject to a weak kind of selection, but it doesn't really evolve in the open-ended way, and it has no genetic program.

So: what do you think would have been Aristotle's position on whether the mesoflage is alive? Would he say it has a soul, and if so which one—vegetative, sensitive, rational? Since it grows and feeds but also moves toward stuff, would he call it a plant, an animal, or some weird intermediate? How much would it bother him that it has no stable form passed down to offspring, or that its reproduction is just passive shearing rather than an active power of generating another like itself? And what would he say to a modern scientist who insists that without cumulative Darwinian evolution it's just a complicated chemistry set, not a living thing?
