Hey — I run a monthly reading group that's roughly half LessWrong refugees and half people who wandered in from an analytic theology reading list, and we keep going in circles on why there is something rather than nothing. Every session someone restates their favourite position and nobody moves. So I want to put something on the whiteboard instead: a decision tree that sorts people by what they say about cases, not by which label they're attached to. We'll go around the table and each person walks their own path down it.

What I want, concretely:

- 7–9 branch nodes, 12–16 leaves. Nested list or ASCII tree, whatever copies into a doc cleanly.
- Pick a start node everybody at the table grants (that at least one concrete thing exists, or that the question at least seems to have a sense) and say why you picked that one.
- Every branch node has to be a question a smart person with no philosophy background could answer without knowing where it leads. Taboo at branch nodes: names of views, names of philosophers, and the words necessary, contingent, grounding, modal, PSR — unglossed. So not "do you accept a global principle of sufficient reason?" but something like: suppose you had the complete list of every fact that obtains and a satisfying explanation of each item on that list — is there a further question left over? That's a node; the doctrine is not.
- Each leaf gets a name (or "unnamed" if nobody actually holds it), one sentence of steelman, and one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

Leaves that definately have to be in there: the brute-fact answer; the modal one where existence couldn't have failed; the external-cause / creation-style one; the value-based one, split into with-a-personal-agent and without-one; the aggregate move (explain every part and you've explained the whole, so there's no leftover question); the physical one where the empty state is unstable and laws do the work; the plenitude one where every possibility is actual, so of course something is; and the dissolution that says the question is ill-formed for logical or semantic reasons. Plus at least two cells marked "unnamed" that are coherent but undefended.

Three nodes I specifically care about, please build around them:

1. Scope. What ranges under "something"? Abstract objects, God, laws, modality itself? If numbers have to exist, has the question just been answered trivially, or has it quietly been re-aimed at concrete things? Make that a fork, not a footnote.
2. How much is being imagined away. Is the contrasting "nothing" a world with no matter but with laws and modal structure intact, or nothing at all, laws included? I think a lot of people are answering a much easier question than the one they think they're asking, and I want the tree to expose that on paper.
3. Inside or outside. If the explanation is itself something that exists, doesn't it fall within the very thing to be explained? Whichever way you cut that, it should be a node.

Also:

- At least three unoccupied combinations of answers, each with a one-line diagnosis of why nobody is there — incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. My own guess is that "nothing-including-no-laws" plus a causal answer is one of them, but you tell me.
- Flag at least two pairs of leaves your tree can't actually separate, and say what further question you'd need to pry them apart. Don't paper over them with a fake branch.
- Tag every leaf with whether it targets the radical existential question or a weaker surrogate (why this cosmos, why this much, why anything given that there are laws).
- A short trap table at the end: four things that come up constantly in our group, each located as an answer, a dissolution, or a refusal — "if there were nothing there'd be nobody to ask"; "because the vacuum is unstable and stuff just nucleates"; "God"; "the question is meaningless, can we move on". For each, which leaf it lands on, or if it doesn't land on one, why not.

I'm not after a literature survey: no names, dates, papers, or reading recs, and please invent a clean label for a cell rather than guess who holds it. Skip the "turtles all the way down" jokes too. What I want is the logical space, the questions that carve it, and the positions sitting in it — including the empty rooms.
