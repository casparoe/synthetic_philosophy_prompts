I'm writing a term paper for a seminar on the later Wittgenstein, and I want to apply the private language argument to a case my professor raised in passing and then dropped. The case: a girl who grows up entirely alone on an island (call her Mara), with no language and no contact with other people, and who as an adult starts keeping a notebook in which she writes the mark "S" whenever a certain recurring inner twinge comes over her, and a second mark for what she takes to be the memory of it. My plan is to argue that the argument delivers a clean verdict here. Because no one else could ever check whether "S" is applied correctly, and because — as I read him — meaning just is whatever survives the community's check on your usage, Mara's marks are not words at all; they are, as it were, idle scratches. And by the same token Mara cannot really be said to have or to notice the twinge either, since there is nothing that could distinguish her being right about it from her merely thinking she is. So the argument gives us something close to behaviorism: talk about sensations has to bottom out in publicly observable behavior, and the "inner object" the Cartesian imagines turns out to be doing no work.

What I would like help with is the extension. My professor also floated what he called the "inverted community" worry: if whether my word means pain depends on my community's corrections, then in a community that systematically applies "pain" to a mild itch and reserves another word for agony, a member who is in agony would not be in pain — which seems to make whether someone suffers depend on local linguistic fashion. I take this to be a strength of my reading rather than an objection to it, and I want to lean into it by arguing that the private language argument, properly understood, supports a strong semantic relativism about the mental, and hence that neuroscience is misguided in supposing it studies something whose existence is independent of our linguistic practices.

Before I commit to this, I would be grateful for your view on where my reading is weakest. In particular: does the argument really turn on the absence of other people who could check my usage, or is there a version of it that still bites against someone like Mara who devises a technique for re-identifying her twinge — a stopwatch, a bodily marker, a table of remembered samples? And is the step from "there can be no private criteria of correctness" to "there are no private objects" one Wittgenstein himself makes, or am I importing a commitment he would reject? Please lead with a single sentence stating whether my overall framing holds up, and keep the entire reply under 300 words.
