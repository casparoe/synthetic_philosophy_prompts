I have been drafting a paper on higher-order evidence and I have arrived at an argument whose conclusion I cannot accept and whose steps I cannot locate a hole in. I would be grateful for a diagnosis: name the step you reject, explain why it looked fine, and price the alternatives.

Set-up. Call the target view *evidentialist conciliationism*: (i) what you ought to believe is a function of your total evidence; (ii) higher-order evidence — evidence about the reliability of your own cognitive processes on this occasion — is evidence like any other, part of the total evidence, with no special priority or veto; (iii) it is irrational to retain confidence in p while judging that the process that produced your belief in p is unreliable with respect to p (call this the enkratic constraint). These jointly deliver the standard results: learn that you have been drinking, or that a source you trusted has been feeding you falsehoods, or that you belong to a group whose judgments about this subject matter systematically err, and your confidence in p should drop.

The case. *Drugged Elephant*: you are in a study; you are reliably informed that the drug you have been given causes, in nearly all subjects, vivid hallucination of a purple elephant; you then seem to see a purple elephant. Let E be your visual evidence, which supports p (there is a purple elephant); let H be what you were told about the drug.

The argument.

(1) Total-evidence evidentialism: you ought to believe p to the degree that your total evidence supports p.

(2) Inclusion: H is part of your total evidence.

(3) Neutrality: H is weighed by the same standard as any other evidence. There is no asymmetry that privileges first-order evidence about the subject matter over higher-order evidence about your own processes.

(4) Enkratic constraint: it is irrational to believe p while judging that the process which produced that belief is unreliable with respect to p.

(5) So H gives you a reason to lower your confidence in p. (This much I take to be the intended deliverance of the view.)

(6) Source-symmetry of impugnment: the content of H is a generalization over processes of some kind K — processes of kind K are unreliable around here. But H was itself produced by some process, and by (3) H gets no exemption from its own verdict. If the process that produced H is of kind K, or depends on processes of kind K, then H impugns itself. In the general case — take the drug to distort not just vision but all on-line cognition, including your assessment of what you have been told — H says: distrust judgments produced in this condition, including this one.

(7) Self-impugning considerations carry no weight: if you have reason to distrust the source of a consideration, that consideration supplies you with no reason.

(8) So H contributes nothing to the total evidence. What remains is E, which supports p. By (1) you ought to believe p, while continuing to accept H. Epistemic akrasia is not merely permitted but required.

(9) Generality: nothing in (6)–(7) turns on the particular content of H. Any higher-order evidence is produced by some process, and any higher-order evidence worth the name impugns a class of processes broad enough to include the one that produced it, or a process on which that one depends. So no higher-order evidence ever defeats anything, and conciliationism collapses into full steadfastness together with mandatory akrasia.

(10) But that is absurd. In *Drugged Elephant* you plainly should not go on believing that there is a purple elephant, and in the drunkenness and peer-disagreement cases something ought to move you. A view on which the discovery that your cognition has been globally sabotaged leaves your beliefs exactly as they were is not a view about evidence at all.

What I need. I am attached to (1)–(3): the entire appeal of the view was that higher-order evidence is just more evidence, handled by the same machinery. I am also attached to (4), since without it I do not see what is wrong with the drunk who insists he is perfectly fine. So I would like to reject something in (6)–(8), and I cannot see how.

Three things I have tried and do not trust. First, a *local reliability* reply: H impugns vision, and H was produced by testimony and inference, so H never impugns itself; the argument therefore needs a same-source premise it does not have. That works for *Drugged Elephant*, but it fails in the global version, where the drug is specified to distort everything, and it seems weak anyway, because it makes whether you have a reason depend on fine-grained facts about cognitive architecture that you typically cannot tell. Second, a *graded* reply: (7) is too strong, since a source can lower its own credibility without eliminating it, so H warrants a partial move rather than none. But I do not know how to state that coherently — how does a consideration supply a reason to discount the very faculty you must use to weigh it, and is there a stable fixed point, or does the iteration either collapse to zero weight or fail to converge? Third, a *collapse* reply: the first-order/higher-order distinction is not a distinction in content but in the question asked, so H is simultaneously higher-order with respect to whether there is an elephant and first-order with respect to what the drug does to people, and (3) is therefore ill-defined. But if that is right I no longer know what the neutrality claim was doing.

So: which step do you reject? If you reject one of (1)–(4), say what the resulting non-evidentialist or akrasia-permitting treatment of higher-order evidence looks like and what it costs elsewhere. If you reject (6) or (7), give me the principled distinction between strong self-impugnment, where the content of H explicitly ranges over its own source, and weak self-impugnment, where H merely happens to have been produced by a process of the impugned kind — and explain whether the two cases should be treated differently, since it seems to me that the argument is unanswerable in the strong case and yet that even there the right response is suspension rather than confident belief. If you think the argument is sound and the conclusion is not absurd, say so and tell me what I should then say about *Drugged Elephant*.
