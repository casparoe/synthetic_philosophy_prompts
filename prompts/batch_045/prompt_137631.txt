Our reading group has an argument I can't get out of, and I don't like where it ends up.

(1) Call higher-order evidence whatever evidence you have about how reliable your own reasoning is. If it tells you your reasoning on some topic is unreliable, that's a defeater for whatever you concluded on that topic. This is the only reason anyone cares about higher-order evidence in the first place.

(2) You always have some of it, about every topic. You know you're a human reasoner, human reasoners get hard questions wrong some nonzero fraction of the time, and "some nonzero fraction of the time my judgment here is wrong" just is "my reasoning here isn't fully reliable."

(3) You can't dodge it by saying you already priced your own fallibility in when you formed the belief. Whether you priced it in correctly is itself a judgment you reached by using the very reasoning you're supposed to be discounting.

(4) And it iterates: whatever confidence you settle on after applying the defeater, you have the same standing evidence about that adjustment, so rationality seems to demand another adjustment, and then another.

(5) A friend says the iteration is harmless and even solves itself: take the fixed point, the confidence level that stops changing when you apply the defeater to it, and that's what rationality requires. But here the fixed point looks like suspending judgment, and suspending about everything, including about whether to suspend, is nonsense.

(6) So either no belief you hold is rational, or rationality can require you to believe p while also believing that your believing p is irrational.

I'd rather keep (1) and (2); without them the whole topic collapse. (3) and (4) each feel like they're only blocking an escape route rather than claiming anything positive, and (5) feels like where something slides, but I can't say what. Which step do you reject, why did it look fine, and what do the alternatives cost? And whichever one you give up, what am I then committed to saying about the ordinary case where my evidence strongly supports p but I also have decent evidence that I botched the reasoning — am I allowed to go on believing p, or is that just akrasia with a good excuse?
