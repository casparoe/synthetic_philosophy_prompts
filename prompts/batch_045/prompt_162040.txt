ok so I'm in this sort of philosophy-of-physics reading group thing at school (me, my friend, and a physics teacher who lets us pick the topic) and I think I accidentally built an argument that breaks the second law of thermodynamics?? which obviously means I messed up somewhere, but I can't see where and it's bugging me. Here it is:

(1) Landauer's principle: erasing one bit of information in a memory at temperature T dumps at least kT ln 2 of energy into the surroundings as heat. That's basically the whole "information is physical" idea.
(2) Getting information, on the other hand, has no minimum price — a measurement can in principle be done reversibly, so there's no entropy cost to finding something out. Remembering is free; forgetting is what you pay for.
(3) Szilard-engine version of Maxwell's demon: one molecule in a box, the demon finds out which half it's in, slides a piston in on the empty side, lets the molecule push it, and gets up to kT ln 2 of work per cycle out of a single heat bath.
(4) Now suppose the demon simply never erases. It writes one bit per cycle onto a long blank tape and keeps going, so by (1) and (2) it never pays anything at all.
(5) My friend said "obviously the blank tape is the fuel," but I think I have an answer to that: swapping two bits is reversible and costs nothing, it's just rewiring. So the demon can keep a tiny working memory forever and swap its used-up bits out for fresh blank ones as it goes along, and swapping is free. Nothing is ever erased, so no kT ln 2 is ever owed.
(6) So the demon pulls as much work as you like out of one lukewarm heat bath, for as long as its stack of blank bits holds out, and the only thing that changed in the universe is that a tape now has junk written on it. Bring a big enough stack and you can run a laptop off a glass of water basically forever.
(7) So either Landauer's principle is wrong, or measurement isn't free after all, or the second law is just false.

I really don't want (7)'s last option, and I'd rather keep (1) and (2) since those are the cool part. My teacher mumbled something about the tape being "in a known state" but that sounds like word games to me, since I'm not saying the tape is magic — I'm just saying the swap costs nothing.

Which step is broken? And if the answer really is "the blank bits were a resource all along," please explain why the free-swap move doesn't get around that, because that's the step I actually feel good about.
