I've been reading LessWrong, the Sequences, and the usual rationalist-adjacent blogs on and off for like a decade, and these days I work on interpretability at an ML lab. I'm not a philosopher — I know the qualia debates at the level of an interested amateur — so I want you to do a translation job for me, and I mostly care about the part that doesn't translate.

Take epiphenomenalism: the view that phenomenal states have no causal effects on the physical world, that they're effects of brain states but never causes of anything. Now take the standard objection to it, the one philosophers call the paradox of phenomenal judgment: if your qualia did nothing at all, then your judgment "I am in pain right now" wasn't caused by your being in pain, and doesn't seem to be knowledge of it either — so either epiphenomenalism undercuts the very grounds anyone had for believing it, or we don't know our own experiences, which is even worse.

Restate that whole package — view plus objection plus the usual moves on both sides (the "it's just a reliable readout" reply, the "causal covariation is enough for knowledge" reply, the "then zombies would say the same thing" escalation) — as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use at work: activations and features, the residual stream, linear probes and classifiers trained on hidden states, readouts vs. writes, ablations and path patching, causal scrubbing, self-reports as just more tokens in the context. My rough starting translation is: the epiphenomenalist says the phenomenal state is a feature that gets decoded by a probe but never written back into the residual stream, so it has no downstream effect on behavior or on the next token; and the objector says that then the "I am in pain" token isn't tracking the thing it claims to be about, and there's no causal path by which it could be. Give me your most honest, most charitable version of both sides in those terms — and don't quietly fix the view while you translate it.

One wrinkle you should deal with head-on rather than dodge: in the systems I actually work with, nothing is strictly causally inert in the way epiphenomenalism wants. Once the model emits "I am in pain," that token is in the context and it changes every subsequent computation, including the model's later self-reports about its earlier self-reports. So the naive model case is disanalogous in an obvious way. Say whether the analogy can be set up so it does the work anyway (e.g. a report emitted on a side channel that's never fed back, or a readout head trained post hoc on a frozen model) and what each of those setups costs you.
