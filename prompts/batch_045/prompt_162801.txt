Write a Swiftian modest proposal in the form of a letter to the editor of a serious newspaper, roughly 600 words, signed by the founding director of an invented government body, the Office of Analogical Review, proposing that no minister, commentator, or general may invoke Munich, Vietnam, or the Thucydides trap in public without first filing a Certified Analogy Impact Statement: a form on which the applicant names which current leader is Hitler and which is Chamberlain, states the interest actually at stake, and supplies the base rate of war among all crises that superficially resemble this one.

The comedy should come from the Office's procedures being applied honestly and in good faith. Two applicants briefed on the same facts reliably fill the Hitler and Chamberlain boxes in opposite directions, so the Office has introduced a tie-breaker that, as the director concedes with mild embarrassment, has never once favored restraint. The interest-at-stake box is nearly always left blank, since an analogy lets a government announce a policy without ever naming what it wants. The base-rate box is waived for cases of unusual gravity, a category that has so far contained every case. Most damaging of all: each filing must pair its analogy with a matched historical Non-Event — a crisis in which concessions were met with restraint, or a threat was called and nothing happened — drawn from the Office's Register of Non-Events, and the Register is empty. Nothing happening leaves behind no declarations, no anniversaries, no memorials, and so nothing to remember; the archive of usable analogies is filtered by outcome, and therefore contains only catastrophes. The director should register this gap with bureaucratic regret, propose a modest appropriation to study it, and not resolve it.

Keep the voice deadpan throughout — a competent administrator defending a reasonable reform — so the reductio lands without the author winking at the reader, and so that a historian recognizes the piece as fair to how analogies actually work. Do not name, quote, or attack any living person, and do not cite any book or article by name. Write in continuous prose: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
