I run a small wiki for a group of us who all came up reading the old rationalist blogs and forum posts, and I want to put together one of those which-precautionary-principle-are-you-actually-running quizzes: the kind that sorts you by your answers to concrete cases rather than by which label you'd slap on yourself. Eight or nine questions, each a scenario with two or three answers, and no question that just asks how risk-averse you are. Cases I want used: a pause on gain-of-function virology work while no pandemic is underway; licensing a pesticide that probably saves a lot of crop yeild but might be quietly wrecking a pollinator population nobody has measured; an asteroid-deflection mission with a small chance of nudging the rock the wrong way; holding back a cheap gene therapy because a rare side effect hasn't been ruled out; a mosquito gene drive; and one Pascal's-mugging-shaped case where the probability is unknowably tiny and the stakes are astronomical.

Two things I specifically can't get right myself. First, build it from the logical space instead of from a list of names: pick four or five yes/no distinctions that actually carve the space (does the principle bind omissions and the status quo as well as actions, is it a decision rule or only a burden-of-proof default, is the trigger irreversibility or unmeasurable probability, does it have proportionality to the cost of the precaution built in, is it a probability threshold or any nonzero chance) and then place the named views in the cells that fall out: strict better-safe-than-sorry precaution, the weak cost-effectiveness version that shows up in enviromental treaties, maximin and minimax-regret readings, ambiguity-aversion readings, the merely-procedural it-just-shifts-the-burden-of-proof reading, and the hard-line rejection that says it's incoherent or paralyzing and you should do expected utility with fat tails instead. Say which cells come out empty and why nobody sits there.

Second, add one axis of your own that isn't in the standard menu. The one I keep chewing on is precautionary recursion: when the precautionary measure is itself irreversible (a permanent ban, burning the last seed stock, a paused field that never restarts) the principle seems to apply to itself, and then either you get paralysis or you need some arbitrary stopping rule. Work something like that in as a real branch, maybe as an option-value node about whether the precaution destroys future options, and tell me where it pushes people into positions they'd otherwise not hold. Also flag any pair of leaves the quiz genuinely can't seperate (strict precaution vs. maximin vs. unbounded Pascalian; weak proportional precaution vs. ordinary cost-benefit with a fat risk premium) rather than inventing a question that pretends to. Deliver the questions, the answer options, the scoring key, and the tree with the empty-cell diagnosis, and keep the named positions out of the question wording: someone should be able to take it cold, without any of this vocabulary.
