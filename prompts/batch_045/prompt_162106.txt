I hold a version of the doomsday argument that I have not seen defended in quite this form, and I want to know how exposed it is. I have been teaching it for years; lately I have become uncomfortably attached to it, and I would rather be talked out of it now than discover later that it was insulated against refutation by construction.

Let me fix the machinery so we are not talking past each other, and let me be explicit that every piece of it is up for grabs in your answer. The argument assumes that I should treat my own position within some class of observers as evidentially relevant to how large that class is — the self-sampling assumption, as against the self-indication assumption on which your being an observer at all is already evidence for many observers. It assumes the prior over the total size of the class is not so diffuse as to swamp any likelihood ratio. And it assumes the class is not chosen ad hoc. Run on the standard datum, my birth rank — on any of the usual estimates, a few times ten to the eleventh humans so far — the argument shifts probability mass toward a future containing not vastly more observers than the past.

The version I actually accept replaces that datum with one about my epistemic position. Say that an observer is anthropically situated when three things hold together: she has a quantitatively well-confirmed cosmology; she knows her species' approximate population history; and her evidence about her species' future is not merely incomplete but self-locating, so that part of what she can infer about the size of the future comes from where in the sequence she finds herself. Call the argument from the datum that I am anthropically situated the Epistemic-Position Argument, EPA for short.

Its load-bearing empirical claim is what I call front-loading: anthropically situated observers belong to the very early career of a technological species or to no career at all. If a species ends soon, all its observers are early, and a fair number of them will be in my position. If it does not end soon — and standard astrophysics makes available a future of many trillions of years, hence, on any colonization scenario, astronomically many observers — then the overwhelming bulk of its observers lies ahead. But those observers, I claim, would not be anthropically situated: beings that far downstream would know whether the future is long, would have direct rather than self-locating evidence about it, and would have no use for the inference I am making now. So my being anthropically situated is evidence against a long future of observers epistemically like me.

Note what this does and does not give. It does not predict extinction. It predicts a disjunction: either the future is short, or it is long but populated predominantly by beings whose epistemic situation is drastically unlike mine. I hold the disjunction, and I take it to be substantive rather than evasive, because it rules out the future most futurists actually describe — a long future inhabited by beings recognizable as our descendants in their evidential situation. I also think EPA escapes the reference-class objection in a way the birth-rank version does not, because the class is not selected by fiat: it is fixed by the very datum doing the work, and it is a datum any of us must have in order to reason anthropically at all.

So: what would count against it? I want the evidence, arguments, formal results, and conceptual developments that should move me, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with an honest separation of what is live from what is merely logically possible. In particular I want you to address the following.

First, front-loading itself. Is there reason to think a long future would contain many anthropically situated observers after all — because intellectual novelty is indefinitely repeatable, because merged or uploaded or simulated minds face self-location problems of their own, or because a civilization could be long and large while remaining, generation by generation, uncertain about its own future? If front-loading fails, how much of the argument goes with it?

Second, whether the argument over-generates. For any property I know myself to have, being among its earlier bearers seems to license a doomsday-style inference about how many bearers there will ever be. Is there a principled distinction between being anthropically situated and, say, being among the first readers of a particular book, and if there is not, is that a reductio or merely an uninteresting consequence?

Third, the measure problem. If there is no fact of the matter how many observers there are — because subjects fission and merge, because the relevant quantities are continuum-valued, because there is no canonical weighting over observer-moments — what happens to an argument whose entire force is a likelihood ratio over class sizes? Does it collapse, or does it survive as a constraint on any measure one adopts?

Fourth, what has arguably already happened: our acquiring precisely the cosmology the argument exploits, the continuing non-detection of other technological species, the possibility that we are inside a simulation with its own measure. For each, tell me whether it strengthens the argument, weakens it, or is simply the datum again.

Fifth, and most important to me: which of these defeaters would a defender of the original birth-rank argument concede as genuinely counting against her, as opposed to what her critics say counts against her? Does EPA inherit the standard defeaters or has it merely relocated them? And be straight with me about unfalsifiability: the conclusion is disjunctive and one disjunct concerns the unobservable epistemic character of future observers, so it is not obvious that any observation could refute it. Say whether it has been qualified into that state, whether that is a defect in an argument of this kind, and how it compares with the original version, which at least puts a finite window on extinction.

What would move me most is a case in which this same form of argument yields a conclusion I reject outside anthropics. Please give the ranked map, state which rival — self-indication, a deflation that puts all the work in the prior, a no-measure view — you think best accounts for whatever force EPA has, and do not give me a bibliography or a reading list; I want the argumentative structure and the exposure, not the literature.
