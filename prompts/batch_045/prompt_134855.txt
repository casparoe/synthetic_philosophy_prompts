I run a volunteer forecasting group, about 45 people, mostly folks who found their way here via the Sequences, and we field a fresh set of questions every month. I'm the moderator, which means every single month I hit the same structural thing: two members disagree, they've both read each others reasoning, and the thread has stopped producing anything. I've got maybe 20 minutes per case, the members are unpaid and have no formal training beyond blog posts, and whatever I decide I have to post publicly as a one-line justification in the channel. So I need something better than vibes.

What I want is a decision procedure I can actually run, grounded in what the actual literature on this says — the Aumann-style agreement results, common priors, and the fact that those results are proved for small worlds in roughly Savage's sense, where the space of possible states is already on the table before anyone starts. My questions are basically never like that. Nobody can enumerate the states in advance, half of what matters gets discovered mid-round (including, sometimes, what the resolution criteria turn out to even mean), and a lot of the genuinely good evidence is the kind you can't hand to another person: I've been reading this lab's commits for a year and it feels slower than the roadmap implies, that sort of thing. Call that *unportable* evidence, versus portable evidence you can paste into a message. I think that distinction is doing a lot of work in practice and I want the procedure to have an explicit slot for it.

So give me: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a settled answer to each one, where the procedure outputs stop, this thread is finished, average the two numbers and move on, and where it outputs that a human has to decide because it's a values call rather than a factual one. Then run it on three real cases from last month:

1. Whether a given lab ships a named product by a hard date. Both sides have portable evidence, they've traded links twice, and they're still at 70 and 30.
2. A domain insider with only unportable evidence at 60 versus an outsider with a clean base-rate argument at 25. I want you to run this honestly even if it lands on average them and move on, which I'll hate, because I privately think the insider is right.
3. A dispute about whether the resolution text covers an obvious loophole case. My instinct is that this is a pure wording fight and I should just clarify and move on; tell me if the procedure says it's something worse.

Finish by telling me plainly what the procedure can't do, especially whether it can distinguish these two have genuinely different priors from one of them is being lazy or playing status games. Hard cap of 700 words, and lead with the checklist itself — I'll skim the cases afterwards.
