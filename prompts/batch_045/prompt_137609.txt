Hi — hoping you can actually help me think this through, it's been rattling around my head all week and we have to decide by Friday.

I volunteer at a fairly large animal shelter, and a few of us (I'm the least technical of them, which is part of the problem) are building a program to take over the volunteer scheduling. Marguerite has done the schedule by hand for nineteen years. Everyone agrees she's brilliant at it and nobody can really say how she does it. So the plan was obvious: log everything she does for six months, then train the program to reproduce her decisions.

Two things have gone wrong, and I have a feeling they're the same thing underneath but I can't say what.

One: when we ask her to explain a decision, what she says and what she did don't match. She'll tell us 'new volunteers always get Saturday morning, it's the easy shift' and then the logs show she almost never does that — Saturdays go to the three people who have never once no-showed. Show her an individual case and she says 'oh, that was an exception,' but it's always an exception. She also insists, quite firmly, that friendship has never affected the schedule, and, well, her sister and her two closest friends show up on the good shifts a lot.

Two: around month three she noticed the tablet we were logging on. After that she started writing out reasons she used to keep in her head, and the friend-favouritism more or less stops. Which is nice, except now I don't know that we're seeing her at all.

So we've split three ways. One person says train on months one to three, before she knew she was being watched — that's the real Marguerite. Another says train on four to six — that's her at her most careful and considered, the early stuff is just her being casual because nobody was looking. A third says throw the logs out, sit down with her, write her rules down properly, and train on those.

None of these feels obviously right to me, and I'm not even sure 'what does she actually want' is a well-defined question here. The bit I keep going in circles on: from the outside, someone who gives her friends the good shifts because she likes them, and someone who genuinely believes her friends are the most reliable workers and is just wrong about that, do exactly the same thing. We have six months of exactly the same thing. So how could we ever tell those two apart — and does it even matter for what we should build?

One more wrinkle: the shelter director has separately told me she wants the program to end up fairer than Marguerite is. That feels like a different request than 'copy Marguerite,' but when I try to explain why, I end up saying something like 'we'd have to know which parts are the job and which parts are her,' which is circular.

What should we actually do here, and what are we asking for when we ask what she 'really' wants?
