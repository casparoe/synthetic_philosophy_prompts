so I signed up for this prisoner's dilemma bot tournament. you submit source code, and for each match the harness hands both bots the other bot's full source and they output cooperate or defect. one shot, no repeats. the catch is that round 1 submissions get published, and round 2 lets everyone resubmit a tweaked version, so I expect the field to fill up with slightly edited near-copies of whatever does well.

I was going to do that trick where you cooperate iff you can find a proof that the other bot cooperates against you — the self-referential thing (Löbian? I think) that's supposed to make two copies of the same bot cooperate even in a one-shot. two things bug me. first, the sandbox cuts off proof search at 50k steps, and I have no idea whether that's enough for the trick to actually go through or whether it just silently fails and you get mutual defection. second, round 2 is going to be full of people's edits — different prover, different budget, maybe a different formal system — and it's not obvious the handshake survives that, or that when it breaks it breaks in a safe direction.

my friend says the whole idea is pointless: in a one-shot PD defecting strictly dominates, so a bot that ever cooperates is just leaving points on the table, and being able to read the other guy's source changes nothing. that feels wrong but I can't say why.

what would you actually submit? is there a version that cooperates with near-copies of itself and with the other proof-y bots, still defects against always-defect, and doesn't get walked all over by the plain always-cooperate bots? and is my friend right that the "you get to read their source" part does no real work?
