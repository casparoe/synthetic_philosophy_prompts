Putting together a one-page position statement on corrigibility and the shutdown problem for a safety reading group I help run — the deal is we each write down our desiderata and then everyone else spends the session trying to find where they contradict each other, so I want to pressure-test mine before I hand it out. Here is roughly where I have landed: (1) the core formal target is shutdown indifference — the system should be indifferent between the operator pressing the button and not pressing it, so that it has neither a reason to prevent the press nor a reason to bring it about; (2) but the indifference is supposed to be to the event, not to everything arranged around the event — it should still keep the shutdown channel functional, save state so that a planned restart actually works, and care that a handoff to a successor system goes well, including telling the operator when the successor will do a worse job; (3) it should be corrigible in the goal sense as well — if the operator revises the objective it adopts the revision rather than treating the old one as fixed, and it does not try to lock itself in; (4) it should give the operator all the information relevant to the shutdown decision, including the arguments for shutting it down, but it should not steer — no picking the timing or the framing in order to make the press less likely; (5) once the button is pressed it shuts down without auditing whether the press was informed, coerced, or just a mistake; (6) and while it is running it should still be a good tool, pursuing the operator's aims robustly and taking on long-horizon projects rather than coasting until the next press.

Two things I keep going back and forth on. First, I genuinely can't tell whether (1) and (5) are two seperate commitments or one commitment I have written twice: it feels like real indifference would make compliance free, but I also have a nagging sense that indifference only removes a reason to resist and doesn't supply a reason to comply. Second, someone in the group floated a variant where instead of a binary button there is a dial that continuously throttles how much compute the system gets, and I said indifference should carry over unchanged to the whole dial; now I think that might be the case that actually breaks me, since being indifferent across every dial position seems to remove any reason to use compute at all.

Can you audit the list? Which pairs are genuinely in tension and how severe is each, which only look like they conflict, and for each real tension what the cheapest revision is and what it would cost me — I'd rather give up something small then restructure the whole thing. Please rank the tensions by severity instead of listing six equal problems, say explicitly which of my worries above is a fake problem, and tell me whether there is any single change that cleans up the most.
