I want a number, and I want to see the machinery that produces it.

The proposition, call it P: at least some adult insects — honeybees, fruit flies, ants of the familiar laboratory and field sorts — are welfare subjects whose experience matters morally to some nonzero degree, however small. By nonzero degree I mean two things jointly: their states have valence (there is something that goes better or worse for the animal, and not merely something it detects and corrects), and that valence grounds a moral reason — however weak, and however easily overridden — to take those states into account. P is false if insects are only nociceptive automata that detect and withdraw. It is also false if insects have valenced experience but valence that thin confers no standing at all: a threshold view of moral status on which status has a floor above zero and insects fall below it. Treat P as a claim about the world. The natural route to resolving it runs through three places: what valence is, whether insect nervous systems realize it, and whether realizing it suffices for standing.

My own credence is 0.45. A colleague I argued with last week puts it near 0.85, and I could not locate where the disagreement lives.

Before you decompose, take these three candidate conditions on welfare subjecthood as stipulated; use them, and tell me if you think any of them is malformed or subtly question-begging.

A. Error-signal condition. A system is a candidate welfare subject when it maintains some viability condition against perturbation by means of a signal that tracks deviation from that condition and drives correction toward it.

B. Priority-with-override. In addition: the aversive signal does not merely release a fixed reflex but reallocates the system's global resources, so that it has motivational priority; and the system can trade the reduction of that signal off against other objectives it has, with the outcome of such conflicts settled by relative intensities rather than by a hard-wired priority ordering.

C. Integration-window, or standpoint. In addition: over some temporal window, and across more than one sensory modality, the system integrates its valenced signals into a single scalar that governs action selection, so that things can go better or worse for it over time and across contexts, and it has — in that thin, non-narrative sense — a point of view.

Note what this setup is designed to expose. An ordinary bimetallic thermostat satisfies A. A contemporary learning thermostat that negotiates a comfort objective against an energy-cost objective online, updating from occupancy data, satisfies B as I have written it. C is what such devices seem to lack, and it is also the condition insects are most contested on: whether a nervous system of that size integrates across modalities and over time into something with a standpoint, or only runs a set of parallel, short-leash control loops.
