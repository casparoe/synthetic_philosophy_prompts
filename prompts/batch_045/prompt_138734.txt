for a reading group on Harnad's symbol grounding problem I want to pin down, before we read any more of the literature, what a *solution* would actually have to deliver: the requirement list, with the case behind each requirement, and your guess at which pair of requirements can't both be satisfied. By solution I mean one good enough for a particular job — deciding whether a system that has never percieved the world but talks as if it has can be trusted when it reports on it — not one good enough to settle whether there's anything it's like to be that system.

Two cases I want any list to handle, because I think they separate genuine requirements from the tastes of one camp:

(1) borrowed grounding. A system with no sensors of its own, but whose internal state for "red" is driven, through a trained interface, by a sighted human's visual-cortex activity while she looks at red things, and by nothing else ever. Its symbol covaries with red things in the world, but only via her. Set it next to the purely text-trained model and next to the robot with its own camera, and say what your requirements predict about each.

(2) the severed channel. A robot that passes every behavioural test anyone has proposed for grounding, but whose sensor cables were cut and replaced by a simulator that reproduces the exact signals they used to carry, so its "red" state has no causal route to red things at all.

My suspicion is the list ends up containing both "grounding has to show up in something we could in principle test from outside" and "grounding isn't constitutively a matter of behaviour", and that those two come apart — but I'd like to hear where else the requirements bite each other. Which single requirement rules out the most candidate theories, and which ones on the list are really just one camp's prejudice?
