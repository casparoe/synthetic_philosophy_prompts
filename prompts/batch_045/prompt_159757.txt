ok here's the situation: I'm a fourth-year PhD student, I run a very small semi-anonymous philosophy substack (like 200 readers, mostly metaontology-adjacent people who will catch everything, plus some friends from undergrad who won't), and our reading group has been grinding through the verbal-disputes literature — the verbal/substantive distinction, Hirsch-style quantifier variance where both parties speak truly in their own language, Carnapian internal vs. external questions, Sider-style ontological realism with its privileged joint-carving vocabulary that he calls Ontologese. The thing we keep getting stuck on, and that I want to make fun of, is this move somebody described to me at a conference and whose author I cannot for the life of me remember — call it second-order quantifier variance: you run the deflationist machinery on the metalinguistic level, so that "verbal dispute" itself is treated as quantifier-variant or framework-relative; then any dissolution can itself be dissolved, and saying "this dispute is merely verbal" is only true relative to a framework the deflationist hasn't non-arbitrarily selected — but equally the deflationist can always reply that the charge that her dissolution is merely verbal is itself merely verbal, so she is simultaneously unable to make her own point substantively and completely immune to criticism. The regress is the joke, and I want the piece to end up in it rather than comment on it from outside.

So: write me a parody internal document from a made-up journal — let's call it The Journal of Ontological Arbitration — consisting of a short cover memo (maybe 120-180 words) from the editor-in-chief announcing a new editorial policy, followed by 6-8 numbered manuscript decision-letter templates with bracketed fill-in fields and a few in-house footnotes, running maybe 600-700 words for the templates. The voice is the managing editor, who is a completely sincere and completely committed deflationist and is genuinely baffled and a bit wounded that people keep submitting arguments instead of retranslations. Her policy, stated deadpan: since every dispute that reaches the journal turns out to be verbal, the journal will stop publishing arguments and will publish only retranslation manuals. Templates should include, at minimum: the standard rejection that declares the submitted dispute verbal (with the diagnostic test done right — agreement on the relevant facts, disagreement only about the language, and the check for whether a neutral paraphrase dissolves it); the "both of you are speaking truly in your own idiolect, please stop writing to us" letter; the letter to the author who objects that his dispute is not verbal, which dissolves that objection too and should be flagged as applying recursively; a revise-and-resubmit converting a paper into a retranslation manual; a letter to an ontological realist who insists on writing in Ontologese, where the editor quietly treats Ontologese as just one more framework while needing something like a privileged notion of "verbal" to say so; and a letter to the authors of the second-order quantifier variance paper, which the board cannot decide whether to accept or dissolve, because dissolving it is exactly what the paper says dissolving is. Include one quoted complaint letter from an invented submitting realist (made-up name, made-up department — nobody real) and the editor's deadpan reply. The final template should turn the policy on itself: the journal's own dissolution policy is, by its own lights, a verbal dispute, so the journal has never said anything — with the only crack in the deadpan being a practical one, e.g. a house-style footnote about subscription renewals or the editorial assistant quietly asking whether the journal still exists.

Constraints: keep it deadpan and let a reader tell what's being ironized without anybody winking — no "of course this is absurd" asides, no meta-commentary from the editor admitting the regress is a problem. The laughs have to come from getting the actual machinery right (the fact-agreement condition, charitable reinterpretation, internal/external questions, joint-carving, the thought that nobody ever actually loses a verbal dispute) so that a metaontologist laughs instead of winces, while a non-specialist can still follow it. And no citations or bibliography at all — invented cross-references to the journal's own house style are fine, real references are not, since half my readers will be reading it in a place where they can't look anything up.
