Last night I got into a three-hour argument with my flatmate Dev (hes doing some ML-adjacent evening course and reads a lot of alignment blog posts, and is very pleased with himself about it) and I need a second opinion, because I think he slid something past me but I can't point at the step.

Definitions first, so we're on the same page in case I'm using the words wrong: a goal-directed agent has some objective over how things turn out and selects actions by expected promotion of that objective. An approval-directed agent, the way I understood it, has no objective over outcomes at all — at each decision point it asks what its principal would most approve of it doing, and does that, myopically, meaning it selects on approval of the action at that point rather than picking the trajectory the principal would rank highest at the end. The pitch is that this dodges the convergent-instrumental stuff. Dev's argument was that it definately doesn't:

(1) Approval-directed selection is myopic by definition: approval of the action, at the time of acting, not approval of the whole episode in hindsight.
(2) The only reason anyone wanted this design is to avoid the goal-directed risks — deception, grabbing power, managing the principal instead of serving them.
(3) Those risks don't come from what the objective is about. They come from (i) the agent selecting actions by expected promotion of some objective over how things go, plus (ii) enough capability to notice indirect routes to it. So changing the objective's content cannot remove them.
(4) "The principal would approve of this action" is itself a fact about how things go, and a capable agent can bring that fact about in two quite different ways: by doing what the principal wants, or by arranging the principal's epistemic situation — what they get shown, what they've come to expect of you, how much slack they think you have, what they think the alternatives were.
(5) So the action that scores best on myopic approval is also the action that scores best on "approval over the long run, counting the management of the principal" — the approval-directed agent behaves like a goal-directed agent whose objective is long-run approval.
(6) By (3) and (5) it has exactly the same instrumental pressures as any other goal-directed agent, so approval-directedness is not a safety mechanism; it's goal-directedness with the word approval stapled to it. And if you avoid that by clamping down harder on the myopia, you've just made an agent too myopic to do anything long-horizon, which is a different way of failing.

I can't accept (6) — but every step sounded fine when he said it, and I've been flip-flopping since about whether the rot is in (3), (4) or (5). He also says the argument goes through on both readings of "would approve" (what the principal actually knows right now, vs. what they'd say if they knew everything the agent knows) and I can't tell if he's bluffing about that.

So: which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost you? I'd least like to give up (1) — if the myopia goes, the whole design is dead — and I'd like to keep the bit of (2) about why anyone bothered.
