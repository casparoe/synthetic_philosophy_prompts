Next week I'm presenting at a political theory workshop (about 20 min of Q&A) the paper I've been stuck on for a year, and I need a prep sheet, not a defense of the view.

The thesis: compromise between two value systems isn't just second-best to one side winning — it can be a genuine Pareto improvement, because each side can give ground where its values are flat and take ground where they're steep. I call that slope "concession elasticity": what it costs you, by your own lights, to give up a bit more on some dimension. Two things I actually argue: (1) split-the-difference compromise is usually Pareto-dominated by a logroll where each side concedes what's cheap for it; (2) the integrity worry — that compromising corrupts you — bites hardest when you concede on dimensions that are steep for you, which is exactly what the model says not to do. There's also a shaky section claiming elasticity is endogenous, that conceding on a dimension makes later concessions there cheaper, so value systems drift toward each other, and I can't tell whether that's a bug in the model or my real answer to the integrity objection.
