I'm a PhD student in philosophy (formal epistemology and decision theory) running a graduate reading group on anthropic reasoning, and I want a decision tree — a sorting instrument, not a literature survey — that starts from a single commitment and ends in a named position about how many observers there are and how much longer our species lasts.

The start node is this: the participant allows that the fact that she finds herself as this particular person, at this particular time, might bear on how large or how long-lasting the world is. Everything downstream should be a question she can answer without knowing which position it funnels her toward.

I want the first three branch nodes to be, in order: (i) whether her own existence and her being this person carry evidential weight at all, or only practical, decision-theoretic import; (ii) who belongs to the reference class — all actual observers, all possible observers, biological humans only, only creatures whose total evidence matches hers, or some weighting across candidates; (iii) what the sampling procedure counts — persons, or observer-moments, so that a hypothesis on which a thousand subjects each have your evidence once is on a par with one on which a single subject has it a thousand times.

Four cases must do the work of the branch questions, ideally one each for the nodes above plus one further node of your choosing:

1. The Archivist: priors even between a civilization of 10^11 observers, all alive at your time and sharing your evidence, and one of 10^21 observers, of whom the first 10^11 are exactly like you and the rest come later. Does your evidence favor the second?
2. The Uplift: the same contrast, except that the extra 10^21 observers are not biological humans at all — alien minds, or simulations run by your descendants. Do they belong in the sample?
3. The Many-Nows: the same number of persons on both hypotheses, but on one hypothesis each of them has your evidence once, while on the other a single subject has it a thousand times.
4. The Sealed Vaults: two hypotheses differing only in the number of observers in epochs causally sealed off from yours, so that no bet, policy, or intervention of yours could ever turn on which is true. Do your credences still move?

Construct the logical space first — which combinations of answers the nodes actually permit — then locate the positions within it, and mark each unoccupied combination with one line on whether it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unclaimed. Required leaves: the Doomsday-argument combination, the presumptuous-philosopher combination, the combination (if any is coherent) in which those two effects cancel, an observer-moment (SSSA-style) leaf, a no-update leaf, and the deflationary position that indexical facts never bear on non-indexical ones. Where two leaves cannot be separated by any question on your tree, say so rather than manufacturing a distinction.

One ulterior motive you should design for: the group cares whether any of this can underwrite or undercut the very large expected-value estimates that longtermist existential-risk work depends on, so wherever a branch turns on whether the anthropic disagreement is genuinely evidential or merely pragmatic, flag it explicitly. Ten branch nodes at most, and please keep to what fits on a single handout.
