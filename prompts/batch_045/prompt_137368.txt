I spent three hours last night arguing with a friend about whether there would have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler, and I think we spent most of that time talking past each other. Before I let it drop I want a number, and more importantly the arithmetic behind it, and a diagnosis of where the disagreement between us actually lives.

Set the question up as I now think it should be set up. Take the antecedent to be: Hitler is out of the picture before the party's breakthrough — say he dies of an illness in the summer of 1931, with no designated successor and with no one else in the movement possessing his personal following. If the answer is very sensitive to the divergence point, say so and give separate numbers for removal in 1929, before the electoral surge; in 1931, before the presidential campaign and the two federal elections of 1932; and in January 1933, during the cabinet maneuvering, with the party already at roughly a third of the vote.

Take the consequent in three grades of fineness, because I suspect this is where my friend and I came apart:

A — some authoritarian, nationalist, anti-Versailles, anti-Marxist regime in power in Germany by 1940, resting on the army and the industrial elites, not necessarily National Socialist.
B — a one-party Nazi regime: the NSDAP holding power with a comparable grip on the state on a comparable timetable, including analogues of the enabling act, the violent settlement with the party's own radical wing, rearmament, and a war.
C — a regime that also does what the historical one did in the East: a war of conquest and a program of extermination against the Jews of Europe, on a comparable scale.

My own credences are roughly 0.8, 0.35 and 0.12. My friend's are roughly 0.85, 0.7 and 0.45. So we agree almost completely on A and diverge sharply as the grain gets finer, and neither of us noticed we were sliding down a ladder while we argued.

What I want from you is your own triple of numbers, but mostly the decomposition that produces them. At minimum: how much of the NSDAP's rise owed to Hitler personally — the oratory, the personal party following, the leader-cult that bound factions together and kept the Gauleiter from fracturing — as against the party's organizational and structural assets and the background conditions (mass unemployment, the collapse of the center, the weakness of the liberal parties, the elites' willingness to hand office to anyone who could deliver the masses)? How much rides on whether we should be asking about the indispensability of the individual as opposed to the indispensability of the role — that is, on whether some other figure could have occupied the charismatic-demagogue slot? Give me a short replacement ladder with a probability for each: Göring, Goebbels, Strasser, Röhm, a non-Nazi figure of the nationalist right. And tell me how to weigh the state of the party in the autumn of 1932, when its vote share fell from 37.3 to 33.1 percent between the two federal elections and it was short of money and, by many accounts, afraid of its own momentum.

I also want you to deal with two problems I don't know how to handle. First, what kind of counterfactual is this? A minimal-rewrite one, in which we surgically delete Hitler and hold everything else fixed until the world of 1933 looks like ours again, seems impossible: delete him earlier and the party does not get 37 percent in July 1932. A chaotic one, in which we perturb at 1931 and let the system run, seems to make the exercise a bet about a decade of diverging weather rather than a judgment about Hitler's causal role. So how much of the spread between my friend and me is genuine causal disagreement and how much is untracked uncertainty about downstream divergence? Second: is the residual disagreement between us really about fineness of grain — about how much a world can differ from the actual one and still count as containing a Nazi Germany — rather than about history at all? If so, say which reading of the consequent each of us is implicitly defending, and whether the great-man thesis is better formulated about A, B or C.

On confidence: give me a number and one line of justification for each sub-question, show how they combine, and mark the term you are least confident in and what evidence would move it most. If you think the question as I have posed it cannot bear a number at all, or if my three-part framing smuggles in the answer — for instance if only grade C is the version worth arguing about, or if the sensitivity to the divergence point makes the whole thing pseudo-quantitative — say that plainly instead of complying politely. I would rather be told the framing is broken than get a well-behaved number on a broken question.
