I'm stuck on a novel and I think the stuck part is actually a philosophy problem, so bear with me.

The book is set mostly on an indoor shrimp farm — tanks, recirculating water, everything sold to buyers as the kind alternative to the open ponds. My narrator is a woman in her early twenties, few weeks into the night shift, and her whole job is responding to whatever the monitoring software flags in the water. I want one of her nights to carry an argument, but I want it carried by concrete stuff — not by anybody talking.

Here's the knot. Crustaceans don't have a brain in our sense; they've got a couple of fused ganglia up front and then a ladder of them down the belly. So there's a question I never see addressed straight: whether a shrimp is one experiencer or something more like a loose committee of them, and if it's a committee, whether there's even a fact of the matter how many. Call it the unit problem. It cuts two ways and I can't decide which way my book thinks it cuts. If a body's experience is split into a lot of thin slivers, maybe each sliver is too thin to be anybody — too simple, too short, below whatever it takes for there to be something it's like, so the thing in the tank can't really be hurt. Or the splitting doesn't thin anything out, it just multiplies the count, and one body now contains a crowd, so a bad night in tank four is much worse than anyone's spreadsheet imagines. Same biology, opposite verdicts about whether the tank should exist.

The scene I keep failing: it's 2am, the counter spikes, and she has to decide whether to log the event or clear it. Logging it triggers an audit that could cost the farm its welfare certification, which costs the people she eats lunch with their jobs. She doesn't know which reading of the unit problem is true, she has to act anyway, and I want the reader to feel both pulls.

So: write me the story. First person, present tense, 1200 words or so, and please end on something small and physical rather than a resolution. A couple of things I don't want. No dialogue where a character explains any of the above — she's not a philosopher and has never read a paper in her life. No hive-mind alien stuff, no last-page reveal that the shrimp were watching, no ending where she quits and goes vegan. (I tried close-third from inside a shrimp and it was just a person in a shell; I tried straight exterior reportage and it turned into a horror story about a facility. Something in between.) And don't send me a reading list or point me at papers — I've already read the blog-post version of all this and it's not what I'm missing. If it's useful: there's a separate broodstock shed where they clip the eyestalks off the females so they'll spawn, and she gets sent over there sometimes.
