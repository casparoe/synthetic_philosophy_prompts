hey — I need a scene for a training thing I'm writing about visual communication, and I want it as a dialogue, not an explainer.

Setup: a county health department is shipping a one-page card about a preventive treatment. It gets handed out at the pharmacy counter and stuck in waiting rooms. The population is genuinely mixed — a lot of languages, low average health literacy, and most people will see the card exactly once, for maybe forty seconds, while holding a kid or a phone. The info that has to get across: take the second dose only if the rash hasn't spread past the marked line within 48 hours; roughly 1 in 400 people get a bad reaction; don't take it at all if you're pregnant or on a certain very common blood pressure drug; and come back if you get a fever.

Two people have to sign off on the layout by friday.

Ines is a designer who works in the Neurath / Isotype lineage — the Vienna Method of picture statistics, standardized pictograms, quantity shown by repeating identical units rather than by scaling one up, no perspective, words carrying the relations between pictures rather than the pictures doing everything. She's also honest about the part clients usually skip: for her the picture language was never a self-explanatory standalone substitute for text, it was a helping language that only pays off if someone runs an ongoing visual-education program — repeated exposure, one institution maintaining the vocabulary over years.

Dev runs clinical comms for the department, used to work as a pharmacy tech, and thinks the whole pictogram push is a boondoggle. His actual argument, which I want to land hard, is that a picture that expressive isn't pre-literate at all: by the time your system can do conditionals, negation, a time window and a base rate, you've built a writing system, and writing systems have to be taught, so you've reinvented literacy with worse tooling and no dictionary.

Give each of them a real win and make them respond to what the other person actually says — no "as you know" exposition, no strawmen, and they can name-drop Neurath only if it comes up naturally. A few things I want chewed on: Ines wants a dotted-out unit to stand for the fraction in "1 in 400" and a three-frame strip for the 48-hour window; she has a rule that a picture only ships if a viewer can read the right instruction back to you. Dev should point out that the read-back rule just measures whether people can guess the designers' convention, and that confident wrong guesses are the failure mode that actually hurts. In their hallway test last spring, most people read the diagonal "do not" bar as marking the medicine itself. Also: legal requires the exact risk number in words somewhere on the card, and Ines thinks that line will do more damage than the pictures do good.

End it with a concrete description of the card they actually ship, plus the one thing each of them still thinks is wrong with it. 600 to 900 words, and please dont have them agree more than they would in real life.
