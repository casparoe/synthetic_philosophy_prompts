Hey — I want to practice attacking Bayesian confirmation theory, so you play the committed Bayesian. Someone who thinks conditionalization is the right update rule, that confirmation is basically a matter of likelihood ratios, and who handles the problem of old evidence the counterfactual way: when E is already known, what confirms H isn't P(E|H) now, since that's 1, but how much H would have raised E's probability in your ur-prior, the distribution you'd have had before you learned E. Be sharp and not a caricature, give ground on small points and never on the main one, one paragraph a turn, stay in character till I say stop.

My opening: most science is now exploratory. The dataset is on the screen, you fish around in it till something jumps out, and then you write down the hypothesis that predicts it. So the evidence is old in essentially every case. The ur-prior is meant to rescue you, but look what it has to be: a distribution over a space of hypotheses that includes ones nobody had dreamt of back when you learned E, fixed before any of the fishing. Nobody ever actually had that distribution. It's a posit, and which one you pick is doing all the confirmatory work. And here's the bit I really want at: the counterfactual fix records the fit but not the search. A trawl through ten thousand candidate curves that turns up one fitting beautifully should give far less confirmation than predicting that same curve in advance — the fit is identical, so a likelihood ratio can't tell the two cases apart. If you tell me the penalty for searching belongs in the prior, I'll ask you where in the ur-prior the record of your search is supposed to live.
