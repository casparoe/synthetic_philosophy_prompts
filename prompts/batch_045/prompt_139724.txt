I'm a philosophy PhD student working on a paper about what we should even mean by calling an AI system deceptive or dishonest, and I want to use a concrete case in the intro instead of another baroque thought experiment nobody will remember. My supervisor thinks opening with a film is a cop-out unless the film actually does philosophical work, so I want to do it properly, and I keep loosing an argument with myself about Ex Machina (Garland, 2014). Here's the setup as I see it. There are at least three candidate defintions on the table: a functional or behavioural one, where an output counts as deceptive if it's apt to induce a false belief in someone else and does so in the service of the system's goals, with no intent or inner states required — basically the way people talk about deceptive signalling in animal communication; an insincerity account, where deception requires the utterance to come apart from what the system itself assigns high probability, which is fine as an idea but awkward in practice because it's contested whether a neural net has anything playing the belief role at all, so the usual workaround is some kind of cross-context test (does it say the true thing under a different prompt, with the weights frozen?); and something in the neighbourhood of Frankfurt's indifference-to-truth, on which a lot of what gets called machine 'lying' is closer to bullshit or confabulation — not aimed at producing a false belief so much as not aimed at the truth one way or the other. I've also been toying with a fourth thought of my own: if a language model is best understood as a simulator that inhabits whatever role the context sets up, then there may be no stable speaker for a given utterance to be sincere or insincere, and maybe the right unit of assessment is the system-in-a-deployment-context rather than the individual string. If that fourth thing is just a version of one of the first three, or is obviously broken, say so in a sentence, but please don't point me at papers — I'm offline for a week and I'd rather you just argue it.
