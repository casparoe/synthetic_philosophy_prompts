I've never studied any philosophy, I drive a van for a living and this is just stuff I've been chewing on since me and my brother had a row about it at christmas. I've tried to write down what I actually believe about whether doing the right thing counts for anything, and I definately feel like I'm contradicting myself somewhere but I can't find where. I don't know what any of these positions are called so please don't assume I'm secretly signed up to some theory.

1) Whether you get any credit for doing the right thing depends on why you did it. My brother found a wallet in a car park and handed it in, but only because he clocked somebody watching him and didn't want to look like a thief. I don't think he gets credit for honesty there, even though the person got their wallet back.

2) But I don't think enjoying it cancels the credit. My mum volunteers at a food bank and she loves it, she'd do it for free if nobody ever thanked her. I don't think that makes her less of a good person than someone who hates every minute of it.

3) I do think the person who has to drag themselves to do it deserves more credit than the person who finds it easy.

4) I think you can judge people for what they would've done, not just what they did. Somebody who never joined in the bullying only because they were never around when it happened, or couldn't be bothered, isn't a better person than the one who joined in.

5) I also think it's unfair to judge people for things that aren't up to them — what instincts you got, whether you were raised to notice other people, whether you're brave. That's mostly luck. Except my uncle was raised by genuinely awful people and turned out the same way and I do blame him for it, and I'm not going to stop.

6) In practice I try not to hand out this kind of credit or blame at all, because you can never really know why anyone did anything, including yourself.

7) If somebody genuinely tries to do the right thing and it goes wrong through no fault of theirs — they try to help and botch it, or the money they gave went nowhere — I think they deserve just as much credit as if it had worked. The results aren't the point.

8) This is the one I go round in circles on. Say there was a pill that gave you true beliefs about what the right thing is and also made you want to do it. I think if you took it, the good stuff you did afterwards would still count. But a pill that just made you nicer — same behaviour, no true beliefs, you simply want to help people now — I think that one's creepy and what you do afterwards isn't really yours. I can't explain why I feel different about the two.

So: which of these are genuinely pulling against each other, and how badly? I'm guessing a couple of them only look inconsistent and are fine once you spell something out, so tell me which those are too. For the real ones, say what I'd have to give up and what it'd cost me, and rank them worst first. If two of them only clash because of something I believe without realising I believe it, tell me what that something is. And if there's one single change that tidies up the most of this at once, I want to know which — I've genuinely no idea which of the eight is the rotten one, so don't just tell me what I want to hear. Don't send me off to read any books or papers, I'm not going to.
