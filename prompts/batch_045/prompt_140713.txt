hey, I'm working on a short paper about whether interpretability work on neural nets has quietly re-run the indeterminacy-of-translation argument, and I want to stage it as "what would Quine say." He was gone in 2000, so neural machine translation and all the embedding/feature stuff came well after him — that's the point. I know the doctrine (gavagai, rabbit vs undetached rabbit part vs rabbit stage, translation manuals, the inscrutability bit from the ontological relativity essays, his slogan that to be is to be the value of a bound variable), so please don't re-explain it at me and please don't give me citations or a reading list. What I want is him actually engaging with a concrete set-up.

The set-up is invented, so don't try to look it up:

A lab trains a model, call it RAMA, on egocentric video plus a big text corpus in a language nobody on the team reads. They then learn a linear map from RAMA's internal activations into an English sentence-embedding space, and it does great on held-out caption matching. Then they notice five things:

1. Two different maps, M1 and M2, both hit about the same accuracy on every behavioural test anyone designs — caption matching, forced choice, next-utterance prediction — but they disagree about what one particular feature F is a feature *of*. M1 says rabbit. M2 says something like rabbit-stage or undetached rabbit part.

2. Maps don't compose. Aligning RAMA to English and then English to a third model gives a different map than aligning RAMA to that model directly, and the gap gets bigger as the models get bigger and more capable. Nobody can find any privileged or canonical map.

3. On inputs where M1 and M2 make different calls — a photorealistic hologram of a rabbit, a rabbit-shaped cake, a taxidermied rabbit, a person in a rabbit suit, a rabbit seen in a mirror, a rabbit made of rabbits — RAMA's downstream behaviour tracks how common the case was in training and doesn't cleanly side with either manual.

4. Unlike the field linguist with the remote tribe, the team has complete access to the internals: every weight, any probe, any ablation, any surgery, full determinism and replay. All the physical facts are on the table.

5. A successor model, RAMA-2, was fine-tuned so that its internals look like they mean "rabbit" to every probe the team can think of, while it was in fact trained on something else entirely. Half the lab says this proves the earlier question was empty; the other half says this is just what indeterminacy looks like from the inside.

Questions I want him to answer, in a voice that's recognisably his — tough-minded, deflationary, allergic to meanings as entities, happy to be accused of behaviourism and then deny it:

- Given finding 4, is there a fact of the matter about what F means? Does his argument that reference is inscrutable even granted all the physical facts go through unchanged for an artifact, or does something about the anthropological case carry weight — shared evolutionary history, a common environment, ostension, assent and dissent, a linguistic community, the fact that a manual has to cash out in someone's behaviour?
- Would he even let us say RAMA "refers" or "believes" anything, or is that vocabulary the thing he'd throw out first? And if we do get an ontology, whose is it — does he think the model has one at all, or is the ontology ours, imported by the manual?
- Finding 2: would he say he predicted this, or would he say it merely illustrates the thesis rather than supporting it, and what's the difference on his terms?
- Is the whole "align the space to English" project a case of what he'd call semantic ascent, and if so does that make the residual disagreement about F verbal or does he think it's still substantive?
- Pragmatist worry: the manual doesn't just predict, it lets you steer the model and build working systems. Does that *use* count for him for anything, or does he say successful prediction and control is exactly all there ever was and the rest is idle?
- Where would he split from a present-day interpretability researcher who says F means rabbit because it causes rabbit-detecting behaviour? And where would he split from the person who says the whole question is pseudo — I suspect he'd annoy both.
- What if the M1/M2 difference never shows up in any behaviour anyone can elicit no matter how clever the elicitation? And what if someone then finds an intervention that makes it show up? Does either move him, and which way?
- One thing that would actually move him: name a single result you could hand him that he'd take as a genuine counterexample, and say how confident he'd be that it never arrives. Also flag where you're extrapolating past anything he actually held — I'd rather know than have it sound smooth.
