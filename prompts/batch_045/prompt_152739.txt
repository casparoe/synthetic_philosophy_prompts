I'm a software engineer with no philosophy background, and I donate a chunk of my income to animal stuff, so I end up arguing about this more then is good for me.

Here's the proposition I want a number on, stated as precisely as I can. Take a hectare of intact lowland tropical forest in a place where the realistic counterfactual is that it gets cleared for low-intensity cattle pasture within about twenty years anyway. Compare two worlds over the next hundred years. World A: it's cleared now, stays pasture for the full hundred years, no intensification, no rewilding, and the beef demand it serves doesn't cause an equivalent hectare somewhere else to get cleared instead. World B: it's left intact. My credence that A contains less total wild-animal suffering than B is about 0.3. A friend who's thought about this way more than me puts it at 0.75 and thinks I'm just squeamish about the word destruction. I want to know where that gap actually lives.

Give me your own number first, then the decomposition. Here are the terms I can see, but please add the ones I'm missing:

- that the animals in B are on balance living net-negative lives across the century, not just that some of them are, and not just that many of them die badly at the end
- that clearing actually reduces how much animal life there is on that land across the century, rather than swapping a forest community for a pasture community with as many or more animals in it, and then what to do with the cattle, the rodents and birds pasture supports, and the insects, since insects are where most of the individuals are
- that the counterfactual holds up: no leakage pushing clearing somewhere else, no rewilding, no later intensification
- that deaths themselves go into the ledger the way I'm assuming, i.e. that removing a net-negative life is a gain rather than a deprivation
- that knock-on effects on predators, scavengers and neighbouring fragments dont swamp the local effect
- that this is the kind of question where the expected value isn't dominated by some tail I can't see coming

Then combine them, tell me which single term your final number is most sensitive to, and which one you trust least. For the one you trust least, say what datum would actually move it, and be realistic: I can't run experiments on a hectare of rainforest, so tell me what's genuinely obtainable rather than what would be ideal.

Two more things, because these are where I get stuck.

First, my friend has this move where she says the right unit isn't the animal but the flow of energy through the food web, so the real question is suffering per unit of net primary productivity per year, and the headcount is just packaging. I find that both compelling and suspicious, because it seems to imply that an intervention which held productivity fixed but rearranged it into many short-lived small animals instead of a few long-lived large ones would be roughly neutral, and that doesn't feel right to me. Is that unit switch doing real work, or is it a way of smuggling in the conclusion?

Second, the tail thing. If the honest answer is that population dynamics under habitat conversion are chaotic enough that I can't even sign the expectation, then my 0.3 is fake precision and I should be doing something else with the money entirely. How much of your number is that worry, and how much is genuine disagreement about the substance?
