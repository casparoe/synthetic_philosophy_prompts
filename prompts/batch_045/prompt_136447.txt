Here's the pile I keep circling, in no order:

- Idle stakes. Encroachment arguments usually lean on the thought that stakes matter because belief is tied to action. But take cases where the stakes are enormous and no live decision is left — you've already irrevocably committed, or you'll never act on it, or you're a pure predictor with no action available at all. If stakes still encroach there, the practical-rationality motivation starts to look like a red herring; if they don't, encroachment owes us an account of what makes stakes "live," and that account might be doing all the real work.
- Engineered or adversarial stakes. Someone else gets to set your stakes — an insurer, a platform, an interrogator, the designer of a system that decides whether its sub-component knows something. If knowledge varies with stakes and stakes are manipulable by third parties, then knowing becomes something agents can be deprived of from the outside. Reductio, feature, or just where the view was always headed?
- Diachronic drift. Low stakes when the belief was formed and the inquiry finished; stakes spike later (or the reverse). Did you know at the earlier time? Whose context settles it — the attributor then or now? This seems to bite differently on contextualism and encroachment, and it looks upstream of a pile of applied stuff: testimony chains, institutional records, stored model outputs that get reused in new settings.
- Whether encroachment is really a rival to contextualism or just contextualism about the subject's practical situation rather than the attributor's conversational one — and whether the assessment-relativist package is what both are groping at. Is the whole dispute downstream of a choice about where to put the shifting parameter?
- The meta-question. The data are basically vignette intuitions about bank-type cases. So is the real question which package explains that pattern most cheaply (invariantism plus an error theory about why the intuitions mislead, contextualism, relativism, encroachment), and if so how much of the debate is just parsimony aesthetics? Relatedly: how much weight should any of that data bear, given how fragile vignette responses turn out to be?
- Design consequences. If stakes move knowledge, should institutions deliberately engineer stakes — oaths, deposits, liability rules — to get the knowledge facts they want? Does that make sense, or does it quietly collapse into straightforward behavior manipulation?
