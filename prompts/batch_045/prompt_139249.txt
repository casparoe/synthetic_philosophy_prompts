Hey — I'm a senior engineer at a company that makes a mental-health app (mood tracking, CBT-ish exercises, a human coach you can text). I've got no philosophy background; I'm just the token engineer on our Language and Safety Working Group, the five-person group that has to sign off on any copy in the app that makes a claim about the user. We meet next Tuesday and I got asked to write the recommendation memo. Please write it for me, in American English, as something I can actually circulate: engineers, two clinicians, our head of support, and the CFO. It should open with the recommendation and a timeline, and it should give the strongest version of the argument for whichever option we don't pick — the CFO will push back and I'd rather the memo say it first than have it ambush me in the meeting.

The case: product wants to ship a "Clinical Handoff" screen. After two weeks of logging, the app would show the person a summary — "your anxiety symptoms have been above threshold for 11 of the last 14 days", a severity band (mild / moderate / severe) derived from a questionnaire, the line "this is a medical condition, like diabetes", and a button to generate a PDF with a billing code on it to hand to a doctor. Our clinicians argue this is the opposite of stigmatizing: it tells people their suffering is real and not a character flaw, and without the code nobody gets paid for treating them, and most of our users can't pay out of pocket.

On the other side, one of our advisors — an ex-patient who runs an advocacy nonprofit — keeps invoking this psychiatrist from the 60s, Thomas Szasz, who apparently argued that mental illness is basically a metaphor: a real disease is a lesion you can find in the body, and calling distress an illness is a way of talking about problems in living that also happens to hand power to doctors and the state. His version of the objection is that we'd be handing a stranger a diagnostic identity generated by a scoring function, and that swapping in "disorder" instead of "illness" is the same thing in a lab coat. Two things complicate it: our terms of service currently say the app is not a medical device and does not diagnose; and last year marketing shipped a whole campaign built on the idea that people aren't broken, they're dealing with something, which now reads like the exact oppposite of the new screen.

Beyond picking one of the three options (ship as specced / ship it with wording rules I can hand to the copywriters / drop the diagnostic summary and do a non-diagnostic handoff), here's what I need the memo to actually settle:

- whether "disorder" or "condition" instead of "illness" changes anything, or whether that's just cosmetics — our clinicians genuinely think it does, and I can't tell
- whether the "like diabetes" line is doing real work or sneaking in a claim we can't back
- whether Szasz's coercion worry touches us at all. We're not committing anybody, but a PDF with a code on it ends up in insurance, custody, and disability paperwork. If the worry only bites in those downstream uses, shouldn't that change what we say on the screen rather than whether we ship the thing at all?
- the bit I keep going in circles on: how to think about a framing that might be true for practical purposes and false as a description at the same time. I suspect I'm just confused here and I'd like the memo to say clearly which way it's cutting it.

Assume whatever you want about details I left out, just flag what you assumed. Keep it under about 1200 words if you can, no header formatting beyond what a memo needs.
