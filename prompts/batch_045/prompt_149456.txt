Not sure this is the right place, but I'm after a name, if one exists. Please answer in American English.

I work in palliative care and I keep noticing that pain seems to come apart in more than one direction, and whenever I try to look it up I only ever find one of them.

Case one is the one I assume is documented: the patient says the pain is completely unchanged — same spot, same burning quality, still rates it 8 out of 10 for intensity — but says it doesn't bother them anymore. They joke about it, they skip doses, they'll say "its there, I just don't care."

Case two is the one I can't find a word for: patient reports no pain at all, rates it zero, genuinely insists nothing is wrong — but they can't sit still, they guard the area, they grimace, they watch the clock for the next dose and get upset if its late, and if you point at the grimace they seem surprised, as if they'd only just learned from their own face that something was bothering them.

And then a third thing I'm not even sure belongs in the same family: patient says everything is awful, unbearable, make it stop — but there's nothing to point at. No location, no burning or stabbing quality, no number they'll give you. Just "it's terrible."

Do these have names, and are they really three things or two? I'm less after a label than after the carving. Which of them is the actual mirror image of case one, and how would I tell case two apart from plain denial, or from just being distracted and not noticing? How would I tell case three apart from anxiety or low mood? And does the direction the split runs in matter for the question of what makes pain bad in the first place — does a case where the sensation stays and the badness goes off tell us something different from a case where the badness seems to stay and the sensation goes off? If case two has no name, give it one and tell me what would count as a clean instance of it and what wouldn't.
