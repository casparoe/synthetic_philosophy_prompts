So there's 12 owners in my building and we're voting next week on whether to spend about $90k of our reserve fund on a rooftop solar + battery setup. The guy who's been pushing it for months presents it and speaks first, then we go around the room in a fixed order and each person says yes or no plus whatever they want to add. I'm sixth.

I've actually looked into it, which most of them haven't. I got a second quote that's about 30% cheaper for basically the same panels, and my brother in law who does electrical work had a look at the roof and says it'll need resurfacing in four or five years, which means paying to have the whole thing taken off and put back. My rough numbers say it pays for itself in something like 14 years, the warranty is 10, and I'll probably have moved before then anyway. So I think it's a bad deal at the quoted price, and honestly the payback is bad for the people staying too, not just for me.

What's bugging me is less the solar thing and more the meeting. The first four people who'll speak are all friends of the guy proposing it, and I'm fairly sure none of them have looked at a single number — two of them never come to meetings at all. Then further down the order there's a woman who's a structural engineer and actually knows about this kind of thing, but she's quiet and I'd bet she just says "sounds good" if everyone before her is enthusiastic. Also, most of them know I'm thinking of moving and that I voted against the last two big spend proposals, so I worry anything I say is easy to wave off.

My friend says I should just say nothing and vote no at the end, because the first four yeses are worthless — they're people copying each other's enthusiasm, not four independent people who checked. That feels too glib though. Surely four people saying yes in a row is at least a bit of evidence that I'm the one who's wrong, even if each of them was influenced by the one before? I genuinely can't work out how much to move.

I also can't tell whether speaking up even helps. If I say no and lay out the second quote and the roof thing, does the room hear "sixth guy is cheap and blocks everything" and discount me even more heavily than they should — the same way I'm discounting the first four? And is the engineer, hearing four yeses and then my one no, actually supposed to just ignore me?

Options I can see: (1) stay quiet and vote no; (2) speak on my turn and give the numbers; (3) push to move the vote to an anonymous email poll before anyone discusses it at all; (4) ask that the engineer be heard before the four friends; (5) round up the two or three people I suspect privately agree with me and get one of them to go first instead of me.

Which of these actually makes a difference and why? What I really want is a way to think about the fact that everybody hears everybody else before deciding — what's the right amount of weight to give four yeses in a row, and is there anything I can do that changes the answer rather than just adding one more voice to the pile?
