I'm teaching a graduate seminar on fundamentality this spring, and I've noticed that nearly all my students now call themselves grounding realists while disagreeing with one another about almost everything that matters. I'd like a decision tree that sorts them by what they actually accept rather than by the label they prefer.

Build the tree from the logical space, not from a list of names: each branch node should be one question about a concrete grounding claim — whether grounding necessitates what it grounds or merely suffices for it; whether Socrates grounds the singleton {Socrates} or the singleton grounds Socrates; whether a fact may be partly grounded in itself; whether every grounding chain must terminate in something ungrounded; whether one fact can have two distinct complete grounds; whether laws are grounded in the particular matters of fact or the reverse; whether grounding is a single relation or a family of them — and each leaf should be a named position: priority monism, grounding nihilism, Humean supervenience, the structuralist treatment of fundamentality, grounding pluralism, infinite descent, and whatever else belongs. Phrase every node so that a student can answer it without knowing which leaf it leads to, and let no node simply ask whether the physical is fundamental.

The distinction I most want the tree to honor is whether fundamentality is defined in terms of grounding or grounding in terms of fundamentality, so that belongs near the top. I'd also like it flagged where two leaves cannot really be separated by the questions you've used, and two or three answer combinations that no actual view occupies should be marked as empty with a word on why — incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unclaimed. Aim for roughly eight leaves.
