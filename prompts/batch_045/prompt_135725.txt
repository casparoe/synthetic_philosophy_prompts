I'm in a phil of physics reading group this term (3rd year, we've had two lectures on GR and exactly one on the hole argument) and I volunteered to make a sorting tree for everyone else — like a flowchart that takes you from "ok, I accept the hole argument is valid" to a named position. Can you build it?

Where we're all starting from: we grant that if you're a plain manifold substantivalist, the standard construction gives you a bunch of diffeomorphic models that agree on everything up to some time slice and then diverge inside the hole, so the theory can't select between them and you're stuck with this really weird indeterminism. So don't make the first node "is the hole argument sound" — start past that, with people who already feel the pressure.

What I want:

- The branch nodes should each be a yes/no (or occasionally three-way) question that someone in my group could answer without already knowing which position it leads to. So not "are you a substantivalist?", but concrete commitments — whether two total histories that agree on every qualitative fact but differ over which point plays which role count as different possibilities at all; whether "the same point" in two different models is even something you can ask about; whether there can be more points than the fields need; whether the surplus structure is something the theory should be cleaned up by subtracting or by adding to. Those are my guesses, swap in better ones.
- Build it from the space, not from a name list: lay out the questions and the combinations of answers they allow first, then place the actual positions in the cells, and flag the empty cells with one line on why nobody sits there (incoherent, just unattractive, or nobody's bothered).
- Leaves I definately want on it: plain relationism (fields only, no points); sophisticated substantivalism; a structuralist/eliminativist "no points, just structure" leaf; something supersubstantivalism-ish where spacetime just is the metric field; the bite-the-bullet indeterminist substantivalist; the "add background structure so determinism comes back" response; the "restrict to hole-free models" response; and the "there are no active diffeomorphisms, only relabelings" response. Add leaves if the space wants them. Each one gets a single sentence on its most awkward commitment — the bit that makes people go "hmm, do I actually want that."
- One case I want used as a branch, because I don't think the tree works without it and it wasn't in our lecture: the "global drag" variant. Instead of a diffeomorphism that's the identity outside some hole, take one that moves every single point and drag the fields along with it. Does the argument still bite on your response? I think this is what separates people who block the argument by ruling out weird models from people who block it by denying that haecceitistic differences are real differences, but im not confident, and I'd rather the tree say so if I've got that backwards.
- Flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely can't pull apart instead of faking a distinction. I have a feeling sophisticated substantivalism and relationism end up in the same cell; if so, say that and tell me what extra question, if any, would separate them.
- Keep it to 5–7 questions and roughly 8–12 leaves, no equations beyond g_ab and a diffeomorphism. It's going on one side of a handout.
