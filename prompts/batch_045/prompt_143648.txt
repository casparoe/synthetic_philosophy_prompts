Here's what I want help with. I keep reaching for the Moloch thing — multipolar trap, race to the bottom, whatever you want to call it, the pattern where everyone ends up worse off and nobody can unilaterally stop — partly as an explanation of why decent people do things nobody wants, and partly as an excuse for them. And I've noticed I hand out the excuse very unevenly. I want to know what principle, if any, I'm actually using.

My verdicts:

1. A junior associate at a big firm billing 2,400 hours a year, sleeping at the office, because the people who don't get quietly sidelined and then don't make partner. She hates it. I don't blame her for staying at all.

2. 6. A finance minister of a small country keeping the corporate tax rate at 12% because the neighbours do. Almost no blame. And yet I'm very confident there ought to be a binding international floor, and that saying so isn't in tension with not blaming her.

9. A trust-and-safety reviewer at a platform who thinks the moderation guidelines are bad, and stays, enforcing them, because if she quits she gets replaced by someone who enforces them more harshly and with more enthusiasm. My own stab at a rule: 'Moloch made me' is legitimate only if (a) you'd rather not be doing it, (b) it would have happened anyway without you, and (c) you're not in a position to change the rules.
