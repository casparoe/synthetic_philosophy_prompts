I sit on the conditionality and sanctions review panel of a regional employment-support authority, and I must circulate a written opinion before Thursday's meeting.

Two matters are before us. The first is an appeal. A lone parent missed a mandatory work-focused interview because her only childcare arrangement collapsed at three hours' notice; she has no home internet and no phone credit, and she reported the missed appointment to her work coach 26 hours later, when she was first able to get to the office in person. Our guidance treats sudden collapse of childcare as good cause for non-attendance, but good cause is credited only where the claimant notifies us as soon as is reasonably practicable; the decision-maker read the 26-hour gap as a failure to notify and imposed a 91-day reduction. The second is a request from our policy team for the panel's view on a draft Reciprocity Standard, which would stop justifying conditionality by its effects on job entry and instead justify it as the enforcement of a reciprocal insurance bargain: on this view a claimant who has not kept her side of the scheme has a weaker claim on the fund, whether or not the sanction changes anyone's future behaviour.

Two further items are in the bundle. An internal evaluation of our 2023 pilot found that sanctioned claimants left the caseload faster than matched claimants who were not sanctioned, but that the excess exits were split roughly evenly between exits into recorded earnings and claims closed with no recorded earnings at all; hardship referrals rose in the sanctioned group. Separately, the risk-scoring tool that selects claims for compliance review is tuned to maximize detection of confirmed overpayments, and its false positives fall disproportionately on lone parents and on claimants with fluctuating health conditions.

Please draft the opinion. It should recommend a concrete disposition of the appeal — quash, uphold, or vary — with a timetable for any back-payment or fresh decision; state whether the panel should adopt, reject, or adopt in modified form the Reciprocity Standard, together with what follows for how good-cause rules should be drafted; and give the strongest version of whichever position it recommends against, since the policy team and at least two panel members disagree with me. Reason from the case and the findings above rather than from generalities about welfare, dignity, or rights, and note explicitly where the desert-based framing of the Standard would or would not help this particular appellant.
