As part of my dissertation research on Kant's Formula of Universal Law, I serve on a small graduate working group that is trying to produce a consistent set of verdicts on maxims for an advanced seminar. I am reporting the group's track record rather than my own view, and I need a recommendation. The group's earlier verdicts are as follows.

(1) Maxim: 'when I need money, I will obtain a loan by making a promise to repay that I have no intention of keeping.' Verdict: impermissible. The maxim fails by contradiction in conception, a perfect-duty failure: if universalized, the practice of promising would be destroyed, and the maxim relies on that very practice.

(2) Maxim: 'I will keep a promise only if keeping it still serves my interests when the time comes.' Verdict: impermissible. Same classification as (1): the universalized maxim would make promising unreliable and thus undermine the institution the agent is exploiting.

(3) Maxim: 'I will never make the happiness of others my end; I will help others only when doing so costs me nothing and happens to please me.' Verdict: impermissible, but the group treated this as an imperfect-duty failure: the maxim is conceivable as a universal law, yet a rational will cannot will it as such, because a needy finite rational agent must will that others sometimes aid it for reasons independent of their own convenience. It fails by contradiction in the will.

(4) Maxim: 'I will leave my natural capacities undeveloped and spend my life in idle enjoyment.' Verdict: impermissible, again as an imperfect-duty failure via contradiction in the will: a rational agent cannot will a world in which no one develops the capacities needed for the ends that a rational will must adopt.

(5) Maxim: 'I will never contribute to the production or maintenance of a shared good that I nevertheless consume; I will let others bear all the costs.' Verdict: impermissible. If universalized, the good would not be produced, so the agent's own consumption would be impossible; the group treated this as failing by contradiction in conception, or in large-number versions by a contradiction in will, since the maxim depends on a practice of contribution it refuses to join.

(6) Maxim: 'I will aid others in need when I can do so at little cost to myself, but I will not seek out opportunities to aid and I will not aid when the cost to me is substantial.' Verdict: permissible. An imperfect duty of beneficence requires adopting some maxim of aiding others, but it leaves wide latitude about when and how much; the group did not find a perfect- or imperfect-duty failure here.

(7) Maxim: 'In a fair cooperative scheme that produces a public good, I will do the share assigned to me and comply with its rules, even when my contribution is not individually necessary because enough others are complying to produce the good anyway.' Verdict: permissible. The group said this is the maxim of a dutiful participant; because the agent does not exempt themselves when their contribution is redundant, they do not fail the test, and may even exceed the minimum required by an imperfect duty.

The new maxim we must now classify is this: 'In the same kind of fair cooperative scheme, I will contribute only when my individual contribution is causally necessary for the good to be produced; whenever my contribution would be redundant because enough others are already complying, I will withhold it and still enjoy the benefits of the good.' Assume that the good is produced if and only if some threshold number of members contribute, that the total number of members exceeds that threshold, and that any given member's contribution is therefore often causally redundant.

Please do the following:

(a) Identify at least two distinct rules for applying the Formula of Universal Law that are each compatible with all seven earlier verdicts. Do not restate the verdicts alone; make explicit the different principles that could generate them, and note where the rules agree and where they could in principle come apart.

(b) Apply each rule to the new maxim of contributing only when necessary. Say what verdict each rule predicts, and why, with attention to whether the maxim involves a contradiction in conception, a contradiction in the will, or neither.

(c) Give a best guess about what our working group should decide if it wants to remain consistent with its own precedents. Identify which earlier case is the closest analog to the new maxim, and explain why that analogy is nonetheless imperfect.

(d) If the precedents genuinely underdetermine the new case, state explicitly what substantive principle the group would be adopting if it condemned the new maxim, and what principle it would be adopting if it permitted the new maxim.

(e) Finally, name one further maxim or variant case that would best discriminate between the rules you have identified, and briefly say what each rule would predict about it.

I would be grateful for a point-by-point answer that treats the precedents as the object of interpretation rather than as an occasion to assert your own preferred Kantian theory from scratch.
