QUESTION

Nozick analyses knowledge as true belief that tracks the truth, and he accepts that on his account knowledge is not closed under known logical entailment.

(a) State Nozick's two subjunctive conditions and explain why, given them, knowledge fails to be closed under known entailment even when the subject competently deduces the entailed proposition and comes to believe it on that basis.

(b) Construct your own case in which a subject tracks some proposition p, knows that p entails q, deduces q from p, and ends up failing to track q. Or argue that no such case can be constructed.

(c) The sceptical argument runs: I do not know that I am not a brain in a vat; if I know I have hands then I know that I am not a brain in a vat; therefore I do not know that I have hands. Which premise does the tracking theorist reject, and why? Say whether you think that is the right premise to reject.

STUDENT ANSWER

(a) Nozick keeps true belief and adds two counterfactual conditions. S knows that p iff p is true, S believes that p, and: (3) if p were false, S would not believe that p — equivalently, in the nearby worlds in which S believes that p, p is true; and (4) if p were true, S would believe that p. These are the sensitivity and the adherence conditions. Knowledge is not closed under known entailment because subjunctive conditionals do not agglomerate: even if S satisfies both conditions for p, and p entails q, S may fail to satisfy them for q. Since q's being false would make p false, the nearest not-q worlds may be worlds in which S gives up the belief that p but still hangs on to the belief that q, so (3) can hold for p and fail for q.

(b) Nadia looks at her desk and sees a mug, so she tracks p: there is a mug on her desk — had there been none, the desk would have looked empty and she would not have believed there was one. Let q be: this is not a simulation that was switched on thirty seconds ago, in which there is no mug and Nadia is being fed an experience exactly as of one. p entails q; Nadia grasps the entailment, deduces q and believes it. But she does not track q: in the nearest worlds where q is false her evidence is by stipulation indistinguishable from her actual evidence, so she believes q just the same, and (3) fails for q.

(c) The tracking theorist rejects the first premise. Nozick can hold that we do know that we are not brains in a vat, and he needs to, since giving up ordinary knowledge of the external world would simply be conceding to the sceptic. Hold fixed that the belief is formed by the method of looking at one's hands: in the closest worlds in which one has no hands, that method delivers no belief that one has hands, so (3) holds for I have hands. And since having hands entails not being a brain in a vat, the same counterfactuals carry over to I am not a brain in a vat, so (3) holds for that as well. The rejection of closure therefore costs nothing: it shows only that you cannot get knowledge of q for free, without drawing the inference. Where the inference is genuinely drawn, knowledge does transmit — that is the point of tracking.
