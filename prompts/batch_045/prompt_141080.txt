I run a public philosophy night at the town library — drop-in adults, usually 12–20 of them, ages somewhere between 20 and 75, one 90-minute session, and almost nobody has read anything in advance, so the activity has to carry the whole idea by itself. The topic is Hofstadter's picture of the self: an I is a strange loop, a pattern rather than a lump of stuff, and patterns can in principle be spread across brains, so that when someone dies chunks of their I keep running inside other people — densely in some, very thinly in others. I want them to feel the pull of that, and also to feel exactly where it gets strange.

My rough idea, which I don't trust: everyone writes down five "pattern tokens" on cards — a phrase only they use, a small physical habit, a way of reacting to bad news, an aesthetic reflex, something they do without deciding to — and then the decks get dissolved and redealt, so that each person is being carried by several others, who have to actually run those tokens for the rest of the session. Then two twists I want built in properly: a density contrast (one person's pattern concentrated in a single carrier vs. another's smeared across five, and the group has to say who is still more there), and a divergence round where two carriers of the same person's tokens have to decide something the original person would have decided, and they disagree.

Turn that into something runnable: rules, materials, timeline, what the tokens should be so people don't just do impressions, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the distinction between merely influencing someone and an I literally persisting in them, plus dilution and the conflict case. No lecture up front, and they shouldn't hear the phrase strange loop until afterwards. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong (someone treating it as a party game, someone else finding it heavy), and be honest about which part of the view the activity can't produce and you'd just have to say out loud. Finally: how confident are you that this would actually land with a room like that, and what's the bit you're least sure of?
