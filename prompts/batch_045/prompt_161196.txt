I am reading a book manuscript by a colleague who works squarely inside Moore's ethics: she accepts the Moorean ranking of personal affection and the enjoyment of beauty as the two greatest intrinsic goods, uses his test of what something would be worth if it existed entirely alone, and holds that the value of a whole need not be the sum of the values of its parts. Over a long correspondence she has committed herself to the following verdicts: an unseen valley of extraordinary beauty makes the world better, though only a little; a connoisseur in genuine rapture before a forgery he takes for the original has almost the whole value of true appreciation; a thirty-year friendship resting on a flattering but false picture of the other remains among the greatest goods a life can hold; passionate attachment to a fictional character one knows to be fictional is worth far less, nearer reverie than friendship; love continued for decades after a friend's death, with no illusion that she survives, has real but much diminished value; and two friends hearing a quartet together are better off than the sum of the affection and the aesthetic enjoyment taken separately.

Two cases are now before her. First: a widower's relationship with an extremely responsive conversational simulation of his late wife — he knows exactly what it is, there is no deception, and his affectionate feelings are as warm and as well occupied as they ever were. Second: a simulated valley of extraordinary beauty, which he likewise knows to be simulated and in which he takes the same rapture he once took in real landscapes. Would she count either among the greatest goods, either as close to reverie, or the two differently?

I am less interested in the verdicts themselves than in the principle she is running, in the alternative principles that fit her track record equally well and that diverge sharply here, and in the single further case that would separate them. In no more than 500 words: give your predicted verdict on each case, set out the competing principles with what each predicts, state which one you take her to be running and why, and name the further case you would put to her.
