I'm setting up a small "moral trade desk" at a philanthropic foundation — moral trade being the idea that two parties with different terminal values each take the action the other prefers, since each would rather the other's thing happen than their own, so both come out ahead. Concretely: an animal-welfare funder pays for bednets while a global health funder pushes for cage-free commitments, though neither thinks the other's cause is the more important one.

I've got two staff, about three years, and enough budget to broker maybe a handful of real trades. So: which conceptual questions should those two people actually work on, and in what order? Some I keep circling: whether there's any non-arbitrary way to check that a proposed trade is really Pareto-improving when the two sides' gains are incommensurable; whether the "now you're doing something you think is wrong" objection — complicity, or trade eroding whatever value your commitment had as expression — is a genuine cost or just squeamishness; whether trading with someone who's empirically mistaken, or who has much deeper pockets, is exploitative in a way that should stop us; and the sequencing worry, that as a party trades away its leverage whatever it has left gets more valuable to it, so early trades can foreclose better later ones.

I want the reasoning more than the list: what criteria you're ranking by, how much other work depends on each answer, whether three years is even enough to settle any of it, and what our first couple of real trades could teach us that would reshuffle the order. Also flag anything here you'd just drop on the floor because settling it wouldn't change what we do.
