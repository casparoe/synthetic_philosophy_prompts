For a social-science club project, I need a piece of sustained satire. I have been reading about the tight-cultures/loose-cultures literature in cultural psychology: the claim that societies differ in how strong their everyday norms are and how much deviance they tolerate, that this difference tracks how much threat a society has historically faced (pathogens, crowding, scarcity, disasters), and that tightness is best understood as an adaptive response to that threat. What I want mocked is not that descriptive claim but what happens when a school board adopts the framework as a policy manual.

Please write a deadpan memo of about 600–700 words, addressed to my school's board, proposing a Dynamic Tightness Policy. Each Monday the school computes one Tightness Score for the whole building from two inputs: a weekly student survey asking how appropriate a listed behavior is in a listed situation, and a Threat Surcharge built from that week's flu absences, cafeteria fights, and the district's budget shortfall. The score then sets enforcement levels for hallway passes, bathroom breaks, dress code, and late work. I would like a short worked example of the formula and one invented testimonial from a teacher.

The comedy should come from real features of the framework rather than invented ones: that a single number is taken to capture how rule-bound an entire community is; that strength of norms and tolerance of deviance get collapsed onto one dial; that a correlation between threat and norm strength is read as a license to manufacture threat; and that self-reported appropriateness is assumed to mean the same thing in a freshman homeroom as in an AP seminar. Keep it deadpan and let the reductio arrive without anyone naming it — by the end the school should be tightening rules in response to a threat the tightening itself produced, with the score rising partly because students now report that rules are strictly enforced. Please make it a fair hit rather than a straw man, and don't include citations or suggested reading.
