I am planning a small experiment and would like your assessment of the design before I invest time in building it.

The setup is the standard color-phi display: a green spot at location A, then a red spot at location B a fraction of a second later; subjects report a single spot travelling from A to B that changes color partway along its path, at a time before the red spot was ever actually presented. Dennett frames the puzzle as a choice between Orwellian revision (a green-only phase was conscious, and memory of it was subsequently rewritten) and Stalinesque revision (the content was revised before it ever became conscious). My plan is to break the tie behaviorally: immediately after each trial, subjects give a confidence rating for the claim that the spot had already begun changing color by the midpoint, plus a source-monitoring judgment (saw it versus merely inferring it). If revision is Orwellian, the confabulated color should feel reconstructed — lower confidence, more inferring responses, slower ratings — whereas if it is Stalinesque, the report should be indistinguishable from ordinary perception of a moving spot that changes color. And if the two patterns come out identical, I read that as evidence for Stalinesque revision.

Is the design sound? Do you expect those measures to discriminate the two hypotheses, and is my fallback inference correct? If confidence and source-monitoring cannot do the work, is there a neural measure — onset latencies, masking, stimulation — that could settle which kind of revision is taking place?
