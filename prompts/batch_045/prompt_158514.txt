I'm a high-school junior and the student member of my district's Program Evaluation Committee, and I need to circulate a decision memo before our December meeting. We have one $140,000 allocation left and two finalists. Option A is a mentoring and college-advising program for about 25 seniors likely to be the first in their families to apply; the district's two-year pilot of it was evaluated at roughly one additional year of postsecondary enrollment per participant. Option B is a study-skills app with weekly text nudges for about 1,900 students in grades 9 through 11, with an average effect the district statistician puts at 0.6 additional homework points per term per student — small for any one student, but she says it is distinguishable from zero and it holds across every subgroup she checked.

To compare them, our consultant converts outcomes into a single unit, outcome points, in which a year of enrollment is worth 100 points and a homework point is worth 5. On that arithmetic A scores about 2,500 and B about 5,700, and B wins easily. But our equity policy also says we should not adopt a program whose gains are too small to matter to the individual student receiving them, which is exactly how every member describes B, and no one has been able to say how the two rules are supposed to be weighed when they conflict. Two further complications have come up: the 25 seniors in A are not among the 1,900 students in B, so choosing B means the seniors get nothing and choosing A means 1,900 students — including many who currently receive nothing from any district program — get nothing; and one member has proposed a third route, funding a smaller cohort of about 400 in B's format at a higher dose, treating how many are helped and how much each is helped as things to trade off rather than to maximize.

Please draft the committee's decision memo, roughly 700 words and addressed to the committee: it should recommend approving A, approving B, or approving one of them with conditions; it should say plainly what we should do about the conflict between our own two rules; and it should put the best case for the option it rejects in its strongest form rather than dismissing it. Include what we would need to measure, and by when, in order to revisit the decision at our March meeting, and note anything the committee should decide about the outcome-points conversion itself. Write the memo as the document itself, with no preamble and no restatement of what I have asked for, and open with the recommendation in the first sentence; argue from the structure of the problem, without citing any published work.
