I've been reading LessWrong and the surrounding agent-foundations material since the Sequences were still being posted, and I'm putting together a short comedy set for the open mic at our regional rationalist meetup this spring — the premise of the set being jokes that only land if you have actually internalized something technical. The slot I'm still missing is logical uncertainty, and I'd like it to be about the logical induction framework specifically: the computable algorithm from the 2016 Garrabrant et al. paper that refines a price on every sentence of a formal language over time, where each sentence is treated as a share worth one dollar if true and nothing if false, traders buy and sell against an underlying deductive process, and the whole thing is pinned down by the requirement that no computable trader can make arbitrarily large profits off the prices. Everything else — convergence to coherence, the roughly one-tenth probability on late digits of pi, trusting later prices more than earlier ones, learning the patterns in a long-running computation long before it finishes — is supposed to fall out of that one criterion.

What I want is a single joke, tellable in under a minute, whose punchline turns on the actual content of that setup rather than on general nerdiness. A joke about someone assigning probabilities to things, or about Bayesians being insufferable, would work just as well in a statistics department, and I don't want it. Some directions that seem promising, though please don't feel bound by them:

- The gap between a price and a proof. The market moves on a conjecture long before the theorem prover has emitted anything, so the humor could come from a character treating a current price as if it settled the question — or, better, from the fact that the price cannot tell you whether it is quoting today's confusion or tomorrow's settled result.
- Price as a decision procedure. Someone uses the current number to decide how long to keep searching for a proof, or whether to bet the house, thereby treating coherence in the limit as coherence now. If you use this, I'd like the joke to be honest about the fact that nothing in the framework lets the inductor know when it has converged, and that this is exactly the temptation the setup creates.
- The self-referential trader. A trader who tries to exploit a sentence about the market's own prices, and is undone — but note that the framework is deliberately built to survive self-reference without paradox, so the comedy should come from the trader's overconfidence, not from the inductor breaking.
- Pseudorandomness and calibration. A character who demands a confident answer about something whose truth values look statistically patternless and is furious to be offered a well-calibrated shrug.

After the joke, give me two or three sentences on which of the framework's properties the punchline actually depends, and one sentence on whether the joke fudges anything and, if so, whether that fudge would mislead a smart newcomer who went on to read the paper. Keep it in-group legible and good-natured: no outgroup punching, and no punning on priors unless it is genuinely worth it.
