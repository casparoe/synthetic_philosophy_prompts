I have to lead the discussion at my philosophy-and-theology reading group in a couple of weeks and we're doing Pascal's wager. My brother is a fairly serious catholic and we argue about this every christmas, so I've been trying to write down where I've actually landed on it rather than just rehearsing the stock objections, and now that it's on paper I can't tell whether it's a position or just a pile. Here's roughly where I am:

(1) I think the mathematics is the least of its problems — infinite payoffs don't break decision theory, any nonzero probability of an infinite good swamps anything finite, so the wager's core machinery is fine. (2) My own credence that christianity is true is maybe 1%, probably lower, and I don't think that touches the argument at all. (3) I also think the wager gives you literally no reason to think God exists, and I don't hold that against it either. (4) I think belief isn't the kind of thing you can just switch on at will, so the wager can't be read as an instruction to believe — at most it tells you to do the things (go to mass, keep the company of believers, go through the motions) that might over time produce faith in you, and the end state it aims at is real belief whatever route got you there. (5) At the same time I think the wager is religiously repulsive in a way that matters: a God who rewards a prudential bet isn't worth worshipping, and a faith that arrives by that route is corrupted by where it came from. My aunt more or less converted on her deathbed to hedge her bets; the math says she does exactly as well as a lifelong saint, and I still think there's something off with her, but I cant say what. (6) Of the standard objections, the many-gods one is the one I think actually kills it — once you let in a crowd of candidate deities every option has infinite upside and infinite downside, and the argument goes silent. (7) Pascal's mugging I don't take seriously at all — the guy who says hand over five dollars or I'll use my magic powers to torture you forever gets nothing from me, and I don't see why that should cost me anything elsewhere. (8) And I also think, and I tell my brother this every year, that no God worth worshipping would damn someone for backing the wrong religion after honest inquiry.

Can you audit this for me? Which of these are genuinely in tension with each other, and which only look like they are? For the real ones, what would I have to give up to fix them, and how costly is each fix? Please rank the tensions by severity instead of listing them all as equally bad, and say whether there's a single revision that would clean up the most. I've read the basics so I don't need a general explainer on the wager or a reading list, I just want to know what my own commitments commit me to.
