My politics tutor is a pretty hardline Nozickian, and I've been keeping a list of her verdicts on cases we've gone through:

- Wilt Chamberlain: the resulting distribution is just, and taxing it back is on a par with forced labour.
- A homesteader who fences off an unclaimed field when there's plenty of similar land left nearby: fine, and she owes later arrivals nothing.
- A painting looted in the 1940s and bought in good faith thirty years later: goes back to the original owner's heir, and no compensation for the buyer.
- A firm that patents a compound a community had been using for centuries and then charges monopoly prices: not a legitimate holding — though she's said she can't decide whether that's because the stuff was never unowned or because the community ends up worse off.
- Copying a recipe out of a published cookbook and opening a rival restaurant that ends up driving the original one out of business: fine, nothing was taken.

New case, which I get to pick for next week: a company copies, without asking anyone, every publicly posted text on the internet, trains a model on it and sells access. Nobody is worse off at the moment of copying — the texts were free to read anyway — but the model then substitutes for a lot of the writers, many of whom lose most of their income. The company makes billions. A hundredth of one percent of writers used the opt-out form.

What will she say about whether that's a legitimate holding? I care much less about the verdict than about the rule she's running. I can see two or three rules that fit all five earlier verdicts equally well and come apart here: something about the proviso being a one-off gate at acquisition vs a standing condition, something about information not being the kind of thing you can appropriate because it isn't rival, something about the wrong being use-without-say-so rather than anyone being made worse off. Tell me which of those (or what else) she's really running, what each candidate predicts here, your best guess with the reason for preferring it over the runner-up, and the single further case that would best tell the top two apart. She also doesnt accept "everyone is better off in aggregate" as an answer to anything.
