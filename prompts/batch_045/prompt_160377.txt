A chapter in a general-audience philosophy-of-science book I was reading summarizes the debate over biological functions roughly like this:

'On one side, the etiological or selected-effects account says that a trait’s function is the effect for which it was favored by natural selection in the past: the heart’s function is pumping blood because pumping blood is why hearts were preserved by evolution. On the other side, the causal-role account says that function is just the useful job the trait is currently doing in the organism: the heart’s function is whatever it now contributes to keeping the organism alive, so a newly evolved enzyme that happens to neutralize a modern pollutant has that neutralizing as its function even though nothing selected for it in the past. The decisive problem for the etiological view is novelty: if a brand-new mutation benefits an organism right now but has never been selected for, the etiological view seems forced to say the mutation has no function at all, which is absurd. That is why the causal-role view has largely replaced it. Once you give up the demand for a selection history and identify function with current beneficial contribution, you get a notion that matches biological practice better. The only remaining issue is whether evolutionary history adds anything important; in practice, biologists should just use “function” to mean current causal contribution to fitness.'

I am not confident about where this goes wrong. I have always been attracted to the historical, “why evolution kept it” notion of function, because it seems to explain why function talk is normative—why we can say a diseased heart is not doing its job—but the summary makes me worry that I am just smuggling in purposes that blind evolution cannot provide.

Please give me an accuracy audit rather than a general opinion. Go through the summary claim by claim and tell me:

- which claims a defender of the selected-effects account would accept, and which it would reject or describe as a caricature;
- which claims a defender of the causal-role account would accept, and whether the summary’s formulation of that account is the one such a defender would actually endorse;
- where the summary quietly changes the subject or overstates a thesis—for example, by treating a standard objection as a refutation, by equating “causal role” with “current benefit to fitness,” or by reducing a live dispute to a single practical recommendation;
- and whether the conclusion that working biologists should define function as current contribution to fitness follows from the accurate parts of the summary, or only from the misrepresentations.

To make this concrete, please apply your audit to the following case, which I have been calling the mid-handover case. A gene-regulatory network in a lineage of soil bacteria originally spread because it buffered cells against temperature swings; call this effect A. In the present environment, temperature is stable, A does very little, but the network now also upregulates a membrane pump that exports a recently encountered industrial solvent. Bacteria whose networks upregulate the pump more strongly are currently leaving more descendants, so the solvent-export effect B is now being favored by selection, although the derived variants have not yet fixed and the older sequence variants still do most of the work. Using the summary’s contrast, this looks like a clean case of “history says A, current usefulness says B.” But once the two accounts are stated more carefully, is that really the choice? Can a selected-effects account say anything about B before fixation, and can a causal-role account distinguish the network’s function in a detoxification system from its negligible role in a thermal-tolerance system? Does the mid-handover case support the summary’s claim that novelty refutes etiological accounts, or does it mainly show that the summary has conflated “currently advantageous” with “functional” and “historically selected” with “no longer relevant”?

I do not need citations, reading lists, or bibliographies; I want to know what to trust in the summary and what a more careful version of each view would say.
