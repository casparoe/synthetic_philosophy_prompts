I'm a PhD student in phil language and somehow got volunteered to run the department's metaphysics reading group this term, which means I now owe everyone a one-page handout for next week's session on the statue and the clay. I want to do it as a decision tree instead of a literature summary, because half the room checks out the second anyone says "constitution" out loud.

The root commitment I want to start from: for the sake of the handout, assume David and the piece of marble are both there on the plinth and aren't just two names for one thing. So the tree starts at "there are (at least) two things occupying the same place, one of them somehow more arty than the other" and its job is to sort people by what kind of two-ness that is. I do need one gate question at the top for the two people who will say there are no statues at all and the one who will cheerfully say there are forty-seven, with a line on what to do with them so we don't lose the session.

Cases I want doing the work as branch questions (use most of them, add at most two of your own):

1. The clay sat in the studio for six years untouched. Was the statue already there during those six years, just badly arranged?
2. The night before the opening the sculptor squashes it into a ball. Did she destroy one thing or two?
3. Over the following year conservators replace every gram of the clay, one piece at a time, keeping the shape. What's on the plinth at the end — the statue, the lump, both, neither?
4. She squashes it into a ball and an hour later reshapes the very same clay back into exactly the same shape. Same statue come back, or a duplicate?
5. Someone finds a lump of clay on a beach that erosion has shaped into something indistinguishable from the statue, same material. Statue or not?
6. An artist signs an untouched lump of clay, puts it on a plinth in a gallery and calls it a work. Statue or not?
7. The statue is 3D printed one thin layer at a time. How many layers in does the statue begin to exist? How many in does the lump?
8. Somebody attaches an extra lump of clay to the base. Is the original lump still there, or did it go out of existence when it gained a part?
9. Alongside the statue that will stand for five centuries, is there also a thing that is statue-shaped and exists only for the ten minutes of the opening ceremony?

Rules for the questions: no use of the words constitute, coincide, persist, temporal part, sortal, or mereological anywhere in them; at most three answer options each; and answerable by someone who has never read a word of the literature — so nobody should be able to tell from a question which view it's a trap for. Sort me by what I say about the cases, not by which label I like.

What I want back: an indented plain-text tree with numbered nodes and about seven to nine leaves; a one-line "what this view has to swallow" at each leaf; then a short table of the answer-combinations the questions allow, with the actual named positions dropped into the cells they occupy and the empty cells flagged with a one-line diagnosis of why nobody lives there (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored?). Names only where you're confident — I don't need citations or a reading list, I can't check them and nobody will read them. Definitely include a cell for the contingent-identity view (Gibbard's Goliath and Lumpl thing, if I've got the attribution right) and one for the constitution-is-not-identity view people associate with Baker; after that I don't care much which leaves you pick.

Second: flag any of my nine cases that turn out not to discriminate between anything, so I can cut them for space.
