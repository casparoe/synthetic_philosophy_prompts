I've been chewing on the divine hiddenness argument and I think I've accidentally turned it into an argument against everything, which makes me suspicious of my own version of it. Here's what I've got:

(1) A perfectly loving being would give any person access to a great good, if it could do so without sacrificing a comparably great good or a greater one.
(2) A conscious relationship with God is the greatest good for a person, and you can't have one without believing that God exists.
(3) God could give any non-resistant person enough evidence to believe, and none of the usual explanations people offer — autonomy, soul-making, keeping the world regular, wanting to be sought out — looks like a comparably great good that would actually be lost if he did.
(4) There have been, and are, people who genuinely don't believe and aren't resisting it.
(5) So there is no perfectly loving God.
(6) But now swap 'relationship with God' in (2) for basically any other great good a loving being could hand over — not being eaten by a predator, not dying of leukaemia at six, having enough to eat — and (1), (3) and (5) seem to go through just as well.
(7) So either the argument is just the old problem of evil in a nicer coat, and premise (2) was doing no real work, or it only gets off the ground if there's something special about divine self-revelation — and saying what that is looks like exactly what the theist and I were arguing about in the first place.

I feel like something's slipped in somewhere, maybe in how (3) is doing its work, but I can't put my finger on it. Which step would you drop, and what does dropping it cost me? I'd rather hold on to (4) than give it up, if that helps.
