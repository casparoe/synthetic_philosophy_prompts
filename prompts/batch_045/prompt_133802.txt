My team does safety reviews before deployment, and there's an argument about "low-impact" agents that has been going in circles for years. I don't need another survey of the approaches and please don't point me at papers — we have the literature. I want a diagnosis of why it doesn't move.

Here's the shape of it as I understand it. You want a system that achieves its objective without rearranging the rest of the world in ways you didn't ask for. The naive version: fix a baseline of what would have happened if the agent had just sat there, fix some distance on world states, penalize deviation from the baseline. Two problems that everyone already knows: it penalizes the agent for succeeding at all (actually fetching the coffee is itself an enormous deviation), and it makes the agent indifferent between catching a vase and letting it fall, because under the inaction baseline the vase breaks either way. So a lot of the field moved to capability-style measures: don't measure how much the world changed, measure how much the agent's power to bring about a broad spread of possible goals changed, and penalize that. That handles the vase, but it introduces offsetting — an agent can manufacture new options somewhere else to pay for the ones it destroys — and it still forces you to choose the spread of goals and the reference policy, and the verdict swings wildly on that choice. Then someone publishes a new measure, someone else publishes a new failure case, and round we go.

My colleague thinks this is terminal, and she puts it as a dilemma: any such measure is either so value-laden that you've just written your utility function down in disguise, or so value-free that it's either constant or gameable, and there's no stable middle. I half believe her, but I also suspect "there's a no free lunch theorem here" is sometimes what people say when they mean "I can't construct a version that works."

So: what kind of stalemate is this, actually? Some candidate diagnoses I can't choose between. It might be verbal — maybe "impact" doesn't pick out a quantity at all, so there's nothing to define. It might be substantive: a real disagreement about whether value can be factored into "here's what I want" plus "don't touch anything else," where the second half is supposed to be specifiable without knowing the first. It might really be an engineering disagreement in disguise — whether the thing you're building is a penalty term in a reward, a hard constraint, a filter on candidate plans, or a signal to a human overseer, since those have completely different requirements and people argue past each other about all of them at once. It might be a hidden formal question nobody has stated cleanly enough to prove or refute. Or the sides might differ on a background picture of deployment — how much oversight is available, whether anyone can be asked — and be answering different questions under one label.

One more thing I want your read on, because it's the move someone makes in every meeting and I can't tell whether it's deep or a dodge: instead of penalizing total impact, give the agent a budget of impact it's allowed to spend, provided it buys it back — break something big and irreversible, but open up an equivalent amount of option-value elsewhere, or pay for one big change with many small reversible ones. My instinct is that this just relocates the value judgment into the exchange rate rather than answering anything. But it might be the right frame if the real goal is only to keep a human's options open until someone can be consulted.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give me the reasoning — no preamble, no restating my question. And given whatever diagnosis you land on, tell me what would actually count as progress: a formal result, a distinction, an experiment, a change of question — or tell me honestly why none is coming.
