I spent most of last night arguing with a friend about whether anyone has an account of how correctness itself — the fact that some moves in thought or argument count as right and others as wrong, that some considerations count in favour of a conclusion — got into a world of matter in motion. He thinks every account on offer cheats, and he has a compact vocabulary for saying why, picked up from Daniel Dennett's *Darwin's Dangerous Idea* (1995): 'skyhook', 'crane', and 'greedy reductionism'. As I understood him to be using them: a skyhook is an explainer that is itself unexplained, something that gets its purchase from above and so merely relabels the mystery; a crane is a mechanism that lifts from below, built out of the very materials it has to work with, so that nothing is left dangling; and greedy reductionism is the vice of trying to explain a phenomenon while skipping the cranes — going straight to the bottom and denying or ignoring the pattern that needed explaining in the first place.

The argument kept stalling on three cases I invented to press him, and on one accusation he kept making.

(a) *The borrowed crane.* Suppose correctness gets explained by a mechanical process — creatures or networks that update on feedback, retain what predicted well, discard what did not. The trouble is that I can only describe that process by saying it responds to *error*, retains what is *better*, tracks what is *correct*. The friend says: there, you helped yourself to the very thing you were explaining, so that is a skyhook. But it seemed to me that on that standard any crane whose parts have to be picked out by what they do would be disqualified, which would be absurd. Is there a principled line between a mechanism that presupposes the property it explains and one that merely gets described in the vocabulary of that property?

(b) *The inherited crane.* Suppose a system exhibits correctness only because it was built, trained, or shaped by agents who already had it — a cultural practice, an institution, a model fitted to the output of reasoners. Relative to that system, the normativity arrives from above, unearned; relative to the history of the universe, it was produced by earlier cranes. He insisted skyhook-hood is not relative to a baseline. I could not see how that could be right.

(c) *The stipulated floor.* Inside a simulation, the lowest-level rules look exactly like skyhooks: unexplained explainers, brute givens. From outside, they are ordinary cranes resting on the hardware. So it looked to me as though whether something is a skyhook depends on where one puts the floor of what needs no further explanation — which would make the slogan 'there are no skyhooks' either nearly trivial or nearly unfalsifiable, depending on where the floor is allowed to sit.

Finally, whenever I tried to give a subpersonal, mechanism-level account, he accused me of greedy reductionism, and I could never tell whether he meant that my account was false, that it was true but not explanatory, that it skipped a level, or that it changed the subject.
