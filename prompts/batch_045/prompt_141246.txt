After a year of reading on free will and moral responsibility I have ended up holding a package of views that I would like audited before I build anything on it. Stated plainly, I hold:

(1) A broadly Strawsonian view: the content and point of responsibility talk is fixed by the reactive attitudes and the stance we take toward one another as participants; there is no deeper property of really deserving blame that this practice tracks or fails to track.

(2) The truth or falsity of determinism is irrelevant to whether we are responsible. The wholesale adoption of the objective attitude is not a genuine option for creatures like us, and the traditional worry is idle.

(3) But history matters. In the standard covert-manipulation cases, where an agent satisfies every plausible current-condition on responsibility — reasons-responsive, no compulsion, values meshing with action — and yet the springs of that agency were secretly engineered, I have a firm verdict that the agent is not responsible, and I do not want to explain it away.

(4) I reject Galen Strawson's Basic Argument. I do not accept that responsibility requires being the ultimate source or cause of one's own character; that is an impossible standard, and the argument gets its pull from an incoherent ideal.

(5) Methodologically, I distrust intuitions pumped by far-out hypotheticals. Here as elsewhere I think theory should be answerable to what our actual practice is for, not to science-fictional cases.

(6) I think responsibility is scalar rather than all-or-nothing, and that blame should be proportional to it.

I cannot locate the strain myself, so please do the audit rather than ask me which item I would surrender. Tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only look like it, and be explicit about the ones that dissolve and why, since I suspect at least one apparent clash is not real. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad; for each, say what exactly would have to be given up and what that costs me elsewhere in the set. Then say which single revision to one commitment would do the most to make the remaining five comfortable together.
