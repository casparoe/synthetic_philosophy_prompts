I've been defending the user-illusion view of consciousness, but with an extra bolted-on claim that I think does most of the real work: the interface is bandwidth-limited in a fixed way. It doesn't just compress the world into something usable — it has a hard resolution ceiling. So what you get is a sparse, task-shaped summary; there's no fact of the matter about what the "full" uncompressed experience of everything you weren't attending to would have been; and you can't actually improve your introspective resolution with training, you can only learn to reinterpret the same coarse summary differently, which is why expert meditators or athletes report things novices don't without literally seeing more.

What would count against this package? I want the observations, arguments and possible results that should move me, sorted from fatal to merely awkward — and for each one, whether a committed defender like me should honestly concede it as a real defeater or whether it's just a case the view cheerfully digests. Two things I especially care about: whether the fixed-resolution clause earns its keep or is only unfalsifiability with a nice name, and if it is insulated that way, whether that's a genuine problem for a view whose whole starting point is that introspection is unreliable; and what the view predicts that its rivals — the rich or "overflow" picture, a global-workspace one — don't, so I'd know where to look to watch it break. Also tell me if it can even state itself without sawing off the branch its sitting on, since its a view about how bad we are at reporting whats in our own heads.
