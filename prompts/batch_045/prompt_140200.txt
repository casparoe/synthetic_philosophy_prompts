For a reading group next week I've been asked to hand round a one page statement of where I stand on shared agency so the others can poke at it before I try to turn it into a thesis chapter. My supervisor's verdict on the last draft was that I've written something that's half one famous non-reductionist view and half the individualistic reductionist picture it was meant to replace, and that the two halves don't talk to each other, which I think is unfair but I can't tell whether its a real complaint or just a vibe. Here's roughly where I've landed, including the case verdicts I keep coming back to:

(a) Anti-reductionism about shared intention. Two people each intending to meet at the station, each knowing that the other intends the same, with full common knowledge all the way up, still aren't doing anything together unless some further thing is there — a joint commitment, a "we", whatever you want to call it. A stack of I-intentions plus beliefs about the other person's I-intentions doesn't add up to a shared one.
(b) But I'm also an individualist about where the mentality lives: all intentionality is in the heads of individuals, and there's no group mind and no irreducible group-level agent that intends things over and above its members. I take (a) and (b) to be perfectly consistent — I think the person who made "we-intentions" famous holds both — but someone in the group keeps saying they can't be, and I want to know if that's right.
(c) Mutual responsiveness is constitutive of joint action: if nobody is tracking and adjusting to what the other is doing, there's no joint action, just parallel activity that happens to mesh. This is why I don't want to call two strangers who end up walking in step on a crowded pavement a case of doing something together.
(d) The pancake case. My nephew and I are making pancakes: I hold the bowl and tilt it and slow him down when the batter starts to go over the rim, and he stirs, and he is three, so he adjusts to nothing at all and would cheerfully stir the whole bowl onto the floor. I want to say we genuinely are making pancakes together, that this is a real joint action and not me acting while a small person flails near me. And I don't want to fudge it by saying it's joint "to a degree" — I need jointness to be the sort of thing that's either there or not there, for reasons that show up in (f).
(e) The tarp case, where I also want retroactivity. Dara and Eli each take an end of a tarp full of garden waste and start carrying it. Dara takes them to be taking it to the skip at the back; Eli takes them to be taking it round the front to use as mulch, and he's just wrong about which end of the garden the skip is. Halfway across Eli works out where they're actually headed, shrugs, and keeps heaving; they tip it into the skip together. I want to say they were carrying it together the whole time — the first minute counts, retroactively — and also that if Eli had instead dropped his end and walked off the moment he realized, then they hadn't been doing it together at all, even though that first minute was behaviourally identical in both versions.
(f) Shared intention generates a distinctive directed obligation: if we're doing something together then I owe it to *you*, not just to morality at large, to do my part, and if I walk off I owe you an explanation or an apology and you're entitled to demand one — and this isn't derived from any promise I made or from general beneficence, it comes just from the jointness itself.

What I want is an audit, not a summary of the literature. Which of these pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it? Rank the real ones by how severe they are rather than listing them all as equally bad, and for each say what would have to give and what giving it up would cost me elsewhere in the set. I genuinely don't know where the problem is — I'd honestly defend any of (a) through (f) if pressed — so please don't let me off by saying they're all "in some tension", tell me which one has to go. And then, at the end: if I only get to revise one thing, which single revision cleans up the most, and what does it commit me to about the pancake case and the tarp case?
