I convene a graduate seminar that, as a term-long exercise, drafts advisory opinions for a federation of states whose charter was compiled almost entirely from Kant's 1795 essay on perpetual peace: the prohibition on forcibly interfering with another state's constitution and government, the prohibition on a state's coming under the dominion of another by inheritance, exchange, purchase, or donation, the requirement that each state's civil constitution be republican, the restriction on national debts contracted with a view to external conflict, and a cosmopolitan right limited to a right of visit rather than a right to be received as a guest.

Our opinions so far: we condemned A's armed overthrow of a tyrant in B and its installation of a caretaker government, although A annexed nothing and withdrew within the year; we condemned C's purchase of an island province from D's ruler, the price paid into D's treasury and the inhabitants never consulted; we condemned E's acquisition of F by bequest of F's late ruler, even though E at once offered F's people equal citizenship and they raised no resistance; we approved G's repulsion of H's invasion, including a temporary occupation of a border district held as security until peace was signed; and we approved the union of I and J in a single federation, ratified by simultaneous referenda in both.

The case now before us: a province of P whose assembly and electorate have voted by a comfortable majority to secede and join Q is to be transferred by a treaty under which Q assumes a fixed portion of P's external debt. The vote was free and informed, the sum corresponds roughly to the market value of the debt, and P without the province remains economically and militarily viable.

What should we rule, if the point is consistency with our own record; which precedent is the closest analog; which of the principles that fit that record equally well come apart here, and how exactly do they come apart; and what single variation on the case would most quickly reveal which one we have really been applying?
