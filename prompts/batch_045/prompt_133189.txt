I'm a PhD student in philosophy finishing a paper on the metaphysics of mental kinds, and I keep running into a line that is doing far more argumentative work than anyone will stop to unpack: the mind is the software, the brain is the hardware (sometimes dressed up as: mental states are defined by what they do, not by what they are made of). I last met it at an interdisciplinary cognitive science workshop, where it was deployed twice in one afternoon and, I suspect, in two different senses. First, a psychologist used it to brush off a question from a cellular neuroscientist: you can describe what a program does without knowing anything about voltages in the circuits, so cellular and molecular detail is simply irrelevant to psychological explanation and psychological taxonomy. Second, a machine-learning researcher used it to argue that a system implemented in silicon, running the right organisation, would have mental states every bit as much as we do, so the biological specifics of neurons are as incidental as the specifics of the transistor. I don't think either inference is as straightforward as the metaphor makes it look, and I'd like to stop gesturing and actually say what is being claimed.

What I want is a disambiguation, not a defence. Please lay out, as a menu, the distinct precise theses the software/hardware line could be encoding — I can think of several candidates that pull apart, and there are probably more: a multiple-realizability thesis about mental kinds not being structural or material kinds; a thesis about the explanatory or taxonomic autonomy of psychology from neuroscience; a portability or substrate-neutrality thesis (the same program can be moved to a new machine); the considerably stronger thesis that the mind literally is a computation of the sort a digital computer performs; a deflationary thesis about levels of abstraction being interest-relative, so that hardware and software mark no joint in nature; and a thesis about phenomenal consciousness itself being implementation-independent. For each reading on your list, say whether it is a platitude, whether it is substantive but defensible, whether it is substantive and probably false, and — the part I actually need — whether it would support the psychologist's autonomy claim, the researcher's silicon-mentality claim, both, or neither. Where a reading fails, say precisely where the burden shifts. If some reading collapses under the familiar worry that under a permissive enough mapping almost any physical system implements almost any formal description, tell me which of the two uses that worry kills.

Two invented cases of my own have convinced me that the interesting action is elsewhere than in those standard moves, and I'd like them addressed rather than paraphrased back at me. Case one: a creature in which the causal profile associated with some mental state is filled not by a single internal state but by a chain — a state caused by tissue damage that in turn produces a further state, which produces still another, with the withdrawal behaviour and the avoidance learning hanging off the last link only, so that no one state in the creature has both the characteristic causes and the characteristic effects. Case two: a creature whose relevant profile is filled by a large, degenerate population of overlapping mechanisms, each individually sufficient and none individually necessary, such that lesioning any one leaves behaviour and reported experience unchanged, and the state seems to be spread across the population rather than located anywhere in it. For each reading on your menu, say what it implies about these two creatures, and say how much detail about the creatures' inner workings we would need in order to settle the matter — because my suspicion is that the readings generous enough to count these creatures as minded are exactly the readings that force the psychologist to care about implementation after all, which would turn the slogan against the first use it was put to. If so, I want that tension stated explicitly, so I can put it in the paper rather than仅仅 hinting at it.
