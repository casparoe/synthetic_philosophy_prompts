"Since nobody can climb inside your head and measure how happy you are, utility is just a name for what people actually choose: if you take the apple instead of the banana, that means you prefer the apple, and there is nothing more to a preference than that. The payoff is that this makes utility testable. If someone's choices obey the weak axiom of revealed preference — once you have picked A while B was available, you never later pick B while A is available — then there exists a utility function they are maximizing, however irrational they look from the outside. Two consequences follow. First, there is no such thing as weakness of will: if you skip practice, then at that moment you preferred skipping, and saying you really wanted to go is just sentimentality. Second, economists can never claim that a policy made people better off than they chose to be, because choice is the only evidence there is."
