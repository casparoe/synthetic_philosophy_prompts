A mate of mine is in a small sports-betting syndicate and ive been trying to work out what their actual rules commit them to. The rules are: size every bet with the Kelly criterion off their model's own probabilities; bet half of whatever fraction Kelly gives, on the grounds that the probabilities are estimated rather than known; stop betting for the rest of the season if the bankroll drops below 60% of where it started the season; and at the end of each season withdraw everything above a fixed starting stake, so compounding restarts at the same number every year, even though they describe what theyre doing as maximizing long-run growth.

What does that package presuppose? Is the thing doing the work really log utility / geometric growth, or is it just unstated risk aversion plus estimation risk, and which parts of the practice strictly require which? Does cashing out every year quietly undercut the long-run growth argument, which needs compounding to get going, and leave only the myopia-of-log argument, or are those the same argument in different clothes? Does the 60% floor presuppose a side constraint or a lexicographic cut-off that no growth-rate story can generate on its own, and does the result that Kelly eventually beats any essentially different strategy even survive once you put an absorbing barrier in the model? Also, over a season of a few hundred bets, isnt the asymptotic result basically beside the point?

Half-remembered and worth checking: in the continuous approximation the growth rate is roughly quadratic in the fraction staked, so anything past about twice your Kelly fraction has negative growth and half Kelly costs you only about a quarter of the maximum growth rate. If thats right, does it make half Kelly defensible on straight growth-maximizing grounds, or does that move smuggle in something extra? And is there a view about pure time preference in here somewhere, given that they discount future bankroll growth at zero while pulling winnings out immediately?

Please keep settled math separate from whats genuinely contested among people who know this material, e.g. whether the growth-rate argument is an independent justification of Kelly or just log utility in disguise, and dont present a contested reading as the consensus. And tell me where the rules are underdetermined, i.e. where several different theories would license exactly the same behaviour.
