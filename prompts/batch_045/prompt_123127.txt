Write me a deadpan internal memo, around 700 words, from a fictional government body — call it the Bureau of Counterfactual Exposure — laying out new procedure for handling acausal threats. Set the premise up in the first paragraph for someone who hasn't met the idea: an agent you have no causal contact with at all (a civilization too far away for light to ever reach you, some future system running simulations of you, whatever) can still make you worse off, because on the decision theory these people accept your decision procedure and theirs are correlated, so how you act here is evidence of — and partly settles — how they act there. The Bureau has decided these demands are real and should be paid. Target the practice, not a named person.

The jokes should come from the genuine structural features of the view rather than invented ones: there's no feedback channel, so the Bureau can never confirm a payment worked or learn anything from experience; threats cost nothing to issue, so demand is unbounded; nobody can tell the simulation from the original, or a real threat from a crank letter; and the supposedly right move is to *be the sort of agent who doesn't pay*, a precommitment you can't fake from inside. Have one throwaway clause where the tiny-probability/astronomical-stakes objection gets handled in a way that's obviously circular. And let the surrogate-goal bit slide in: staff are graded on a Quantitative Counterfactual Exposure Score, and by the metrics section it should be clear they're optimizing the score instead of what it stood for — quietly dismantling the capacity it was supposed to track, or the like.

Never let the memo say 'this is absurd'; keep it straight so it lands as a reductio and the reader recoils on their own. Numbered sections with headings (definitions, intake and triage, payment protocol, performance metrics, appeals), and include at least one worked example of a processed claim.
