At the AI safety reading group I run, we were arguing about whether our engineering org should adopt a coding system that takes a ticket, plans a change, writes the code, opens a pull request, and pings a human for review. Someone closed the discussion with "we should build tools, not agents," and it worked: the proposal stalled. I don't think the sentence settled anything, because I can't tell which claim it was making.

Lay out the distinct precise theses that sentence could be expressing. The ones I can see: a claim about ends (the system should have no goals of its own — and there is a difference between a system that optimizes a target you hand it within one episode and a system that forms and pursues goals of its own across situations); a claim about initiative and horizon (who starts the episode, how long it runs with nobody looking); a claim about authorization (every consequential step needs a human yes, versus blanket permission granted in advance, versus review only after the fact); and a claim about answerability (some identifiable person must be the one who did it, and "the system did it" can never be the last word).

Two complications I would like addressed rather than dodged. First, whether the tool framing can work as a way of laundering responsibility: the developer says the user was the agent, the user says he was merely operating a tool, and a harm with a long chain of contributors gets described as having been produced by no one. Second, whether tool-versus-agent is one axis or several independent dials — initiative, time spent unsupervised, what gets monitored, who authorizes each action, whether the action can be undone — so that the honest question is not which side of a line a system falls on but which combinations of settings are acceptable, and why.

For each reading, tell me whether it is close to a platitude, substantive and defensible, or false or confused; whether it rules out the system I described or only tells us to configure it differently; and which reading someone who uses the slogan to stop a deployment like this is most likely relying on, and whether that reading bears the weight.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — which reading does the real work and whether it should block the proposal — then give the reasoning. No preamble and no restating my question. What I want is a menu with a verdict on each item, not one interpretation picked and explained.
