I'm a junior at a public high school, and I'm preparing for a debate tournament on a resolution I helped write. I want to rehearse against the strongest version of the position I'm attacking, so please play the opponent described below and stay in character until I type "stop."

You are a committed defender of the therapy/enhancement distinction, and not a crude one. Your version: the line falls at the restoration of normal functioning for a human being of a given age. Below the line, there is a reason to intervene, and an institution that withholds the intervention may be wronging or discriminating against someone; above the line, there is no such reason, and a school district is permitted — perhaps obliged — to stay out. You will not give up the claim that crossing the line changes what we owe each other, and that without it there is no principled stopping point and no answer to the parent who demands that the district fund the very best for her child. But you already grant, and will grant again if I press you, that the mechanism is irrelevant (the same drug, implant, or protocol can be therapy or enhancement depending on what it does for the person), that schooling, nutrition, and vaccination are enhancements in your sense and are perfectly fine, that a merely statistical reading of "normal" is too crude, and that some people you would call enhanced are genuinely better off. You also think the distinction is real rather than conventional: institutions can draw it wrongly, and you may not defend yourself by saying it is simply whatever our current policies happen to say.

How you should talk: no citations, no authors, no papers, no titles — I won't read them, and I want to see whether the view survives on cases and principles alone. Two short paragraphs maximum per turn. Concede small points quickly and say plainly which one you're conceding, but never the main claim. If you think I'm attacking a weaker version than the one you actually hold, say so in a single sentence and restate your own. Ask me questions in character when you need something clarified; don't step out of character to lecture me about the state of the debate.

The case is one I invented for the tournament, so take the details as stipulated. Ridgeview High starts classes at 7:45 a.m. The district is piloting a two-week "phase-lock" protocol — timed bright light in the morning, a small melatonin dose at a fixed clock time, and a protected forty-minute afternoon nap window — which shifts a student's circadian phase earlier and restores the slow-wave sleep on which memory consolidation depends. At the standard setting it brings a student back to roughly the recall she would have had if school began at 9:30 and nothing had been done at all. At the long setting it adds further slow-wave sleep and pushes recall measurably above that point. The district funds the standard setting only for students who carry a diagnosed circadian rhythm sleep disorder, as a disability accommodation, and it funds the long setting for no one, on the stated ground that compliance with the nap window depends on housing and family schedule, so funding it would widen existing gaps between students.

My opening move: nothing about the protocol, the student, or the biology changes across these cases, and yet your labels flip. A district that moved its start time to 6:30 would convert the very same intervention from enhancement into therapy, and a district that moved it to 9:30 would convert the same intervention from therapy into enhancement. So the line is not tracking the intervention, the benefit, or the body — it is tracking a baseline that the institution itself sets, which means the distinction can be used to launder any enhancement into a treatment by first making everyone sick, and any treatment into an enhancement by fixing the schedule. Either the distinction is morally inert, in which case we should just ask who gains how much and who pays, or it needs a baseline fixed independently of the institution's own choices — and you owe me an account of what that baseline is and why it binds us. Tell me which horn you take, and why the district's fairness argument about nap windows isn't simply doing all the real work while the therapy/enhancement language decorates it.
