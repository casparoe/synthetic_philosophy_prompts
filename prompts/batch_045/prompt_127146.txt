I'm a TA for a second-year metaphysics course and I have one 50-minute discussion section next week, about 18 students, on dispositions. They will already have met the conditional analysis of ascriptions of the form disposed to M in C, and the three families of hard cases: finks, where the stimulus itself removes the disposition; masks, where the disposition is retained but something extrinsic blocks the manifestation; and mimics, where the manifestation shows up without the disposition ever having been there. I don't want to re-lecture any of it.

Design a game or structured activity in which students start out treating disposed to M as nothing more than would M if the test were run, and then run into cases where that equivalence breaks and where they have to choose, under time pressure, between classifying an object by what the test actually produced and classifying it by what the object is like now. Two things matter to me. First, some of the hidden conditions should be second-order: a device that disables a fink device, a blocker that the stimulus itself removes, a mimic whose trigger is finkish. I want them to notice on their own that settling whether a case is a fink or a mask seems to require a further disposition ascription, and to feel the pull of that regress rather than hear about it. Second, the scoring should force a bet before each test and make at least one family of cases systematically uninformative or actively misleading, so that nobody can win by trusting outcomes.

Please give me the materials, rules and scoring scheme with no real stakes, a minute-by-minute timeline, and a debrief that maps specific moments in play onto the fink/mask/mimic distinction and onto the regress, and that leaves students able to say why a mask and a fink are not just the same counterexample twice. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — I expect at least one student to insist the whole dispute is merely verbal — and be honest about which feature of the distinction the game cannot stage and where I will have to supply it myself.
