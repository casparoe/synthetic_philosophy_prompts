A port city with its own legislative assembly decides some questions by binding referendum, and a standing Franchise Board fixes the demos separately for each referendum instead of applying one permanent franchise. On the last referendum, about harbour dues, its verdicts were: resident citizens, in; non-citizen residents of eight or more years' standing, in; seasonal workers present four months of the year, out; former residents who have moved away permanently and retain neither property nor tax liability, out; and, over recorded dissent, absentee owners of businesses registered in the port, who live abroad and visit twice a year, in. Its written reasons invoke two formulas: that the demos for a decision should contain everyone whose serious interests that decision will materially affect, and, in the case that produced the dissent, a nowhere-else-to-be-heard test — those whom no other assembly has any reason to weigh, because their own government will not take up their case and they have standing nowhere else.

The next referendum is on closing the container port and returning the estuary to wetland. The question before the Board is whether to enfranchise the residents of a town twenty kilometres downstream, across the border in the neighbouring country, whose piped water and whose single large employer both depend on the estuary remaining open, and whose own government has shown no interest in their position. They are not subject to the city's laws, owe it no taxes, and cannot vote in its elections.

Predict the Board's ruling on the downstream town, and say what it should rule if it wants to be consistent with its own record. Set out the competing rules explicitly — affectedness, subjection to the city's coercive power, durable stake or social membership, the nowhere-else-to-be-heard test, or something better — say which of them fit the earlier verdicts and which come apart on this one, name the single further case that would best tell the surviving rules apart, and identify which precedent is genuinely the closest analog. Finally, separate what is comparatively settled about the boundary problem from what is contested among people who work on it, and mark clearly whenever you are taking a contested side.
