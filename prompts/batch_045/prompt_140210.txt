Write a sestina — six sestets in unrhymed, roughly iambic pentameter, followed by a three-line envoi — that presents Hofstadter's strange-loop account of the self: the 'I' as a self-referential symbol, the thing that comes into being when a system's symbol for the system as a whole is itself caught up in what it symbolizes, in a tangled hierarchy with no top level and no floor.

Constraints:

The six end-words, which must rotate through the stanzas in the usual way and all reappear in the envoi, are: loop, symbol, mirror, level, name, self.

The word 'I' — first-person singular, in any form, including contractions and including the envoi — appears nowhere in the poem. The self has to be made by the returning pattern of those six words rather than named by a pronoun.

Somewhere in the fifth or sixth stanza the hierarchy must actually tangle: a line or image that reads as being about the speaker or about the world has to turn out to be about the poem's own machinery. The reader should feel the level-crossing rather than be told about it, and should be able to say which stanza it happens in.

Do not let 'loop' collapse into feedback. A strange loop is not a thermostat or a cybernetic feedback cycle, and 'mirror' should not be used as though reflection alone would do the work: what is needed is the crossing back through the symbol. Anyone who conflates the two has misread the view.

A reader who knows the material should find nothing to wince at; a reader who does not should still find a poem about what it is to be a self. Keep the whole thing under 45 lines.
