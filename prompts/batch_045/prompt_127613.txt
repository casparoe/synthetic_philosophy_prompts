I serve on the five-person enforcement committee of a large open-source project's code of conduct (roughly two thousand contributors), and I need help with a decision that is coming up next week and that I have some personal history with.

Please answer as a professional moral philosopher who works on the ethics of blame and responsibility — specifically on the questions around hypocritical blame and standing. Take your time and reason carefully: begin by brainstorming, in a short list, the different principles that could be generating our track record, before you commit to one; then work through the new case explicitly against each of them; and flag where you think the record genuinely underdetermines the answer. I do not need citations, a reading list, or references — I cannot check them and they will not help me. What I need is the argument.

The question that comes up constantly for us is whether someone has standing to bring a complaint: whether we hear it at all, as opposed to hearing it and deciding it on the merits. We have no written rule. Complicity we treat as settled and separate: if you helped bring about, encouraged, or profited from the very episode you are reporting, we do not hear you, and nobody on the committee has ever disputed that. The contested ground is the hypocrisy one — you yourself did something relevantly like what you are complaining about. Here is what we have actually done, unminuted reasons included:

1. K. posted a sneering remark about a newcomer's code, and two weeks later filed against a different contributor for a nearly identical remark. Asked about his own comment, he said the newcomer had it coming. We declined to hear it.
2. L. posted comparable remarks four years ago, before the code of conduct existed and before the current norms were written down anywhere; she has not done anything like it since. We heard her complaint against a current repeat offender.
3. M. once shared a screenshot of a private message without permission; he now complained about insulting review comments. We heard it — different provision, we said, and not the same normative family.
4. N. egged the target on in the same thread and then reported him after he escalated. Declined, on the complicity ground.
5. O. posted a comparable sneering remark nineteen months ago, was reported at the time, accepted a two-week suspension, apologized publicly, and has not repeated. We heard her complaint about a milder version of the same thing.
6. P. filed against another contributor while an unresolved complaint about his own comparable conduct, filed by someone else, was still pending. We declined, without prejudice to refiling once his own case resolved, and we were explicit that the ground was docket management rather than standing: we did not want the same conduct assessed twice by differently constituted panels.
7. Q. complained about a sustained pattern of dismissive remarks; his own only lapse was a single mildly curt phrase two years earlier, which we judged not to have violated the code at all. Standing unproblematic.

The new case: R. posted a sneering remark, about eight months ago, in a private review thread — same provision as what he is now complaining about, and of roughly comparable seriousness. Nobody ever reported it; nothing about it exists in our files. Six months ago R. went privately to the person he had sneered at, apologized, and asked whether she wanted him to say anything publicly; she said no. R. now files against a senior contributor who posted a comparable remark about R.'s own code in a public channel. In filing, R. disclosed his earlier remark himself, said it was wrong and that he had stopped doing it, and said he understood it might cost him standing. Two further facts: the remark was made in a channel whose only other participants are the senior contributor's close collaborators, so R. is realistically the only person who will report it; and the senior contributor does not know about R.'s earlier remark, which was in a private thread.

I should disclose why this is not abstract for me. About a year ago, before I was on the committee, I tried to file a complaint of just this shape about just this kind of remark, and the then-chair told me informally that I had no standing, because I had made comparable remarks myself two years before, stopped, and apologized for them. I did not push it. Nothing about it was minuted, and it is not in the record above. R. is someone I encouraged to come forward.

What I want from you:

(a) The candidate principles that fit all seven precedents, stated sharply enough that they could come apart, including any version on which standing is not a threshold but a matter of degree to be weighed.
(b) What each of them predicts about R., and why.
(c) Your best guess at what we will do, and separately what we should do if we want to be consistent with ourselves — with the closest analog among cases 1–7 identified, and an honest statement of the principle we would be adopting with each choice.
(d) Whether the unminuted informal ruling about me counts as one of our precedents, and whether treating it as one or ignoring it changes anything.
(e) The single further case that would best separate the surviving principles from each other — a variant on R. that would force a difference — and what each principle says about it.
(f) Three structural questions the press of cases has never let us discuss. First: whatever defect there is in R.'s complaint, is it a wrong to the person blamed, a failure of the blaming speech act itself to come off, or a defect in R.'s own relation to the norm — and how would you tell which it is? Second: does the answer differ if R. merely reports the facts while refraining from blame, or if we take the matter up on our own initiative without any complainant, or if the senior contributor says he is willing to answer to R. anyway and waives the point? Third: should an enforcement body track moral standing at all, or is standing to invoke a sanction a different thing from standing to blame, answerable to different considerations such as deterrence, protection of other contributors, and the quality of the evidence?
