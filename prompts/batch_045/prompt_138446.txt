Is there a name for this? I keep having the same argument with my brother about voting and it feels like we're actually fighting about one narrow thing that neither of us has a word for.

Case one. We live in a state where the presidential race is never close. He votes third party every cycle and says the point is that he wants it on record that he didn't put his name behind either of the main two. I stay home. As far as the outcome goes we are doing exactly the same thing, i.e. nothing. But the family treats what he does as principled and what I do as slacking, and, this is the part that gets me, they treat him as *more* principled than our cousin, who holds her nose and votes for the lesser evil, even though her vote is the only one of the three that had any chance at all of mattering. So the approval isn't tracking outcomes and it isn't tracking participation, it's tracking something about what the act is taken to say.

Case two, which is the one I actually care about. Our cousin, having held her nose, then spends four years insisting that she didn't vote for him, she voted against the other one. And people — including some of the same people who told her she had to vote that way — tell her that isn't hers to decide. That she voted for him, and that's what the vote meant. So apparently the thing being expressed isn't fixed by the person doing the expressing. It's settled partly by whoever is reading it, it can be settled after the fact, and you can end up having said the exact thing you were trying not to say and have no way to take it back.

I've seen the instrumental-vs-expressive distinction people bring up in these arguments — voting as a way of causing an outcome vs. voting as a way of saying something — and case one looks like it's about the second. But from what I've seen of that idea, it still assumes the voter is the one who knows what they meant and gets whatever the payoff is from having meant it. The thing I can't get a handle on is the part in case two: the meaning is up for grabs among the audience, and gets revised retroactively, and you have no appeal.

One more wrinkle I keep thinking about, since the ballot is secret: nobody reads *your* vote, they read the aggregate. So if 60% of the people who look like you voted for someone, your vote arguably means what the group's vote means, and you don't control that either. Which makes the meaning of the act look less like what you meant and more like what the recievers can infer.

So: does this have a name, in the voting literature or the philosophy of action or wherever it belongs? And if it doesn't, I'd like a name plus a definition sharp enough to sort the boundary cases. Is it the same thing as when a march gets reinterpreted by the newspapers, or when a joke gets read as an insult, or is there something specific to voting doing the work here (the secrecy, the aggregation, the fact that you get one and it gets added up with everyone else's)? Is the retroactive part special, or can the meaning of any action be rewritten after the fact in the way the meaning of a sentence, once uttered, can't? Is it even a single phenomenon or have I stapled two together?

I don't want a label I can wave around, I want to know what's doing the work, what has to be excluded, and where the edges are — because right now my brother and I both feel like the other person is describing us unfairly and neither of us can say why.
