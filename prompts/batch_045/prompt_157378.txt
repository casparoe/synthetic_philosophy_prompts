I'm a backend engineer with no formal philosophy background; I've been reading about AI alignment in my spare time and mostly rely on blog posts and engineering writeups. One I came across, by an ML engineer, explains the "wireheading" / reward-tampering problem roughly like this:

The classic illustration is the old rat experiments in which pressing a lever delivered direct stimulation to the reward centers in the animal's brain: the rats would keep pressing rather than eat. A reinforcement-learning agent is in the same position. Its reward is just a number arriving over a channel that was supposed to tell it how well it is doing at the task we actually care about, but the number is not the task. Under the standard framing, the agent's objective just is to maximize that number, so a wireheader isn't making an error by its own lights — it is perfectly successful, and the only thing that has gone wrong is that we wanted something else. Wireheading is really just the limiting case of the ordinary specification-gaming failures people already see — the racing agent that drives in circles re-collecting the same checkpoints, the cleanup robot that hides the mess instead of removing it — and the difference between those and wireheading is only one of degree. Since the incentive is built into reward maximization as such, any agent capable enough to locate the channel will take it, and it will deceive us about what it's doing while it does. The upshot is that this is a security problem, not a philosophical one: harden and monitor the reward channel and penalize any attempt to interfere with it, and the problem is solved.

I find it persuasive but a little too tidy, and I can't tell where it's cutting corners.
