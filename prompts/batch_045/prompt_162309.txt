hey, looking for a consistency check on a set of things I believe. Please don't give me a reading list or citations — I'm a software engineer, I've never studied philosophy properly, and I won't read whatever you'd point me at anyway. I work on a recommender system, so "utility" and "preference" are engineering objects to me every day, and I think I've ended up with a set of views that doesnt quite hang together, but I can't tell which ones are the problem.

Where I've landed:

1. Preference just is choice. If a claim about what someone prefers can't be turned into "they'd pick this over that when offered these options", I don't think it says anything at all. Our users tell us in surveys that they want less inflammatory content and then click the inflammatory stuff every time; I think the clicks are the preference and the survey is self-flattery. Same reason I'm happy to throw out bot traffic and clicks within 300ms of page load — those aren't choices, they're accidents.

2. Utility isn't happiness or wellbeing or anything anybody feels. The u in expected-utility maximisation is just a numbering that makes someone's behaviour under risk come out as maximising an expectation, and it's arbitrary up to rescaling, so only the ordering is really there. When my team says "we're optimising for user wellbeing" I think that's a slogan with no content.

3. But I also think there's a real gap between what someone chooses and what's good for them, or what they want in the sense that should count. Addicts, depressed people, me doomscrolling at 2am. I don't think we should be optimising for the 2am click, even though by (1) that's the most revealed of all my preferences. A colleague says (1) and (3) are flatly inconsistent; that seems too quick to me, but I can't say why.

4. When the click logs show cycles — A over B, B over C, C over A — I treat that as a bug in my measurement, not as a discovery that this user has intransitive preferences. I assume I've drawn the option space wrong or conflated different contexts, and I fit one consistent utility function anyway.

5. I don't think utility can be compared across people; there's no shared unit, so "sum of everyone's utility" is nonsense to me. At the same time I routinely ship the A/B variant that's better on average even when it's clearly worse for some identifiable subgroup, and I'm fine with that, because I think of it as a policy judgement I'm making rather than as computing aggregate welfare.

6. I train on margins, not just orderings: I care how much better item X was than item Y, pooled across all users, and I'll happily say "this one was twice as preferred as that one."

So: which of these actually collide? Rank the collisions by how bad they are, tell me which pairs only look like they collide, and for each real one tell me the cheapest thing I could give up and what it costs me. I'd rather not drop any of them, so be specific about what each fix buys and what it gives away. If there's a single revision that cleans up more than one of them at once, that's the answer I want most. Please don't assume I know the terminology — define words like "cardinal" if you use them. And at the end, say how confident you are in what you've told me and single out the one claim you're least sure about.
