My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about the thesis of Hofstadter and Sander's Surfaces and Essences: that analogy is not one faculty among others but the core of cognition, so that categorization, perception, and even the feeling of a familiar situation are all acts of analogical retrieval. We got nowhere, because we kept trading labels. So I want a taxonomy for our reading group — about a dozen people, mostly cognitive science graduate students, several of them deep-learning researchers who will not sit still for a list of -isms — built from the logical space rather than from a roster of names: six or seven branch questions, each phrased as a concrete claim or case someone can endorse or reject without knowing where it leads, and eight to ten leaves.

The distinctions that matter to me are these. First, whether the core claim is a claim about the algorithm, about the representation, or only about the computational-level description — my friend's real objection was the bookkeeping one, that any successful process can be redescribed after the fact as retrieval of a stored case, so the thesis is unfalsifiable. Second, whether the mechanism retrieves a stored episode or constructs a novel one. Third, whether what gets mapped is structured relations with explicit role bindings, similarity in a learned representational space, or sensorimotor schemas. Fourth, whether a system counts as cognizing analogically if it is fluent at verbal proportional analogies but never uses a source to reorganize its own categories, or if it transfers structure in cases where no single retrievable source episode can be identified — call it source-free transfer.

Leaves must include, at minimum: the strong identity thesis that categorization just is analogy; structure-mapping as a derived operation over independently given structured representations; the Bayesian or predictive-processing view that the real core is structure learning and analogy is only a symptom; the embodied-metaphor view; the deflationary view that the word covers several unrelated mechanisms and no natural kind; and at least one leaf for the position that a system such as a neural network can pass every behavioural test for analogy while nothing analogical is going on inside it, together with what that position is committed to about us. Please use at least one case that is not in the usual canon; I suggest a double dissociation between a subject who categorizes reliably within familiar domains but can carry no structure across them, and one who maps exuberantly across domains while her category boundaries slide so freely that she mislabels ordinary objects. Mark the empty regions of the space and say briefly whether each is empty because it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unclaimed, and flag any two leaves your tree cannot pry apart.
