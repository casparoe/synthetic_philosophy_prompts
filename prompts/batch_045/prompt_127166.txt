Say Nozick somehow got to see the world of 2025 and started thinking properly about animals: lab-grown meat so nobody has to kill a cow for a burger, cheap gene-editing that could dial down pain in wild animals, and the option of running animal minds on servers so you could spin up a million simulated cows whose entire lives are just pleasant experience, no bodies involved at all.

He's known for a kind of two-tier view in Anarchy, State and Utopia — roughly, aggregate animal welfare utilitarian-style, while persons get Kantian side constraints — and he's also the guy who argued we'd refuse to plug into the experience machine even though it would feel great.

So how do you think he'd handle these three cases? Would factory farming come out as a rights violation in his framework (and if so, whose rights, given that he thinks animals dont have them), or just as a cost-benefit question where cheap burgers might win? Does he have any resources at all for caring about wild-animal suffering, where the harm isnt caused by any agent? And what about the million happy simulated cows: is he commited to wanting to tile the universe with them, or does the experience machine give him a way to say no — and if it does, does that way out also undercut the utilitarian aggregation he wants for real animals? Definately give me your sense of where the tension is worst.
