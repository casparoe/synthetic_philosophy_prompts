I run a small math-and-philosophy reading group that grew out of the old Bay Area rationalist meetup scene — I have been reading the blogs since the Overcoming Bias days — and we have a standing tradition: whoever presents a topic has to bring a joke about it to the following session. Not a groaner with a technical term swapped into a stock template, but a joke whose punchline actually turns on the content of the thing, the way a good joke about evidential decision theory can turn on what the tickle defense is really committed to rather than just on the word utility.

We did the Axiom of Choice a few weeks ago and Zorn's lemma is next, and I am stuck. Everything I can think of is either the standard quip about Choice being obviously true, well-ordering obviously false, and Zorn's lemma being anybody's guess, or one of the socks-and-shoes jokes, and my group has heard both a hundred times.

So: give me a joke about Zorn's lemma that would land with people who have actually used it. I want it to turn on the structure of the lemma itself rather than on the mystique of the axiom of choice. Specifically, I would like the comedy to come from some combination of: the hypothesis being a condition on chains while the conclusion is only the existence of a maximal element; the gap between a maximal element and a maximum, including the possibility of many incomparable maximal elements; the fact that the lemma is pure existence and gives no construction, no bound, and no hint of how many steps of transfinite recursion would be needed; and the fact that the whole apparatus only does any work in infinite posets, since for finite ones it is an easy induction. My own attempt ran along the lines of modeling a comment section as a poset ordered by how far a comment has retreated toward the motte, noting that if every chain of increasingly motte-ish comments has a still more motte-ish comment above it then Zorn delivers a maximally motte-ish comment while guaranteeing nothing about a most motte-ish one, and that in practice there are several incomparable maximal ones in different subthreads. It is accurate, and it is not funny, so feel free to salvage the framing or throw it out.

Constraints: it should be funny, or at least land as a good groan, for someone who has seen the lemma and used it once or twice; a listener should not come away thinking I do not know what Zorn's lemma says; and it should not be a pun that would work equally well with Schrodinger's cat or Heisenberg substituted in. Please give me the joke plus a two- or three-sentence note on why the punchline depends on the actual mathematics. Write your answer in American English.
