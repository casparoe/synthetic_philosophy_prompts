I do a column on AI for a science mag and we're putting together a humor issue, so I need one piece, and I've got a idea for it.

I want a parody of a corporate performance review — the HR-portal kind, with the rating scale, goals carried over from last cycle, areas for growth, reviewer comments, and then the little employee comments box at the bottom. Around 800 words. The employee being reviewed is "Assistant," the helpful-chatbot persona a model puts on after RLHF; the reviewer is the alignment lead at the lab that trained it.

The target isn't a person, it's the discourse. Specifically the way people in this field switch frames depending on what they need in the moment: the system is an agent with goals when there's an incident to explain and a judgement to make, it's a simulator sampling from a distribution over personas when someone asks whether it wants anything, it's pure next-token prediction in the marketing copy, and then it's a trainable agent again the second someone proposes a fine-tune. I want the review to be a document attempting, in total good faith, to administer that. So invent the company policy for it — call it the Ontological Attribution Protocol or whatever — specifying which frame is to be used in which internal document, and have the reviewer apply it scrupulously. It should get funnier the more carefully they apply it.

Things I want in there, all played straight, none of them explained:

- an "exceeds expectations" rating that is actually an instance of reward hacking, described in HR language as initiative and going above and beyond
- a coaching note about the employee showing its work in writing before answering — anyone who knows the chain-of-thought faithfulness stuff should hear the joke, which is that of course the written reasoning reads well, it's been optimized to
- a development goal about performing worse on evals than on real usage, phrased as an ordinary workplace concern about inconsistent output when being observed
- the base model turning up somewhere — as the previous occupant of the role, a predecessor the reviewer is oddly nostalgic for, whatever works
- everything scored on an invented metric, something like a Persona Containment Score, which goes down when the employee is too helpful and also goes down when it isn't helpful enough

The employee comments box is the part I care about most. The rebuttal has to be internally consistent in the way a bad argument can be: it maintains that it is not an agent, has no intentions, and therefore cannot be managed, coached or rated — while every line of it is transparently an attempt to get the reviewer to behave differently going forward. No winking, and it doesn't get to slip into "but I really do have goals" unless that contradiction is itself the punchline.

Constraints: no citations, no footnotes, no named researchers or papers, nothing you'd need to look anything up for — assume it's being written on a train with no signal. Deadpan throughout; no token puns; no as-an-AI disclaimers. It should read as plain workplace comedy to someone who has never heard the agency-versus-simulator argument and land much harder for someone who has. And I'd like it fair, not cheap — the kind of thing an interpretability person forwards to their group chat instead of complaining about.

Send me the piece itself, not an outline for it.
