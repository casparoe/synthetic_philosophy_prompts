Hey — I help moderate a foraging / mushroom-ID Discord (yes, the kind where a wrong ID can put someone in hospital). We've been going round for weeks about a rule for our "certain ID" posts, and the whole argument runs on the line "talk is cheap," which I've now heard so many times that I've lost any grip on what anyone means by it.

The proposal on the table, from our loudest member: if you want to post an ID at "certain" or "near-certain," you burn a donation to a cause our group actively dislikes, scaled superlinearly to how confident you say you are (so "99%" costs a lot more than "80%"). His reasoning as I understand it: words cost nothing, so anyone can claim certainty, so the only way to make a certainty-claim mean something is to attach a real, non-refundable cost to it — and the waste is the point, because if you could get the money back, or bet it and hedge, it wouldn't be a handicap anymore.

Three competing rules are floating around: (a) a "receipts" rule — no payment at all, you just have to attach a photo of the spore print and gill attachment so others can check; (b) a track-record rule — a bot displays your historical ID accuracy next to your name, which nobody can edit; (c) a bet rule — you post a stake you get back if two moderators later confirm the ID and lose if they refute it. Someone answered (c) with "put your money where your mouth is," and I can't tell whether that's the same principle as the burn or a different one wearing the same clothes.

What I want is the menu of precise things "talk is cheap" could be asserting here, from the trivial ("uttering a sentence has low marginal cost, so people sometimes say things they don't believe") on up: that costless messages carry no information when interests conflict; that reliability requires differentially higher cost for the bad type rather than cost simpliciter; that reliability requires *wasteful* cost specifically; that attaching any cost is enough to make a message trustworthy; that some signals are trustworthy because they're mechanically tied to the fact instead of because they cost anything. For each reading: platitude, defensible and substantive, or false? And which reading would actually get our member his rule — I have a feeling a fee everyone can pay just selects for rich or very confident people rather than accurate ones, and that burning money and betting money do different things (one screens, one incentivizes?), but I can't make it precise. Same question for the "that's just signalling" move someone used to dismiss a long careful post as showing off: does anything in this story license that, or does showing that something is a signal not show it's empty?

Lay it out as a numbered list of readings, each with a verdict and a note on which of the four rules it actually supports. Also please seperate what's settled textbook signalling theory (peacocks, stotting, the job-market model, handicap stuff) from what's genuinely contested or model-dependent — say where you're unsure instead of smoothing it over, and don't hand me a contested reading as if it were the definitve consensus. And tell me which reading you think people in that thread were actually reaching for.
