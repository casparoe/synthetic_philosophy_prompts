I got hold of this thing called the grue problem from somewhere — the bit where every emerald anyone has ever checked has been green, but somebody invents a word meaning green if you check it before 2030 and blue if you check it after, and that word fits every single observation just as well as green does, so the evidence alone cant tell you which word to carry forward. I've been chewing on it by myself (never took a philosophy class) and I made up five predicates of my own that feel sort of grue-ish, though I suspect one of them is just wierd and not a real case at all.

Rank them worst to least bad on one dimension only: how much damage each one does to plain old induction — that is, how easily two people who have seen exactly the same evidence could both use it honestly and still end up confidently predicting different things about the next case. I cant look anything up, so please no citations or reading suggestions, just your own reasoning.

1. blane — green if examined before 1 January 2030, blue if examined after. Everything examined so far was examined before then.
2. sneen — green for the first thousand emeralds anybody ever examines, blue for every one after that. About 400 examined so far.
3. vlat — green on this side of the river, blue on the other side. Nobody has crossed yet.
4. prin — green if you look at it in spring, brown if you look at it in autumn. Every leaf I've looked at, I looked at in spring.
5. zop — green until the sun burns out, blue after that.

Give me exactly five numbered lines, one per predicate, one or two sentences each, saying where it lands and why, and in each line say straight out whether you'd actually predict with that word. No intro, no wrap-up at the end.
