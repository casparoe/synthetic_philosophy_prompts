Hey — I'm a journalist doing a magazine piece on how museums turn objects into claims about what people actually believed, and I want a small flowchart to run as a sidebar.

It should walk a curator from "we have this object, from this context" to the strongest claim about belief or institutions they can honestly put on a wall label — and, just as often, to "we can't say". Every branch node should be a question someone can answer about the actual find: how it was deposited, whether there's use-wear, whether it's one-of-a-kind or one of two hundred, whether comparanda turn up in graves vs. middens vs. hoards, whether it sits alongside anything that looks like accounting (sealings, tallies, weights). Leaves should be named claims about the past — cult practice, status competition, gift exchange, bureaucratic record-keeping, household craft — plus a real agnostic leaf.

Two things I want forced. First, belief-talk shouldn't be the default: make the move to "they thought X" a node with its own failure conditions, not the endpoint every branch drifts toward. Second, I want the analogy problem in there — a node for when the comparison class is a much later or very distant culture that got used because it was convenient, with the way that goes wrong spelled out.

Flag any pair of leaves the evidence genuinely can't separate (votive deposit vs. a rich household's rubbish, say) rather than splitting them by vibe. And call out two or three combinations your questions allow that nobody in the field really occupies, with a sentence on whether that's because the combo is incoherent, unatractive, or just unexplored.

Keep it to about eight branch nodes — I don't want jargon I'd have to footnote.
