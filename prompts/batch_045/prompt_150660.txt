I'm a PhD student in philosophy finishing a chapter on what the algorithmic-fairness impossibility results actually establish, and I want a number on a diagnosis I have been defending. Set the results up as I would: for a binary decision rule scored against a single observed outcome Y, you cannot have calibration within each group, equal false positive rates, and equal false negative rates simultaneously, unless the groups share a base rate or the rule is already perfect. The standard reading is that our fairness criteria are mutually unsatisfiable. My diagnosis is that their force comes entirely from treating Y as decision-independent: in the cases that motivate the literature — pretrial release, credit, hiring — the decision is itself an intervention on the outcome, so the honest variables are potential outcomes Y(0) and Y(1), and the base-rate gap that drives the incompatibility is in part something earlier decision rules produced. On that reading the theorems constrain a static snapshot and say little about which rule is fairest.

A number and one line of justification for each term, then the combination.
