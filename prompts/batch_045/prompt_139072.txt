hey — i manage a small user-research team at a mid-size software company and this has suddenly become my problem. Our new director of research came in with a chip on her shoulder about folk psychology: she says belief/desire explanations are pre-scientific, that a mature cognitive neuroscience wont quantify over them, and that our reports should describe mechanisms rather than what users supposedly think they want. Half my team is now rewriting findings in the language of affordances and reinforcement histories; the other half points out that a lot of what we sell is literally about what people expect the button to do. I dont want to settle the metaphysics, I just have to sign off on fifteen-odd reports a month and right now its case by case.

What I need is the eliminativist challenge turned into a procedure one of my writers can run on a single finding in under ten minutes, with no philosophy background, before I sign it. Ordered tests, I think, for whether a belief/desire attribution in the report is doing work nothing else in the report is doing — predicting behavior in a new setting, generalizing to a slightly different user, distinguishing two users who act identically — versus being a redescription of the behavior that would have survived whatever we found. Give me explicit stopping points: drop the attribution; keep it but tag it as a placeholder standing in for a mechanism nobody has yet; keep it plain. And mark the spots where its not a factual question at all but a call about audience or stakes — an engineer who has to build something vs. an exec who needs a story, and whether the company believed X counts as the same kind of claim as the user believed X.

Please run it on two findings we actually have. One: people on the checkout page click the big green button within about 400 milliseconds and later say they knew it would apply the discount, but the discount only applies on Tuesdays. Two: our power users keep using the old dashboard even though they tell us the new one is better and cant say why — that second one is where I expect your procedure to tell me something I dont want to hear. Finally, say plainly what the procedure cannot do, in particular whether it has anything to say about a case where the attribution is genuinely load-bearing for prediction today but you expect it to get cashed out mechanistically in ten years.
