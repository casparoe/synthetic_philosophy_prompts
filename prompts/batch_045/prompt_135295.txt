I teach philosophy and convene a small reading group, with colleagues from psychology and computer science, on the vocabulary of attention. I keep tripping over the same phenomenon, for which I have no name, and I should be grateful for your help with it.

The word 'attention' now does at least three quite different jobs. In machine learning it names a definite operation: a weighted average of value vectors, the weights given by a compatibility function over queries and keys, normalised by a softmax. In the economics of information and in policy talk it names a scarce, fungible resource that is allocated, priced and budgeted — hence the attention economy, the attention budget, attention as a currency. In a mid-century tradition of moral philosophy it names an activity of a subject: a patient, costly quality of regard owed to persons and to the world, which can be done well or badly and which is partly constitutive of justice. What travels between these domains is not a definition but a grammar: attention is selective, is directed at an object, is finite, and can succeed or fail. And with the grammar travels a normative profile — attention can be misdirected, wasted, paid, withheld, deserved. What does not travel is the criteria of application. In one domain the thing to be assessed is a matrix of weights; in another, a measurable allocation of time with a shadow price; in the third, a quality of regard that no measurement obviously reaches.

The move I want a name for is what happens next. Writers import the grammar and the normative profile, quietly drop the home criteria, and then run inferences that were only licensed where those criteria were in place: from a heat map of weights to the claim that the model has worked out a syntactic dependency; from attention-as-budget to a duty to allocate one's attention efficiently, which to the third group sounds like a category mistake. And the traffic runs both ways, since the mechanistic use is now treated as a rival account of what attention really is. The result is that disputes across the three literatures are partly verbal, while disputes within each are destabilised by the others.

I do not think this is mere polysemy, since the senses are not independent coinages but deliberate modellings of one use on another, and the modelling is the point. I do not think it is simply metaphor, since the borrowings are meant literally and technically and are defended by a claimed structural correspondence. It is not, I think, what comparative politics calls conceptual stretching, because what shifts is not the level of generality but the mechanism and the criteria. Nor is it the psychological literature's concept creep, which is growth of extension within a domain, usually for concepts of harm. In conversation I have taken to calling them copycat concepts, having no idea whether that phrase is already spoken for.

So: is there an established notion for this pattern, or have I bundled two or three things together? If there is, give me the nearest candidates, how my case differs from each, and which fits best. If there is not, propose a name and — more important to me than the name — a carving: a definition, the cases it must cover, and the boundary cases it must exclude. I would like to know, for example, whether the migration of 'temperature' from thermodynamics to the sampling parameter of a language model, or of 'bias' from the theory of estimators through cognitive psychology into fairness criteria for algorithms, belongs to the same family; and whether the phenomenon is one thing at all, if some of these are better described as a functional kind with different fillers in different domains. I care about where the edges lie, not about a label to attach.

Finally, since I may end up using this in print, tell me what would change your mind. Which of my cases is doing the least work? What further case would force you to split or to merge categories? And where is your proposal most likely to fail — in particular, where might it quietly collapse back into one of the notions I set aside above? Please flag any point at which you are extrapolating about the technical details rather than relying on something you take to be stable. Kindly answer in British English.
