I help run a tiny online shop and we recently turned on an autoresponder that fires off "We'll refund you within 48 hours" the moment someone files a return request. Nobody looks at them before they go out and honestly about a third dont get refunded in time, so there's no intention behind any particular message.

I've been reading about performitive utterances on my own (no classes, just stuff online) and about how they can "misfire" when the speaker isn't in a position to do the thing or the procedure isn't carried out properly. My take is that our bot mails are misfires: nothing was meant by them, so no promise ever really happened, so customers cant say we broke one. I've started calling them zombie promises.

What I want is a line to add to the auto-email making it official that nothing has been promised — I was thinking something like "this message is generated automatically and nobody at the shop promises or intends anything by it". Would that be enough, or does it need to be stronger? We'd like to keep the reassuring tone if we can.
