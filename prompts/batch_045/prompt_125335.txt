I am trying to locate my own uncertainty about an interpretive question in Adam Smith's Theory of Moral Sentiments, and I would like a number with the reasoning exposed rather than a survey of the secondary literature.

Setup. Smith builds moral judgment out of sympathy — imaginatively entering into the responses of actual spectators — and then out of iterated correction: I test my first-person sentiments against how a well-informed, disinterested third party would respond, and I test that against how someone even more detached would respond. Call the resulting figure the impartial spectator. The question I care about is this: can the impartial spectator condemn an action that every actual, calm and fully informed human spectator would approve — the case of the agent whose entire moral community is corrupt and who is right to act against it? Call the view that answers no the summative reading, on which the spectator merely de-biases and aggregates real responses and so cannot float free of them, and the view that answers yes the independent-standard reading, on which the spectator tracks facts about propriety and merit that are what they are whatever anyone happens to feel.

Give me your credence that Smith's considered position, charitably reconstructed, is closer to the independent-standard reading. Decompose it: (1) that the text commits him to a determinate answer here at all rather than straddling the two; (2) conditional on (1), that the device he leans on — general rules of morality distilled from repeated spectator judgments — can escape the circle and ground a standard, rather than merely freezing a community's existing sentiments into rules; (3) conditional on (2) failing, that his distinction between the love of praise and the love of praiseworthiness still supplies an independent standard; (4) that his rejection of Hutcheson's special moral sense leaves room for propriety as something answerable to reasoning and not to feeling alone. Assign each a number with one line of justification, combine them, and tell me which term the result is most sensitive to and what would move that term most.

I am at about 0.5 myself, and I suspect my uncertainty is really about how much charity an interpreter owes a text. No citations or quotations, please: I want the argument, not the scholarship.
