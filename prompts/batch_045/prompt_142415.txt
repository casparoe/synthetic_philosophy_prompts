For a graduate seminar on Dennett's three stances (physical, design, intentional), I want the specification rather than the survey. Write the desiderata that any account of when a pattern is real — and, correlatively, of when adopting the intentional stance is explanatory rather than merely instrumentally useful — has to meet in order to count as adequate, with the case or consideration behind each requirement.

Fix the purpose as follows: the account has to be good enough to adjudicate two live disputes. First, whether attributing beliefs or goals to a large language model can do genuine explanatory work in a science of its behavior, or is only a convenient façon de parler. Second, whether a purely behavioral criterion is acceptable, so that any two systems with identical input-output histories share all of their patterns, or whether implementation and compression history can matter.

Cover the demands people standardly make — predictive or compressive payoff, counterfactual robustness, integration with causal-mechanical explanation, some form of non-relativity about at least some patterns — but also sort them: which are genuine adequacy conditions for this purpose, which are negotiable preferences, and which are really only the prejudices of one camp (realists about content, mechanists, anti-realists about folk psychology). Say which single requirement does the most work in excluding otherwise attractive accounts, and flag any subset you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, since if the list is internally inconsistent that is itself the result I want.

One candidate requirement I want argued for or against rather than assumed. Call it the off-policy condition: a posit counts as tracking a real pattern only if the predictions it licenses continue to beat the lower stance under interventions its author never saw in the data the description was inferred from — the maze rat's apparent belief about where the food is must survive the experimenter rotating the maze and adding a scent trail; the chess program's apparent desire to win must survive unfamiliar openings or a change in board size. Is that a distinct requirement or a restatement of one already on the list, and what exactly does it rule out?

Keep any scorecard of the main contenders to a few lines. Please write in American English.
