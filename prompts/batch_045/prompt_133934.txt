Here's my opening, and it's the pair of cases I keep choking on. Ada glanced at the department calendar three weeks ago and now believes the meeting is Thursday. Her evidence is thin, the belief is true, and it was formed in a halfway decent way.

(1) Missing the meeting would cost her a job she badly wants. Your side says: no knowledge, and it's reasonable for her to go check again.
(2) Same evidence, same true belief, same everything about her — except the meeting is one she is dreading, and being wrong about the day would be a small win: she'd miss it, feel briefly guilty, and be secretly relieved. There is no risk here at all; if anything, error is mildly good for her.

Two things I dont think you can both have.

a) If stakes can raise the bar, can they lower it? On your own account variant (2) looks like an easy-knowledge case: zero or negative cost of error, so a weaker epistemic position suffices, so — her evidence unchanged — she knows in (2) and doesnt in (1). But then you've made knowledge easier to get exactly when being wrong would benefit her, which is just the old William James will-to-believe move sneaking in the back door: she'd prefer to be wrong, and on your view that preference helps make her a knower. Why isn't that a reductio rather than a cute consequence?

b) And here's the version I actually care about. Variant (3): everything as in (1) — she believes missing the meeting will cost her the job — except she's wrong about that. The committee decided last night to hire her whatever she does, so objectively nothing is at stake. Knowledge or not? If you say yes, knowledge now depends on facts she has no access to, and you owe me a story about why she isn't being perfectly reasonable when she goes and rechecks the calendar anyway. If you say no, knowledge tracks her *mistaken* beliefs about the stakes, which looks worse: it floats free of both her evidence and the real practical situation, and a subject with a phobia about meetings knows less than her calm colleague with identical evidence. So which is it, and what's the principled — not ad hoc — way to pick?
