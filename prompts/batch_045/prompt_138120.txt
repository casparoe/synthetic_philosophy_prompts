I am a philosopher of science with a working — not native — command of statistical vocabulary: I am comfortable reading material pitched at the level of a graduate text on statistical decision theory, I can follow arguments about risk, KL divergence, proper scoring rules, and model misspecification without hand-holding, but I do not think in these terms automatically. I want a translation, and I want it done strictly: where fidelity to the philosophical material and idiomatic usage in my target vocabulary conflict, keep fidelity and flag the strain rather than smoothing it over.

The material to be translated is the logical problem of verisimilitude in its post-Popperian form. Start from Popper's motivating picture: since the greatest theories in the history of science are strictly speaking false, scientific progress toward the truth can only be intelligible if one false theory can be closer to the truth than another. Take Popper's own formal proposal — closeness to truth as truth content minus falsity content, understood over the consequence classes of a theory — and the well-known collapse objection pressed in the mid-1970s, to the effect that on that definition no false theory can be closer to the truth than any other, which Popper himself is said to have conceded almost at once. Then take the main alternative, the likeness or similarity approach associated with Tichý, Hilpinen, Niiniluoto and Oddie, on which a proposition's likeness to the truth is a function of how like the actual world the possible worlds are in which the proposition would be true — including the weighted measures in that tradition that try to combine a best-case term (how near the closest world compatible with the theory is to actuality) with a term that aggregates over the worlds the theory admits — together with the standing complaint that all such measures depend on a chosen metric over the space of possibilities, and that the choice looks arbitrary in a way that infect the comparisons.

Restate this in the vocabulary I named: parameter spaces, estimators and estimands, loss and risk under a sampling distribution, divergence between distributions and its two directions (the zero-forcing and zero-avoiding projections), proper scoring rules, estimation under misspecification and the pseudo-true parameter, model selection and the capacity or regularization knob, and the bias–variance way of separating under-commitment from over-commitment.

My tentative mapping, which I want you to test rather than adopt, has four parts: the truth factor and the content factor in post-Popperian measures correspond to the two directions of a divergence; the best-case and aggregate terms correspond to a distance from actuality to the nearest model-world and to a spread or dispersion penalty on the set a model admits; the free weighting parameter in those measures corresponds to a capacity-control parameter; and the arbitrariness of the metric corresponds to the dependence of a divergence or a loss on a choice of dominating measure, partition of the outcome space, or feature representation, which in my vocabulary is normally settled by fixing a loss tied to a question of interest.

Then crank the translation through three cases it has to handle, and report what happens rather than what you would like to happen. First: the claim that there are nine planets versus the true but nearly vacuous claim that the number of planets is some number other than nine. Intuitively the precise falsehood is closer to the truth than the vacuous truth; I suspect my vocabulary says the opposite, because a maximally sharp false model is catastrophic under log loss, and because the intuition seems to need a metric on the outcome space that a pure predictive-loss framework does not supply. Second: a tautology, which should come out minimally rather than maximally close to the truth. Third: the fact that comparative verisimilitude is supposed to be a logical relation fixed independently of any evidence, prior, or sampling distribution, whereas everything I know how to define in my vocabulary is an expectation taken over some distribution.

Finally, the residue report. Which claims translate cleanly; which translate only if you import something the framework does not contain; which cannot be expressed at all. In particular: is the geometry that the likeness approach needs something my vocabulary is simply missing, and if so is that a defect of the vocabulary or a sign that the likeness approach helped itself to an arbitrary choice? Does my vocabulary have any way of marking the difference between being closer to the truth and being better supported by the evidence, or does the translation collapse them? And is my four-part mapping honest translation, or does it quietly change the subject by relativizing closeness to truth to a chosen loss or question? Please be blunt about where my mapping fails; do not pad the answer with background I already have, and do not cite or recommend any reading. Under fifteen hundred words if you can manage it.
