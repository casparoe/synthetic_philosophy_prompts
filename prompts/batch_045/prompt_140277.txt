Me and my mate had a massive row last night about whether you can rank historical atrocities at all. He says any attempt to put the Holocaust above or below, say, the transatlantic slave trade or a famine caused by colonial policy is morally obscene full stop; I said that can't be right, since we obviously do think some things are worse than others and we act on it all the time — who we commemorate, what we spend on prevention, what we bother to teach. We got nowhere because we were arguing about vibes, so I want the spec instead.

Write me the desiderata any account of comparative atrocity severity would have to meet — the criteria for saying A was worse than B — with the case behind each requirement. The ones I want it to genuinely settle rather than hand-wave:

- what is even being ordered: badness of the outcome, wrongness of the acts, culpability of the people who did it, or how much reason the rest of us had to intervene. He kept sliding between these and I reckon half the row was that.
- worse than what baseline: an atrocity that kills a million people who'd mostly have died of disease soon anyway, versus one that kills a hundred thousand and destroys a language, a religion and a whole form of life nothing will bring back. Death tolls versus irreplaceable loss.
- how much of the downstream centuries count — does an atrocity get worse as its effects ramify, which would make anything recent look artificially small?
- diffuse agency: the slave trade or a policy famine has no villain in the way a pogrom does. Less bad, or just harder to blame someone for?
- whether the result can be a total order at all, or whether incommensurable is sometimes the right answer and where the line falls.

For each, say why it's a requirement and whether it's non-negotiable or just a prejudice of one camp (I suspect must yield a complete ranking is a prejudice, and must not shift depending on who's doing the ranking is not). Also: is resistance to political weaponisation — successor states using a ranking to minimise their own crimes — a desideratum of the theory or only of its reception? Flag any pair you think can't be jointly satisfied; if the list turns out self-contradictory that's the interesting result and I'd rather know. Then tell me which single requirement kills the most of the obvious approaches (body count, intention-based, counterfactual damage, what the atrocity expresses about its victims' worth) and only very briefly how those four do.
