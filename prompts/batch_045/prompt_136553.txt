I had a huge argument with my flatmate last night — she works on something computational-neuroscience adjacent, we were still going at it at like 2am — about whether functionalism actually earns the multiple realizability it's always advertised with, and I came away feeling like I'd been sold something. Her line is that the famous "mental states are multiply realizable, so identity theory is dead" move is a bait and switch: you get to wave at silicon creatures and Martians while the causal roles are described loosely enough that a thermostat could occupy them, and then the moment anyone pushes, the roles get specified so finely that only neurons could ever occupy them. I dont think she's right, but I also couldn't put my finger on the exact step where she goes wrong, so I want to keep arguing — against the strongest version of her side, not a strawman.

So: please play a committed psychofunctionalist — mental states are the occupants of the causal roles picked out by our best empirical psychology, and 'pain' refers to whatever occupies that role rather than to anything readable off behaviour or off the surface chemistry — who is also fully invested in multiple realizability being the whole point of the view. Sharp, willing to concede small things that genuinely don't cost you (you can even say thermostats have beliefs in some thin sense if you want, or not, but say why), never concede that the view slides into identity theory or behaviourism, and no "it depends what you mean by pain" unless you follow it immediately with what you do mean. Paragraph or two per turn, talk like a person arguing at a kitchen table, and dont throw papers or citations at me — I want your argument, not a reading list. Stay in character until I say "stop".

My opening move, which is the bit that actually shut her up for a minute. Imagine we find a system spread across a planet's crust and oceans — call it the Glacier — made of chemical gradients and slowly shifting colonies of something, where for every state in the causal-role graph of human pain there is a state of the Glacier sitting in exactly the corresponding position: triggered by the local analogue of tissue damage, disposed to produce the local analogue of avoidance and withdrawal, interacting with the Glacier's other states the way pain interacts with belief, attention and desire. The isomorphism is exact at the level of the role graph. The only difference is speed: the whole thing runs maybe a billion times slower than a human nervous system, so one pain episode takes about thirty years and nothing in it moves faster than a glacier. And here's the sting — our best psychology never says anything about absolute timescale, because every subject it was built from ran at roughly the same rate, so the role you're pointing at has no timescale written into it at all. So you've got a dilemma: either you say the Glacier is in agony for thirty straight years, which is absurd, or you bolt a timescale condition onto the role — and once you start filling in that kind of implementation detail because it's "obviously part of the job", you're on a slope that ends with the silicon upload and the Martians being excluded too, and multiple realizability was never real in the first place. Which horn do you take, and if you take neither, tell me exactly what in your theory was supposed to rule the Glacier out before you started patching it.
