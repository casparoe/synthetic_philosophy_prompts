my mate has been saying for about three years that he wants out — leave the job, leave the city, start again. He goes on about it constantly and in really specific detail, and he's obviously not having a good time. But he doesn't go. Last year a role came up that was more or less exactly the thing he describes wanting, on better money, and he didn't apply; he said the timing was wrong. Mostly he doesn't even say no, he just lets the deadline go past.

I'm the one he phones at midnight, and I've started to find it wearing. Our other friend has gone further and basically told him: nobody should take your words seriously when your behaviour says the opposite, wanting something is just whatever you actually end up doing, everything else is cheap talk you produce for free. Part of me thinks that's brutal and wrong. Part of me thinks he's right and that I've been enabling something by listening.

What I can't get straight is this: it's never been a clean choice in front of him. Every version of leaving that's actually come up has been bundled with something — a pay cut, his kid's school, his mum who's not well and who he'd be three hours from. So "he chose to stay" seems to be carrying a lot of weight in that argument, when the situation never really offered him staying versus going on its own. And a third person suggested a test: put a small, reversible version in front of him — a month's leave, a trial somewhere — and if he won't even take that, then it's talk. I half like that, but I also don't see why turning down a small version tells you the same thing as turning down the real one.

And underneath it I'm stuck on a weirder question: if I do take what he says at face value, what am I taking seriously? Is there some fact about what he wants sitting inside him that his behaviour is just a bad guide to, or is there nothing there beyond the pattern of what he does?

So, two things I actually need. One: is the behaviour-is-the-only-real-evidence line right here, and if it's missing something, what? Two: what should I concretely do — keep taking the calls, push him toward the trial-run thing, or tell him I'm done talking about it until he moves? Also, please write in British English, the American spellings grate on me.
