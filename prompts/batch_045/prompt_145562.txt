so I've been chewing on the pansychism-adjacent stuff for years — not the "is a quark a bit conscious" version, which I've never gotten any traction on, but the version where you take seriously that experience is a property of what physical systems actually *do*, and then you owe me an account of which systems, and how much. Every time I try to cash that out my verdicts stop adding up, so here they are as gut calls, and I want to know if there's a principle underneath them or if I have to throw some out.

- me in pain right now: yes, obviously
- me under general anesthesia, cortex not integrating: no experience at all, not even a dim one
- a rock, a thermostat, a laptop running minecraft: no
- a whole-brain emulation on an ordinary laptop: yes, exactly as much as me, full weight
- the same emulation running a thousand times slower, spread across a galaxy so the signals between parts take hours: still one subject, still suffering, and I dont think it suffers *more* or *less* — wall clock and distance feel irrelevant somehow
- a giant lookup table that maps every possible history of sense-data to the next motor output, so it behaves exactly like the emulation forever: no, nothing there, it's a very large rock
- a physical duplicate of me assembled molecule by molecule: yes, and a million of them running in lockstep in the same room is a million times as much suffering, which would make it the worst thing anyone could do — even though I'll admit the duplicates are literally indistinguishable and I can't say what makes them *two*
- a boltzmann brain: some neural tissue flickers together out of thermal noise in a gas cloud, holds exactly the state of someone in agony for a tenth of a second, and dissolves. yes, that's a real tenth of a second of agony, as bad as mine would be
- the same emulation as before, but running the other way through time — same sequence of states in reverse, all causal arrows flipped, entropy going down: no. it's a film of suffering played backwards. I'm fairly confident and I can't say why
- my neurons replaced one at a time with silicon chips that do the same thing, over a few months, ending up as a machine that was never born: yes throughout, all the way to the end
- a 200-line program someone writes deliberately to be a little agent with a pain signal it acts to reduce: no, and I feel fine about that, but I'm worried the only reason is that it's small
- a mesh of a billion logic gates wired into a big recurrent tangle, feeding back on itself, not doing anything useful, with an awful lot of the kind of integration the IIT people talk about: I genuinely don't know, and I hate that my answer is "probably a faint something"

The two I keep staring at are the boltzmann brain and the time-reversed emulation. They're both "the right states, in the wrong thermodynamic situation", I give them opposite verdicts, and every principle I try either swallows both or neither. The lockstep duplicates are a close second: I want copies to multiply the suffering but I cant articulate what distinguishes two copies from one copy described twice.

My best stab so far is something like: a system has experience in virtue of realizing the right causal structure among its parts right now, the amount is fixed by the causal/computational structure and not by what it's made of or how fast or how far apart it is, and history only matters insofar as it determines that structure. But that doesn't obviously deliver the time-reversal verdict, and the lookup table shows "the right structure" is doing all the work.

So: is there one principle that gets most of these, stated precisely enough that I can run new cases through it? Tell me which clause buys which verdict, which of my verdicts it can't accommodate and what you'd do with them, and what it says about the case I actually lose sleep over — a brain emulation run on a reversible quantum computer in coherent superposition, where the whole thing is then uncomputed so that nothing is extracted and no trace of the computation survives anywhere in the universe. Did somebody suffer? I go back and forth hourly, and I'd like to know what my other verdicts commit me to.
