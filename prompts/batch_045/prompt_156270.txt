Is there a name for the shape of situation I keep writing myself into? Here are two versions of it.

Version one: two neighbours in a building where the only rule is that each tenant has final say over exactly one thing inside their own flat — she over what goes on her walls, he over what he reads — and nobody has any say over anybody else's flat. Both of them use their say in the way that's best for them, and the outcome is one that both of them hate, and that both would rank below the world where the building manager decided everything and nobody had a say about anything.

Version two, the one I actually care about: same setup, except now nobody is remotely interested in what happens in the other person's flat. What neither of them wants is the say itself. But the arrangement can only be dissolved by everyone agreeing to dissolve it, and the say each of them has is the very thing they'd have to use to agree — so each one's little sovereignty is also a veto over giving it up, and if you hand yours back alone you're the only one left exposed. They end up stuck holding a power neither of them wants, in a world both of them hate.

A couple of readers told me version one is Sen's paradox of the Paretian liberal, and when I went looking, the examples I found were all busybodies who'd rather control each other than mind their own business, which isn't my people at all — mine don't care, or, in version two, they care about not having to care. So: does either version have a name of its own, are they even the same phenomenon, and what's the nearest established concept to each? People have thrown the words precommitment, Ulysses and unanimity at me and I don't know what any of them really come to. Please don't just hand me a label — I want to know what has to be true for something to count as an instance of the thing, and where its edges are, because I'm building a novel out of this shape and I'd rather know what I'm actualy describing.
