I'm a journalist — I cover science and ideas, mostly — and I'm working on a piece about how different countries decide what actually counts as corruption, so I've been going through the published rulings of one national integrity commission (I'll keep it anonimous). Their track record so far:

- a minister gave a road contract to a firm that had given his daughter an unpaid internship the year before; the firm scored top on the technical eval anyway — corrupt
- a district water officer gave a maintenance contract to her own clan's cooperative; fair price, good work, and everyone locally says that's what the office is for — corrupt
- an officer mediated a land dispute and then accepted a goat and a meal from the winning family, as local custom requires — not corrupt
- a councillor bought a generator for a clinic out of her own salary, and the neighbourhood swung to her party — not corrupt
- a financial regulator took a job at a bank he had supervised, six months after leaving, with no decision shown to have been bent — corrupt
- a supplier funded scholarships at the ministry's own training college while its contract was up for renewal; no individual official gained — corrupt

Next month they've got a new one, and it's the reason I'm writing the piece. The ministry has replaced discretionary tender scoring with an automated formula run on uploaded documents. A supplier paid a mid-level official, off duty and off the books, to help them format and upload their submission so it scores higher. No lies, every document true, she has no override, and she has no official duty to advise suppliers at all.

What will they rule, and what rule are they really running? I can see at least two that fit all six precedents — something like corruption is a private consideration displacing the reasons the office exists to act on, versus something like corruption is any practice that, allowed generally, would build a market in official access or wreck the trust the office needs — and they come apart on this case. Give me your best guess with the reasoning, tell me which precedent is the closest analog and which one cuts the other way, and name the single further case that would settle which rule they're on. Also worth saying whether their willingness to condemn the clan contract that everyone locally accepts means they're quietly running a Western definition. No literature review, please — I want the argument, not the reading list.
