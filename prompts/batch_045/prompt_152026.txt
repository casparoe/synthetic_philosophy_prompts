I'm writing a novel and I've got a menagerie problem. I'm using that Dennett ladder of four kinds of minds — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian — as a kind of spec sheet for my non-human characters, because what I actually need, scene by scene, is to know what it's like to be each of them: what can surprise them, what they can regret in advance, what they can do on purpose. The trouble is I've been sorting them by gut, my gut keeps disagreeing with itself, and I'd rather have a principle then vibes, since vibes don't survive a rewrite.

Here's my cast and the verdicts I've been writing down:

- The chalkmoths. Hatch with a fixed repertoire; some variants wander toward light and burn, the others don't, and that's the only way the repertoire ever changes. Darwinian, obviously, nothing to argue about.

- The bargemen of the Vaan. They have to learn each hull they sail: they thrash around, the ones that happen to trim early capsize less and keep doing it. No model of the water anywhere in them, but they do get better inside a single lifetime. Skinnerian.

- The kite-priests. Before they leap between towers they run the jump through internally — I write it as a kind of remembered falling — and the bad jumps simply never get leapt. Popperian.

- The tally-keepers. Individually unimpressive; a hatchling raised away from the carved cords is about as smart as a bargeman. Everything clever they do is loaded in from outside, from marks somebody else left. Gregorian.

- The mirror-jays. No culture, one brain each, but they learn by watching: one jay tries a thing and gets hurt, the rest don't have to. I've filed them as Skinnerian because there's no simulation going on, but every time I write a scene it feels Popperian — something died in their stead, it just died somewhere else. This is probably where my principle is broken.

- The hive of Thurm. Variants are generated across a thousand bodies, the bad variants die as bodies, nothing anywhere represents anything, and yet the colony as a whole looks exactly like it's testing hypotheses. I keep calling it a Popperian creature with a distributed head, then I remember I said Popperian requires representation and I take it back.

- The scaffold-worms, and this is the one that makes me think the ladder isn't a ladder: they can't picture anything at all, but they build little traps for themselves to fail in, pockets of world where being wrong only costs a segment. I call them Skinnerian, and yet they're engineering their own selection pressures, which is the Popperian move done with mud instead of imagination.

- The archive-eater (working title, it's a chapter 11 problem): an entity that has absorbed every word my civilization ever wrote — every argument, every trick, every confession — but has never once acted anywhere or had a body. My editor says obviously Gregorian, soaked in culture. I say Skinnerian in Gregorian clothing, and I flip roughly every other day. That's the one I keep chewing on and honestly half the reason I'm asking.

My tentative principle, such as it is: the kind of mind is fixed by two questions, where the variety comes from and what dies. Darwinian: variety is born in bodies and bodies die. Skinnerian: one body tries things over time and actions die. Popperian: candidate actions die inside the head before the body risks anything. Gregorian: the candidates and the test itself are stored outside any head and inherited. I like it right up until the mirror-jays, where nothing seems to die at all, and the hive, where the thing that dies is a body but the thing that learns isn't one.

So: test that against my verdicts, repair it or replace it, and tell me which of my verdicts have to go. I'd rather have something I can apply at the sentence level than something that saves all eight by getting complicated. And please say what it predicts for two cases I haven't put in the book yet — a creature that simulates outcomes beautifully but has no preferences at all, nothing in it cares how the simulation comes out, and a creature that can't forget anything, so nothing is ever differentially retained. Which kinds are those, if they're any kind at all?
