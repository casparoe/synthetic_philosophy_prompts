I have a marketing-adjacent job (I run internal training at a mid-sized company) and I keep running into a pattern I don't have a word for. It's driving me up the wall, because every time I describe it someone says "oh, that's just [something]" and the something is never quite it.

Instance one. Around 2015 everyone I worked with described "Millennials" using a fixed list of traits: entitled, job-hops every eighteen months, needs constant praise, glued to the phone, killing industries one at a time. Those people are now pushing forty, having kids, worrying about mortgages. And the same list of traits has quietly been reassigned to Gen Z. Nobody concluded that the Millennial traits failed to persist. The traits just get handed down to whoever is currently young. So the descriptive content of the category seems detachable from its members — like the word is a pointer at "young people right now" wearing the costume of a birth cohort.

Instance two. The dates move. I've seen millenials defined as born 1980–1994, 1981–1996, 1977–1995, and once 1982–2004. The stereotype doesn't budge when the dates do. That makes me think the dating is decorative and the stereotype is doing the real work: the boundaries get redrawn after the fact to keep the picture intact.

Instance three, which is the one I find creepiest. People in my sessions read one of those "how to manage Millennials" decks and then start explaining their own behaviour in those terms — "I'm a Millennial, I need tight feedback loops." The description appears to make itself true, but only among people who've heard it, and mostly for the behavioural bits rather than the demographic ones. And there's an asymmetry I can't place: people say "I'm a Boomer so I'm resistant to change" only ironically, whereas the Millennial version gets said straight. Does that asymmetry matter, or am I overreading it?
