I'm in a third-year seminar on consciousness and we have each been asked to circulate a short profile of our commitments so that the others can audit it before next week's session on the access/phenomenal distinction and global workspace theory. Here is mine, and I would like to know whether it hangs together.

1. The access/phenomenal distinction is a genuine distinction that could in principle be settled empirically: the two can come apart, and this is not a merely verbal dispute.
2. Global workspace theory gives the best account of access consciousness: for a content to be access-conscious is roughly for it to be globally broadcast — available at once to report, reasoning, voluntary action and long-term memory, rather than confined to a single modular consumer.
3. Phenomenal consciousness overflows access. I take the partial-report advantage in Sperling-style tasks, and the persistence of late neural markers in no-report paradigms where subjects are never asked to report anything, to show that a subject can be phenomenally conscious of more than she can access.
4. If global workspace theory is right, it is the whole story about consciousness rather than only a story about one species of it: once broadcast, availability and control have been explained, there is no further phenomenal residue left over to explain.
5. There is no "rich" unconscious experience — no fully determinate, specific experience that is in principle unavailable to any consumer system. Unconscious perception, if there is any, is sparse.
6. Verbal report is a poor gold standard for consciousness: a subject can be conscious of something and yet unable to report it, as tip-of-the-tongue states and memory degraded under load remind us.

I have a suspicion that the package is unstable somewhere, but I cannot locate the fault line, and I would rather not be told simply to adopt whichever view you happen to hold. Go through the pairs, say which are genuinely in tension and which only look as though they are, rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would cost me; then say which single revision would clean up the most. Please treat this as an audit of this particular package rather than as an occasion to argue for your own theory of consciousness. The last line of your reply should be the commitment you think I ought to drop or weaken first, in the form \answer{n}, where n is its number.
