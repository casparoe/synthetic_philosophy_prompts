Hey — could you help me draft a short research proposal? Would like the answer in American English please.

I'm puting in for a small internal grant in my department: ten months, enough for one RA and some subject payments, to run a single pilot study with a motor-control lab on campus (they're up for it, we've already talked). My half-formed idea is this. The classic Libet-style results are almost always read as claims about timing — some neural precursor of the movement starts before the subject reports being aware of any intention, so the conscious decision arrives too late to be the cause. But what actually does the work in the free-will argument, it seems to me, is a different and stronger claim: that the *content* of the decision (which hand, now or later, this action rather than that one) is already settled at that earlier point, so that by the time you're aware of anything the outcome is fixed. Timing and content-fixing can in principle come apart. You might have an early, content-free build-up of readiness and a late settling of what you're actually going to do — in which case the timing result alone wouldn't threaten much at all. I also think the standard free-wont reply (fine, the urge is unconscious, but you can still veto it) mostly relocates the problem rather than answering it, since whatever we say about the urge we can say about the veto, and I want that dealt with instead of gestured at.

So write me the proposal. It needs: the question stated tightly enough that a pilot could actually come out one way or the other; why the usual framing leaves that question open; what we'd actually do — the design in rough outline, what the philosophical half of the project is while the data comes in, and how the ten months break down; what the possible outcomes are, and which of them would mean the project failed as opposed to just returning a null result; what a deliverable looks like for the two non-philosophers on the committee; and an honest paragraph on why this might be way too much for ten months with one RA. About two pages. Don't give me any citations or a reading list — I'll fill those in, and the committee doesn't want references anyway.
