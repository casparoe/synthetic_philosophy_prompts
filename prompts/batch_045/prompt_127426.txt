hey — practical one. At work we're about to ship a model that ranks job applicants for a first-round interview, and it'll run in two countries at once, with different base rates of "would have been a good hire" and, because of local headcount, different cut-offs. I'm the one who has to write down what "fair" means for it before it ships, and I don't want to just copy a defintion out of a library.

So: write me the desiderata. Not a list of published criteria — the spec those would have to meet. What would a fairness criterion for a ranking model in this situation have to deliver to count as adequate here? The distinctions it has to keep intact, the cases it has to get right, and the facts about our setup it has to be consistent with. The distinctions I have in mind (tell me if I'm drawing them in the wrong place): "a given score means the same thing in both groups" vs "the false-positive and false-negative rates match across groups"; fair at the threshold we happen to use vs fair at every threshold we might move to; fair for the two groups we wrote down vs fair for every subgroup someone names at us next year; and fair in the statistical sense vs fair in the causal sense, i.e. about why the model scored this person differently. Give me the case or consideration behind each requirement, so I can tell a genuine desideratum from one camp's prejudice dressed up as a principle, and mark which ones are non-negotiable for us and which are preferences I could trade away.

Two constraints, built into the list rather than bolted on at the end: we only ever observe whether someone was a good hire for the people we actually hired, and the model gets retrained every quarter on the outcomes of its own past decisions — so whatever we adopt has to be checkable from data that has been filtered by, and partly created by, the model itself. And it has to be statable to a rejected candidate or to a regulator without collapsing into something vacuous.

The part I most want: I know the standard impossibility results — you can't satisfy all of these across groups at once once the base rates differ. So tell me which subset of my desiderata is provably unsatisfiable together, because if that's the situation then the real question isn't which criterion to pick, it's which requirement I drop and what I say out loud when I drop it. And if there's one requirement that does most of the work of knocking candidate definitions out, point at it.
