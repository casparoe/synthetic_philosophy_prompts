I'm on our town council's by-law cleanup working group (unpaid, endless minutes) and we're going through the surviving by-laws one at a time. I'd like your read on next week's one, because I can't work out what rule we've actually been running.

Where we've landed so far:

- Market stalls to be struck by 2pm on Sundays. We found the 1901 minute: it was so the square was free for the afternoon cattle market, which shut in 1954. Nobody could find anything the rule does now. Repealed, 5–2.
- Households keeping more than three dogs to register them. Nothing in the archive, the by-law predates the minutes that survived. Then it turned out the animal warden and two vets use the register as their only index when tracing a bite complaint. Costs nothing. Kept unanimously.
- No structures on the strip along the river. Rationale unknown, no current use anyone could name, but repealing it is what lets us grant the 25-year boat-club lease, which we could never undo. Kept 4–3.

Next week: By-law 34 says the hatch on the mill stream has to be kept shut from Michaelmas to Lady Day (roughly October to March). Nobody knows why; the archive has nothing before 1890. It's a nuisance: the contractor on the flood scheme says it conflicts with the works and the clerk thinks it's dead wood. On paper repeal is trivially reversible, one resolution. But the stream is due to be culverted next spring under a scheme that's already approved and funded, so after that nobody can ever find out what the hatch was for; and a retired surveyor has written in saying that shutting it over winter is what keeps the stream scouring rather than silting, which is why the culvert under the high street has never backed up. Nobody has checked that, and nobody much wants to.

So what do you think we do, and what should we do to be consistent with ourselves? I care less about the vote than about the rule we seem to be running. Is it a Chesterton-fence thing where not knowing why is itself the reason to leave it alone, or just option value (cheap to keep, expensive to put back), or current function, or something about what we'd lose the chance to learn? Which precedent is the closest analog? I'm also stuck on whether the surveyor's letter should count at all: letting an unchecked but plausible story discharge the explain-the-fence-first burden makes the presumption unfalsifiable, but ignoring it seems wrong too.

Give me a table, one row per candidate rule, with these columns: rule (one sentence, stated so it could be applied to a by-law you've never seen), which of our three precedents it fits and which it strains on, verdict on by-law 34, and one further by-law (which you describe) that would best separate it from the other rows. Then three or four sentences: your best guess at our vote, the closest analog, and what principle we'd be committing to either way.
