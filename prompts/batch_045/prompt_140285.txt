In my interpretability reading group we've been going in circles for over a year on whether trained networks really represent anything, or whether saying "the network represents X" is just a useful façon de parler. The realists point at probes, sparse-autoencoder features and activation patching: you find a direction that tracks something like deception or Python-ness, intervening on it changes behaviour the way you predicted, and similar features turn up in models trained seperately — that looks like discovery, not projection. The anti-realists say all of that is equally compatible with the directions being artifacts of the geometry plus our choice of basis, that "it represents X" says nothing more than that a thermometer represents the temperature, and that once there's enough superposition there may be no fact of the matter which of the many equally predictive decompositions is the one the net is using.

What makes me think its not just stubbornness: imagine we had a synthetic training set where we know the true latent variables (colour, material, whatever), and GD reliably finds features that are stable across seeds and genuinely interventionally useful, but which are linear mixtures of the true latents rather than the latents themselves — and no training tweak ever recovers the latents. Assume everyone grants that result. My hunch is the argument still wouldn't budge, because it was never about features: it's a proxy fight over whether representation requires tracking the causal structure of the world, or whether being a stable, counterfactually efficacious element of the model's own processing is enough. Is that right, or is it really a verbal dispute about the word, a clash over which evidence counts (prediction vs. intervention vs. uniqueness), or an ill-posed question? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress?
