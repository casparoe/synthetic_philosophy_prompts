I write novels. My fourth is narrated by a woman named Ingrid who has spent twenty years telling herself that she left her marriage because her husband was cold, when what she is actually protecting herself from knowing is that she left because she was afraid of becoming her mother. For the last eight months I have been using a language model as a drafting interlocutor — I give it a scene, or a chapter, or a paragraph I cannot make work, and it gives me back prose. Some of what comes back is better than what I would have written. Once, working from a scene where Ingrid's ex-husband asks her a question she does not answer, the model gave her the line: she had not been cold, she had been somewhere else entirely. That is the line. It is the sentence the chapter exists to arrive at, and I did not have it.

What I cannot settle is whether anything in the production of that line understands Ingrid. I want to be exact about the claim, because I think this quarrel is usually allowed to float. Call it U: the model's continuations about a fictional character are governed by something functionally analogous to an author's or an attentive reader's grip on that character's standing motives and dispositions, where governed by means at least that the continuation is counterfactually keyed to the character — it changes in the right way when the character changes — and not merely interpolative, driven by the pressure of what sentences resemble the ones it has seen. U is meant to be weaker than consciousness and stronger than fluency. If you think the dispute is really only about where between those two to draw the line, say so, and tell me how much of my credence should be reassigned to the branch on which the question has no determinate answer.

My own credence in U is roughly 0.35. A poet I trust puts it near 0.8: if the sentence moves a professional reader, she says, something knew something. A friend who writes criticism puts it near 0.05 and calls the whole performance a stochastic-parrot effect over my own style. Two observations from my own desk are doing most of the work in my number, and I suspect they are weak evidence.

First: I told the model in a setup note that Ingrid played the cello seriously until she was twenty-two and then stopped. A writer would use that fact almost nowhere — it matters only in the one late scene where she passes a busker in a station. But in draft after draft the model found places for the cello: it behaved like someone who has been handed a fact and is looking for somewhere to put it. Is knowing when not to use what you know part of understanding a character, or a separate virtue — restraint, taste — that can come apart from it?

Second: I revealed late in the manuscript that Ingrid had read her mother's letters years before she claims to have found them, and asked for the earlier discovery scene to be revised. The model inserted foreshadowing: a hesitation, a hand on a drawer. It decorated the scene; it did not reinterpret it. It never proposed that the scene was now about a woman performing surprise, and that its rhythm and its sentences had to become different. Is the capacity for retroactive reinterpretation — letting a later fact change what an earlier scene was about, not merely what it hints at — a condition on understanding a character, or a distinct authorial skill that many human readers also lack?

Give me your own number for U, but mainly the decomposition: how much rides on U's having a determinate truth value at all; how much on whether counterfactual grip is genuinely required for understanding as opposed to reliable scene-local continuation being enough; how much on whether the shallowness I am seeing is architectural or an artifact of context limits, instruction tuning and preference training; how diagnostic you take my two observations to be; and how much my own sense of being understood should count, given that I supplied the prose and am an extremely willing reader of intention. Assign a rough probability to each with a line of justification, show the combination, then tell me which term you are least confident in, which single experiment I could run at my desk this week would move your number most, and where the gap between my poet friend at 0.8 and my critic friend at 0.05 most likely lives.
