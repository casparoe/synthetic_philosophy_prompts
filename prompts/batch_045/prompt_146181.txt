What I keep chewing on: the "does philosophy make progress?" debate always ends up either comparing philosophy to science or playing example tennis — look, we reached agreement on X; look, we still disagree about free will — and I think both moves measure the wrong thing. What philosophy mostly produces is distinctions: new variants of old positions, new failure modes for views that used to be coasting. That output is invisible if your only metric is how many questions got settled. So I want to measure progress in the shape of the option space instead — how many live positions there are, how fine-grained they've become, and whether any have actually been eliminated rather than quietly renamed.

Concretely, please give me:

- Progress split into at least two metrics that can come apart — I'm imagining something like elaboration (branching, finer distinctions, more nuanced versions) versus elimination (positions genuinely off the table) — and a verdict on which, if either, deserves to be called progress, and whether the other is a kind of failure or just a different thing entirely.
- A decision on whether to borrow a Lakatos-flavored test here: does a subfield's problem-shifting anticipate and absorb novel cases, or does it just patch old ones after the fact? Or is borrowing that just smuggling a scientific standard back in through the side door? Take a side and argue it, don't list both.
- A method section for one person and one year. I was vaguely picturing auditing a single subfield across roughly fifty years, mapping its live positions at two time-slices, but I don't know how to stop that from collapsing into my own impressions.
- Which subfield to audit, with the cherry-picking worry addressed head-on: pick the field that already looks progressive and of course you'll find progress. Say what would make the choice fair, or how to correct for the bias if no choice is fully fair.
- Honest failure conditions and an honest scope check: what finding would mean the project didn't work, and what version of it is too big for a year.
