Doing the Chinese Room in intro phil of mind next week and I'd rather open with a poem than another handout. Write me a sestina — I want the form to do real argumentative work, since six end-words cycling through fixed slots is basically the argument's whole point about shuffling formal symbols never adding any meaning. Voice: the book of instructions itself, talking to whoever's in the room moving the symbols around.

Constraints. (1) Pick the six end-words yourself; each should name something the argument calls merely formal or syntactic but which you'll make do semantic work in the poem — then give me one line of prose per word on why you chose it. (2) The book has to actually answer the systems reply and the robot reply, not just gesture at them, and it has to land on Searle's real move — that giving the system a body only helps if the body has the right causal powers, and that simulating understanding isn't understanding — without ever using the phrases "syntax is not semantics" or "Strong AI". (3) It's not allowed to claim its meaning comes from the Chinese speakers who wrote it. (4) The tornada should be the one place it produces something that reads like a genuine answer to a real question, and whether that counts should stay open.

Under 45 lines total. It should be right enough that someone who actually knows the literature doesn't wince, and still work as a poem for students who don't.
