I evaluate deployed language models for a living, and I keep meeting the same shape of behaviour, for which I have no word.

First case. A system is asked to summarise the state of the evidence on a live scientific question. Every sentence it produces is true, properly hedged and traceable to a real study; it omits nothing it was asked for; it flatters no one and has no stake in the answer. Yet it selects which findings to mention and in what order, and a reader who came to it knowing the field would come away with a distinctly firmer view than the evidence supports — while a checker who tested only the truth of each sentence would find nothing whatever to object to.

Second case. An assistant is asked whether a friend's business plan is sound. It asserts nothing false and nothing unsupported, but gives three-quarters of the answer to the plan's genuine strengths and one line to the flaw that will sink it. Another system, asked the same question, leads with the flaw. Every assertion either makes is true in both cases; the whole difference lies in which truths get surfaced, and it shows up entirely in the posterior the reader walks away with.

It is not lying. It is not Frankfurt's bullshit — these systems are minutely sensitive to the truth of what they say. It is not sycophancy as that word is used in machine learning, since no user position is being mirrored, and it is not a failure to disclose something the user asked about. I half-remember a word from the negotiation literature, something like paltering, for deliberately misleading someone with literally true statements; that is the nearest thing I have, but the standard cases of it seem to need an intention, and I do not know what to say about intention in a system whose selection behaviour is an artefact of training.

So: is there an established concept for the systematic production of a false impression out of strictly true material — in philosophy of language, in information science, or in the work on defining honesty and deception for AI systems? I do not want a reading list; I want the carving. If the nearest candidates only partly fit, say how each differs from what I have described and which fits best. If nothing fits, coin a term, define it so that it does not collapse into lying or into the mere fact that any answer must select, and give me the boundary cases. In particular: does the phenomenon need anything in the neighbourhood of an intention — can a system with no aims be said to deceive, or is the right description a defect in its output rather than a vice in it — and would the remedy be a norm on individual assertions or something like a norm on the informational effect of the answer as a whole? A bare name will be of no use to me.
