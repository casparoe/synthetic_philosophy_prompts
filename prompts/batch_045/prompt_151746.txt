Before I commit to any answer about the teletransporter, I want a specification of what an account of what matters in survival would have to deliver. Fix the purpose: the theory has to be good enough to do three jobs — tell an agent standing in front of the scanner what she has prudential reason to prefer, fear, or regard with indifference; tell her what she owes, and what is owed to, whoever comes out at the far end; and tell her whether the question "will it be me?" has a determinate answer at all, or is indeterminate in a way that ought not to worry her.

Write the desiderata with the case or consideration behind each: the verdicts the theory must be able to give, the distinctions it may not collapse (being me versus mattering to me; what matters versus what matters most; the question of who is who versus the question of what to care about), the constraints it must respect from ordinary psychology and neurobiology (sleep, gradual cerebral replacement, dense amnesia, dementia), and what it must say about whether survival admits of degrees. Mark each desideratum as non-negotiable or as merely the prejudice of one camp — I suspect both the demand that survival be one–one and the demand that it always be determinate belong in the second category, but argue the point rather than conceding it.

At least three desiderata should be motivated by cases that the usual presentations skip. (i) Partial scan: 97 per cent of the synaptic weights are captured before the original is destroyed, the remainder filled in from a population-average model; the result is recognisably you to everyone who knows you, but carries a patch of confabulated memory. (ii) Stored scan, staggered instantiation: one copy made at year five and dead by year ten, a second made from the same scan minutes afterwards; in a variant, both run concurrently for a decade. (iii) Re-fusion: after ten years apart, the two branches' connectomes are averaged into a single recipient.

Then: which pair of desiderata cannot be jointly satisfied, and which single requirement does the most to exclude otherwise attractive views. A compressed scorecard for animalism, the psychological-continuity view, the further-fact view, and the no-further-fact view is welcome, but the criteria are the deliverable.
