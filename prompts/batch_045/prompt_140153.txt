I've more or less swallowed the Dennett line on Orwellian vs Stalinesque revision. Set up the case I keep chewing on: two taps delivered in quick succession, one near the wrist then one near the elbow, and people feel a little sequence of taps hopping up the forearm, including at places nothing ever touched — the cutaneous rabbit version of the same problem you get with color phi. Two stories. (O) the subject first consciously felt two taps at the two real locations, and then the record was rewritten on the way into memory and report. (S) the rewrite happened on the way in, so what was experienced was already the hopping sequence, and there was never a conscious wrist-then-elbow moment at all. My position: there's no fact of the matter about which happened, and more generally no determinate fact about *when* an experience occurs or what its content was at a given instant at this timescale — the two hypotheses only come apart relative to a Cartesian theatre that isn't there. And I want to add the stronger claim, that this isn't an empirical gap we might close someday but a badly formed question.

Tell me what would count against that. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and for each say whether it's live or just logically possible, and whether a committed defender would concede it as counting or would say it smuggles back the very picture the view rejects.

The cases I want you to actually grapple with, not wave at:

1. The pre-emptive probe. Suppose subjects are trained to make a speeded response inside the revision window — tap the *real* location of the second stimulus, say — and on critical trials they do, while their later report describes the hopping sequence. suppose too that we can decode from early sensory maps activity that looks just like what a real tap at the intermediate locations produces. Is content that is decodable and guiding action inside the window thereby experienced, or is that just the pipeline doing its thing? Sharper version: a disruptive pulse at one latency abolishes the rabbit and restores a veridical two-location report, with a clean latency boundary. Do we now have a third-personal fact about when the revision happened, and isn't that the fact I'm denying exists?

2. Nested revision. Suppose the revision itself gets revised — a later event rewrites the rewritten version, so there are three candidate times of experience. Does the no-fact claim just iterate cost-free, or does the Orwellian get a regress complaint that I don't? And if the Orwellian has to say which revision was the conscious one, why don't I?

3. Trained observers. If extensive practice with feedback makes people report the veridical sequence in the very same displays, have we made their reports accurate or just stopped the revision from happening? How could we possibly tell apart, and does it matter to my view which it is?

4. Scope pain. If the Stalinesque story is right for a brief pain that the system then edits out, was the subject in pain? If some version of that made suffering disappear retroactively, that's a consequence I'd reject — so does my view have to be quietly restricted to the perceptual-timing cases, and is that a scope problem? Same question for willing and agency.

Be honest about whether this thing has been insulated to the point where nothing could touch it, and if it has, whether that's fine for a claim like this — I genuinely can't tell if I've got an empirical thesis or a conceptual one, and if it's conceptual then calling it unfalsifiable might just be name-calling. Also: what does the view predict that its rivals (say a broadcast-style view that thinks there's a determinate moment of access) don't? The thing that would move me most personally is if you can find something I'm committed to that I can't even state, because that's where I suspect the whole thing quietly collapses into one of the views it was supposed to beat.
