hey — I'm one of the two undergrad reps on the committee that allocates the Student Services and Activities Fee at my uni (big public place, about 4000 fee payers). We have to spend or hand back roughly 60k of unspent reserve at Monday's vote, and I'm meant to circulate a short memo with a recommendation beforehand. I'm also taking a seminar on distributive justice this term and we literally did the levelling down objection last week, so this is sitting weirdly in my head, and I'd like the memo to actually use the ideas rather than just saying that equity matters.

The live options:

A — Campus Life Grants: no-strings emergency micro-grants of 500 dollars to students flagged high-need by our hardship index (parental income, rent burden, dependents, whether someone is on a meal plan). Staff think about 120 students would qualify and maybe 60 percent would actually claim it, after around 8k of verification and admin cost, so a real chunk of the money goes unclaimed and reverts to the general fund. The money would come out of the Wellness Pass.

B — keep the Wellness Pass and put the 60k into expanding the professional-school prep program (mock interviews, suit lending, alumni dinners). The pass is free printing, the campus shuttle and the climbing wall. It's mostly used by students who are already fine, but about a fifth of its regular users are Pell-eligible, and it's one of the few places where students from really different backgrounds mix. Rough numbers: the pass gives each of about 900 regular users maybe 70 dollars of value a semester, and cutting it is what frees up the 60k. The prep program is projected to raise starting salaries for around 150 mostly-already-advantaged students.

C — skip verification entirely and give every fee payer a flat grocery credit of about 15 dollars. No admin waste, no stigma, full take-up, but most of the money lands on people who don't need it.

So the shape of it: A probably makes total benefit go down — better-off students lose more than the worse-off gain, plus the admin waste and the unclaimed money — but what gain there is lands on people who are much worse off. That's exactly where the priority view bites and where the levelling-down objection tells me to be suspicious. Please don't give me a memo that just says prioritarianism escapes the objection in the pure levelling-down case; I know that, and our case isn't the pure case, because the worse-off do get something.

What I need in the memo:

- a bottom line — A, B, C, or something in between, and if it's in between say exactly what the split is
- a number we could actually put in the motion: how many dollars of loss to a comfortable student are we willing to accept per dollar of gain to a high-need student before we stop? 1 to 1, 5 to 1, or no limit? And why that number rather than the ones either side of it, because the committee will definitely ask
- how to handle the fact that the pass isn't purely a rich-kid perk, since a fifth of its users are the same population the grants target. Is that a reason to cut less of it, or just noise?
- timing and mechanics. We could cut the pass now, or keep it this year and let it lapse next year with notice while funding grants from the reserve now. Our precedent treats cutting an existing service very differently from declining to start a new one, and I suspect that distinction is doing sneaky moral work — say whether it should
- the strongest thing the losing side can say, stated as well as it can be stated. My co-rep thinks A is obviously right; I think the folks who loose the pass have a real complaint, since some of them commute on that shuttle and organised their year around it. Put that in properly, not as a strawman

Keep it under about 900 words, memo format, something I can paste straight into the agenda packet. Thx!
