I'm prepping a session on statutory interpretation for my jurisprudence seminar, and I'd rather run it as an adversarial collaboration than another shouting match. Could you draft the joint statement that a committed textualist and a committed purposivist could both actually sign?

Format: a numbered section of agreed claims, then a disagreements table where each position is stated in wording its own side would accept, then the cruxes, and for each crux a concrete case or piece of evidence both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down.

Two constraints, because these are exactly where my students stall. First, keep textualism seperate from originalism -- they collapse the two constantly -- and say explicitly whether the textualist is reading for the ordinary meaning of a reasonable reader today or at the time of enactment. Second, anchor the whole thing to a made-up statute rather than real cases:

"Any person who knowingly brings a dangerous weapon onto school property is guilty of a felony."

Assume a jurisdiction with no other relevant law beyond a general ban on carrying a concealed weapon, and run these four through both methods: a teacher who keeps a folding knife in a locked glove compartment in the school parking lot; a senior with a two-inch nail file in her backpack; a shop teacher who uses a box cutter to open supply crates in his classroom; and a visiting parent with an unloaded hunting rifle in a case in her trunk while picking up her kid.

Things I'd especially like handled: whether the two camps really disagree about what the text means or only about what to do once meaning runs out; whether "purpose" is the legislature's expected applications or the abstract point of the statute; and how each handles a case where the text is perfectly clear but the application is one every legislator would have disavowed. If you think some of the dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why. And give me a line or two on what each side would count as evidence against it's own view -- my students assume neither camp has any.
