ok so im in yr 12, we did like two lessons of philosophy in general studies and now I cant stop thinking about this. The position ive ended up at is: you can be morally responsible for something even if you couldnt have done otherwise — the principle that blame requires alternative possibilities is false, and Frankfurt-style cases show it. You know the setup: someone decides to do the thing entirely on their own, nothing touches them, but there's a device or a person in the background that would have forced it if they'd shown any sign of wavering. No alternatives, but we still blame them. Seems obviously right to me.

Except I think the usual version of the case has a cheap answer, which is that the backup plan has to wait for some reliable sign that you're about to not-do-it, and if there is such a sign then there must have been somthing you could have done that stopped the intervention kicking in, so a little scrap of an alternative survives. So the case I actually want to defend is one I made up to get round that: the intervention is unconditional and happens *beforehand*. Call it the lockout. Ten minutes before you choose, without you noticing, every other option is quietly taken off the table — the other doors are locked, the message you were going to send is unsendable, the person you'd have called is unreachable. Then you pick the one remaining option for your own normal reasons, the lockout plays no part in why you picked it, and you never find out about it. You really couldnt have done otherwise, not even a flicker — but the lockout also isn't *why* you did it. If that works, blame doesn't need alternatives at all.

- Deal properly with the "yeah but you could still have *tried* otherwise" move — my lockout only removes the outer options, not the inner ones. Is that a real answer or is it goalpost-shifting? And what would it take to build a version that closes even that gap — and would closing it break the case somewhere else?
- Is there anything my view actually risks? Does it predict anything its rivals don't, and is it stuck with any consequence that turns out unacceptable (something about luck, or about what an excuse even is, or about manipulation)?
