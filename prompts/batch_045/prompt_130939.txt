hey — I've somehow agreed to run next week's session of a general philosophy reading group on Molinism and middle knowledge, 75 minutes, no whiteboard, just me talking and maybe a bit of scribbling on paper. The group is mixed in an annoying way: two people come from a Reformed background and will assume this is Calvinism with extra steps, two lean open theist and think counterfactuals of freedom just don't have truth values, and a couple of regulars are atheists who suspect the whole machinery exists only to smuggle predestination back in. I know the doctrine well enough; what I don't have is a plan for the session.

So: don't explain Molinism to me, plan how I explain it.

What has to be in place before "a third kind of knowledge, between the natural and the free" even sounds like a real option rather than a word trick? I want the actual dependency structure — what the audience has to already be holding in mind (the free-will assumption, what "free" modifies in "free knowledge", the idea of a world as a package of counterfactuals God surveys rather than scripts) and in what order, since I suspect most of the confusion comes from people hearing the threefold distinction before they can feel why two won't do.

One example has to carry the whole thing. I was going to use a casting director who knows exactly how an actor would play a role, but I think that secretly makes it prediction-from-disposition rather than middle knowledge. Tell me what the carrying example must have and must lack — it needs genuine indeterminacy in the agent, no simulation or foreseeing mechanism, and it has to leave God's authorship of the counterfactual truth out of it — and what you'd use instead of my casting director.

Then the misconception map, in the order they'll actually surface, with the point in the sequence where I head each off: "that's just foreknowledge", "if he knows what I'd do I couldn't do otherwise", "Leibniz already did this with possible worlds", "there's nothing there to be known", and — the one I think my own example will create — "so God is just stuck with the hand he's dealt and picks the least bad world". Should any of these be invited rather than defused?

Also: I made up a worry I call the fragility problem. If the circumstances are specified slightly differently — she gets one more minute of sleep — is there still a determinate truth about what she freely does? Molinism seems to need yes, and I'm not sure the group needs to hear this at all. Where, if anywhere, does it go?

End me with one question I can ask that separates people who've got it from people who can just recite the three kinds of knowledge, and tell me what I should openly refuse to get into at this level (I was going to skip the grounding objection and the later free-will-defence version — correct me if that's the wrong cut).
