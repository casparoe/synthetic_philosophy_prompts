I've been reading LessWrong-adjacent material for about a decade, and over that time I've ended up with a cluster of views about the symbol grounding problem and about what we should say concerning systems trained only on text. Before I build anything on top of it I'd like to know whether the cluster is stable. Here it is, as six commitments.

(1) Grounding. I think the Chinese Room argument is basically sound in its negative claim: manipulating symbols according to rules is not sufficient for those symbols to mean anything to the manipulator, and what is missing is the right kind of non-linguistic causal contact with what the symbols are about. A symbol for a cat has to be tethered to cats by perception and action, not merely by its relations to other symbols. So training on a corpus of text — which is, at bottom, more symbols — does not in itself confer meaning, no matter how much of it you do. The map has to be hooked up to the territory by something other than more map.

(2) Internalism about content. I have never been persuaded by Twin Earth-style arguments. I think what a representation inside a system means is fixed by the role it plays in that system's own processing, and that a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me, with whatever internal history I have had, would mean exactly what I mean even if its environment contained XYZ rather than water. I know this is not the majority view; I hold it anyway.

(3) Inherited reference. Despite (1), I do not accept the flat conclusion that a text-trained system refers to nothing. I think it can inherit reference from the linguistic community that produced the texts: its intentionality is derived rather than original, in roughly the way my use of a name for a person I have only ever heard described borrows its reference from those who have met her. So I want to say that such a system does talk about cats, elections and invoices, and that many of its internal states are genuinely about those things and genuinely true or false.

(4) A verdict on a case. I do not want to say it refers to red. A system with no sensors, trained only on text, has never stood in any causal relation to the property, and I think nothing in it is a representation of red however well it plays the language game with the word — it has mastered the inferential relations among redness-talk and none of what red is. But I do want to say it refers to electrons, to inflation and to democracy, on the ground that these get their content from their place in a theory or a social practice rather than from causal contact with their objects.

(5) What understanding is. When we ask whether a system really understands a domain, or has merely latched onto surface statistics, I think the thing to point at is whether it has an internal model supporting counterfactually robust prediction and intervention: one that would still track the domain under interventions that break the superficial patterns, and that degrades gracefully rather than catastrophically off distribution. I also think understanding so understood is a matter of degree rather than a threshold.

(6) Method. I think the way to settle these questions is to look at what the system actually represents internally, not at how it behaves; in particular I think behavioral indistinguishability from a properly grounded system, even across a very large battery of tests, is not by itself sufficient for understanding.

What I cannot see clearly is whether (1) through (6) are jointly tenable. (3) looks like a flat denial of (1). (4) looks like it needs a principle that (3) does not supply. (2) seems to sit badly with the causal-history story doing the work in both (1) and (3). (5) may simply hand back to text-trained systems, by a different route, exactly what (1) denies them. And (5) and (6) pull against each other. But I don't trust my sense of which of these are real and which are verbal.

So please audit the set rather than lecture me on the topic. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and for each real tension, what exactly would have to be given up and what that would cost me elsewhere in the set. Rank the tensions by severity instead of treating them as equally bad, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. If one of the six is doing nothing but expressing an intuition whose usual motivation my other commitments take away from me, say which one and why.
