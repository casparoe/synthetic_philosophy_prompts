I am a retired natural scientist who has lately taken up philosophy of mind, and I would like a thought experiment built to order for a seminar on whether a book could be conscious, in the spirit of the Einstein's brain discussion in The Mind's I. The point is to test whether a functionalist theory of mind is really committed to saying that the right causal organization, realized in an artifact that looks inert, suffices for consciousness, so the verdicts must be generated by the theories rather than stipulated. Please construct one scenario, realistic and no longer than a paragraph, in which a bound volume that we would still count as a book is conscious according to functionalism but not conscious according to a biological theory that ties consciousness to ongoing organic or metabolic processes. Constraints: use only ordinary materials such as paper, ink, gears, and springs, with no electricity, silicon, or exotic physics; the object must not be a disguised computer, and its mental states must be realized by the arrangement and mechanical motion of its own pages rather than by an inner operator or external reader. After the scenario, give the verdict each theory delivers and the reasoning behind it, identify the single feature of the case that is doing the work, explain what happens to each verdict when that feature is removed while everything else is held fixed, and sketch how a defender of whichever theory ends up with the less appealing result would attack the case as cheating, for instance by arguing that the object is no longer really a book.
