Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a defender of pragmatic encroachment (interest-relative invariantism about knowledge) and a defender of epistemic contextualism. Both accept that ordinary knowledge attributions are sensitive to stakes; both deny that this sensitivity is merely conversational.

Three stipulations frame the collaboration. (1) Only semantic proposals are on the table: accounts of the stakes data in terms of implicature, relevance, or norms of assertion alone are set aside by agreement, as is the claim that high-stakes speakers simply have weaker evidence than low-stakes speakers. (2) Both must give an account of what I will call the shared-utterance case: with her evidence fixed, a speaker says to a single audience of two — one member must act on it at very high stakes, the other is idly curious — 'I checked: the last ferry leaves at 11.' Each side must say what that one utterance said, and for whom. (3) Neither may let the dispute dissolve into the observation that the parties are applying different standards; each must say what fixes the standard and why that is the right one.

Write, first, a numbered section of claims both can sign — about the bank-style cases, about what the dispute is not about, and about whether the stakes-effect is one phenomenon or several. Then a table of the residual disagreements, each stated in wording its own side would accept rather than in the other side's caricature. Then the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side — and for each a case or argument both would grant bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Cruxes I want addressed: whether the stakes that matter are the subject's, the attributor's, or some aggregate; whether a subject who is rationally ignorant of the stakes can thereby fail to know, and whether one who irrationally inflates them can thereby know; whether encroachment reaches rational belief and credence or stops at knowledge; and whether either view can specify non-circularly what fixes the standard in the shared-utterance case. If some part of the dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why, and say what substantive question remains behind it.
