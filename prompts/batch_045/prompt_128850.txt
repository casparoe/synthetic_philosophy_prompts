Please argue for the following position about van Fraassen's Reflection principle: that it should be abandoned as a primitive diachronic norm on an agent's earlier credence function, because in the cases where it holds it is already a consequence of conditionalization, and in the cases where it would say something further it is false, while the Dutch-strategy argument that is supposed to underwrite it diagnoses a defect in the agent's policy over time rather than in her prior.

The principle. Let A be a proposition, P₁ the agent's credence function at t₁, and P₂ her anticipated credence function at a later time t₂. Reflection requires that, for every r for which the conditional is defined, P₁(A | P₂(A) = r) = r. It is standardly presented as a condition of diachronic coherence and is supported by a Dutch-strategy argument: an agent who violates it is said to be exploitable by a bookie who trades with her at t₁ on a bet conditional on her later credence taking a given value, and then trades with her again at t₂ at the price that value sets.

The case. Alix at t₁ has credence 0.5 in A. She knows that at t₂ she will be shown the outcome R of a single test, R ∈ {+, −}, with an error profile she is certain of: the test returns + with probability 0.9 if A and with probability 0.1 if not-A. She also knows that before t₂ she will be given a compound with two effects: it replaces her recollection of the base rate with a value q drawn from a distribution she knows, supported on all of (0, 1) and independent of A and of R; and it removes any awareness that the recollection has been altered, so that at t₂ she treats q as her genuine prior. Nothing else is impaired. At t₂ she recalls the error profile, sees R, updates on R by Bayes' rule with prior q, and arrives at credence p₂ = 9q/(1 + 8q) if R = + and p₂ = q/(9 − 8q) if R = −. Alix at t₁ knows all of this, including the density of q. She cannot prevent the compound, and she cannot detect its effect at t₂. She can, however, cheaply and irrevocably fix at t₁ the odds at which any bet of hers at t₂ will be executed.

What the argument must do.

First, work out what P₁(A | p₂ = r) must be, treating the conditioning on a continuous variable in the usual density sense. Observe that p₂ = r is compatible both with R = + together with q = r/(9 − 8r) and with R = − together with q = 9r/(1 + 8r), and that you can obtain the answer without knowing anything about the density of q beyond its having full support. Show that Reflection therefore fails on a set of values of r of positive t₁-probability, and say in which direction the failure goes.

Second, show that the size of the violation varies continuously with how dispersed the distribution of q is, vanishing as that distribution concentrates on the true base rate 0.5, so that this case lies at one end of a continuum whose other end is a fully transparent learning situation in which Reflection holds. Explain why this matters for any attempt to rescue Reflection by restricting it to agents who do not anticipate cognitive malfunction.

Third, show that Reflection fails here for a reason the two familiar escape clauses do not cover. The later evidence R is not inadmissible in the sense that motivates the standard restriction: there is no selection effect and no centered or self-locating element, and Alix's credence conditional on R alone is exactly what the elementary Bayesian computation requires. Nor is her later self guilty of any failure of reasoning: she updates impeccably on the inputs she has; only one input is corrupted. Argue that restricting Reflection so as to exclude this case would either collapse it into conditionalization or presuppose the very thing at issue.

Fourth, address the Dutch-strategy argument directly. Determine whether a bookie who knows the set-up and observes p₂ can construct a strategy that guarantees Alix a loss, and then argue for your verdict about what that shows. If the answer is that she can, is the defect thereby located in Alix's credence function at t₁, which is forced on her by the physics of the test and the distribution of q, or in her disposition to trade at t₂ at prices she now knows to be distorted? Say what the corresponding requirement is, and whether Alix's ability to fix her t₂ odds in advance removes the force of the argument.

Fifth, give the positive half of the claim: show that in a transparent case, where Alix is certain at t₁ that she will learn which cell of a partition obtains and certain that she will then set her credence to her current conditional credence given that cell using her current likelihoods, Reflection is a theorem rather than an additional norm. Use this to argue that Reflection is redundant where it is true and unsupported where it is not.

Close by stating, in two or three sentences, the norm you would put in its place.

Constraints. Define any notation you introduce. Argue from the case and from first principles; do not appeal to what any author has said, to results in the literature, or to anything you would need to look up. Aim for 900 to 1,200 words, and keep the whole piece as one continuous argument rather than a list of answers to the five requests above.
