I never studied philosophy or economics or anything like it — I work in a warehouse and think about this stuff on the drive home. I got nerdsniped by something and I want to know whether it already has a name.

I'm starting from an argument I half-remember reading about: if someone's likes go in a circle — they'd rather have A than B, B than C, and C than A — then you can sit next to them and offer them a small payment each time to swap to whichever thing they like better, and after three swaps they're back holding what they started with and they're down three payments. Fine, I get that one. But the thing I keep noticing in real life is a version where the swaps never actually happen.

Example one. My old flatmate worked out that I'll always swap a shift with her for a small favour, and then swap back a week later, and keep going indefinitely. Instead of ever swapping, she'd just say: do me this favour or I start the swaps. So I'd do the favour, since one favour is cheaper than what the swapping would of cost me. She never swapped once in two years. I paid, and nothing happened.

Example two. A bloke at my local knows that if he asks, I'll say yes to one more drink, and then one more after that, and each yes is fine at the time but the night ends badly. He charges me a tenner to spend the evening not asking. Every Friday. He never asks. Again, I'm paying for nothing to happen at all.

So the shared shape seems to be: I'm being drained by the *threat* of a sequence, and every single step of that sequence is something I'd happily agree to at the moment it came up. Nothing in the threatened run is against my interests at the time — only the whole run is. And if I refused to pay, the run would happen and I'd lose as much or more, so each payment looks reasonable on its own, which is exactly why it can go on forever.

The things I've half-heard of don't seem to fit. The circular-drain argument isn't it, because there the trades really happen and my likes really do go round in a circle. Blackmail isn't it, because a blackmailer threatens something I *don't* want, whereas here every threatened step is one I'd say yes to. And the boiling-frog sort of thing isn't it either, because that's about failing to notice a gradual change, and I notice perfectly well — I'm paying precisely because I can see the whole thing coming.

I definately don't just want a label. I want the carving: what exactly this is, what it isn't, and where its edges are. In particular: does it need the threat to be credible, does it matter that each step is consensual, what changes if I can tie myself down in advance (delete the app, hand my wallet to someone, announce I'll never pay), and does the thing apply at all to someone whose likes don't go in a circle?

Please answer as exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no intro and no sign-off, covering in order: (1) whether this has a name or a literature of its own; (2) how it differs from the circular-drain argument; (3) how it differs from blackmail or extortion; (4) the edge cases I listed; (5) whether it shows anything about the claim that being open to this kind of exploitation means there's something wrong with you. Don't give me papers, links or a reading list — I can't get to a university library and I wouldn't know what to do with one anyway.
