I run a tiny reading group (we meet in a pub, six of us, one is a formal-verification engineer who keeps dragging in annoying real examples) and we've spent the better part of a year grinding through the logical uncertainty material — the market-based criterion where no efficiently computable trading strategy can make unbounded gains off your prices, and the Bayesian pushback on it. Two of the group have now stopped making progress and mostly just restate their side louder, so I want to hand them the document they should have written at the start: a joint adversarial-collaboration statement.

Side A, call them the coherentists: there is nothing special about logical uncertainty, there's one normative theory of uncertainty, and the only reason anyone has credence below 1 in the twin prime conjecture or in what a specific enormous computation outputs is that conditioning on all logical facts is computationally intractable; the ideal is still a single coherent distribution over completions (or models), updated by conditioning on proofs as they arrive, and synchronic incoherence is a defect because in principle it's exploitable. Side B, call them the convergence crowd: "in principle exploitable" does no work when working out the exploit requires settling the very mathematics you're uncertain about, a noncomputable prior over completions is not an ideal but a fantasy, and the right normative target is a property of the whole time-sequence of belief states — don't get exploited by any feasible trader, converge to 1 on things that get proved, learn to be calibrated about the class of questions you can eventually settle — rather than coherence at each instant.

What I want is the joint text both of them could actually sign. Start with a numbered agreed-claims section, and I'd like it to include: what the probability calculus does and doesn't constrain once the agent can't compute its own updates; whether the threat of a money pump is itself hostage to the same logical uncertainty it's supposed to punish; whether uncertainty about a bounded computation (does this SAT instance have a solution) is even the same kind of thing as uncertainty about an unbounded conjecture; what an agent may assume about its own future proofs, given the usual self-reference headaches; and whether either framework so much as has an opinion about the number you should plug into a one-shot decision today. Then a disagreements table: neutral statement of the disagreement in one column, each camp's own preferred phrasing that they'd sign in the other two. Then the cruxes, and for each crux a concrete case both would accept as evidence bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. The cases I want used are these: (1) the sign-off case — my verification engineer has to certify a numerical simulation / crypto construction this week, the relevant claim won't be settled for decades, so what does an in-the-limit criterion say about the number she acts on now, if anything; (2) the sieve case — a fast heuristic conjecture engine flags whether some hard claim is provable and a slow verifier settles it a year later; what's the right interim credence, and how much do you discount that flag, i.e. how does either view handle logical testimony from a correlated-but-fallible source; (3) settlement-lag — a prediction market on an open conjecture that has to cash out at a fixed date long before anyone proves anything; who holds the residual, and does it behave like ordinary risk or like something the two accounts have to treat differently; (4) mirror markets — run the same setup twice, seeding one copy with an extra axiom or a leaked hint that a proof exists; do the two price series converge, and would convergence (or failure to) actually discriminate between the accounts. Finally, say explicitly which parts of this dispute you think are merely verbal — e.g. if they're using "probability" to mean a fair betting price, a decision-guiding number, and a limiting frequency of proof-events respectively — and which parts are real. Don't tell me who's right; I want the text they could both put their names to, with what's left over stated exactly.
