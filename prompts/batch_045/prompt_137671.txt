I'm in a small reading group on confirmation theory — five of us, mostly philosophy grad students plus one physicist who keeps rolling his eyes — and we got stuck on the problem of old evidence weeks ago, and I've been going back and fowrth with a friend about it ever since, without either of us budging. Here's where I've ended up. Call the mundane story this: in real historical cases where already-known evidence seems to confirm a theory (everyone reaches for the anomalous advance of Mercury's perihelion and general relativity), the confirmatory force is entirely accounted for by ordinary Bayesian conditionalization on propositions the agent genuinely learns only after the theory is on the table — that the theory really does entail the known result, that it predicts the precise value rather than some vague range, that the rival candidates don't — and we never need counterfactual priors, ur-priors, logical learning, or any story on which conditionalization itself gets given up. My credence in the mundane story is about 0.55. My friend's is about 0.2, and she thinks my reasoning for it is basically circular.

The reason I'm at 0.55, briefly: I tried to do the arithmetic on a toy model. Say that before I'd ever heard of the anomaly I was at 0.3 on some theory T, 0.5 on "T entails the anomaly", the two independent, and 0.1 on "the anomaly holds for unrelated reasons". Conditioning on the anomaly then puts me near 0.7 on T; and years later, when someone actually works out that the entailment goes through, I get pushed to something like 0.81. So the part that feels like old evidence doing work is really new evidence about the derivation — call it the entailment-opacity route. My friend says the model cheats: it assumes I already had T in my hypothesis space back when I first learned the anomaly, whereas in the actual case T hadn't been formulated yet, so there was no proposition I could have been at 0.3 about, and if I appeal to what I would have said about T had I considered it, I've smuggled in exactly the counterfactual prior that was supposed to be the exotic part of the exotic story.
