I'm a software engineer with no philosophy background, and I keep noticing that institutions handle inherited wrongs in a way I can't square with what they say elsewhere. When a data breach turns out to be caused by code nobody currently at the company wrote, my employer apologizes in the first person — we let you down — and issues credits or settlements, while its lawyers argue in court that the harm is too old and the affected users have no standing. States do the same thing at larger scale: a head of government apologizes for something done decades before he was born, often for acts that were entirely legal under the law of the time; the apology is addressed to surviving victims and then, as they die off, to descendants; who counts as a descendant, and what evidence of descent suffices, is decided by the apologizing state itself; any payments are framed as a final settlement, sometimes explicitly aimed at closing off further claims, rather than as damages a court awarded; and the statement frequently adds that acknowledging the suffering is not an admission of legal liability.

What does this package presuppose? Does it require that a state or a firm is an agent that persists through a complete turnover of its members, so obligations attach to the entity and not to the people, or could a view on which only individuals are ever responsible license the same practice as a convenient fiction? Does it presuppose that duties to repair can be inherited even though guilt cannot? Is the point of the thing acknowledgment or compensation, and can a payment discharge an apology, or are those two incompatible ideas bolted together? And where the acts were legal when they were done, is the state apologizing for the suffering or for the wrongfulness? Please distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, say whether its parts are consistent with one another, and tell me what would have to change — or what would be left of the practice — if we adopted the stricter view that only the personally responsible can apologize and only the directly wronged can be owed anything.
