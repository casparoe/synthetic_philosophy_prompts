ok here's what I want: before I read any more teleosemantics — Millikan-style biosemantics, indicator theories, the whole naturalizing-content program — I want the spec I should be holding them to. Write me the desiderata: what any theory of how a mental (or neural) state gets its content has to deliver, with the case or consideration behind each one, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are just what one camp happens to care about.

The cases I keep circling: plain misrepresentation (a detector that fires on something that isn't what it's for — the error has to be an error and not just an unusual causal upshot); the proximal/distal thing (why the state is about the distal thing and not about the proximal stimulation or some disjunction of whatever reliably causes it); internal duplicates in different environments getting different contents; a creature assembled by chance with no history at all; and the one I actually care most about, a trained network whose unit fires reliably on cats, where the only history involved is a training distribution it will never see again — no evolution, no reproduction, nothing that looks like function settling. Whatever the theory says there, I want it to say it for a reason that also covers the biological cases, not as a separate story bolted on the side.

Purpose, since I guess adequacy is relative to one: I want a notion of content good enough to underwrite the way cognitive scientists actually talk — someone saying a population of neurons is for X, or misrepresents X, should be able to cash that out.

Also: flag any pair of desiderata you think can't be jointly satisfied, especially around whether normativity has to show up at all versus whether the theory is supposed to get by without it, and tell me which single requirement knocks out the most candidates. A couple of lines on how the main contenders do against the list is fine, but the criteria are the part I want.

And commit: give me a definite ranking and definite verdicts on what's really required, with reasons, not a menu of options for me to pick from. Say where you're going out on a limb and where you're on well-trodden ground, and say it plainly if you think the whole list is unsatisfiable as stated — that's a result I'd recieve happily.
