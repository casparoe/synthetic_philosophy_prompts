Write a parody of an official government leaflet — the numbered, deadpan public-information FAQ a citizen receives when a new statutory duty comes into force — announcing the launch of the National Transparency Register. In this jurisdiction every adult must file, and periodically re-file, a machine-readable "decision program" that determines how they will act in any one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma with any other citizen; the Register publishes everyone's source code and, at the start of any interaction, feeds each party the other party's current filing. The premise being satirized is the optimistic reading of program equilibrium: that once agents can read each other's decision procedures, mutual cooperation becomes available and rational. Push that premise to its institutional conclusion without ever naming it as an assumption. Around 700 words, formatted as numbered questions and answers, in the voice of a ministry that is entirely satisfied with the scheme.

The jokes should come from the actual structure of program equilibrium rather than from generic tech satire:

- Filings must halt within a fixed number of clock cycles; include the guidance offered to citizens whose programs get stuck simulating each other.
- Describe the standard model filings: unconditional cooperation, unconditional defection, and the sophisticated filing that cooperates only with parties it can prove will cooperate with it — and which therefore defects against anyone programmed to cooperate unconditionally. The leaflet should present that last behavior as the mark of a prudent, responsible citizen, and should note how well such citizens do.
- Include the self-referential trick by which such a program cooperates with an exact copy of itself, and a question from a citizen about twins, clones, or spouses who file identical programs.
- Introduce "Verified Clique" certification: filings that cooperate only with holders of the same certificate. Cover the status of citizens who decline certification, and of those who file in a form the Register cannot read — an Approved Readability Standard, compiled or minified submissions rejected, and an appeals process for citizens whose programs are unclassifiable and are therefore treated by default as defectors.
- Note that universal unconditional defection is also a fully compliant filing: the Register mandates transparency, not cooperation, and the leaflet should report this as one of the scheme's great successes, with statistics.

Keep the irony deadpan and legible. No winking, and no explicit statement of the objections — that transparency licenses exploitation as much as cooperation, that "cooperate only with provable cooperators" functions as a trust machine and a loyalty test at once, that the framework has nothing to say about which equilibrium gets selected. Those should accumulate for the reader from the guidance itself. Everything the leaflet asserts about the behavior of the programs should be technically correct, so that someone who knows this literature recognizes the satire as fair.
