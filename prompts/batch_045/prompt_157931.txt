So my uncle Ray is retiring after something like 38 years with the state fish and wildlife people, and I'm doing the toast at his party on Saturday — four or five minutes — and I'm properly stuck.

The room is going to be maybe half his work people (biologists, a couple of hunters, some guys from the conservation district) and half family, church people, and his grandkids. So I can't get technical and I can't be a downer at a party.

Here's the actual problem. I've been reading and thinking on my own about wild animal suffering for the last couple years — no class, no background, just me — and I've ended up with a way of scoring a piece of land that I genuinely believe. Basically: for an average animal there, how much of the year does it spend in a state it would take a cost to get out of — hungry, cold, hurt, sick, terrified. Bad hours, I call it. I know it's crude and I know I made it up, but it's the thing I actually think when I think about this stuff.

And it gives me verdicts on his career that don't match the room's at all. On bad hours, the winter feeding he did at his own house for years — the thing his own agency tells people not to do, which his old boss will probably tell as a funny story about him being a soft touch — is maybe the single best thing he ever did. The deer culls he ran, which the work half of the room will raise a glass to and the family half finds kind of grim, I think were fine. But the thing everyone will actually be celebrating — he spent six years and a lot of political capital stopping a housing development from going in on a big tract, and he won, and the toast is supposed to be about that — I think might have made things worse for the animals. What he protected is a place that manufactures a lot of animals and a lot of bad hours every year, more or less forever, and what would have gone in instead would have been lawns and fewer of them.

I'm obviously not going to say that out loud at his retirement party. But I also don't want to stand up there and say things I think are false, which is what the normal toast would be — "he gave the deer a future," "that land will stay wild because of him," and so on.

So: how do I write five minutes that are honest by my own lights, don't turn into a lecture or an argument with the room, land as love for a guy who, if he ever heard any of it, would think I'd gone completly around the bend, and still give the work half of the room something real? Is there a way to praise the rule-breaking and the culls and just decline to lie about the land? Or is that an impossible brief and I should swallow it and do the normal toast?
