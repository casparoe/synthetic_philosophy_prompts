hey — I run the philosophy club at my school, we're around 14 people and I've got the library for 70 minutes after school on thursdays. Last time we tried to talk about consciousness and it totally collapsed into everyone going "yeah but how do you KNOW" in a loop, and the week before we did the global workspace idea (the thing where being conscious just is your content getting broadcast to the whole system so every part can use it) and half the club walked out thinking that basically settles it. So I want them to run into the gap themselves this time rather then me standing at the front explaining it — I'd rather not say the words "access" and "phenomenal" until after we've played.

What I want them to come away seeing: that the thing the broadcast theory points at (information being available to everything — reportable, reason-guiding, action-guiding) and the thing people actually care about (there being something it's like) can come apart inside the very same machine, and that from the outside — from the transcript of what the system did — you can't tell the cases apart. Ideally they build the cases themselves round by round and only at the end notice that they've been scoring two different things as if they were one.

Design me the game. Concretely I need: the roles (I was thinking each person is a specialist module with private input — edges, motion, color, word-shape, memory of the room, interoception like hunger, a language/reporter module, an action module — but you fix it for 14, with spares), what the workspace physically is (I can get a desk lamp, and I've got poker chips from the math department, a projector, index cards, a bell, and zero budget), the bidding rules for getting your content into the light and what it costs, a scoring scheme that gives the team something and each module something and doesn't need money (candy, or picking next week's topic, whatever), and a timeline with hard round lengths so we actually get through 5 or 6 rounds in 70 minutes.

The rounds I want built in, roughly — change them if you've got better mechanics:

- one normal round where everything lines up, so the identification feels obvious
- a flicker round: a coalition wins the light, gets knocked out before it stabilises (say 3 seconds), and so never counts as broadcast even though the content did reach the workspace and did a bit of work — I want them to argue about whether that was conscious
- a masked round: one module gets a genuinely vivid private stimulus (a real colored image, a strong smell, whatever) but gets blocked before broadcast by a rule the other modules can trigger, so the reporter says "nothing happened" and the team loses the point while that player is sitting there insisting something happened
- a dark-relay round: one module's content wins, gets broadcast, drives the report AND the action — but that player has secretly been told they're dark: they hold their card face down and read the decoded line without ever getting the content themselves. Everything downstream is identical to the normal round.
- a round where the reporter is muted but broadcast still happens and the action module still has to act on it — so they can see that report is just how we probe the system, not the thing itself
- and I want to smuggle in a mechanic where a module can spend chips to boost somebody else's bid without joining the coalition, so its content changes what gets said without ever itself being broadcast. influence without availability.

Two people should be the lab notebook — they can only write down what's publicly available: what's in the light, what the reporter says, what the action module does. At the end I want to hold up their notes for the normal round and the dark-relay round and have them be identical, and then ask what's supposed to settle whether it was conscious.

Then the debrief: map each round onto the bit of the idea it breaks, tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (the loud kid hogging the bidding is my main worry, also the dark player will probably cheat), and be honest about which part the game can't actually produce and I'll have to just say out loud — I'm guessing it's the what-it's-like part itself, since nothing I do with cards and lamps manufactures that.
