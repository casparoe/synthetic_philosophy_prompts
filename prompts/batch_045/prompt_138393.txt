my group chat got into it again about rome, and I noticed we're all using the same picture without noticing: rome is a building that fell down. There's a date, there's rubble, and if you can find the bad load bearing wall you've explained the whole thing. That framing is doing a lot of work — it's what makes people assume there has to be *one* cause (barbarians, lead pipes, a nasty climate stretch, christianity, take your pick), and it's also what lets everyone slide from "rome fell" to "so we're next" in about thirty seconds flat.

Can you audit the analogy? I want the correspondences spelled out, a table is fine: if the roman state is a building, what plays the role of the foundation, the load bearing walls, the ground it hits, the collapse, the rubble, and the people still living in it afterwards. Then the disanalogies, and I want them sorted into the ones that actually matter for the two moves above and the ones that are just precision nitpicking. Some things I keep tripping on: nobody in 476 seems to have thought anything happened (the west's emperor job just quietly stops getting filled, and the east keeps going for another thousand years), there's no agreed ground level to hit, and a building doesn't get to decide it's now several buildings.

My old history teacher used to push a different one: rome is like latin. Nobody can say when latin died because it just turned into italian and french and spanish, so asking when rome fell is like asking when latin died. Audit that one too — where does it fit better than the building (continuity, no single moment, the thing survives by turning into something else) and where does it fall apart (a language has no army, collects no taxes, can't be conquered, wasn't trying to stay the same)?
