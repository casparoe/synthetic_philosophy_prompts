So the doc should be written by an engineer who is fully bought into the naturalist way of doing this: a disease is just some part of the body failing to do what parts of that type are supposed to do, statistically atypical for people of that kind, nothing about whether its bad for you, and that's the whole story. Every design decision should follow from that seriously — I want someone who works on this stuff to read it and recogise the actual position rather than a strawman of it.

The jokes should come from where the position genuinely strains, written as ordinary engineering detail: how we pick the reference class (age, sex, population — including the bits that get uncomfortable), what "normal environment" means when half our users sit all day in bad air, the fact that the most common things in our own dataset come back as not-a-disease under our own rules and the workaround we ship for that, and how "does this hurt survival and reproduction" smuggles in exactly the judging we were told not to do.

~700 words, deadpan, RFC section headings, no winking. Close with a seperate rejected-alternatives section for the other camp's design — the one where you need the dysfunction plus somebody thinking it's a bad thing — dismissed for the real reason it gets dismissed (things the manuals used to list: homosexuality, for one), with the author completely blind to the fact that his own design has the same bug one layer down.
