So i've been stuck on this for a couple of weeks and my book club was no help at all, so i thought i'd ask here.

I read that dialogue in The Mind's I a while back — the one where a guy has a conversation with Einstein's brain in a jar, and somewhere near the end the idea gets floated that the whole conversation could just as well be sitting there in a book, and then you're meant to wonder whether it's the book that's the conscious thing (or whether we're the ones inside a book, or something along those lines). I've got no philosophy background whatsoever, I've just been turning it over in my head on my own.

The objection everyone gives me is obvious and i think it's a bit of a cop-out: "a book can't actually talk to you, it's just got one conversation printed in it." Okay. So here's my fixed version. Say the book isn't a transcript of a conversation that really happened with a real person. Say instead it's like a choose-your-own-adventure blown up to an insane size: for every question anybody could possibly open with, there's a page with the reply Einstein would give, and then pages for every possible follow-up to that, and so on. Nobody ever has to physically print it — say the book just exists as a pattern, and whenever somebody walks up and says something, the right page gets found. You could keep the conversation going for as long as you liked and never tell the difference. And there's no trick with a person hiding behind a curtain: all the replies were worked out in advance by some mechanical procedure, not by a mind.

So: why do people still balk at saying the book is conscious? The way I see it there's only two options and you have to pick one. Either (1) having the right responses available, however they got there, just is what consciousness is — in which case the book is conscious, and so is any other book with that same shape, and I guess there are then as many Einstein-consciousnesses as there are copies of the pattern, or readers of it, or whatever. Or (2) the book isn't conscious because nothing is happening inside it, it's just pages getting found, and that means consciousness has to be something extra — what the thing is made of, or what's going on in it moment to moment — and once you say that I can't see how you stop short of admitting you don't really know that anybody else is conscious either.

I can't find a third option and both of those feel wrong to me, so I've started to think the actual answer is that there just isn't a fact of the matter and it depends who's asking: the book is conscious for me if I'm treating it as conscious, and that's all there is to it, same as with people. Is that right? And if it is, how do I explain to the group why the question felt so loaded while we were reading it — like something was genuinely at stake and not just a word game?

One more thing I keep flip-flopping on and i'd definatly like to know: does it make any difference whether only one copy of the book ever gets made versus a million copies, or whether it never gets printed at all and is just the pattern? I can't tell if that's a real distinction or if I've simply confused myself.
