hey — I'm in year 3 of a pol-theory PhD, finally writing the chapter where I have to say what a criterion for democratic inclusion is even supposed to do, and I've realised I keep scoring views against criteria nobody ever wrote down. So: give me the spec first, scorecard later.

Purpose, since adequacy is relative to it: I want a criterion that a constitutional draftsperson could actually turn into a rule about who gets on the electoral roll — not a principle that sounds nice at a conference. So being operable under ordinary factual uncertainty matters to me, and I suspect that's definately the requirement most of the standard views quietly flunk.

Three cases I keep testing candidates against — I stitched these together myself, they're not from the literature, so please actually reason through them instead of pattern-matching:

- the associated region: a region is inside a federation for criminal law and taxation, has its own assembly that the federation can overrule, and gets no vote in federal elections. Its residents are subject to a great deal of federal power and to nothing else that looks like membership.
- the gradient proposal: instead of in/out, give people fractional votes in each polity whose law materially shapes their lives, so one person might hold 0.6 of a vote in one system and 0.4 in another.
- the long-run decision: siting a nuclear waste repository, or setting a carbon budget — the people most affected don't exist yet, can't be coerced, and can't answer back. And in the version I care about, whether a given population is affected at all turns on a disputed causal claim: this state's emissions, that island's flooding.

Two things I'm stuck on and want you to adjudicate rather than just assume: whether a criterion has to output a clean binary in/out or is allowed to output degrees and weights (is binary a real desideratum or just a one-person-one-vote prejudice?), and whether escaping the circularity — whoever applies the criterion is itself some demos — is a genuine requirement or a philosophers scruple that no working criterion has ever met.

Format please:
1. "The list" (~600 words): every requirement, each with the case or consideration behind it, each tagged non-negotiable / preference of a particular camp / genuinely up for grabs.
2. "Where the list eats itself" (~200 words): the subsets that can't be jointly satisfied. If the whole list turns out to be unsatisfiable, say so plainly — that would be a result I'd happily report instead of a scorecard.
3. "The one that does the most damage" (~100 words): the single requirement that knocks out the most candidate criteria, and why.
4. "Scorecard" (~200 words, keep it brief): all-affected, all-subjected, and subject-to-coercion accounts, a line or two each. No names, no citations, no suggestions for further reading — I've read them and I have library access.
