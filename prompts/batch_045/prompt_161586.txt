Two members of the consciousness-science reading group I sit in on have been arguing for weeks about method, and at the last meeting they turned to me and asked me to settle it. The lab they belong to is designing a study on near-threshold vision: a stimulus is flashed at intensities at which subjects sometimes say "I saw nothing" and yet still discriminate the stimulus above chance in a forced-choice task. Their disagreement is about what the report is evidence of, and it ran as follows.

(1) Nina, defending heterophenomenology: The virtue of the method is that it does not presuppose the answer. You take everything the subject produces — what she says, her key presses, her confidence ratings, her reaction times — as a text, and from those texts you construct the subject's world: the world as she takes it to be. Then you look for third-personally stable correlates of that constructed world. At no point do you have to rule on whether the subject's avowal is true, so the method cannot be held hostage to the very thing under investigation.

(2) Marcus, defending trained descriptive interviewing: That is exactly what is wrong with it. At the point where it matters your method goes quiet. The whole question in this study is whether "I saw nothing" is correct — whether there was an experience the subject did not avow. You cannot answer that by accumulating more texts, because every channel you add is still behaviour. You have quietly swapped the explanandum: the output is a science of what subjects say, plus a correlation, and the thing we wanted — the experience — never appears in it.

(3) Nina: I do not go quiet, I go quieter, and that is the right setting. The finding here just is the divergence: the subject's constructed world contains no stimulus while her discriminative behaviour tracks it. That divergence is third-personally checkable, replicable, and it survives every subsequent change of mind about what consciousness turns out to be. Yours does not. The moment you treat the avowal as authoritative you have adopted an unvalidated measuring instrument, and one you cannot check, because you have no access to the quantity independently of it.

(4) Marcus: Every science begins with unvalidated instruments and calibrates them by triangulation, and you cannot triangulate without some assumption about what the reports are about. Here is what calibration looks like. After training in descriptive interviewing, subjects stop using one category — "I saw nothing" — and start using three: "nothing"; "something I could not identify"; "something indefinable but present". Those three track different levels of discrimination performance, stably, across subjects. That is an instrument being calibrated against an independent measure. On your view all three are just more texts, and you have no way of saying that the later vocabulary is more fine-grained than the earlier, so you cannot even describe the improvement, let alone exploit it.

(5) Nina: I can rank texts — by intersubjective stability, coherence and predictive power — so I can absorb your trained reports as better data. But note the asymmetry: you assume the trained report is nearer the facts, and you have no check on that, whereas I can detect a report going wrong third-personally. Hard case. Take patient R, who has dense amnesia for ongoing experience: while a stimulus is in front of him he describes it accurately; a minute later he has no memory of it and, asked to describe it, confabulates fluently and with total confidence. My method is untroubled: the concurrent report is good text, the retrospective one is bad text, and I can tell which is which without knowing anything about his experience. Your method is built on retrospective explicitation, so on R it manufactures confident confabulation and then calls it data.

(6) Marcus: Granted, and I would happily run concurrent reports as well — but that is a concession about timing, not about metaphysics, and you have not answered my objection. Mirror case. Take subject S: no memory deficit, full attention, and in real time she reports nothing whatsoever — no avowal, no confidence shift, no behavioural signature. Two weeks later, under the trained protocol, she reports a stable, structured and richly articulated experience of that stimulus, insists it was there all along, and can now be trained to discriminate it on demand. You have two texts and a change in behaviour. If your response is that the texts are what they are and there is nothing further to say, then your method is compatible with every possible distribution of first-personal facts, and therefore detects nothing. Calling that neutrality does not make it metaphysically innocent; it is the claim that nothing exists beyond the third-personal trace, wearing a methodological hat.

(7) Nina: Then let us have that claim in the open rather than smuggled in. There is a genuine, third-personally statable question nearby — what S would have reported at the time had she already been trained — and I will record it as data. What I deny is that this is the same question as "was the experience there at the time". The counterfactual about her earlier reporting is one thing; a further fact of the matter that outruns everything third-personally available is another, and I do not think the second has any content. You owe me an account of what would make the later report correct rather than merely later, and I do not think you can give one without either collapsing into more behaviour or appealing to a private object.

I sat there taking notes and genuinely cannot tell who is winning, and neither of them would budge. Please adjudicate move by move: for each of the seven moves say whether it strikes you as clearly right, mostly right, mostly wrong or clearly wrong, and give the reason briefly. Then: who is more right overall; whether the two hard cases are really symmetric in the way each side claims; which parts of the dispute, if any, are verbal rather than substantive; and what both parties are missing — in particular whether their disagreement is about method at all, or about what a science of consciousness is supposed to be explaining.
