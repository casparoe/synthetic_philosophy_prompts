Two colleagues have been sniping at each other for years about the Born rule in the many-worlds picture: one thinks the squared-amplitude rule falls out of rational constraints alone, once you think about an agent who knows she's about to branch; the other thinks every such derivation quietly assumes what it was meant to prove, and that the most you can do is make the rule look natural. They've finally agreed to an adversarial collaboration and I've got to knock the write-up into shape — can you draft the document?

Two stipulations so it doesn't get eaten by side issues. First, branching is exact and discrete, so nobody can escape into the preferred-basis problem. Second, and this bit is hypothetical, not something you'll have seen: suppose someone publishes a proof that any decision rule for branching agents which is invariant under fine-graining of branches has to agree with the Born rule whenever the amplitudes are rational multiples of one another, and that the argument dies completely for irrational ratios and can't be patched with a continuity assumption. Both of them accept this result. What I actually want to know is whether it settles anything between them, or whether one says "told you so" and the other just shifts the goalposts.

So: the claims both of them can sign — about the formalism, about why naive branch-counting fails, about what would have to be delivered for something to count as a derivation at all; the disagreements, each in wording it's own side would accept; the cruxes, with the case or argument both agree would bear on each one and each side's prediction written down in advance; and a straight answer on which parts of this are really a fight about words. Numbered agreed claims, then a table for the disagreements. No citations or reading lists — I want the arguments themselves, not the bibliography.
