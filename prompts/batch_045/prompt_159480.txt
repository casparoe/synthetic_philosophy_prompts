I could use some help getting ready for a talk. I should say up front that I have no academic background in any of this, so please keep everything plain and in normal words.

Bit of background on me: I work in IT procurement, and since the energy crisis a few years ago I got kind of obsessed with energy questions — reading whatever popular stuff I can find, spreadsheets people post online, arguments in comment sections, that sort of thing. A local environmental group here in Germany asked me to come and speak: about 20 minutes, then maybe 30-40 minutes of questions. The group was founded back in the 70s around the anti-nuclear protests and these days mostly does rooftop solar, bike lanes and blocking new highways. They agreed to do my talk in English because my German isnt good enough to argue in public.

What I want to argue — and I want to be clear that I am not defending a nuclear programme for Germany, only arguing against the shutdown — is basically three things:

1. Once a reactor is built and paid for, the comparison everybody reaches for (new nuclear is expensive, wind and solar are cheap per unit of electricity) is the wrong comparison. The actual choice, both when the phase-out was sped up after Fukushima and again when the last plants went off the grid a couple of years ago, was: keep running equipment you already own, or close it and fill the hole with gas for ten or twenty years while the renewables build-out happens. My claim is that the hole-filling is what did the damage, not the closing as such.

2. Neither country settles anything for anyone else. France having a fleet does not show nuclear is right for everybody, and the German exit does not show its wrong for anybody — they were different decisions because they started from different places, one already had the thing and the other was deciding whether to keep it. I want to use this to defuse both directions of "look at France / look at Germany and you have your answer."

3. My own hobby horse, which I am least sure about: shutting things down isnt automatically reversible. If a country stops building and maintaining reactors for a couple of decades it loses the suppliers, the specialist welders, the regulators and the engineers, and the option of building later quietly stops existing. So an early closure has a hidden cost that shows up in no cost comparison anyone makes. I want to know how much weight this will actually bear.
