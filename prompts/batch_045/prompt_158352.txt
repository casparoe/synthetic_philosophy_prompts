me and my friend had a massive argument last night and we need a tiebreaker. So: LLMs now apologise really convincingly, express gratitude, act put out when you interrupt them, and some designers apparently want to go further and build systems that can accept blame and 'forgive' you so that human–AI relationships go better. She says Strawson would have thought this is straight-up manipulation: you can only occupy the participant stance toward something that can actually have attitudes of its own, so any resentment or gratitude directed at a language model is self-deception. I said that misses his whole point in Freedom and Resentment — the reactive attitudes aren't adopted on the basis of a theory about freedom, they're just what happens to us when we read goodwill or ill-will in how something behaves, so they'll switch on whether or not the system can deserve them, and the real question is what we do with that. So what would Strawson actually have said, writing long before any of this existed? Is resentment toward an LLM an error of the same kind as resentment toward a doormat, or is it just the human commitment to ordinary inter-personal attitudes doing its thing and refusing to be argued away? Does the distinction between a real and a merely elicited reactive attitude even survive in his framework, or does it collapse? And would he worry that if we spend most of our waking hours in the objective attitude toward things that can never recipricate, our grip on the participant stance with actual people starts to weaken?
