So before we argue about whether there'd have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler, i want the spec: what would a theory of causation in history have to deliver to count as settling a counterfactual like that one? Give me the desiderata, each with the case or consideration behind it. A few i already know i want on the list: the theory has to separate the role from the role-filler -- "someone else would have done it" is ambiguous between a slot that would have existed anyway and a person whose particular actions nobody else reproduces -- and it has to leave room for saying Hitler was irreplaceable in one sense and dispensable in another, if that's what's true. It has to cope with the fact that we cant rerun history, and with the verdict flipping depending on how coarse-grained you make the outcome (a Nazi-ruled Germany, some other authoritarian regime, a big European war, the Holocaust). Add whatever else belongs, and say what a theory like this is even for -- are we allocating blame or trying to predict the next one? Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's preferences, flag any pair you suspect cant be jointly satisfied, and then score the great-man and structural pictures against the list in a couple of lines at the end -- the criteria are the part i actually care about.
