Two things in the past month have turned a question I had always treated as academic into one I actually have to settle. My father was declared brain-dead after a hemorrhage and kept on a ventilator for two days so that organs could be retrieved; my sister and I disagreed sharply about whether he was still alive during that time. And my partner and I have two embryos in frozen storage from an IVF cycle, and we now have to decide what to do with them, which seems to turn on whether there is a living thing in that tank.

Here is where I think I have landed:

(1) Being alive is not a property of the matter something is made of but of how that matter is organized: a thing is alive just in case it is a self-maintaining, self-producing system — an ongoing metabolism that continually rebuilds the machinery that carries it out.

(2) My father was not alive during those two days, even though his heart was beating, his blood was being oxygenated, and his cells were metabolizing.

(3) The embryos are alive. They are in liquid nitrogen with no chemical reactions going on at all, but I do not think that stops them being alive — and if it did, then what we are deciding about is not a living thing and the decision becomes a different one.

(4) Viruses are not alive. They replicate and evolve, but outside a host cell they are inert particles, and inside one the work is done by the host's machinery.

(5) A patient in a permanent vegetative state is alive. Several people in the family thought this sat badly with (2); I do not think it does.

Please audit this set for me: which of these commitments genuinely pull against each other, which only appear to, and, for each real tension, what exactly would have to give and what that would cost me elsewhere in the set. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equal, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I have no settled view about which of (1) through (5) goes. What I do not want is a general discussion of how life is vague, contested, or interest-relative — I have read that already, and it does not help me make either decision.
