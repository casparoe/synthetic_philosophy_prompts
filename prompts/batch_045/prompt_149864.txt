You are a professional philosopher of language and metaphysician working on theories of reference and the metaphysics of modality. Think the problem through carefully before you write: sketch two or three candidate scenarios, test each against the constraints below, discard the ones that fail, and present only the one that survives. I would rather have one case that actually works than three that nearly do.

What the case is for. I am writing a paper arguing that the standard Kripkean route to the necessary a posteriori — two rigid designators for one object, fixed by different descriptions, the identity discovered empirically — is not the only route, and that epistemic two-dimensionalism in particular has no account of any route that does not pass through reference-fixing descriptions. I need a case that generates the verdicts from the theories plus the facts of the story, because the standing reply to my other examples is that they are Hesperus–Phosphorus in disguise.

Specifications.

1. One paragraph, realistic and unexotic: a hospital ward, a courtroom, an auction room, a shipyard, an archive. No twin earth, no science fiction, no omniscient informants, and no modal vocabulary inside the story itself.
2. The story must contain at least two proper names introduced by bare demonstrative baptism with no accompanying sortal or identifying description: someone proposes, in effect, to call that one N, about a thing they cannot see clearly, cannot classify, and know nothing else about. No description may be available, even implicitly, to serve as a reference-fixer.
3. The speakers must nevertheless know perfectly well, when they use the names, which objects the names designate: no uncertainty about reference, no guessing, no causal chain that might have gone astray.
4. From the story there must fall out a single ordinary declarative sentence S, which you state explicitly, that is necessary if true and knowable only a posteriori. S may contain no modal operator, no actually, no indexical, no identity statement or negation of one, no natural-kind or theoretical-kind term, and no rigidified description. Its necessity must follow from the semantics of the designators plus the facts of the story, not from anything you stipulate.
5. Because of 2 and 3, the a posteriori-ness cannot be explained as ignorance of which object a name picks out, or as two descriptions turning out to be satisfied by one thing. It has to come from somewhere else: say exactly where, and give the mechanism a name.

Deliverable. The paragraph, then S, then the check. For Millian direct reference with causal-historical reference determination, and separately for epistemic two-dimensionalism, state what proposition S expresses, what modal and epistemic status each account assigns it, and identify precisely the step at which the two accounts come apart. I expect the trouble to be that a descriptionless baptism leaves the two-dimensionalist with an object-dependent primary intension, but argue it out rather than assuming my diagnosis is right. Then: which single feature of the case is doing the work; what happens to both verdicts if the baptism in 2 is replaced by an ordinary description-backed one, since that is the test of whether the feature is really load-bearing; and how a two-dimensionalist would attack the case, with your honest view of whether that attack works. If your case needs a contested essentialist premise, for instance about the necessity of origin, flag it and say how much weight it bears. No citations; I will add the references myself.
