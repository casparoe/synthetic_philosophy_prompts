I'm trying to say what privacy is, and I'd rather start from my own verdicts on cases than from the literature. My tentative principle is this: A's privacy is violated by B if and only if B acquires, or is placed in a position to acquire at will, information that is true of A, without A having licensed that acquisition in the context in which it occurs.

Now the cases. (1) Thermal imaging of my house from the street, revealing nothing but a heat signature: a violation, and a serious one, though no one entered and no one saw anything. (2) My neighbour, from her own kitchen, seeing into my uncurtained living room after dark: not a violation — tactless if she lingers, but I have no complaint. (3) The till clerk seeing what I buy: not a violation. The same purchases fed to a model that infers from them alone that I am pregnant, and a coupon arriving before I have told anyone: a violation, and to me a worse one than (1), though what is exposed is the very same items. (4) A city's cameras recording everything, the recordings sealed and destroyed unopened: I feel violated, but distinctly less than in (3), and I cannot say what the difference consists in. (5) A one-bit monitor I agreed to for a lower premium, which reports only whether today's conduct departs from my own month-long baseline: no violation, or so nearly none that I do not care. (6) A false rumour about me, widely credited: worse for me than (3), and yet not, I think, a violation of privacy. (7) A model trained on my data that answers strangers' questions about what I would do in some hypothetical, accurately, while disclosing no episode of my past: a violation — or so my gut says, which sits badly with (6).

Please test the principle clause by clause against these verdicts, telling me which clause each case is meant to earn and which clause breaks; then repair it, or replace it. I don't mind if what survives is a control account, an access account, a contextual-integrity account, or a reductionist dismantling of the notion, but I want it stated precisely enough to apply, I want its verdicts on cases I haven't listed, and I want to be told plainly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate. Two points I especially want addressed: whether the principle should be scalar, given that (4) and (5) differ for me in degree in a way my principle has no room for, and what (7) really shows, since a false belief about me is no violation while an accurate model of me is. The case I keep going back and forth on: a research group publishes only group-level statistics, but anyone holding the rest of the data set can reconstruct my row from them. And if no single principle survives, say what that shows about whether "privacy" ever named one thing at all.
