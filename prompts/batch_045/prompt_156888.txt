I am trying to predict how a friend will respond to an offer she has just received, and I care much more about the reasoning than about the answer.

Marta made her living for about a decade as a professional gambler and then as a small-stakes proprietary trader. Everyone who has dealt with her agrees that she sized every position by a single rule and never deviated from it. Here are the verdicts of hers I know about, with my reconstruction of the stakes:

(a) A bet offered daily and repeated thousands of times, even money, her probability of winning 0.55. She staked 10 per cent of her bankroll every time.
(b) A one-off she could not resize: she had to stake exactly half her bankroll at even money, her probability 0.55. Expected value was positive, plus 5 per cent of the bankroll; she declined. I read this as the verdict that shows she applies her criterion to single decisions and not only to long sequences.
(c) Even money, her probability 0.502, repeated daily. She staked 0.4 per cent of the bankroll; she did not round a negligible edge to zero.
(d) Even money with her probability only 0.45 in the offered direction. She took the other side at 10 per cent of the bankroll.
(e) Two even-money bets offered simultaneously, her probability 0.6 on each, both resolving on one and the same event so that they win together or lose together. She put 10 per cent of the bankroll on each, 20 per cent in total, rather than 20 per cent on each.
(f) A bet at three to one, risk one unit to win three, her probability 0.3. She staked 6.7 per cent of the bankroll.

What matters for my question is that every one of those verdicts comes from the years when her gambling bankroll was essentially her entire net worth: she lived off it, had no salary, no portfolio, no house. So the record pins down a great deal — the formula, the fact that it is expected logarithmic growth rather than expected value that she maximizes, the fact that she aggregates correlated exposures across simultaneous bets, the fact that she will take either side and will act on arbitrarily small edges — but, as far as I can tell, it says nothing about whether the thing whose growth she maximizes is the bankroll or everything she has.

That has now become load-bearing. She is semi-retired. Her liquid betting bankroll is two hundred thousand dollars, held in a separate account, and she still quotes stakes as percentages of it. The rest of her wealth is a single concentrated, locked-up position: equity in her former employer, locked up for three more years, which she can neither sell nor pledge nor hedge with any derivative, plus a consulting retainer that renews only if the firm's new product succeeds. She values that block at about 1.3 million dollars if the product succeeds and about 500,000 dollars if it fails. A former colleague has now offered to take either side, at even money, of up to sixty thousand dollars on whether the product succeeds: one shot, settling at the launch. Marta's own probability of success is 0.6; assume it is well calibrated, and assume the offer is genuine at those terms rather than a signal about her probability. Nothing contractual stops her from taking either direction, and sixty thousand is comfortably inside her bankroll.

Please do the following, with the arithmetic shown so that I can check it.

First, predict her verdict as a signed stake — some dollar amount on success, some dollar amount against success, or a pass — and say why.

Second, set out the rules that fit her entire track record equally well, and say what each of them predicts here. I can see at least three candidates: geometric growth applied to the betting bankroll alone, with outside wealth treated as irrelevant; geometric growth applied to total wealth with the state-by-state correlation between the bet and the locked-up block worked out explicitly; and something like an abstention rule, on which she declines to take a position on outcomes her own holdings or her own information are already implicated in, while maximizing growth as usual everywhere else. There may be a fourth I have not thought of, and if two of these collapse into each other, say so.

Third, give me your best guess with the reasons for preferring it, and tell me which of her earlier verdicts does real work in narrowing the field and which does none. The tension I cannot resolve is this: verdict (e) shows that she aggregates correlated exposures inside the bankroll, which by analogy suggests she would do the same with wealth outside it; but if she genuinely maximized the expected logarithm of everything she owns, then as things stand today she ought to stake a fifth of her entire net worth on any uncorrelated favorable bet, which is several times her bankroll, and I have no verdict of hers on anything like that.

Fourth, name the further case, or cases, that would separate whichever rules survive, and say what each predicts about it. In particular I want to know whether the question of whether the bankroll or total wealth is her unit of account can be settled by any single offer I could describe to her, and what that offer would have to look like.
