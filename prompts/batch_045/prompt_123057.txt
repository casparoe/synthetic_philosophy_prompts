Ok so context: I got roped onto the panel for a very small grant program, and we get to spend something like six researcher-years over two years, split however we want — I'd honestly rather fund three projects well than six thinly, if that changes your ordering. The remit is narrow on purpose. It's Hofstadter's picture from *I Am a Strange Loop*: the claim that a person's "I" is a pattern that isn't sealed inside one skull, that it's spread over a bunch of brains at varying doses, and that the big case he dwells on — a dead spouse's patterns still running in the living one — is just the loud version of something that's true in small amounts for everyone you've ever been close to. The panel mostly buys that something in this neighborhood is true, though we don't agree on how literally to take it. What we cannot agree on is what to do with it, and that's why I'm asking you.

The money isn't pure curiosity-driven. Renewal depends on us being able to point at practice that would change: (a) how clinicians, families and the rest of us handle grief and dementia — is the person partly still here in a way that should alter what we do, and is a brain losing its hosted patterns a second, slower death? and (b) the dead-person chatbot stuff that's now an actual product category — whether any of those systems is even a candidate for carrying a fraction of someone, or whether every one of them is a puppet wearing a voice.

So: of the open questions in this area, which five or six should we actually fund, in what order, and why? I want the reasoning, not the list. For each one: what else is blocked until it's answered (what sits downstream of it), how tractable is it in two years for someone working conceptually rather then empirically, and how much it matters for the practice angle as opposed to just being a cool puzzle. And tell me what would move things up or down the list.

Things I've been turning over — tell me which are the real cruxes and which are red herrings, and flag anything more upstream that I'm not seeing:

- Carrying capacity. Is there a budget? Does hosting a big chunk of someone else's pattern crowd out your own, or do copies just sit there like files? What happens when two people you hosted have incompatible "voices" — do they conflict, merge, vote? And does anything follow about how many people a single self can be spread across before it stops being that self?
- Lossy transmission. Everything that passes between brains arrives degraded. Is there a principled line between "a piece of her is running in me" and "I'm running a warped fan-fiction of her"? If it's lossy all the way down, does the whole picture quietly collapse into a fancy form of memorialism — a way of talking about memory, not survival?
- Measurement. Amounts here look wildly asymmetric (much more of X in Y than of Y in X), and first-person reports of "I just knew what she'd say" are totally uncalibrated. Is there any route to evidence about magnitudes — behavioural prediction tests, say, badly confounded as they'd be — or is the quantity of a person in a person permanently first-personal and unverifiable? If it's unverifiable, say so, and tell me what that does to the funding case rather than papering over it.
- The committee case. A self spread so thin across twenty people that no single carrier has enough to matter. Is that survival times twenty, or survival times zero, and is there even a coherent way to add these fractions up?
- Duties. If a real chunk of my dead mother runs on my hardware, do I owe her something I don't owe to a stranger's memory? Does the answer change if the carrier is a language model trained on her emails instead of her daughter? And do we get preservation-style obligations toward carriers — including obligations not to "tidy up" a dead person's simulated version to make it kinder?

Last thing, on how to write it: please keep visibly separate what's actually settled in this area from what's contested among people who work on it, and don't let a reductionist-ish reading sneak in as the neutral background — tell me when a priority only makes sense if you already accept some contested metaphysics (animalism, psychological-continuity views, no-self or empty-self views, four-dimensional stuff) versus when it's robust across all of them. Mark the places where you're going out on a limb beyond anything anyone has really argued, and give me a rough confidence on the ordering itself.
