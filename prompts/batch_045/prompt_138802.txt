I'm a science writer with four months and one feature-length piece on imprecise probabilities — the view that a rational agents credences are better modeled as a set of probability functions than as a single one. I cant cover the whole field, so I want the load-bearing questions, and I want your ranking defended rather than a guided tour.

From the outside it looks like three clusters: (a) what to do with a set once you have one — Γ-maximin vs maximality vs E-admissibility, and whether choosing a rule is a decision-theoretic question or really a moral one about ambiguity; (b) how to learn, including the thing where sets get wider rather than narrower when you condition on new evidence, which to an outsider definately sounds like a bug; and (c) whether the program is distinct from Bayesianism at all, since any set of priors can be rewritten as one precise prior over a space that includes which prior is correct — in which case the whole fight is about whether that rewrite is cheating.

Two things I've been chewing on that nobody seems to say straight, so tell me if they're real: first, the gap between treating the set as a parliament of precise advisors whose unanimous verdicts you aren't allowed to overrule, and treating imprecision as a primitive feature of the chances themselves — coin flips that aren't really 50/50 in any further sense — does anything downstream turn on which of those you mean? Second, everyone talks about agents, but the institutions that actually publish uncertainty, pandemic modelers, intelligence analysts, central banks, mostly report something set-shaped while never committing to a rule for acting on it: is that a scandal, or is it the honest response to questions the field hasn't settled?

So: give me three or four questions ranked by (1) how much else hangs on the answer, (2) whether someone with undergrad probability and four months can actually get to the bottom of one instead of just reporting that reasonable people disagree, and (3) which answers would change how a real institution reports or acts on uncertainty. Name explicitly the question you'd cut as philosophers talking past each other, and say what finding would make you reshuffle the list.
