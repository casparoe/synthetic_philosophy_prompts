I run a monthly internal session at the company I work for: ninety minutes, usually sixteen to twenty engineers, mostly backend and infrastructure, almost none of whom have studied philosophy. The format is roughly here is an idea from outside our field, let us poke at it, and I try to make the sessions do something rather than talk.

The next one is on the argument that qualia, the what-it-is-like properties of experience, are not a genuine subject matter but an incoherent posit: that the standard way of packaging the concept bundles four commitments, ineffable, intrinsic, private, and directly or infallibly apprehensible, which cannot all be held at once, and that once you see this the term has no well-defined extension left for philosophy to be arguing about. I want the room to feel that, and I do not want to stand at the front and explain it. If you simply ask a room of engineers whether qualia exist you get twenty minutes of people pointing out that they are having one right now, and nothing moves. So I would like an activity in which the incoherence is something participants run into while trying to build something.

The shape I had in mind, which you should feel free to replace, is to stage it as a compliance exercise. A regulator requires that any system marketed as conscious ship with an audit: a written specification of the property in question, plus a procedure a third-party auditor can run to decide, for any given system, whether that property is present. Small teams each write a spec, then swap with another team and run the other team's procedure against a fixed case list. Then the four clauses are introduced one at a time; after each, teams must revise their spec to honour the new clause and re-run the audit. My hope is that the specs degrade in a legible way and, under the clauses taken together, converge on something that certifies everything or nothing, so that the procedure becomes a no-op and the participants notice they have deleted their own subject matter.

What I would like from you:

1. A concrete design: team sizes, the artifacts and materials, the case list, the order in which the clauses are handed out, and a timeline for ninety minutes that includes setup, the rounds, and cleanup.
2. The handout text for the audit brief and for the four clauses, in wording a working engineer will treat as a specification rather than as poetry.
3. What each team must produce at each round, so that the deterioration is visible on paper and I can point at it in the debrief instead of asserting it.
4. A case list that pushes in both directions: one case where the property changes while nothing behavioural or functional does, one where behaviour and function change while the property is stipulated to stay the same, a system that reports the property alongside a functionally identical one stipulated to lack it, and at least one subject whose reports are unreliable. I also want the brief to require an error-handling section stating what happens when a report turns out to be wrong or when two auditors disagree, because I suspect that is where the infallibility clause does its damage.
5. A final step in which someone has to decide whether the client can be billed for an audit that no possible system can fail, and what the room is likely to conclude from that.
6. The debrief: which moment in the exercise maps onto which part of the argument, how I get from our spec became vacuous to a claim about the concept, and how to handle the reply I expect from the sharpest person in the room, that of course no third-person procedure could capture the property, since that is exactly what privacy and ineffability assert. I take that reply to be the crux and I do not know how to meet it without sounding as though I am denying that anyone has experiences. Include how I get them to name which of the four clauses they would give up and what giving it up costs them.
7. What will predictably go wrong. My guesses are that the session collapses into a debate about AI consciousness, that someone smuggles behavioural criteria back in after being told the property is intrinsic, and that the first round eats the hour. Tell me the rest, and how to design around each.
8. An honest note on what cannot be delivered by any arrangement of rules and must be supplied by me in words.

Two constraints. The room has none of the vocabulary, so either avoid the technical terms or define them inline. And I have no journal access from here, so please build the session on the structure of the claims themselves rather than on any particular paper, author, or doctrine I could not check; no reading lists or citations. If anything in my description above misstates the position, say so first, and then design around the corrected version.
