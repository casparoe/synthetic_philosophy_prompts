I keep tying myself in knots about embedded agency and I'd really like someone to just explain it rather than point me at papers. Here's roughly where I'm stuck.

In the normal, un-embedded setup I'm fine with: there's an agent, a prior over environments, actions are the agent's to choose, the world is exogeneous, and you get regret bounds and all that conditional on the true environment actually being in your hypothesis class. Then people say "now embed it" and everything is meant to break, and I think there are several *different* things getting bundled together under slogans like "you can't fully model yourself":

- the size thing: the agent is smaller than the world, and its own future computations are part of the world, so it can't fit a complete model of the world inside itself — any self-model has to be lossy;
- the self-reference thing: predicting your own output seems to require already having computed it, and then there's the Löb-flavoured result where an agent that trusts its own proofs apparently can't prove "if I do X then X is safe" and conclude X is safe;
- the Vingean thing: a successor that's more capable than you isn't reliably predictable by you, and may have reasons to *look* like it's playing nice;
- the realizability / grain-of-truth thing: once the environment contains you, the true environment isn't in your hypothesis class at all, so the usual bounds don't kick in.

What I want is to understand what each of these is really saying, exactly which assumption of the clean picture each one breaks, and how they relate. Are any of them secretly the same claim wearing different clothes? Does one entail another? And is the size argument actually load-bearing, or is it a bad pop-science version of the grain-of-truth problem? One guy in my reading group insists it's basically empty — he says a phone can hold a photo of itself, so obviously a system can contain a model of itself, end of story — and I can't put my finger on why that feels like a dodge.

Then the thing I most need sorted: the group's proposed fix is that the agent reasons about a deliberately simplified *surrogate* of itself, something small enough to actually fit in its own head, proves the properties it wants about the surrogate, and then transfers them back to itself. They keep comparing it to a compiler bootstrapping. Does that work? Where does it break — at the transfer step, because the surrogate is lossy in exactly the ways that matter? Is it just the size problem pushed down one level? And if there *is* a version that genuinely works, what would have to be true of the system for it to?
