I write about science and ideas for a living, and I'm finishing a long piece on what people mean when they call a kind *socially constructed*. Before I commit to a position in print I'd like you to audit the package of claims I've ended up holding, because I can't locate the weak joint myself. Please rank whatever you find by severity rather than flagging everything as equally bad.

1. Construction is a claim about what a kind depends on, not about whether it is real or optional. Money is my working model: nothing about a disc of metal or a row in a database makes it money, and yet money is as real and as causally forceful as anything. I'd add that constructed kinds can fail — a currency can stop being money while every holder still believes in it — so *real* here does not mean stable, rational, or self-sustaining.
2. A construction claim has to be filled in: constructed out of what, held in place by what. *It is caused by people* is vacuous. If you cannot say which practices sustain the kind and at what point they would stop sustaining it, you have not said anything with content.
3. Race is constructed out of practices of classifying and subordinating, and since we have no good use for a classification whose content just is that history, we have a standing reason to retire it — in medicine, in official statistics, in ordinary description — except as a stopgap for things we cannot yet measure directly.
4. Gender is constructed too, but not by imposition alone: a person's own say-so about their gender is constitutive, not merely excellent evidence. There is no further fact of the matter that a sincere and informed self-ascription could get wrong.
5. I also want to report sympathetically, and without condescension, what trans people very often say about themselves: that they knew from early childhood, that this was something found rather than chosen, something internal that social pressure opposed rather than produced. I do not think this self-description is confused.
6. The sex/gender distinction is itself a mid-century artifact of a particular research program rather than a discovery, and which bodily features we treat as sex-defining is itself a choice — so sex is not the unconstructed bedrock against which gender's constructedness gets measured.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look like it and why the appearance dissolves, and — for each real tension — what exactly would have to give. In weighing the costs, note that (5) is a reportorial commitment as much as a philosophical one, so treat it as expensive to drop. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and what I would be unable to say in the piece if I made it.
