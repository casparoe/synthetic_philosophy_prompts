Hey — I run a lunch-and-learn thing at work every other Friday, audience is backend engineers (Go and Rust mostly). Most of them did a first-year linear algebra course and remeber roughly what a vector is and that you square amplitudes to get probabilities; that's the ceiling. Next session is mine and I volunteered to do "is the wavefunction real?" because I got nerdsniped by it on a podcast and now I actually have to deliver. 40 minutes, whiteboard, no slides.

Don't explain the topic to me — I need a plan for how to explain it.

What I care about:

The example I keep reaching for is a compression analogy: the wavefunction as a lossy sketch of some underlying state (a minhash, a bloom filter, a hash of a big object), where two different sketches can both be consistent with the same underlying data. My worry is that this either smuggles in the answer or breaks somewhere I can't see. So: is that the right thing to build the session on, what does it have to have in it, and where exactly does it stop matching the real case? (I think the fancy words are psi-ontic and psi-epistemic, if that helps.)

I also want to sort people's instincts into three bins early, by show of hands: (a) the wavefunction is a thing that's out there, (b) it's information about a thing that's out there, (c) it's neither a thing nor information but more like a law or a rule. Is (c) genuinely its own bin or does it quietly collapse into (a) or (b)? I like it but I might be making it up.

Wether these are the misconceptions to expect, and where in the sequence to kill each: one guy will say Bell's theorem already settled it (nonlocality and so on); one person has read a bit of QBism and will say it's all just personal belief; someone will assume that if it's "real" then it's a wave in ordinary 3D space; someone will bring up decoherence as if that ends the discussion. Tell me if I'm missing the big one.

Also: what should I flat out refuse to explain at this level, and say out loud that I'm refusing it? And one question to end on that shows me they got the distinction instead of just the vocabulary — I'd rather they leave able to say why epistemic isn't the same as subjective than able to name any view.
