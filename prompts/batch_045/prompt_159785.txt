I'm a novelist and I need something translated, not explained. Four novels and a story collection, I've taught maybe a dozen workshops, and the vocabulary I actually think in is craft vocabulary: point of view, free indirect style, focalization, psychic distance, scene versus summary, unreliable narration, whose chapter this is, backstory, motivation, the implied author, show versus tell. I'm fluent in that and basically illiterate everywhere else — I lasted about three weeks in a philosophy survey course before dropping it.

I got into an argument at a dinner party about whether sentience comes in degrees and whether moral status gradates along with it, the thermostat-to-human scale, and I keep feeling like I half get it in my own terms and then it slides away. So:

Take the view that sentience — and the moral status people hang on it — is a matter of degree rather than all-or-nothing, and restate it in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can. Do the same for the rival view that it switches on at some threshold and is binary below it. Then give me the residue report.

What I specifically want:

- One creature at a time — a thermostat; a housefly or an ant; a dog; an adult human — and for each, the degree of narrative access my vocabulary would naturally give it, plus whether that mapping is honest or whether I'd be smuggling something in and calling it translation.
- There seem to be two different things getting run together in this debate: how much can be at stake for a creature, and how much what is at stake for it counts. Tell me which of those my vocabulary has terms for. My guess is I can say how big the scene is but not how much the creature counts, because for me weight is always weight for a reader.
- A suspicion I want tested: that the axis really doing the work isn't how bright or rich the moment-to-moment experience is, but how temporally thick the creature's stake is — how far into its past and future its concern reaches — so that what separates the fly from the dog isn't a dimmer light inside but that the dog has something like a plot while the fly only has scenes. Is that distinction real, have I just reinvented something that already has a name, and if it is real, where does it stop lining up with what the philosophy people are measuring?
- The residue, in both directions. What in this debate has no counterpart in craft vocabulary at all? My two guesses: that everything I have is observer-relative, since a standpoint is something a narrator grants for a reader, so I have no way to say the creature has one whether or not anyone renders it; and that my toolkit is asymmetric, since I'm trained not to hand interiority to things that don't have it but there's no craft rule at all against withholding it from something that does, which is precisely the direction the moral question cares about. And the other way round: is there some distinction I use every day that this debate is missing and should be embarassed by? The one I keep circling is that in my trade there is all the difference in the world between nothing being there and something being there with the narration unreliable about it.

Don't send me a reading list, I definately won't read it, and don't start from scratch on what sentience is — start from my words.
