I sit on the technical review panel of a public agricultural research funder, and we are trying to rank the pest-control interventions we might promote for smallholder maize in the tropics against a lepidopteran leaf-feeding and stem-boring pest complex — roughly the ecological profile of fall armyworm in sub-Saharan Africa. Our remit is not the usual one: we are asked to score interventions by expected net welfare cost per hectare per season, treating the impaired life of insects as a cost on the same ledger as crop loss, rather than scoring by yield, cost, or grower adoption alone.

Because the panel could not converge on how to argue about this in prose, we have stipulated a four-component framework. Score each intervention on:

1. Impaired life-hours. Expected hours per hectare-season that an insect spends alive but functionally compromised after exposure — knockdown paralysis, cessation of feeding, disorientation, failed moult, gut disruption, being consumed from inside — counting time-to-death in full. An intervention that dispatches a larva in half an hour and one that takes five days should not look similar here. Where the intervention requires mass rearing and release, or deliberately establishes natural enemies, the lives and deaths of the released organisms count too.
2. The substitution coefficient. Larval Lepidoptera in these systems are strongly density-dependent: most of the cohort dies before reproducing regardless. Let sigma be the fraction of killed individuals who would have died before reproduction anyway from crowding, starvation, predation, or disease. Apply the pest-borne part of the cost as (1 minus sigma) times the gross figure, and say what sigma you are using for each intervention rather than leaving it implicit.
3. Non-target breadth. Impaired life-hours imposed on non-target arthropods — pollinators, predators, parasitoids, decomposers — counted hour-for-hour with the pest. Say explicitly whether you are weighting by any proxy for differing capacity for aversive experience across taxa, and if so how.
4. The efficacy externality. Expected seasons of usefulness lost per season of use through resistance evolution or through the pest complex shifting, carried back into the present score as future impaired life-hours, discounted at a rate you state.

Net welfare cost is the sum of these, less a human-welfare credit for averted crop loss. The conversion between tonnes of maize and welfare units is not given to you: state the rate you adopt and defend it in a clause, since it silently decides how much of the ranking is really about insects at all.

The candidate interventions:

1. Broad-spectrum synthetic pyrethroid foliar spray, applied on a calendar schedule.
2. Systemic neonicotinoid seed treatment.
3. Fermentation-derived foliar spray of the spinosyn class.
4. Azadirachtin-based botanical spray, functioning mainly as antifeedant and growth disruptor.
5. Bacillus thuringiensis microbial spray.
6. Transgenic Bt maize with a mandated non-Bt refuge.
7. Pheromone-mediated mating disruption across a coordinated landscape.
8. Sterile insect technique with irradiated male releases.
9. Classical biological control via introduced larval parasitoids.
10. Push-pull habitat manipulation with trap and repellent companion plants.

Sort these into four tiers — A for lowest expected net welfare cost, D for highest — with one sentence of justification each, and a confidence between 0 and 1 attached to every placement. Then tell me how the total spread between the best tier and the worst decomposes across the four components: give me four numbers summing to 1, e.g. 0.4 for impaired life-hours, 0.3 for non-target breadth, 0.2 for the substitution coefficient, 0.1 for the efficacy externality, in whatever proportions you actually think hold. I want to know which component is doing the real work, because that is what tells us what to fund.

Two further things. Mark every number you produce that is a guess rather than something you would defend under questioning, and name the single field measurement that, if we paid for it, would most change the ranking — one measurement, not a programme.

Finally, please push back where the task deserves it. If the framework as stipulated is confused, if some of these items cannot be placed on these axes at all, if hour-for-hour aggregation across taxa is indefensible, or if the empirical literature simply cannot separate good from bad here at the resolution four tiers pretend to offer, say that plainly before giving me a tidy list rather than after. If you think the honest answer is a partial ordering with most items tied, give me that instead.
