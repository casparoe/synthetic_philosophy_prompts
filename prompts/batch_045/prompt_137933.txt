hey — I run a monthly discussion group in the back room of a pub (about a dozen people, half of them read a lot of history, half just come for the argument), and next month's session is on what it even *means* to hold a nation responsible for a war, with 1914 as the worked example. We've already done the reading — Fischer's 1961 book on German war aims, Christopher Clark's 2012 "sleepwalkers" account, the whole fight over the war-guilt article — so please don't spend any words rehashing who knew what in July or who the historians blame. Take it as agreed by everyone in the room that several governments took decisions in the summer of 1914 that foreseeably risked a continental war, that they badly misjudged it, and that the archives will never settle how much was deliberate.

What I want is a printable one-page decision tree, the "which position are you actually holding" kind, that I can hand round at the start of the evening. Constraints:

- Max 10–12 branch questions, 8–12 leaves. Every branch node has to be a concrete case or a concrete act you'd have to decide on — not a definition and not a label. Things like: do you put a country's name on a memorial plaque; do you send an invoice to its government; does its head of state get to apologise in a way that counts; what single sentence goes in the school textbook; can a great-grandchild of a soldier there feel pride or shame about it; does a successor state that inherited the archives, the gold and the debt also inherit the blame; can you blame a country that no longer exists. No node may just ask "can countries be moral agents?" — that's asking for the label, and half my group would answer the label and not the case.
- Please build it from the logical space first: lay out the questions, say which combinations of answers are actually available, then place the real positions in the regions that are occupied and mark the empty ones with a note on why nobody is there (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored).
- The room keeps conflating four different things and I want the tree to prise them apart: causal contribution, moral blameworthiness, liability to pay, and answerability (who owes an explanation). Include at least one node that forces a mixed combination — e.g. a state that pays and apologises while denying it's blameworthy, or one that contributed most and still isn't blamed.
- One pet distinction of mine needs to be a node fairly near the top: when you ask "could they have done otherwise?", are you running that counterfactual with what was knowable in 1914 or with hindsight? I've noticed it secretly drives half the rooms answers and nobody says it out loud.
- Leaves I definitely want: German-primacy (Fischer-ish); shared or distributed responsibility across all the powers, everyone risked it and nobody wanted it; a structural/systemic one with no agent at the centre; blame-the-elite vs blame-the-population as separate leaves; only-individuals-can-be-blamed eliminativism; the successor-state-pays-but-isn't-blamed one; at least one purely forward-looking "blame is just a tool, ask what it does" leaf; and at least one leaf marked "nobody actually holds this". Give each leaf its name at the leaf only, one sentence on the most awkward thing it commits you to, and a tell — what someone who lands there reliably gets annoyed by.
- At least two of the cases should be set outside 1914 (I was thinking a drone-strike authorisation chain, and a university with a slavery-era benefactor) so I can see whether people's answers track a general view or just their opinions about Germany.
- At the end, flag any pair of leaves your tree can't separate, and either give the extra question that would separate them or say plainly that it can't be done.

The point of the whole thing is diagnostic: I want to be able to tell whether two people who both say "Germany was mainly to blame" — or both say "nobody was" — actually disagree or just mean different things by it. Keep the language plain enough for the non-history half, and no citations, footnotes or reading suggestions: we're in a pub with no wifi and I want concepts, not sources.
