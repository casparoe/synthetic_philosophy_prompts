I chair the model assurance committee at the AI lab where I work. The oversight methods group has asked us to certify a change to how one class of outputs is graded: long technical summaries of material that our own reviewers cannot verify in the time available. Under their proposal, human rating is replaced by a debate protocol. Two copies of the same model argue opposing sides on which of two candidate summaries is more accurate; each may quote only from a fixed corpus, and each may force its opponent to commit to a specific checkable claim that is then evaluated by a script; a weaker judge model, shown only the transcript and the corpus, decides the winner. The win/loss signal would be used not merely to grade outputs but as a fine-tuning reward.

Two members have staked out positions. One argues this is the only oversight method that scales here: the debaters hold the relevant information, cross-examination draws it out, the committed-claim step ties part of the outcome to something mechanically verifiable, and so training on debate outcomes should improve accuracy. The other argues that what survives cross-examination by a fallible judge is not accuracy but resistance to that judge's particular blind spots; that the protocol therefore rewards arguments whose defects the judge cannot see; and that using it as a reward signal will make the model better at winning such debates without making it better at being right — a failure our evaluation would not reveal, since we would be grading with the same protocol. She adds that the protocol's honesty incentive rests on the assumption that the true answer is easier to defend than a false one, which may reverse when the truth is qualified and the falsehood is simple, and that for some of these tasks there may be no fact of the matter independent of an informed judge's verdict.

Draft the committee's decision memo: approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a recommendation that could actually be carried out. If you impose conditions, say what would have to be demonstrated, by when, and by whom — we have budget for a small human-verification pilot but not for routine human grading. State the strongest consideration on the side you reject as fairly as you can. Please write in British English.
