I have been collecting verdicts from a friend who works on the metaphysics of science, and I want to work out which principle she is running before I put a new batch of cases to her next week. She has strong intuitions about finks and masks, has thought about them for years rather than answering off the cuff, and has been consistent across the following. Please do not give me a reading list or tell me which way the professional literature comes down; I want the rule she seems to be applying, stated precisely enough that its predictions can be read off, and then her verdicts on the new cases.

Her verdicts so far:

1. A mains wire that is live at t but is wired through a device that would cut its supply the instant anything conductive touched it: still disposed at t to conduct.
2. A vase of ordinary brittleness sealed inside a vibration-damping field that would absorb any impact before a crack could propagate: still fragile.
3. A wire that is not live at t but is wired to a device that would energise it the instant it was touched: not disposed to conduct.
4. A granite pebble sitting in a chamber where a hidden apparatus would dissolve it the instant it were immersed in water: not soluble.
5. A box of matches left out overnight in the rain and still sodden: not disposed to light when struck.
6. A vase of ordinary brittleness at the bottom of the Mariana Trench, where nothing will ever strike it: still fragile.
7. A sugar cube each of whose crystals is individually encapsulated in a monomolecular polymer film that water cannot penetrate: still soluble in water.
8. A vase of ordinary brittleness inside a damping field whose independent power supply fails on one strike in four: still fragile. She added that when it does shatter, it is the brittleness and not the power failure that does the explanatory work.

New cases, on none of which she has committed herself:

A. Chance fink. A wire that is live at t, wired through a cutout which, on contact, fires with objective chance 0.9 and de-energises the wire before any current flows. Is it disposed at t to conduct?
B. Chance reverse fink. A wire that is not live at t, wired to a device which on contact energises it with objective chance 0.9. Is it disposed at t to conduct?
C. The annealing glass. A material with the room-temperature microstructure of ordinary soda-lime glass, in which the nucleation of a crack triggers an electronic rearrangement of the surrounding network that heals the crack within nanoseconds; the response is intrinsic to the material, has never been observed to fail, and occurs at every temperature, pressure and impact geometry anyone has tested. Is a vase made of it fragile?
D. The thermostatic cutout. A wire that is live at t and wired through a cutout that fires if and only if the ambient temperature exceeds twenty degrees; the room is at twenty-two. Is it disposed at t to conduct?

What I would like from you:

1. The two or three principles that best fit verdicts 1 through 8, each stated so that its prediction in a given case is computable, including whatever supplementary clause each needs in order to accommodate the reverse-fink, mimic and wet-match verdicts.
2. What each principle predicts about A through D, and where two of them come apart.
3. Your best guess at her answer to each of A through D, with the reason for preferring the principle you are relying on.
4. Which of 1 through 8 is load-bearing in narrowing the field, and which is consistent with almost anything and so doing no work.
5. One further case you would put to her that would separate the principles that survive, together with what each of her possible answers would show. If two of them genuinely agree on A through D, say so rather than manufacturing a difference, and if one of A through D is not a real discriminator, say which and why.
