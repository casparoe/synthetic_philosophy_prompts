I work in survey sampling and causal inference, and I am trying to get a philosophical dispute into a form I can actually evaluate. The dispute concerns Sleeping Beauty's credence, and I have a translation of it in mind that I do not fully trust.

The protocol, stated precisely. On Sunday a fair coin is tossed. If it lands heads, Beauty is awakened once, on Monday. If it lands tails, she is awakened on Monday and again on Tuesday. Each awakening is followed by the same question — what is your credence that the coin landed heads? — and each is followed by a memory erasure that leaves her unable to tell whether any earlier awakening occurred or what day it is. One family of answers says one half; another says one third.

My provisional translation is that this is a disagreement about sampling design. On the first reading, her present epistemic situation is a draw from the set of experimental runs, with the awakenings a deterministic consequence of the draw; the fact that she is awake at all has probability one under both hypotheses, the likelihood ratio is one, and there is nothing to update on. On the second reading, her present situation is a unit drawn from the set of awakening-events, tails runs contribute twice as many units, the sample is therefore size-biased, and the run-level quantity has to be recovered by inverse-probability weighting. What I want to know is whether that is an explanation of the dispute or merely a redescription of it.

Please do the following.

1. Write out, as explicitly as you can, the probabilistic model each position is committed to, in the vocabulary I use: sample space, random variables, sampling design, inclusion probabilities, weighting scheme, and — separately — the estimand. For each position, state whether the target of Beauty's credence is a per-run probability, a per-awakening relative frequency, or something else, and be explicit about whether the two positions are estimating the same quantity at all.

2. Give me the residue report. Which parts of each position translate cleanly? Which translate only under distortion or renaming? Which cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all? Three places concern me especially, though add others if they matter: whether the evidence that one is awake at present can be given a likelihood at all, given that neither the present time nor the identity of the subject occupying it is a random variable in any ordinary model; whether the frequency a credence is supposed to match is design-relative, so that no appeal to long-run frequencies can settle the dispute; and whether the requirement that Beauty return the same answer at every awakening — and that this be the answer of a single agent extended across person-stages — is the sort of demand my framework has a place for. If exchangeability has anything to say about whether the present awakening is exchangeable with the others, say so.

3. Assess two test cases of my own. In the first, heads produces one Monday awakening while tails produces one Monday awakening plus two Tuesday awakenings, the two Tuesday awakenings being indistinguishable from each other. As far as I can work it out, the size-biased reading then yields one quarter for heads unconditionally and one half conditional on being told it is Monday, while the run-level reading yields one half unconditionally and three quarters conditional on Monday. Check that arithmetic and tell me what it shows: which position generalizes mechanically, and which has to take on a further commitment in order to answer at all — that is, which of them is a rule rather than a verdict about the original case. In the second case, hold the original protocol fixed and vary only the scoring. Under design A every awakening is scored with a strictly proper scoring rule. Under design B exactly one awakening per run is scored, selected uniformly at random from the awakenings in that run, so that a tails awakening has inclusion probability one half while a heads awakening has inclusion probability one. Over many runs the fraction of scored reports coming from heads runs tends to one third under A and to one half under B, and inverse-probability weighting recovers the run-level fraction from either. Does that defuse the argument from calibration to a particular credence, or does the choice between A and B turn out to carry the whole philosophical weight?

4. Give me a verdict on the residue. Does it point to a gap in my vocabulary — a notion of de se or indexical content, a normative account of which design fixes the estimand, a notion of synchronic coherence across the stages of one person — or does it suggest that the dispute is less substantive than it looks, so that once the designs are made explicit it becomes a disagreement about which estimand or which loss function is the right one, and hence a normative or partly verbal disagreement rather than one about evidence? I should say that I find the size-biased-sampling reading genuinely persuasive, and I suspect that the no-update argument quietly assumes a run-level design; test that suspicion rather than flatter it, and tell me where my own reading breaks down.

One constraint: keep the translation honest. Do not present the assumptions that all uncertainty is uncertainty about which element of a fixed sample space obtains, or that all rational updating is conditioning on a likelihood, as if they were neutral translation; flag them when you rely on them, and say what either position would lose if it accepted them.
