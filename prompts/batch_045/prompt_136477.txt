I host a podcast on the history of ethics, and next month's guest is a philosopher who has published a book-length defence of a Benthamite sexual ethics, extending Bentham's own arguments for decriminalising same-sex acts into a general theory. Her stated commitments are: that the only currency is pleasure and pain, measured by intensity, duration, certainty, propinquity, fecundity and purity and never ranked by kind, so that no sexual act can be called degrading or unworthy in itself; that disgust, tradition, or the charge of unnaturalness can never be a reason to punish, since on her reading of Bentham grounding morality in sentiment rather than consequences is no moral theory at all; and that punishment is itself an evil, admissible only where it averts greater pain.

Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate these. I want at least one each on: an act performed on a sleeping or anaesthetised person who suffers no pain and never learns of it, and whether a purely pain-based theory can condemn it at all; the bootstrapping worry that if my distress at knowing what others do is itself a pain, then punishing them is licensed by exactly the sentiment she says carries no independent weight, together with a follow-up pressing her for a test that separates genuine alarm from moralised disgust; what work consent is really doing, given that for Bentham it is not a primitive moral power but matters through security and frustrated expectation, with a follow-up keyed to her likely reply; whether the pain of frustrated appetite generates any positive obligation on the state to facilitate its relief, and what she says when the cheapest reliable relief is itself offensive to many; and what an age threshold could possibly rest on if nothing turns on the kind of pleasure, only on its quantity. Close with a question about what sort of case would make her abandon the framework.

For each question, add a note on the reply she is most likely to give and the one follow-up that would make that reply costly. Also mark, question by question, where she is reporting Bentham and where she is revising or going beyond him, and separate the points on which historians of utilitarianism would agree from the ones that are genuinely contested or for which the literature has no settled answer; I do not want contested readings presented as consensus.
