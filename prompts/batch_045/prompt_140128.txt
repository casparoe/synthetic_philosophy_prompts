hey — I'm a junior and I run the ethics club at my school (its like six people and one of them only shows up for the snacks lol). We got into a huge argument in the group chat that started from something really dumb: someone said no amount of people enjoying ice cream could ever make up for one person being tortured, and someone else said yeah, but you'd still eat the ice cream, so what does "no amount" even mean? And then it slid into whether there's some point where pain just stops being tradeable for pleasure at all, or whether everything is secretly on one scale and we just feel weird saying so. Nobody could tell if we were disagreeing about facts, about math, or about words, which is why I want to build something structured instead of arguing in circles.

So: for the club zine I want a sorting quiz / flowchart — call it 8 to 10 questions, each one a small concrete scenario with two or three answer options, that takes you from "pain is bad and pleasure is good" to a named position, plus a little scoring key at the end. The thing I care most about is that the questions do the actual work: no question should be "do you think suffering matters more than happiness?", and from reading a question you shouldn't be able to tell which box it's trying to push you into. Please build the tree out of the distinctions first and only then drop the real views into the space it makes, rather than starting from a list of names. Distinctions I definately want to be load-bearing: whether pain and pleasure really are one scale with a cliff in it, versus not really the same kind of thing at all; whether the cliff, if there is one, is about how bad a single experience is at its worst moment or about how much suffering piles up across someone's whole life; whether it only runs one direction (bad stuff lexically beating any amount of mild good) or also the other (some peak of bliss that beats any amount of mild bad); whether you're allowed to add up a billion different people's tiny pleasures to get over the cliff or whether the cliff is per person; and whether it changes anything if the pleasures go to people who already exist versus the alternative being that those people never exist. Please keep the scenarios things a 17-year-old could picture — dentist appointments, migraines, bad breakups, lying in the sun — not thought experiments with invented machines.

The leaves I want on there: plain total utilitarianism, prioritarianism, negative utilitarianism, the version of negative utilitarianism with a lexical threshold built into it, and whatever you'd call the view where mild pleasant and mild unpleasant experiences trade off normally against each other but there's some intensity past which the trade just stops. If your tree opens up combinations of answers that nobody actually defends, keep them as empty cells and tell me whether that's because the combination is incoherent or just unappealing, and flag any two leaves your questions can't really pull apart instead of pretending the tree separates them.
