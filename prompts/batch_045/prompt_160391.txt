**Problem 3 (20 marks).** Judy Benjamin is dropped by parachute into an area divided into Red and Blue territory, each of which is subdivided into a Headquarters (HQ) area and a Second Company (2nd Co) area — so four quadrants: RH, R2, BH, B2. Her prior is: P(Red) = P(Blue) = 1/2; within Red territory, P(HQ | Red) = 4/5; within Blue territory, P(HQ | Blue) = 2/3. Throughout, Judy updates by minimising relative information, i.e. by choosing the posterior that minimises Kullback–Leibler divergence from her prior subject to whatever constraint the new evidence imposes.

(a) [6 marks] Her radio operator, whom she has learned not to trust completely, transmits that she is in Red territory. Judy takes this message to settle her new probability for Red territory at exactly 3/4, and to convey nothing else. Give her posterior over the four quadrants, showing your work. Explain the relation between what you just did and Jeffrey conditioning on the partition {Red, Blue}, and state the condition under which Jeffrey conditioning is the correct rule here.

(b) [7 marks] A second, independent transmission (different operator) tells her that half of the drops into Red territory land in the HQ area — i.e. it fixes P(HQ | Red) = 1/2, and says nothing whatever about whether she is in Red territory. Compute the minimum-relative-information posterior obtained from the original prior. Does Judy's probability that she is in Red territory change? If so, why, given that the message appears to concern only what is going on inside Red territory?

(c) [7 marks] Now suppose she receives both messages and updates sequentially, one after the other, minimising relative information at each step. Does the order in which she takes them matter? Compute both orders, compare them with the result of imposing both constraints at once, and say in a sentence or two what this shows about updating on conditional information. (For contrast, you may also note briefly how this differs from conditioning on the material conditional Red ⊃ HQ.)

---

**Prior.** P(RH) = 1/2 × 4/5 = 2/5, P(R2) = 1/10, P(BH) = 1/2 × 2/3 = 1/3, P(B2) = 1/6.

**(a)** Minimising D_KL(p‖π) subject to p(RH) + p(R2) = 3/4: the solution is p_i ∝ π_i·e^{λ·1[i ∈ Red]}, which multiplies the two Red cells by a common factor and the two Blue cells by a common factor, so the odds within each block are preserved. That gives 3/5, 3/20, 1/6, 1/12. This is exactly what Jeffrey conditioning on {Red, Blue} with new marginals (3/4, 1/4) gives, because minimum relative information and Jeffrey conditioning coincide whenever the constraint fixes the probabilities of the cells of a partition. Jeffrey conditioning is the right rule here because the rigidity condition is satisfied: Judy's experience bears only on how confident she is of Red vs Blue, so the conditional probability of each cell of the partition given each hypothesis is left unchanged — P(Red | HQ) stays at 6/11 and P(Blue | HQ) stays at 5/11.

**(b)** The constraint P(HQ | Red) = 1/2 is linear in the cell probabilities, since with only two Red cells it amounts to p(RH) − p(R2) = 0. So the same exponential-tilting solution applies: p_i ∝ π_i·e^{λ a_i} with a = (1, −1, 0, 0). The constraint gives (2/5)e^{λ} = (1/10)e^{−λ}, so e^{2λ} = 1/4 and e^{λ} = 1/2, giving unnormalised cells 1/5, 1/5, 1/3, 1/6. These sum to 9/10 rather than 1, so after renormalising: 2/9, 2/9, 10/27, 5/27. So yes, P(Red) falls, from 1/2 to 4/9. The reason is that the tilt drags mass out of RH into R2, and the normalising constant does the rest: min-KL keeps the Blue odds at 2:1, but it has no way of holding the total mass on Red fixed while the two Red cells are being pulled towards each other, so some mass leaks across the Red/Blue boundary. Note this is different from conditioning on the material conditional Red ⊃ HQ, which would give 4/9, 0, 10/27, 5/27 — same marginal for Red, but all of Red's mass sitting on HQ.

**(c)** Both constraints are linear and mutually compatible, so the projection is order-independent: whichever order she takes them in she lands on the I-projection of the prior onto the intersection of the two constraint sets, namely 3/8, 3/8, 1/6, 1/12. Sanity check: that satisfies both constraints, P(Red) = 3/4 and P(HQ | Red) = 1/2. Minimum relative information is a projection onto a convex set, and for linear constraints iterated projection converges to the projection onto the intersection, so the sequence does not matter. What this shows is that minimum-relative-information updating gives a well-defined answer for evidence that arrives as a conditional probability, unlike plain Bayesian conditioning, which here has nothing to condition on.
