I'm in my third year of a neuroethics PhD (philosophy side, but I share an office with the connectomics and emulation crowd) and I'm putting together a short "which view of survival through gradual replacement do you actually hold?" instrument. I want to run it pre and post around a session we're doing on whole-brain emulation and cell-by-cell neural prosthetics, so the respondents will be neuroscience and ML PhD students plus a few clinicians — no philosophers, and no one who should need to know what any of the positions are called.

What I'd like from you:

Eight diagnostic questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key. The point is to sort people by what they say about the cases, so no question should just ask whether you are your body, your brain, or your memories — every question should be a case where the standard views genuinely come apart, phrased so someone can answer it cold. Keep each question to maybe forty words and write the whole thing in British English.

Cases I definitely want in there (fill the rest with whatever you think separates views best):

- one neuron at a time swapped for a functionally identical silicon unit, all the way to 100%, with behaviour and reported experience apparently unchanged throughout;
- the identical end state reached in a single operation under general anaesthetic;
- a swap sequence where behaviour and verbal reports stay perfect but where, on one way of thinking about it, consciousness would quietly dim and go out, and on another way of thinking the idea of a silent dimming is incoherent because you'd always be talking about it if it happened;
- substitutes that reproduce the input-output function of the replaced population only at the level of the whole network, so that swapping any single unit in isolation would not look behaviourally identical — no pairwise substitution test works;
- substitutes whose internal dynamics run on a different timescale from the originals (they predict ahead to keep timing at the interfaces right), so behaviour and the abstract causal structure are preserved but the fine-grained real-time dynamics are not;
- replacement to around 60%, then reverting to biological units cultured from your own cells;
- the gradual sequence reaching a point at which the system is copied and both copies then continue being gradually replaced.

Build it from the logical space first: the questions, then the answer combinations they allow, then locate the actual positions in that space — psychological continuity with and without a no-branching clause, the animalist/organism view, pattern or functionalist views, embodied/enactivist scepticism about substrate-neutral replacement, the vagueness or no-fact-of-the-matter option, and the deflationary no-self option. Note the combinations nobody occupies and briefly say why (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored), and flag any pair of views your instrument can't separate instead of pretending it can.

One thing I want your help with: my hunch is most of my respondents will say yes to gradual replacement and no to the instantaneous version, and I can't tell whether that's a stable position or whether it collapses once you put in the population-level and time-dilated swaps. Tell me which of your questions would actually break that pattern apart versus which ones would just confirm it, and say what I should do with the answer sheets that don't match any leaf cleanly.
