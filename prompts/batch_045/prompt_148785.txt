our lab shares a GPU cluster and its become a whole thing this semester. one of the senior grad students keeps shutting down discussion on the slack with "its a textbook tragedy of the commons, its just an N-person prisoner's dilemma, defection dominates, so the only real fix is hard per-group quotas enforced from above." I think the analogy is carrying way more weight than he notices but I can't put my finger on exactly where it breaks, and I want to say something more useful than "you're oversimplifying."

Two features of our situation that I don't think the pasture story has:

(1) the damage isn't smooth in usage. below ~85% utilization nobody notices anything at all, and above that the queue times blow up superlinearly and basically everyone's jobs that week are shot. so the harm shows up at a threshold rather than accumulating a little with each added cow.

(2) its not symmetric. one group is submitting something like 60% of all jobs but eats maybe 5% of the pain, because they mostly run short interactive jobs that finish before congestion really bites.

So: can you audit the analogy? I want the mapping spelled out first as an actual table — pasture ↔ what, herders ↔ what, "adding one more cow" ↔ what, overgrazing ↔ what, Leviathan / mutual coercion ↔ what — and then the disanalogies.

The part I actually care about is the triage: which of those disanalogies matter for the conclusion he's drawing, and which are just noise you'd wave away. His conclusion is the enforcement one: since restraint is strictly dominated, nobody will ever voluntarily cut back, so we need quotas plus someone watching the logs. Does that survive the threshold thing? Does it survive the asymmetry? My half-baked guess is that the threshold pushes it away from PD and toward more of a coordination problem (assurance? chicken? whichever fits) — and if that's right then the natural fix is norms and visibility rather than a quota, which kind of matches what happened when someone threw up a usage dashboard for a week and submissions dropped noticeably. He says that was a fluke and proves nothing.

And if the pasture is just the wrong source domain here, give me a better one for a shared resource with a congestion cliff and a big asymmetric user. I'd rather have the right bad analogy than the wrong famous one.
