so I've agreed to give three graduate lectures next term under the general heading of truth and structure, and I've finally been forced to write down what I actually think instead of just doing the history of the problem. Reading it back it sounds less like a position than like a wish list, so I want an audit before I say any of it out loud in front of twenty people who will enjoy this far too much. Here's where I've landed.

(a) Propositions are structured. Not sets of possible worlds, not simples: the sentence expresses something with parts, and the parts line up with the referring expressions and the predicate. I want this because I want compositionality to be a real feature of the world's representable structure, and because I want the attitudes to be fine-grained in a way I don't think a worlds-based semantics can deliver.

(b) Bradley's regress is a genuine, vicious regress, and none of the usual stopping moves satisfies me. Unsaturatedness just shifts the bump in the carpet: to get a concept to work as unsaturated you already have to have it at work inside a judgement, so nothing is explained. And the older multiple-relation theory of judgement fails for, as far as I can tell, the same underlying reason. That's why I take the problem to be deep rather than a puzzle about how we happen to describe facts.

(c) So I take what I call the top-down option: facts are prior to their constituents, the constituents are abstractions off the concrete whole, and there is no relation of instantiation and no further tie. I've started calling the resulting items "thin facts" — not an extra entity sitting alongside the object and the property, but not built up out of them either. I'm also a realist about universals in the boring repeatable sense, and I'd like to stay one.

(d) I'm a truthmaker maximalist, and the point of it is explanatory: true propositions are true because of what there is, not merely extensionally in step with it. I dont want this to collapse into a biconditional with a thump on the table.

(e) On the semantic side I'm a Fregean: senses, predicate senses as unsaturated functions, the lot of it, I still teach it, and I claim it's doing its work somewhere other than at the unity problem.

(f) I don't think any of this is a special problem about representation or truth. The same shape of puzzle turns up for a table and its parts, so whatever I say ought to generalise to ordinary wholes.

(g) Propositions are mind-independent and could have been false, and I need the proposition that a is F to be exactly as unified when there's no corresponding fact as when there is. I've started calling this "unhosted unity", and I'd rather not pay for it with merely possible facts or unactualised states of affairs, becuase I'm a fairly hard-nosed actualist.

What I want is not a reading list and not a tour of the literature, I can do that myself. What I want is this: go through the set and tell me which of these are actually in tension, rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them flat, and for each say what specifically would have to give and what giving it would cost me. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. And flag the ones that only look like tensions — I've got two or three candidates in my head and I genuinely can't tell which are the real ones. For what its worth, every time I reread the list my suspicion lands somewhere different, which is not a good sign.
