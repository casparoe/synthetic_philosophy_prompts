Here's how one of them put it: "Arendt's point was that evil is banal: perpetrators are ordinary, decent-ish people rather than monsters, and they do what they do because of the situation they're placed in — Milgram's obedience experiments and the Stanford prison experiment later showed that nearly anyone can be pushed into it, which is basically what happened to Eichmann. He was a desk clerk with no motives of his own, a mere cog in the machine, following orders inside a system, and her claim was that the evil came from thoughtlessness rather than from any decision he made — so really we should be looking at institutions and procedures, not at blaming individuals. How much of that would she actually own?
