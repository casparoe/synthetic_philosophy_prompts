Something about the indefinitely iterated prisoner's dilemma bugs me and I think Ive been telling myself the wrong story. Standard line: finitely repeated with a known last round, backward induction unravels cooperation; instead give every round a fixed continuation probability w and grim trigger works once w is high enough, the usual gloss being that there's no last round to start the induction from. But the game ends with probability 1, so some round is the last one, and on that round defecting costs nothing, so "there is no last round" can't literally be what saves cooperation. What is actually blocking the induction here: the fact that no player is ever in a position to know that this is the last round, or something about the shape of the game tree itself? I want the gut-level version, not just a pointer at the one-shot deviation condition.

The part where I actually got stuck: suppose the continuation probability isn't constant but decays as the pair keeps meeting (relationships in this population wear out), and the players only carry a bounded record of the past, so once the relationship is older than their memory window every round looks the same from the inside and they can't tell whether the remaining shadow is long or short. Is cooperation still an equilibrium there, and is the quantity that matters the objective hazard at that round or the hazard they'd infer from what they can see? I keep flip-flopping on whether the "shadow of the future" framing is quietly assuming the players have a clock that the model never gave them, so an explanation of where exactly that framing breaks would help most.
