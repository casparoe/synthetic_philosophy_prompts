I am assembling a handout for a graduate seminar I teach on the metaphysics of propositions, and I have decided that a dialogue would serve my students better than another survey: the published material on the regress argument associated with Bradley leaves them with the sense that there are exactly two respectable verdicts — either the regress of relations is real but benign, a harmless infinite hierarchy that a proposition simply has, or it is vicious, and shows that a relation cannot be one more constituent alongside its relata waiting to be tied to them by a further relation. I find both verdicts unsatisfying, and the seminar discussion last week suggested my students do too: the benign-regress reading seems to leave the unity of the proposition unexplained rather than explained by an infinite structure nobody ever surveys, while the vicious-regress reading tends to be cashed out in the direction of monism or of a primitive, brute unity, which is more of a refusal to answer than an answer.

The view I have been defending myself, and which I would like one character to press, is what I call the Format Hypothesis. Its starting point is an offhand remark from a colleague in computer science who works on knowledge-graph representations: in her formalism a relation is not an extra node in the graph but a labelled, directed edge, an ordered pair of slots into which the relata are fitted. On that picture the regress never gets started, not because it is benign and not because unity is brute, but because the question what ties a, R and b together presupposes a format in which R already appears as a further item on a list alongside a and b — a term-format, we might say — and the question simply cannot be posed in a slot-format. The obvious objection, and I want it pressed hard, is whether the format here is merely notational. If it is, then the dissolution looks like a dodge: the metaphysical question reappears one level up, as the question of what in reality makes the slot-format the correct articulation of the proposition. If it is not merely notational, then my character owes a metaphysical account of slots, edges or unsaturatedness that does genuine work, and it is far from clear that such an account can avoid reinstating the very regress it was meant to dissolve.

I would like the other character to defend what I have been calling infinitism about propositional structure: the view that we should take the regress at face value and accept that the proposition that Twain wrote Huckleberry Finn has infinitely many constituents, arranged in a hierarchy that is generated by a finite recursive rule, so that a finite mind can grasp the proposition by grasping the rule rather than by surveying the levels. Please write the dialogue for roughly 1,200 to 1,600 words, and make both characters genuinely competent — neither should be a foil, and each should have at least one moment where they concede a real point to the other and then rebuild their argument around the concession rather than simply reasserting it. Three questions should be forced into the exchange rather than left at the margins: why the proposition that Twain wrote Huckleberry Finn differs from the proposition that Huckleberry Finn wrote Twain (or, for a symmetric case, what distinguishes the proposition that Twain is Clemens from the mere list of its constituents); why either account yields a unified proposition rather than a mereological sum or set containing Twain, the writing relation and the book; and whether the infinite structure posited by the infinitist does any explanatory work at all, given that grasp and truth-evaluation seem to operate entirely at the first level. Assume an audience that knows the standard dialectic, and let the characters argue the positions out rather than appeal to authorities: I want no footnotes, no citations, and no name-checking of the literature, since I can supply the reading myself. By the end I would like the two of them to have arrived, jointly, at a sharper statement of what their disagreement actually turns on — my own suspicion is that it bottoms out in whether representational format is notational or metaphysically load-bearing, but I would rather the dialogue reach that by argument than be handed it at the outset.
