i'm stuck on a section of a paper and could use a case built to order.

I want a Sen-style liberal paradox case, but with the dialectical polarity reversed from the usual one. In Sen's own version, where the whole thing runs on two people having oppinions about what the other one reads, everyone's reaction is "fine, so much the worse for Pareto". I want a case where that reaction isn't comfortable: where the outcome that respects both rights is one that *both* people agree is worse than some alternative they'd both rather have, so the pressure lands on minimal liberalism instead.

Spec:

- Two people, four feasible outcomes, ordinary realistic setting (a shared flat, a small office, a two-person clinic, whatever). No novels, no prudishness, nobody getting a say over what anyone else reads or does with their own body.
- The four outcomes form a 2x2: each person has one dimension they control, with two settings.
- Minimal liberalism stays minimal: each person is decisive over at least one pair of outcomes differing only along their own dimension. Keep both rights plausible — something a person would really have a right to decide — and dont make either right obviously worthless to its holder.
- Nobody is meddlesome. No paternalism, no prudishness, no "I want you to not be allowed to X". A person's ranking over their own two settings *may* flip depending on where the other person's dimension sits, but only because of a real interaction effect in the situation — a draft, noise, crowding, one indivisible thing they have to share — not because they hold any view about how the other person should conduct themselves. This is the constraint I actually care about: the standard move for defusing the paradox is to restrict the domain to non-meddlesome preferences, and I want a case where that escape hatch doesn't obviously apply, because nobody here is being nosy in any ordinary sense and the cycle still turns up.
- Strong form please: the two rights plus Pareto (plain unanimity sense — if both strictly prefer a to b, b is out) should give you a real cycle over three of the four outcomes, and it shouldn't be a one-off: every outcome you can select while respecting both rights should be Pareto-dominated, and no Pareto-undominated outcome should respect both rights.

What I want back:

1. The scenario itself, one short paragraph.
2. The four outcomes and both people's full rankings over all four. For each person, flag the comparison that shows the flip, and say in a sentence why that flip is an interaction effect rather than meddling.
3. The three steps of the cycle, with which principle forces each step.
4. A check of the strong form: which outcome the rights force, what Pareto-dominates it, and whose right the dominating outcome violates.
5. Then: does restricting the domain to non-meddlesome preferences dissolve your case? Does rethinking rights as game forms, or letting people's preferences be conditional on what others do, dissolve it? If neither does, say what you think the case actually shows.
6. Which feature is doing the work; what happens to the case if you make the preferences separable again; and how someone determined to keep both principles would attack your scenario.
7. A sentence or two on why, in your case, giving up minimal liberalism is less comfortable than it is in the original.

Under 700 words if you can manage it — I want to be able to lift the scenario more or less straight into a footnote.
