For the chapter of a guidebook I'm putting together for long-stay visitors — the bit where you tell newcomers which local conventions they actually have to follow and which they can ignore — I want the spec before the advice. So: write the desiderata an account of conventional obligation has to meet. What does a theory have to be able to do to be good enough to settle when an everyday convention (tipping, queueing, jaywalking, taking a number, standing on the right of the escalator) generates a real moral obligation, as opposed to mere etiquette, prudence, or a reason to avoid a dirty look? For each requirement give the case behind it, and say whether its non-negotiable or just the hobbyhorse of one camp.

Cases I want the list to have room for, and please add the ones I've missed: the phantom queue (at the rural bus stop the regulars wait in the cafe across the street and board in order of arrival, so a newcomer sees an empty stop and no line); the tip screen on the tablet at a counter where staff already get a proper wage, and the menu that says service included; jaywalking at 4am with no traffic and no witnesses; the half-dead convention that roughly half the people still follow; the visitor who genuinely has no idea versus the expat on year nine who still doesn't like it; a locally entrenched convention that shifts costs onto the worst-off locals (tips that stand in for wages, a "fee" that moves your permit up the pile); and conventions where your compliance is invisible to everyone, so nothing you do updates anyone's expectations.

Two things I'm convinced a theory must not collapse: whether you must follow the convention and whether you may enforce it on other people, and whether the convention binds and whether it's a good thing to have at all.

Then: which pair of requirements do you think can't both be met? My guess is that "the obligation has to hold even when nobody is affected and nobody is watching" and "the obligation has to lapse when the convention is extractive" pull in opposite directions, but tell me if the real conflict is elsewhere. Which single requirement does the most work in ruling candidate theories out? A short scorecard for two or three of the standard stories is fine, but the list is what I need.
