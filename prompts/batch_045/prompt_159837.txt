ok so i fell into a wikipedia hole the other night and found this word "sphexishness" — it's about a wasp that paralyses a cricket, drags it to its burrow, leaves it on the doorstep while it goes inside to check the place out, then comes back and hauls it in; and if you move the cricket a few inches while it's inside, it comes out, drags it back to the doorstep, and goes in to check again, and apparently you can keep doing that to it for ages. looks like it's adapting, actually it's just running the same loop. now i can't stop applying it to people and i don't think i have a consistent rule at all.

my verdicts, going on gut:

my little brother on minecraft from 4pm to 10pm — sphexish. he'll tell you he wanted to.
my friend who's played the same piano piece so many times his hands do it while he's talking to you — not sphexish, feels like the opposite somehow. like peak agency.
me laughing at a joke i don't think is funny because everyone else is — sphexish, even though i can give a perfectly good reason for it (fitting in).
my cousin having to touch the doorframe three times before she leaves a room — sphexish-ish, but in a different way, and i really don't want to say she chose it.
my friend texting his ex at 1am knowing exactly how it's going to go — he says he couldn't help it and i sort of believe him, but i also think it's the most him thing he does all week. so not sphexish? that one contradicts the doorframe one and i can't see how to fix it.
my grandad driving on autopilot to a hardware store that shut down in 2019 — sphexish, obviously.
a classmate who was raised in a really tight religious community and says he's chosen to stay — genuinely 50/50 for me.
me studying four hours for a test i don't care about because i'm scared of my mums reaction — no idea.

my guess at a principle was "it's mechanical if you'd keep doing it after the reason stops applying," but i think the piano case and the party-laugh case both break that.
