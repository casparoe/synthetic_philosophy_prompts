I am writing a novel set in Lisbon in the year after 1 November 1755. Take the facts as given: a very large earthquake in the morning on a holy day, the tsunami that followed, the fires, tens of thousands dead, the churches crowded when the shaking started. One of my characters is a young, well-read optimist who has actually worked through the *Theodicy* and is trying to say something honest to a woman who has lost her whole family. I need to know what he can and cannot honestly say, so I need the episode analyzed through the lens of Leibniz's claim that this is the best of all possible worlds.

Do the mapping carefully rather than loosely. What is the unit of comparison on Leibniz's own terms — whole worlds, or events within a world? Does the thesis entail anything whatever about the frequency, scale, or distribution of natural disasters in the best world, or is silence on such matters built into it? Is the pressure coming from the bare existence of suffering or from its quantity and its concentration on people who did nothing to incur it? Then the defense my character keeps reaching for, which I would call the integration defense: a possible world is a causally seamless whole, so this world with the earthquake deleted is not a better candidate but no candidate at all, and no local horror can therefore count against the whole. Is that Leibniz's own move or one imported into it, and does it cost the theory the ability to say anything about any particular event? I also suspect it quietly conflates two claims Leibniz need only be committed to one of — that nothing is better than the whole, and that nothing within the whole is gratuitous or wasted. Tell me which of those the objector standing in the ruins actually needs to deny.

Finally, run the analysis the other way: what did this episode really do to optimism? Did it refute a thesis Leibniz held, or did it expose that the thesis was never about events of this kind at all? Is a theodicy that falls silent at the bedside still worth having, and is that silence a disciplined refusal to overclaim or an admission of bankruptcy? Where the theory has nothing to say, say so plainly instead of making it speak.
