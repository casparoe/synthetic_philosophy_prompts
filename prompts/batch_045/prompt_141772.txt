I've got a capital projects committee meeting Thursday and I need something to circulate beforehand.

The case: the university wants to put a satellite engineering campus on 180 acres of old pasture and scrub it already owns. Our environmental assessment says it's decent habitat — lots of small rodents, ground-nesting birds, brutal juvenile mortality, plus a pair of harriers that hunt the site. A student animal-advocacy group filed a comment saying the committee should record "expected reduction in wild animal suffering" as a mitigation benefit of the project, since fewer animals living short miserable lives is a welfare gain. One of our ecology faculty floated something similar last year — she wants to score projects in "welfare-adjusted habitat units" — and there's also an offer from a landowner to fund wetland restoration offsite as an offset, which everyone assumed was an obvious win until this argument. County rules are silent on all of it.

Draft the memo I'll send: should the committee count anticipated reductions in wild animal suffering as a benefit in its findings, and if so under what limits, or should it decline to, and why. Give me a bottom line the committee can actually act on with a concrete next step and a deadline, take the students' argument seriously instead of dismissing it, and give the strongest version of the losing side — including what evidence would make you change your mind.
