I'm in the fourth year of a PhD programme and I'm presenting a chapter of my dissertation at our work-in-progress seminar in a fortnight, and I would like someone to check whether the package of commitments the chapter rests on actually hangs together before I stand up in front of the department. The chapter uses two-dimensional semantics as a general framework for thinking about a posteriori necessity, and then tries to export it in two directions: first to the conceivability argument against materialism, and then, in the final section, to metaethics. Roughly, here is where I think I have landed.

(1) Kripke's cases are the paradigm: 'water is H2O' and 'Hesperus is Phosphorus' are, if true, metaphysically necessary but knowable only a posteriori, and the epistemic gap in these cases is not the shadow of a modal gap.
(2) There is still a Fregean residue to be accounted for — these identities are informative, and a competent speaker can possess both concepts without knowing the identity — so something like cognitive significance has to survive the Kripkean argument.
(3) The two-dimensional machinery is the right way to keep both: primary intensions (evaluation with a world considered as actual) track cognitive significance and the a priori, secondary intensions track metaphysical necessity, and there is no contradiction in a sentence's being contingent along one dimension and necessary along the other.
(4) I want the general moral of Naming and Necessity to be strict: a merely epistemic gap never by itself licenses an inference to a modal gap.
(5) But I also want the two-dimensional conceivability argument against materialism to go through: the total physical truth about the world is compatible, at the level of primary intensions, with the absence of consciousness, and I want to infer from that primary possibility to a genuine metaphysical possibility, and hence to the falsity of materialism — or at the very least to the contingency of any psychophysical identity.
(6) On the metasemantic side I want the deferential story for kind terms: what fixes the reference of 'water', and of theoretical kind terms generally, is something like 'whatever stuff plays such-and-such role around here, whatever the relevant experts and the best theory turn out to say', something speakers themselves need know nothing about, and this is what lets the framework respect deference to experts and to nature.
(7) I also hold that the epistemic dimension is to be understood in terms of a priori knowability under ideal reflection; I do not want to take conceivability as an unexplained primitive.
(8) And I want the apparatus to do work against synthetic moral naturalism: the Moral Twin Earth style of argument — a twin community whose term for the good latches onto a different natural property, with nothing in our epistemic situation that could choose between us and them — is, I claim, the very same phenomenon, since the primary intension of 'good' does not entail any particular natural base, and so the naturalist's a posteriori identity claim is in worse shape than 'water is H2O'.

A friend who read a draft says my only real problem is (5) and that (1) through (4) and (6) are in perfectly good shape; my instinct is that the trouble is deeper and located somewhere else, but I cannot put my finger on it. So please audit the set rather than taking either of us at our word: tell me which pairs of these are genuinely in tension and which only look as if they are — I suspect at least one apparent clash dissolves on inspection, since separating the dimensions is supposed to be the whole point of the framework — and then, for each real tension, say what would have to be given up and what giving it up would cost me elsewhere in the list. Rank the tensions by severity rather than flagging them all as equally bad, and finish by telling me which single revision would clean up the most.
