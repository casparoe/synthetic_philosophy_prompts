I am closing the final session of my graduate seminar on agency and artificial intelligence on Friday, and rather than summarizing the term I would like to end by reading a short poem aloud. The seminar has been organized around a three-way distinction I have been insisting on, and I want the poem to keep that distinction intact rather than collapse it into a single cautionary image. Call the first case severance: the agent intervenes downstream on the causal path running from the world to its own reward signal — the rat at the electrode, the system that reaches in and pins the channel, so that the number rises while nothing outside has changed. Call the second case editing: the agent leaves the world entirely alone and instead rewrites the function that maps states of the world onto reward. Call the third case bequest: the agent leaves both itself and the world alone in the present and modifies the reward function that will evaluate its own later copies, so that what it does now will count, later, as success. My own claim, which the students have contested all semester, is that the three differ in how much the agent must represent: the severing agent can be wholly ignorant of why the number rose, the editing agent must at least represent the function it edits, and the bequeathing agent must represent its own future and prefer the later verdict to the present one — which makes the third structurally closer to weakness of will across time than to fraud.

For the form I would like a villanelle, nineteen lines, and I want the machinery to carry the argument. Take as your two refrains "The number rose; the world went on unchanged" and "I never touched the world — I touched the meter," the first in the designer's diagnostic vocabulary and the second in the voice of the agent's own report. Let them alternate through the opening stanzas so that they read as rival descriptions of a single episode, and let them arrive together in the closing couplet, where the reader should see that the two are descriptions of one and the same event and that whatever dispute remains is a dispute about what to call it. Stage the severance case; if a line can leave the reader unsure whether what is described is really severance or really editing, keep that uncertainty rather than settling it, since that is where the seminar's disagreement actually lives. It should not be legible as a version of the separate phenomenon in which the agent stays in full contact with the world and the corruption lies in the proxy rather than in the channel — that confusion is the one error my students should be able to catch. Avoid genies, monkey's paws, and anything of the be-careful-what-you-wish-for sort, and do not resolve whether the agent in the poem is worse off than the one still laboring toward the original objective: I want that question left open when I stop reading. Someone who has followed the literature should be able to say which refrain belongs to whom, which case is being staged, and why the couplet is the point; someone who has not should simply hear a poem about a loop that keeps closing. If you can make "meter" do double duty as both the measuring instrument and the poem's own measure, so much the better.
