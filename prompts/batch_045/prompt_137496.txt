Hi — I need you to argue for a position, hard, as if you beleived it, and I'd rather you not hedge your way out of it.

Context so we don't talk past each other: the fight in the literature is over what you have to have in order to properly assert that p. Standard candidates: you have to know p; p merely has to be true; you have to reasonably believe p. The cases everyone wheels out are roughly: the lottery ticket (you're statistically all but certain your ticket lost, and it did lose, but you don't know it, and saying 'my ticket lost' strikes people as off in a way that's hard to explain), the lucky guess (p is true, you have zero evidence, asserting it still seems off), and the Moore-flavoured cases ('it's raining and I don't believe it' sounds crazy even though it could easily be true).

I don't want a fourth entry in that list. Here's the position: assertion is essentially an act of taking responsibility for a claim in front of someone who can demand your reasons, and the knowledge norm is just the shape that responsibility happens to take. So it's derived, not basic — there's no free-standing epistemic requirement on speech floating out there, there's just what you owe an audience you're answerable to. The upshot I want you to actually cash out rather than assert: when the answerability relation gets severed or scrambled, the norm should shift, and the reason the debate looks deadlocked is that everyone is implicitly describing fully answerable assertion and then generalizing from it.

Please work through at least these three, and don't hand me a both-ways answer on any of them: a sealed diary or a message in a bottle, addressed to no one who can ever ask you anything; a genuinely anonymous broadcast to millions, where nobody can trace it, sanction you, or ask how you know; and — the one I actually care about — asserting to an audience that structurally cannot check you, like a doctor talking to a patient or a teacher to an eight-year-old. Does the norm get weaker there because they can't hold you to it, or stronger precisely because they can't? Pick one and defend it.
