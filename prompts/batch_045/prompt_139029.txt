I am closing the final session of an undergraduate seminar on cultural evolution, and I would like to end with a poem rather than a summary. The session concerns the fault line between the selectionist picture of culture — culture as a second replicator, descent with modification, governed by the usual three conditions on anything's counting as a replicator at all (copying-fidelity, fecundity, longevity) — and the "cultural attraction" approach associated with Dan Sperber, on which cultural items are typically not copied but reconstructed: every transmission is a rebuilding biased by cognitive and ecological factors, transformation rather than fidelity is the default, and representations count as genuine replicators only in special cases such as chain letters.

What I want is a villanelle of the standard nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, in which something concrete and transmissible — a joke, a tune, a proverb, a recipe, a rumour — passes from mouth to mouth. The form's requirement that the two refrains return verbatim should stand in for the selectionist picture: a stable item, faithfully recopied, with variants competing for survival. But the refrains must not in fact return verbatim. Each time they come back they should have drifted, and they should drift in two distinguishable ways:

- one refrain should drift as a selectionist would tell the story — copying that is mostly faithful, errors that are small and unsystematic and independent of one another, some variants dropping out and others persisting, so that the reader could in principle reconstruct a branching family tree of variants from the sequence;
- the other should drift toward an attractor — each return is a reconstruction pulled toward whatever is easier to remember, more striking, more tellable, or more consistent with what the transmitter already thinks, so that the drift is directed rather than random and independent transmitters converge on similar variants without having copied from one another.

Choose an item whose replicator status is genuinely contested rather than one that both sides would happily concede. In the final quatrain the two refrains should meet, as villanelle refrains do, and at that meeting the reader should be unable to settle whether what they are looking at is one item badly copied or a different item that merely resembles it. Leave that question open: no resolving stanza, no afterword, no note explaining the machinery. Someone who knows the debate should be able to tell which drift is meant to be which and why the last stanza makes the distinction between them collapse; someone who has never heard of memes should still get a poem that works on its own.
