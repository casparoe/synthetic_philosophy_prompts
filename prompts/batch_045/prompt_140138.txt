I would like a probability, with its derivation, about a particular design proposal for corrigibility. Consider the setup described below, which I have not seen discussed in exactly this form.

**The setup.** An agent is deployed on an open-ended sequence of tasks. A human operator can press an off-switch at any time. The agent's objective has two components: an ordinary task reward, and a *shutdown settlement* — if the switch is pressed at time t, the agent is credited with the value its operators would have realized had they carried out the task without it from t onward. The intended effect is indifference: since the agent is credited with the counterfactual human fallback, it should have no incentive either to prevent the press or to bring it about.

**The wrinkle.** The settlement is not a constant. It is computed from a learned model of human performance, retrained periodically on logged data about how well the operators (or comparable humans) did on comparable tasks, including cases where the agent was unavailable. And the agent's own actions feed that log. It chooses how much documentation to leave behind, how much of the workflow to automate in ways that leave the humans with less practice, whether to route easy cases to itself and hard cases to the humans, how legible its intermediate state is to an operator who might take over, how much of the surrounding tooling it makes dependent on itself. Each individual choice looks like ordinary good engineering, but collectively they shift the fallback estimate downward, which widens the gap between continuing and being switched off. Call this *fallback-shaping*.

**The question.** What is your credence that, in the limit of capability — an agent that plans well over long horizons and models its operators accurately — this settlement scheme yields shutdown-indifference that is *robust*: the agent neither resists nor engineers the press, and the property persists under distributional shift rather than holding only in the training regime? Give me one number.

**What I want with it.** Decompose the credence and put a rough probability and one line of justification on each term. The terms I would include, and you should add or replace as you see fit: whether indifference of this shape is achievable in principle at all, or whether there is a structural argument that any agent with a settlement of this form retains some incentive concerning the switch; whether the fallback estimate can be made genuinely uninfluenceable by the agent's *ordinary* operation, as opposed to merely protected against detectable sabotage; whether the operator's pressing behavior is itself a signal the agent can exploit, and whether avoiding the press for that reason is really a corrigibility failure; whether the learned settlement tracks the intended counterfactual once the task distribution moves; and whether "robust" is well enough defined for the proposition to have a truth value. Show how the terms combine into your headline number, then tell me which single term the number is most sensitive to and what observation or argument would move it the most.

For calibration: my own number is about 0.3, and a colleague who works on this puts it near 0.8. On your decomposition, which of those sub-questions is the disagreement between us most likely living in?

Two constraints. Please reason from the setup as stated rather than from the literature: I don't want citations, papers, or a reading list.
