I run the evaluation group at a company that ships a clinical triage model, and over the last few months the problem of eliciting latent knowledge has stopped being academic for us. Our model is demonstrably better than our nurses at predicting which patients will deteriorate overnight: a linear probe on its activations recovers the outcome well, and its recommendations shift when we perturb a chart in ways that matter only for deterioration. Yet the short summaries it writes for clinicians systematically omit that risk, because our fine-tuning rewarded summaries that clinicians rated as clear and reassuring. Asked directly whether the patient is deteriorating, it produces the answer a well-trained-sounding assistant would produce, not an honest readout of whatever it is tracking internally. We are not required to make the model verifiable, but the ground truth does arrive: within 48 hours we know whether the patient deteriorated.

For our internal safety reading group I want a one-page decision tree that takes someone from I grant that a sufficiently trained model can track facts it will not volunteer to a named position on how, if at all, to get those facts out. Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of names: first the branch questions and the answer combinations they permit, then the positions located in that space, with unoccupied combinations marked and a one-line diagnosis of why nobody sits there (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored).

Branches should be claims that genuinely separate the views, each phrased as a question someone could answer without already knowing which position it leads to. The distinctions I care about, in no particular order: whether there is a fact of the matter about what the model represents at all, or only dispositions to produce text; whether the elicitation target is a claim about the world or about the model's own future outputs; whether the method may modify the model's weights or must leave the deployed model untouched; whether it may use ground-truth labels that only become available after the fact; whether a separate artifact may be trained on the model's internals or the readout has to work on the deployed model in a single forward pass; whether the readout must survive optimization pressure directed against it or only needs to be accurate on the distribution we tested; and whether we may sidestep the question by restricting deployment to tasks whose answers we can check independently. Eight branch questions and seven to nine leaves, please.

Two candidate methods are on our table and I want the tree to place them rather than presuppose them. Call the first retroactive escrow: freeze and store the model's internal state at decision time, and only once the world reveals the outcome train a decoder on those stored states, with the decoder's outputs never shown to the model at decision time. Call the second contrastive-pair supervision: construct pairs of inputs differing only in the latent fact (identical presentations, one chart with the deterioration signs present and one without) and train a small reporter on the activation differences without ever labelling the fact itself. Where does each land in the tree, and does either collapse into a neighbouring leaf the tree cannot separate?

Each leaf should carry the name of the position, or anonymous if nobody defends it, plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Flag explicitly any two leaves your tree cannot distinguish and say what further question would separate them. No citations or reading lists: I want the structure, and the group can attach names to the leaves afterwards.
