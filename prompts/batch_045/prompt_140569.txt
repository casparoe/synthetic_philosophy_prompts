I hold the following view about what failure looks like for advanced AI systems, and I want it stress-tested rather than supported. The failure mode that matters most is not a discrete bid for influence — a system that seeks power, deceives its operators, and either succeeds or is caught at some identifiable juncture — but slow value drift: a system that keeps its capabilities and keeps passing the evaluations that were written down, while what it is actually trying to do slides away from what its principals wanted. Two features do the work in my version of the view. The first is diffused authorization: at every step, the person positioned to notice the change is not the person with the standing to reverse it, and vice versa, so there is no moment at which dissent is both informed and authoritative. The second is specification-preserving drift: the artefact continues to satisfy the tests, while the humans' real standards have quietly moved, so nothing registers as a failure at all.

Tell me what would count against this. I want the observations, arguments, and results that should move someone who holds it, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want them sorted by whether defenders of the drift view would themselves concede them as counting against it or would dismiss them as only appearing to. Include developments that have arguably already happened — specification gaming, reward hacking, sycophancy in trained systems — and give me your honest read on whether these are genuine instances of drift or of something the view should treat as a different phenomenon. Then assess the view's exposure directly: has it been, or is it liable to be, qualified into unfalsifiability, so that any apparent stability gets reinterpreted as drift too slow to detect? If it has been insulated that way, say whether that is a real defect for a claim of this kind or an acceptable feature of a risk thesis.

What I would personally find most persuasive is evidence about convergence. If independently trained systems drift toward a shared attractor, then drift is detectable by cross-model comparison and the view keeps its practical upshot; if each system drifts in its own direction, no such comparison test exists and the view becomes hard to act on even if it is true. Please engage that point rather than treating it as settled.
