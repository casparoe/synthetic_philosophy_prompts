I work on a small forecasting team at a mid-size AI lab, and I've gotten myself properly confused about van Fraassen's Reflection principle. Hoping someone can tell me where I've gone wrong, or tell me I haven't, which would also be useful.

The version I'm working from: at time t, my credence in A, conditional on my later self at t+ having credence r in A, should itself be r, for every value of r I give positive probability to. Treat your future selves as experts. And the consequence everyone repeats is that my current credence has to come out to the probability-weighted average of the credences I might have later.

Here's the concrete thing that set me off. We have a deployment gate in about six weeks. After the red-team audit (internally we call it the Second Look) I'll land either near 0.8 or near 0.1 on whether the model is safe to ship, and I'd say its about 50/50 which way the audit pushes me, so I sit near 0.45 today. My boss wants a number now, and I've been telling the team we should use what I've started calling the pass-through rule: put down the number you expect to end up with once the evidence is in, since future-you is the expert, and future-you defers to later-you, and so on all the way down, so really you should be deferring to your final end-of-inquiry credence.

But when I actually try to apply it, I get nothing: the number I expect to end up with is 0.45 again, because that's the average of 0.8 and 0.1 at 50/50. So the pass-through rule looks like a no-op, and that makes me think the principle itself is empty. Any time it seems violated you can restore it either by moving your current credence or by adjusting what you'd think conditional on future-you saying r, so it never actually forces anyone to change a number, and I'd of expected a real norm to bite somewhere. I also don't want to lean on the Dutch book defence, which I'm told is contested anyway.

So: is Reflection just bookkeeping, or is there something it forbids that my 0.45 / 0.8 / 0.1 setup is hiding? And relatedly, doesn't it follow that I can't expect to learn anything from the audit, since I can't expect my credence to go up rather than down? That last bit feels obviously false, which is what makes me think I've misread the principle somewhere.
