I am a novelist, three volumes into a science-fiction sequence set on a terraformed world whose biosphere I have to keep internally consistent across some nine hundred pages and a series bible that a continuity editor and a scientific consultant now maintain alongside me. In the current book my narrator repeatedly has to say what an organismal trait is *for*: that a chambered organ is for pumping, that a cranial crest is for display, that a gland is for signalling distress. I gather that the philosophy of biology splits roughly between accounts that fix a trait's function by its history — what it was selected for, or what a designer installed it to do, in the lineage — and accounts that fix it by the contribution it currently makes to the capacity of some containing system. I do not need the literature; I need the distinction turned into something I can run.

So: write me a decision procedure for assigning a function to a trait in my fictional biosphere, which I can execute in about fifteen minutes at the point of drafting, with no more biology than an educated general reader has, and whose output is a one- or two-line entry my continuity editor can file and my consultant can check in our monthly pass. Give me the questions in the order I should ask them; what counts as a sufficient answer to each; the point at which the procedure returns a firm no; and the points at which it hands the decision back to me because the remaining question is one of narrative emphasis rather than fact. It must have explicit branches for traits that were designed rather than evolved, for traits whose history my world has a documented break in (several species were re-engineered by an earlier terraforming programme and then left to selection for centuries, so record and subsequent history come apart), and for traits whose history I have deliberately left blank and do not intend to fill in — tell me what the procedure does when the historical evidence is simply unavailable, since that will be most of my cases. Tell me which distinctions from the philosophical debate have to survive the simplification and which I can drop without the procedure breaking.

Then run it on three entries from the current manuscript, in full: (1) a hollow cranial crest inherited from a burrowing ancestor, now used by the animal in courtship display; (2) an implanted filtration organ, originally installed by the terraformers to remove a toxin, which in the descendant population now mainly drives circulation fast enough that my characters call it a second heart; (3) a bioluminescent frill on an organism whose lineage record I have intentionally left unwritten. I expect to dislike the answer on at least one of these — say which and why, rather than steering toward what I would prefer.

Begin with a single sentence stating the bottom line of the procedure, the one rule everything after it is a gloss on, and only then give the procedure and the worked cases; no preamble, and no restating back of what I have asked.
