ok so I've been going back and forth on the why-are-some-countries-rich thing for years and I want an actual number out of you, not a tour of the literature.

Frame it like this. There's an imaginary device — call it the swap machine — that lets you re-run world history from 1500 with everything held fixed except one family of causes at a time. You can take a region and swap its whole institutional trajectory (how secure property is, whether rulers can expropriate at will, how the state taxes and how it staffs its bureaucracy) for some other region's, while leaving its geography and its culture in place; or swap geography; or swap culture. Then you watch where income per person lands in 2000. The proposition I want a credence on:

P = institutional swaps move long-run income per person more than geography swaps and culture swaps put together — institutions carry more of the causal load than the other two families combined, not merely more than either one on its own.

I'm at about 0.65. A friend of mine who's read more history than economics is at 0.3, and half the time I think she's not disagreeing about the world at all but about whether the question has a determinate answer.

Give me your own number, but mainly the decomposition — a probability plus one line of justification for each term:

- that the swap-machine setup is even coherent, given path dependence and the possibility of multiple equilibria;
- that institutions are a genuine causal variable rather than a label we stick on whatever equilibrium happens to persist (you know the circularity worry: half the time we tell which places have good institutions by noticing that they got rich);
- your rough split of causal load across institutions, geography, culture, and sheer conjunctural luck — accidents, specific individuals, timing;
- that the three families are more like three keys to one lock than three independent knobs, so that which one explains most is a badly posed question — and if that's likely, say what the number above is even a number about;
- that the standard natural-experiment designs people wheel out (colonial settlement patterns, partitioned countries, arbitrarily drawn borders) can be carried out of their own century and into others;
- how much of your uncertainty is about what actually happened versus about what the word explains is doing here.

Then combine it into one number, tell me which term you trust least, and tell me what kind of finding would move it most — if it's movable at all by more data, as opposed to being stuck in principle. And last: where do you think my friend and I genuinely disagree?
