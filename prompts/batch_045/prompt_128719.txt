I build LLM-based agents for a living, so I keep running into two flatly opposite claims from people in my field. One side says a mind is just computation, the substrate doesnt matter, and a good enough simulation of a person would be a person with the same claims on us. The other says a simulation of a mind is like a simulation of a rainstorm: it isnt wet, nobody is home. Someone told me the strong version of the first claim is the gradual one, where you swap one neuron for a chip at a time and ask where exactly the lights would go out, and also mentioned a hypothetical system that has nothing but a canned response stored for every conversation it could ever have, which is meant to show that behaving like a mind isnt enough. I dont know the proper names for either move.

Then I rewatched Nolan's The Prestige and now I cant stop reading it as a thought experiment about copying minds. Angier's machine produces a duplicate across the theater every night and the one left behind drowns in a tank, and he does it a hundred times, and the film puts almost none of its moral weight there: the twins and the sacrifice get all of it. So read it for me as an argument about copies and moral status. Is the thing that appears at the back of the hall a person with a full claim, a second person, or a very good simulation with nobody inside? Does it matter that the whole film is about audiences being unable to tell the trick from the real thing, and that we would have to judge from the outside? Does the fact that the swap is all at once rather than one piece at a time change which answer I should give, or is my intuition flipping there definately just a slippery slope doing rhetorical work? Say honestly where the film resists the reading, and whether it adds anything to the argument that the thought experiments alone dont.
