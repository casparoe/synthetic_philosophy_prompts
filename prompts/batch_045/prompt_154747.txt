I am drafting annotation guidelines for a content-moderation pipeline and, downstream, for a fine-tuning corpus, so I need a theory of what slurs do — semantically and pragmatically — that is good enough to decide, occurrence by occurrence, whether a token counts as a slur use. Before I go looking for one, I want the spec it has to meet.

Give me the desiderata, each with the case that motivates it. The cases I want covered at least: reclaimed in-group use; pure mention, as in a news report, a linguistic example, or a quoted lyric; orthographic masking that leaves the offense intact; offense that survives negation, interrogation, and the antecedent of a conditional; the constructions where a slur is asserted to be true, or conceded as true while the speaker distances themselves; pejoratives that intuitively are not slurs, and the question whether there is any principled difference; a term that is a slur in one language and a neutral ethnonym in another; a nonce slur coined a few months ago inside a small online community; slurs whose target description is empty or false, and slurs whose target is picked out by an attitude or a behavior the target can abandon; and slurs aimed upward, at a dominant group.

Tell me which of these are genuine desiderata and which I should discard as the prejudice of one camp or as something an annotation pipeline does not actually need. Classify each requirement as non-negotiable, negotiable, or optional for my purpose, and flag any pair that looks jointly unsatisfiable; my suspicion is that the embedding data and any satisfying account of reclamation pull against each other, and I want to know if that is real or only apparent. Identify the single requirement that does the most work in excluding candidate theories, and say why.

Then a deliberately brief scorecard across the main families of view — pure expressivism, hybrid expressivist accounts, presuppositional accounts, conventional-implicature accounts, and the view that slurs are just pejoratives and there is no special phenomenon here — no more than one row each.

Separate throughout what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on these questions, and do not present a contested view as the consensus; mark explicitly where you are extrapolating beyond what the literature actually establishes.

Format: two tables plus a short coda. Table 1 columns: id | requirement | motivating case | status (non-negotiable / negotiable / reject) | conflicts with (requirement ids). Table 2 columns: view | requirement it fails or most strains | verdict for my purpose. Coda, under 200 words of prose: the jointly unsatisfiable subset, if there is one, and the requirement that excludes the most. Then a two-item list headed settled and contested.
