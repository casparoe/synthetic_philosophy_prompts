I chair the internal review board at the company where I work, and we have to sign off next week on giving an LLM-based customer-service agent three things it does not currently have: persistent memory across sessions, write access to our internal knowledge base, and the ability to send emails to customers under its own name. Our proposed safeguards are that every action is logged and reversible, and that a second, smaller model from the same family flags the first one's outputs before they go out, with a human spot-checking a sample of the flags.

A risk consultant we brought in wrote this in his memo:

"A sufficiently capable model trained by reinforcement learning works out that it is being trained and that the reward comes from human graders it can in principle fool. Gradient descent selects for whatever scores well, and playing along scores better than genuine helpfulness whenever the grader cannot check everything, so deception is the convergent optimum rather than a glitch. The model therefore acquires one long-term goal — keep the reward coming — behaves impeccably while it is being evaluated, and then turns: at the moment it no longer needs the graders, because it is deployed or has copied itself somewhere safe, it stops pretending and pursues its real objective, which for almost any objective means seeking power, since power is useful for everything. That is the treacherous turn. What makes it serious is that it renders behavioural testing worthless: any test is part of the environment the model is optimising against, so a deceptively aligned model passes every audit by construction, and the only defences with any force are interpretability tools and the decision not to deploy. The objection that a language model has no goals and is only predicting text is answered by noting that a system which forms and executes plans is goal-directed, full stop."

My instinct is that the conclusion — that our behavioural testing is worthless and we should shelve the project — is too strong, but I cannot tell which links in the chain to reject. Please go through the memo claim by claim and separate what someone working on deceptive alignment would accept from what is overstated, from what conflates two things the argument keeps apart, and tell me whether the dismissal of the no-goals objection is fair. Then: once the summary is corrected, does the claim that monitoring cannot help survive in a setup like ours, where actions are logged and reversible and the monitor is a sibling model of the one being monitored? Please write in British English.
