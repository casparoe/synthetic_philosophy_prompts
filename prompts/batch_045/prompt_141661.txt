Looking for a word, or a distinction, whichever one it is that exists.

I keep running into the same shape of story and I cant find a name for it that isn't wrong in an obvious way. The version that got me going is the case for farming being a bad bargain: some groups take up planting, planting feeds more people per square mile than foraging does, the extra numbers are an advantage whenever there's a scrap over land, so the holdouts either plant too or get shoved off, and a couple of thousand years on everyone is working longer hours for worse food than their great-grandparents did and nobody can go back, because going back would starve most of the people who are now alive. No one in the story is a villain, no one chose the outcome, and no shared resource gets used up in the process. Also, somewhere along the way the same shift produces stored surplus and fields you can defend, and with that you get heritable property and ranks of inequality that a foraging band apparently can't sustain -- and I can't tell whether the inequality is part of the same mechanism or just a side effect that happens to travel with it.

The other cases I test it against, to see whether it's the same shape: everyone I know in academia is on a credential ratchet where each extra degree or postdoc is individually rational and collectively nobody is getting hired any better than before; and every parent in my town schedules their kid's afternoons, where each family would rather stop and none of them can stop first.

Labels I've tried and rejected: tragedy of the commons (nothing is being depleted -- if anything the problem is that the resource gets more productive); arms race (there's no party that wants to hurt the other, and the thing competed over isn't even wanted by anyone); Malthusian trap (that seems to be about population eating up productivity gains, which is downstream of the bit I'm pointing at); ordinary collective action problem / prisoners' dilemma (closest, but every version I can find assumes the people who get locked in are the same people who'd have to coordinate to get out, whereas in my cases the ones who'd have to coordinate are descendants who never chose any of it).

So: does this shape have a name of its own, and if it does, is the name for the whole thing or only for the "can't opt out" part? Is the "can't reverse it because your population size now depends on it" bit a separate mechanism from the "can't opt out" bit, or are they one? Does it stay the same phenomenon when the entrants were better off for a few generations and only their descendants ate the cost -- and does it stop being the phenomenon in the rare cases where some group does coordinate its way out? Someone told me the label is "Moloch," which I don't find helpful because it's a vibe, not a definition. I don't want a label anyway, I want the carving: what exactly this is, what nearby things it isn't, and where its edges are. If nothing established fits, coin something and tell me what its borderline cases would be.
