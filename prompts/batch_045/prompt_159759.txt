Here's where I've landed on laws of nature. I've more or less converted to the Humean side — the view people associate with David Lewis, where the laws aren't these extra prescriptive things standing over the world and telling it what to do, but just the most efficient summary of everything that actually happens. The best system: you take the whole list of events, and you look for the package of a few simple axioms plus a statement of the starting conditions that compresses that list as tightly as possible while still being informative. Whatever wins that competition is the laws, and there's nothing more to them than that. I like it because it doesn't need any mysterious "necessitation" relation and it explains why we can only ever find out the laws by looking at what happens.

I've also bolted on a bit of my own that I don't think is in the standard version. I call it the any-slice rule: a genuine law has to be recoverable from any large enough and varied enough chunk of the mosaic, not just from the whole thing taken at once. So there's no such thing as a law that only shows up once you've seen literally all of history, and there's no such thing as brute local bylaws — two regions with genuinely different "laws" where the difference between them isn't itself traceable to something in the mosaic. A law you could only read off the complete archive, or only off one neighbourhood, isn't a law on my view, it's an artifact. I also think the cost of the starting conditions should be counted in the same currency as the cost of the axioms — a theory that shoves all the explanatory work into a very specific initial state doesn't get a free lunch for having short equations.

What would count against this? I want the evidence and arguments that should move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be, from fatal down to merely awkward. Some specific things I want covered:

And then the meta-question, which honestly is the one I care about most: has this thing been qualified into unfalsifiability? My worry is that because the laws are defined as whatever best summarises the actual mosaic, any mosaic whatsoever gets some laws, so nothing that happens could ever contradict me. If that's right — if I've bought into a view that can't lose — is that actually a problem for a view of this kind, or is it fine because the view was only ever meant to be a metaphysical account of what laws are rather than a prediction? The other one that nags at me is why we're entitled to bet on tomorrow at all if the laws are just summaries of what has happened so far, including the future.
