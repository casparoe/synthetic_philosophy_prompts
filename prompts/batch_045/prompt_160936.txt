I'm a philosophy PhD student working on personal identity and the ethics of neuroprosthetics, and I'm convening a two-day closed session between two researchers who have been arguing past each other for roughly a decade. Both have agreed in advance to sign whatever joint text comes out of it. One of them (I'll call her the preservationist) holds that what matters in survival is carried by the continuing causal and material life of one and the same brain, and that the only non-lethal route to a substrate-independent mind is slow, in-place replacement while the organism keeps running. The other (the patternist) holds that what matters is causal-functional organisation, so that a scan-and-emulate transfer which destroys the biological brain preserves everything of value, and slow replacement is at best an engineering convenience and at worst a slow way of building a duplicate and killing the original.

What I need from you is the document itself, and I'd like it written against one fixed protocol so the two of them cannot quietly drift onto different cases. Use the following, which I have already got them to accept as the reference case. Call it the cross-fade protocol: for a target cortical region a neuromorphic surrogate is implanted alongside the intact tissue and trained online for several weeks, receiving the same afferent input and being corrected against the tissue's own output; control over the region's efferent output is then handed from tissue to surrogate continuously, by moving a gain parameter lambda from 0 to 1 over about four weeks, where for intermediate lambda the driver output is a weighted combination of the two systems' outputs and need not match either system's output taken alone; the biological tissue stays alive and metabolically supported throughout, and is surgically removed only after lambda = 1 has been stable for a month.

Three things are stipulated for the purposes of the document. Treat them as hypothetical findings that both parties accept as accurately described, not as real results: (a) at lambda around 0.5, dynamical measures suggest the wider system is no longer fully integrated, showing two partially decoupled complexes and a longer latency before the usual late cross-area response, while the subject, questioned daily, reports no change at all and is unimpaired on every behavioural and report-based test available; (b) after lambda = 1 and excision, the subject volunteers that one highly familiar experience, the taste of a dish eaten weekly for twenty years, now has a subtly different phenomenal character, while nothing else has changed, all discrimination and recognition behaviour is intact, and the surrogate's input-output function matches the recorded function of the excised tissue to within measurement error; (c) a complete state backup was frozen at lambda = 0.4 and later instantiated in a spare surrogate-only body, and that individual behaves exactly as the original would have and claims to be the original.

Please structure the document in four parts.

First, a numbered list of eight to twelve agreed claims. They must be non-vacuous: each should be such that a well-informed outsider can see what it would take to deny it. I want at least one bearing on what the protocol does to behaviour and to functional organisation over time, one on whether survival or what-matters admits of degrees and what that implies at lambda = 0.5, one on the epistemic standing of the subject's own report during the fade, and one on whether the excision at lambda = 1 does any further work over and above the fade itself. Where the honest answer is that only a platitude is available, say so and explain why the genuine agreement stops there.

Second, a disagreements table with these columns: the disputed claim stated neutrally; the preservationist's own formulation, in wording she would sign; the patternist's own formulation, in wording he would sign; and a one-line diagnosis of whether the disagreement is substantive or merely verbal, with the reason. Where you judge a dispute verbal, rewrite it as whatever substantive question survives the translation, or say that nothing does.

Third, four to six cruxes: sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each, give the question, the stipulated finding or case that both would accept as bearing on it, what each side predicts, and what each side would concede if its prediction failed. I expect the list to include at least: whether preservation is transitive across the fade or fails by increments; whether what survival requires is continuity of matter or of causal-functional role, including how the preservationist answers the objection from ordinary metabolic turnover; whether the subject's clean reports settle anything given the possibility of phenomenology that silently shifts or gradually fades while function and report are held fixed; the backup case in (c); and what the protocol ought to do at lambda around 0.5 given finding (a), since the two may be able to agree on a policy while disagreeing about the metaphysics.

Fourth, a short closing section: anything both would accept as genuinely capable of moving the debate, and, separately, each side's own statement of what would count as evidence or argument against its own view.

Two constraints. Keep it self-contained: no citations, no named authors or works, no footnotes, no appeal to anything I would have to look up, since I'll be reading this in a room without library access. And do not put either party in a position it would refuse: the preservationist must not be made to treat the surrogate's activity as an inert simulation, because her view requires the surrogate to be conscious by the end, and the patternist must not be made to deny that continuity is worth paying for. Aim for roughly 1,200 to 1,600 words, in the register of a document two people are about to sign rather than a report about their dispute.
