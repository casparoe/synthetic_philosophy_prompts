Im RAing for a philosopher who's finishing a book on why pain is bad, and her chapters are basically a list of cases with verdicts stuck on the end. I have to draft the last chapter and I'd like to guess what she'll say before I commit her to something in writing.

Her verdicts so far (every one of them is about whether the pain is intrinsically bad, not about whether anyone should treat it):

- ordinary burn on the hand: bad, obviously.
- textbook asymbolia — patient can tell a pinprick from heat, can rank intensities, but says it doesn't hurt, doesn't mind it, doesn't flinch or pull away, will happily let you keep going: not bad. In one draft she says the sensation survives but the badness doesn't.
- phantom limb pain in a leg that's been gone for years: bad, even though there's no tissue there and nothing is being damaged.
- the burn from hot chilli that someone deliberately goes back for and is glad about: bad, but worth having all things considered — wanting it doesn't cancel the badness.
- the rugby player who plays eighty minutes on a torn hammy, only notices at the final whistle, and then says it hurt the whole time: bad the whole time, including the stretch when nobody was attending to it.
- a patient who says the pain is worse than last month, is genuinely glad to be alive, and would turn down an analgesic if you offered one: bad, but not bad for her overall, and not something we'd be required to remove.

The case left for the last chapter is one she made up — nobody has ever reported it. Someone who reports the full hurt: same awful quality, says it hurts, rates the unpleasantness 8 out of 10, remembers exactly what it was like before, would still call it the worst thing in the room. But the pain has gone completely quiet in every other respect. No flinch, no withdrawal, no urge she can find, she shrugs off the morphine, and she'll say "it's not bothering me" in the same breath as "it hurts". I'm not even sure I've described it without begging the question.

What will she say? I can see at least two rules that fit all six of her verdicts equally well and disagree here — something like badness = hurtfulness, versus badness = the pain's stop-this-now character — and maybe a third about there being reason to prevent it. Don't just tell me yes or no: tell me which rule she's actually running, what each of the live ones predicts about the quiet-pain case, which verdict I should draft if I don't want to contradict chapters two through six, which of her existing cases is the closest analog, and what case I should put in a footnote to split whichever rules are still tied afterwards. Also: is "it hurts but it doesn't bother me" even coherent, or should she refuse to rule on a case like that?
