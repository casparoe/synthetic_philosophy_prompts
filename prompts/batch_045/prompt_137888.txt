You are a poet with graduate-level training in formal epistemology — someone who has actually worked on the problem of how a bounded agent should manage uncertainty about claims that are settled by proof rather than by observation. Take your time: think carefully about the philosophical content before you write a line, and do that thinking in two explicit stages before giving me the finished poem.

The task: write a pantoum about logical uncertainty.

The idea I want carried, stated plainly so that nothing here depends on your having read any particular paper. Consider a claim like "this program, which has been running for a year, eventually halts" — or, if you prefer, "this long-standing conjecture has no counterexample below 10^18." Such a claim is not empirical: no observation of the world will settle it, and waiting does not gather evidence about it. The only thing that settles it is proof, and the analogues of observation here are pieces of logical work — a lemma established, a case checked, a finite search extended one more order of magnitude. Three things matter about an agent's credences on claims of this kind, and the poem should be able to bear all three:

First, coherence at a time is not the same as coherence over time. An agent can hand out numbers at every moment that satisfy the probability axioms perfectly, and still be systematically exploitable, because of how those numbers move. If your price on the same claim swings 0.62, 0.38, 0.62, 0.38 in a pattern a feasible trading strategy can see coming, that strategy buys every low and sells every high, and its holdings grow without bound. The vice is not that any single number was incoherent; it is that the sequence leaked value. So the virtue we want is diachronic: not that you knew the answer, but that no strategy drawn from some fixed feasible class could reliably make unboundedly large gains against you. That is a weaker and attainable thing than certainty, and it is worth distinguishing sharply from certainty in the poem.

Second, the standard has to be relativized to feasibility. You cannot demand that a computable process which assigns these credences already knows every theorem; in a language rich enough to encode arithmetic, the set of theorems is not decidable, so logical omniscience is not on the menu for anything that actually runs. What can be demanded is that the sequence of credences eventually settles down and cannot be exploited by any feasible strategy — that the market is inexhaustible rather than omniscient.

Third, not every price movement is equally innocent. A move prompted by a proved lemma or a checked case is earned; a move prompted by nerves, or by a rhythm in your own head, is a wobble, and wobbles are what the traders eat.

Now the form, which is where I want the structure to do philosophical work rather than decorate it. A pantoum is built out of quatrains in which the second and fourth lines of each stanza reappear, verbatim, as the first and third lines of the next stanza; the repeated lines therefore keep returning in new company and mean something slightly different each time they come back. Write five to eight quatrains in alternating rhyme (ABAB, with the interlocking that the repetition forces), and close the poem by repeating the first line of the poem as the final line of the poem. Use this rule exactly as I have stated it, so that nothing hangs on convention.

That machinery should be load-bearing. The returning lines are the natural place for the claim that will not go away and for the pressure to act before it is settled; their reappearance in changed contexts should be what dramatizes the difference between a price that has been moved by real logical work and a price that has merely wobbled. Let at least one return be earned by something that actually happened in the poem — a lemma, a checked case, an exhausted search — and let at least one other return be a pure wobble that a trader could see coming. Give me at least five explicit numbers as the price moves across the poem; let the movement be visible and intentional, and let the poem either end in settlement or in a refusal of settlement that is clearly a choice rather than an accident, with the repeated closing line legible either way.

Some constraints. Do not cite, footnote, or name any paper, program, or author; the idea must be carried entirely by the verse, and nothing in the poem should require the reader to know the literature. Prefer image to proper name: if any canonical name appears, it must do work a paraphrase could not. Avoid the stock furniture of uncertainty poems — crystal balls, fog, crossroads, forks in roads — and find your images in computation and in wagering instead. The poem should be a good poem for a reader who has never heard of any of this, and it should be recognizable as *right* to someone who works on it: a specialist should be able to point to the couplet where the question is coherence over time rather than coherence at a time, and to the couplet where feasibility, rather than truth, is doing the work.
