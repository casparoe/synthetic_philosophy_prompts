I'm a quantitative psychologist — third year in a psychophysics lab, mostly fitting hierarchical Bayesian measurement models to forced-choice and self-report data — so the vocabulary I actually think in is signal detection theory, item response theory, latent variables and their identifiability, measurement invariance and non-invariance, criterion and response bias, construct validity, and, from some side work decoding fMRI data, the usual anxieties about reverse inference. I've been reading about Dennett's heterophenomenology on my own, and last night I had a long and entirely circular argument with a friend who is finishing a PhD in philosophy of mind and is fairly committed to it. My claim was that heterophenomenology is just what any careful psychometrician already does — treat verbal reports as fallible third-person observables, model the reporting process rather than trusting it, refuse to let the subject's authority settle what the data mean — with the extra and to me puzzling twist of deleting the latent variable at the end. Hers was that I have it exactly backwards: the method does not model a noisy channel from a hidden state to a report, because on her reading there is no hidden state for the channel to be noisy about, and the hetero-phenomenological world is not a latent space but something more like the text of a play assembled from what the subject will stand by under questioning. When I asked whether the method then assumes that reports carry any information at all about what the subject is going through, she said no — no sensitivity assumption, nothing like a positive d′, and that measurement with zero sensitivity is not a contradiction but the point. We stalled there, because in my vocabulary an instrument with no sensitivity to the thing being measured is not an instrument. She also said something I couldn't place at all: that the hetero-phenomenological world is not a model of the subject's experience, is not in the modelling business, and that this is precisely why it can be neutral between realism about conscious experience and its denial.

Here is the case I want the translation tested on, because it is the one I actually run. We present two brief flashes at separated locations with a short onset asynchrony. At short asynchronies subjects reliably report a single spot that moves, and, under gentle questioning, most of them say something like: it was already at the midpoint before the second flash came on. The reports are stable within subjects, highly consistent across subjects, and strictly inconsistent with the timing of the stimulus. I know roughly what I do with this: a reporting function with a bias, a construct I refuse to interpret, a lot of hand-waving about inference. I have no idea what a heterophenomenologist does with it, and my friend's answer — that it simply goes into the subject's world as a fact about it — sounded to me like either profundity or a refusal to do science, and I couldn't tell which.

So I would like a systematic translation rather than a verdict on who won the argument. Please: (i) restate the method itself — the protocol for recording and editing reports, the suspension of belief about their truth, the construction of the hetero-phenomenological world, and the accompanying positive picture on which there is no single place or time at which content becomes conscious — in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, without smuggling psychometric assumptions in and calling them translation; (ii) run that translation on the case above; (iii) give me the residue report, in three parts — what mapped cleanly, what mapped only with distortion (naming the distortion), and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all; and (iv) take a view on the residue: does it indict my framework, in that latent-variable modelling cannot help positing a hidden state and so cannot even state the quietist option, or does it show that the methodological claim was doing less work than it looked like it was doing, or that the claimed neutrality was a commitment in disguise? Finally, and concretely: if I wanted to run an honestly heterophenomenological study in my lab next month, what would the protocol look like in my terms, and what would I have to give up in order to run it that way?
