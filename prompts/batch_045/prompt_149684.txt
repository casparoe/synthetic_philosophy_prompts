Here's the half-formed angle I want to develop, and I'd like you to sharpen or replace it if you think it's wrong. I think people run together two things when they say "decomposition preserves alignment and competence," and they fail for different reasons. Call the first one the legibility tax: distillation has to compress the amplified procedure's output into something a single forward pass can produce, and what gets compressed away is mostly the *reasoning that made the answer trustworthy* — the subquestions asked, the criticisms considered, the aggregation. If alignment lived in the procedure rather than in the final string, then a lossy distillation may systematically discard exactly the part that was aligned, and we'd have no signal that this happened because the distilled model still produces the right-looking answer. Call the second the composition gap: the recursion assumes that correct answers to the subtasks compose into a correct answer to the parent task, but the decomposition is increasingly chosen by the system itself rather than the human, so the thing that has to be trustworthy is not the subtask answers but the *choice of decomposition*, and amplification gives that choice no separate training signal and no separate oversight.
