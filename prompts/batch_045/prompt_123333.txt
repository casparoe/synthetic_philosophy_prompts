Writing a one-page position statement for a reading group on whether history has a shape, and I'd rather have it torn apart now than in front of everyone. Roughly where I've landed:

1. Cyclical accounts are genuinely explanatory for pre-modern polities at about the level Ibn Khaldun works at — a cohesive group on the periphery takes power, the cohesion decays over a few generations of city life, and a more cohesive group replaces it.
2. But the "lessons of history" genre is mostly worthless: the cases don't repeat in the respects that matter, so nothing transfers to anything we decide now.
3. Since roughly 1800 there's been real cumulative growth in productive capacity, and it's a ratchet, not a phase of a cycle.
4. Spengler's cultures-with-fixed-lifespans and Polybius's regime cycle (kingship, tyranny, aristocracy, oligarchy, democracy, mob rule, round again) are both dead: the first is an organic metaphor with no mechanism behind it, the second is a classification of constitutions with a succession story bolted on.
5. Toynbee's challenge-and-response is fine as description but can't be falsified — any outcome whatsoever can be filed as a response to some challenge or other.

The way I keep 1 and 3 together is what I've started calling the two-series move: political cohesion can cycle on top of a productive base that ratchets, and "rise and fall" language conflates two series that come apart after industrialization. I genuinely can't tell whether that's a distinction of a dodge.

And separately, this might be the real crux: if someone handed you a single 2000-year series — tax receipts per head, say — I don't know what test would tell you it's cyclical rather than trending or just noisy, and I suspect a good chunk of the cycle-vs-linear argument is that problem in a costume.

So: which of 1-5 actually conflict with each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest revision? Rank the problems by how bad they are, and for each one say what I'd have to give up. If the two-series move is load-bearing, explain why; if it's a way of changing the subject, say that instead.
