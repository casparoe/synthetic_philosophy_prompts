As I take it from the draft paper she sent me, the view has three moving parts: first, the negative thesis in the spirit of al-Ghazālī's attack on the philosophers (the famous case of the fire and the cotton, where the burning is not produced by the fire but directly by God, and where the interpretive dispute about whether he denied causation outright or only necessary connection is one I'd like us to sidestep rather than settle); second, the claim that this is not Humeanism, since on her account the regularities are not brute but hold by divine decree — her stock line, which she used four times in the draft, is that 'Hume left the regularity unexplained and I don't'; and third, the genuinely new bit, namely that the manipulationist or interventionist accounts of causation that working scientists and philosophers of science use are not rivals to occasionalism but ride on top of it: what it is for A to cause B is for a hypothetical surgical intervention on A to be followed by a difference in B, and on her reading that counterfactual is simply a fact about which sequence of events God would directly produce were the intervention to occur — the intervention pushes nothing, it occasions. I've read the basics of occasionalism and of interventionist theories of causation, so the questions shouldn't spend time explaining either.

What I want from you is eight to ten interview questions, in the order I should ask them, each followed by a sentence sketching the answer she's most likely to give and then the follow-up that answer invites — I want to be a step ahead of her, not scrambling.
