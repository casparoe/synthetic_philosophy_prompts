I am writing a novel in which a young man, raised as an only child, learns that the embryo he grew from was chosen more or less arbitrarily from several fertilised in the same IVF cycle; the others were frozen and later discarded. In a late scene he tells his mother that if a different one of those embryos had been implanted, the child who grew up in that house would still have been him. She answers that a different embryo would simply have been a different person, someone who took his place and was mistaken for him. I need to know which of them has the better metaphysics on the page, and I would rather have a number than a survey of the literature.

Give me your credence in this proposition: had a numerically different sperm from the same father fertilised the same egg, the resulting child would have been that very individual, and not merely someone very like him. Then decompose it.

At a minimum I want your probabilities for:

1. that some form of origin essentialism is true, i.e. that a concrete organism could not have grown from entirely different originating gametes or matter;
2. that, if it is true, the requirement tracks the individual gametes themselves rather than the genetic profile they carry. The test case is a sperm from the father's monozygotic twin: near-identical genome, a different gamete from a different man. Does the resulting child inherit the identity anyway?
3. that the question has a determinate answer at all, rather than being settled only by a choice among rival treatments of transworld identity, so that whether that child would have been him is something to be discovered and not something to be stipulated;
4. that ordinary talk of who someone could have been is answerable to a different, interest-relative rule than metaphysical modality is, in which case the son's line may be defensible as something other than a claim about possible worlds.

Show how these combine into the number you gave. Then tell me which single term the final figure is most sensitive to, what argument or evidence would move that term furthest, and, in one line, which of my two characters you would let win the exchange.
