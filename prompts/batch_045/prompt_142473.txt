hey, so I'm a junior and I'm one of three students on our school's Conduct Board (its 2 teachers, 3 students, the dean, and our faculty advisor Ms. Okonjo). I have to write the opinion the board will vote on this Thursday and im kind of stuck on it.

The case: a senior, Marcus, stole another student's laptop over spring break and sold it, then lied about it for about two weeks to his friends and to us. In the middle of all this he got an MRI for something unrelated and they found a cyst pushing on the part of his brain involved in impulse control. The neurologist's note says it may have been effecting him for months. Its been removed, he's medicated now, and he says he's fine.

Three things are making this hard. Priya (whose laptop it was) says what she wants from us is for the board to say out loud that Marcus did it and that it was wrong, and that if we turn the whole thing into a medical matter we're telling her nothing real happened to her and that he isn't someone who owes her anything. Marcus said at his hearing, basically, don't give me the therapy excuse, I did it, give me what you'd give anyone. And Ms. Okonjo keeps pushing this two-hat rule: she says at the hearing we should relate to him as a fully responsible agent (hear his reasons, accept his apology, tell him the school is angry on Priya's behalf), but then when we set the consequence we should switch to a purely forward-looking, managing mode — what keeps him well and keeps this from happening again, with no payback element in it at all. Half the board says that's just having it both ways: either you blame him or you dont, and blame with a consequence that has no bite is just theater. Our counselor said something different again — that telling someone we've stopped blaming him and are only managing him is itself something we'd be doing to him, and it can land on him worse than a punishment would.

Also honestly half the teachers seem angry mostly because the whole thing got posted online and there's a question about whether an expulsion has to be reported to colleges, so idk how much of the boards anger is even about Priya.

Can you draft the opinion I circulate? It should say what we should actually do, with dates — what the consequence is, when it starts, when we reconvene, what goes in the letter to Priya and in the letter to Marcus — and it should take Priya's point seriously instead of just concluding he was sick, and it should give the strongest version of whatever goes against your own recommendation, because Marcus's dad is going to read it and take it apart if its mushy.
