I teach a graduate seminar in philosophy of biology, and I want the unit on the units-of-selection debate built around a diagnostic handout rather than around a reading. Please write that handout: a decision tree that takes a student from a bare description of a population and a changing trait to a named position, constructed from the logical space of possible answers rather than from a list of doctrines.

Requirements.

1. The root split should be about the question, not the answer. Before anything else, sort readers by what they take 'which unit?' to mean: which level gives a coherent bookkeeping of the change; which level's causal powers produced it, i.e. which counterfactual interventions would have altered the outcome; or which entity, if any, the trait is adaptively for. Readers answering different questions should diverge at the top and never be forced through the same downstream nodes.

2. Seven branch nodes in total. Each must be phrased as a claim or a concrete case with two or three answer options, answerable by someone who has never heard the labels: no node may ask whether one is a 'multi-level selectionist' or whether groups 'really' reproduce. Prefer counterfactual and intervention formulations (what would change if you moved this individual, split this group, froze this variance, suppressed this transmission route) over covariance formulations, and say explicitly where the counterfactual test returns no verdict. Where a node separates two positions only by convention or taste rather than by a difference in what anyone expects to observe, flag it as a stipulation rather than pretending it does substantive work.

3. Ten to fourteen leaves, each with the name of the position, or 'unnamed' if nobody defends it, and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. These must all appear: gene-level monism of the selfish-gene sort (only lineages with the requisite fidelity can be units, everything else is a vehicle or an aggregate); gene-level pluralism (several independent inheritance channels, so genes are not privileged); organism-level selection read off personal fitness; organism-level selection with inclusive fitness, i.e. related partners' effects folded into the individual's own fitness; transient or trait-group views, on which groups matter only as interaction neighbourhoods; multi-level selection of the group-affects-average-individual-fitness sort; multi-level selection of the groups-reproduce sort; the position on which apparent group selection is only a redescription of individual-level processes in a structured population; the superorganism or emergent-colony position, on which the colony is the organism and its members are parts; sub-organismal selection, with intragenomic conflict as the case; species- or clade-level selection on lineage-level traits; the pluralist position on which every coherent partition is an equally good description of one process; the deflationary position on which selection is an algorithm that runs wherever variation, heredity and differential output obtain, so that asking after the unit is vacuous or merely context-relative; and the position on which the interaction or remainder term is a real causal contribution attributable to no single level.

4. These cases must do the sorting work, each used as a branch node or as a probe beneath one: a eusocial colony with sterile workers and a colony-level trait such as nest thermoregulation, where within-group variance among the workers is zero; a sentinel or alarm-calling trait in a flock that assembles, interacts and dissolves within a season, with no group reproduction; a driving genetic element or organelle whose transmission advantage lowers the fitness of the organism that carries it; a species-level trait such as dispersal mode that affects speciation or extinction rates with no population-genetic mechanism underneath; a two-level partition that leaves a nonzero cross-level remainder; and a matched-swap intervention, in which exchanging half the members of two groups leaves both groups' trait values and outputs unchanged.

5. One case I want used twice, because it is the one my students always mishandle. Stipulate, for the purposes of the handout, an organism of the following kind: individuals aggregate each generation into feeding groups drawn from a spatially structured propagule pool; the groups never reproduce, dissolving at the end of the season and being reconstituted by fresh sampling; a group-level trait, say the ratio of soma to reproductive cells in the aggregate, varies between groups, causally affects the number of propagules the group releases, and is partly predictable from the composition of the nearby propagule source, so that high-soma groups tend to be followed by high-soma groups locally. There is thus between-group variance, group-level causal efficacy and something close to group-level heritability, but no group lineage and no group reproduction. Ask the heritability-versus-reproduction question at one node and the intervention question at another, and report whether the tree sends this case to the same leaf by both routes. If it does not, say which route you trust and why.

6. Two short closing blocks after the tree. First, the pairs of leaves your tree cannot separate, with the reason in each case: formal equivalence, absence of any discriminating observation, or a shared commitment that does all the work. I expect the gene-level and inclusive-fitness descriptions, and the two multi-level descriptions, to be candidates here, but give the ones your tree actually has. Second, the cells in the logical space that your branches allow but that no position occupies, each with a one-line diagnosis: is nobody there because the combination is incoherent, because it is coherent but unattractive, or because it is merely unexplored.

7. Length: about a thousand words, dense rather than padded, laid out as a handout a student could work through in ten minutes. Begin the response with the tree itself, the root node on the first line, with no preamble, no restatement of what I have asked for and no commentary before it; if you disagree with my framing, register that in a short note after the tree, not before it.
