so I've more or less come around to the Dennett-ish line on color phi, and I want to know how exposed it actually is. The setup, so we're on the same page: flash a red dot at one location, then a green dot some distance away maybe a tenth of a second later, and people report a single dot that glides across and changes color partway along the path — i.e. they report the color change as happening before the green dot has even been shown. The move I've accepted is that there are two stories you can tell about this (you really did see red-ish motion and then the memory of it got quietly edited; or nothing was conscious until after the green flash and the whole episode, including its apparent timing, got laid down in one go), and that there is no fact of the matter which one happened, because nothing anyone could ever measure would tell them apart. And I hold the stronger, general version too: for no case at all — phi, backward masking, the cutaneous rabbit, flash-lag, the Libet stuff — is there an empirically discoverable answer to when in processing a stimulus became conscious.

What would count against that? Not the general grumble that the question is confused. I want defeaters ranked fatal to merely awkward, including ones that have arguably already shown up, and I want you to distinguish what a committed defender would themselves concede from what only critics shout. Three things I keep circling on:

The click probe. Stick a brief click at a variable delay in the same trial and have people judge whether the click came before or after the dot changed color. Say the point of subjective simultaneity lands well before the second flash — people treat the color change as happening at a time when the green stimulus didn't exist yet. Have I now got a real-time fix on when the change was experienced, or is the obvious reply (the comparison itself, including where the click is placed in time, is reconstructed after the fact) enough to make the whole design uninformative? Is there any version of a cross-modal probe that isn't?

The online decoder. Train a classifier on MEG to discriminate experiences of red from experiences of green, run it time-locked on phi trials. If it flips to green at the second flash and never before — is that evidence for anything, given both stories seem to predict it? If it flips early, mid-trajectory, who's embarrassed and how badly? And does a neural readout even count as a measure of the timing of experience for someone holding this view, or is that the exact thing they deny?

The amnesic subject. The Orwellian horn is a story about memory getting rewritten. Take someone with dense anterograde amnesia who still does phi and still reports a mid-path color change. Is there anything left for the Orwellian story to be a story about? And if one horn collapses, does the no-fact-of-the-matter claim go with it, or does it just get reworded? I might be butchering this one, so tell me if I am.

Also: does the view risk anything at all — does it predict any result its rivals don't, or does every finding get absorbed? And is the general version more or less exposed than the phi-specific one? What would move me most is a case where the two stories differ in something the subject does rather than says, landing before the window closes. If you think no such case is constructible, say whether that's a discovery about the mind or just what the words mean by then — and whether that's a problem for a claim of this kind.
