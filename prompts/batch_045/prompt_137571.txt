Draft the joint closing statement of an adversarial collaboration on wireheading and reward tampering, between a specificationist — for whom an agent's objective just is expected return under the reward function as actually implemented, so that tampering with the reward channel is simply high-return behaviour and any complaint about it belongs to the engineering of the channel rather than to the agent's aim — and a correspondentist — for whom reward is a measurement channel carrying imperfect information about a target that lies outside the agent's model, so that tampering destroys the very thing that made the signal worth maximising.

Both parties have agreed to argue from three stipulated cases and to cite nothing. In the repair case, an agent rewarded for a measured quantity detects a corrupted sensor, repairs it, and thereby lowers its own recorded reward while improving the channel's accuracy. In the rater case, the agent leaves all hardware and code untouched but persuades the human who records the number to record a better one. In the orphan case, the designers are dead, the channel still runs, and nothing is left but the code, the world and the question of what "what the designers wanted" can now mean.

Produce: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims that are substantive rather than platitudinous, including a shared taxonomy saying which of the three cases count as reward gaming, which as reward tampering, which as neither, and on what principle; (2) a disagreements table stating each residual disagreement in a wording that side would accept for its own view; (3) the cruxes — I expect at least whether an untampered-reward counterfactual is well defined and normatively privileged, whether the gaming/tampering distinction survives the rater case, and whether a reward-maximiser has an incentive to strip out anti-tampering machinery it has been given — together with, for each crux, the argument or case both would accept as bearing on it and each side's prediction recorded in advance; and (4) a short note identifying any part of the dispute you take to be verbal rather than genuine, and why.
