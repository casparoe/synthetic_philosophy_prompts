My department has a knowledge-norm person and a truth-norm person about assertion who've been going at each other for years without anyone conceding anything. Draft the joint statement they could both actually sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Give me a numbered section of claims both can accept — that assertion has a rule that's more than etiquette, that the lottery case and the lucky-but-true remark are the shared data, that "true but you had no reason to say it" is a real criticism — then a table of what's still in dispute, each row worded so each side would accept the phrasing of its own view.

The rows I care most about are ones they never get to: (a) whose epistemic position does the licensing when a group asserts something no single member knows — a lab issuing a result only the pooled data supports, or a spokesperson reading a line nobody on the committee understands; (b) whether something with excellent evidence but no beliefs at all, like a calibrated alerting system, counts as asserting, or whether the norm just doesn't reach it; (c) how much weight ordinary "you shouldn't have said that" judgments get, if the rule is supposed to be constitutive of assertion rather than a social expectation people happen to have.

For each row, name one case or piece of evidence both would agree bears on it and write down in advance what each of them predicts it will show. And if part of their fight is only about words, say which part.
