I'm setting the final for an upper-level seminar on G. E. Moore's *Principia Ethica*, and I want one question on the closing chapter — where he ranks the pleasures of friendship and the enjoyment of beautiful things as by far the two greatest intrinsic goods — that can't be answered by restating the organic-unity doctrine or by running the open-question argument again. This has become personally uncomfortable for me: for twenty-odd years a friend and I went to the same concert hall together twice a month, and she is now in a care home, deaf, still insisting that we go. So I keep wanting an answer to something Moore is oddly well placed to address: whether two people enjoying one and the same beautiful object together, each aware of the other's enjoyment and of its being shared, is a good that is more than — or merely other than — the sum of two solitary appreciations plus an affection. Call it the duet problem.

What I want from you:

1. The exam question itself, written for a 45-minute in-class essay. It should stipulate the duet case and require students to use three pieces of Moorean machinery: the method of absolute isolation, the claim that the value of a whole need not equal the sum of the values of its parts, and Moore's suggestion that these states of consciousness are partly constituted by objects that really exist and really are as they are taken to be. The question should force them into a tension: isolating one friend's enjoyment from the other's seems to destroy the very thing being evaluated, while the existence condition seems to do no work in the shared case that it doesn't already do in the solitary one.

2. A model answer of roughly 700 words that takes a position rather than surveying the options.

3. A marking scheme out of 20, with five must-hit moves, the marks for each, and one line on how students typically get each one wrong. One of the five should concern Moore's doubts about whether doubling the amount of a good doubles its value, and why that cuts against the natural way of summing the duet.

The whole point is to separate students who have genuinely understood Moore's axiology from those who have only memorized its labels, so please build the question around the crux rather than around coverage.
