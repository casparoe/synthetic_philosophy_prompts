I write backend code for a living and read changelogs instead of books, so I have zero philosophy background and apologies if this is a thing with a name. Details kind of are the point here, sorry.

Seven months until I turn 35 and I want to run a 5k before then. Every Sunday night I am totally sincere about it. I lay out the shorts, I put the alarm across the room, I feel excited. Tuesday at 5:50am the alarm goes off and some other guy takes over. That guy is extreamly good at arguing. Just skip today, hit Thursday and Saturday, still three runs. Then Thursday comes and he says the same thing about Saturday. In seven months he has never once honored the same deal twice in a row.

Some things I noticed that feel like they matter but I don't know how:

I built myself a small app. If the phone hasn't seen a run by 7:30 it charges me $40 and emails a screenshot of my sad sedentary morning to my mom, who will 100% bring it up at Thanksgiving. It works, sort of. Turns out the threat only works if I decide far enough ahead. If at 6pm I decide "run at 6:20," I just do it, no drama. If the run is right now, I fold. Same person, same tired legs, twenty minutes apart. So which one is me? It doesn't feel like one of them is stupider or missing information. The 5:50am guy knows he'll feel great afterwards, he'll admit he'll feel great afterwards, he doesn't care, and then ninety minutes later he's annoyed about it.

The reward version of the app failed way worse: I'd skip the run and then also not log the skip. I was lying to my own script. I have also started leaving my phone charging in the kitchen, despite being the person who wrote the app.

Also, does it matter that the only reason I'm currently running is that I don't want my mom getting an email? Is a run I did out of spite still, like, my run? My roommate says I don't have a self-control problem, I want different things at different times and there's no fact of the matter about which one is the real me, and Sunday-night me is basically a dictator ruling Tuesday-morning me. My brother says if I actually wanted it I'd just do it.

I'm about to upgrade to the hard version: a real signed contract with my roommate, $200 a miss, no takebacks. And I realized I don't know whether I'm allowed to do that to myself or whether it's even me doing it. What do people who think about this sort of thing actually say, and what would you do with the app?
