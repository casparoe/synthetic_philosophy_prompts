I help run a tiny charity that gives out one-off grants to families in crisis, and we can fund maybe a third of the applicants each round, so we sit round a table and rank people by need. Everyone does this fluently and treats it as a factual question with a right answer. Then I also host the monthly drop-in where the applicants all meet each other, and there the exact same comparisons get treated as impossible and a bit rude — you can't compare, everyone's situation is different — and nobody thinks that's a claim we could settle. Same people, same kind of judgement, two totally different stances on whether it can be done at all.

Three things I cant sort out. One: is there a name for that split, comparisons being licensed when a committee makes them about strangers and unlicensed when they're made between the people involved? "You can't compare" sounds like a factual claim but it seems to switch on and off depending on who's talking.

Two: in the committee we slide between two questions that I think are actually different — who is worst off right now, and who would get the most out of the money. They come apart: the person in the worst shape may be past helping much, and two thousand pounds barely registers for someone whose problems are enormous. Does that distinction have a name? Half the time I think the whole can't-compare worry only attaches to one of them and the other one is fine, but I can't say which or why.

Three: applicants can come and speak to us, and when they do we write down what they say but we weight income, diagnoses and rent arrears much more heavily, even though what we're supposedly ranking is how things are for them. That feels like two mistakes stacked on top of each other and I can't pull them apart.

A friend in health policy says her whole field runs on scoring treatments by the years of life they buy, adjusted for how bad those years are, so this isn't just us.

Is any of this an established thing, and if so which part? I don't want a label, I want to know what the phenomenon is, what it isn't, and where its edges are. If there are near-miss concepts someone might think I mean, tell me those and how mine differs from each. If there's no name for it, coin one and give me the boundary cases. Under 400 words please.
