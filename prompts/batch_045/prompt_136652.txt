My friend's mom runs the county animal shelter and she asked me to say a few words at their spring fundraiser brunch — like four minutes, in a room of donors with their kids, volunteers, the vet who does the euthanasias, and half my school. I'm 17 and I've been reading a lot about suffering-focused ethics, the idea that stopping something from hurting matters way more than making something already-fine a bit happier, and I mostly believe it. Which is a problem here: the blunt version of that view is something like "it would be better if a lot of these animals had never been born," and I can't say that at an adoption fundraiser, and I definately can't say anything that makes the vet who put animals down this morning sound like a murderer, or that tells the donors the pug they rescued was a mistake.

What I want the speech built around is a distinction I keep coming back to and haven't seen named anywhere: there's suffering you can reach — the one with a name, a photo, and a donation page — and there's suffering nobody's donor base will ever cover because it doesn't have a face yet, which is why I think the clinic's spay/neuter work is the actual heart of the place even though it gets none of the cute pictures. Please write it so that distinction carries the whole thing, so it's honest without saying the quiet part out loud, and so it doesn't collapse into either a donation pitch or "every tail deserves a home." No quotes and no statistics — I'll cringe and lose my place if I have to read numbers out.
