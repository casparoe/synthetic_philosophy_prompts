I build forecasting models for a living — mostly state-space and regime-switching models fitted to messy panel data, all of it self-taught, with no training in history or philosophy. Lately I keep meeting the claim that large-scale history can be quantified, modelled, and even predicted: researchers assemble century-scale series for things like population, real wages, state revenue, and some proxy for the number of aspirants competing for elite positions, fit a dynamical model with a few latent variables to them, and report that the result both reconstructs well-known periods of state breakdown and shows the same wave repeating with a period of two or three centuries.

I would like this claim rebuilt in the vocabulary I actually use, and then a report on what the rebuilding loses. Concretely: first, restate as faithfully as you can what such a model is asserting, using only my terms — latent state, observation and transition equations, identifiability, non-stationarity, regime duration, in-sample fit against out-of-sample scoring, degrees of freedom spent on model selection, comparison against a naive baseline. Second, give me the residue: which parts of the macrohistorical claim translate cleanly, which only go through if distorted, and which cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all — I am thinking of things like a mechanism that is supposed to operate over centuries, or the claim that two widely separated episodes are instances of the same cycle, but report whatever residue you actually find. Third, say which of my tools have no counterpart on their side of the translation.

Two points I especially want your view on. With only a handful of completed cycles in the record, and every episode differing from the last in institutions, technology, and how well it was measured, is anything here identified, or is the cycle length in practice fixed after the fact? And a model that can always re-situate the present somewhere along a long wave seems very hard to falsify: how would I distinguish, in my own terms, between that and a model genuinely forecasting, and is there anything in the forecasting toolkit that would settle the difference?

Please also say, of each piece of residue, whether it is a blind spot in my framework or a sign that the original claim is less clear than it sounds. I do not want a reading list or citations — I cannot check them anyway — only the translation and what it fails to carry.
