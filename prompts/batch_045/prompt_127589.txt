I am giving a colloquium talk next month to a mathematics department, and I would like a Q&A preparation sheet. The room will be mostly analysts — several working in partial differential equations and numerical analysis, one in Banach space theory — together with a set theorist who regards the whole debate as settled by topos semantics, and a computer scientist in dependent type theory who will be sympathetic but impatient with anything I overstate.

My thesis is that the cost of giving up the law of excluded middle in analysis has been systematically misdescribed. Instead of counting the classical theorems a constructivist loses, I propose to grade each one by the weakest omniscience principle that suffices to recover it: the limited principle of omniscience, according to which every binary sequence either is identically zero or has a nonzero term — equivalently, trichotomy for the reals; the lesser limited principle of omniscience, according to which every binary sequence with at most one nonzero term has its nonzero term, if any, at an even or at an odd index — equivalently, every real is ≥ 0 or ≤ 0; and beneath both of these, Markov's principle, ¬¬∃n P(n) → ∃n P(n) for decidable P on the naturals. My claim is that most of what a working analyst actually invokes requires no more than dichotomy, so the familiar verdict that constructive mathematics cannot support real analysis rests on a small and local set of theorems. I illustrate with the intermediate value theorem, whose constructive substitute is only approximate — for every ε > 0 there is an x with |f(x)| < ε — and with the least-upper-bound principle and its Brouwerian counterexamples.

Give me the eight or so objections I will in fact get from this audience, in their idiom rather than mine, ordered by how much damage each would do, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is soft. One point I know is exposed: the analytic forms of these principles imply the arithmetical ones outright, while the converses require weak countable choice, and I am unsure how much of my grading survives if that is withdrawn. Include, separately, the objection I am hoping nobody asks.
