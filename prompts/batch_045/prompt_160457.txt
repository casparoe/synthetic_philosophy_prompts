I sit on my university's dining procurement and sustainability committee. We are renewing the wholesale contract for the main dining hall, and the live option is to shift roughly two-thirds of our beef volume to chicken: the same menu slots, the same price point, and a large reduction in the emissions we report per kilogram of protein served.

A student group has asked us to decide the question by expected vertebrate deaths per thousand kilocalories instead, and we do not want to wave this away. Their reasoning: beef's far worse feed-conversion ratio puts far more hectares under annual cultivation, so more mice, voles, rabbits and ground-nesting birds die to ploughing, harvest and rodent control on the grain supplier's fields; but chicken is one small bird per few servings while a steer is one large animal spread over thousands, so a switch that lowers field deaths raises the count of domesticated animals killed by a large factor. Two further complications are on the table. The beef comes from rotational grazing on marginal pasture that our own biodiversity office counts as semi-natural habitat, so the land-use comparison is not simply crop against crop. And the money saved by switching could instead buy pre-harvest deterrence plus unharvested set-aside strips on the grain supplier's land — an option that appears to sever the link between buying less beef and sparing field animals.

Treat the above as stipulated; do not try to settle the empirical literature. Draft the decision memo I will circulate before the vote. It should: recommend one course of action with a timeline; say what currency the committee should actually be ranking options in — emissions, expected deaths, expected suffering weighted by the capacities of the animals involved, deaths we cause against deaths we merely fail to prevent — and defend that choice; say what the deterrence-and-set-aside option does to the argument for switching, and whether it undercuts the student group's case or completes it; and state plainly which numbers the recommendation is most sensitive to and which uncertainties are not a reason to postpone deciding. Give the strongest version of the option you reject.
