I've been hand-lettering a lowercase alphabet for a school project, and I keep running into something I don't have a word for.

The terminal on my *a*, the ear of my *g*, and the tail of my *y* all strike me as the same stroke — not merely similar strokes, but one and the same object appearing three times. They point in different directions, sit at different heights, and are different sizes, and when I tried making them literally identical the alphabet looked wrong. But when I changed the *a*'s terminal to a flat horizontal cut, the *g* and the *y* immediately looked wrong too, though I hadn't touched them, and I felt I had to go change them. It cascades: I switched to a single-story *g*, which forced changes in the *a* and the *y*, which made the *s* look foreign, which made the *e* look too wide.

What I can't pin down is that I can't state the style as a list of rules. Every rule I write down has exceptions I can see instantly but can't formulate, and the exceptions don't feel arbitrary — the rule feels inexact rather than false. My friends can tell at a glance which letter is "off" in a set, and none of them can say how they know.

Is there a name for this? Not *style* or *consistency*: consistency would be satisfied by making those three strokes identical, which is exactly what looks wrong. Is there an established concept for whatever makes two different marks in different letters the *same* stroke, and makes a change to one propagate through the others? If nothing quite fits, I'd want a term with a definition and boundary cases — cases where it nearly applies but doesn't. And I'd especially like to know whether this is the same problem as what makes every *a* an *a*, or a separate one. I'm not after a label; I want to know what the thing is and what it isn't.
