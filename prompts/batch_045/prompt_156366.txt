I'm drafting a short position paper for a graduate seminar in philosophy of mathematics, and what I need is the argumentative core written out, not a literature survey or a tutorial. Assume a reader who knows the standard statement of Zorn's lemma and its usual applications but has never thought about how it is packaged.

Please argue for the following position, in the strongest form you can give it.

Thesis: Zorn's lemma is mispackaged as a principle about maximal elements. What it does in mathematical practice is guarantee the stabilization of a certain transfinite process, and the maximal-element formulation disguises both what a Zorn proof requires and what it does not.

I want the case built around three distinctions I have half-worked out. Sharpen them, or replace them with better ones if you can.

1. Uniform versus non-uniform bounding. Call an application bound-explicit when there is a single operation U assigning to each chain C an upper bound U(C) — typically the union of C, or the closure of that union — so that no choice is needed to produce the bound at all. In the applications that dominate practice (Hamel bases, maximal ideals containing a given ideal, maximal consistent extensions, Hahn–Banach extensions), the entire mathematical content sits in the chain-closure lemma: the constraint defining the relevant subposet survives U. Maximality then follows from a stabilization argument that is indifferent to what the constraint is.

2. Relative maximality. Most applications want an object maximal among those satisfying a constraint, and the standard trick of restricting to the subposet of constraint-satisfying elements is legitimate only because the constraint survives U. Maximality is thus an artifact of the setup rather than an extra assumption supplied by the lemma.

3. Where the choice actually goes. Given U and a progress operator assigning to each non-terminal element a strict successor inside the subposet, the transfinite iteration that takes successor steps by the progress operator and limit steps by U is fully deterministic; it must stabilize, since otherwise it would inject the ordinals into a set, which Hartogs's theorem rules out. Stabilization is maximality. So the only choice in the proof lies in the progress operator: there is no further choice made at the top. The familiar complaint that Zorn's lemma is non-constructive therefore conflates two separable defects — non-canonicity of the object produced, which is real and is a fact about the multiplicity of maximal objects, and the alleged impossibility of reaching one, which is largely illusory and is entirely a matter of how the progress operator is obtained.
