I got into a three-hour argument with a friend last night — he's in a philosophy department and does the experimental-philosophy-adjacent thing where you hand the same vignette to people in different countries and see whether the verdicts come out different — and I still cant tell where it went wrong, so I want to try running it in my own vocabulary.

Background on me: I've spent about twelve years doing cross-national survey work, mostly attitude batteries with some item response modelling. The vocabulary I actually think in is: latent construct, indicator, measurement invariance (configural, metric, scalar), differential item functioning, anchoring vignettes, response styles like acquiescence and extreme responding, nomological net and construct validity, reliability and standard error of measurement, intraclass correlation and clustering, sampling frame and coverage error. I know basically nothing about the philosophy side — I've read maybe two of these papers — so please don't asume I know the positions by name, and don't give me a reading list; I just want the translation.

Here's the ask. Take the debate about whether cross-cultural variation in philosophical intuitions should make us stop treating those intuitions as evidence. The version that says variation is a reason for suspicion and for restricting their use. The reply that the variation isn't really in the intuition at all but in how people are hearing the case or what they're silently filling in. The expertise-flavored reply. And the view that says the intuitions were only ever evidence about our concepts, so variation isn't a threat but the thing being measured. Restate each of those, as faithfully as you can, in my vocabulary — construct, item, invariance, DIF, bias, validity — and for each one say explicitly what it would have to be claiming about the measure for its argument to actually go through.

Then the residue report: which claims carry over without loss, which translate only if you quietly change them (tell me what you changed), and which have no expression in my vocabulary at all. I have a guess about the last category: nothing in my vocabulary can say that a response is evidence for a fact that isn't a property of the responders or of the construct. My whole framework asks whether responses track a latent attribute, and "tracks the truth about knowledge itself" seems to have no slot in it. Is that right, or am I overstating it? And if it is right, is that a hole in survey methodology or a sign that the philosophers' claim is shakier than it sounds?

One more thing, because this is what we actually ended up shouting about. Suppose a study came out like this: the marginals are basically identical — the same share of people in each population say no, that's not knowledge, in a Gettier-ish case — but the correlates are completely different. In one population the response loads with the rest of the epistemic battery and with independent checks of how carefully people read the vignette; in the other it loads with acquiescence and a social-desirability scale and correlates with nothing epistemic. In my terms that's scalar invariance with different nomological nets: same answers, different thing generating them. Does the philosophical debate have any way to see that as a difference, or does the whole argument run on marginals and go blind here? If it does go blind, does that hurt the skeptical argument more than an ordinary finding of different marginals would? And is there any version of the that's-not-real-variation reply that survives this case, or does that reply only get traction when the marginals differ?
