I work in mainstream analytic epistemology, mostly on the modal conditions on knowledge, and there is a pattern I keep running into in my own writing that I cannot label cleanly. I would like help carving it up, not a name on its own and not a map of the literature.

The pattern is this. A subject has a true belief that satisfies the subjunctive condition a tracking account asks for: had the proposition been false, she would not have believed it. But it satisfies that condition by default, so to speak. The nearest worlds in which the proposition is false are worlds in which she, or her method, or the mechanism that would have to produce the belief, is simply absent. Nothing in those worlds makes her withhold; she is not in a position to withhold. The counterfactual profile is exactly the one the account demands, but the explanation of the profile is not the one the account was supposed to be capturing. The belief is not answering to the way the world is. It is merely unable to outlive it.

Three cases I use in seminar. First, an astrophysicist who concludes, on the basis of a long instrumental chain, that the universe is very old: had it been young, there would have been no instruments, no training, no investigator, and no belief. The profile is satisfied and the verdict is the one I want. Second, a pathologist who concludes that a death was a homicide: had it been natural, no inquiry would have been opened and she would have formed no view at all. Now suppose the review board is changed so that it audits a random five percent of natural deaths. Her evidence is untouched, but sensitivity now fails, because in the nearest worlds where the death was natural she investigates anyway and reaches the same conclusion. Her knowledge appears to turn on a bureaucratic sampling policy. Third, a court propagandist who believes, on party authority alone, that the regime is stable: had it been unstable, he would have been shot before forming any view. Everything about the case is rotten, and the condition is met.

So the phenomenon cuts both ways: sometimes obstruction is benign, sometimes pernicious, and the modal profile is the same in both. What I want is the carving. Is there an established handle for this? It feels near, but not identical, to the familiar vacuity worry about necessary truths, where the nearest false-worlds are empty; near the method-relative and were-to-use-M machinery in the official formulation of tracking, which is arguably designed to defeat exactly this; near the complaints that sensitivity is at once too strong and too weak; near the basing relation and the literature on deviant chains; and near, in a quite different neighborhood, the Frankfurt-style case, where the alternative possibility is removed by something other than the agent's own responsiveness. Each of these seems to me to capture part of the phenomenon and leave part out, and I want the differences stated precisely rather than gestured at. If nothing fits, say so and give me a term, a definition, and the boundary cases that put pressure on it, including cases that are nearly instances and are not.

I also want the carving to earn its keep on a question I actually care about. My tentative line is that the standard method-clause fix does not dissolve the pattern so much as relocate it: if the method is specified finely, obstruction is blocked, but then the counterfactual antecedent requires the use of a method whose very operation depends on the truth of the proposition at issue, and we are evaluating something very close to a counterpossible; if the method is specified coarsely, obstruction returns. And this matters for the closure dialectic rather than merely for sufficiency, because the anti-skeptical use of non-closure needs the tracking conditions to fail for the denial of the skeptical hypothesis while holding for the ordinary proposition, for the right kind of reason. If obstructed sensitivity can be manufactured cheaply for some propositions and is unavailable for exactly the propositions the skeptic cares about, then either the skeptic can grant us every instance of it and still deny that anything we believe is responsive to the world, or the phenomenon is epiphenomenal to the closure argument and I should stop worrying about it. Tell me which, and tell me what test separates the benign obstructions from the pernicious ones if the subjunctive profile cannot.
