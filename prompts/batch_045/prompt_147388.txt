Scenerio: it's 2097 and someone finally publishes a derivation of the Born rule from inside the many-worlds framework that nobody can find a flaw in. Given the branching structure and some pretty weak constraints on rational preference, an agent who doesn't want to be money-pumped has to weight branches by squared amplitude, and everyone agrees the math is right. My question is whether that would actually close the problem of probability.

Give me a number: your credence that, in that situation, the many-worlds picture is in as good shape on probability as a single-world theory where the outcome really is chancy at the chance the amplitude specifies. Then decompose it. I want the terms you're multiplying through: whether a derivation like that is proving the right kind of thing at all (it constrains preferences, but does it tell you anything about what you'll see, or how much your future branch-mates matter to you now); whether it smuggles probability in through the rationality axioms rather than getting it out; whether talk of how much to care even makes sense when there will be many successors of you rather than one; whether branches are sharply enough defined for their amplitudes to be exact, or whether decoherence only gives approximate branches and the weights inherit that vagueness; and whether the weights can be read as anything like a share of your future selves once the number of branches is infinite, or continuum-sized, so that ratios of counts stop making sense.

Give each term a number and one line of justification, say how they combine, and tell me which term your final number is most sensitive to. Then give a separate number for the special case where the world turns out to be finite-dimensional with only finitely many branches above any non-neglible amplitude: I suspect most of the worry is really about the infinite case, and I want to know how much. My own credence for the general case is around 0.6 and my supervisor says 0.9; if that gap is tracking a genuine disagreement, tell me which sub-question we most likely disagree about, and what sort of result would move your number the most.
