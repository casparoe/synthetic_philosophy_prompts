I've been going in circles on something in the metaphysics of what I'll call ontology identification, and I want to try reflective equilibrium on it rather than read another paper about it. So: verdicts first, principle wanted.

Setup. There's a target system we only ever meet through a fixed interface — call it the channel. The channel gives us (i) a finite set of readouts at each time step, (ii) a finite menu of interventions we can perform (knob settings, plus doing nothing), (iii) a fixed time resolution. Ontology identification is the task of recovering, from everything the channel could in principle give us — unlimited samples, every sequence of interventions on the menu, run forever — what the system is made of: how many variables, what their state spaces are, and what the dynamics is, i.e. which variables depend on which and via what functions.

There will always be information about the system that no amount of channel output discriminates: two candidate ontologies can agree on every accessible trajectory under every available intervention while genuinely disagreeing about what is going on in there. Sometimes I think that means "harmless redundancy, ignore it"; sometimes I think it means "we don't know what the system is." I can't get the the distinction right, so here are my cases.

1. Candidate B is candidate A with two hidden variables permuted and the dynamics relabeled to match. Verdict: same ontology, nothing to choose. Same for any state-space isomorphism.

2. H is a deterministic function of the other variables and nothing depends on it. Verdict: not real, throw it out, and I feel no pull toward agnosticism here.

3. H is undetermined by the dynamics and affects nothing ever — the modeler can set it to anything, like a gauge. Verdict: not part of the ontology, an artifact of the representation. (Though I'm not sure 2 and 3 are the same kind of not-real, and I'd like to know if the difference matters.)

4. Gas in a box; the channel gives only pressure and temperature and lets me move a piston. We posit ~10^23 particles. Verdict: obviously the ontology. Inaccessibility in practice is no bar, and in fact we get indirect purchase through fluctuation behavior, so this one feels easy.

5. Bookkeeping case: the accessible quantities obey a conservation law, and the books don't balance unless something unseen is carrying the remainder. Verdict: identified and real, even though the carrier itself is never read. It's pinned down by what it carries, not by being observed.

6. Shadow sector (my name, possibly just a name for my confusion): H is dynamically active — the evolution of accessible variables depends on it — but its influence is exactly cancelled by a compensating parameter, so every accessible trajectory matches the H-free model. Not isomorphic to it, though. Verdict: this is a real disagreement about what's in there; I won't say "no difference," and I won't say "both true," and I also can't see what could ever settle it.

7. Measurement incompatibility: reading H would require interacting with the system in a way that resets the very register I'd be reading. I can describe the intervention; I can't perform it. Verdict: H is real if the dynamics says so, the ontology question is perfectly well posed, we are merely data-poor.

8. Washed-out initial conditions: a variable set once at startup whose only effects are erased by mixing within a hundred steps, while my channel doesn't switch on until step thousand. Verdict, and this is the one I distrust: I find myself saying "don't count it, no fact of the matter." But structurally 7 and 8 are the same — information definite in the model and inaccessible to me — and I don't want to be inconsistent.

9. Embedded observer: a subsystem inside the box has H in its input map, and I can see that subsystem's reports through the channel, though I can neither set nor read H myself. Verdict: H is real. But note what this does: if "accessible to someone" is what makes it real, then 6 should have come out the other way; if dynamic activity is what does the work, then the embedded observer's access is idle. Either way one of my reasons is doing nothing.

10. Saftey case: H in {safe, lethal} determines whether intervention I kills the subject, is dynamically active, and no accessible evidence discriminates. Verdict: I must act as if it might be lethal, but I resist saying the stakes make it more real — and yet I'm much more reluctant to say "no fact of the matter" here than in 8, and I don't know whether that's tracking existence or just risk.

A grad student told me I'm running together "is there more in there?" with "could we ever pin down which?" I half believe her and half don't: I can't see how separating them saves both 6 and 8.

Two I'm genuinely torn on: (a) the self-referential one, where the register that stores the identifier's own model of the system is itself a variable inside the system and the channel can't output its full contents — well-posed but hard, or does the question move when you ask it, because identifying it changes it? (b) the multiscale one, where coarse time resolution yields three variables with nonlinear dynamics and fine resolution yields five with nearly linear dynamics, both exact at their own resolution and neither obviously reducible to the other — is the ontology resolution-relative, which feels like giving up, or is there a unique finest grain, which may not exist?

What I want: one criterion, stated up front as a single principle before you go through the cases, that sorts these — not a list of intuitions. If it is really two-part (a condition for there being anything more at all, and a separate one for identifiability), say which part does which work. Test the one I keep falling back on — posit a variable only if it isn't eliminable by isomorphism and the evolution of some accessible variable depends on it under some intervention — repair it if it breaks, and tell me honestly which of my verdicts it can't keep. Then run whatever you end up with on (a) and (b), and on one case I haven't thought of that you think is diagnostic. Numbered replies keyed to my cases are fine.
