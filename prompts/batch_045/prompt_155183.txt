i work in site reliability at a mid-size company and a lot of my week is incident reviews. Almost every postmortem we write ends up naming something nobody did as the root cause: nobody rotated a cert, nobody updated the runbook, nobody put an alert on that queue. I've also messed around with causal DAGs and the do() notation enough to be dangerous — I can read a graph, I know the difference between conditioning on X and intervening on X, but that's about where it stops.

I found out recently that philosophers argue about whether an omission — a thing that didn't happen — can be a cause at all, and that some of them say no, because causes are events and there's no event of not watering the plant. I'd like that whole debate restated as honestly as possible in the only vocabulary I'm fluent in: nodes, edges, do(), counterfactuals over a structural model, config defaults, alerts that fire and alerts that don't.

The cases I want you to run through it, because my team argues about them every week: (1) a missed step — the runbook says rotate the cert monthly, nobody did, cert expired, site down; (2) a default nobody chose — the load balancer timeout ships as 60s, no one ever set it, and that's why the retry storm cascaded; (3) an unsent signal — no alarm existed on queue depth, so nobody knew, so nothing got done; (4) the one that feels weirdest — the bad deploy didn't go out because the release manager was on a flight and couldn't approve it, so somebody's not-doing-something seems to be what saved us.

Then the residue report: which of these go into a DAG plus do() cleanly, which only go in if you cheat (inventing a node for something that didn't happen, or treating a shipping default as if someone had chosen it), and which won't go in at all. My hunch is that my framework can't even say "that's an omission" unless something outside the graph tells it what was supposed to happen, which is why we keep dragging runbooks into postmortems. If that's right — is it a bug in the toolkit, or is the philosophers' problem with omissions actually a problem about norms wearing a costume? Is there anything left over that the graph still can't say even after I bolt on a "what the runbook said" node? Also: our review template says only name causes the team could have acted on. That rule feels seperate from the causal question but I can't say why — where does it sit relative to what they're arguing about?
