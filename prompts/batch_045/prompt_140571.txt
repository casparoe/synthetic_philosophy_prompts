I'm on the committee at the small college where I teach that has to decide whether students can use AI writing help on take-home essays — we vote next week. I argued for a full ban and I won, but I'm pretty sure I won badly. What I actually did was walk people up a ladder of invented students: one uses it to fix commas, one to smooth a paragraph, one to rewrite a whole section, one pastes the question in and submits whatever comes back — and after each rung I asked where exactly they'd draw the line. Nobody could, so by the end everyone was for banning it. Later I ran the same ladder from a different starting point — a spellchecker, a thesaurus, a writing tutor, a friend who reads your draft — and it "proves" just as smoothly that we'd have to ban spellcheckers too, which is obviously wrong. I also notice I gave my imaginary students personalities (the one using it was a bit sneeky about it) while the student who works honestly never got a face. So: how do I tell whether an example like that is showing me something real about the policy or just steamrolling the room? Is nudging the line a little at a time ever a fair way to argue, or is it always a trick? And whichever way that goes, what should I actually do with the vote — is there a better way for the six of us to think it through?
