I used to run a wet lab, and now in retirement I poke around philosophy, so forgive me if I use the wrong words. Something puzzles me and I'd like the implicit theory spelled out.

Take two things we actually do. First: if an adult walks past a shallow pond where a toddler is face down and doesn't wade in because it would ruin his shoes, we treat him as having done something seriously wrong — Singer's pond case leans on exactly that, and plenty of countries criminalise failing to render assistance. But nobody thinks a person who spends their savings on a new kitchen instead of on insecticide-treated bednets has done anything wrong; we call that generosity, and optional.

Second, the bit I keep chewing on: the health service here will fund a treatment that costs something like £25,000 per year of decent life gained, and refuse one that costs more, and we accept that a particular named patient then dies of a treatable condition. Same shape as the pond, it seems to me — known person, known cost, known outcome if we don't pay — except we do it by committee and call it not merely permissible but required.

So what do these two practices presuppose? Do they need the thought that nearness or identification matters morally in itself, or can you get the whole pattern out of something else: an acts/omissions line, a division of labour between individuals and institutions, the fact that in the health case thousands of others could also pay, or just the idea that a duty stops being a duty once it stops being cheap? I'd like you to separate what the practice strictly requires from what it merely fits well with, and say what would actually have to change in either practice if we dropped the near-and-identified requirement.

My own half-baked version: charity evaluators put the cost of saving a life through bednet distribution in the low thousands of dollars, so why not treat donating as rescue-at-a-distance and let people discharge the duty that way? Does my version break anything the ordinary version doesn't, and does it make the pond intuition look like an accident of proximity?

Finish with one line: Verdict: <short phrase saying whether the practice strictly presupposes that nearness or identification matters, or whether several theories would license it>.
