In my lab's cognitive-science reading group we keep falling back on one picture of Dennett's four kinds of minds — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian and Gregorian creatures — namely that all four tiers are the same generate-and-test loop, variation plus selective retention, successively relocated: from populations to an individual's lifetime, from an individual's overt behaviour to its internal model, and from a creature's own model to a culturally inherited stock of mind-tools, each relocation buying speed at the same trick. A postdoc used it last week to end an argument about RL-trained systems: if the loop is one and only its bandwidth differs, then scaling a fixed architecture with more search delivers Gregorian generality for free, and talk of a qualitative gap between an agent shaped by reward and one whose candidate actions fail inside a model before it acts is just speed talk in disguise.

I want to push back but precisely, so please audit the analogy. Give me the correspondence table — what plays the role of the population, the source of variation, the heritability or memory, the fitness function, the generation time, and the retention substrate at each tier — and mark row by row whether the Darwinian machinery is literally instantiated there, only metaphorically similar, or simply absent. Then separate the disanalogies that bear on his conclusion from the ones that don't. Three I especially want addressed: whether reinforcement within a lifetime is selection at all when the entities selected never multiply and only one organism is being reshaped; whether the variation available to a creature that prescreens candidates in a model, or inherits words and other mind-tools from conspecifics, can still count as blind; and whether cultural transmission of acquired tools is Lamarckian in a way that makes the Gregorian tier a different algorithm rather than a faster run of the same one. If the picture cannot carry the conclusion, say what should replace it — credit assignment or hill-climbing, a ratchet-and-accumulation picture, or something else — and whether any amended version could, perhaps by adding a further tier for creatures whose preselection is scaffolded by other agents' models rather than by their own.
