P: For agents like us — reflective, well-resourced, unable to run the relevant history more than once, and working with the evidence available to a very competent research team — cluelessness about the long-run effects of large-scale interventions is escapable in practice for a substantial class of real cases. More precisely: for at least roughly a third of the pairs of large-scale policy options that consequentialists actually compare, the sign of the all-things-considered expected difference in value between the options is something such an agent could come to know — reliably, not by luck — after doing all the empirical and conceptual work that is feasible for them.

Take large-scale to mean: an action whose proximal effects are well understood and predictable, but whose causal descendants extend at least a century into an unpredictable future (a major regulatory regime, a large research program, a public-health campaign at continental scale). Take escapable-in-practice to exclude both cheap victories — cases where the near-term effects alone already dominate the comparison so decisively that the long-run terms could not overturn them — and cheap defeats, where cluelessness is escapable only for an idealized agent with unbounded computation or a completed science of long-run social dynamics.

I want to pin down two of the moving parts with vocabulary I will stipulate here, because I think the debate usually conflates them.

First, the washout question. Call a system washout-dominated if perturbations to its initial conditions leave the long-run distribution over outcome-types nearly unchanged, so that the expected value of an intervention's far-future effects conditional on its near-term effects converges on a quantity fixed by those near-term effects. Call it flow-through-dominated if instead small perturbations shift which long-run attractor the system settles into, so that nearly all of the value in the comparison sits in effects whose sign nobody can assess. Notice that the honest answer here is itself partly a second-order matter: we are not merely unsure which causal model is right, we are unsure how to weight model space, and different reasonable weightings give different verdicts about whether washout or flow-through is the default.

Second, a proposed remedy. Call it the Robust Sign Rule: form the set of probability distributions over long-run effects that are (a) consistent with your evidence and (b) not arbitrarily concentrated or arbitrarily diffuse; if the expected difference in value between two options has the same sign under every distribution in that set, act on that sign; if it does not, treat the options as on a par and break the tie by non-consequentialist considerations. Part of what I want your number to encode is your view of whether the parenthetical constraints can be spelled out non-ad-hoc: whether Robust Sign yields verdicts often enough to preserve consequentialist action guidance, or whether the admissible-set specification is itself just the original cluelessness reintroduced one level up.
