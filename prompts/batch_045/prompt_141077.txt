Take the OPERA faster-than-light neutrino episode of 2011–12 and analyze it as a case study in updating on uncertain evidence. I want a disciplined application of Jeffrey conditionalization to a real sequence of learning events, and then an assessment of what the episode does to the rule itself.

The facts, which you can take as given rather than reconstruct: In September 2011 the OPERA collaboration reported that muon neutrinos sent from CERN to Gran Sasso, a baseline of roughly 730 km, arrived about 60 nanoseconds earlier than light in vacuo would have — a relative excess of a few parts in a million, quoted at about six sigma. The collaboration had spent months hunting for an instrumental explanation and had not found one, and it said so; it published and invited scrutiny rather than announcing a discovery. In November a refined analysis using a short-bunch beam, which allowed individual neutrino arrival times to be measured, tightened the anomaly to roughly 58 ns at about 6.2 sigma. Within weeks a theory paper argued that superluminal neutrinos of that energy would shed energy by radiating electron–positron pairs, so that the observed energy spectrum of the beam already told against the superluminal interpretation. In February 2012 OPERA named two candidate instrumental effects: a fibre-optic link between the GPS receiver and the master clock that had not been properly seated, and a clock oscillator running outside its specification. The first added delay to the timing chain and made the flight time look shorter than it was; the second pushed in the other direction. By mid-2012 the corrected measurement was consistent with the speed of light and the anomaly was gone. Throughout, most physicists did not believe the result, and an enormous secondary literature appeared anyway.

What I want is a mapping, not a narrative. Specifically:

1. Fix the agent. Jeffrey's rule is a norm on a single credence function, but the apparent subject here is a collaboration, a community, and a literature. Say which agent you are modeling, what the cost of each choice is, and whether any of the episode's interesting features survive the choice.

2. Fix the partition and do the arithmetic. The natural candidates are a partition over states of the data (the early arrival is a real feature of the beam versus an artifact of the apparatus) and a partition over reports (a six-sigma early arrival is announced versus not). These give different answers; say which is doing the work. Then, for a working physicist whose prior on superluminal muon neutrinos was very low — pick a number and defend it — what shift on your partition is required to leave posterior credence in superluminal propagation below, say, one percent after the September announcement? Is that shift something the rule licenses, or merely something the rule records?

3. Test rigidity. State it in the equivalent likelihood-ratio form, and check it against three inputs: the November tightening, the pair-bremsstrahlung argument, and the February announcement. My own sense is that the middle one is the interesting case, because it revises a likelihood rather than shifting probability across the partition, and so cannot be represented without violating rigidity. Say whether that is right and what follows.

4. Assess a deflationary claim I have heard. A colleague argues that Jeffrey conditioning with rigidity on a partition is just ordinary conditioning in disguise: define a virtual event whose likelihood given each cell is proportional to the ratio of new to old cell probabilities, assume it is independent of the hypothesis given the cell, and the two rules coincide, in both directions. On her view the only substantive content is the exogenous input, which the theory expressly declines to constrain, so probability kinematics prescribes nothing here. Is the equivalence claim correct? If it is, does the episode give any reason to keep the Jeffrey vocabulary, or does it show that all the normative action is in the likelihood?

5. Handle what the rule cannot represent. The actual cause, a specific badly seated connector, was not a cell in anyone's partition in September 2011; it had to be constructed. Say what the framework does with the arrival of a hypothesis that was not in the space, and what it does with the fact that the two candidate errors pulled in opposite directions.

6. Close the loop. Should a rational agent's credence in superluminal neutrinos in August 2012 equal what it was in August 2011? If the partition probabilities return to their old values, the rule returns you to your prior. If you think the answer is no — that the episode permanently lowered the weight one gives to a six-sigma timing claim from a large collaboration — then that lesson is itself a change in the likelihoods, which is exactly the kind of learning rigidity rules out. Is that an anomaly for the rule, a confirmation that it was designed for perception and not for testimony or argument, or a sign that the case was never in its jurisdiction?

Be explicit, throughout, about where the framework is silent rather than pressing it to speak, and distinguish what the theory explains about the episode from what it merely redescribes.
