I'm writing up my position on self-locating belief for a graduate seminar in formal epistemology, and what I want before I defend it is an audit of the package for internal coherence rather than for truth. Where I've landed: (1) in the standard Sleeping Beauty setup — a fair coin tossed on Sunday night, one awakening on Monday if Heads, awakenings on both Monday and Tuesday if Tails, induced amnesia, no clock in the room — my credence in Heads when I wake is 1/3. (2) My reason is the sampling one: I should distribute my credence as though I had been assigned at random to one of the awakening-events the experiment produces, since the three candidate awakenings are subjectively indistinguishable to me at the moment of waking. (3) But I also hold that my own existence is no evidence whatever for hypotheses that predict enormous numbers of observers; when anthropically flavoured reasoning delivers that conclusion I treat it as a reductio of the reasoning, not as a bullet I should learn to bite. (4) Methodologically, I think credence is answerable to betting: the correct credence is the one whose odds I would accept, and I distrust any answer that cannot be cashed out as stakes I would actually take. (5) If I am then told truthfully that today is Monday, I go back to 1/2; if I am told it is Tuesday, I go to 0. (6) And I do not want to surrender that on Sunday night I am flat-out certain that I will be woken on Monday — certainties, I want to say, do not erode merely because time passes. (7) Finally, I want the same 1/3 verdict in the variant where the experimenter does not wake me twice but instead makes an exact duplicate of me while I sleep and wakes each of us once; the numbers look the same to me from the inside, so I think the answer should be too.

My interlocutor in the seminar is a halfer and keeps insisting the set is unstable, but the only joints he points at are (1) against (5) and (2) against (3), and I genuinely cannot tell whether either of those is the real trouble or whether the problem is somewhere he hasn't looked yet.

So: give me the audit. Work through the combinations, pairwise or larger, and say which are genuinely in tension and which only look like it; rank the genuine ones by how much damage they do to the set rather than listing them all as equally serious; and for each, say exactly what I would have to give up or reformulate and what that costs me elsewhere in the package. If a single commitment is silently doing the work of two others, say which one. If (2) as I have phrased it is ambiguous in a way that matters — for instance, between counting only my own past and future awakenings and counting any subjectively indistinguishable observer-moment wherever and to whomever it occurs — say so, and say which reading (3) and (7) push toward and whether they push toward the same one. Then name the single revision that would clean up the most, and what it would leave me unable to claim. Please don't simply tell me to become a halfer or a thirder, don't restate the standard arguments on either side, and don't cite or summarise the literature: I know the terrain, and what I need to know is which of (1) through (7) has to move and why. Keep the whole thing under 600 words — ranked list or dense prose, whichever carries more per line.
