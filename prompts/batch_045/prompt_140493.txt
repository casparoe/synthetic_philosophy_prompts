I work in causal inference and machine learning: Pearl's structural causal models, do-calculus, the potential-outcomes/Rubin framing, abduction–action–prediction for counterfactual queries, off-policy evaluation, ignorability, positivity, and the distinction between point identification, partial identification, and unidentifiability. I use these daily and can take them as given. My philosophy is limited to an undergraduate survey, plus a rough grip on what divine foreknowledge and libertarian free will are supposed to be.

The view I want translated is Molinism. As I understand it: in addition to God's knowledge of necessary truths and of what he himself freely decrees, there is a *middle* knowledge of counterfactuals of freedom — for any possible free creature and any possible circumstance, what that creature would freely do — held logically prior to any decree to create, and used to select which feasible world to actualize. God actualizes the circumstances; the creature's foreseen free response completes the world, so providence is preserved without the response being caused by the decree. The well-known objections I want carried along on the same trip are: the grounding objection (that true counterfactuals of freedom would need a truthmaker, and nothing in reality can play that role); the worry that God's plans are hostage to what creatures would do, so that some goods may be unactualizable and every feasible world may contain wrongdoing, the transworld-depravity shape of the problem; and the objection that Molinism buys providence only by positing a class of facts whose truth is fixed by nothing.

My own working hypothesis, which I want you to test rather than indulge, is that Molinism is close to the thesis that what a creature would freely do under an intervention do(C = c) is a well-defined layer-three counterfactual: a random variable fixed once the exogenous noise is abducted, with feasibility amounting to a support or positivity constraint on which worlds are reachable, and middle knowledge amounting to possession of the full noise distribution together with the structural equations, hence of the entire counterfactual table. So: translate each of the commitments and each of the objections above into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, and then tell me where that hypothesis distorts things — in particular whether the priority of middle knowledge to the creative decree, and the idea that these counterfactuals are *known* rather than estimated from any data, have counterparts in my framework at all.

Then the residue report. Which theses translate cleanly; which translate only if I let my framework smuggle in a substantive metaphysics (for example, modelling free choice as an exogenous noise term feeding a structural equation is already a determinism-ish commitment, and treating counterfactual definiteness as a modelling choice rather than a claim about reality); and which cannot be expressed at all, because my vocabulary has no slot for them — I suspect grounding, truthmakers, and non-inferential knowledge are in this class. For each item, say whether you think the residue indicts my framework or indicts Molinism.

Finally, the part I most want: does the causal-inference toolkit generate an argument against or for Molinism that the theological literature has not already made? I have in mind the in-principle non-identifiability of layer-three queries from any combination of observational and interventional data, the fact that no run of the actual world could ever discriminate between rival counterfactual models, and the extrapolation problem created by the enormous space of merely possible circumstances in which no positivity assumption can be earned. Say how much weight these bear, and be explicit about which moves in my framework would beg the question if used against the Molinist.
