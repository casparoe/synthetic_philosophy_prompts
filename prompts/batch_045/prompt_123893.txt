ok so heres the context: I spent 35 years as a spectroscopist, retired now, and a few of us (mostly other retirees plus two very patient grad students from the university) run a reading group at the local library. Last week we did that dialogue in The Minds I where you get to talk to Einsteins brain floating in a jar, and the bit where someone suggests you could in principle write down the whole thing as a book of a hundred billion pages where each page tells you which page to turn to next, and then whether that book understands relativity. We argued for two hours and got nowhere, mainly because my friend who used to be a neurologist kept saying the whole question is silly since a book isnt causally hooked up to anything, which feels to me like he is smuggling the answer in. So I want to make a one page handout for next month: a proper sorting tree, from a root where we all grant that a normally embodied awake person is conscious and that a brain kept alive and stimulated in a jar would be too, down to about 9 to 12 leaves, each with a name if it has one (or marked unnamed if nobody actually defends it) and one honest line about that leafs most awkward commitment.

The rules I want you to follow: build it from the logical space rather than from a list of theories, so give me the branch questions first and then slot the actual views into the cells that their answers allow, and tell me which combinations of answers are empty and why (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored). Every branch question has to be a concrete case my group can answer without knowing any labels, so nothing like do you think consciousness is substrate independent, and no question that just asks whether someone is a functionalist. The cases I care about, in roughly the order they came up: the transition book sitting unopened in a sealed vault; the same book except the pages are printed in a cipher nobody will ever break, so the pattern is physically there but there is no interpreter; a variant I invented where instead of a web of conditional transitions the book is just a giant timeline listing the exact state of every neuron at every millisecond of his life, no if-then structure at all, and I genuinely dont know whether removing the counterfactual structure should change anyones answer; the book plus a dumb machine that only follows the turn-to instructions and understands nothing; Einsteins neurons replaced one at a time with tiny paper slips that reproduce each neurons input-output behavior, until the whole thing is paper; a cryogenically frozen brain that is physically like a conscious brain but completely at a standstill; and a book whose pages get turned in a scrambled order. Please also include, as one leaf, the answer that the book is only conscious in some derivative way, relative to whoever is reading it, and then tell me honestly whether that is a distinct position or just another leaf wearing a hat, and flag any other pair of leaves your tree cant actually separate, plus what a person who gives you an inconsistent set of answers should be told theyre inconsistent about.

My own hunch, offered as a hunch and not as something to flatter: the real hinge is whether having the right counterfactuals is enough or whether you need the actual causal dynamics running, and I suspect most people give answers to the sealed vault book and to the frozen brain that quietly contradict each other. But I want the tree to be able to catch that rather than assume it, and I would rather you told me my hunch is confused than built the whole handout around it. Keep the wording plain enough for people who have read exactly one philosophy book.
