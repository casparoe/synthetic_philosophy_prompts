hi -- i do the secretariat work for a small professional college (around 900 members) that puts out position statements. Each one answers a block of four to eight yes/no items that are all about the same topic, members are expected to act on them, and they get quoted back at us in complaints and now and then in disciplinary hearings. Four to six of these a year, half-day meeting, 12-15 people (mostly clinicians plus two lay members), and I get about one afternoon to prep. It has to run on a shared spreadsheet and a show of hands, and the chair is a clinician who will read about one page of instructions and no more.

Last year we published one where a clear majority backed item 2, a clear majority backed item 3, and a clear majority backed item 5, and it turned out that no single member had actually voted for all three — someone complained that the statement tells them to do two incompatible things, and they were right. Someone on council muttered something about "the discursive dilemma" and said we should vote on the reasons rather than the conclusions, which sounds right but I don't know how to actually do that on a Tuesday afternoon with fifteen people.

So: give me a procedure I can run, in order, for one statement.

Specifically I need: how to break a topic into votable propositions before the meeting and lock that agenda before anyone discusses the substance (and what to do when someone proposes a new item at 3pm anyway); how to make sure the conditional, normative, "if the facts are like this then we should do X" item gets its own explicit vote instead of hiding inside the other items; how to decide which items get voted on and which are just read off the ones that passed, and what to do when the headline recommendation isn't entailed by anything the committee actually accepted and also isn't contradicted by it; whether it's safe that our bylaws require a two-thirds supermajority on some items and a plain majority on others inside the same statement; what I do with a ballot that's internally inconsistent, or with abstentions and "can't say" answers; and at which point the procedure stops and says this is a values call, go ask a person. I also need the output written up so that an outside person, a year later, can rerun it from the record and get the same answer — council wants an audit trail, not just a decision.

Please also be blunt about where this misfires. Our items are English sentences with "should" in them, sometimes with an implicit "all else being equal", and half the time the committee insists two items aren't inconsistent when they plainly are. I'd rather hand council a list of known holes than have them find one after publication.

Then run the procedure on these three:

(1) A screening programme. Items: "the test's false positive rate is acceptable"; "if the false positive rate is acceptable, the college should endorse routine screening"; "the college should endorse routine screening". Split is 4 members yes/yes/yes, 3 yes/no/no, 4 no/yes/no.

(2) The one I know they'll hate. A public statement on a staffing crisis: 9 of 13 badly want to say something, but the two supporting items — "workforce planning is within the college's remit" and "the data on staffing levels are solid enough to make a public claim" — each fail by a single vote, and a standing statement from 2021 says the college doesn't comment on workforce issues.

(3) Two items that may or may not clash: "clinicians should not prescribe X off-label" and "clinicians should exercise their own judgement where the evidence is thin".

Tell me what comes out of each, including the case where the answer is one the chair is going to fight me on, and say plainly what the procedure can't do.
