I've been using probabilistic heuristics for primes for a while and I've realised I dont have a clear rule for when it's OK to treat things as independent. Here are my verdicts:

Twin primes: pretend n and n+2 are each prime with probability 1/ln n, multiply by the correction for the small primes, get about 1.32 x/(ln x)^2 twin primes below x. I believe that number, and I'm not bothered that the uncorrected version is off by a constant.

Triples p, p+2, p+4: naive independence predicts lots, but there's only 3,5,7, since one of the three is always divisible by 3. That bothered me for about a minute and then I decided it just means you condition on the small primes first.

Fermat numbers: for k at least 2, every prime factor of F_k = 2^(2^k)+1 is 1 mod 2^(k+2). If I condition on that the same way I conditioned on mod 3 above, then for large k nearly every number of that size whose prime factors all sit in that class is prime (composites are rarer by roughly ln ln F_k over 2^(k+1)), so I get P(F_20 prime) = 0.99999, and running the same thing at k = 5 where I can check it gives about 0.95 even though F_5 = 641 x 6700417. I think the method has broken down here, and yet it looks like exactly the move I praised in the first two cases.

Gaps: model consecutive n's as independent coins with probability 1/ln n and you get the largest gap below x around (ln x)^2. I have a vague memory of a Maier-type result saying counts in intervals of length (ln x)^A fluctuate more than that model allows, and my gut says this is a genuine failure rather than a wrong constant, because inside a short block of consecutive integers the multiples of 3, 5 and 7 fall in a completely rigid pattern.

Coprimality: two random integers are coprime with probability 6/pi^2, from treating divisibility by each prime as independent. I trust this more than any of the others, even though the events aren't literally independent.

My tentative principle is: condition on everything you can prove about divisibility by primes up to some cutoff, then assume independence above it. That handles the twin prime, triple and coprime cases and I think it dies on the Fermat case and maybe the gaps one. Can you repair it? What is the actual difference between conditioning on mod 3 for twin primes and conditioning on the Fermat constraint: is it about where the cutoff sits, about the class you condition down to being no bigger than the primes inside it, or something else entirely?

Give me exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no preamble and no summary at the end: the repaired principle, why it flips on the Fermat case, what it says about gaps, a case I haven't listed that it decides, and which of my verdicts I'd have to give up.
