I've been going round in circles on the Ellsberg thing and I think I've fixed myself, but my partner says I've fixed the wrong thing and I can't tell which of us is confused.

The setup as I understand it: an urn with 90 balls, 30 red, and the other 60 are black and yellow in some proportion nobody tells you. Most people would rather bet on red than on black, and would also rather bet on black-or-yellow than on red-or-yellow, and apparently no single probability assignment can produce both of those preferences at once, which is supposed to show that something irrational is going on.

I tried it on myself but with matching instead of just picking: I asked what chance of winning on a wheel with *known* odds would make me indifferent to betting on black, and I came out at about 20%. Same question for yellow: also about 20%. So my numbers for black and yellow add to 40% when they ought to add to 66%. That's the incoherence, right? My degrees of belief literally don't add up.

So I've been training myself out of it: whenever I notice I don't know a probability, I force it to 50/50 and then just do the expected value calc. Being ambiguity-neutral just means treating unknown chances as even, doesn't it? I've been using it on real decisions too. We're arguing about whether to get a damp patch on the ceiling opened up, and I keep reasoning: could be nothing, could be a rotten joist, I have no idea, so 50/50, so definately get it looked at. My partner says that isn't what the paradox is about at all, that any coherent set of numbers would do, that I've confused the numbers my choices reveal with the numbers I actually believe, and that the 50/50 move is just inventing knowledge I don't have. But surely some numbers are more rational than others? And if my probabilities don't add to one, can't a bookie just take free money off me?

I also wonder if this is even a fair test. In real life when a probability is unknown it's usually because somebody else knows it and I don't — the bookie, the seller, the surveyor — so isn't hanging back just correct, and the lab version only looks bad because it strips that away?
