I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I sit on the grants committee of a small foundation that is spending down its remaining funds over roughly five years, with a mandate to focus on wild animal suffering. We can fund conceptual work, empirical welfare biology, or field studies and pilot interventions, and we do not have to pick only one.

What I want from you is a defended priority order, not a survey, and I'd rather you reason from first principles than reach for references. Here are the questions as I currently see them; please reorder them, cut any that don't deserve a place, and add any I'm missing.

1. Is the net welfare of animals living in the wild positive or negative? How much does the answer turn on the fact that in many species most individuals born die very young, and how much on how we count the experiences of very small or very differently organised animals?
2. How much of the case for non-intervention is genuinely empirical caution about knock-on ecosystem effects, and how much rests on a value that isn't about animal welfare at all — something like the value of wildness, of ecological processes running themselves, or of not treating nature as a system to be managed?
3. Given that question 1 may not be settled for decades, what is the value of buying information now — funding small, contained, well-instrumented interventions whose main purpose is to find out what happens and how bad the side effects are, rather than to help directly?
4. Should the money go to wild animals that exist today, or to the norms that will govern wild animals who don't exist yet: large-scale rewilding and species reintroduction, ocean fertilization for carbon capture, or eventually the introduction of animal populations to other planets, any of which could bring into existence enormous numbers of wild animals who would otherwise never live?

For each, tell me how much of the rest of the picture depends on it, how tractable it is in five years with money and a handful of people, and how neglected it already is. Then commit: which two would you actually fund now, at roughly what split, and what single result, if we saw it in three years, would most change your mind?
