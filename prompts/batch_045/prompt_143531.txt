Not sure this is the right venue but here goes — I keep noticing the same shape of thing and I'd like to know whether it has a name, or whether I'm just describing three different things as one.

The shape, as best I can spell it out. Two or more parties are in some competitive situation. Each of them takes a step that costs them something and removes their own future ability to back out: they sign a contract they can't cancel, pre-buy something they can't resell, announce a date in public, hand the decision to somebody else who won't give it back. Crucially nothing about their actual capability improves. The only thing that changes is what the *other* side now believes. And the value of doing it seems to depend almost entirely on doing it first — the one who locks in first gets whatever there is to get (the reputation, the scare, the funding, the credit), and the second one gets nothing, or just ends up stuck. So it isn't a race to build a better thing. It's a race to be the one who got locked in first.

The bit I find hardest to describe is where the credibility comes from. You can't just tell the other side you can't back out; nobody would believe you. What seems to actually happen is that each side goes and recruits some third party who would punish them for reversing — shareholders, voters, a regulator, a journalist who now has the story — and then the escalation takes the form of shopping around for a harsher audience, because whoever has the nastier audience wins. Everyone involved says privately they'd sign a binding agreement tomorrow, but any pause to sit down and negotiate is exactly the moment the other side can lock in.

Things I already know about and think this isn't: the burning-your-boats idea from game theory (that's one side doing it once, to make a threat stick, and the point is the threat — here there's often no threat at all, and it's the ordering that matters); the thing where you tie yourself up because your future self will be weak-willed (I don't change my mind; I would reverse this afternoon if I could get away with it); the two-drivers-swerving setup (there you want to *look* committed while keeping the option to swerve — here you have to actually destroy it); and the auction where everyone bids and everyone pays (there's a prize in that one, and the money goes to somebody). I've also come across the phrase 'commitment race' muttered at me at a conference, but I've never managed to find it used for anything, and it implies a finish line, which mine doesn't have.

So: is there an established concept here, and if the nearest ones only half fit, tell me exactly how mine differs from each and which comes closest. If nothing fits, coin something and define it tightly enough that I can tell a real case from a near miss. And give me a diagnostic — what would I have to observe in the wild to conclude this is what's happening rather than an ordinary capability race or plain positional one-upmanship, and what observation would make me say no, it's just one of the things I listed above? I don't want a label on its own; I want the carving.
