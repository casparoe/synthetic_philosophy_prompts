So before I write anything I want to see what my own verdicts add up to. Here they are, as honestly as I can report them: skipping a national election — not wrong, no duty, though voting is fine too; buying a supermarket chicken where the bird is already dead and the shop's ordering algorithm will not react to my single purchase — wrong; eating the leftover roast chicken in my flatmate's fridge that she's about to bin anyway — totally fine, not even slightly bad; buying from a butcher who kills to order — clearly wrong and, weirdly, about as bad as the supermarket one; taking a long drive on my own just for the view — not wrong, I basically agree with the reading on that; having a third kid, which by any accounting emits vastly more than the driving or the chicken — also not wrong, and I don't want to give that one up; leaving the heating on all day in the six-person house where the bill is split equally — mildly wrong, real but small; paying my taxes when roughly a tenth of it goes to weapons, and my individual payment changes nothing either way — fine, I should pay; dropping twenty bucks in a collection for a group that will put up a billboard I find genuinely hateful, knowing they'll hit their target without me — wrong, and not just a little; buying extra electricity on a grid where the total emissions are fixed by a binding permit cap, so my extra usage just nudges the permit price and someone else uses less — not wrong, though this is the one I'm least sure about.
