Consider the following set-up, a variant of the familiar careenium-and-simmballs scenario. A shallow tank is filled with *careenium*, a viscous medium in which there floats a finite lattice of *simmballs*. Each simmball has an internal two-state degree of freedom — a "pip" that is either extended or retracted — together with a position and a velocity. Neighbouring simmballs interact through the medium according to a classical, deterministic, microphysically complete dynamics: fix the exact microstate of the tank and of the medium at one time and the state at every later time is settled. There is no fundamental indeterminacy, no non-physical force, and no creation of energy or momentum anywhere in what follows.

At a coarse grain, the extended and retracted pips form patterns. Some of these patterns are *legible*: the array can be read as encoding a rule, an instruction, or a number. The disputed question is this. When a legible pattern is followed by the medium shoving a target in the direction the pattern "says" — and when, had the pattern been different, the shove would have been different in the way the pattern-level rule predicts — has the pattern, *as a pattern*, done any of the pushing? Or has the microstate done all the causal work, with the pattern being merely a way of picking out which microstate happened to be there?

Four stipulations are in force, both camps accept them, and they are meant to stop the usual moves from settling the matter by themselves:

1. *Cryptic microdynamics, generous realisation.* Every legible pattern has astronomically many realisers, differing in exact pip geometry, in the position of simmballs within their cells, and in the thermal jitter of the medium. Within a realisation class the micro-variation appears pseudo-random: no simpler or stronger lawlike description of the micro-to-micro map exists than the microdynamics itself, and the same pattern recurs in disjoint microstates.
2. *Invariance.* Hold the pattern fixed and wiggle the microstate anywhere within its realisation class, and the coarse-grained outcome is unchanged. Change the pattern by any physically available means — which of course also changes the microstate — and the outcome changes as the pattern-level rule predicts. The pattern-level law is simple, exceptionless, and supports interventions; neither camp disputes that it holds.
3. *The Scrambled Twin.* An identical tank, with the same number of simmballs, the same total energy and the same microscopic interaction law, but with the pip-bits scrambled so that no legible pattern is present. Its target is not shoved, or is shoved only erratically.
4. *The Re-encoding Drift.* A run in which the tank gradually replaces every realiser of the pattern with a microstate disjoint from all earlier ones — the pattern is rewritten into a different physical code, cell by cell, over an extended period — while the pattern itself persists and the target's behaviour remains continuous throughout. Here there is pattern-level continuity without micro-level continuity.

Two camps have been arguing past each other about this set-up for years. Call them *Substrate* and *Symbol*. The Substrate exclusivist holds that the microdynamics is causally complete, that the microstate at one time is nomologically sufficient for the microstate at the next, that the pattern supervenes on the microstate, and hence that once the microstate is fixed the pattern makes no further difference: it is screened off, and "the pattern caused the shove" is at best a convenient shorthand for a claim about which of the many realisers obtained. The Symbol autonomist holds that the pattern-level law is invariant under a wide class of interventions, is simple, exceptionless and counterfactual-supporting, is not tractably derivable from the microdynamics, and is the level at which we manipulate, predict and explain; a causal claim that is true where invariance and intervention live is not mere shorthand, so the symbols do push the substrate around — without thereby adding any force to it.

Your task is to draft the joint statement these two camps could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. It is not an adjudication: neither side may win by stipulating a definition, and no verdict is to be given.

Please produce, in British English, three sections.

**Section 1 — Agreed claims.** A numbered list of the claims both camps accept. Include at least: what each camp grants about the completeness and determinism of the microdynamics; what each grants about supervenience and about the invariance of the pattern-level law under realisation-class interventions; what each grants about screening-off and about the empirical adequacy of each description at its own grain; and what each grants about what would have to be explained by anyone. Every item must be substantive. If you catch yourself writing a claim that is true but trivially so, either strengthen it or mark it explicitly as "agreed, but does no work".

**Section 2 — Disagreements table.** One row per crux, with these columns: the crux; the Substrate formulation, in wording an exclusivist would sign as a statement of *its own* view; the Symbol formulation, in wording an autonomist would sign as a statement of *its own* view; a case, argument, or formal test that both camps agree bears on the crux — drawn from stipulations 1–4 or from a further variant you construct and describe precisely; each side's prediction, written in advance, of what that case or test will show; and what each side would count as evidence against its own position. Your rows should cover at least: whether "cause" is to be read as production or as difference-making; whether screening-off or invariance is the decisive test; how counterfactuals are to be evaluated in a deterministic setting, given that the microstate cannot be held fixed while the pattern is changed; what the Scrambled Twin and the Re-encoding Drift respectively show; what a standard causal-discovery procedure would return when given micro-variables only, pattern-level variables only, or both, and what follows from that; and whether "pushing" requires the transfer of something conserved. Where no formulation can be found that both camps accept, leave the item as an explicit disagreement rather than smoothing it over.

**Section 3 — What is merely verbal.** A short section, signed by both, identifying which parts of the residual dispute are disputes about words, and saying precisely why.

Do not cite, name, or quote authors, works, or literature: the document must stand entirely on the set-up and the arguments, and should be intelligible to a reader with no access to any library. Aim for roughly 1,000–1,400 words in total, and use the terminology introduced above throughout.
