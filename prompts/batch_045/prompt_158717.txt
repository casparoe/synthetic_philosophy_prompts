Hey, I'm putting together a short chapter on what individuals owe in situations where the harm only shows up collectively, and before I even look at what people have already said I want the spec nailed down first. So: write me the desiderata that an account of a single agent's reasons in collective-harm cases would have to meet.

Fix the purpose like this: it has to be able to tell one person what she has most reason to do on an ordinary Tuesday, and separately say something coherent about whether anyone may blame or criticise her if she doesn't — I don't want those two questions quietly treated as the same question.

Cases it has to get right (add any I'm missing): the tiny-contribution case, where a few thousand people each do something whose effect on the victim is imperceptible on its own; the lumpy-threshold case, where the harm comes in discrete units, e.g. a canteen that restocks whole animals in batches sized to last week's sales, so whether your sandwich matters depends on where in the batch it lands and you can't tell which; the constitutive rather than causal case, like a vote whose only real effect runs through the size of the winning margin instead of through who wins; the repeat-player case, where the same agent faces the same negligible choice hundreds of times over a life; and the awkward one where the party being harmed is arguably better off existing at all, since the demand that created them is also what creates the harm.

Then say which distinctions the account must not collapse: making a difference versus being one of the people who make it, expected versus actual efficacy, acting wrongly versus being aptly blamed, what I should do versus what we should do, and reasons not to contribute versus reasons to pay for it afterward.

Mark which of these you take to be genuine requirements and which are just a prejudice of one camp — I think the idea that if your act makes no difference then you can't have a duty not to do it is an assumption smuggled in as a result. And tell me whether any subset of the desiderata can't be satisfied together, since that's the part I actually care about, plus which single requirement eliminates the most popular existing answers.
