I teach a short decision-theory module and I am assembling discussion material on money-pump arguments for diachronic rationality. A colleague of mine keeps insisting that the argument is really only about synchronic coherence: get your preferences into a complete, transitive ordering and nobody can pump you, and conversely, if somebody can pump you then some time-slice of yours must have been incoherent. I want to break that link from both directions at once, in one tightly matched pair of cases, and I want the pair engineered so that neither of us can wave it away as a trick of presentation.

What I need is two cases set in the same shop, over the same three items and the same handful of decision points, with the same information conditions. In the first case the agent's preferences are intransitive — an outright cycle over the three items, stable across the whole episode, and known by her to be a cycle, with a realistic generator behind it (pairwise comparisons that shift which dimension is salient, say, rather than a bare stipulation that she likes A to B and B to C and C to A) — and yet she is not pumped: she finishes with the item she walked in with and every dollar she arrived with, against a shopkeeper who correctly predicts each choice she makes and who would have taken money from a less careful version of her. In the second case the agent's preferences are complete and transitive at every single moment, she never chooses anything other than her current favourite, she is fully sophisticated in the sense that she predicts her own future preferences correctly and backward-inducts over them, and she still finishes strictly poorer, holding exactly what she could have kept for nothing.

Constraints, applying to both unless stated: keep it realistic and small — a bike shop with a swap counter, a phone trade-in kiosk, a flat-share, nothing fantastic — one paragraph per case plus the arithmetic. No demons, no predictor better than the agent's own self-knowledge, no uncertainty, no information arriving mid-episode, and no change in what she believes about the world. A dollar is worth the same to her at every moment, so nothing may turn on discounting or on when payment falls. No commitment devices: she cannot bind her future self, quit the scheme, or park her wallet with a friend; all she can do is decline an offer and leave with what she has. In the first case the shop's offers must be posted in advance and honoured as posted, and the broker may not improvise when she declines; the same posted schedule must drain a myopic version of that same agent of the full amount, so that her escape cannot be blamed on a shopkeeper who priced badly. In the second case the shop may tailor each fresh offer to whatever she currently holds and may keep doing so for a finite number of rounds whose length she knows from the outset, but the way her preferences move must fall out of one stable rule stated up front — a function of what she currently holds or has just consumed — and not from a brute stipulation that in week three she prefers the folding bike after all; and if the best you can manage is that she loses one round's fee and no more, say that explicitly and show why the loss cannot be driven to zero without commitment, since that result is itself worth having.

Then, which is most of what I want: for each case, set out the preference structure explicitly — what she prefers to what, when, and why it moves or does not move — state which offers she accepts and which she refuses with the reasoning that yields those answers, and give the net result in money and in kind. Next, identify the single structural feature doing the work in each case and show that removing or exchanging it flips the verdict: the cyclic agent should get pumped once you make her myopic or let the broker re-offer after a refusal, and the second agent should walk away clean once her preferences are held fixed across time or she is given one free opportunity to bind herself. Finally, give me my colleague's strongest reply — the best available version of the claim that pumpability, actual or hypothetical, still tracks irrationality even granting that your two cases come out as advertised — and state plainly what you take the pair to establish and what it does not. Derive the verdicts from the preferences you specify rather than announcing them; and if one of my constraints forces a weaker result than I have asked for, tell me which constraint and why instead of quietly relaxing it.
