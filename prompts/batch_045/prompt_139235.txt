I write the moderation tooling at the forum I work at — the word filter, the appeal queue, all of it. The actual calls get made by a woman in Trust & Safety (I'll call her Dee), and I lurk in the channel where she explains them, because I'm the one who has to turn whatever she's doing into routing logic. I've watched about a year of her rulings and I cannot reverse-engineer the rule, and it's starting to matter: I have to write the guidance for which appeals get auto-closed and which go to a human.

Her track record, roughly. I'll call the word in question "the word": it's a slur for an ethnic minority with a long history of discrimination, and there's a real but contested thing where some members of the group have reclaimed it.

1. A member of the group posts "us [word]s gotta stick together" in an in-group space — allowed.
2. Someone outside the group uses it as a direct insult in an argument — removed, temp ban.
3. An educational post that writes the word out in full as an example of how slurs work, with a content warning — allowed.
4. "some guy in a parking lot called me a [word] today" — allowed.
5. Someone posts a screenshot of a threatening DM they recieved, word right there in the image — allowed.
6. A slur for white people, used as an insult — allowed. She said something like "rude, but not what the rule is for."
7. A fan who is *not* a member of the group posts a song lyric containing the word as a caption, clearly as homage to an artist who is a member and who reclaims it — removed. He appealed that he meant it with respect and she didn't budge.
8. Someone uses a *different* group's slur as a generic insult at a person who isn't even in that group — removed.
9. A post that uses the word in order to denounce it ("[the word] is a vile word, nobody should say it") — allowed, word left intact.
10. A respelled / emoji-substituted version that obviously means the word — removed, even though our filter missed it.

So it isn't the string, isn't "did he mean to be mean" (7 wasn't mean at all), and isn't just "is he in the group" (3, 4 and 5 aren't). I keep trying to write it as a 2x2 — speaker in-group or out-group × friendly or hostile — and the thing I noticed is that she's only ever ruled on three of the four boxes. The empty one is: a member of the group using the word as an insult *at* another member of the group.

That's the appeal in the queue right now. Guy in a ~200-person group chat, mostly members of the group, calls another member the word in the middle of a fight. His defense is "I'm [group], I can say it." The person he aimed it at didn't report it, a third member did, and the chat is split down the middle — half say in-group means in-group, full stop, half say that only covers the affectionate uses.

I genuinely don't know what the right call is either; I just need to predict her. What do you think she does with it? More usefully for me: what rule does the list of ten above actually fit, what other rules fit it just as well, and how do those rules come apart on this case? Give me a best guess with your reason for preferring it, and tell me the one further appeal I should watch for that would settle which rule she's really running.

(One theory of mine, in case it's useful: I thought the test was substitution — it's a slurring use if swapping in the neutral group term changes what's conveyed. But that seems to flag case 1 and case 6 too, where the word clearly adds something the neutral term doesn't, and she let both through. So either my test is junk or I'm measuring the wrong thing. Is there a version of it that survives?)
