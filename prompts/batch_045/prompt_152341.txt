Quick setup: it's set on a generation ship a few centuries out, and the ship maintains something everyone just calls the Ledger — a running tally of the crew's hedonic states (the ship can read them off directly; the tech is handwaved and I don't want the scene to be about the tech). The Ledger's job is to decide whether some proposed change to life aboard is worth it, by adding up pleasures and subtracting pains.

Two of my characters are arguing about how the Ledger should actually work, and I want both of them to be scary good at this — nobody gets the dumb version of the other side's view, nobody wins on a technicality. So:

- Mara holds something like lexical threshold negative utilitarianism: below some threshold of intensity, suffering counts lexically — no amount of mild or even moderate pleasure anywhere in the crew can outweigh a single instance of serious pain. Above the threshold, pains and pleasures trade off against each other in the ordinary utilitarian way, at ordinary exchange rates.
- Dev holds the opposite: there's one hedonic scale, pain is just a negative magnitude on it, everything is commensurable and tradeable, no thresholds and no lexical priority.

I'd like them to get into these specifically:

1) Whether there really is one scale at all, or whether pleasure and pain differ in kind. Pain has this compulsory, demanding, drop-everything-and-deal-with-me character that pleasure doesn't seem to have; is that a difference in magnitude, or a difference in the kind of reason it gives you?
2) The threshold itself. Mara's view has a cliff in it: take a given pain and slowly dial its intensity down, and at some point the lexical priority just switches off. An arbitrarily small change in intensity then produces an unbounded change in how the pain can be traded off. And if the threshold has to be sharp to do any work, where does it get its sharpness from? (I have a half-baked idea that Mara replies with a fuzzy band instead of a line, and I genuinely can't tell whether that helps her or just relocates the bump — use it or throw it out, whichever makes the better scene.)
3) Aggregation by tiny steps. Even granting the threshold, Mara's view seems to have trouble with cases where nobody is above the threshold but an awful lot of people are just barely under it. Dev should press that, and Mara should have a real answer rather than just biting the bullet very hard.
4) A pet idea of mine that I like but can't tell if it's dumb: the crew discovers a converter that can turn any given pain into a pleasure of the same felt duration, but there's always a conversion loss — a kind of heat tax, you never get out quite as much as you put in. Whether the converter is even coherent is meant to be a test of whether the two states are commensurable in the first place. Work it in if it's useful.

Also: is the zero point on the scale a real experience or just a bookkeeping convention? I have a hunch that matters for Dev's one-scale claim, but I can't say why, so let the characters figure it out.

Write the scene as a dialogue, 800–1200 words, in which they actually respond to what the other person just said rather than each delivering a set speech, and where both of them concede a couple of small things along the way. End it unresolved — I don't want a winner, I want the reader still arguing with themselves.
