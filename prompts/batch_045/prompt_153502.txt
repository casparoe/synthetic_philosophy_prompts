So I've been chewing on the wrong-kind-of-reasons thing for a while now, and I came at it from two odd ends at once. One: the move — which keeps turning up whenever people here try to cash out what they even mean by a cause being worth working on — of analysing value in terms of what there's reason to desire or admire or whatever. It struck me that unless you've got a handle on which reasons count, that move is either empty or straightforwardly false, since a demon offering you a fiver to admire something would thereby make it valuable. Two: the Pascal's-mugging-ish set-ups, where it's *having* the attitude that pays — the belief, the confident credence, the "act as if p" rule — and everyone I've argued with agrees there's something off about the reason there without agreeing on what. Anyway, before I go and read a stack of actual proposed solutions I want the spec, so I can tell which of them are even aiming at the same target.

So: give me the desiderata a solution to the wrong kind of reasons problem has to meet to count as adequate — the cases it has to sort, the distinctions it can't collapse, the data it can't contradict — each with the case or consideration behind it, and with a note on which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just what one camp happens to want. Some things I want explicitly adjudicated rather than assumed. (a) Is it non-negotiable that the demon's prize gives you a genuine reason that's simply of the *wrong* kind, or is it a legitimate solution to say those aren't reasons at all? I keep meeting people who quietly redefine "reason" until the problem vanishes and I want to know whether that's a cop-out or the actual answer. (b) How much independence does the criterion need? "Right-kind reasons are the ones bearing on whether the attitude is fitting" is worthless if fittingness can't be spelt out without already having the distinction in hand. So what non-circular cash is even on offer, and does any of it survive the awkward cases — fearing the dog because being scared keeps you sharp, trusting someone because trusting makes the relationship work, intending to do it because intending solves your coordination problem — where the reason looks like it's about the state you'd end up in rather than about the object, but still sounds right-kind? (c) Does one distinction have to cover every attitude, or is a pluralism that says each attitude has its own correctness condition acceptable rather than lazy? And does the distinction survive being pushed onto intentions and plans, onto caring about something, onto second-order attitudes (the Frankfurt-style case where you want to want the drug so you can understand your patient), and onto credences? I especially want to know whether the problem arises for credences at all, given that they're graded and accuracy-normed — if it doesn't, why not, and if it does, what does a demonic-prize-for-a-credence case even look like? (d) The account has to tell the would-be buck-passer something usable, without collapsing into "value = what there's a right-kind reason to desire", which just relocates the bump — or else say why that whole ambition was hopeless.

Then the part I care most about: which subset of these desiderata do you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable? My hunch is that "genuinely independent criterion" + "one criterion for all the attitudes" + "wrong-kind reasons really are reasons" can't all be had together, and that what gets quietly dropped is generality — but I want your read on where the pressure actually sits, and which single requirement does the most work in knocking candidates out. Don't give me a big table or anything resembling a literature review; but do spend a couple of sentences each on how the three broad styles of answer look against the list: the object-given vs state-given line, the "a right-kind reason is one you could base the attitude on / one that could be a premise of good reasoning" line, and the "it turns on what the attitude is directed at or represents" line. And please flag it if you think one of my desiderata is itself confused, cos I'd rather find that out now.
