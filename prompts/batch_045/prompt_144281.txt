My roommate and I got into a massive argument at dinner last night and I need someone to settle it properly. The question: if you already grant that animal suffering matters at all, is switching from beef to chicken actually an improvement, or does it just relocate the harm?

My case was the usual one: a steer needs many times the feed per pound of meat that a broiler does, all that soy and corn has to be grown somewhere, and growing it kills field mice, voles, nesting birds and an absurd number of insects — plus the cow lives a year or two instead of six weeks, so more suffering-days per animal. Beef means way more cropped land means way more wild animal death.

Her counter, which I couldnt knock down, was that a chicken is about a kilogram and a cow is several hundred. For the same meat you need a couple hundred chickens. If the currency is deaths or lives rather than acres, beef-to-chicken means vastly more individuals created, confined and killed — and those birds are bred to grow so fast that plenty of them are in chronic pain for the last stretch of their lives, while a pasture-raised cow has a decent year and one bad afternoon. On her accounting chicken is worse in every currency except land, and land only matters because of crop deaths, which are mostly insects, whose lives are short and cheap.

The bit that actually broke us was a pair of stipulations I threw out to test her: (1) a new pesticide that kills insects instantly and with no distress whatsoever, but works so well that farmers spray twice as often and kill three times as many per hectare; (2) a decent field study showing an acre of cereal supports about as many mice and voles as an acre of rough pasture, so the cropland is uniquely full of dying rodents premise is just false. With both on the table my land-use argument basically evaporates and I'm not sure what I have left.

So write the dialogue between us two — sharp, neither a strawman, actually responding to each other rather than trading slogans. Get past the opening moves into what the currency even is (deaths, suffering-days, number of lives that are net-negative, something about creating rather than ending lives), whether bringing a creature into existence is a benefit or a harm when its life is mostly bad, what the comparison looks like if you take seriously that most wild animal lives might be net-negative — in which case more land under the plough might come out as good — and whether the painless-pesticide case shows my framing was never really about the numbers at all. Fine if one of us concedes one thing and not everything, and I'd love it to end with the disagreement narrowed to a single precise claim we both now agree is the real one. Call us Acre and Headcount if you want names. Around 900 words, ordinary speech, no citations or studies — we've stipulated all the empirical stuff already.
