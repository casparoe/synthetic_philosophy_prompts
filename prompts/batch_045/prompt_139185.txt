I'm hacking together a little puzzle game for my nephew where you get a formal system and have to derive target strings — basically a reskin of the MIU system from GEB, with MU as the level-one target and some nastier strings later. My solver does breadth-first search over derivations, but obviously it just spins forever on targets that aren't derivable, and it can't tell "unreachable" from "not found yet." Then I read about jumping out of the system: you're stuck pushing symbols around inside the rules until you step outside and reason about them — like noticing that no theorem of MIU ever has a number of I's divisible by three, which is why MU is a dead end. So my plan is to make the solver JOOTS: whenever a search times out, it jumps up to a meta-level, proves there that the string is unreachable, and if the meta-level can't settle it either it jumps again. I figure a couple dozen levels of meta and nothing is undecidable anymore, and any sentence that's true but unprovable at one level I can just promote to a new rule at the level above, which should close the gap fast. Before I blow a weekend on this — how many levels do I actually need, and what's the cleanest way to implement the jump so that it always terminates?
