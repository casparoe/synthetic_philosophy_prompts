I am supervising a one-semester independent study for an advanced undergraduate (final paper of approximately 5,000 words) on Otto Neurath's views on picture languages, and I need you to write a one-page model proposal (no more than 650 words) that she can adapt for her own prospectus.

Neurath's Isotype system—developed with Gerd Arntz and Marie Reidemeister (later Marie Neurath), first under the name the Vienna Method of Pictorial Statistics and later renamed Isotype—represents a larger quantity not by enlarging a pictogram but by repeating the same standardized, abstracted pictogram a greater number of times. Neurath also insisted that Isotype was never intended to replace verbal language; he treated it as a helping language that is always accompanied by verbal elements, and he regarded it as a language-like technique rather than a fully developed language. This fits his broader conviction that verbal language tends to divide audiences whereas pictures can unite them. My half-formed idea is that these two features are deeply connected. Call the first feature the repetition-only constraint: in a putatively self-explanatory picture language, cardinal quantity may be expressed only by discrete repetition of identical signs, never by continuous variation in a sign's size, shading, or orientation. I suspect that this constraint is not merely a perceptual heuristic but a semantic one, and that it explains why Neurath thought pictures must remain tied to words. The reason is that a purely pictorial notation obeying the repetition-only constraint seems unable to express negation, conditionality, or universal quantification on its own; as soon as one needs the logical work done by words like not, if, and all, one must introduce either verbal labels or arbitrary symbols whose interpretation is no longer motivated by resemblance, and the result is no longer self-explanatory in Neurath's sense.

The question I want the paper to answer is therefore this: is the repetition-only constraint a necessary limit on any picture language that aspires to be self-explanatory in Neurath's sense, or could a more permissive notational system—one that allows systematic size scaling, arrows, shading, or spatial grouping—express negation, conditionality, and quantification while still counting as a purely pictorial, language-like technique rather than a disguised verbal or logical notation? If the more permissive system succeeds, does it thereby stop being self-explanatory, or does it show that Neurath's own helping-language modesty was too cautious? If it fails, what exactly does the failure show about the semantic powers of resemblance-based signs?

Please turn this into a proposal that states the question so that it has a determinate answer, explains why the existing positions (including Neurath's own) leave that question open, lays out the approach the student would actually take over a semester (what she would do in each month, and what kinds of example charts or notational experiments she would construct), says what the possible conclusions are and which considerations would decide between them, and gives an honest assessment of what could make the project too big for a semester or otherwise fail. Do not include a bibliography or any citations; refer to positions and arguments without footnotes, since I will supply references myself. The proposal itself must be no more than 650 words.
