i need a villanelle for the last session of my Hegel reading group, on the master-slave dialectic — recognition, the whole lordship-and-bondage bit. The idea: the two refrains are one line belonging to the master and one to the slave, and they stay word for word identical every time they come back, but the master's line should get thinner and more hollow with each return (he gets his recognition from someone who doesn't count, so his self-certainty quietly drains out of the same sentence) while the slave's line gets heavier and more solid, because she's the one who actually works the thing and gets formed by it. The object she labours on should turn up in the middle lines but never get a refrain line of it's own, so it does the mediating without ever getting to speak. The slave's line should also carry that note of absolute fear, the fear of death that shakes everything loose, without ever naming it. In the final quatrain both refrains land together and it should be clear they've traded places, though nobody says so out loud — and don't smooth it into a warm moment of mutual recognition at the end, stop at the reversal. Someone who's read the chapter should be able to tell which line is which and why the turn happened, and the form itself should be doing that work rather than a rhymed paraphrase of it.
