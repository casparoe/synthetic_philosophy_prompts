The reading group I'm in is doing a month on self-reference, and I got handed Russell's 1908 paper "Mathematical Logic as Based on the Theory of Types" to present, paired with Escher's Drawing Hands. The paradoxes at the start are fine, but this bit near the end of the first section is where I lose the thread, and its the bit I actually need for the Escher half:

Thus all our contradictions have in common the assumption of a totality such that, if it were legitimate, it would at once be enlarged by new members defined in terms of itself. This leads us to the rule: "Whatever involves all of a collection must not be one of the collection;" or, conversely: "If, provided a certain collection had a total, it would have members only definable in terms of that total, then the said collection has no total."

(And his footnote: "When I say that a collection has no total, I mean that statements about all its members are nonsense. Furthermore, it will be found that the use of this principle requires the distinction of all and any considered in Section II.")

So two things: help me understand what the rule is really saying, and then tell me what, if anything, it has to do with the inviolate level in Drawing Hands. The specific things I'm chewing on:

1. What does "involves" mean — mentions, quantifies over, or *depends on for its definition*? The converse clause ("members only definable in terms of that total") pushes me toward the definitional reading, but then I made up a test case to check myself and it broke: a library catalogue that lists every book in the library except catalogues. It doesn't involve *all* of the collection, so read literally the rule shouldn't bar it from being one of the collection — and yet it still ping-pongs: put it on the shelf and it's a catalogue, so it excludes itself, and once excluded it can be listed after all. Is my self-excluding catalogue a genuine counterexample to the rule as stated, or is it caught by some reading of "involves" I'm missing? And is the ping-pong it produces the same animal as Russell's "enlargement," or a different one?

2. Is the rule a ban on self-reference, or a ban on *totalities* — a claim about what sentences may say, or about which collections exist? This matters a lot for the picture: Drawing Hands is obviously self-referential in some sense, and its obviously not nonsense, so if the rule is a self-reference ban then the lithograph shouldn't be possible, and it is.

3. The inviolate level. As I've been using the term in my notes (correct me if I've got it wrong): it's the level the loop can't reach — the one that draws without being drawn, that makes the loop possible rather than being caught in it. People say the sheet of paper is "the" level, or that it's Escher. But Russell seems to be saying the opposite: whatever totality you name, statements about it generate new members outside it, forever, so "all propositions" is a meaningless phrase and there's no final outside. So: is the inviolate level in Drawing Hands a *discovery* (there really is a level the picture can't touch) or a *stipulation* (we just stop asking, and call that level inviolate)? And can a hierarchy be tangled while having no single inviolate level — only what I've started calling a non-vanishing outside, where at every level there's something not at that level but no level is safe? Is that coherent, or does it just collapse into "the hierarchy is open upward"?

4. Last one, about the picture specifically: each hand is the drawer or the drawn depending on where you look, and the cuffs they emerge from are part of the drawing too. Does Russell's machinery say anything about a case where the *activity* and the *object* trade places, as opposed to the liar sentence where one sentence does both at once? I feel like these are different failure modes and can't say why.

Please don't give me background on the history or point me at secondary lit — I need to stand up in front of six people and explain the rule in my own words and then say something true about the picture. And if either of my made-up framings (self-excluding catalogue, non-vanishing outside) is confused, say which and why.
