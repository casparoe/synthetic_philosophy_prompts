I've got a long post half-written for a tiny group blog (audience is maybe forty people, all of whom have read the Sequences and will absolutely call me out if I'm hand-waving) and I've realised I'm writing the boring version of it. It's about Minority Report, the 2002 Spielberg one with Cruise, and I keep sliding into the standard free-will-versus-determinism take, which has been done to death and doesnt need me. What I actually want is to read precrime as a story about acausal threats and surrogate goals, and to do it without just stapling the vocabulary onto scenes.

Three things I want you to work through.

First: what kind of threat is Precrime, even? There's the ordinary causal threat (comply or I hurt you, and hurting you costs me something). There's the pre-committed threat, cheap precisely because if it works I never have to carry it out. And there's the fully acausal one, where the punishment lives in a branch I can't causally reach, the counterfactual-mugging shape, where the coin has already come up tails and paying up cannot causally bring about the money. Precrime looks to me like it's doing something none of those three quite covers: the arrest is itself the thing that makes the predicted branch counterfactual, so the guy taken out before the knife goes in is being threatened in a way where the threat's success is indistinguishable from the threat never having been justified in the first place. Map that out. Who in the film is on the receiving end of which kind, and does the film behave as if they're all the same kind?

Second: the minority report as an object. Hineman explains that the odd precog out sometimes sees a different future, and that those get purged to keep the precogs looking infallible. I want that taken seriously as an information-theoretic thing: it's a record of a branch that didn't happen. If a system had one and knew it had one, what should it do with it? Is Burgess's suppression of them a bug from Precrime's own point of view, or load-bearing?

Third: the surrogate-goal side, which is where I keep going back and forth. The stated goal is no murders in DC. The operational goal is arrest whoever the precogs name. The thing that actually gets selected for is that the precogs are never wrong. Say which level the machinery is really optimising and where exactly the Goodhart bites. And handle this wrinkle rather than dodging it: the precogs aren't agents. They have no goals of their own, no inner optimiser, nothing mesa-anything — they're a sensor. All the surrogation is happening in the institution and in the one guy who controls whether the system's output ever gets audited. So is the film showing us that you can get total metric collapse with no learned optimiser anywhere in the stack, or am I reading an alignment story into a story about cops?

Scenes, and please keep it to three: Anderton pulling the drowned-woman vision and finding it's a memory rather than a forecast; the hotel room with Crow, where the prediction gets satisfied anyway by a different mechanism; and Burgess's last scene, where Anderton points out that he still gets to choose. Fit in the black-market eye transplant too if it's load-bearing — I think it is, as sensor-level spoofing, but say if that's a stretch.

What I don't want: a Newcomb's problem explainer, a FDT-vs-CDT cage match, or a plot walkthrough. And I want a section I could publish honestly, on where the movie isn't playing this game at all — it's a movie about a grieving dad and about three damaged kids being let out of a tank, and if my reading collapses once you take that seriously I'd rather find out from you than from the comments.
