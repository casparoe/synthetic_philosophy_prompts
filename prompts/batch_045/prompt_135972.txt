I'm a postdoc, and last year three groups in my field — mine among them — converged independently on essentially the same method within about five months. Since then I have watched it was in the air do two opposite jobs in the same conversation: comfort (you would have got there anyway, so do not despair) and diminishment (you would have got there anyway, so it was not really yours). I have been asked to draft the credit paragraph for a joint retrospective, and I would rather work from a specification than from a slogan.

So: what would a theory of discovery inevitability — call it ripeness — have to deliver before it could settle anything about credit? Give me the desiderata, each with the case or consideration behind it, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are preferences of one camp. My provisional list, which you should correct, extend, or delete from freely: (i) it has to separate the finding from the package — the formalism, notation, proof technique or choice of system the finding arrived wrapped in — since one can be ripe while the other is not; (ii) it has to say how much weight to give n-fold multiplicity as against a scatter of near-misses, given that we only ever see the near-misses that surfaced; (iii) it has to distinguish removing one person from removing an entire research programme, and it has to fix a time window, since within five years and within fifty years are different claims; (iv) it has to say whether ripeness is a claim about a community's shared inputs or about the result's being there regardless; (v) it must not let the outcome's being known after the fact count as evidence that it was coming. Flag any pair of requirements you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and tell me which single requirement would knock out the most of the ways people actually talk about this.

Two further things. Give me your confidence on each item, and be openly critical rather than accommodating: if ripe collapses into highly probable given shared inputs and does no further work, or if a graded inevitability score cannot in principle do the job I want of it, say so plainly instead of handing me a tidy list. British English, please.
