I have recently joined the board of a 270-year-old land endowment whose holdings lie along a tidal estuary, and we must now decide whether to keep or divest farmland that will be increasingly exposed to saltwater intrusion over the next century. Every argument in the room splits along the same fault line: our hydrologist holds that the river, the sediment load, and the elevation of the marshes fix the outcome whatever we do, while our estate historian holds that what actually happened turned on three or four decisions by three or four people. Before I read further into the literature, I want the specification.

Write the desiderata a theory of historical explanation must meet to be adequate for that purpose: what it has to let me say about the relative weight of slow structures such as geology, climate, demography, and the built environment, as against events and individuals, with the case or consideration behind each requirement. Two things matter most to me. First, some way of telling, within a single case, whether I am looking at a structure working itself out or at a string of contingent events; I have been calling this the recognition problem, and my own candidate test is the reversal window, meaning how long the pattern takes to reassert itself after a deliberate intervention, if it reasserts itself at all. Say whether that test belongs on the list and what it would systematically miss. Second, a verdict on which of the demands usually made in this area are genuine desiderata and which are the prejudices of one camp: forward predictiveness, quantifiability, refusing to let individuals count as causes at all, treating the explanation as scale-invariant, and the rest.

Mark each requirement as non-negotiable or as a preference, and flag any subset you take to be jointly unsatisfiable, since that would be the most useful thing you could tell me. Assume throughout that the theory is to be used prospectively by an agent who has to act now; that fixes what adequate means here. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases. I will score the contending approaches myself.
