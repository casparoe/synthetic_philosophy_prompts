I am a junior at a large public high school, and I serve on the committee that is rewriting our student government's constitution. I have become fairly convinced by the case for selecting at least some representatives by lottery rather than by election, and equally convinced that pure rotation discards something real, since it takes most of a year to learn where anything is kept and whom to ask. So I have drafted a compromise that I cannot find defended anywhere, and I would like to test it on my classmates before I defend it to the committee.

Under my proposal the main chamber is chosen by lot from the whole student body for a single non-renewable term, except that the outgoing chamber may designate one of its own members to stay on for one additional year as a non-voting advisor who holds the files, makes the introductions, and is barred from holding any other office afterward. I call it the memory seat. A friend on the committee objects that this just manufactures a miniature career politician with an inside track, and that term limits do not abolish careerism so much as redirect it, away from voters and toward the people who will be in a position to hand you something after you leave.

Could you design a ninety-minute session for our political philosophy club, eighteen students from all four grades, one double block, no advance reading, in which they actually operate my mechanism on a decision that binds us for real rather than hearing me argue for it? The small real stake I have available is about eighty dollars of unspent club funds, and I am willing to let the session genuinely decide how it is spent. Please keep any lecture out of the front of the room: introduce the two selection procedures only as Procedure A and Procedure B, described purely by their mechanics, and hold the terms sortition, term limits, and career politician back until the debrief.

In your answer I would like the setup and printable materials, a minute-by-minute timeline, any role or instruction cards, the payoff and information structure that generates the pressure, a short script for the few moments when I do have to speak as facilitator, and a debrief that maps particular moments in the session onto the competing claims about descriptive representation, accumulated expertise, and the incentive to cultivate a future patron. Please also tell me candidly what will go wrong the first time I run this and how to handle each failure, and be frank about which part of my argument the activity cannot test at all, so that I do not oversell it to the committee.

Please write your response in American English.
