(indeterminacy-of-translation-flavoured, the "two unknowns and not enough equations" kind of thing.) she says: that's exactly what triangulation is supposed to rule out. two creatures both responding to the same object in the world; the causal lines running from the object to each of them converge on the object; the intersection is what fixes the content of their responses. like two surveyors sighting a distant tree from different stations — neither can fix its position alone, but together they do. so content isn't determined from the inside out by the interpreter's theory of one speaker; it's anchored from outside, by the thing itself plus a second line onto it. and that, she says, is also why content can't be private and why thought essentially involves more than one creature.
