I've been working through the radical probabilism posts — Jeffrey updates, rigidity, the whole "you never learn anything with certainty" package — and I think I've built an argument that shows the package is empty. I'd rather it be broken.

Setup, the usual cloth-in-bad-light case. H = the cloth is red. E = "it looks reddish to me right now". My experience moves P(E) from 0.3 to 0.8, and it doesn't touch anything else directly, so I hold P(H|E) and P(H|¬E) fixed and get P'(H) = 0.8·P(H|E) + 0.2·P(H|¬E). Fine.

(1) There's always a finer description of the same experience available: the parition into exact total phenomenal characters {e_j}. And it refines {E, ¬E} — every e_j settles whether E.
(2) After the experience I'm certain which one I'm in. Call it e*. That's just what introspection is; I know how the cloth looks to me now.
(3) Jeffrey-conditioning on a partition one of whose cells has gone to 1 is just ordinary conditionalization. So P'(H) = P(H|e*).
(4) e* entails E, but P(H|e*) ≠ P(H|E): the exact shade, the sharpness, how the colour sits in the folds, is precisely where the information about the cloth lives. So the coarse update and the fine one give different answers, sometimes wildly different.
(5) Both are supposed to be the update this experience mandates. They can't both be.
(6) And (1) generalises: pick any coarse partition you like, there's a finer one, and at the limit I'm certain of the cell. So JC is only ever correct on the maximally fine partition.
(7) Therefore there is no such thing as uncertain evidence. Every update is really conditionalization on a proposition about how things presently seem to you, and rigidity and the weighted average and radical probabilism are either vacuous or wrong.

I dont believe (7). I'd like to keep (2) — that's about as close to bedrock as I get — and (3) is basically bookkeeping, so I'd sooner give up the setup or (6). Where does it actually break, and what does the fix cost me?
