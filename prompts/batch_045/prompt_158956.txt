I help run a volunteer bike workshop, and after a blowup last month about our unwritten rules I've somehow ended up in charge of new-volunteer onboarding, which now includes a half-hour slot on why we do things the way we do and how that ever changes. I want to build it around the Lewis-style account of convention as an equilibrium of a coordination problem rather than around rules, agreements and enforcement. Don't explain the account to me — plan how I'd get it across to adults with no philosophy, most of whom think an unwritten rule is either a habit or just peer pressure, and who assume all our rules are the same kind of thing when I'm pretty sure they aren't.

What I need: the order the pieces have to land in and what breaks if two of them get swapped; which of our two contested rules should carry the whole session — (a) whoever last used a tool leaves it out on the bench overnight instead of putting it back, or (b) nobody takes donated parts home during their first month — and what the carrying example has to have and has to lack so it doesn't smuggle in a second layer too early; the misconceptions to expect at each stage (I'm guessing "if its arbitrary then I don't have to follow it" and "we'd just need everyone to agree to switch at once") and the point in the sequence where each should be headed off; one closing question that shows me they've got the expectations structure and not just the vocabulary; and what I should openly refuse to go into at this level.
