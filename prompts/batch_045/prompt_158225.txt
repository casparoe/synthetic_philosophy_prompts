I have just joined the staff of our region's Common Resources Board, the body that decides whether a resource should be brought under a formal commons regime — defined membership, rules governing use, a monitoring roster, graduated sanctions, and in some cases a levy — or left to private ordering, ordinary pricing, or referral to the finance department as a public good to be tax-funded. The enabling statute is maddeningly vague, so the real content is in our precedents, and I am trying to work out what we have been doing before the next petition arrives. The Board has ruled in: the inshore groundfish stock; a network of farmer-built irrigation canals, where the worry was as much about who clears the silt as about who draws the water; an alpine pasture held under medieval common rights; the city airshed's capacity to absorb particulates; and the valley's darkness and quiet at night, even though any single porch light or late delivery is trivially small, because the Board held that cumulative trivial subtractions count. It has ruled out: the national time-signal broadcast, referred upward as a pure public good with nothing to appropriate; a patented seed variety, on the ground that exclusion is cheap and the market can order it; a congested toll-free causeway, on the ground that rivalry at peak is a pricing problem rather than a commons problem; an openly licensed mapping library used by hundreds of firms, on the combined grounds that use subtracts nothing and that its users had already organized a foundation that paid for upkeep without visible free-riding; and a historic district in which every façade owner holds a veto over alterations, which the Board called the mirror image of a commons — too much exclusion rather than too little — and dealt with by recommending pooling of rights instead of a commons regime.

The new petition concerns an openly licensed clinical-records library on which every hospital in the region, two municipal agencies, and a dozen or so private clinics now depend. It is maintained by one unpaid volunteer who has announced that she will stop at the end of the year. Copying and use are free and lawful, and one hospital's use subtracts nothing from another's; but the maintenance — security patching, standards updates, the review of contributed fixes — is unpaid, and once a fix is contributed upstream no one can be excluded from its benefit. Fourteen identifiable institutional users have petitioned the Board to place the library under a commons regime with a compulsory maintenance levy, a governance charter, monitoring of contributions, and graduated sanctions for non-payment. Two wrinkles: there is a live fork option, so any user threatened with a levy can in principle exit and carry the code off rather than pay, which cuts both ways as bargaining power and as an enforcement problem; and the Board's toolkit — caps, rosters, sanctions — was built entirely to limit withdrawal, so a levy compelling contribution would be an instrument we have never used, and our statute speaks only of regulating appropriation.

What I need is a recommendation, not a general discussion of public goods. First, set out the two or three candidate rules that fit our whole track record equally well — something like a rivalry-and-excludability rule keyed to subtractability in use, a collective-action rule keyed to whether dispersed actors are producing an outcome none of them wants, and an institutional rule keyed to whether there is a bounded, identifiable, mutually monitoring community capable of crafting rules — and say what each predicts here. Then tell me which precedent is genuinely the closest analog: the open-source mapping library we waved through, or the irrigation canals and the night sky we took in. Then give me the decision that best coheres with what we have already done, and if our precedents really do underdetermine it, say plainly what principle we would be adopting with each choice — including whether ruling this in commits us to treating every under-maintained open library anywhere as a commons, and whether that floodgates worry is itself a reason. Finally, name the one further petition that would best separate the surviving rules from each other, and tell me what we should expect to learn from it.
