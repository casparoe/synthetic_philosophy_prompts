I've been reading about functionalism on my own — no formal training, just books and argument sketches I've found — and the worry I keep coming back to isn't the usual one about absent experience. It's what I think of as the mirror image of multiple realizability: call it divergent realization. Multiple realizability is the many-to-one point, that very different physical states can all realize the same mental state. But the relation looks like it can run the other way too. Take some neural state N in a creature wired the way ours are: it is brought about by tissue damage and goes on to bring about withdrawal, avoidance learning, and the rest of what we count as the pain role. Now imagine that same state N, intrinsically unchanged, sitting in a differently arranged surrounding network — after an anomalous rewiring, say — where it is instead brought on by food in the mouth and goes on to bring about chewing, salivation and approach. Nothing about N itself differs between the two cases; only what surrounds it does. So if a mental state just is its causal role, one and the same physical state seems to realize pain in one setting and something quite unlike pain in the other, and which mental state is present is not settled by the physical state doing the realizing.

Please argue this as forcefully as you can: that the case exposes a defect in functionalism the standard objections miss — roughly, that the assignment of roles is extrinsic in a way that either empties the theory out, leaving only the claim that whatever plays the role plays the role, or forces it to lean on a non-functional constraint about which surrounding system counts, which it cannot supply without going in a circle. Argue it in your own terms; please don't give me a bibliography, a reading list, or the names of papers, since I have no way to look anything up and I want the argument rather than the literature. And be straight with me rather than polite: if the case as I have set it up is confused, or rests on a mistake about what functionalists actually claim, or if the real difficulty lies somewhere else nearby, say so plainly and give me the strongest nearby argument instead. Mark where you are confident and where you are extrapolating — I would rather have a strong argument with its weak joints labeled than a smooth one I can't check.
