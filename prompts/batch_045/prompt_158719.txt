I'm doing my EPQ on free will and I've got a pile of gut reactions to cases that I cant get to add up, so I need a principle to test them against.

Start with the classic set-up: someone's standing by who'd have made you do the thing if you hadn't been about to do it, but you do it on your own anyway. So: there's a chip in my head during a mock exam; if I'd been about to not cheat it would of fired and made me cheat; I cheat by myself. My verdict: definitely my fault.

Same again, but if it had fired it would have made me copy off the person behind me instead of the person next to me — so technically I could have done otherwise, just by cheating differently. Still my fault, and I don't think "he could have copied off a different desk" is the bit that's doing the work here.

The praise version: the chip would have made me help someone up who got shoved over; I help them anyway. I deserve credit, but it feels a bit hollow, less than if there'd been no chip. Which sits badly with giving full blame in the first one.

The chip was broken and wouldn't have fired: still my fault.

No chip: someone brought up from birth in a strange cult so that they were bound to want X, they do X, and they've thought about it properly and still endorse it. Nothing's standing by making them do it. Not their fault — or way, way less.

No chip, but a recommendation algorithm knows me well enough that it only ever shows me the option I'd have picked anyway, so the other one was never on screen. I pick the thing. I want to say less their fault, or that the app shares it — and I know that's basically the same shape as the first case, which is the bit I really can't square.

My rough principle: you're on the hook when you did it for your own reasons and nothing actually made you do it, and a backup plan waiting in the wings isn't the same as making you. But that seems to say the cult person is on the hook too, which I don't want.

So: is there one principle that gets all six? What does it say about the case I keep flip-flopping on — the cheating one where I only find out afterwards that the chip was switched off the whole time? Give me a case it decides that I haven't thought of, so I can try to break it. And be straight with me about which of my verdicts it can't keep.
