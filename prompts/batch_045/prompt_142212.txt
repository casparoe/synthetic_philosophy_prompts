I've been reading up on Hofstadter's superrationality for a reading group I'm in and I honestly dont see what's new about it. The way I've got it: a superrational player says, my situation and the other guy's are the same, so whatever reasoning gets me to an answer gets him to the same answer, so I should just pick the best symmetric outcome. But isn't that just the symmetric Nash equilibrium? And if so it buys you nothing in the one-shot prisoner's dilemma, where mutual defection is the only symmetric equilibrium anyway and defecting is better for me no matter what he does. I also tried it on a version where one player's payoffs are nudged by a dollar so the two aren't literally running the same computation, and it just seems to stop having anything to say, which makes me think it's only even defined for perfectly symmetric games. So: superrationality applies only where Nash already applies, and there it picks what Nash picks, plus a warm feeling about people who reason like you. Where am I going wrong?
