I am a novelist, and I have agreed to run a single ninety-minute breakout session at a writers' conference next spring: sixteen adult fiction writers, a few of whom play online games regularly and a few of whom have never touched one, in a room with a whiteboard, index cards, pens, and no dependable internet. It has been advertised to them as a workshop exercise for writers, not as a lecture, and I intend to keep it that way. My current novel is set largely inside a persistent online world, so the session is also research: the sentences people produce when they are being robbed of something they simultaneously insist is fictional are precisely the sentences my narrator needs, and I would rather harvest those in a room than manufacture them at a desk.

My own thinking keeps snagging on two features of the world I have built, and I want those features to structure the exercise rather than being asserted in it. First: in this world almost anything can be duplicated at no cost, but anything a player has made herself is attuned to her. It cannot be given, sold, or stolen; it can only be destroyed. Destroying it removes nothing from anyone else's holdings and reduces no one's stock of stuff, yet it removes a capacity that exactly one person could exercise. Second: the world is backed up every few minutes, so any loss can in principle be reversed at the level of the record — same entry, same numbers, same item in the same place. My character is restored, and still feels that something was taken from her. I do not want the exercise to settle that in advance, either by staging her as obviously right or by staging her as obviously confused.

What I need from you is a runnable design. Concretely:

The world's rule-set, short enough to post on a board, together with a production phase in which participants do real work in the room — drafting, drawing, writing to a constraint — and that work creates items which exist nowhere except as entries in a ledger held by a designated server. Participants should be able to point at nothing.

An event phase in which losses occur in distinguishable shapes: destruction of an attuned item; duplication of an unattuned one, so that nobody is deprived but exclusivity is gone; and a restore-from-backup that returns the item numerically intact. Add a fourth shape only if you think it earns its place — I have been toying with a restore that returns the item but not the record of who made it. These should be administered without anyone announcing what is being tested, and ideally without the participants knowing which shape any given player has suffered.

A claims phase with small but real stakes, since the writers in the room care about very few things more than being read: items should be redeemable for something genuinely wanted — a place in the reading order, time in a critique queue, the right to have your page read aloud — and participants should have to choose among forms of repair: exact restoration, a replacement, a public record of the loss, an apology, or nothing at all.

Then the analysis I cannot do myself: why the shapes should be expected to land differently from one another, and which variable is actually doing the work when they do — labour invested, uniqueness of the record, irreversibility, exclusivity of use, or whether the loser knew a backup existed. Tell me how the rules should be arranged so that the participant who arrives certain it is all only data is put in a position where she must act on some other assumption rather than merely assert her view, and say what I should do if she simply refuses to invest.

Give me a debrief that maps particular moments in the session onto the distinctions at issue, naming which moment is meant to move intuitions and which will not; a candid account of what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — participants gaming the facilitator, someone treating the whole thing as improv comedy, the server role curdling into tyranny, pre-existing status hierarchies in the room leaking into the ledger — and how to handle each; and an honest statement of which part of the problem an exercise like this structurally cannot reproduce, and which I will therefore have to supply in prose. Close with a short list of the kinds of lines, gestures, or refusals to watch for, since those are what I am really there to collect.
