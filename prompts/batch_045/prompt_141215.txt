Hey — I need a document for a reading group I'm running on the timing of experience, and I'd rather you drafted it than me.

Setup: color phi. A red dot is flashed at one location; a fraction of a second later a green dot is flashed a few degrees away. Subjects report a single dot moving from the first location to the second that changes color somewhere in the middle of its path — that is, they report a color change at a time when only the red dot had been shown. Two camps: the Orwellians, who say there was a determinate experience at the time of the first dot and that the apparent mid-path change is a later revision of how that earlier experience is recalled and reported (experience first, revision after); and the Stalinesques, who say nothing with the content "red, then turning green mid-flight" is experienced until after the second dot is in, so there is no earlier experience of that content for anyone to have revised — the content gets settled only once the later input arrives. (Dennett's labels, roughly, but don't cite anything.)

What I want is the joint statement these two could actually sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration:

1. A numbered section of claims both can accept — about the psychophysics, about what the verbal report does and doesn't establish, about whether the distinction is empirical in the first place, and about which reading of "experience" is doing the work.
2. The residual disagreement stated twice, once in wording an Orwellian would sign and once in wording a Stalinesque would sign, so neither side can complain of being strawmanned.
3. The cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. I think they've been running two questions together and you should pull them apart: whether there is any experience at all at the time of the first dot, and whether whatever is there has the content the report ascribes to it.
4. For each crux, a case or manipulation both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

For (4) here's a design I've been kicking around, please use it: on some trials, at a variable delay after the second dot (roughly 50–400 ms), a brief patch is flashed at the midpoint of the apparent trajectory and the subject is cued to report the color the moving dot appeared to be at the midpoint; and a second version where the subject makes a speeded discrimination of the dot's color at the first location while the trial is still running. Add your own manipulations too, but I want at least these two with predictions attached.

Two things be honest about rather than papering over: whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal — e.g. whether Orwellian and Stalinesque come apart at all once both sides grant that the underlying neural processing is spread out in time and that reports are generated late — and whether there's a coherent third option (the experience is determinate but temporally smeared; or the revision is of something that was never experiential to begin with). If you conclude the whole thing collapses into a question about what we're willing to count as a subject's experience at a time, say so, and say what formulation would make it a testable question again.

Numbered agreed claims first, then a disagreements table, then cruxes with predictions. Under 1200 words, and no citations — I want the arguments, not the literature.
