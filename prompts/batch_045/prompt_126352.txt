I run a Friday paper club at work (I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy background beyond reading some LessWrong years ago), and I volunteered to do a 45-minute whiteboard session on Jeffrey conditionalization — updating when your evidence itself is uncertain. Usually 10-15 engineers show up. They all "know Bayes" in the sense that they've written a spam filter or read an A/B testing blog post, and basically none of them have thought about what happens when you don't become certain of anything, you just end up like 70% on the hypothesis because the evidence was fuzzy.

Don't explain it to me, plan how I should explain it. Specifically:

The thing I actually care about is a distinction I half-remember and might be getting wrong, so correct me if I'm misremembering it: there seem to be two different ways of handling uncertain evidence. One is the Jeffrey version — you get pushed to a new probability across a partition of possibilities and you hold fixed the conditional probability of your hypothesis given each cell (the "rigidity" thing, which I don't totally get — is it an assumption or a theorem?). The other is the version where you don't get told a new probability at all, you get told a likelihood ratio, like "this signal is 3x more likely if the thing is red than if it's green," and you multiply. A colleague told me these are secretly the same operation in disguise and another told me they come apart in cases that matter. I want the session to build to that question rather than answer it, but I need to know which order to set the two up in so the comparison actually clicks instead of looking like two formulas.

I also want to use two things from our actual work as the spine, because engineers zone out otherwise:
(a) order dependence — as far as I can tell, two Jeffrey updates in a row give you different results depending on which order you apply them, which is terrifying to anyone who thinks about state as a fold over an event log. There's a monitoring analogy here: our alert pipeline applies noisy signals one at a time and everyone quietly assumes the order doesn't matter.
(b) something I made up and can't find discussed anywhere: what do you do when you're not sure what your prior was? Like a service that restarted without persisting state — you need your old credence to do the update, but your memory of your own old credence is itself unreliable evidence about what it was. Is that just the same problem one level up, or is it a genuinely different failure mode? I'd like to use it as the closing thing, but only if it's not nonsense.
