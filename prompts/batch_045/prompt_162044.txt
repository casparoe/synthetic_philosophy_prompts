hey — I'm on a tiny committee that has to write internal guidance about which systems count in our welfare assessments (lab vertebrates, insects, and eventually maybe AI systems), and they asked me to write my own position down first so everyone can poke at it before we start arguing about actual policy. So here's my profile, roughly in the order I'd defend it:

1. Consciousness comes in degrees. There's no sharp line anywhere between a thermostat and a human — just a very long continuum.
2. Moral status also comes in degrees, and it's fixed entirely by degree of sentience. Species, rationality, social relationships, being alive: none of it adds anything on top.
3. Only valenced experience does the moral work. A being with incredibly rich but entirely non-valenced experience would have zero moral status.
4. I'm a functionalist about consciousness, so substrate doesn't matter in principle. But in practice I lean hard on evolutionary homology for animals — shared circuitry plus shared pain behaviour means shared experience — and I think current AI systems are almost certainly not sentient.
5. Death only harms a creature if it frustrates a future-directed desire, i.e. if the creature has some sense of itself over time. So making a merely momentary experiencer suffer is a harm to it; painlessly killing it isn't.
6. Practically: insect suffering is a real issue worth money and attention, but no number of insects could outweigh one human's comparable suffering. I'll swat a mosquito without a second thought and still think boiling a lobster alive is genuinely bad.
7. All sentient beings get equal consideration — no discount for species, and no discount for being made of silicon.
8. There's a determinate fact of the matter about whether a honeybee feels pain, even if we'll never be able to settle it.

I genuinely don't know where the trouble is, which is sort of the point. Go through the pairs and tell me which ones actually collide and how badly, which ones only look like they collide, and for each real collision what would have to give. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Rank them by severity rather than listing everything as equally bad, and don't just tell me the literature is split — for each one I want the cheapest fix and what it costs me elsewhere.
