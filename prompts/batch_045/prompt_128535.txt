Me and my mate had a two hour argument last night that started about a wasps nest in her shed and ended up about agriculture, and the annoying thing is we basically agreed on the policy the whole way through and disagreed about literally every reason for it.

She's on the ecosystem side: insecticides are bad because of what they do to everything else — pollinators, the birds and bats that eat the insects, the sheer amount of neurotoxic stuff we pour onto fields that we dont really understand, the fact that insect biomass seems to be crashing. I'm more on the individual-welfare side: what actually gets me isn't that the insects die, its how they die. Alot of the common stuff works by making them twitch and convulse and then lie paralysed for what might be hours or a couple of days, and I've started calling that gap the distress window — the stretch between being dosed and being incapacitated, in which the animal is in the sort of state we'd call agony in anything with a spine. We both want way less broad spectrum spraying. Then we got to replacements and it fell apart completely: she likes the targeted stuff — Bt, pheromone confusion, sterile insect releases — because it doesn't wreck everything around it, and I pointed out that some of it kills slower, that Bt supposedly stops them feeding and then they die over a day or three and growth regulators kill them at the next moult, so if what you care about is the distress window you might sometimes prefer the fast horrible neurotoxin, which sounded insane coming out of my own mouth and is the bit I most want help with. And then it got properly dark, because I said that if a pest insect's life is mostly starvation or being eaten alive from the inside by a parasitoid, the welfare view might actually favour suppressing the population before it exists, and she said that was the most horrifying thing I'd said all night.

Can you write the joint statement we should have produced instead of all that? Like an actual adversarial collaboration document:

- a numbered section of claims we could both sign — about what the chemicals do, about how uncertain the whole insect-mind question is, about why we both want less spraying, and about what each of us would count as evidence against our own side; lead it with whichever claim is doing the most work
- then a disagreements table: each disagreement stated in wording its own side would accept as a fair description of itself, a column on whether it's genuinely about facts, genuinely about values, or just about words, and for the real ones the crux underneath it plus a concrete case or piece of evidence we'd both agree bears on that crux and what each of us predicts it will show

The two cruxes I care about are the substitution one — swapping a broad spectrum neurotoxin for a slower, more targeted method, where one of us says less harm and the other says more, and I want both predictions written down even if mine is the embarrassing one — and the averted existence one, whether that's a real moral disagreement or just us using the word harm for different things. Don't tell us who's winning and don't conclude we were both secretly right. If I've got the biology wrong somewhere, stick it in a footnote at the bottom and write the document as if the facts were whatever they actually are. Start with the document itself, no intro, no commentary before or after.
