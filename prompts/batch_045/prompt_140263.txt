Two of us at a small research group that funds behaviour-change campaigns keep talking past each other. The question underneath our disagreement is the old one about individual contributions to collective harms, but I want to pin it to a case narrow enough that we can see which sub-question is actually carrying the weight. So: give me your credence on a single proposition, decomposed.

Assume the following as settled for the purpose of the estimate, so that we are not arguing about empirical guesswork:

(i) A single consumer's purchase at a supermarket changes the number of animals eventually bred and killed only by shifting a downstream bulk order: with probability roughly 0.005 their purchase triggers one extra batch (on the order of dozens of animals), and otherwise it makes no difference to any outcome whatsoever. Treat the expected effect as somewhere around 0.05 to 0.5 animals.

(ii) With probability roughly 0.03, one person's abstention — observed only by their own household, since nobody announces it publicly — leads one other person to reduce their consumption over the following years.

(iii) Choices are correlated rather than independent: conditional on your buying, there is a substantially elevated probability that around ten people in a demographically similar bloc also buy, because of shared background causes you do not influence.

(iv) The agent can never learn which of these obtained in their own case.

The proposition I want a number on:

P: A person in a high-income country who knows (i)–(iv) and buys factory-farmed meat anyway thereby does something morally wrong — where "wrong" means they failed to do what they had decisive reason to do, so that the failure is criticisable, and not merely that they did something less good than they could have done.

Treat P as resolved true if that description holds, and false if the most such a person can be accused of is suboptimality, poor character, or missed supererogatory goodness.

Give me a headline number, then the decomposition. At minimum I want separate probabilities, each with one line of justification and a rough confidence tag, for:

- whether a small and uncertain piece of expected difference-making can ground an obligation at all, rather than an optional good;
- whether non-outcome-based considerations — participating in a wrongful practice rather than merely causing harm, what the act expresses, being someone who knowingly profits from it — can ground such an obligation even when the causal difference is essentially nil, and whether that route is genuinely independent of the first one;
- whether an obligation requires that the agent could in principle know they were making a difference, and whether the stipulated unknowability in (iv) defeats it;
- whether group-level duties of the form "we ought not to be doing this" distribute to individuals in the right way, or whether that move is a category mistake;
- whether the common-cause correlation in (iii) adds anything morally once it is properly conditioned on, or whether counting it is simply an error;
- how sensitive the verdict is to the magnitude in (i): where is the threshold at which the act stops being required, and would your number flip if the expected effect were 0.001 animals, or 2 animals;
- how much of your headline number rides on (i)–(iii) holding outside the stipulation, on your own sense of the real-world version of the facts.

Then show how the terms combine into the headline number, flag any dependence between them that makes straightforward multiplication misleading, and tell me which single term the number is most sensitive to.

For calibration: I sit at about 0.65 on P, my colleague at about 0.2. Tell me which of the sub-questions above most likely explains the gap, and why the other candidate explanations do less work.

Two constraints on the answer. First, do not close with "it depends which normative framework you accept": name the framework your number comes from, and tell me how far the number would move under the runner-up framework you take most seriously. Second, state how confident you are in each component claim and in the headline number, and single out the one point in the whole answer you are least sure of — including any place where you think my stipulations have smuggled in a normative assumption I have not noticed.
