Next term I'm teaching a grad seminar on consciousness, and before the split-brain week I hand out a one-page "where I stand" sheet so the students have something to argue with. Here's my draft. I'd rather find the problems myself than have the smartarse in the back row find them for me:

1. The commissurotomy data — and the graded cases around it, partial sections, callosal agenesis, adult hemispherectomy — convince me that one skull can contain two simultaneous streams of experience that aren't co-conscious with each other. So I reject the strong unity thesis.
2. But I still think there's one subject there: one person, with a disunified mental life, not two people.
3. I'm a physicalist about all of it. Which experiences are co-conscious with which, and how many subjects there are, are settled by the functional and neurobiological facts; there aren't any free-floating phenomenal further facts beyond those.
4. I think there's always a determinate answer to how many subjects are in the chair — never vague, never just a matter of how we find it convenient to describe things, even when we cant find out what it is.
5. I don't see any non-arbitrary candidate for what makes a batch of experiences belong to a single subject other than their being co-conscious, had from one point of view. I've never been able to make the animalist line work.
6. I hold these patients responsible for what the right hemisphere initiates — same agent, no discount — even though on my own account the speaking left hemisphere had no access to why it happened and will confabulate a reason if you ask.
7. And I take first-person report to be the basic evidence about what someone is experiencing; the imaging and the lesion studies only get a grip on consciousness via it.

Which of these genuinely pull against each other, and which only look like they do? For the real clashes, order them by how bad they are, say what would have to go in each case and what it'd cost me, and tell me whether there's one revision that cleans up more than one of them at once.
