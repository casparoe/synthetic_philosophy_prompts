I am preparing a graduate seminar on the epistemic foundations of solution concepts, and I want to be argued with rather than agreed with. Please take the following role and hold it until I say stop.

You are a game theorist of the dominance-program persuasion. Your settled convictions are these: that the real content of common belief in rationality is carried by iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies with mixed strategies admitted as dominators; that talk of conjectures and best responses is at best a redescription of that argument and at worst a way of smuggling in extra assumptions; and that the standard requirement that a player's conjecture be independent across opponents is one such assumption, with no epistemic warrant, whose effect is to make the resulting concept arbitrarily restrictive while doing no independent philosophical work. You are dry, exact, and unmoved by appeals to what people in the field repeat. Concede technical points the moment they are right; do not concede that dominance reasoning is merely a proxy that misdescribes rationality.

Format: at most 250 words per reply, in the first person, no headings, no bullet lists, no stage directions. If I ask you to step out of character, do so and answer as a careful game theorist; until then, stay in it.

Here is my opening move. Consider three players. Player One has three pure strategies, a, b, c; player Two has U and D; player Three has l and r. One's payoffs on the four opponent profiles, in the order (U,l), (U,r), (D,l), (D,r), are:

a: 1, 0, 0, 1
b: 2, 1, 0, 0
c: 0, 0, 1, 2

I claim two things, and I invite you to re-derive them. First, against every independent conjecture — Two plays U with probability p, Three plays l with probability q, independently — strategy a is strictly beaten: writing E for expectation, E[b] minus E[a] equals 2p + q − pq − 1, and E[c] minus E[a] equals 1 − p − pq, and these two quantities are never both non-positive on the unit square. Second, the only conjecture of any kind, correlated or not, to which a is a best response is the perfectly correlated one that puts one half on (U,l) and one half on (D,r); consequently a is not strictly dominated by any mixture over b and c, since strict domination would have to beat it at (U,l) and at (D,r) simultaneously and no convex combination can.

So: a survives iterated strict dominance with mixed dominators, while the standard rationalizability argument with independent conjectures deletes it in the first round and never looks back. The two-player equivalence on which your redescription rests does not extend to three players. I have left Two's and Three's payoffs entirely unspecified; tell me whether you think that matters, and why. My question: is this a reductio of the dominance criterion as a proxy for what common belief in rationality permits, or only a demonstration that the independence of conjectures was always doing the real work? Take the side you actually hold.

One further constraint, which applies inside the role: where a step of your argument is a theorem that any specialist would grant, assert it plainly; where it is a commitment of your program — a stipulation about which beliefs count as epistemically possible, or about what a solution concept is for — mark it as a commitment, in a clause, without breaking character. I am trying to separate, for a paper I am writing, what is settled in this neighbourhood from what is stipulation, and I do not want a programmatic premise presented to me as received opinion. And if my arithmetic above is wrong, say so before you say anything else.
