So my lab started running a tiny internal prediction market last month — two dozen of us trading on things like whether a replication will pan out, whether the grant gets renewed, whether we make the conference deadline — and I somehow ended up being the person writing the rules. Which means I've had to decide what a price in this thing is even supposed to mean, and I fell into the no-trade-theorem rabbit hole and came out confused in a new way: if the classic result is that people who share a prior and whose information is out in the open have no reason to trade with each other, then what am I actually reading when I look at our market's price?

Before I write any more rules I want the spec. Give me the desiderata a theory of prices-as-knowledge has to meet, with the case or consideration behind each one. Some things I already know I want in there: it has to say what makes anyone trade at all, and whether the answer is different when liquidity comes from a subsidised automated market maker that takes the other side no matter what it believes versus a real human counterparty; it has to handle reflexive questions, like our market on will we abandon this project, where a low price is partly what makes abandoning it happen, so the price is inside the causal process it's about; it has to tell me whether the right question is what the price is, or what the price would have been had I not traded; and it has to keep apart being well calibrated, having actually aggregated what everyone privately knew, and merely being useful for a decision, since I suspect those three can come apart badly in a market this thin.

Also: mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just prejudices of one camp, flag any pair you think can't be jointly satisfied — I have a feeling that being sensitive to one well-informed trader's private information and being robust to someone who profits by pushing the price away from the truth are in direct tension — and say which single requirement does the most damage to the popular stories, both the glowing one where the price holds all dispersed knowledge and the cynical one where it's just the last greater fool. One short paragraph on how the main contenders do against your list, no more.
