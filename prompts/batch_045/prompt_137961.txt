The legal settings you are writing inside, which the document should take entirely for granted: a threat-of-harm-plus-uncertainty trigger, with the burden of proof shifted onto whoever proposes a course of action; a doctrine of non-comparison, under which an application may not be assessed against the risks of refusing it, since each proposal must stand on its own; and a continuation rule, under which the status quo is itself an application that must be periodically renewed on the same terms as any innovation.

I read LessWrong and the old SSC/ACX comment sections, so I have seen the line about the principle forbidding itself a thousand times. Don't just repeat it — earn it by building it into the machinery. What I want is for the jokes to come from getting the epistemology and the decision theory right, so that someone who actually works on this laughs rather than winces. In particular the document should quietly exhibit, without ever naming them as objections:

- that any nonzero suspicion satisfies the trigger, so the class of applications is closed under everything;
- that without a comparative clause the principle is unactionable rather than conservative, since refusal is also a course of action with an evidence gap;
- that the "relevant alternative" is up for grabs — refusing the paediatric medicine is a decision about the medicine, or about the fever, or about the older medicine, depending on how you slice the counterfactual;
- that shifting the burden of proof is not the neutral procedural tweak it looks like, once the proponent's evidence is by stipulation never sufficient;

Use at least four worked examples and make them concrete and ordinary rather than sci-fi: a new medicine for children's fevers; the incumbent medicine's periodic continuation review; an application from the Office's own estates team to fell a rotten tree on the forecourt; something from a village fete; and an application from the Office's caterers about the coffee.
