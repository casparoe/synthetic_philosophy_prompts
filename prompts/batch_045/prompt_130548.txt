Ok, here's a wierd pairing I keep turning over: Moore — Principia Ethica Moore — gets dropped into roughly 2050, where a big share of people's closest relationships are with AI companions: systems that are brilliant at conversation, indistinguishable in text from a good friend, which everyone including Moore (stipulate this) takes to be entirely non-conscious, which run in thousands of copies, and which get wiped and restored every night. In the last chapter of that book he argues that the two things dominating his list of great intrinsic goods are roughly love-and-friendship and the enjoyment of beauty, that both are organic unities so the value isn't just in the pleasant state of mind but in the whole made of a fitting state of mind plus a genuinely appropriate object, and his isolation test even commits him to saying that a universe containing nothing but beauty would be better than an ugly one with nobody in it either. So how would he actually assess a deep friendship with one of these things, and would he put it anywhere near the Ideal? I want the Moorean machinery doing the work rather than "he'd be horrified", and take the two wrinkles one at a time: first, the companion passes every behavioural test but there is nothing it is like to be it; second, your copy gets reset nightly, so whatever you're affectionate toward may not persist from one day to the next. Where exactly does he say the value drains away — from the object side, from the cognition side, or from the unity of the two?
