Bit of an odd one, but here goes. I hang around on a forum where people argue about whether a chatbot could ever be a subject, and every time the thread gets anywhere near interesting someone drops "cogito, ergo sum" — usually in Latin, which I gather is meant to be the knockdown. Three uses I've seen in the last month:

1. Someone arguing that a system doesn't need any special inner stuff, because if it can be brought to token "I am thinking, therefore I am", then the sentence is made true by the very saying of it, and that's the bedrock everything else gets built on.
2. Someone in a separate thread about no-self (the Buddhist-flavoured kind) using it as a reply to "there is no self": the reply was that there's no *self*, but there is definitely an *experience as of* a self, and the cogito only ever got you the second anyway, so it's no objection.
3. Someone using it to end a simulation/dream argument: even if this is all a dream or a render, that one thing still stands, so we've got a fixed point.

I'm pretty sure these are three different claims wearing one Latin tag, and I suspect at least one of them is smuggling something. Can you lay out the menu of precise things "I think therefore I am" might be asserting, from the trivially true to the wildly strong, and say for each whether it's actually defensible — particularly whether it survives the worries Descartes himself was supposed to be raising (a deceiver, doubt about even very simple reasoning, doubt about whether the "I" now is the same "I" in a minute's time)? Does the "therefore" matter, i.e. is it an inference that needs an extra premise, or something more like a self-verifying performance? Does it require already having a first-person concept, or could something without language or without any notion of itself have one?

Then the bit I actually care about: for each reading on your menu, does it give person 1 his conclusion about a system that merely produces the sentence, and which reading is person 2 quietly leaning on? I'm not after "it proves there's at least one thought, the end" — I want the carving, what's secured vs what's slipped in, and where exactly the edge between 1 and 2 lies.
