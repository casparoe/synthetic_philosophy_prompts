I am an applied statistician, and I would like an argument from evolutionary biology restated in the only vocabulary I am fluent in, together with an honest account of what that vocabulary cannot hold.

My working life is the design and analysis of cluster-randomized trials in which units interfere with one another. I am at home with potential outcomes indexed by an individual's own treatment and by the treatments of others in the same cluster; with partial interference, where interference operates within clusters but not across them; with the stock contrasts between direct, indirect (spillover), total and overall effects; with estimands defined at the individual level and at the cluster level, and with the way the answer to a question can change depending on which of them one declares to be the object of interest; with identification that rests on cross-world independence assumptions no experiment can check; with single-world intervention graphs; with the fact that a split of an effect into a direct and an indirect component is not unique but depends on which mediator one writes down; and with the difference between a variable that causes an outcome and a variable that merely predicts it. I have no biology beyond a first-year course, and I do not know the technical literature I am asking about.

The dispute I want translated is the one about the units or levels of selection. As I have encountered it, it contains at least four claims I need rendered:

1. The gene's-eye claim: the only thing that persists across generations and is copied with high fidelity is the gene; organisms are the disposable things genes build; asked whether a trait such as sterility in a worker or restraint in a forager exists "for the good of the group," the right question is what maximizes the expected number of copies of the allele, and the answer is that apparent design at the group level is really design at the gene level, with the group being a site of interaction rather than a beneficiary.
2. The multi-level claim: selection acts at several levels simultaneously, favouring selfishness within groups and altruism between groups, the two components pulling in opposite directions, with which one wins depending on population structure; and the two components can be separated and compared in a covariance or variance decomposition of total change.
3. The distinction between selection of and selection for: a trait can increase in frequency without being the thing selection was acting on, because it is correlated, for instance by physical linkage on a chromosome, with the trait that did the causal work.
4. A further distinction I have half-understood, between group-level quantities that are just aggregates of individual fitnesses and group-level traits that belong to the group and to no member of it — colony output, patch productivity, division of labour — where the second kind seems to require groups that themselves reproduce and transmit group-level properties to descendant groups.

To force the translation to do real work, here is a concrete case. A stored-grain pest lives in discrete patches. Patches periodically go extinct and are recolonized by founders drawn from a single surviving patch. A transgene is released into this population with four features: (i) carriers have roughly thirty percent lower lifetime fecundity than non-carriers within the same patch; (ii) carriers secrete a digestive enzyme that raises total patch biomass, by an amount that increases with the frequency of carriers in the patch; (iii) the enzyme is not expressed at all below a quorum of roughly forty percent carriers in a patch, so the group-level effect is strongly non-additive; (iv) transmission is imperfect, the construct being sometimes lost and sometimes transferred horizontally to unrelated individuals in the same patch.

What I want, in order:

A systematic restatement of claims 1 through 4 in my vocabulary, using my notation rather than explaining mine back to me. Where one of them becomes an estimand, name the estimand; where it becomes an assumption about an assignment mechanism, say which; where it becomes a claim about identification, say so.

Then, run the restatement on the case above. Which estimand or contrast corresponds to the within-group component, which to the between-group component, and which to the claim that the gene is the unit? What would have to be true of the world for "the gene is the unit of selection" to be the correct description here rather than "the group is," and is that a difference in what I would estimate, or only in how I would describe the same estimates?

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Say what translated cleanly, what translated only after being bent to fit, and what has no expression in potential outcomes, assignment mechanisms, mediation, and interference at all. In particular I want your view on: whether my vocabulary can register the difference between a thing that is copied and a thing whose properties cause differential reproduction, and what breaks if it cannot; what it costs that the "treatment" here is not assigned by anyone but is generated by the very process whose effect is being estimated, and is regenerated each generation from the previous generation's outcomes; whether my framework can say anything about cumulative adaptation, or about what the process is producing design for, as opposed to which contrasts are nonzero in a single round; and whether the choice of level is a modelling choice, like the choice of a mediator or of an estimand, or a fact about the world that my framework is silent on. My clusters do not have children; I suspect this matters more than anything else on the list.

Finally, the verdict. A colleague who is not a biologist, on hearing the dispute described, said it sounded like Simpson's paradox plus a variance decomposition, and that once you have both of those there is nothing left to argue about. I want to know how much of that is right and, to the extent it is wrong, whether what survives is a genuine question my vocabulary is too thin to pose, or a sign that the original question was doing less work than the people who ask it take it to be doing. If the former, tell me what minimal addition to my framework would let me pose it.

Please keep the translation honest: do not quietly substitute an assumption of my framework for one of theirs and call it a translation, and mark clearly where you are reconstructing a position rather than reporting one, and where you are uncertain.
