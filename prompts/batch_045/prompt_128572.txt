Draft the joint statement that a hypothetical frequentist and a single-case propensity theorist about objective chance could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Assume both reject any purely subjectivist reading of chance, and that they work under a stipulated background finding: for every chance setup in this world there are at most finitely many instantiations — no setup is ever repeated infinitely often, and some are repeated exactly once.

Open with a numbered section of claims both can sign, including at least: that single-case chance attributions are intelligible and not trivially false or trivially true; that chance is not reducible to any agent's credence or evidence; and that the finiteness stipulation is not by itself decisive between them. Then give a table of the residual disagreements, where each row states one crux, the frequentist's position in wording a frequentist would accept, and the propensity theorist's in wording they would accept.

The cruxes I want covered: whether the limiting-frequency analysis can pick out a unique hypothetical sequence for a given setup, or whether the sequence is irreducibly interest-relative and so must be supplied from outside the theory; whether a single-case disposition can be said to have a determinate strength without presupposing that the frequencies it would generate match it; whether propensities, being causal and time-asymmetric, can be treated as probabilities at all, given that the inverse conditional of a propensity ascription does not look like a propensity; and whether genuine chance requires indeterminism, so that a deterministic but chaotically sensitive setup fed a smooth distribution over initial conditions has a chance of heads or merely a frequency.

For each crux, name one concrete case both sides would accept as bearing on it — I suggest, respectively: a coin tossed once on an island that sinks the same day; a single unstable atom of a synthesized isotope that will never be re-created; a process whose forward conditional is a well-behaved propensity but whose inverse conditional is being asked for; and a mechanical coin-flipper run a million times with micro-jittered initial conditions under known deterministic dynamics. Record, in advance, what each side predicts is the correct thing to say about that case, and what each would count as evidence or argument against their own view. Close with a short paragraph identifying any part of the dispute that is verbal rather than substantive, and saying why.
