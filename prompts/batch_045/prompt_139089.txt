hey — I'm TAing an undergrad seminar on consciousness this term and I keep having the same 1am argument with a friend who does visual psychophysics in the lab next door. She says the only thing worth theorizing about is what gets into the global workspace and that the rest is metaphor; I keep saying that if you define conscious as broadcast-to-consumer-systems you've relocated the mystery rather than solved it. Before I make the students read another paper, I want the specification first.

So: write me the desiderata list for a theory of conscious access — what any account of what it is for a state to be globally available or accessed (as opposed to merely phenomenal, or merely processed) has to deliver, with the case or consideration behind each item, and mark which ones you think are non-negotiable and which are just the prejudices of one camp. I care much more about the criteria than about scoring theories, but a very short scorecard at the end for the Block-style overflow picture, the Baars/Dehaene global workspace story, and a higher-order-ish view is fine if you keep it to a few lines each.

Here's what I want the list to deal with, in no order. (1) Who are the consumers? Split access into at least three grades — available for online action, available for verbal report, available for arbitrary reasoning and delayed use — and say which is constitutive, because they come apart. Take as stipulated a made-up patient, call her the echo patient: she'll trace the contour of a briefly flashed shape with her finger and flinch correctly at a looming version of it, she says she saw nothing, she drops to chance if the response has to be delayed more than a couple of seconds, and she never improves with training. Is that access? Say what the theory is committed to there. (2) I keep wanting to split access into two components — the state being put into a format the downstream systems can read, versus its actually being routed to them — and I can't tell whether global workspace theory is a theory of the first, the second, or both. Build that into the list and tell me if it's a real distinction or a confusion. (3) Whenever I read the empirical stuff I can't tell whether the all-or-none ignition signature and the early-vs-late neural marker stuff are predictions of the theory or have quietly become its definition of access, wich would make it unfalsifiable; what should I demand so the theory stays testable? (4) The measurement trap: any report-based measure presupposes access, and no-report paradigms have to justify themselves somehow, so what if anything could be a non-circular gold standard for this state was accessed? (5) Cases it can't dodge: Sperling-style partial report where phenomenology looks richer than what gets reported; binocular rivalry and the attentional blink; change blindness and inattentional blindness; dreaming; and whether one attribute of a single stimulus (motion, say) can be accessed while another (colour) is not. (6) Distinctions it must not collapse: access vs attention, vs working-memory maintenance, vs metacognitive confidence, vs accuracy. (7) Anatomy: it has to take a stand on whether the workspace is essentially prefrontal or whether prefrontal activity is mostly the report machinery bolted on the side, and on whether it can stay a workspace theory if the answer is the latter. (8) What is the scarce resource anyway — if there's a bottleneck, say what is being economized (bandwidth? time? number of recipients? metabolic cost?) and why anything would evolve a system that throws information away. (9) And the bit I actually want most: is the access/phenomenal distinction even coherent, or does any functional account of access either collapse into behaviourism or sneak phenomenology back in? Put that on the list as a requirement, tell me whether it's jointly satisfiable with the others, and tell me which single desideratum does the most work knocking out candidate theories. Oh — and whatever I hand the students should also say something about whether a system with a bottleneck plus a broadcast architecture (a transformer with a tiny scratchpad, say) counts as access-conscious on the theory's own terms, and whether that result is a feature of the theory or a reductio of it.
