I'm writing up my position on luminosity and the KK principle and would like a consistency audit before I commit to it in print. Here is the package:

(1) I accept the margin-for-error argument against luminosity — the Williamson-style argument run on a gradual series of cases, such as feeling cold in a slowly warming room — and I accept its conclusion for paradigmatically mental conditions: no non-trivial condition is such that, in every case in which it obtains, one is in a position to know that it obtains.

(2) I deny KK, and I deny it where it counts: even in a clear, non-borderline case of knowing that p, one need not be in a position to know that one knows that p. I had been taking this to fall out of (1) applied to the condition of knowing that p.

(3) I am an accessibilist about justification: what fixes whether I am justified supervenes on what is available to me from the inside — my evidence and how it hangs together — rather than on external facts about my reliability or environment.

(4) I hold the knowledge norm of assertion (it is permissible to assert that p if and only if one knows that p), together with an applicability constraint: a norm governing assertion must be one that a speaker is in a position to tell whether she is satisfying.

(5) I also hold that knowledge is the norm of practical reasoning, and I do not think (2) threatens it: it is in order to treat what one knows as a reason even when one does not know that one knows it.

(6) Finally, I rely on a claim I call local failure: what the anti-luminosity argument establishes is that each non-trivial condition has failure cases — cases in which it obtains and one cannot know that it does — not that one can never tell where one is. So in a good case, one well clear of the boundary, one can be in a position to know that one is in a good case, and thereby in a position to know that the condition obtains. This is what lets me say that ordinary self-knowledge is largely in order even though luminosity fails.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look as though they are. I am aware that (1) and (6) can be read so that they do not collide, and that (4) and (5) are doing different work, so please be explicit about where a reading is doing the reconciling and whether that reading is one I am entitled to. For each real tension: how severe is it, and what exactly would have to give? Then rank them and say which single revision buys the most consistency at the least cost.
