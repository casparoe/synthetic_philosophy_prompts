I'm a PhD candidate in philosophy of cognitive science, and what I need is a specification, not a survey. The chapter I am writing turns on one question: when a system has no usable design-stance story — no rational reconstruction of how its parts are supposed to contribute to what it does, because its organization was arrived at by training rather than by design — can the intentional patterns an interpreter finds in its behavior still be real patterns, and if so, what does their reality consist in? Before I commit to a criterion, I want to know what I am entitled to demand of one.

So: write the desiderata for an account of pattern reality. Hold the account to one job, which is mine and not realism-in-general: given a system, a body of observations of it, and a proposed description that compresses those observations, the account must be able to say whether the pattern the description picks out is (a) an artifact of the descriptive vocabulary or encoding chosen, (b) merely useful to some interpreter, or (c) real — where real means real enough to underwrite explanation, prediction of further observations, prediction under interventions, and, where the pattern is an intentional one, some modest claim about content.

Give me the list as a table with four columns: the requirement; the concrete case that shows why it is a requirement (a sentence or two each, an actual case rather than a label); whether you take it to be non-negotiable or a preference of one camp, with a note on which camp; and what enforcing it would exclude. At minimum, the account must respect and not collapse these distinctions:

- reality of a pattern versus usefulness of a pattern to an interpreter
- availability of a stance versus reality of the patterns found from that stance
- a pattern in a data set versus a pattern in the system or the world, since the same system under two measurement vocabularies can be patterned in one and not the other
- compression versus export: licensing predictions about further unmanipulated observations versus licensing predictions about what would happen under interventions that have never been performed
- degrees of reality versus a binary verdict

And it must handle these cases explicitly, in whatever order fits your list:

1. The no-design-story case: a trained system whose behavior the intentional stance predicts well, while no decomposition of it into parts with purposes yields predictions anyone could actually use. Does a real intentional pattern require design-stance backing, or can it float free of one?
2. The contrived-code case: a sequence that compresses spectacularly only under a code tailored to it after the fact, the sort of thing you get by fitting a scheme to noise. Whatever the account says must charge for the code and not only for the data given the code; show how it does that and what the maneuver costs it.
3. The unmanipulable domain: a pattern in an observational science — something in the fossil record, or in large-scale astronomical data — that compresses beautifully and predicts new observations, but where no intervention on the system is available even in principle. If your account demands intervention-robustness, it must either handle this or concede that it is a criterion of causal structure rather than of pattern-hood.
4. The near-miss: the intentional stance is the best available predictor of a system overall but mispredicts a specifiable subclass of its behavior systematically. Is the reality of the pattern graded, does it come with an exception clause, or does the existence of that subclass show the stance was instrumental all along?
5. The grain case: a regularity that shows up at one coarse-graining of a system's behavior and disappears at a finer or a coarser one.
6. The reflexivity case: the account must be able to say, in its own terms, whether the distinction it draws between real and merely apparent patterns is itself a real pattern, and what follows for it if the answer is no.

Then, in prose after the table:

(a) Which pairs or triples of your own requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. This is the part I most want. Candidates I have in mind: requiring export under intervention while refusing to restrict pattern-hood to manipulable domains; requiring a principled restriction on admissible codes while keeping the criterion usable by a bounded investigator; requiring that real patterns be anchored in mind-independent structure while allowing a pattern to be real from the intentional stance and not real from the physical stance. For each, tell me whether it is a genuine inconsistency or a fake tension, and what gives way.

(b) Which single requirement does the most exclusionary work, and whether imposing it is worth what it costs you elsewhere on the list.

(c) A short scorecard — a paragraph each, no more — for four candidate views: the compression criterion associated with Dennett's real-patterns paper; a minimum-description-length or two-part-code refinement of it; an interventionist or difference-making account that ties patterns to causal structure; and a deflationary treatment on which pattern-hood is projection relative to an interpreter's interests. Mark for each whether it violates a non-negotiable or merely disappoints a preference.

Please do not supply citations, a bibliography, or a reading list; I know the literature and I will do that part myself. And where a desideratum leans on technical apparatus — Kolmogorov complexity, two-part codes, interventionist counterfactuals — state the apparatus in a sentence rather than assuming I share your formalism.
