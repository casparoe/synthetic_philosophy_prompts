Hey — I run a tiny reading group (five philosophers, three engineers who've each read roughly one encyclopedia article), and last month we did Gettier and it went about as well as you'd expect: everyone agreed the cases are cases, then everyone proposed a different fix and nobody actually argued with anybody. So I want to build a "which post-Gettier diagnosis are you actually committed to?" quiz — sort people by what they say about cases rather than by which label they signed up for.

What I need:

Ten concrete cases, each with two or three verdict options ("knows", "doesn't know", and where it matters something like "knows but the credit is somehow diminished" or "the verdict shifts with the stakes / with who's asking"). Then two extra tiebreaker cases for people whose answers come out inconsistent.

The gimmick I want: put all twelve cases in one setting with one recurring cast — a night-shift avalanche forecasting desk, a hospital lab, a satellite ground station, whatever you like — so that respondents can't tell which structural feature is being probed from the surface story. No fake barns, no sheep, no stopped clocks, and no case that's recognizably the famous one.

The structural features I want covered, each by at least one case: false lemma vs. luck that's merely in the environment vs. luck that intervenes between fact and belief; a case that's safe but not sensitive and one that's sensitive but not safe; a reliable process operating in an abnormal environment while a second, equally reliable process the subject didn't use was sitting right there; a belief with loose existential content that comes out true by accident; an apparent defeater that is itself misleading; a testimonial chain where a false link gets repaired downstream; belief that's true by luck though the evidence supports it.

Please build it from the logical space rather than from a list of theories: give me the dimensions and the answer-combinations they allow first, then drop the actual positions into the cells. Mark the empty cells with one line each on why nobody is there — incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Leaves should include at minimum: a modal safety condition; a sensitivity/tracking condition; a general anti-luck condition that isn't just one of those two; no-defeater/indefeasibility; an ability- or credit-based virtue view; reliabilism restricted to normal conditions; a knowledge-first or abductive "take knowledge as primitive" option; an error theory about the cases themselves (deny the justification, the belief, or the intuition); and a stakes- or attributor-sensitive option. Cap it at nine leaves and flag clearly any pair your instrument simply can't separate — I expect safety vs. anti-luck to be one, and I'd guess the knowledge-first leaf and the error-theory leaf overlap more than their fans admit.

Give me a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto leaves, and tell me what to do with mixed profiles: which single case is doing the most discriminating work, and what a tiebreaker would have to look like to split the ones that cluster.

No author names, no papers, no reading list — I want the structure of the space, not the map of who said what.

One more thing: at least one case should be one where the verdict is genuinely contested among people who know this literature, and I want you to say which one it is and why. More generally, separate what's settled from what's actually contested as you go — including whether "justified true belief that isn't knowledge" is even the right description of the cases, and where the mapping from a case to a position is itself disputed. Don't present any single fix as the consensus one, because as far as I can tell there isn't one.
