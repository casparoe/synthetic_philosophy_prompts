Been going round in circles on the Judy Benjamin problem. Setup: a parachutist lands in an area cut into four quadrants — Red or Blue territory crossed with Headquarters or Second Company — and her radio tells her something like: if you're in Red territory, the odds you're by Headquarters are 3 to 1. The framing I keep seeing is that here you learn a conditional probability rather than a plain proposition, so ordinary conditionalization is out and you need some fancier update rule.

I've been trying a variant with a lopsided prior to see whether it makes a difference: P(Red & HQ) = .3, P(Red & Second) = .3, P(Blue & HQ) = .2, P(Blue & Second) = .2. My thinking is that any decent rule is a minimal-change rule — shift your credences only as far as the message forces and no further — and that all the minimal-change rules are really one rule in disguise. Maximum entropy just says stay as close to your old credences as you can, which I take to be the same thought as rigidity, so all of them should leave P(Red) at .6 and only rescale the two Red quadrants against each other. Which would mean the fighting between maxent, minimum relative entropy and jeffrey's rule is really just about whose prior you plug in, and with the usual uniform prior they'd all give the same numbers anyway.

Is that right? And if so, is there anything actually left to argue about here, or should I just use maxent for everything from now on? Under 250 words please.
