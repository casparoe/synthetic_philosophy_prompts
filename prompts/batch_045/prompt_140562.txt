Hi there — hoping someone can help me get clearer on this, because I've been going round in circles with it for weeks. I help put together our town's little community newsletter, and for the last year or so I've been sitting in on the meetings of the council's Democracy and Boundaries working group, which is the bunch that decides who actually gets to vote whenever the town runs one of its referenda. I've started keeping notes on how they've ruled, and what's interesting is that they don't have a fixed franchise, they argue it out fresh every time. So far: for the referendum on shutting the outdoor pool they used the ordinary resident list, but they explicitly added 16- and 17-year-olds, non-British citizens living here, and the prisoners at the local prison, who got postal votes; they refused about forty people from nearby villages who held annual swim memberships and were in the water most mornings. For the referendum on merging the secondary school they did something odd and let residents of a hamlet that sits in the next parish vote, because their kids would otherwise be bussed twelve miles, though two members dissented and said it opened the floodgates. For the car park charges they said no to commuters from three local villages who buy most of the annual permits, and the notes record the reason as roughly this: once you pick the electorate by who is affected, you can draw the line wherever you like. And for the budget referendum they said no to people who own holiday lets here and pay council tax on them, on the grounds that they don't have to live with it day to day. Apart from the hamlet business, every vote so far has used the same basic list.

Now the new one, which is why I'm writing. There's a proposal for a twenty-five year groundwater abstraction licence for a big bottling plant on the trading estate, and the council's own hydrology consultant's modelling says that in dry summers the pumping would drop the water table enough to dry up private boreholes in three villages about four miles away — different parish, different district council, no shared services with us at all — and would cut the flow of a chalk stream that people there care about enormously. Our town is all on mains water, so nobody here goes thirsty either way. The referendum is technically only consultative but the council has said it will treat the result as binding on whether it objects to the licence. One village has petitioned for its residents to be put on the franchise. Another wants a veto, meaning the whole thing shouldn't proceed if a majority there says no. The third parish council wants the three villages together to be given forty per cent of the total votes, weighted rather than added as ordinary voters. On top of that, a newer housing estate inside our boundary is on its own metered supply and is unaffected however it goes, so I can't work out whether they should still get to vote; there are about two thousand homes planned here over the twenty-five years, so I also can't tell whether people who don't live here yet ought to count for anything; and the company already employs two hundred people at its existing plant in the town. My brother-in-law lives in one of the villages and his well is apparently one of the ones at risk, so I'm not a neutral observer.

What I'd really like from you is not just a yes or no. Can you work out what rule the panel seems to be running from the decisions above, set out the other rules that would fit that same track record just as well, and then say what each of them predicts about the villages getting votes, about the veto, about the forty per cent weighting, about the unaffected estate inside the boundary, and about the people who haven't moved here yet? Give me your best guess on what they'll actually do and why you'd back that one rather than the others, tell me honestly if their past decisions just don't settle it, and if so say what each choice would commit them to going forward. And name the one further case you'd want to put in front of them that would tell the competing rules apart — I keep thinking it might be something like people who move into an affected area after the decision is made, but you tell me. Please write your answer in American English, my sister-in-law in Ohio is going to read it and she gets grumpy about our spelling. I did one evening class in philosophy years ago so please don't assume I know any of the technical vocabulary, but I can follow a proper argument if you lay it out.
