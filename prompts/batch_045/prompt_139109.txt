My instinct was that this is just insularity, so we tried the obvious cure: we pinned a post with links to proper sources, said we welcomed debate, and let a few outsiders post rebuttals in the comments. It went badly. The outsiders were immediately labelled as paid shills, engagement went up but the hard core got noticeably tighter and more affectionate with each other, and two of the reasonable long-standing members quietly left. A friend who reads a bit of philosophy told me I'd confused two things: there's a distinction — some philosopher whose name I've forgotten — between an epistemic bubble, where people are just cut off from outside sources through no fault of anyone's, and an echo chamber, which is actively engineered so that outside voices are discredited in advance. He offered a different analogy instead: the group is like a body with an immune system, and my outside links weren't a pin, they were an exposure that triggered a response and left it stronger than before.

Here's the bit I can't find discussed anywhere, and it's why I'm asking rather than just reading up on it. Our group has two habits that don't obviously fit either picture. Second, back in the spring someone pinned a prophecy that "they" would send people to confuse us before the AGM, so when the rebuttals arrived in September a lot of members treated them as confirmation. I've been calling that a pre-baked rebuttal: the discrediting work is already stored up and gets triggered by the very event it describes.
