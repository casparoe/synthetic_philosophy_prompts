Ok weird bet with a colleague — we both teach intro epistemology and she says: take the reductionist reading of Hume on testimony, where testimonial warrant is just my own observed constant conjunction between other people's reports and how things actually turn out, induction all the way down. Then whatever happens when I act on a chatbot's answer is not testimony at all, because I have no track record of my own for that system and no way to run the base rates, so Hume would have to class it as something else entirely. I say a Reid-style anti-reductionist just shrugs: credulity and veracity get triggered by the appearance of someone addressing you, not by a personal track record, so either the model inherits default credibility or it's a case that shows the principles were too crude to begin with.

Settle it. Assume each of them has been shown a working demo and given a rough explanation of how it was built.
