I'm closing out our reading group's last session on free-floating rationales — the claim, as I've been putting it in my notes, that a mindless optimizer can be an automatic reason-finder: it tracks reasons that nobody represents, so that there are reasons before there are reason-representers, and those reasons are supposed to be no more spooky than numbers or centers of gravity. I'd rather end on a poem than a summary, and I'd rather the poem did the work structurally than described the view.

Write a pantoum spoken in the first person by one such rationale — a reason that is nobody's reason. In a pantoum, the second and fourth lines of each quatrain become the first and third lines of the next, and the last stanza usually returns to the opening line; I want that repetition to carry the argument. Pick two or three lines that will come back, and let each return be quietly colonized: a line that first states the reason bare comes back having acquired a beneficiary it didn't have before — for the nesting bird, for the chick, for the predator who is fooled — so that by the final stanza the reader has noticed that reasons, unlike numbers, smuggle in a for, and can feel the strain of whether the bare statement can still be said once the for is taken away. Your last line should be your first line, and it should not sound the same as it did.

The wrinkle I want pressed is the one the group kept circling and that the standard examples don't reach. In the usual cases the reason-finder goes on to build the representer, so the reason eventually becomes someone's: the bird gets its broken-wing display, and the reason lands. So give me a rationale that never gets a holder — one tracked by a process whose beneficiary was never built. An architecture that was searched over and never instantiated; a copy that was simulated and then not run; a reason discovered for a mind that never arrives. Is that still no more ghostly than a number, or is that exactly where the analogy to numbers and centers of gravity breaks? Take a side, and let the closing quatrain show it rather than state it.

Constraints: four to six quatrains, roughly twenty to twenty-eight lines. Keep the competence-without-comprehension point intact — whatever found this reason did not understand what it found, and the voice shouldn't let the reader forget it. No causes dressed up as reasons, no goals smuggled into the optimizer, no ghosts. Name no one, cite nothing, no footnotes or glosses: it should read as a poem to someone who has never heard the term and be recognizable as the view to someone who has.
