I'm trying to work out what I actually think speaker meaning and implicature are, so here are my gut verdicts on a pile of cases, and I want the principle behind them.

- A reference letter that says only "his handwriting is excellent and he was always punctual": the writer implicates that the guy is a bad philosopher, but doesn't say it, and can't be called a liar.
- "I ate some of the cookies": implicates not all, even though nobody consciously worked anything out.
- The same sentence from a non-native speaker who meant to leave it open whether she ate all of them and would deny implicating anything: I still want to say she implicates "not all", which seems to break the bit where the speaker has to intend it.
- A recommendation letter drafted by a language model, skimmed and sent out by a professor: same shape as the first case, and I still feel like something was implicated, even though the thing that produced the words has no intentions and the professor never thought about it.
- Someone's hands shake while answering a question, I work out from that that they're lying, and they know I will: I don't want to call that speaker meaning. This is the pair I can't square — unintended uptake in both cases, opposite verdicts.
- A message in a group chat phrased so that only one member will get the point, which the other one works out anyway: I want to say she meant it for the first and not the second, which is weird for a public utterance.
- Asked point blank whether she'd read the report, she says nothing, and everyone takes that as a "no": that's meaning, even though nothing was said.

My half-baked principle is that what a speaker implicates is whatever the audience is entitled to hold them accountable for, but I dont think that survives the shaking-hands case. Is there one principle, or a small set of close ones, that gets all of this? Give it to me clause by clause with the case motivating each, tell me which verdict I have to drop if I want the simpler version, and say what the account predicts for a case I haven't listed — mine is: an utterance in a language nobody in the room speaks, which the speaker knows will be recorded and decoded later. Also say how confident you are in each part and flag the bit you're least sure of.
