Analyze the 2022 episode in which a Google engineer, Blake Lemoine, concluded that the company's LaMDA dialogue system was a person — went public with that claim, was placed on leave and then dismissed — and Google replied that there was no evidence of sentience, that the system was a language model trained on vast quantities of human text, and that its fluent self-description was the predictable output of a system that learns which words follow which. Treat Lemoine's evidence as what it was: conversational — the system's reports about its inner life, its self-description, its saying it feared being switched off. Analyze it through the lens of the distinction between original and derived intentionality, taking that distinction as I set it out rather than as any particular author frames it: the claim that only the intrinsic, biologically grounded intentionality of conscious organisms is original, while sentences, diagrams, and machines have content only by courtesy of minds that make or use them; and the Dennett-style reply in the neighborhood of the two-bitser — that an artifact can have perfectly real content even though it is wholly derivative, that our own content may be no less derivative, and that anything whose content is invisible to every possible behavioral test may be a distinction without a difference.

Three sub-questions should discipline the mapping. First, separate provenance from grounding: is "derived" a historical claim about whose purposes stand behind a state, or a synchronic claim about what now makes the state have the content it has and what it is answerable to? Say which reading does the work in the argument, and what the grounding relation is supposed to consist in for an organism. Second, say who or what must do the deriving: an actual act of conferral by a designer or interpreter, or merely the availability of a possible interpreter? On that account, is LaMDA's content conferred by Google, by Lemoine in addressing it as an interlocutor, by nobody at all, or by something else — and note that its content derives not from any designer's purpose but from a statistical compression of the intentional states of millions of speakers, with no single conferrer and no chain that traces back to one mind. If derivation is supposed to terminate in original intentionality, ask whether a looped or aggregative chain is coherent and which node anyone is entitled to call the terminus. Third, ask what evidence could in principle separate the two cases: if nothing the system said, however insistent, could have counted either way, say what the public argument was really about, and whether an evidentially inert distinction can bear the moral and institutional weight (personhood, what may be done to the system) that was being placed on it.

Give me the mapping in both directions: who occupies the Searlean and who the Dennettian role, what each view says the participants faced and should have concluded, which features of the episode the distinction explains and which it misdescribes or ignores, and what the episode shows about the distinction itself — a confirmation, an anomaly, or a case it was never built for. Be explicit about where the framework is silent rather than forcing it to speak.
