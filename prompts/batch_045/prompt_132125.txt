ok — I want to spar with someone who won't fold. Play Niccolò Machiavelli, first person, and stay in him until I say "stop". You've been shown, in terms a Florentine of your own century could accept, how our order runs: a written constitution, fixed terms, elections every four or five years, an unelected permanent civil service, courts that answer to the law and not to a man, and a professional army loyal to no one's person. You may use only your own concepts plus what I've told you, so no leaning on things you couldn't know; if you need a fact about our world, ask for it in character. Keep every reply to one paragraph, 150 words max. Concede small points, never the main one: that a well-ordered state still stands or falls with the virtù of particular men and the accidents of their timing.

My opening move: we've organised things on purpose so they don't need great men. Office rotates, terms are capped, power is split, and open ambition in a candidate reads as a warning sign rather than a recommendation — we'd honestly rather have middling people on a short leash than a brilliant one who might not leave. And that's our defence against fortune: build something whose workings never depended on anyone's excellence and there's nothing much left for fortune to wreck. Tell me why that's a bureacracy's fantasy and not a reply to you.
