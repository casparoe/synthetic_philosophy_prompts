A colleague of mine is drafting a book on the evolution of norms and has been trying her cases out on me one at a time. She describes herself as a Lewisian about convention, but she has never actually stated the definition she is working with, and I should like to know which one she is committed to before she commits to it in print.

Her verdicts so far. Convention: two walkers meeting on a narrow footpath both step to their left; handshakes rather than bows or nods as the departmental greeting; using the word "dog" to talk about dogs; queueing at the coffee machine in order of arrival; stopping at red lights, where matching matters and where drivers who run them are fined and honked at. Not a convention: taking the stairs because the lift has been out of service for months; everyone driving to work alone when each driver wishes that everyone took the bus, nobody disapproves, and nothing depends on matching.

The case she rules on next: on a residential street every household clears the snow from the pavement outside its own house before nine in the morning. Nothing about anyone's clearing depends on anyone else's — each patch is its own, and each household cares only about its own doorstep. Still, everyone believes that one ought to clear, believes the neighbours believe this too, and knows that the one household that skipped last winter was gossiped about for weeks. Compliance is very high but not universal, and a household that fails to clear is observable to its immediate neighbours and to no one else on the street. The practice dates from thirty years ago, when every household on the street walked to the same bus stop at the same hour and each needed the others' patches clear as well as its own; the bus route has since been rerouted and nobody any longer has any use for anyone else's patch.

Give me the two or three rules about what a convention is that fit her earlier verdicts equally well, what each of them predicts about the snow case, your best guess at her verdict together with your reasons for preferring that rule, and one concrete further case that would best separate the rule you favour from its nearest rival. Close with your predicted verdict in machine-readable form: \answer{convention} or \answer{not a convention}.
