Bit of background in case it matters: I did about forty years in experimental condensed matter (mostly NMR and instrument design), retired five years ago, and for the last year I've been sitting in on a philosophy of physics reading group at the local university. We've been through Bell's theorem and I think I've got the basics straight — the thing that gets ruled out isn't signalling, it's that much stronger factorisation condition where the hidden variables screen off one side's outcome from the other side's setting and outcome; Bohmian mechanics is the stock example of a theory that's nonlocal in that sense and still doesn't let you build a telephone. Fine, I'm with it so far.

What I can't swallow is the third assumption, the one about the settings being free, or statistically independent of the hidden variables. To me it looks like it smuggles in indeterminism by the back door: in a deterministic universe every event has a cause, so of course the settings are determined by something, and if that something overlaps with whatever determines the pair then you've got your conspiracy. So the theorem seems to reduce to: locality is dead unless you're willing to be a superdeterminist.

Here's the bit I want to bounce off you, which is my own idea and may well be nonsense. Everyone tells me superdeterminism is unfalsifiable, because a sufficiently conspiratorial model just reproduces the quantum predictions exactly. But that treats the conspiracy as all-or-nothing, and surely it should come in degrees. So: run a Bell test in which you swap out the source of the measurement settings between conditions and hold everything else fixed. Condition one, a pseudorandom generator in the lab seeded off the clock. Condition two, a human pressing buttons. Condition three, radioactive decay. Condition four, bits extracted from the arrival times of photons from distant quasars. My reasoning is that a genuine causal link between what sets the detectors and what sets the particles has to be arranged further and further back in time as the setting source gets more remote, and there's presumably some finite amount of correlation that has to be manufactured and carried forward, so it should get harder to arrange and leak a bit. Hence: the violation should weaken as you go down the list, S drifting down monotonically from condition one to condition four. Flat result, no conspiracy; a monotonic trend, the conspiracy is real and we've got a handle on how bad it is. I gather people have actually run versions of the quasar-based settings business, but as far as I can tell nobody has set it up as a graded comparison across sources, and that's the part I think its new.

So where does this go wrong? I'd especially like to know whether how independent is this random source is even a well-defined quantity to be asking about, or whether I've just reinvented something that already has a name I don't know. Please don't just tell me the consensus and leave it there: I want the load-bearing step in your own argument singled out, what would change your mind about it, and where you think your reasoning is most likely to fail. We had a very confident visiting speaker last term who turned out to be skating over exactly the bit I was asking about, so I'd rather have the honest version than the tidy one.
