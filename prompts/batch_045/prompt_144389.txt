right, I need a sestina for a friend's zine and I've got it into my head that it should be about the doomsday argument — the anthropic one, where you treat yourself as a random sample from the set of all observers and infer from being at roughly this birth rank that the total won't be much bigger than what's come so far. Full sestina please, six stanzas of six plus the three-line envoi, end-words rotating properly, all six of them back in the envoi. I was thinking RANK, RANDOM, CLASS, SOON, DOOM, SAMPLE but swap any you dont like.

The important bit: the form has to be doing the work rather than decorating it. A sestina never introduces a new end-word after the first stanza — the same six keep coming back, only slotted differently — and that's basically the shape of the argument: nothing new about the world is being learned, the same bare indexical fact ("I'm this one, here, now") just keeps turning up in a different position and is somehow supposed to tell you how many there will be. The envoi, where all six crowd into three lines at once, should be where the reference class problem finally shows its face.

And here's the wrinkle I actually care about: dont run it on humans. Run it on the poem's own readership. Each reader finds themselves at some rank among everyone who'll ever read this thing, treats themselves as a random draw from that class, and concludes the readership is nearly over. Then let the poem notice out loud that printing that argument in the poem is exactly the sort of move that ends a readership — the prediction and the cause are the same act. It's got to be accurate enough that someone who's read the anthropic stuff can point at the line where the sampling assumption is doing its work, where a flat prior over the total is smuggled in, and where the alternative (the one where you count yourself as more likely to exist in a world with lots of readers, which washes the whole effect out) is quietly sitting there unpicked. No name-dropping, no footnotes, no gloss before or after. It should also just be a good poem for someone who's never heard of any of this, and it should definately not resolve — I want the last three lines to leave you unsure whether you've been told something or conned.
