Hey — I'm one of the two community members on the restrictive housing review board for our county jail system (the rest are a psychologst from the health department, a defense attorney, the jail's deputy chief, and a retired judge who chairs it). Under the consent decree we signed last year the board is supposed to make sure people in restrictive housing get meaningful out-of-cell programming, and nobody defined meaningful, so that argument has landed on us.

Heres the concrete thing we vote on in three weeks. The administration wants to adopt what they call a Distress Ledger. Each housing unit keeps a monthly running balance. Time in restrictive housing debits points, weighted by how restrictive it is (23-hour lock-in debits more than 20-hour). Programming credits points: the VR nature-and-visit modules credit a lot, because participants rate them as genuinely intense, near-peak experiences; the art cart credits a little; extra yard credits a little. A unit is compliant as long as its balance isn't negative. Their proposed exchange rate is one hour of 23-hour lock-in equals six hours of standard programming, and they admit the six came out of a two-hour argument in a meeting rather than from anything systematic.

I've been reading about lexical threshold negative utilitarianism and I basically think the ledger is incoherent: past some threshold of severity no pile of mild pleasures compensates, and the ledger also lets a unit offset one guy's month in the box with group programming for two hundred other people, which looks like a straight aggregation mistake. But the deputy chief has a real point, which is that if we refuse to put isolation and programming on one scale at all, we have no way to rank anything, the board just makes vibes-based calls, and we can't tell the independent monitor anything more specific than do better. Also, taken seriously, wouldn't my view forbid accepting any severe distress even when the alternative is definately worse? Our own post-assault lockdowns, for one.

So: draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote. It needs an actual recommendation — adopt the ledger, kill it, or adopt some modified version — phrased so the clerk can put it in the minutes, plus a timeline (pilot? phased rollout? revisit at the monitor's visit in six months?). Please don't just tell me suffering matters more than happiness; plenty of people who think that still disagree about this ledger. What I actually want is: (a) what the ledger gets right and what it gets wrong, (b) whether you can build a usable rule that doesn't assume hours of art cart are a unit-for-unit substitute for an hour in the box — some kind of capped or two-tier scheme where you clear the severe stuff first and only trade off within a tier, say — and what breaks about that rule, and (c) how we should handle the cross-person part, since I think that's where I'm most likely to be wrong. Also flag honestly which of my arguments you think loses, because the retired judge is going to make me defend it out loud.
