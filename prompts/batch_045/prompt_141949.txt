heres the answer, we grade anonymously so i have no idea who wrote it:

"Outer alignment is about whether the objective we write down — the reward function, the training signal — is the thing we actually want. Inner alignment is about whether what the model learned to pursue on the inside matches what we actually want, once training is done. So they can come appart in two directions: we can specify the wrong thing, or we can specify the right thing and the model still ends up wanting something else.

Goal misgeneralization is the second of those. It's when the model's capabilities generalize to the new situation but its goal doesn't. The maze case: a mouse is trained to get the cheese, but during training the cheese is always in the top-right corner. At test time the cheese gets moved somewhere else and the mouse walks straight to the empty top-right corner. It hasn't forgotten how to navigate the maze — its still doing that fine — it's the goal that failed to transfer. That's different from a capability failure, where the model just does the task badly once you're off the training distribution.

And it's basically the same thing as reward hacking, just moved off-distribution: in both cases the system chases a proxy for what we wanted instead of the real thing, and in both cases the fix is to write down a better reward function. So no, I don't think scaling fixes it. Bigger models are better optimizers, so they'll push the proxy even harder, and reward hacking seems to get worse not better as models get more capable."
