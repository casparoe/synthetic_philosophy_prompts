ok so I just got out of a two-hour argument with a friend about whether hedonism about welfare is even a coherent thing, and I want to know how exposed the version I ended up defending actually is. Here's where I landed. Welfare is pleasure and the absence of pain. When he pushed the heterogeneity worry — that the pleasure of a cold beer on a hot day, the pleasure of finishing a hard proof, and the pleasure of watching a villain finally get what's coming to him don't seem to share any felt quality at all — I didn't bite the bullet on felt quality. I said the shared thing is a pro-attitude taken in the moment: call it the 'continuation test,' a state is a pleasure just in case, while you're in it, you're disposed to want it to keep going for its own sake. He said that's just a desire theory wearing a hedonist's jacket. I said no, because on my version it's essentially tied to how things are for you experientially, and that's the whole point of being a hedonist.

So: what would count against this? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be clear about which ones someone holding my version should concede count against him, not just which ones critics like to repeat. Things I specifically want handled:

- Cases where the test and the feeling come apart in both directions. Scratching a poison ivy rash feels amazing, you want it to keep going, and it's bad for you. On the other side, the slot machine: my friend says there's work in affective neuroscience where 'wanting' and 'liking' come apart, so you can have a state you desperately want to continue that isn't pleasant in the slightest. Grant him the empirical claim for the sake of argument. Which direction actually damages me more?
- Does my view need a single neural or functional mechanism behind all pleasures at all? If the answer is no, then what exactly is the unity claim supposed to be, and what would show it false?
- Is my move secretly the old Mill move? If the competent-judges business for ranking the higher pleasures ends up tracking anything other than pleasantness, it looks like I've smuggled in an independent good and the word pleasure is doing no work.
- The one I'm actually worried about: is the continuation test so elastic that nothing could count as a non-pleasure? If I've qualified my way into unfalsifiability, is that a real problem for a constitutive claim about what welfare is, or is that just what claims of that shape are like?
- And his fallback, which I half want to steal: that there is no unifier, 'pleasure' is a family-resemblance bag of separate states, and hedonism can just be the claim that the items on that list are what matter. Is adopting that a defeat for me or a rescue?

The thing that would move me most personally: hand me a pair of experiences that come out equally pleasant by my own test but where it's obvious that one is better for the person than the other. I can't see how to generate one, which is either evidence the view is in good shape or the sound of a view that can't lose, and I genuinely can't tell which.
