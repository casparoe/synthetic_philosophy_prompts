The hole argument has now been running for more than a century, and the three camps — manifold substantivalists, sophisticated substantivalists who let diffeomorphic models represent a single physical possibility, and relationists or eliminativists about spacetime points — seem to me to have ended up roughly where they began. All sides accept the same field equations, the same models, and the same empirical content; they differ only about what follows. I want a diagnosis of the impasse, not a verdict on it.

Here is my hypothesis, which I would like tested rather than confirmed. The argument's bite, I suspect, comes entirely from the order of two steps. If you first read an ontology off the formalism — points are individuals, because the theory quantifies over them and assigns them properties — and only then ask whether the theory is deterministic, the hole construction looks devastating. If you first impose diffeomorphism-invariance as a representational constraint, on the grounds that a theory must not manufacture extra possibilities by relabelling its own points, and only then ask what exists, the argument never gets going and looks like an artefact of a bad semantics. If that is right, the dispute is not about spacetime at all but about whether metaphysics gets to go first or must answer to a prior theory of representation.

So: is that it? Is it instead a disagreement about what determinism is for — a predictive virtue a theory may lack without reproach, or a constitutive requirement on anything deserving the name of a physical theory? Is it a disguised disagreement about haecceitism, about whether bare numerical differences among points are the sort of thing a physical theory is permitted to be blind to? Or are the camps simply answering different questions under one label? Defend a diagnosis using what each position actually commits its holder to, and then tell me what would count as progress given that diagnosis: a formal result about determinism in theories with gauge redundancy, a general criterion for when a piece of formalism is representational surplus, a better-posed replacement question — or nothing at all, in which case say why no progress should be expected here.
