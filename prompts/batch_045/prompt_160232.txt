Bit of a long one, sorry. I'm retired now — spent forty years in solid state chemistry, mostly lattice dynamics and defects — and in the last couple of years I've drifted into a philosophy reading group at the local U3A, which is how I ended up with a Hofstadter essay about a substance called the careenium whose particles clump into what he calls simmballs, and the question of whether the simmball level — the symbols, the higher-level description — ever actually shoves the particles around, or whether it's all the particles shoving each other and the rest is just a way of talking.

One of our number (Ray, who used to write firmware) settled it in about thirty seconds with the analogy everyone reaches for: the mind is the software and the brain is the hardware, and software doesn't push electrons around, the electrons push each other; a program is just a pattern we find useful to describe them. So, he says, in the careenium the simmballs don't push anything, the symbol level is causally idle, and if you want to know who shoves whom the answer is the substrate, full stop — and by extension my wanting a cup of tea doesn't do anything either, the neurones do.

I found myself pushing back with an analogy from my own patch, which I suspect is doing just as much work as his. A phonon is nothing but a coordinated wobble of atoms: nothing is added to the world, no extra force appears, and in principle you could tell the whole story atom by atom. And yet phonons carry heat, they scatter electrons, they have a mass and a lifetime and you can bounce them off surfaces. A hole in a semiconductor is an *absence*, and holes carry current. So on my analogy the simmball is like a quasiparticle — a perfectly respectable causal player that exists only at the level of the pattern.

So: could you audit both analogies for me? I'd like the correspondences laid out explicitly (who plays the instruction set, the compiler, the user, the clock, the crystal, the atoms, whatever), where each mapping breaks, and what each one quietly imports that the careenium hasn't got — my worry for Ray is that the careenium isn't running a stored programme at all and nobody wrote it anything, so it's not obvious "software" means anything there. Then the bit I actually care about: does his conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying it? And mine presumably over-generates — the centre of mass of the solar system and the shadow of a tree are patterns too and they don't shove anything — so what is it that separates a phonon or a simmball from a mere pattern, and is there a better analogy for the symbol/substrate relation than either of ours? Definately don't just tell me the answer is "supervenience", I want the argument.
