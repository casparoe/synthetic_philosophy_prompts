Hey — I need a decision tree and I'm a bit out of my depth on how to build one properly.

Bit of background: I help run a tiny forecasting group inside a climate/energy consultancy — five of us, mostly forecasting things like "will at least one G20 government impose a hard cap on data-centre electricity use before 2035". Last week two of us ended up at 40% and 12% on the same question, read each other's full writeups, and neither of us moved. Someone dropped Aumann's agreement theorem into the group chat, and now every disagreement gets met with "well, Aumann says one of us is being irrational" — which is true-ish but useless, because we still can't tell which bolt in the machine came lose. So I want a diagnostic instrument, not a verdict.

What I'm after: a decision tree that starts from "these two people have stated different probabilities for the same event and haven't converged" and ends at a named diagnosis of what broke. Nine or ten branch nodes, no more than eight leaves. Every node has to be a question I could read aloud in a Monday standup to someone who has never heard of the theorem — so no node may use the words prior, Bayesian, posterior, common knowledge, partition, or Aumann, and none of them can just be a dressed-up "do you have a common prior?". Concrete cases only: as a style example (please don't reuse it verbatim, and skip trivial ones like "are you sure you actually disagree?") something in the vein of "if you each wrote out every piece of evidence you have and the other person genuinely read all of it, would you still say the same number?". Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of jargon labels: first the questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then which combinations real diagnoses actually occupy, and note which combinations stay empty — and whether that's because they're ruled out by the theorem, merely unattractive, or just unexplored. Territory I definitely want reachable as leaves, wherever the logic puts them: the two are weighting shared evidence differently; one has private evidence that hasn't successfully made it across, and I'd like that leaf to distinguish "they never said it" from "they said it but the other person can't tell how much weight they personally put on it" (that second one feels like our actual failure mode); the event isn't operationalized the same way for both of them (our "hard cap" question is a mess — what counts as a cap, what counts as imposed); someone's stated number isn't their belief because of how our scoring rules pay out; the clean Bayesian updating story just isn't how people update and at least one of them is anchored on an old number; and there was never any shared starting point to appeal back to. I also want one leaf for the case where the disagreement is worth keeping on purpose — where the group learns more from two people staying apart and going out to find more evidence than from splitting the difference at 26% — and I want it treated as a genuine leaf with its own logic, not a cop-out, even if the theorem's idealized agents couldn't do it. Also, one wrinkle I keep banging into: even when someone tells you their number, they haven't told you how much they trust their own calibration on questions like this one, and that seems like it should show up somewhere in the tree rather than being ignored.

For each leaf give me a short name, one sentence on the most awkward thing you commit to by landing there, and one sentence on what I'd actually do next in the standup. And please flag any two leaves your tree can't really separate — I suspect "different weights on the same evidence" and "no shared starting point" are going to be indistinguishable from the outside, and I'd much rather have that flagged than smoothed over. Don't cite anything or point me at papers or reading lists, I just want the thing itself — a one-page handout I can pin in the chat.
