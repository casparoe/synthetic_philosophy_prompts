I sit on an internal review committee that has to write down, in advance, what would have to be established before we sign off on a model trained by iterated amplification and distillation — the scheme where a task is handed to a decomposer that splits it into subtasks, each subtask is answered by copies of the current model with a human somewhere in the loop, the answers are recomposed into a solution to the whole task, and that solution's behavior is then distilled back into a fast model that does the task in one pass. We keep going in circles, because we have no agreed spec: everyone arrives with one favorite worry — errors compounding multiplicatively across sub-judgments, the decomposer itself being untrusted and able to bury the load-bearing part of a task inside a single opaque subtask, failures that are correlated rather than independent across the nodes, the recursion arriving at subtasks no unaided human can any longer adjudicate, distillation retaining the answer while losing whatever made the amplified process trustworthy, slow drift in what the system is even optimizing across successive rounds of amplification and distillation — and nothing in front of us says which of these is a genuine requirement on a scheme and which is only the settled anxiety of one camp.

So I want the specification, not a verdict on any particular pipeline. Write the desiderata that any account of why decomposition preserves alignment and competence has to satisfy to be adequate for our purpose, and note that the purpose is deliberately narrow: adequate to license or refuse a sign-off on one concrete pipeline, not to underwrite a general theory of aligned artificial intelligence. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it — the failure it exists to rule out — and mark it as either non-negotiable or a preference of one camp. Three points I would like handled rather than gestured at. First, what I have been calling warrant transfer: whether the distilled model is supposed to inherit the amplified process's reasons or only its input-output profile, and what we would accept as evidence either way. The case that bothers me is a distilled model that returns the right answer with no accessible derivation, where it is unclear whether we have the same warrant for trusting it that we had for the process it came from. Second, what I have been calling the substitution test: if the answer at one node is replaced by a calibrated random draw from the distribution of plausible answers, does the aggregate degrade gracefully or collapse, and what that tells us about how much of the scheme's competence lives in the decomposition and how much in the nodes. Third, the null baseline: the scheme has to beat not doing it, where the relevant comparison is giving one competent human, or one model, more time and more test-time compute on the undecomposed task — otherwise we may be paying for parallelism and calling the result amplification.

Two further asks. Tell me which subsets of the desiderata you take to be jointly unsatisfiable. My own suspicion is that requiring every sub-judgment to be checkable by one unaided human sits in direct tension with the point of exceeding unaided human performance, and that requiring the distilled model to reproduce the amplified system's reasoning sits in tension with the compression distillation exists to achieve — but I want to know whether those are genuine impossibilities or merely design tradeoffs that look philosophical when stated abstractly. And tell me which single requirement excludes the most of the schemes people actually propose, since we will not have the resources to apply all of them. A few lines of scoring at the end are welcome, but keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases. Finally, mark clearly what is settled or near-consensus among people who work on this and what is genuinely contested, and flag any place where you are giving me a contested view: I need to know which lines of the spec a reasonable reviewer could refuse to sign, and I would rather have the uncertainty marked than smoothed over.
