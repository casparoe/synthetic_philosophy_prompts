So I'm trying to work out what I actually believe about subsidiarity by going case by case, and my verdicts dont obviously add up to anything.

- Safety standards for goods sold across the whole internal market: EU level, no hesitation.
- Building codes in a Swiss canton: cantonal, even though one national code would be cheaper for firms that build in several cantons.
- Framework legislation from the federation that the Länder then fill in: I dislike it on principle, and yet I keep concluding it's fine in practice.
- EU-level minimum wage floors: no — even though wage competition between member states is real.
- Travel and quarantine rules in a pandemic: should have been coordinated from the top, and I'm still annoyed it wasn't.
- Licensing for a trade whose clients never cross a border: state or Land level, obvously.
- A lower level voluntarily handing a competence upward because it can't fund it: fine by me, which is odd for a principle that's supposedly about protecting the lower level.

My own stab at a principle: a competence belongs at the level where the people who'd pay the cost of getting it wrong are the same people who'd pay the cost of fixing it. But I suspect that gets either product standards or the pandemic case wrong, and I can't tell which — and it says nothing at all about competences that follow a person instead of a place.

So: is there a principle that gets most of the seven? Which verdict does it force me to give up? And what does it predict for the case I can't place — say, who sets the rules for a remote worker who lives in one canton, is employed in another, and whose platform is hosted in a third country, so there's no territory whose population is the affected one?
