i keep coming back to Weil's essay on abolishing the political parties whenever I think about Syriza in 2015, and I want somebody to actually run the case rather than gesture at it.

Facts as I have them, so we're not arguing about them: Syriza won in January 2015 on an anti-memorandum programme, spent six months negotiating with the troika, called a snap referendum on the creditors' terms at the end of June, got something like 61% No with capital controls in force, and then within about a fortnight signed up to a third bailout that was if anything harsher than the one the voters had just rejected. Varoufakis went, a chunk of the party's left broke off into Popular Unity, and Tsipras got re-elected in September and spent the next years implementing the thing.

Weil's claim, as I read her, is structural rather than a complaint about bad people: a party is a machine whose operative criterion is its own survival and growth, it manufactures collective passion, and it necessarily subordinates truth and justice to that, so no party can be a vehicle for whatever it says it's for.

So do the mapping properly. Who or what is the agent here — the party, the leadership, the parliamentary group — and what was it actually optimising across those six months and then in the fortnight after the referendum? What does the theory say it should have done? Then say what the lens explains (the No-to-Yes reversal, the split, the MPs who voted for the bailout while saying they didn't believe in it) and where it's silent: it looks to me like Weil has nothing to say about the creditors or about the eurozone architecture, which closed down the options at least as much as any party machine did.

And I want the bit that runs back the other way, with two things in particular. First, seperate the corruption reading — party membership corrupts individual judgement — from the substitution reading, on which even a party that does the right thing is defective because its members act from party spirit rather than from justice. Which does 2015 support, and can it support both at once? Second, the falsifiability worry: if Tsipras had gone through with Grexit, would that have refuted Weil, or could she absorb it anyway — and if she can absorb both outcomes, what would a case have to look like to count against her? Related: Syriza was more of a front than a mass party, so does her critique reach horizontalist movements too, and does that make it too broad — any large organisation — or too narrow?

Finish with one line: is this Michels with metaphysics bolted on, or does the bit about truth and justice earn its keep here.
