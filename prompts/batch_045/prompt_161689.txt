There's a guy at our Wednesday games night — maths PhD, does something combinatorial-game-theory-adjacent, call him R. — who has a stock verdict on every game anyone brings along: he'll say whether the strategy-stealing argument shows anything about it, and if it does, whether it gets the first player a win or only a draw. (You know the move: assume the second player has a winning strategy, make some throwaway move, then run that strategy against them and treat your throwaway stone as a free gift.) His verdicts so far, more or less as he put them:

- ordinary Hex on the rhombus, any size: stealing wins it for the first player
- Chomp, the chocolate-bar game with the poisoned corner square: same, first player wins
- 3x3 tic-tac-toe: stealing runs, but all it buys you is that the second player can't win — draw at best, and of course it is a draw
- that game where you take turns claiming numbers from 1 to 9 and win if you end up holding three that add to 15: he said its tic-tac-toe in a costume, so draw at best again
- chess: tells you nothing at all
- take-away with a single heap where you must take exactly one counter and whoever takes the last one loses: tells you nothing, and the reason he gave was that here an extra move of yours isn't free at all, it just flips who gets stuck with the last counter
- a Hex variant we tried where one player's two sides are further apart than the other player's (a handicap thing): doesn't run, you can't borrow the other guy's strategy when the two jobs aren't the same

He's never given a verdict on go, which is part of why I can't work out what he's actually doing.

The new game next week is one of ours: anti-Hex on a 5x5 rhombus. Same board, same two pairs of opposite sides, same alternating placement of one stone of your own colour on an empty cell, no passing, stones stay put — but you lose the instant you complete a chain of your own stones joining your two sides, and the game stops right there. Since a full Hex board always has somebody connecting, there's always a loser, so no draws are possible.

What do you think he'll say about anti-Hex: stealing wins it for the first player, stealing only shows the second player can't win, or stealing tells you nothing? I care much more about the why than the answer: what rule is he running, what other rules fit those seven verdicts just as well, which of them come apart on this game, which of his earlier verdicts is the closest analogue, and what game we should bring the week after to tell the rules apart. If his record genuinely doesn't pin it down, say what he'd be committing himself to with each answer. And tell me how confident you are in each bit, and which bit you're least sure of.
