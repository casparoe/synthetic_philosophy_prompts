hey — ive got roughly six months of spare research time, basically one paper's worth, and I want to spend it on imperfect recall in decision theory. The reason isn't purely academic: I care about LLM-based agents that run in loops with lossy memory, so at a given step they often can't tell whether they've already made this decision earlier, or how far into the loop they are. I want to come out the other end with something that would actually change how someone scaffolds such an agent, not just another taxonomy for its own sake.

The setup I keep coming back to is a generalisation of the absent-minded driver. Take N identical-looking exits in a row; exiting at the k-th pays f(k), increasing in k; driving past the last one pays less than any of them. Now add what I'll call a noisy odometer: at each exit the driver gets a signal that with probability r correctly says which exit this is, and with probability 1-r is just noise, independent across exits. The classic two-exit version sits at the degenerate corner (N = 2, no signal, textbook payoffs: 0 for the first exit, 4 for the second, 1 for driving off the end). There, going straight with probability 2/3 maximises expected payoff from the starting point, but what you'd want to do once you're actually at an exit depends on how you fix your self-locating belief, and that's exactly where the competing solution concepts come apart: planning-optimal, consistent planning, evidential-style reasoning, or just refusing to assign a credence at all.

So: within and around that noisy-odometer family, what should I actually work on, and in what order? I'm much less interested in the list than in the reasoning behind it. For each candidate: why it's upstream of the others, whether one person can genuinely make progress on it in six months, and what evidence or intermediate result over the next few months would move it up or down. And please say which ones you'd actively drop — questions where the answer, whichever way it goes, wouldn't change anything downstream.

Some things I've been circling, to get you going, but don't feel bound by them: do the competing solution concepts still disagree once 0 < r < 1, or do they collapse onto the same recommendation away from the extremes (which would suggest the whole debate is an artefact of total amnesia)? Does anything interesting happen at intermediate N, or does the structure kick in immediately at N = 3? What if the noise is correlated across exits rather than independent — does that just rescale r, or is it a seperate problem? And what if the agent can pay some cost to make its own memory reliable: does the value of that information have the usual shape under imperfect recall, or does it do something weird?
