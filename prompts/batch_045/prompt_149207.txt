I've been keeping a notes file of my epistemological commitments for years, and a friend read it and said it sounds like three different people wrote it. Here's the package:

1. Closure: if you know p, you know that p entails q, and you come to believe q by deducing it, then you know q.
2. Tracking: knowledge requires that had p been false you wouldnt have believed p — at least not by the method you actually used.
3. Anti-luck: on top of that, knowledge requires that you couldnt easily have been wrong, i.e. in the nearby cases where you form the belief the same way, it's true.
4. Ordinary perceptual knowledge is real and I dont want to lose it: the visitor at the zoo knows that the animal in the enclosure is a zebra.
5. Testimony gives knowledge too, even when the only thing behind it is that the source is reliable.
6. My fix for keeping 1 and 2 together, which I've started calling "narrow closure": closure only transmits across entailments whose conclusion doesnt add a new way of being wrong — if the conclusion's falsity is something your original method was never sensitive to, deducing it gets you nothing.

Which of these actually clash, which only look like they do, and in each case what's the cheapest thing to give up? Rank the clashes by severity instead of flagging everything as equally bad, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
