My Hegel reading group has a convenor, call her Miriam, who keeps issuing rulings on whether a given relationship counts as a relation of recognition — Anerkennung in her usage, the thing a self-consciousness supposedly can't manage on its own. I've written down her verdicts after each session:

- two warriors who fight to the death over reputation: yes, though she grumbled that it's too tidy to be an example of anything
- master and bondsman, after one of them yields instead of dying: yes, but the kind that can't deliver what it promises, structurally unstable, bound to break
- a mother and her newborn: yes, but only because it's on its way to being given back
- a hermit carving spoons alone in a forest for decades, to no standard but his own, selling nothing, teaching nobody, no audience at all: no
- a man and his dog: no, but it's the case that makes her want a different word
- two strangers meeting as equal citizens in front of a modern court, no history between them whatsoever: yes, and better than the warriors
- a wealthy man and the valet who has dressed him for thirty years, devoted on both sides, never a cross word, the valet's entire sense of himself wrapped up in the job, the wealthy man unable to pick him out of a lineup: no, and not even an interesting no

The case for next week is one I made up, and I want to know what she'll say. An elderly woman in a half-empty village has spent twenty years finishing an enormous handwritten encyclopedia of local plants that her teacher began and abandoned when he died. She works from his notebooks and his marginal comments, to his standards, argues with him out loud when she thinks he's wrong, says the plants answer back, and says she feels judged by him — judged, not comforted. She refuses to publish and nobody is ever going to read it.

I care less about the verdict than about the rule. I can see at least two that fit all seven of her past calls: something like recognition needs another subject who could in principle withhold it, and something like recognition needs an other that resists you and in which you can find yourself objectified, which can be a thing or a practice so long as it carries somebody's stance toward you. Both seem to survive her track record whole, and they come apart on the encyclopedia woman. There's maybe a third about stakes or struggle that fits too. Tell me which rule she's actually running, what each candidate predicts about my case, your best guess with the reasons for preferring it, and what further case would seperate the rules cleanly — and be straight with me about what would change your mind and where your guess is most likely to be wrong. I'd rather have one shaky claim flagged than three confident paragraphs.
