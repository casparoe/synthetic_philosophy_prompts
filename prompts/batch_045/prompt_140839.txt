You are producing the joint statement of a two-year adversarial collaboration on the epistemology of psychiatric classification. The two parties are:

Camp V, the validators camp: psychiatric disorders are, at least in principle, discoverable structures in nature; the job of a classification system is to carve at the joints, and the way to tell whether a proposed category is carving correctly is by converging validators — family aggregation, course and outcome, treatment response, and, more recently, genetics and neuroimaging. Where the current manual fails to carve correctly, the remedy is more and better validators, not less classification.

Camp L, the looping camp: following Hacking's work on interactive kinds, at least some psychiatric categories are moving targets — classifying people changes how they experience themselves, what they report, what clinicians notice, what institutions fund, and therefore what the category turns out to be. For such categories the apparent convergence of validators may partly be an artifact of the classification's own uptake. Where looping is heavy, the remedy is epistemic modesty about the category's depth, not necessarily abandonment of it.

To make the collaboration concrete, the parties have adopted two devices that do not currently exist and that you should define as part of your answer before using them:

The Reactivity Quotient (RQ) of a category: the ratio of its incidence in a population where the label is publicly available, identity-available, and institutionally consequential, to its incidence in a population matched on symptom burden and help-seeking where the label has never been available as a self-description. Alongside it, Self-Report Loading (SRL): the fraction of a category's criteria that can only be ascertained from the patient's own report.

The sealed-derivation protocol: re-derive a category's criteria and boundaries using only archived data collected before the label entered public discourse, then compare the resulting cluster and its validators to the cluster defined after the label circulated.

Both camps have also agreed to rule on a live hypothetical: the proposed category Protracted Withdrawal and Derealization Syndrome (PWDS), adolescent-onset, defined by five of eight criteria, seven of eight ascertainable only by self-report, characterized by subjective detachment and by retreat from school and peer contact. It surfaced through online communities rather than clinics; its measured prevalence has tripled in five years; there is an active identity community around the label; school screening pilots and an industry-funded drug trial are pending; and the only longitudinal data available were collected after the label became widely known.

Write the document they could both sign. It should contain:

A numbered section of agreed claims — claims both camps accept, formulated so that neither is vacuous nor a disguised concession to the other. These should cover at least: whether classification demonstrably affects some of the people classified; whether that fact by itself establishes that a category is unreal or invalid; which structural features of a category predict loop-proneness; whether heavy looping is compatible with the category still tracking something; and what the presence of a distress-or-impairment clause does and does not show about the value-ladenness of psychiatric kinds.

A disagreements table, with one row per residual dispute and columns for: the issue; the formulation Camp V would accept for its own position; the formulation Camp L would accept for its own position; and your verdict on whether the dispute is genuine or merely verbal, with the reason if verbal. Include at least the dispute about whether convergent validators reveal pre-existing structure or partly manufacture it, the dispute about what stability across decades would show, and the dispute about what a classification system is ultimately for.

A cruxes section. For each crux — the point where settling a sub-question would move at least one camp — state the crux precisely, and then specify the case, study, or argument both camps would accept in advance as bearing on it, with each camp's prediction written down before the outcome is known. At minimum: what the sealed-derivation protocol would find if applied to a heavily looped historical category; what a high RQ with high SRL should lead a manual to do; and what would happen to PWDS incidence and symptom profile in a jurisdiction that declines to introduce the label while a matched jurisdiction introduces it.

Finally, apply the agreed claims and the cruxes to PWDS: what both camps can jointly recommend to the committee deciding on its inclusion, what each camp would separately predict about its trajectory if included, and what follow-up data both camps would want collected regardless of the decision.

Answer in the voice of the joint statement: neutral, precise, and free of rhetoric. Do not adjudicate the dispute in the sense of declaring a winner, and do not blur a disagreement into compromise language that neither camp would actually sign. Where no jointly acceptable formulation exists, say so explicitly and state why.
