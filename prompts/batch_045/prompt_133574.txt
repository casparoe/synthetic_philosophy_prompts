I work in security studies, and I keep running into a configuration that I do not have a word for. Before I coin one for a chapter I am writing, I would like to know whether there is an established concept for it, and I would rather have the carving than the label.

The instance that made the shape obvious to me is the dark forest setting from Liu Cixin's fiction: the sky looks empty because every civilization stays quiet, and every civilization stays quiet because it believes the sky is dangerous. If the belief is true, we should expect silence. If the belief is false but universally held, we should also expect silence. And the one observation that would separate the two — something announcing itself, or something being destroyed for having announced itself — is precisely what nobody can afford to produce. Announcing yourself is costly only if the hypothesis is true, and that is exactly the case you were trying to rule out. So the claim is not vague or gerrymandered about what would refute it; it is perfectly definite. It is just that refutation requires performing the single act it identifies as fatal.

I see the same shape elsewhere. Two states keep an expensive mobilized posture; the resulting peace is evidence both that deterrence worked and that there was never anything to deter, and the only test — unilateral stand-down — is cheap if the other side is benign and catastrophic if it is not. And I have been toying with a nastier variant, which I have been calling the planted-forest variant: some agents benefit from others holding the fearful belief, so they manufacture or amplify the signs of danger — decoys, rumours, conspicuous strikes against third parties. If that is possible, the belief can be stable, self-sealing, and false all at once, and every apparent confirmation of it is itself the product of agents with an interest in confirming it.

What I want to know is whether this has a name and, more importantly, what exactly the phenomenon is and where its edges are. I do not want a bibliography or a literature survey. Specifically:

Is there an established concept for a hypothesis that is unfalsifiable not through vagueness but through its own actionable content — a claim whose only possible test is the act it forbids, held by agents who each know this?

If yes, what are the nearest neighbours, and how does each of them differ from what I have described? I can think of several candidates I would want separated out: the ordinary self-fulfilling prophecy, where the belief brings about its own truth; the self-defeating or self-effacing prediction, where acting on the belief prevents the predicted outcome; equilibria in which players' beliefs are correct along the path of play but unconstrained off it; precautionary measures whose success cannot be tested because the measures cannot be removed; and selection effects in which the relevant data is missing for observer-relative rather than agent-relative reasons. If one of these is simply what I am describing, say so, and then tell me what is gained or lost by describing it in my terms rather than in the formalism.

If nothing established fits, coin a term and give me something I can actually use: a definition, the minimal set of conditions that jointly produce the effect, at least two cases that look like instances but are not, and — since the whole point of the phenomenon is that the obvious ways of checking for it are themselves compromised — some account of what a non-question-begging test for it would look like.
