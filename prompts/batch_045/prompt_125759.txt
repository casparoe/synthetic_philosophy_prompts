Write a joke for my epistemology study group about epistemic closure and Nozick's tracking theory of knowledge. We've been messing around with our own variant — call it fixed-evidence tracking: you know that p only if, across the closest possible worlds in which your total evidence is exactly what it actually is, you believe p if and only if p is true. The joke has to turn on the fact that this variant still breaks closure in a single modus ponens step: you can satisfy the condition for p, satisfy it for p implies q, competently deduce q, and still fail it for q, because the closest not-q worlds hold your evidence fixed and you'd go right on believing q there. So the punchline should be doing work with the shape of those nearby worlds, not just falling back on a generic brain-in-a-vat gag — someone who only half-remembers Gettier shouldn't really get it. Bonus points if the Nozickian character is in a totally ordinary situation, like lost keys or a burnt dinner, and gets into trouble purely by being consistent. One setup, one punchline, keep it short.
