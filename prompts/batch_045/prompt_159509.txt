2. The simple conditional analysis is dead. Masks kill it: Mara can be brave even though, if the firing squad actually showed up, she'd be paralyzed by the sedative in her water. Being disposed to F in C doesn't mean she'd F if C.
3. But I won't accept finks. If the arrival of the stimulus itself destroys the basis — someone's generosity evaporates the moment they're asked for anything — then I say there was no disposition there to begin with, just something that looked like one from far away.
4. Traits are intrinsic-ish: two characters alike in every intrinsic respect have the same traits whatever is going on around them. Surroundings can hide a trait but can't make one.
5. A trait is multi-track. Courage is a whole cluster of stimulus/manifestation pairs, and no single one of them is essential to having it.
6. And yet I want to say that the man who'd walk into the firing squad but can't tell his wife the truth is not brave — that one failure definately costs him the trait.
