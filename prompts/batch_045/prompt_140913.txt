I'm in a reading group on Strawson's reactive attitudes and everyone's been asked to write up where they actually stand so the others can take shots at it, and I'd like a consistency check before I hand mine round. Here's roughly my position:

(1) To be morally responsible just is for reactive attitudes like resentment and indignation to be fitting toward you — there's no extra fact about desert sitting behind our practices, so that once you've said everything about the attitudes and what they do in a relationship there's nothing further to ask.
(2) Our actual blaming culture is mostly unjustified — pile-ons, the way we moralise addiction and mental illness, how much of the criminal system runs on blame — and we have real moral reason to cut a lot of it away wholesale, not just fine-tune it case by case.
(3) Nobody can just decide, on some particular occasion, not to feel resentful about a slight. The attitudes aren't under that kind of control.
(4) The objective attitude is the right stance exactly when someone's conduct is better explained by causes than by reasons — small kids, someone mid-psychotic-episode — and those people are off the blame hook.
(5) Blame is at bottom a kind of address: it's a demand the victim makes on the wrongdoer, and someone who never blames the person who wronged them is failing to take themselves seriously.
(6) You can perfectly well go on blaming someone who wronged you badly twenty years ago and who now has advanced dementia and couldn't take any of it in.

I can't see a contradiction anywhere in that, which definatly means I'm missing something. Can you audit it: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and for each real one what the cheapest revision is and what it'd cost me? Rank them by severity rather than flagging everything equally, and say whether there's a single change that would clean up more than one at a time.
