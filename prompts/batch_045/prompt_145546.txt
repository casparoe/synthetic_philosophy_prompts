here's my opening move.

Original nozick: a million fans each drop a quarter into a box to watch Wilt play, nobody is forced, and whatever pattern you liked about the starting distribution is gone. Fine, I grant the intuition. Now imagine the same million people and the same pile of money arriving a different way: their phones autoplay his highlight reels and micropay a fraction of a cent per view, default-on, buried in terms nobody read, opting out takes forty minutes of menu-diving, and no individual viewer's contribution makes any difference to whether he ends up rich — he'd be just as rich if you personally stayed home. Every transfer there is uncoerced in your sense, and at the same time nothing is chosen by anybody. So which is it: does justice in transfer need actual consent, or is it satisfied by the absence of coercion plus the bare fact that a transfer occured? If it's the second, I dont see why the principle generates entitlements at all rather than just describing how the money moved; if it's the first, then your example is doing all its work with a quarter-into-a-box picture of agency that has definately stopped being the normal case, and you owe me a story about why the second case is still one of persons acting.
