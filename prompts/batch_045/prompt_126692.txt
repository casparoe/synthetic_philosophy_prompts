I'm TAing a methods recitation this term and I want a one-page decision tree on the can culture be a cause question — we just did Weber's Protestant Ethic essays from 1904/05 and half the class came away thinking correlation between Calvinist regions and commercial success, therefore ideas cause economies, while the other half thinks that's just institutions or trade routes in a costume.

Not an essay, a tree. Start node: you have a decent, replicated correlation between a widely shared value or belief in some population P and an economic outcome E, and you want to know what you are licensed to say. Every branch should be a single question a student could answer without knowing where it leads — no node that just asks whether values matter — and every leaf a named position, or unoccupied if you think nobody holds it, plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Six to nine leaves, aimed at first-year grad students.

Three distinctions I want the tree forced to handle, because the seminar keeps collapsing them:

- Mechanism: can you trace a path from the content of the belief to individual action without the path going through and then they wanted to get rich, which is just the outcome restated. Where does a story that fails this test land.
- Composition: does the explanation need most of P to hold the value, or only a minority. Weber's own case looks like it works if the ascetic entrepreneurs are a small group who set terms everyone else then has to meet, which is a different causal animal from a population-wide ethos, and I want a node that separates them and a line on why it matters.
- Persistence: what becomes of the causal claim if the belief dies out but E keeps going — roughly the situation in the famous closing image where the religious shell outlives the faith — or if E turns up in a population that never had the belief but did have similar institutions.

Then flag any two leaves your tree cannot actually separate, and for each empty combination say in one line whether it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored. Please commit: give me the one branch ordering you would actually hand out, with your reasons for it, rather than three alternative trees with footnotes, and say plainly where the tree goes silent — if you think no amount of node-answering settles whether culture was a cause here, tell me that instead of inventing a node that fakes it.
