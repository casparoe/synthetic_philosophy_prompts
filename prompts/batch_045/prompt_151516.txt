Help me work out when I actually think drawing lots is legitimate, because my gut keeps going different directions and I can't tell whether there's one distinction behind it or whether I'm just inconsistent.

Definately fine: who does the dishes in my shared house, drawn from a hat each week; juries; which of two medically identical patients gets the one available kidney; oversubscribed school places; random tax audits; who has to take the minutes this month. Also fine, which surprises me a bit: military conscription by lottery, at least compared to any alternative I can think of.

Not fine: flipping a coin between two finalists the search committee has rated exactly equal for a professorship — that feels like refusing to make a judgement, not like being fair. Not fine: choosing the whole legislature by lot. Not fine: lawyers striking people off a randomly drawn jury panel until they like how it looks. Not fine: a citizens' assembly on something charged like abortion, if it was recruited from whoever answered a mailer and only about a third of the original draw was still showing up by the final weekend — even though I do think a properly drawn assembly with recommendation-only power is fine, and I even like the idea of a lottery-drawn second chamber that can only delay legislation and send it back.

My tentative rule is that lotteries are for being fair to people while elections and exams are for getting good outcomes, but juries and the veto chamber are supposed to deliver good outcomes too, so the rule doesn't obviously survive my own verdicts.

The one I keep going back and forth on: filling a role by lot from among everyone who already passed a competence threshold, so the lottery only chooses between people known to be able to do the job.

Is there a single principle that gets all of this, which of my verdicts would it force me to drop, and what does it say about the qualified-pool case?
