I am writing the specification for the decision rule of an agent I am designing — a software agent that will act in environments containing other agents and systems that model, forecast, or simulate it. Before I choose among the existing candidate rules, I want the specification they are supposed to satisfy.

The rule is to be used first-personally and ex ante: it takes everything the agent knows at the moment of choice and returns an act. It is not a rule for grading choices after the fact, and it is not a theory of what kind of agent it would be best to be. Please write the adequacy conditions for a rule with that job, as a list in which each item gives (i) the requirement, (ii) the case or consideration meant to motivate it, and (iii) whether the requirement is non-negotiable or merely a preference of one camp in this dispute.

The cases I expect any such list to be anchored in: the standard Newcomb setup with a highly but not perfectly reliable predictor; a case in which an available act is strong evidence of a bad state it has no causal tendency to produce; a case in which the agent faces a near-exact copy of itself in a prisoner's dilemma; a case in which, whichever act the agent settles on, it can see that it would have done better to have settled on the other one; and the demand, whatever its source, that a rule not lead its user to be predictably poorer than a user of some other rule across a long series of choices.

I also want two cases I have not seen treated, because I think they separate requirements that happen to coincide in the familiar set:

- *The open loop.* The predictor does not infer from your history; it runs a simulation of your deliberation in real time. The simulation is displayed to you on a short delay, and it updates on your reaction to what you see, so that what it shows both influences and is influenced by what you do. Its track record is effectively perfect. What should a rule say here, and how many of the requirements on your list are really requirements that the situation be representable as an acyclic causal structure with the prediction screened off from the act?
- *The mixed population.* On half the agents the predictor is near-perfect; on the rest it is no better than chance. You do not know which kind you are, there is no test you can run, and your own sense of how predictable you are may be unreliable. Is it a requirement that the rule recommend the same act to an agent who knows it is predictable and to one who knows it is not? Is it a requirement that its output not depend on facts about the agent that the agent cannot in principle have? What does each candidate rule make the answer depend on?

Three more things. First, tell me which distinctions a rule must be able to mark, and which it may collapse — in particular the distinctions between what the agent does, what it is disposed to do, and what procedure produced the doing. Second, if some subset of your requirements cannot all be satisfied together, identify the subset and say what follows: I would rather have a short coherent list than a longer wish-list, and I want to know which requirement to give up and what giving it up costs. Name the requirement that, in your judgment, excludes the most otherwise-attractive candidate rules, and the requirement that is most often insisted upon but really only registers what one camp refuses to accept.

Keep most of the weight on the criteria. A brief scorecard at the end is welcome — a few lines each on how the rule that scores acts by the evidence they provide, the rule that scores them by their causal or counterfactual consequences, and the rule that treats your act as the output of a procedure shared with whatever is predicting you come out against the list — but I care about the specification, not the scoring.

Finally: separate clearly what is settled in this area from what is genuinely contested among people who work on it, and do not report a contested verdict as the majority one. Treat my two cases as contestable too — say so if one is a disguised version of a familiar case, or if a requirement I have implied is not really one — and mark the places where you are working something out rather than reporting how the literature comes down.
