I have to hand in a one-page "metaphysical profile" before my supervisor will sign off on my thesis chapter on time, and she wants the position stated as a numbered list of theses so the committee can pick at them. Here's my list. I've put them roughly in order of how confident I am, but I've reordered it three times this week, so don't read anything into that.

1. Special relativity gets the local structure right, and there's no absolute simultaneity and no privileged foliation. I also don't think the "the universe has a preferred frame — the average-matter/CMB rest frame — so there's your present" move helps. That's a contingent fact about how matter happens to be spread out, not something a metaphysics of time should lean on. And I'm told the argument doesn't really need a distant galaxy anyway: someone walking past me in the corridor has a simultaneity slice tilted by nanoseconds, and you can chain those.
2. So the block universe is true. Past and future events are as real as present ones.
3. But I want to soften the result. I want to say the boundary of the present is vague: the argument only ever shows that what's simultaneous-for-me-now is fuzzy over tiny intervals when the observer is nearby, and a few nanoseconds of fuzziness is harmless.
4. There's an objective asymmetry between past and future, and it isn't indexical — it's not just that I remember the past and not the future. It's grounded in something physical, the thermodynamic gradient, the way radiation goes outward rather than inward.
5. Becoming is real, and my experience of passage isn't just an illusion. The future genuinely becomes present; it isn't merely "later than" the past.
6. Deliberation is rationally required and my deliberating makes a real difference to what I do — and I don't think that even needs an unreal future, since a fixed future isn't fatalism. I'm also drawn to the "thin red line" thought: even in a branching structure there's a privileged actual branch.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture. Go through these and tell me which combinations actually bite and which only look bad — I'd guess at least one of the scarier-looking clashes is fine on inspection, and at least one thing I think supports another thing doesn't actually. Rank the real tensions by how bad they are, and for each one say exactly what would have to give and what that costs me. Then tell me if there's a view that keeps all six and what it would commit me to that I haven't put on the list.

Two constraints: I genuinely don't know which of these I'd drop, so please don't ask me to choose before you've laid it out. And "just give up the block universe" isn't an answer unless it's really the cheapest thing on offer — say what else it would take me down with it.
