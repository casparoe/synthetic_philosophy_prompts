i'm putting together a one-page pitch for a small workshop on failure modes in capable AI systems, and i want to do it on this. people tell two stories about how a system goes wrong: the influence-seeking story (it starts angling for resources, resisting being turned off, getting itself copied) and the slow-drift story — i've been calling the key mechanism *approval drift*: every individual update is signed off by someone with good local reasons, a new tool, a retrain, a handoff between teams, a slightly different spec, but nobody ever endorsed the trajectory, and after a few years the thing is optimising for something no one chose.

my half-formed idea is that these get treated as rival explanations but they're only rivals if you assume you could tell which one you're in, and i suspect for a lot of practically relevant cases you can't — mild power-seeking-ish behaviour plus mild goal drift is compatible with both, and the interventions come apart enough that guessing wrong is expensive.

turn that into a proposal: the question stated precisely, why the usual framing of each story leaves it open, the method (six months, no big training runs — conceptual work plus toy models or small simulations, and it shouldn't rest on empirical claims about today's models), what a deliverable would look like for someone actually running evals or writing governance rules, and what would count as it failing. also give me a line against the objection that if the two stories have the same observable signature the distinction is empty — i think that's wrong but i can't say why yet. no citations needed, i'll add those myself.
