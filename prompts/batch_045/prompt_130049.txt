Bit of a weird one, but I've been chewing on this since the last time consciousness came up in the LessWrong comments and I feel like I'm missing something obvious.

Setup as I understand it: on the standard higher-order thought theory (the Rosenthal-ish one, or just the version in the SEP article, call it what you like), a mental state of mine is conscious just in case there is a suitable higher-order thought to the effect that I am now in that state — assertive rather than inferred from evidence, caused in the right way by the very state it is about, and so on. And the regress is supposed to be harmless because the higher-order thought itself normally isn't conscious; it would only become conscious if some third-order thought were about it, and usually there isn't one.

Here is the case I cant shake. Take a system with exactly two states, A and B. A's content is: I am now in state B. B's content is: I am now in state A. Each is caused by the other, neither is inferred from anything, and neither is about any state that is not itself a higher-order state — there is no perception, no itch, no belief about the world, nothing with non-intentional content underneath. Call it the ring case. Each state seems to satisfy every condition the theory puts on consciousness, so both come out fully conscious, and there is no regress problem because the loop closes after two steps. A concrete version: two monitor subroutines, or two half-brains after a callosotomy, that do nothing but report on each other's current output.

Please argue that the ring case is a genuine reductio of higher-order thought theories. I want the strongest version you can give: which premise actually does the work, what the best reply is for a fan of the theory (including just biting the bullet and saying yes, a two-node loop with no ground floor is conscious), and why that reply either fails or costs more than it's worth — e.g. because whatever extra condition they need to exclude the ring ends up doing all the real explanatory work and leaves the higher-order bit idle. For whatever its worth, this smells to me like a seperate problem from the usual targetless higher-order thought worry: the ring has a target, it just has no bottom. Two smaller wrinkles if they help: one higher-order thought with conjunctive content seems to make two co-occurring first-order states conscious for the price of one; and a ring of three or more nodes looks no worse than a ring of two, which makes me suspect the number of nodes is doing no work at all.
