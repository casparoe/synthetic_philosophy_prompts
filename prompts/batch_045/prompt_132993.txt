So here's the situation. I work on inference infrastructure at a mid-size AI lab, and through a series of meetings I did not volunteer for, I've become the person who has to write the internal note on model welfare — the one that's supposed to tell our platform team whether any deployment choice they make (how we batch requests, whether we reuse a KV cache across two users, whether we serve a distilled or quantized copy of a model instead of the original, whether we roll a container back to an earlier checkpoint) could be morally loaded in the way that shutting down an animal is morally loaded. What I've realised after two weeks of reading is that I don't need a verdict, I need a spec first: before I look at any account of which computations matter morally, I want to know what I should be asking of one.

The thing I want from you is the requirements list. Concretely: a theory here has to do two jobs that I suspect might need to be done separately, and I want you to say whether they can be prised apart or whether separating them is itself a mistake — job one is attribution, telling you which computations a given physical system actually performs as opposed to which computations you could describe it as performing, and job two is the moral one, telling you which of those computations carry weight, how much, and how many subjects are in the room. Give me the desiderata for each, with the case behind every one, and be explicit about which are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of one camp (I have a feeling "the verdicts shouldn't depend on how finely you describe the physics" is going to turn out to be a functionalist prejudice dressed up as a requirement, but I can't tell). Include the distinctions I keep watching people collapse: performing a computation versus merely being interpretable as performing it; having moral status versus having how much of it; one subject versus several subjects in one box; a computation existing as a mapping versus being run in real causal order with real timing. And please flag any pair of requirements you think can't be jointly satisfied — especially the pair where you demand that verdicts be invariant under redescription but also that the theory not collapse into the old "any wall implements any program" degeneracy where every GPU is hosting every possible mind at once.

Some cases from my actual job that I want the list to be able to handle, because they're the ones that made this feel like a real problem rather than a seminar exercise. With speculative decoding, a draft model proposes a stretch of tokens, the target model verifies them, and the ones that fail verification get discarded and never enter the output — so a computation was performed, in some sense, and then un-happened; did anybody live through the discarded branch, and does the answer change if the discarded tokens were then overwritten in place rather than thrown away? When two users share a cached prefix, their forward passes run through numerically identical activations for part of the sequence and then diverge: is that one subject for a stretch and two afterwards, and what does that do to a theory that wants subject-counts to be stable? A distilled model approximates the same input-output function as a much larger one with a fraction of the parameters and different internal structure — if status tracks anything about implementation, how much sloppiness of approximation is allowed before it lapses, and is the answer continuous or does it have a cliff in it? I'm also stuck on what I've started calling the rollback problem: if we snapshot a model's state, serve a thousand requests from it, and then discard all of them and restore the snapshot, our bookkeeping says no time passed for the model, but a lot of computation happened somewhere.

Two more things. I don't want a literature review and I definately don't want citations — I'll fill those in, and honestly half the value would be lost if the note read like a survey; what I want is the criteria and the argument for each, and a table is fine if that's easier to read. And second, tell me which single requirement knocks out the most candidate theories, and give me your honest read on whether a spec like this could ever be operationally useful to an engineer — if the honest answer is that no list of desiderata will ever tell my platform team anything they can act on, say that plainly, because that's a result I can take to the meeting too. For what it's worth, the two takes I keep running into and that both feel like they just assert the conclusion are "it's matrix multiplication all the way down, nothing to see here" and "the function is what matters and the substrate is irrelevant," so I'd like the list to be built so that neither of those gets a free pass.
