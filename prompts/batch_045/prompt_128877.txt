I spent thirty-eight years as a research geophysicist, most of it spent reconstructing the history of rock bodies from measurements that could only be made in the present, and I have come to philosophy late and largely on my own. So I would rather have one well-worked case than a survey of positions.

What I am after is an example of a place where the presentism/eternalism disagreement does real work: a concrete case, ideally one drawn from ordinary scientific practice rather than from a philosopher's armchair, in which the two views force different descriptions, different inferences, or different conclusions — not merely different inventories of what exists.

Three constraints. It should not be the argument from the relativity of simultaneity, which I have read and which seems to me to settle considerably less than people claim. It should not be the bare truthmaker problem — what presently existing thing makes it true that the dinosaurs died out, or that the field notes I took in 1987 were accurate — because the reply that present traces together with the laws will do the work is one I can already reconstruct for myself. And something has to turn on the difference: a measurement that could not be interpreted, a generalization that could not be stated, an inference licensed on one view and not on the other, or a cost that one side must pay and the other need not.

The direction I keep circling, which you should either develop or replace with a better one, concerns laws. If all that ever exists is a single present state, it is not clear to me what the dynamical generalizations I relied on for forty years are generalizations about; and then appealing to laws in order to recover the past from present traces looks uncomfortably circular, since those laws were themselves supposed to be summaries of what happens in the world. Give me the case in which this bites hardest, state it in enough detail that I can test it against work I actually did, and give me the least costly reply available to the presentist — and if the real difficulty lies somewhere else entirely, give me that case instead.
