I've been reading about nudging on my own — no academic background, just stuff I find online — and I keep running into the same argument: nudges are the gentle option and bans or mandates are the heavy-handed one, because a nudge leaves your options open and a mandate doesn't. I think that framing is missing something, and I want a case that shows what.

Build me a scenario where a nudge and a hard rule are aimed at exactly the same goal, are both paternalistic in the same way (both are trying to make the people affected better off by their own standards, not by somebody else's), and both work about equally well — but where the nudge is clearly the worse thing to do.

Constraints, so it isn't an easy win:
- Realistic and ordinairy: a workplace, a clinic, a city office, an app. One paragraph. No sci-fi, no made-up technology.
- The nudge can't be sneaky. Everyone affected has been told in plain language that it's in place and roughly how it works.
- Its badness can't come from it being ineffective, from the people running it having bad motives, or from attributing a bias to the targets that they don't actually have.
- It also can't rest on the bare "you were manipulated, your autonomy was bypassed" complaint. I want the wrong traced to something more specific than that.

Here's my half-baked version of what the specific thing might be, use it if it helps or replace it if yours is better: what matters isn't how much force gets used but whether the influence is answerable — whether I can notice it acting on me, argue with it, complain about it, and whether it fails visibly when it's mistaken rather than quietly. I've been calling it the answerability test.

After the scenario: say what the "your options are still open, so what's the complaint" view says about the case and what the case says back, point to the single feature that's doing the work, tell me what happens if you remove that feature, and then give me the strongest reply a nudge defender has — the one that isn't just repeating that the options were left open. Please write in American English.
