My study group got into the socialist calculation debate and one of us turned Mises's side of it into an argument that seems to break itself. Roughly:

(1) Rational allocation of capital goods needs commensuration — you can't weigh more steel for the bridge against more steel for the tractor works unless both can be put into a common unit.
(2) The only common unit on offer is money prices, and prices for factors of production only get formed where those factors are actually bought and sold by people who own them.
(3) Socialism abolishes that market, so a socialist economy can't calculate; it can only grope.
(4) But the same looks true inside any big firm: the car company doesn't buy components from its own components division on a market, it moves them by instruction, and nobody thinks the car company therefore can't calculate.
(5) And there's no place to stop: a firm that has internalised 10% of the transactions in an economy can calculate, and so can one that has internalised 20%, and going from n% to n+1% doesn't break anything.
(6) So one that has internalised all of them can calculate too — and (1)–(3) prove too much, meaning the argument was never really about socialism.

I also have my own fix, which might be what's wrong with it. What calculation seems to need isn't private property as such but that whoever decides an allocation personally eats the losses. You could get that without private ownership of the means of production: managers post non-transferable bonds, and their own future consumption claim rises or falls with the profit or loss on the inputs they took, so they can't just pass costs along. If that's enough, socialism is out of the argument — but then I can't see the the argument was ever about calculation at all, which smells wrong to me.

Which step do you reject, and what does giving it up cost? I'd like to keep (4) if I can, since the alternative seems to be saying big firms are secretly socialist and hopeless at arithmetic. And does my bond scheme actually change anything, or does it smuggle ownership back in under another name?
