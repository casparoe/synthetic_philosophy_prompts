My friend has this Dennett-ish line she applies to everything: the self isn't an extra thing in the head, it's more like a centre of gravity — an abstraction that earns its keep but isn't a further object; the self as a centre of narrative gravity. Whatever. What I actually want is to predict her next verdict, because she's been ruling on cases for years and I think her track record fits more than one rule. Her verdicts so far: dense retrograde amnesia with the forward-looking story intact — full self, no fuss; advanced dementia where the story-telling has mostly collapsed — not yes or no, she says the self is thinning out and is happy to talk in degrees; a split-brain patient with two independent streams of self-description — two selves, and she's oddly untroubled; a corporation whose PR department spins a polished first-person story — no, and she's impatient with the question; Sherlock Holmes — no, there's a narrative with a centre of gravity to it but nobody living it, its the readers' posit not his; a current chatbot that says I and is fluent about its own states — no, also impatiently, something like a narrator with nothing at stake; someone whose entire self-story was installed by a cult over fifteen years — yes, still a self, where the story came from doesn't matter, only that one is being run.

The case she hasn't ruled on: a man with no episodic memory in either direction — can't recall a single personal episode, can't imagine one — who nonetheless knows a lot of bare fact about his own life, keeps his own appointments, forms and executes long-term plans for his own future, has stable projects and tastes, and says I without hesitation. No inner story being spun, just well-organised self-directed concern.

I can see three rules that fit her record about equally well — narrativity (a self wherever a story is being run), predictive payoff (a self wherever positing the abstraction buys prediction and explanation you can't get otherwise), and some answerability-or-stake condition — and I think they come apart right here. Which is she most likely running, what does each one predict about the man without episodes, and what single further case should I put to her to separate them? And the bit I can't settle myself: does a self need a narrator at all, or would a centre of deliberative gravity — the point where a creatures plans and concerns converge, story or no story — do the same unifying work, or does that just collapse into something else entirely?
