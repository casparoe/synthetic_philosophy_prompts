i'm putting together a short handout for a seminar section on naturalizing content, and I want to break the usual script. Everywhere you look the dialectic runs one way: the informational/covariation story (Dretske-style, plus Fodor's asymmetric dependence) is supposed to be the one that can't make room for misrepresentation — it collapses into the disjunction problem, every token comes out true — while teleosemantics is the theory that gets error, because a state can have the proper function of indicating A and still be tokened by something else. I want a case where that flips.

So: build me a single-paragraph case, ordinary terrestrial biology (no Twin Earth, no Swampman, no brains in jars, no implanted chips), in which

- some creature has an internal state type S, produced by an innate mechanism with a real selected-effect proper function; nothing is malfunctioning, the mechanism is doing its job the way it was selected to;
- S actually gets tokened by two different distal conditions A and B, where B's power to cause S is genuinely *derived* from A's (mimicry, a shared cue, a derived law) rather than independent of it;
- the etiological/consumer-style teleosemanticist is pushed either to a disjunctive content ("A or B") or at least can't settle between that and "A" — so the theory cannot determinately say that a B-token misrepresents — while the informational account, using asymmetric dependence, gets to say S means A and the B-tokens are errors;
- and this has to *fall out* of the case: show that the selection record is genuinely indifferent between the two mappings, i.e. nothing in the actual or counterfactual fitness history discriminates between "S tracks A" and "S tracks A-or-B." Don't just announce that teleosemantics is stuck — earn it. If the flip only works because you snuck in a broken mechanism or stipulated the mapping, that's cheating for my purposes.

Two more constraints: the two candidate contents shouldn't be related as determinate and determinable, and the content should be distal, not the proximal cue.

After the paragraph: a table of what each theory says about the B-tokens and why; which feature of the case is doing the real work; what happens to both verdicts if you remove that feature; how a teleosemanticist would push back on the case; and an honest flag if the specs can't all be satisfied at once, telling me which one you had to bend. American English please.
