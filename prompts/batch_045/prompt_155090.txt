Me and a colleague — call her Priya — have been running a little internal forecasting thing at work for about three years. Roughly 40 questions we both logged before the fact, mostly about whether our own projects would hit their milestones: software delivery dates, enrollment targets, whether a vendor would actually ship. We've always filed separately. Now, starting next quarter, we've been told to hand in ONE number jointly per question, and of course the first question this applies to is the one we disagree about most: whether the data platform migration will be done before the contract penalty date in Q3. I'm at 72%, she's at 34%. We have to submit Monday.

The annoying part is that we've already done everything you're supposed to do. We've swapped written reasoning, we agree on the base rate for projects of this size, we agree about the team's track record, we agree neither of us has private evidence the other lacks. The only first-order disagreement left is how much to lean on one analogous migration from 2022 that I think is informative and she thinks is disanalogous in a specific way. And then there's this extra fact we both accept: over our 40 logged questions she's slightly better calibrated than me overall, but on the 11 questions where we started more than 15 points apart, the straight average of our two numbers would have scored better than either of us alone. She thinks that's a small-sample artifact of one cluster of questions; I think it's the whole ballgame.

We've been going in cirlces for two months, so we've agreed to stop arguing and write it up as an adversarial collaboration instead: I keep playing the conciliationist ("you're smart, you've seen everything I've seen, move toward me"), she plays the steadfast one ("I have the better argument on the 2022 case, and your disagreement is just one more piece of evidence to weigh, not a reason to discount my own"). She is not a pushover here — the track record is on her side and she has a real reply to the averaging fact — so please don't make her side the weaker one.

What I want is the document we'd actually both sign:

1. A numbered section of claims we can both accept — including about peerhood (is it even symmetric here, given she's better calibrated but I've read more about this specific vendor?), and about what kinds of things disagreement can rationally do to a person.
2. A disagreements table: each remaining dispute stated twice, once in the wording I'd accept and once in the wording she'd accept, so neither of us has to sign a sentence that misdescribes us. Please separate anything that turns out to be merely verbal.
3. The cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one of us. I suspect at least these: (a) whether a full body of evidence pins down one uniquely rational credence or leaves a permissible range; (b) what our own track-record fact really shows — noise cancellation, one biased person, or selection artifact; (c) the self-application thing, i.e. whether the conciliationist rule is supposed to apply to itself, since we now disagree about disagreement; (d) whether what we owe each other is a change in belief or just a number on a form, since we're submitting one joint forecast and maybe averaging is right as an aggregation rule even if neither of us moves.
4. For each crux, a concrete case, argument, or bit of evidence we'd both agree bears on it, with each of our predictions written down in advance.

End with a short coda: for each of us, what would actually make us change our mind, where each position's reasoning is most likely to fail, and flag the places where the agreed wording is doing more work than it looks like — sentences we'd both sign but for different reasons.
