I'm writing a seminar paper on naturalizing mental content, and I keep sliding between a Millikan-style etiological teleosemantics and a Dretske-style indicator view, because my own verdicts on cases don't line up the way either theory says they should. So I want to work verdicts-down rather than theory-up. Here is roughly where I land going by gut, in no particular order. (i) The standard frog case — a retinal state that fires for any small, dark, moving thing and triggers the snap, in an environment where nearly all such things are flies, but where I also shoot BB pellets past the animal and it snaps at those — the state means *fly there*, not *small dark moving thing*; the pellet case is the state misfiring, not the content turning disjunctive. (ii) Take the magnetosome bacteria, and imagine a variant population in which the magnet is reversed, so the internal state that ancestrally indicated the direction of the deoxygenated sediment now reliably indicates the direction of oxygenated water and the bacterium swims into it and dies: the content stays fixed on what it ancestrally indicated, and the reversed bacterium is systematically misrepresenting rather than newly meaning something else. (iii) Swampman has no content at all, and I am untroubled by that. (iv) A creature whose prey-detector evolved in a niche where flies and a certain edible beetle always co-occur and which eats both, with nothing in the selection history distinguishing them: I want to say the content is just *prey* or *food*, not *fly*, because selection never cared about the difference — but I notice this sits badly with (i), since selection never distinguished flies from small-dark-moving-things either. (v) A toad-like creature whose predator-detector has been rewired by a mutation so that it now drives grooming instead of fleeing: I want to say the state still means *predator*, and is simply not being put to its proper use. (vi) I also do interpretability work on neural networks, which is why these last two keep creeping in: a probe finds a direction in a frozen network's activation space that separates cats from dogs perfectly, but the network never uses it for anything. Verdict: no content, a mere indicator, like tree rings or a thermometer nobody reads. (vii) The same network, but the feature emerged as a side effect of training, was never intended or selected for and is not needed for the loss, and yet downstream circuits exploit it and task performance improves: I am genuinely torn. I want to say it represents the cat/dog distinction, but I cannot see what confers the function. (viii) Colonists in a new environment where an old detector now reliably indicates something new and their descendants are selected for responding to it: I want to say that after enough generations the content really has shifted, but I also do not want content to come in degrees or to pop into existence at some arbitrary generation.

My working principle has been: a state of a system represents P only if it has the function of indicating P, where functions are conferred by a history of selection (or deliberate design) for producing effects of a certain kind, and where P is whatever had to be indicated in order for those effects to be produced; states with no such history represent nothing, however reliably they correlate with things. Please test that principle against my eight verdicts and repair it rather than replacing it wholesale unless replacement is really required.

Concretely, I'd like: the principle restated precisely, with the case that motivates each clause; an account of which clause fails first and on which case — I suspect the third clause collapses (i) into (iv), and I want to know whether I should give up the specificity of (i) or the generality of (iv), and what each costs; where the repaired principle leaves (vii), which is the one I actually care about, since I can't tell whether letting a consumer's exploitation confer content makes content too cheap or is exactly the right generalization; and its verdicts on two cases I haven't listed — a state selected at once for two quite different effects (two contents, or one disjunctive one?), and the very first creature whose state is selected for the very first time (does content appear at the first selection event, the second, or only once the lineage is established?). Please be explicit about which of my verdicts your principle cannot accommodate and which one you would overturn.
