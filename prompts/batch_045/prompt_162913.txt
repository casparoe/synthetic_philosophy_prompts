I'm a teaching assistant for an undergraduate seminar on philosophical questions about artificial intelligence, and there is one question in the syllabus that keeps surviving every attempt I make to dissolve it. So I'd like a number with the reasoning attached, not a verdict: tell me where the weight sits.

Fix the target case stipulatively, so that we are not arguing about empirical facts. Suppose a future system is trained purely by next-token prediction on a large corpus of English text, with no perceptual input, no embodiment, and no interaction with anything; suppose it performs at or above expert level on arbitrarily many novel and adversarially written probes concerning the physical and social situations its texts describe, including counterfactual setups that occur nowhere in the corpus and never occurred in the world; and suppose that intervention-based probing shows its internal representations to be causally efficacious in the strong sense that there are directions in activation space along which pushing the model changes its later predictions the way that changing the corresponding feature of the described situation would, with this result surviving retraining on corpora in which the vocabulary has been systematically scrambled (color terms permuted, spatial terms relabeled, names replaced). Call anything satisfying all of that an unbound simulator. The proposition I want priced: that an unbound simulator does not understand the situations its texts are about, because a learner exposed only to linguistic form, and never to the things the language is about, cannot acquire meaning from form alone, and so is at best an extremely accomplished mimic. Assume the question has a truth value, and count it as resolved against the proposition if the form-only argument fails in principle for such a system — that is, if nothing about form-only training as such bars it from understanding, whatever we end up saying about today's models.

My own credence in the proposition, so stipulated, is about 0.35. A friend who is not in the habit of dismissing these systems puts it at 0.05, and I cannot tell whether the gap between us is philosophical or just a difference in how impressed we are by the stipulated facts. Give me your number, decomposed. I suggest at least these terms, but add any you think belong: that semantic content constitutively requires a relation to something beyond linguistic form, whether causal-perceptual contact with the referents or embedding in practices and purposes, rather than being fixable by internal causal-inferential role alone; that if such a requirement holds, a text-trained system could nonetheless inherit or satisfy it, whether by deriving it from the outputs of creatures who do have such contact or by having internal structure that plays the same functional part; that the stipulated behavioral and interventionist evidence is even the right kind of evidence, as opposed to evidence of something that merely covaries with understanding and could in principle be produced by a system that lacks it; that the argument has the scope it needs, which you can test against cases we are reluctant to surrender, such as a person blind from birth who learns everything they know about color from text and testimony, or a historian's grasp of a period they have only read about, and where the line is drawn if any of those are conceded; and how much of the live dispute is verbal, in the sense that the word is doing different theoretical work in the philosophical and the engineering literatures. Give a number and one line of justification for each term, say how you are combining them and why that combination rather than another, and tell me which term you would least confidently defend.

Then two closing things. First: which one consideration, if it came out the other way, would move your final number most, and roughly how far — I care less about your headline figure than about what it is hostage to. Second: between the person at 0.35 and the person at 0.05, where does the disagreement most likely live? I don't want it depends what you mean by understanding as a way of stopping; if a large part of your uncertainty is verbal, price that as one term alongside the others and let the rest of the decomposition stand.
