hey — I edit a small internal newsletter for the team that writes analogical-reasoning items for a benchmark (the "abc is to abd as xyz is to ?" kind), and I want a satire piece for the next issue: a parody of our own Item Writer's Manual, about 600 words, set out as a numbered list of house rules, deadpan, in a voice that takes itself completely seriously.

The jokes have to come out of the real difficulty rather than out of a silly made-up version of it, because that's who reads the thing: in that little microdomain the concepts genuinely won't sit still. "Successor" means one thing if the string is read against the whole Roman alphabet and another if the letters present in the item define their own tiny alphabet — and whether z has a successor at all, or what it is, is exactly where it bites; "the same letter" slides between letter-type and letter-token; whether you change the last letter or the whole run of identical letters at the end depends on pressures inside the item, not on anything you could have stated in advance. The Manual's response to all of this is to legislate it clause by clause, so let the reductio land on its own: the more exactly it pins the concepts down, the more sub-clauses, exceptions and designation tags it needs, and it still keys something wrong. Keep it fair — someone who has actually worked on analogy-making should recognize the slippage as the real article.

I'd like: one rule that quietly reverses a rule stated three clauses earlier without admitting it; one sample item with its official answer key and an explanation that uses two incompatible readings in the same paragraph; and a footnote or appendix from a dissenting writer who thinks the slippage is the whole point, written in the dry language of a bug that got reclassified as a feature. No real people named, no winking at the reader. American English, definately under ~600 words.
