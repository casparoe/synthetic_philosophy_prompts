Write a villanelle in the voice of a medieval Ashʿarite occasionalist, on the doctrine that created things have no productive power: the fire does not burn the cotton, God creates the burning at the moment of contact, and what we read as necessity is only His custom, which He is free to break. Let the form do the metaphysical work. The two refrains should be, first, the denial that any creature brings anything about, and second, the concession that the order of events is merely customary and custom admits exceptions; let them keep returning because for an occasionalist nothing persists across two instants — the world is created anew at every moment, so the same lines come back around a scene that is never the same scene twice. Give the tercets distinct cases: fire and cotton first, then a pairing with no flame and no hand in it at all (a neural network's weights and the token it emits, say, or a gene and the trait it is said to produce), then one case in which the custom fails. In the closing quatrain let the two refrains meet, so that a reader who knows al-Ghazālī's discussion of causation can tell which line is doing which work and why the poem has to end holding both: the denial of creaturely power on its own would slide into a general skepticism about causes, and the custom clause on its own would slide into the merely epistemic claim that we cannot observe necessary connections.
