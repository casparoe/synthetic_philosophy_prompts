I am revising a chapter of my dissertation on the varieties of free will worth wanting, and before I commit to the argument I have been developing, I would like to know whether compatibilists have a reply to it that does not simply amount to changing the subject.

The chapter tries to make "worth wanting" tractable without tying it to any single one of our practices. Let the modal spread of a decision at t be the proportion of the nearest possible worlds — those whose total prior state differs from the actual one only minimally — in which the agent decides otherwise. My proposal is that the free will worth wanting is, at bottom, non-trivial modal spread combined with sensitivity to reasons, and hence that spread behaves like a scalar: the more of it an agent's decision involves, other things being equal, the freer she is.

If that is right, the case against compatibilism looks short. On any reasons-responsive compatibilist model, the agent's deliberative process issues in one determinate decision given the whole prior state of the world, so her spread is nil, and her deliberation therefore makes no difference to what she does — it merely redescribes an outcome that was already fixed. Compatibilists would then be in the position of securing only the deliberative variety of free will while conceding the variety that does the real work in grounding desert, which is the one I care about.

I have a refinement for the standard objection. Mere neural noise raises spread but delivers nothing anyone wants, so I stipulate that the spread must be generated inside the agent's own reason-forming mechanism rather than in its peripheral implementation: the alternatives must open up because she is weighing considerations, not because her motor system is jittery.

My question is whether that refinement makes the argument sound, and in particular whether the monotonic step holds: if I locate indeterminacy properly, inside reason-formation, do I thereby get strictly more of the variety that grounds desert than any compatibilist model delivers — more spread, more freedom, more desert — or does adding agential indeterminacy fail to increase that quantity, or even diminish it? I set aside exotic cases in which deterministic chaos lets arbitrarily small differences amplify into different decisions, since spread of that kind is not agential in the relevant sense.
