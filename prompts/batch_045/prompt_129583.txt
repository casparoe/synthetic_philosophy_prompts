I want a specification rather than a survey: the list of requirements an account of reasoning under self-locating uncertainty would have to satisfy in order to be usable as an input to decision-making about how many observers there are, have been, or will be. That is the purpose I care about — the contexts where these rules earn their keep are estimates of the total future population of a species, arguments from our apparent earliness or from the apparent absence of others, population-weighted assessments of existential risk, and the choice of a measure in cosmology. Adequacy here means: the rule has to return something an agent can actually act on, in cases where the numbers involved are astronomical, the boundaries of the relevant population are vague, and the prior is not up for revision.

Produce at most ten requirements, each stated as a demand on what a theory must deliver (or avoid), each immediately followed by the case or consideration that motivates it, and each tagged as non-negotiable, negotiable, or a preference of one camp. I want the reasoning behind the tagging: which of the things people standardly demand of these rules are genuine adequacy conditions and which are simply one camp's intuitions wearing a label.

The cases I take as fixed points, which your requirements should be anchored to rather than merely decorated with, are these: the case in which one cosmological theory predicts vastly more observers than another and the reasoner is invited to settle the matter from an armchair on the strength of her own existence; the case in which the fact of being very early in a population sequence is used to argue that the population will not be large; the first-two-people case in which the reasoners draw a counterintuitive conclusion about whether they will have descendants; the case of the awakened subject who is woken once or twice depending on a coin and cannot tell which; and three cases I want added because I think the standard framing neglects them. First, a single undivided life: one person, no duplication anywhere, who undergoes a very large or a very small number of subjectively indistinguishable episodes because a memory condition erases each from the next, and who is asked on each occasion whether the total is large or small. Second, a sorites case: candidate observers come in a continuous range of cognitive sophistication, the number of things counted as observers swings by orders of magnitude depending on where the line is drawn, and no line is principled — I want to know what a rule owes us when its output is hostage to a vague threshold. Third, a case in which the hypothesis space includes hypotheses predicting infinitely or indefinitely many observers, as cosmological hypotheses tend to.

Two distinctions I want drawn explicitly, because I suspect a good deal of the literature's confusion lives in the space between them. The first is between constraints on the self-locating rule and constraints on the prior over hypotheses or on the measure over observers: state, for each requirement, whether it is really a requirement on the rule at all, or a demand that the prior or the measure behave, and say what follows for the familiar complaint list if several of those complaints have been misfiled. The second is between what the agent should believe and what the agent should do: a rule that returns credences still leaves open whose utility is to be aggregated and at what level — the individual episode, the whole person across time, the collection of all copies — and I want a requirement, or an explicit refusal to give one, addressing whether a theory of self-locating reasoning must come bundled with a decision rule or whether the decision rule is an independent module that does all the work.

Then the part I most want. Identify any subset of your requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied, and present that as the result rather than as an apology: if the list is internally unsatisfiable, tell me which requirement has to be abandoned, what is lost by abandoning it, and what the best theory available under the weakened list looks like. Say which single requirement does the most work in excluding otherwise attractive candidates, and say which requirement you would drop first if you had to. Finally, and briefly — no more than a fifth of your answer, since the criteria are where I want the effort — score the self-sampling rule, the self-indication rule, and a no-anthropic-update rule of the kind that treats self-locating evidence as no evidence, against your list, including a note on what to do when an agent is uncertain which of them is correct.
