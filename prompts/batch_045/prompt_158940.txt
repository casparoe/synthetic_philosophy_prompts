Write me a short piece of institutional satire: the standing orders of an invented standards body — say, the Standing Committee on the Random Chord, or rename it if you have something better — as they would appear, deadpan, in their own procedural handbook.

The target is the thing I keep watching people on here do with the principle of indifference. Set up Bertrand's chord paradox: pick a chord of a circle "at random," ask whether it is longer than the side of the inscribed equilateral triangle, and you get 1/3, 1/2, or 1/4 depending on which quantity you spread your indifference over — the endpoints around the circumference, the distance of the midpoint from the centre, or the midpoint across the area of the disc. Then there is the usual rescue: insist that the answer be invariant under the transformations that leave the problem as stated unchanged, which singles out one of the three, but only because the symmetries of some particular physical setup have been smuggled in under the word "noninformative." What I want ironized is the two-step laundering operation: an arbitrary modeling choice gets dressed up as a neutral prior, and the resulting number gets dressed up as rigor.

Requirements:

- The Committee must never notice that there is anything to resolve. It responds to the paradox the way a bureaucracy responds to everything: with more procedure, licensing, registration, and forms.
- The jokes have to come from the mathematics being right. The three canonical answers, the change-of-variable that moves between them, and the invariance argument that pretends to break the tie should all be recognisable to someone who knows the literature, and at least three of the five orders should arrive, in the same confident neutral voice, at different numerical probabilities.
- Invent at least two pieces of internal jargon and use them as though the reader already knows them. I would suggest the Bertrand Dividend for the gap between the least and most favorable admissible answer — which, given a sufficiently clever change of variable, is the entire interval from 0 to 1 — and rehypothecation for the practice of scoring one physical chord under several parameterizations at once. Replace them if you have better ones.
- Do not name, quote, or cite any real author, paper, or text. No bibliography, no footnotes, no quotation marks around anyone's actual words: the Committee speaks entirely in its own voice and refers only to its own imaginary instruments.
- Stay inside the voice. No winking at the reader, no explained jokes, no closing moral, no line where the Committee admits the emperor has no clothes. A reader should be able to tell what is being sent up without being told.
- If one of the five orders wants to be about the Committee's sister division on duopoly price competition — where the embarrassment is the opposite, because every reasonable parameterization yields the same answer — that is welcome.

Format: reply as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. A single short title line is the only unnumbered text I want. Aim for roughly 200 to 300 words in total.
