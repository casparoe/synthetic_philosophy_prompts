Here is why I care. I tried to get clear on this by going through cases and trusting my gut, and the result is a mess. My verdicts:

2. In a detection task I am warned the blips will be frequent, so I report more of them; my ability to tell blips from noise is unchanged. 5. The chess player sees that the knight is en prise; the radiologist sees the lesion in the film where I see a smudge. Both are expertise, both deliver a high-level, conceptually articulated content straight into experience. Verdict: yes, penetration, and it is the same phenomenon as 4.

So here is where I am stuck, and I have three specific problems rather than one.

The first is that cases 3, 4, and 5 all look like the same thing — trained retuning of a perceptual system by experience — and I say no to one and yes to the other two. The second is that cases 2 and 6 have the same structure — a verbal suggestion, a changed report — and I call 2 a response bias and 6 a real change in what the beer tastes like. The third is the thing I most want your help with. Here is the principle I have been trying to make work, which I do not trust:

P. A background state B perceptually penetrates a subject S's perception of a distal stimulus O if and only if (a) B is a personal-level, conceptually articulated state — something S could in principle avow as a belief, desire, or expectation; (b) holding O and the sense organs fixed, B makes a difference to the phenomenal character of S's experience of O or to what S can discriminatorily respond to; and (c) the difference is not merely a change in which object gets selected, nor merely a change in response criterion, nor a change in peripheral gain.
