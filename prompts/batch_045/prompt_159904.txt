You are a philosopher of mind with a working knowledge of the clinical literature on pain. Think the question through carefully and brainstorm the candidate mappings before you commit to an answer: what I want is an audit, not a verdict.

In a seminar I TA, one analogy keeps doing all the work: pain is like a burglar alarm. The alarm's job is to tell you that someone is at the window and to get you to do something about it; the danger isn't in the noise itself; the alarm can ring with no burglar present and fail to ring while one is climbing in. A student used this to argue that the badness of pain is exhausted by its informational and motivational role — the hurt just is the volume of the alarm — and hence that pain asymbolia is no paradox at all: patients who report locating and rating a stimulus as painful, sometimes at high intensity, while insisting that it does not bother them, are simply hearing the alarm with the action-system disconnected, and have lost nothing that matters.

Lay the mapping out explicitly — what plays the burglar, the alarm, the noise, the volume, the householder, and whoever installed the alarm (does the analogy quietly import a designer and a purpose?). Then where it breaks. In particular: does the analogy presuppose a separation between a signal and its significance that someone who takes pain's badness to be intrinsic to the sensation should reject? Is the noise really not bad in its own right, and can the analogy claim that without begging the question? What does it predict about whether asymbolics stop acquiring avoidance behaviour, about whether they would decline an analgesic that left the alarm ringing, and about a hypothetical in which the affective component could be dialled down continuously while sensory intensity is held fixed — is there a setting at which such a person's life has gone worse even though they deny minding anything, and what should we say if at that setting they also stop protecting an injured limb? Separate the disanalogies that matter for the student's conclusion from the cosmetic ones, tell me whether the conclusion survives once they are on the table or whether the analogy was carrying it the whole time, and propose a better analogy if one is available.
