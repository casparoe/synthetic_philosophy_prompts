I'm putting together the text for a small display on Otto Neurath's picture language (the Vienna Method, later Isotype) and I want to close it with a poem rather than a paragraph. Could you write me a sestina: six stanzas of six lines, the same six end-words permuted, then a three-line envoi that gathers all six back.

The six end-words should each name one pictogram from a single chart — say COAL, MAN, WOMAN, CHILD, LOAF, HOUSE, or swap one out if something fits the form better — and here's the conceit I want the form to carry. In Isotype more is never a bigger pictogram, it's more of the same one, repeated, and a sign has to mean the same thing wherever it turns up: flat silhouette, no perspective, one sign one job. So the six signs should stay stubornly fixed in meaning while their slots keep getting permuted around them, and the envoi should line them up in one row like the summary row at the foot of a chart.

Where I want it to bite: the chart in question is one that can't really be drawn. It's meant to record the deaths that didn't happen after some mine safety reform — a counterfactual — and someone at the workshop has proposed a new sign for it: the ordinary pictogram crossed through with a diagonal bar, a negation bar. Take the proposal seriously and then show what it costs. The bar is itself an abstraction that has to be taught; it doesnt read as absence so much as prohibition; and once you've admitted it you're most of the way to admitting Neurath's own position, that Isotype was a "helping language", a "language-like technique", never a replacement for words, and that pictures have to be learnt too — visual education was half the work.

Accuracy bar: someone who actually knows the Isotype material should be able to tell which sign is doing which job and shouldn't wince at what the poem claims pictures can and can't do (more and fewer, fine; not, if, because, and all, much harder). But no footnotes and no gloss of the sign vocabulary inside the poem itself — it should still read as a poem to someone who's never heard of Neurath. Plain, ungrand diction please.
