I'm a dev with no philosophy training, been reading about utility in econ and decision theory on the side, and I want a consistency check on where I've landed before I write it up as a blog post. Here's the position:

1. Utility isn't a quantity in the head — no "happiness fluid". It's bookkeeping: any function that orders a person's options the same way their behaviour orders them counts as their utility function, and asking which one is the real one is a category error.
2. But I also think expected utility is the right account of choice under risk, and I'm happy saying someone is risk-averse because their utility in money is concave — which looks like it needs utility to be cardinal, not just an ordering.
3. What someone wants = what they choose. There's no fact about their "true" preferences sitting behind the behaviour.
4. Except I really dont want to give up that an addict reaching for the drug, or a user who caves to a dark pattern in a checkout flow, is acting against what they actually want.
5. And: I think choices are noisy. Same person, same options, flips across repetitions — so I've been toying with reading revealed choice the way you'd read a signal through a noisy channel, stable underlying ranking plus error.

I can see 3 and 4 rubbing against each other, but 1, 2 and 5 also seem to be pulling in different directions and I can't tell which is the load-bearing one. Audit it for me: which pairs genuinely conflict, which ones only look like they do, and for each real conflict say exactly what has to give and what that costs me. Rank by severity rather than flagging everything as equally bad, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
