I teach a graduate seminar on moral responsibility: 14 students, one 75-minute session, one long table, and a whiteboard I would rather not use until the last fifteen minutes. Everyone has already worked through Strawson's essay on freedom and resentment and the familiar optimist/pessimist back-and-forth about determinism, so they have the vocabulary; what they lack is any sense that the vocabulary carves at a joint.

Design a staged activity in which students run into the following before it is ever named: that holding someone responsible is a stance with an addressee, not a verdict about what caused an action. More narrowly, I want them to feel three things I have not seen handled in a classroom.

(a) The positive side is the real diagnostic. If a student has genuinely shifted to a cool, managerial, case-handling attitude toward someone, that should have cost them their *gratitude* for that person's good acts, not merely their resentment at the bad ones. I suspect most will discover they have not lost it, and I want that discovery to be theirs.

(b) Exemption may be domain-bound in an unstable way. A student may stop resenting this person's unreliability while still feeling warmly toward them as a friend. Is that two stances in one head, or self-deception about having taken up the detached one at all? I want them to notice the leak, or the absence of it.

(c) What a wronged party wants from a wrongdoer is an attitude, not a behavior — so that a wrongdoer's remorse should lose its point for them if they learn it was induced rather than arrived at.

Constraints. No lecture before the activity. The terms reactive attitude, participant stance, objective attitude, blame, responsibility, and compatibilism may not be spoken by anyone, me included, until the debrief; every instruction must be given in plain language, so the detached stance has to be introduced as something like handling a person as a case to be managed. Each student works with a real, genuinely minor wrong done to them by someone they still know; no one is ever asked to disclose its content, only the grammatical form of what they write and whether it survived.

The machinery I have in mind, which you should improve rather than copy: privately recall the wrong; write one sentence in the second person beginning You owed me —; read it aloud to a neighbor; privately predict, on a slip, how much each of three new pieces of information will defuse it, and rank them. Then the three cards in turn — the action was the settled outcome of causes the agent did not choose, described so that it is metaphysically airtight; the agent did not know and did not aim at the harm; the agent is not the kind of thing to which demands of this sort can be addressed at all. After each card the student must do something physical with the sentence: leave it standing, soften it, or convert it into a third-person report to whoever arranges things. After each card, three probes: can they still be grateful to this person for a good act; does the new handling spread to an unrelated domain involving the same person; and does it come more easily when the wrong was done to a friend rather than to them. Finally, each writes the one sentence the wrongdoer would have to say for things to be repaired, and then learns that the wrongdoer's remorse was produced by a technique they themselves arranged — and must say whether the repair still counts.

Answer as exactly six numbered blocks, in this order: (1) setup and materials; (2) phase one, the demand sentence and the predictions; (3) phase two, the three cards and the three probes; (4) phase three, the repair sentence and the induction card; (5) the debrief, mapping at least six specific moments in the room onto the three points above and onto the general claim about stance versus verdict; (6) what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this, how to handle each, and — honestly — which of (a), (b), (c) the activity cannot actually establish and which I will therefore have to argue for myself. No introduction, no conclusion. Each block at most 140 words, telegraphic or imperative in style.
