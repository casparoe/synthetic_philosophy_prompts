I teach a 90-minute upper-level seminar, about 14 students, philosophy of mind / well-being unit, and I want a runnable in-class activity that makes them feel that "pain is bad" is really two separable claims stuck together — there's the sensory side (where it is, how strong, burning vs. crushing) and the unpleasantness side, and once you pull them apart somebody has to say which one the badness was ever supposed to live in. No lecture up front; ideally they find the split themselves by actually undergoing a mildly painful thing (ice water, a wall sit, a snap on the wrist — nothing that leaves anyone genuinely suffering, opt-out should be easy and unremarkable) alongside something genuinely horrible but not painful (a sustained nasty tone, an intensely bitter strip, that held-breath / air-hunger feeling), and then being asked, first privately and then out loud, what they'd give up to skip each one and what they're even trying to avoid. Two wrinkles I want built into the mechanics and not just mentioned afterwards: (1) selective pain asymbolia — feels it, locates it, reports the intensity, totally unmoved — versus a global indifference where the person is unmoved by anything at all; I think people collapse these and the difference decides whether the interesting claim is about pain specifically or about caring in general; (2) the mirror case, where the sensation is intact and does hurt but the subject is fine with it, even likes it, and we have to say whether that thing is bad for them. Give me the setup, any roles or stations, a timeline that actually fits 90 minutes, the exact questions at each stage, and a debrief mapping what happened onto the live options (badness = unpleasantness, vs. badness = the way pain forces you to care about a part of your body, vs. badness = it's hijacking of attention and action). Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about which part of the disagreement the activity can't stage at all and I'll just have to explain. They shouldn't hear the word asymbolia until after.
