I'm in eleventh grade, and for a peace-and-conflict elective I have to hand in a one-page statement of where I stand on war and killing. Before I hand it in I'd like to know whether the things I believe actually fit together: my teacher read a draft and said some of them pull against each other, and wouldn't tell me which. This is the set, roughly in the order I'd defend each one.

(1) It is never morally permissible to intentionally kill another human being — not in war, not in self-defense, and not even when killing one person is the only way to stop that person from killing five.

(2) There is no deep moral difference between killing and letting die: someone who watches a child drown in a shallow pond he could easily pull her out of does something as bad as someone who holds her under. So I have real obligations to prevent deaths I could easily prevent — I give away a good part of what I earn to effective charities — and I've argued in class that a government which stands by while a genocide happens does something close to murder.

(3) Force that isn't lethal can still be permissible, including force against persons: restraining someone, tackling him, tear gas, and, if that is what it takes to stop an attacker, seriously and permanently injuring him — destroying his eyesight, say, and crippling him. I would rather be blinded and crippled myself than kill someone.

(4) I'd flip the switch in the trolley case so that one dies instead of five, but I wouldn't push the man off the footbridge and I wouldn't shoot one hostage to stop a gunman from shooting five.

(5) Even so, I think enlisting can be honorable, and I don't think less of people who serve.

What I want from you: go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and how badly, ranked by severity rather than all flagged at once; for each real tension, say what I'd have to give up and what that would cost me; and say which of the apparent tensions dissolve once the right distinction is drawn. One friend says (3) is the weak one, because I've also said I'd rather be killed than blinded and crippled — I don't see why that should matter, so tell me whether he's right.
