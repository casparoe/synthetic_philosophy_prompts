Hey — I'm revising for a game theory exam (first-year political-economy type course) and I keep going in circles on one thing about iterated deletion of strictly dominated strategies. My TA made up this practice game: Row, a firm, picks between A, B and C; Column, its rival, picks between L, M and R. Payoffs, written against (L, M, R) for Row and against (A, B, C) for Column:

Row: A = (4, 0, 6); B = (1, 4, 6); C = (1.5, 1.5, 3)
Column: L = (3, 0, 0); M = (0, 3, 0); R = (1, 1, 6)

I went through it comparing pure strategies only to other pure strategies, and nothing is strictly dominated for either player — so deletion does nothing at all here and I'd have to go straight to best responses and Nash equilibria. But the TA said the game is supposed to partly collapse if you let mixed strategies count as dominators, and that the whole point is that the first deletion unlocks a second one. I dont see how that can be true, and I'd rather figure out the hole in my reasoning than memorize a procedure.

Here's my argument. Suppose some mixture m of my own strategies beats my strategy s against every pure move of the opponent. Fix one of the opponent's moves: against that fixed move, m's payoff is just a weighted average of the payoffs of the pure strategies in its support, so at least one of those pure strategies does at least as well as m does there — and hence better than s there. So for each opponent move there's some pure strategy of mine that beats s against that move. And since s loses to m everywhere, it feels like one of those pure strategies has to beat s everywhere, which would mean mixtures can never eliminate anything that pure-strategy deletion wouldn't also eliminate, and the mixed version is basically bookkeeping for that theorem about never being a best response to any belief. I was discussing it with a friend and she said the averaging step is exactly where it breaks, but neither of us could say why, and if the argument did work it would also make the standard order-independence claim for IESDS totally trivial. So: is the TA right that this particular game collapses once mixtures are allowed, where exactly does my averaging argument go wrong, and does anything change if I redo the whole thing with weak dominance instead of strict — I vaguely remember that the order you delete in starts to matter there, but I cant see why order would matter in either version if the argument above were sound.
