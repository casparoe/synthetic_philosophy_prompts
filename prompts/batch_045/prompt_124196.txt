I am part of a small penal-reform reading group that meets after lectures, and last week we got stuck on the argument between Daniel Dennett and Gregg Caruso about just deserts and punishment. As I understand the dialectic, Dennett defends a compatibilist, forward-looking practice of holding people responsible, while Caruso argues from free-will scepticism that nobody has basic desert — nobody deserves to suffer or be censured simply in virtue of having wrongly chosen, apart from any deterrent, rehabilitative, or protective benefit — and that the state should therefore replace retributive punishment with something like a public-health-quarantine model, allowing incapacitation, treatment, and risk-reduction but not blame. I do not want another pro-and-con list; I want the specification that any adequate theory would have to meet if it were to serve as the normative backbone of a criminal code in a liberal democracy whose citizens disagree about free will. The purpose is practical: the theory must be good enough to tell a sentencing commission whether desert should be retained as a limiting principle on punishment, whether it should be replaced by the quarantine model, and, if some hybrid is needed, how to resolve cases where blameworthiness and dangerousness come apart. To make this concrete, suppose the commission is experimenting with a two-number sentence worksheet: judges must record a desert score reflecting how much blame the offender is judged to deserve for the offence, given their reasons-responsive capacities at the time and the harm actually caused, and a risk score reflecting an actuarial estimate of the probability of serious reoffending if nothing is done. The draft rule says the censuring element of the sentence may not exceed the desert score, but the duration of preventive supervision may not be shorter than the risk score requires. When the two scores diverge, the worksheet produces results that strike some of us as unfair or incoherent.

Please write the desiderata: what any theory of punishment, or any account of just deserts, must deliver to be adequate for this purpose — the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must get right, and the distinctions it must respect — with the case or consideration behind each requirement. Use at least the following stress cases: the high-desert/low-risk elderly fraudster who is unlikely to offend again; the low-desert/high-risk offender whose dangerousness is largely the product of a neurological disorder or severe duress rather than a blameworthy choice; the successful killer versus the attempted killer where the only difference is luck; the offender whose high risk score is driven mainly by poverty, addiction, or postcode rather than by culpability; and the offender who repents and reforms before sentencing, so that desert and risk appear to move in opposite directions. For each desideratum, say whether it is non-negotiable for any theory adequate to this purpose or merely a preference of a camp — for example, a retributivist insistence on strict proportionality, a forward-looking insistence that every sanction be justified by expected harm reduction, or a quarantine model's refusal to censure — and flag any requirement you suspect is really a prejudice rather than a genuine desideratum. I am especially interested in the subset of desiderata that may be jointly unsatisfiable: if treating offenders as agents conflicts with a pure quarantine model, or if desert-as-a-side-constraint plus actuarial prevention cannot both be honoured in the moral-luck or social-determinist cases, locate the collision precisely and say which requirement would have to give. Tell me also which single desideratum, if accepted, would exclude the largest number of otherwise attractive theories.

Please do not give me a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I am working offline and only need the substance. If it helps, you may end with a one-sentence verdict on each of three families of view — traditional retributivism, Dennett-style compatibilist and forward-looking accounts, and Caruso-style free-will-sceptical quarantine models — but no table, no extended comparison, and no citations; the criteria are the deliverable, not another survey. In your answer, separate what is settled among people who work on punishment theory and the free-will debate from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus: where you recommend relaxing or dropping a requirement, say whether that is a confident verdict or a conjecture, and flag any place where the answer depends on how persuasive one finds the sceptical argument about basic desert rather than on something every competent theorist accepts. Please write the response in British English.
