One camp says this makes the prior acausally exploitable and unusable for prediction; the other says it's an artifact of idealization — uncomputability, anthropic assumptions about which copy of P is "you", and decision-theoretic premises that only bite if P is itself a consequentialist.

I want these pinned down rather than hand-waved:

- the cancellation question — for every threatening simulator is there a bribing one, and if so does that restore the prior or just turn prediction into a bargaining problem;
- whether the "quarantined prior" (restrict to hypotheses causally inert w.r.t. P's future outputs) is well-definable without smuggling the conclusion in, and what it costs — do you loose the convergence guarantee;
- whether malignity is only a property of the limit or already shows up in computable approximations with a bounded description budget, with one concrete test case both accept as bearing on it and each side's prediction written down.
