Hey — I'm the token physics person in a philosophy of physics reading group (everyone else is philosophy, and they keep using words like "nomic" without flinching), and last week we did laws of nature and I came out of it realizing I have a pile of really confident gut reactions that I suspect don't add up to anything coherent. I'm not asking for a reading list, I've had enough of those; I want someone to actually try to extract the principle.

Here's where I land, case by case. (1) A universe with exactly one particle in it, coasting in a straight line forever: I want to say there IS a law there — inertial motion — even though there's a single instance and nothing else in the universe for it to interact with. I resist "it's just what happened." (2) A world that is nothing but a billion coin flips, half heads, with no pattern in the sequence at all: I want to say there's an objective chance of a half there, that that's the law, and that the exact sequence isn't law-governed in the slightest — and I'm weirdly comfortable saying the chance is a half even though, as far as I can tell, nothing in the total history of the world settles it. (3) "All gold spheres are less than a mile across": exceptionless, short to state, and not a law. What's more, I don't think it fails because it isn't simple enough — it feels like the wrong *kind* of thing. (4) A world just like ours except that in 2031 energy fails to be conserved once, visibly, and then everything goes back to normal: I say conservation was a genuine law before and after, and that "conserved except in 2031" is not a law. So a law can have exceptions, but you can't stitch the exception into the law. (5) A world whose entire history is a perfect palindrome — same sequence of states forward and backward about the midpoint. "The history reads the same in both directions" is one sentence that, given the first half, pins down every detail of the second half; that's about as much strength for as little simplicity as you could ask for, which I gather is exactly what the best-system account (simplicity, strength, fit, as I understand it) rewards. My verdict: not a law, gigantic coincidence. (6) Our own universe starts in a freakishly special low-entropy macrocondition and then runs on simple dynamical rules: I can't shake the feeling that this starting condition is law-ish, something that belongs in the book of laws rather than being a brute fact we're just handed — and I have no idea why I feel that, given how I answered (5). (7) The specific numbers — the fine-structure constant, the particle masses — those feel brute and contingent, not laws; and yet I also want to say two universes could match perfectly in every particular matter of fact and still disagree about the laws, which as I understand it is exactly what the Humean denial of necessary connections in nature is supposed to rule out.

The only principle I've managed to articulate is what I've started calling the briefing test: L is a law iff (i) no complete description of the non-nomic facts of any single region entails L, and (ii) L together with such a description of one region would let an ideal predictor derive the non-nomic facts of other regions — laws are just whatever you have to be *told* to get from here to there. I notice it probably overgenerates badly (it wants to call the palindrome a law, and the initial condition, and maybe the constants), so I'm guessing it needs repair rather than defense. Can you do better? I'd like one principle, or at most two or three candidates, stated precisely enough that I can test it: which of my cases motivates which clause, what it says about the cases I haven't listed — a law with exactly one instance ever (one particle decay in the whole history), a law that only operated during the first second and never again, and the update rule of a cellular automaton like Life, which everyone in the room agreed is a law "of" that world and which makes me suspect my reaction is just "it's the rule somebody wrote down," which for us collapses into theology — and, most importantly, an honest list of which of my verdicts it can't save and which one I should probably give up. And please tell me straight if the honest answer is that there's no single package here and I have to be a Humean about some of these and not about others. Under 900 words if you can, but don't gut anything to fit.
