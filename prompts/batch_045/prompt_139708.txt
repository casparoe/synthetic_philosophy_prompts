I'm on the rules committee for the town I moved to last year — about eighteen hundred people, one road in and out, economy is summer visitors plus the fishing co-op. I got put on it because I built the app everyone uses for the storm alerts and the volunteer rota, not because I know anything about this stuff. Which matters, because I'm the one who keeps getting asked "well, can't the app just track that," and now that's sort of eaten the agenda.

How we've ruled so far, best I can reconstruct:

- Bins in by eight on collection night, anything overhanging the pavement cut back. Passed, with a fine.
- No outdoor lights after eleven. Passed, with a fine — we're going for some kind of dark sky designation, the visitors come for it.
- Shopfronts in the old bit: one of four colours, no neon, no lit signs. Passed.
- Dogs on leads on the beach in July and August. Passed.
- One adult from every household at the two work days a year (harbour wall, beach clean). Rejected, even though turnout has been garbage for years and the wall is genuinely falling down. The chair's line was roughly that we're not fining people for not volunteering.
- Ban on "offensive" bumper stickers and flags on your own car and your own fence. Rejected.
- Curfew for under-sixteens. Rejected — "that's parenting."
- Every household keeps a seventy-two hour kit: water, torch, car phone charger. Rejected last winter, right after the road was shut for four days and two people got lifted out of a ditch with hypothermia. Same chair: "that's a cupboard question, not a town question."

New proposal on the table is the same two rules as before, dressed up because of my app. Everyone already has an account, so: (1) once a year you get a notification, you tap a box to say you've checked your kit, optional photo if you feel like it, fifty quid if you don't tap; and (2) the app assigns each household a slot at one of the work days and fines you if you don't show. Nothing else has changed — the road still closes, the wall is still falling down, turnout is still garbage. The only difference is that now we'd know who didn't do it, and it'd cost us nothing to know.

A couple of the older members hate it for a reason I cant quite get straight in my head: they say the work days used to just happen, everyone knew everyone would be there, and turning it into an assigned slot with a fine will make it feel like a tax and we'll end up loosing the people who currently turn up on their own. Which I think is a different objection from the one the chair made last time.

So: what do we do if we want to be consistent with ourselves? I can sort of see two different things we might have been doing all along — one where the line is about *what kind of thing* gets ruled on at all, one where the line is about whether we can actually catch people — and they only come apart now that catching people got cheap. Which fits our record better, what does each one say about these two proposals, and is there a third principle I'm missing that fits everything we've voted on? One thing that bugs me: tapping a box isn't the same as the kit existing, and I don't know whether that's a detail or the whole crux. And whatever rule you think we're really running, what's the next proposal I should expect to come in that would force us to show our hand?
