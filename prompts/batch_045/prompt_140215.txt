hey — I run a tiny reading group on Wittgenstein's On Certainty (a few grad students, two undergrads who are way too confident, one guy who definitly thinks Moore already settled everything) and we keep going in circles, so I want an actual instrument instead of another evening of discussion.

Can you build me a decision tree / flowchart that takes someone from a bunch of concrete answers to a named position on what a hinge actually is. Concretely:

- 8 branch nodes max, and every node has to be phrased as a question a person could answer having read maybe forty paragraphs of On Certainty and zero secondary literature. Nothing like "are hinges truth-apt" or "do hinges have an epistemic or a non-epistemic role" — that just sorts people by how much they've read. Plain cases and plain questions.
- Build it from the logical space first: lay out the questions and the combinations of answers they allow, and only afterwards drop the positions into that space. Where a combination is a hole, say so and say why you think nobody lives there — incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.
- 6 to 8 leaves. Ones I need to be able to land on: (a) the grammatical / anti-propositional reading, where hinges aren't beliefs at all but something like rules or ways of going on, so they aren't in the truth business in the first place; (b) the entitlement reading — I've seen the word entitlement attached to this, I think Wright or Pritchard or both, I always mix them up — where they are beliefs, held without evidence, and rationally held anyway; (c) the reading where a hinge is just an ordinary empirical belief that happens to be extremely well supported, so Moore was more or less right and Wittgenstein's contribution is about how justification bottoms out; (d) the reading where hinges are relative to a framework or practice, and if a whole community ran on different ones there'd be no neutral court of appeal; (e) the reading where hinge commitment is the exercise of reliable cognitive competences, so the Moorean claim that I have hands is plain knowledge and the real point is that its support isn't propositional evidence. Add leaves I haven't thought of if the space allows them.
- Two axes I especially want split, because I think our group is conflating them and that's why nothing lands. First: is a hinge not a candidate for knowledge because of the job it does — an enabling condition on enquiry rather than a premise inside it — or because we lack grounds for it? Those feel different and we keep sliding between them. Second: when people say a hinge can't be doubted, do they mean psychologically can't, can't coherently (the doubt wouldn't even be intelligible), doesn't need to be justified, or isn't the kind of thing that could turn out false. Ideally the tree separates those four.
- At least three of your nodes should use cases that aren't the standard hands-and-sceptics stuff. The ones I want in there: a lab's assumption that its instruments were calibrated and nobody fabricated the data; a historian's or a court's assumption that testimony and documents are mostly reliable; whether a five year old who believes the earth is round because her parents told her so is exercising a hinge or just acquiring a belief on evidence; whether something a whole scientific practice once treated as fixed (phlogiston, spontaneous generation) was a hinge that later got replaced; and whether a dog has hinges. Swap any of them out if you've got a case that splits better.
- Each leaf gets the usual name (or "unnamed" if nobody really holds it) plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment.
- Flag any two leaves your tree can't actually separate, and be straight with me if two of them are the same position in different words. We spent an hour on whether (b) and (e) are really different views and got nowhere, so if they aren't, say it rather than inventing a node to pry them apart.
