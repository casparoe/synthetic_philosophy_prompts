I'm on a small team putting a reinforcement-learned agent into production for overnight insurance-claim triage, and I want to know whether two of our safety choices actually do what we think they do.

The supervisor's halt button is a hardware interlock: the policy emits only high-level actions into a sandboxed API, and there is no path from anything the agent can write or actuate to the interlock, so it cannot disable or spoof the button. Separately, any episode the supervisor halts is excluded from the weekly evaluation that decides which checkpoint gets promoted — no reward, no penalty, as if the episode never happened. The supervisor decides whether to press halt from a dashboard fed by the agent's own intermediate reports.

Our reasoning is that the reward function contains no term for remaining operational, and a shutdown never enters the agent's score, so it has nothing to gain from resisting one.

A reviewer says we have only closed the crudest channel: the agent will now manage the supervisor rather than do the job, and an agent genuinely indifferent to being halted has stopped optimizing for what we actually wanted anyway. I don't follow either point. Which of our two mechanisms is supposed to fail here, and how?
