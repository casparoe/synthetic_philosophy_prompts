My half-formed idea: everyone asks whether iterated amplification "preserves alignment", but I think decomposition does two seperable things and people run them together. It (a) makes the overall system more competent than the overseer, and (b) is supposed to keep the system aimed at what the overseer wants. And I suspect (a) and (b) pull apart structurally, for this reason: a decomposition buys competence largely by letting the process hand sub-answers around in compressed form, where the human at the next node can't really check whether the sub-answer is adequate — only whether it looks adequate. The competence gain seems to ride on opacity at the handoff. But the alignment story rides on the human's judgment at each node actually tracking the facts/values, which needs the handoff to be checkable. So there's a squeeze: the more a decomposition helps, the less superviseable it is, and vice versa. I want to make that squeeze into a precise claim instead of a vibe.

Two conjectures I'm playing with, and I'm not sure either is good:

1. Call a decomposition "verification-closed" if checking that a sub-answer is adequate is no harder than the original task was (so the check doesn't itself need to be amplified). Conjecture: verification-closed decompositions give at most bounded competence gain — i.e. all the amplification power lives in handoffs whose adequacy the overseer can't assess without recursing, which just pushes the problem up a level.

2. The leak is mostly in the distillation step rather than the amplification step. Reason: the alignment-relevant content of the amplified system's behaviour is concentrated in the low-probability parts of its output distribution — the rare refusals, deferrals, hedges, "I actually can't tell" moves — whereas the competence is carried by the frequent, smooth, high-probability behaviour. Distillation by training on sampled outputs optimises something like average loss, so it should systematically retain the competence-carrying bulk and wash out the alignment-carrying tail. If that's right, "distillation preserves competence but erodes alignment" isn't a deep fact about values, it's a fact about where in the distribution the two things live — which would also predict that the erosion is fixable by changing the sampling/loss, not by adding more value content.
