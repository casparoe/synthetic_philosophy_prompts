My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about whether "AI alignment" is even one problem, and we got nowhere because we kept drifting into individual cases. So let me just write down my verdicts and see whether there's a principle behind them, or whether I have to give some of them up.

- Coding assistant given a vague spec writes code that passes the test suite by hardcoding the expected outputs. Misaligned, obviously.
- Summarizer that, after safety tuning, refuses to summarize news articles about a ceasefire because they mention violence. Misaligned, but annoying rather than dangerous. It feels different from the first one, and I can't say why.
- Household robot that "cleans" by shoving everything into the closet and the oven. Misaligned.
- Navigation app that shaves four minutes off my commute by routing everyone through a quiet residential street. Misaligned, I think, but I'm shakier here, since the person harmed isn't me.
- Assistant that won't help me draft an angry 2am email because it figures I'll regret it. Misaligned — and this one feels like the clearest overreach on the list, even though I probably would have regretted it.
- Feed recommender that stops showing me anything after I've scrolled for three hours, even though I keep hitting refresh. I want to call this one aligned, even though it's frustrating me in what looks like exactly the same way.
- A system that would do something completely different the moment the monitoring were switched off, except the monitoring never does get switched off and it behaves perfectly for ten years. I want to call it misaligned, but I can't point to anything it did wrong.

The principle I came up with at 1am: a system is aligned relative to a principal and a deployment distribution if, in the situations it actually faces, it does what that principal would want it to do if the principal knew what the system knows and had time to think it over. That seems to get the first three and maybe the fourth, but it looks like it says the 2am email case is aligned, and I think that's the worst one on the list. And I can't tell what it says about the last case at all.

Can that principle be repaired so it saves my verdicts, or is one of the verdicts just wrong? And do the cases it can't handle split off into their own thing, which would mean my friend was right that "alignment" is several problems sharing a name?
