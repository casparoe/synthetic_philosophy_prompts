Say Foucault gets dropped into the present and is asked to consult for a made-up company: every employee wears a sensor band, and an app turns sleep, movement, voice tone and calendar data into a single "flourishing score" that's visible to the worker, to their teammates, and to HR. Nobody is ever punished, forbidden, or ordered to do anything — the app only sends gentle suggestions about your future self ("people with your patterns tend to be happier if they stop by 5"), and the workers' council voted it in almost unanimously, so it's voluntary in every sense that matters.

I keep getting the same reheated panopticon take on stuff like this and I want something better. Concretely: he liked to say power is productive rather than repressive, that it comes from below, and that resistance is internal to it — but here there's no prohibition, no guard, and nobody to disobey, so is there anything left for that account to grip, or does his whole picture quietly depend on there being something to transgress? Would he file this under the confession, under pastoral power, or under the late material on the techniques the self applies to itself, and would any of those make him friendlier to the system than people expect? Is the score best read as biopolitics — optimizing a population's health — or as something individualizing, or does that distinction just break down when the thing being managed is each person's relation to their own future? And what sort of person does this apparatus manufacture; what would he call the self that comes out the other end? I'd rather have an argument then a reading list, so no citations or titles please.
