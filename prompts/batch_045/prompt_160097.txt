i'm trying to work out what i actually mean by disciplinary power, as opposed to just 'someone getting you to do something', and my gut calls don't obviously add up. Case by case:

- prison timetable, guard on the wing, solitary for violations: discipline, obv
- a sovereign who punishes by public spectacle: not discipline
- piece-rate pay in a factory: not discipline, that's just a price on output
- a national census whose numbers set benefit rates: not discipline, it works on populations, not persons
- exams and tracking in a school: discipline
- diagnostic categories in a hospital: discipline, i think
- weekly confession to a therapist: discipline
- a sleep app that scores my nights, that only i ever see, that gates nothing: i still want to say yes. the number tells me what a good night is and i move my bedtime to chase it, so it feels like the panopticon with the guard removed
- opting into biometric monitoring at work for an insurance discount: no, that's a contract
- a credit score, computed by a firm, never reviewed by a human expert, nobody ever sits me down and corrects me: yes, and i'm more confident here than about the diagnostic categories
- a surgical checklist that standardises what the team does, and leaves a record: no

My rough rule has been: discipline = a durable record of an individual + a norm that ranks + an operation aimed at correcting capacity or the use of the body and its time. But i dont see how that delivers the sleep app (yes) and piece rates (no) at once, since both just act on self-interest with nobody watching, and it doesn't obviously explain the census verdict either.

Is there a cleaner principle that recovers most of these, which of my verdicts does it force me to drop, and what does it say about the one i keep flip-flopping on: a tutor system that builds a permanent model of each student, adapts itself to them, never grades and never compares them to anybody?
