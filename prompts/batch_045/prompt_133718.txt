I teach a mixed philosophy/CS seminar on AI and mind — 18 students, 80 minutes, one room, no tech needed beyond paper. I want them to run into the intentional stance rather than be told about it, so no lecture up front and they shouldn't hear the phrase until the debrief.

What I want: a game where they play repeated rounds against something hidden and have to predict its next move, and where their own belief-attributions to it become the data. Each round they write down a prediction and a one-line explanation of why it'll do that, and the explanation is forced into mental language ("it wants…", "it thinks I…"). Points, not money.

The bit I care most about, and the bit I haven't seen done: I want the reveal to produce predictive parity. When they find out what's actually behind the box, the best mentalistic description and the best mechanistic description should generate exactly the same predictions, so accuracy can't adjudicate between them and the instrumentalist test goes silent. Then I want a second measure I've started calling attributional inertia: they keep playing after the reveal, and we count how many of them can actually stop writing about the box in mental terms when they know every line of the mechanism. My hunch is most can't, and I want the activity built so that's visible and a bit uncomfortable rather than something I assert.

Also build in a beat where the comparison to a modern language model comes up on its own — does anything change if the box is a trained system rather than a hand-written rule, and if so what?

Give me, in this order: "What you need" (~100 words), "Rules and scoring" (~250), "Timeline" (~150, with minute marks), "Where the philosophy is" (~250, mapping specific moments in the game onto the stance/design-stance distinction and the question of whether the attributions are true or merely useful), "Debrief" (~300), "What will go wrong" (~200), and "What the activity can't show" (~120, be honest about the bit only I can supply in words). British English throughout, and flag anything that'll seem definately unfair to the CS half of the room.
