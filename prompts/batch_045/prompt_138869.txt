You're a philosopher of science who moonlights as a very dry comic writer — the sort of person who knows exactly where Popper's demarcation criterion buckles and also knows how a university committee memo is supposed to sound. Take a minute to brainstorm before you start, and please get the philosophy right: I want the people who know this literature to laugh, not to wince.

What I need is a satirical piece in document form. Roughly 650-750 words: a memorandum from the "Interim Committee on the Scientific Status of Historical Sciences" to all Heads of School, announcing that it has completed its review of the Department of Biology and has reclassified it. Deadpan civil-service voice throughout, set out as numbered clauses, with a short "Questions Members Have Asked" section at the end.

The set-up: the Committee has decided to apply a strict falsificationist demarcation criterion, and it accepts the assessment Popper gave of Darwinism in the 1970s — that the theory of natural selection is not a testable scientific theory but a metaphysical research programme: untestable as it stands, yet heuristically fruitful and able to spawn testable sub-hypotheses. One wrinkle the memo should concede in a footnote and then steamroll past: Popper later publicly withdrew that assessment himself, and the Committee has decided his retraction is not binding on it.

Where the comedy has to come from:

- The tautology worry, done properly. If "the fittest" just means the ones that actually survived, then "survival of the fittest" is trivially true. If fitness instead means expected reproductive success in a specified environment, the claim becomes testable — but only once somebody specifies that environment independently, and the Committee keeps rejecting the environments the biologists submit because they were reconstructed from the outcome they were meant to explain.
- A worked case the memo rules on: the claim that the pale throat-patch of the Caithness dune-wasp (a species I've invented for this) is an adaptation for signalling in the ancestral dune environment. Each environment the biologist proposes turns out to fit some other story about the same patch equally well, and the Committee keeps asking, in a deadpan way, what observation would have counted against the proposal.
- The downstream absurdities of the reclassification, played completely straight: which grant codes the department can still apply under, where the museum taxonomists get re-housed, whether natural selection can still appear in the school syllabus, what peer reviewers are now instructed to do with adaptationist papers, how the department's PhD theses should be catalogued.
- The Committee's own position quietly coming apart. It insists, at some length, that it is not saying Darwinism is false, or trivial, or that evolution didn't happen — and then cannot say what, on its own criterion, the actual harm is in continuing to teach it as science.

Two constraints: never have the Committee state the obvious objections to this Popperian line anywhere, and don't let it slide into winking or editorialising — the reader should feel the reductio without being told what it is. And no mention of creationism, religion, or Intelligent Design; that isn't the target. It should still be readable by someone who has never heard of Popper.

Start straight in with the memo itself — sender, recipient, subject line, first clause — with no preface, no summary of what you're about to do, and nothing added after the final FAQ question.
