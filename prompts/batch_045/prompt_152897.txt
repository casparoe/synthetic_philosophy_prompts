My flatmate and I had a blow-up last week and I keep turning it over. Background: I'm about three months into a self-imposed year of buying no new clothes, which I started after spending ages going on about fast fashion to anyone who'd listen. About two weeks ago I cracked and bought a jacket and just... didn't mention it to anyone. Then at dinner my flatmate was showing us a cheap Shein order and I went off on her, properly self-righteous, and she said "you're one to talk," and later "people in glass houses shouldn't throw stones."

I do think I was being a hypocrite. What I can't work out is whether that has anything to do with whether I was *right*, or whether the thing I did to her — blaming her, I guess — was somehow invalid, out of bounds, didn't "land." She also seemed to think it means I owe her an apology, and I don't see why my being a hypocrite gets *her* an apology.

So: can someone take "people in glass houses shouldn't throw stones" / "you're one to talk" and lay out the precise claims it could be making, from the trivial to the wild? Here are the things I keep conflating:

- whether it's a claim about the truth of what I said (that she did something wrong) vs. about my entitlement to say it vs. about whether my outburst even counted as blaming vs. about whether anyone should believe me
- what the hypocrisy is even supposed to consist in: that I did the same act, that I hold myself to a different standard, that I don't really care about the value I invoked, that I'm not willing to bear costs for it
- whether losing standing is global (I can't blame anyone for anything right now) or local (not her, not about this)
- whether it can be got back, and how: confessing, being punished, never doing it again, having already paid a cost — or whether the clock just runs out

Two cases I want every reading tested against, because they're what make me doubt the simple version:

1. Our other housemate has never once been remotely tempted by cheap clothes — buys nothing, wants nothing, thinks the whole thing is obvious. By the letter of the slogan she has perfect standing. But she has no idea what it's like to want the thing, and what I said at dinner came partly out of having failed at exactly this. It feels backwards that the person who never struggled gets to say it and I don't. Does any reading make standing depend on the struggle rather than the clean record, and is that insane?
2. Suppose I'd been caught the day after I bought the jacket and properly taken apart for it — jacket given away, everyone knew, I felt like an idiot. Would that have given me my standing back in time for dinner? Some readings seem to say yes (debt paid), others that it makes no difference, and I can't tell which is less stupid.

Also: does any of this change if, instead of blaming her to her face, I'd complained about her to our other housemate afterwards? Because that's maybe what actually happened, and it feels worse.

Give me the menu of readings with verdicts — which are platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, which are false or just confused — and tell me plainly which one my flatmate was probably invoking and whether it actually gets her the "you owe me an apology." Please commit: pick the reading you think is closest to what the slogan is really doing in ordinary use, give one definite answer on whether her objection worked, and say why. Don't lay out the options and leave me to choose. Flag anywhere you're resting on a contestable assumption about what blaming even is, and say how much of your answer would change if the other view of that were right.

Then, after the list, a JSON array, one object per reading, sorted best-first, with exactly these fields: reading_id ("R1", "R2", ...); claim (one sentence, the precise thesis); what_it_constrains (one of: "truth of the fault judgement", "entitlement to blame", "whether the act counts as blaming", "credibility of the speaker", "effects on the relationship"); verdict ("trivial" | "substantive" | "false" | "contentious"); strongest_objection; supports_comeback ("yes" | "no" | "partly"); owes_apology ("yes" | "no" | "for harshness only"); untested_virtue_case (one sentence on what it implies about housemate 2); paid_debt_case (one sentence on what it implies about case 2).
