hey — I'm in a senior seminar on moral emotions and I present my paper on thursday, and I'd rather walk in with the hard questions already answered.

My thesis in short: the psych literature treats guilt as the healthy one and shame as the toxic one, but that's only because it keeps measuring shame in its global form — "I am a bad person" — while the version worth defending is what I'm calling witness shame: being seen, by people who keep seeing you afterwards, as having done this specific thing. Two bits of machinery I lean on: the reintegration test (a moral emotion is healthier the more its natural ending is re-entry rather than exile) and what I call uptake debt — you can't install a moral emotion by policy or manifesto, because the emotion is trained by daily practices and those practices are slow and lumpy, so a culture "choosing" shame is really institutions making a bet about what ordinary life will feel like in a generation. Conclusion: shame can be the better moral emotion, and a culture can shift toward it, but only by redesigning who the audience is and what the audience does once it has seen you.
