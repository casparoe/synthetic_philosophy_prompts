im working on the strange-inversion story — Darwin and Turing both supposedly showing that the competences we explain by citing reasons can be produced by processes that have no grasp of any reasons — and before i read any more of it i want the spec rather than another tour of the idea.

Write me the desiderata for an account of how comprehension gets built out of processes that don't have any. Good enough means: given a system whose competence I didn't put there myself, the account lets me decide whether to say it understands what it's doing or only looks like it does. Thats the purpose, so judge the requirements against that.

My starter list, which i want you to cut as much as extend: it has to say what the difference is between acting for a reason and being caused to act in a way that merely tracks one; it has to handle the difference between a competence whose generating process had comprehension somewhere in it (someone designed the thing, or wrote the objective, or labelled the data) and one where nothing in the lineage understood anything; it has to handle the case where the system emits a correct rationale for what it does but the rationale comes from a part of it that has no influence on what it does; it has to handle the reverse mismatch, a system or person who can state the reasons perfectly while performing at chance; it has to say whether comprehension is graded and, if so, what fixes the threshold; and it must not quietly reinstall the understanding it was meant to explain away — no designer who sees the point, no inner explainer, no selection process that has to appreciate what its selecting for.

Give me the case or consideration behind each requirement, mark which are non-negotiable and which are just what one camp wants, add the ones ive missed, and then tell me two things: which pair of requirements you think cant actually be jointly satisfied, and which single requirement knocks out the most candidate accounts. Also: is wanting a threshold at all — a definite yes or no on does it understand — a real desideratum for my purpose, or me dragging in a prejudice from the ordinary other-minds debate? Commit to a line on all of this and defend it, including the part you're least sure of, instead of laying out the options for me to pick from.
