A friend and I argued late into last night about the four grades of mind — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian — and I came away unconvinced by him but unable to close the gap myself. His claim was that the Gregorian grade is not a grade of mind at all: take away what the culture hands a Gregorian creature — words, numerals, maps, diagrams, routines, everything that lets it think thoughts no member of its species could invent alone — and you are left with an ordinary Skinnerian creature, and a fairly slow one. All the interesting difference, he said, is in the niche and the props, not in the animal. My reply was that he is looking at the wrong seam. The gap he is exploiting is real, but the more interesting gap lies lower down, because the standard account of the Popperian grade runs two things together that ought to come apart: carrying a small-scale model of the world, and treating the model's verdict as a reason not to act. So I proposed an intermediate grade, which I call the Icarian grade: a creature that simulates candidate actions in an internal model and yet learns nothing from a simulation that fails, because nothing in it treats a crash in the model as a substitute for a crash in the world. It imagines the flight and then flies anyway. The Popperian innovation, on my view, is not simulation but the authority granted to the simulation.

Please write the piece that would settle this argument, or at least make both of us laugh at ourselves.

The deliverable: an internal management-consulting document — a Competency, Grading and Credit-Assignment Framework — circulated at an invented company that has reorganised its entire workforce by grade of mind. Roughly 800 words, in numbered clauses, in deadpan consultant prose. No winking, no footnotes explaining the jokes, no real philosophers or living people named, no invented scholarship.

What it has to do:

1. Get each grade right at the level a specialist would check. Darwinian staff are hard-wired: reliable, fast, untrainable, and no performance-improvement process applies to them, since improvement happens to a lineage, not to an individual. Skinnerian staff learn within a single lifetime, but only by actually incurring the consequences, so their training budget is denominated in real losses. Popperian staff pre-screen candidate actions internally — bad hypotheses die inside them instead of killing them — and must therefore never be sent on a costly field trial, because sending them is precisely what wastes their distinguishing competence. Gregorian staff acquire mind tools from the culture around them and thereby gain access to entire new planes of possible behaviour which they did not have to discover.

2. Make the engine of the satire credit assignment, not stupidity. The framework should contain a Mind-Tool Attribution Audit that steadily reassigns a Gregorian employee's output from the employee to the artifacts — language, notation, spreadsheets, procedures — until the artifacts are the competent parties: promoted, retained, awarded the equity, while the creature is reclassified as a hosting and maintenance cost. It should follow this through consistently, including the consequences for performance review (reviews must be conducted in the presence of the employee's mind tools, since without them there is no employee to review) and for offsite teambuilding events, at which the props are removed and Gregorian staff revert to something noticeably lower.

3. Recognise the Icarian grade as a salaried band, with a job architecture that makes clear why it is not Popperian: Icarian staff simulate beautifully, are still sent on field trials, and still have to be paid for in actual losses, because the model's verdict carries no authority for them.

4. Stay consistent from inside the position. The memo is itself composed entirely of mind tools — numbered clauses, decimals, the very idea of a competency framework — and its author must apply the audit to himself without breaking character and without escaping into the thought that the tools are mere aids and the competence is really his. That escape is the target. Include one forwarded dissenting comment from a Skinnerian-grade employee.

5. Be fair: exaggerate a real feature of the view rather than inventing one, so that someone who knows this literature recognises that you understand what is being sent up.

Finish, outside the memo, with a single final line in this form: a sentence naming which of the five grades the memo's author belongs to once the audit is applied to him, and what that implies about his authority to have written it, ending with \answer{<one of: Darwinian, Skinnerian, Icarian, Popperian, Gregorian>}.
