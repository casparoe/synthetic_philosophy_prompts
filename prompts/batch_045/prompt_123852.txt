Here's what I want them to walk into. Phase one: they should discover that they aggregate costs and benefits across *time within a single life* completely happily — sacrifice now for a much better later, obviously fine, no one bats an eye. Phase two: the structurally identical trade across *different* people, and I want the mechanic arranged so that someone actually has to be picked and actually bears something, and so that the group can feel the difference between "the total went up" and "this person has been answered." I've been toying with a rule where the chips are colour-coded per person and can't be traded, only spent on the person whose colour they are, and where anyone who ends up net-negative gets to stand up and state a complaint that has to be answered *to them* rather than to the ledger. Not sure that's the right device.

Then the part I really can't get to work, and which is the reason I want them to hit the thing rather than hear it: I want a later round where the "persons" aren't cleanly separate, so their resistance in phase two gets destabilised from the inside. Two variants I've been playing with, both of which I've only got half-built: (a) tell the group that two specified participants are, for the purposes of the exercise, sharing a single stream of consciousness for the session, and see whether the group now happily aggregates between them the way it refused to before — and then, the nasty bit, whether a good experience shared by those two counts once or twice when they tot up the ledger; (b) a case where one person's stream continues as *two* equal continuer-streams afterwards, so there's a genuine question whether the cost you impose is imposed on one person or two, or on one-and-a-bit. The point I want ambushed is that the force of the objection seems to track how *distinct* the subjects are, and distinctness looks like it comes in degrees — which is something I don't think any of them will have considered, and which the tidy version of the objection doesn't have room for.
