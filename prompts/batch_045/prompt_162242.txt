I teach a comparative ethics course to undergraduates who read no classical Chinese, and I want a sorting instrument for the early Confucian debate about human nature — Mencius and Xunzi, plus the rival theories Mencius's own interlocutors enumerate — that works as a quiz given before the readings rather than after them. Please write me a ten-question "which account of human nature do you actually hold?" quiz together with a scoring key.

Constraints on the questions:

- No question may name Mencius, Xunzi, Gaozi, or any text; no question may use transliterated Chinese; and no question may ask whether the student agrees with a label. Each question is instead a concrete case or claim someone with no background could answer: the bystander who feels an immediate alarm at a child in danger; the person whose spontaneous resentment at a slight is then talked into a calculated retaliation; the student who has to be pushed to finish work they say they enjoy; the person who becomes reliably generous only after years of drill and correction; the community that runs on generosity until a famine removes the surplus.
- Tag each question with one of three kinds, and give me at least two of each: a repertoire question (what is in the spontaneous, unlearned repertoire?), a trajectory question (left to itself, or given only ordinary cultivation, does that repertoire tend toward order or away from it?), and a source question (where does the authority of ritual and propriety come from — something already present, the artifice of the sages, or something else?).
- At least one question must be one on which Mencius and Xunzi would give the same verdict, so that a student who simply picks the anti-Mencius option every time gets mis-sorted.
- One question must be Xunzi's own regress worry in concrete form: if the raw repertoire contains nothing that reaches toward propriety, what did the first sages use to construct ritual, and why doesn't the capacity to construct it belong to the repertoire too? Pair it with the question Mencians press in return: if the beginnings are already in place, why do people ruin their own chances — through neglect, through failing to reflect, or through choosing against what they already had?

Constraints on the tree and key:

- Build the logical space first: state the branching questions and the combinations of answers they permit, then locate the positions inside that space — Mencius, on both the reading where the beginnings are incipient tendencies needing cultivation and the reading where they are fuller moral dispositions already in place; Xunzi, on both the thin reading where the nature-claim concerns only what is unlearned and the substantive reading where it is a psychological claim about innate self-regard; the view that the nature is indifferent to good and bad; the view that it can be made to go either way; and the view that some people are naturally good and others are not. Name each leaf, and write "unnamed" where nobody occupies a combination. (The scoring key, unlike the questions, may gloss the terms for nature and for artifice.)
- For each unoccupied or over-crowded combination, commit to a one-sentence diagnosis — incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored — and say which.
- Flag any pair of leaves the instrument cannot separate, and say what question, if any, would separate them.

My own hypothesis, which I want tested rather than flattered, is that the two thinkers are not really contradicting each other: one uses the nature-word for what is distinctive of us taken at its best end, the other for the spontaneous repertoire you have before any training, and nearly all the heat in the debate is a dispute about which of those deserves the word. Commit to a verdict: given the tree you have just built, is that hypothesis mostly right, mostly wrong, or does it dissolve a distinction the tree shows to be load-bearing? Give the reason.

Wherever you gloss a classical Chinese term, say explicitly whether you are confident or guessing, and resolve each guess the same way. Commit to one quiz and one key; do not offer me alternatives to choose between.
