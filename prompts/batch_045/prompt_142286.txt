hey — I'm putting together a short position paper for a formal epistemology workshop in the spring and I want someone to audit my commitments on updating before I commit to them in print. Here's roughly where ive landed:

(1) I'm a radical probabilist: the demands of rationality on degrees of belief are all synchronic coherence, and there's no extra diachronic norm over and above what that already implies.
(2) Jeffrey conditionalization is still the right rule for uncertain evidence: posterior P'(H) = Σᵢ P(H|Eᵢ)·qᵢ, where the Eᵢ partition the space and the qᵢ are the new probabilities I give the cells.
(3) What justifies (2) is conservatism — among all posteriors satisfying the new constraint, take the one closest to your prior, with distance measured as KL divergence.
(4) Rigidity, P'(H|Eᵢ) = P(H|Eᵢ), routinely fails in ordinary perceptual cases: the same experience can change how strongly you take the hypothesis to bear on the evidence, given what else you know, and I dont want to call that irrational.
(5) Path-independence is a genuine desideratum — whether the evidence comes in one step or two shouldn't affect where you end up.
(6) Much of the evidence I actually care about isn't new probabilities over a partition at all. A public-health report that pins the expected infected fraction of a population at 0.4, or an instrument reading that constrains an expectation rather than an event — I want one framework covering that too.

Does this hang together? Give me the tensions ranked by how bad they are, which pairs are only apparently in conflict, and for each real one say exactly what would have to give and what it'd cost me. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Please skip the survey of update rules — I know that literature and I know where I depart from it.
