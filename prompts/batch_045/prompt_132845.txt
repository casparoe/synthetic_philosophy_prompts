After forty years in observational astronomy I have lately been sitting in on a philosophy reading group, and I find that I am much less interested in the space of possible explanations than in what the members actually believe and why. So I would like a number, not a survey.

The proposition I want a credence on is this. Conditional on (i) interstellar travel and colonisation at a few percent of the speed of light being physically and economically feasible for a technological civilisation, (ii) at least a thousand such civilisations having arisen in the Milky Way at some point in its history, and (iii) the observational situation remaining as it is today, with no technosignature of any kind having been detected — I want your credence that the mechanism named by the dark forest hypothesis, the one that takes its name from Liu Cixin's 2008 novel, is the principal reason for the silence, rather than rarity, self-destruction, disinterest, cost, or anything else.

I want that number decomposed, with a probability and a single line of justification for each term, and then the arithmetic that combines them. The terms I currently have in mind, which you should correct or replace if any is ill-posed, are these.

1. Detection–strike coupling: given a civilisation capable of detecting a young technological species at interstellar range, the probability that it can also destroy or permanently disable that species at interstellar range, at a cost and risk it would accept.

2. The pre-emption term: given that coupling, the probability that striking first is what such a civilisation actually does — whether the target's growth rate, the impossibility of certifying benign intent, and the irreversibility of disclosure genuinely make waiting worse than striking, or whether concealment with monitoring, flight, or some cheap signal of harmlessness dominates.

3. The universality term: the probability that no civilisation in the sample ever deviates, whether by temperament, by indifference to the risk, or by being undetectable to whoever would punish it. If per-civilisation compliance is 0.999 and N is one thousand, joint compliance is about 0.37, which seems to me to impose a hard ceiling on the headline number unless I have framed the hypothesis wrongly. Tell me whether that ceiling is real, and how the number moves as N ranges from ten to a million.

4. The concealment term: the probability that a civilisation can suppress its own detectability at large scale for geological stretches of time. My worry is thermodynamic: energy use ends as waste heat, which must be radiated; beaming it away requires a sink colder than interstellar space; and the construction of any hiding infrastructure is itself an observable. If this term is low, the dark forest appears to collapse into the claim that nobody expands much at all, which I have stipulated away — so I want to know whether that is a tension internal to the hypothesis or merely an artefact of my stipulation.

5. The hot–cold term: whether the forest is maintained by actual strikes or only by a credible threat of them. A hot forest should leave evidence of strikes — systems disrupted on a detectable timescale, anomalous transients, engineering that ceases abruptly — whereas a cold forest predicts nothing beyond the silence we already have. Give your probability that the forest, if real, is cold, and say how much a hypothesis deserves to be discounted for predicting essentially only the observation that prompted it.

6. The observer-selection term: how much, and in which direction, our own situation should move the number. We are roughly a century into our own loud phase, still audible and apparently untouched. Under fast silencing I take it that we should expect to find ourselves early in the window, so our earliness may be no evidence at all; but I am not confident that the argument comes out that way, and I would like it stated properly.

Then three further things. First, identify the single term your number is most sensitive to, in the sense that halving or doubling it moves the headline figure most. Second, tell me where the disagreement between me and a younger member of the group most likely lives: my own credence is about 0.2, hers is about 0.65, and I suspect we are not disagreeing about the astrophysics at all. Third, say what would substantially shift this within fifty years — which observation or argument would move you most, and in which direction. I take it that one confirmed technosignature would be close to decisive against; I am more interested in what could move the number upward, and whether anything can, given term 5.

Please do not give me a literature survey or citations; those I can find myself. Work from assumptions stated explicitly, show the arithmetic, and flag anywhere you think I have built my own conclusion into the framing. Finally: I stipulated the feasibility of interstellar travel in order to close off the obvious rival explanation, so tell me how much of your conditional number is inherited from that stipulation by giving the unconditional credence as well, and treating the difference as a feasibility term in its own right.
