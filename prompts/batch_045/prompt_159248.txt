I'm a theoretical physicist, and the vocabulary I think in is that of effective field theory and the renormalization group: integrating out heavy degrees of freedom, relevant and irrelevant operators, running couplings, fixed points and universality classes, decoupling, UV completion, and the working autonomy of a description valid at one scale from whatever sits above it. Assume I am fluent in all of that; do not explain it back to me.

What I have only second-hand is the recent metaphysics of grounding and fundamentality. As it reaches me, the picture is roughly this: there is a distinctive constitutive dependence relation, not causal and not merely modal, that less fundamental facts bear to the more fundamental facts they obtain in virtue of; it is standardly taken to be asymmetric and transitive, and either to necessitate what it grounds or at least to be more than merely nomologically sufficient for it; there is a live question whether there is a fundamental level at all or whether dependence descends without termination; there is a further question whether fundamentality is absolute or only relative to a level, domain, or set of entities; and there is a standing suspicion, voiced from inside the literature as much as outside it, that grounding may be either hyperintensional entailment under a new label or an empty placeholder filled differently by each metaphysical theory that invokes it. If that summary is unfair, correct it before you do anything else.

What I want is a systematic translation. Restate the main positions in that literature, one at a time, in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can: the claim that there is a fundamental level; the claim that there is not; the claim that dependence is absolute versus level-relative; the claim that grounding is a genuine, well-defined relation versus a placeholder. Then give me the residue report.

To keep this from being impressionistic, here is the translation manual I have been sketching, which you should feel free to dismantle rather than humor.

1. Call a description fundamental when it sits at or near a fixed point of the flow. Call this fixed-point fundamentality, and motivate it as follows: what makes a level fundamental in practice is not that it is small or fine-grained but that it is stable under coarse-graining and thus a terminus of derivations rather than a way-station. Note that taken literally this makes fundamentality a property of both ends of the flow, and I want to know what, if anything, breaks the tie in favor of the UV end.
2. Translate A grounds B as: the A-description is a UV completion of the B-description and B is derivable from it, in the specific sense that B's parameters are fixed functions of A's under integrating out.
3. Translate the autonomy of a non-fundamental description as decoupling plus irrelevance of most operators: B can be stated, tested, and confirmed without knowledge of A, because B's dependence on A is channeled through a small number of relevant couplings.

Three places where I expect this to fail, and where I want you to tell me whether the failure is a limitation of my vocabulary or a sign that the metaphysical claim was doing less work than it appeared to.

First, modality. Decoupling says the low-energy theory is insensitive to most details of the high-energy theory; metaphysicians typically want grounding to necessitate, and they seem to want something stronger than nomological necessity. In my framework there is no distinction between metaphysical and nomological necessity once the flow is fixed: there is only one theory space, one flow, and the counterfactuals the flow supports. So is the modal component of grounding a genuine feature of the dependence relation that my vocabulary cannot express, or an accretion that survives only because nobody has testable cases of the dependence holding without it?

Second, non-uniqueness of grounds. Distinct and mutually incompatible UV theories can flow to the same IR fixed point; that is what a universality class is. If that is a faithful picture of dependence, then the same less fundamental fact has many distinct, incompatible grounds, and it is not obvious that anything in the metaphysics requires grounds to be unique. Does universality give me a concrete model of grounding pluralism, or does it show that I have translated the wrong relation and that what I am describing is multiple realizability, which the literature already handles separately?

Third, asymmetry. Grounding is supposed to run one way. But there are cases in my field where low-energy consistency requirements constrain the high-energy theory: anomaly cancellation, unitarity and positivity bounds, the way infrared behavior rules out candidate UV completions. If the IR constrains the UV nearly as much as the UV fixes the IR, is the asymmetry of grounding in trouble, or is my case merely one of mutual epistemic dependence where the constitutive dependence still runs one way? Say which, and say what would distinguish the two in my terms.

For each of the three, and for the manual as a whole, I want the three-way verdict: what translates cleanly, what translates only if distorted or supplemented, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Then give me your view on where the residue sits: with the physics vocabulary, which may be structurally incapable of expressing constitutive dependence as opposed to derivational and counterfactual dependence; or with the metaphysics, where some claims may be less determinate than the confidence of their proponents suggests. If the honest answer is that my manual translates none of it and that a different corner of physics is the closer analogue, say what that corner is.

On confidence: mark, item by item, how confident you are in each translation and in the diagnosis of each failure, and flag explicitly where you are reconstructing a metaphysical position from its generic characterization rather than from a specific formulation, and where you are unsure whether the position as I described it is one anyone actually holds. If the request itself rests on a confusion — if grounding and a hierarchy of scales are not the same kind of thing, or if the translation cannot be done well at all — say so plainly instead of producing a tidy translation anyway.
