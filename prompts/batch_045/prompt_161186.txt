I'm a journalist writing a piece on whether Dutch book arguments really establish norms on belief, as opposed to norms on betting behaviour, and I want one concrete case I can put to a philosopher in an interview: something I can describe in a paragraph and then ask, so is this person irrational, or not?

The specifications. The agent's actual credences must be probabilistically coherent and properly responsive to their evidence, and the agent must be a scrupulous expected-utility maximizer with a well-behaved utility function, since I want the disagreement to be about whether the book shows anything about belief rather than about the agent being bad at arithmetic or easy to fleece. There must nonetheless be a bookie who can extract from the agent a certain net loss in money, using only transactions the agent would accept by their own lights at the time they are offered: no misdescribed bets, no hidden terms, no informational advantage on the bookie's side, nothing exotic or science-fictional. The gap the bookie exploits has to come from the agent's own attitudes to risk, not from a transaction cost or a market-making fee, and the loss has to be one the agent would actually care about rather than a rounding error. Set it somewhere ordinary, a small business or a clinic or a family farm, and keep the scenario to a paragraph.

Then I need the argumentative part, with three verdicts coming apart in a fixed pattern: the classic Ramsey–de Finetti Dutch book argument says the agent's beliefs are defective; the accuracy-dominance argument, the strictly proper scoring rule version, says the credence function is above reproach; and the preferences-first reconstruction that measures utility before reading credences off gambles sides with the accuracy argument and clears the agent. Show the reasoning in each case. Then tell me, in a sentence apiece, which single structural feature of the scenario is doing the work, what happens to the book if that feature is removed, and what the strongest reply is from someone who wants to keep the Dutch book argument. I expect that reply to involve shrinking the stakes, and I want to know honestly whether it answers the case or only relocates it, given that I asked for a loss worth caring about. If some of my specifications cannot be met jointly, say which one has to give and why.
