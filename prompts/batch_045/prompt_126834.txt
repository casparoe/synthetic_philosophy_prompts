I sit on my employer's Deployment Safety Committee, which signs off on proposals to give a capable model real actions in a real environment. Our charter states that a measure has to keep whatever guarantee it claims even on the assumption that the model is actively trying to defeat it, so no one may argue from the model not being inclined to do a thing. Our record so far:

1. Approved: a narrow output channel — outputs must parse into a fixed schema, tool calls restricted to an allowlist, no network egress — with no attempt at all to read the model's intentions.
2. Rejected: putting the monitor's per-output risk score and written rationale into the model's own context so that it could revise a flagged output before release.
3. Approved: retrospective audit of reasoning traces by a smaller model we trust more, where the deployed model is never told the audit exists and the audit does not gate release.
4. Rejected: monitoring the deployed model with a fresh instance of the same model under a different system prompt.
5. Approved: tripwires — in a random 5% of tasks, plant a plausible but fake opportunity (an unguarded credential, a flag that looks like it disables logging) and count any move toward it as a detection, with the model told neither the rate nor the locations.
6. Rejected: letting the model keep a self-written scratchpad that persists across sessions, even though every write was logged and reviewed.

The new proposal: take copies of the deployed model (same weights, fresh context, sandboxed), ask each to produce an output that achieves a specified bad outcome while passing our current monitor, show each copy the monitor's pass/fail verdict and let it iterate up to twenty times, and add whatever defeats the monitor to the trusted monitor's training and evaluation set, patching over several rounds; red-team outputs never reach production, no weights or transcripts flow back to the deployed model, and it is not told what the hardened monitor looks for. The same proposal also promotes the monitor from retrospective flagging to being the gate an output must pass before it executes.

Some of us think this is the only way to make a weak monitor robust against a stronger model; others think a model that wanted to defeat the monitor would hold back its best attack, or leave a blind spot it intends to use later, and that we have let the untrusted system author part of the measure that constrains it.

Give me the decision that best coheres with our precedents, name the closest analog, tell me which principle we would be adopting with each choice if the precedents genuinely underdetermine it, and say whether some variant of the proposal (splitting the hardening from the promotion, or changing what the red team is rewarded for) would let us get the benefit without the commitment. Where our precedents leave it open, name the further case that would tell the competing rules apart. Finally, mark which of the considerations you rely on are near-consensus among people who actually work on this and which are live disputes, and do not present a disputed one as settled.
