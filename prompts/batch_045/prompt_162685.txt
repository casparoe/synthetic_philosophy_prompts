I have been defending a resolution-relative account of comparative value against the spectrum argument for the intransitivity of better than. The argument as I set it up: take a series of options A₁ through Aₙ in which each adjacent pair differs only by a very small loss in one respect (a slight decrement of achievement) compensated by a very small gain in another (a slight increment of comfort), so that A₁ is a life of great achievement and little comfort and Aₙ one of much comfort and little achievement. Each adjacent pair looks like a case of no worse than rather than worse than; no worse than is transitive; yet A₁ is clearly better than Aₙ. So either better than is intransitive, or one premise fails, or the logic of the argument is not classical. My response is to make betterness a three-place relation: x is better than y only relative to a standard of discrimination, transitivity holds within any fixed standard, and the spectrum argument equivocates, judging adjacent pairs at a coarse standard and the endpoints at a fine one.

Tell me what would count against this view. Which cases, which results about the semantics and logic of comparative adjectives, and which facts about choice behaviour would tell against it, ranked from fatal to merely awkward; what the view predicts that the standard rival responses do not; and which objections a defender should concede as real defeaters rather than dismiss as resting on a rival's assumptions. I care most about two exposures: whether the view is a genuine third option at all — whether the relativity parameter either does no work and collapses into the plain imprecision or vagueness reply, or does so much work that no standard can be privileged and it collapses into incomparability or into a merely pragmatic story about discrimination; and whether it is testable at all, given that any apparent counterexample can be blamed on an unannounced shift of standard. What I would find most persuasive is a case in which the standard is fixed by stipulation — a currency of comparison with a smallest unit announced in advance, all pairs ranked in a single session — and the cycle reappears anyway. Say also what the view commits itself to about cyclical choice and vulnerability to a money pump, since that is where it seems most exposed to evidence. If the view turns out to be insulated even against the fixed-standard case, say so plainly, and tell me whether that immunity is a defect in a position of this kind or simply what a correct account of comparative value should look like.
