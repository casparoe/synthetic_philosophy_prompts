I'm putting together handouts for my second-year distributive justice seminar and I want one piece of satire to sit next to the readings.

Write the annual impact report of a large foundation that has fully committed to prioritarianism. Title, a short from-the-desk opener, three or four programme entries with numbers, a closing note, and a brief technical appendix. 600-700 words. Voice: warm, upbeat philanthropy-speak — dashboards, cost per unit of priority-weighted welfare, a quote from a regional director.

The jokes have to come from the real shape of the view, so please respect the following:

- Priority attaches to absolute lifetime well-being, not to gaps between people. Nobody in the document should ever say they care about equality or about closing the gap — that language has to be visibly useless to them, and they should be faintly puzzled that donors keep using it.
- Their weighting function is w(u) = u^(1-ε)/(1-ε), currently set at ε = 2.5, on a dial: ε = 0 is plain utilitarianism, turn it up and you slide toward leximin. Have somebody turn the dial up over the course of the report, and let the reader see where the programmes go as it rises.
- Prioritarianism is still Paretian, so straight levelling down isn't available to them: every grant needs at least one person who gains. Make that constraint the engine of the comedy — the hunt for the smallest benefit to the worst-off person that licences the largest destruction of value for everyone else. Spend a billion, deliver a biscuit, that shape of thing.
- They're ex ante prioritarians, so a grant that in fact helped no one at all still gets reported as a success, because expected priority-weighted value was positive when they signed it.

Include one holdout on staff who keeps asking who actually recieved anything, dismissed in a footnote. Nobody should say levelling down objection out loud; the reader should just feel it. No real philosophers named, nothing cited. And dont make them villains — they're sincerely doing the maths. I'll be reading this aloud, so keep it tight.
