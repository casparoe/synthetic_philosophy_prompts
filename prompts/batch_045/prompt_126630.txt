hi — I'm putting together something short for a reading group I go to (we blitzed through the consciousness bit a few weeks ago and basically stalled on epiphenomenalism: the idea that qualitative states don't do any causal work, they're just along for the ride). The thing that got stuck in my head is that puzzle where, if the feel of a pain causes nothing, then my judgement "I am in pain right now" isn't caused by the pain — so either I don't really know my own experiences, or I'd emit exactly the same judgement with no experience there at all, and my introspective beliefs start to look like a lucky accident.

I've been kicking around a reply that I've never seen written down anywhere, which probably means it's rubbish, and I honestly can't tell whether it's a real solution or just relabelling the mystery. Call it *constitutional anchoring*. The thought is that a phenomenal judgement isn't a report that needs a causal channel running from the state it's about — it's more like an indexical self-location, on a par with "I am here" or "it is now". On its thinnest reading, "I'm in pain" means something like "the experience I'm undergoing right now is of this kind", and that judgement is partly *constituted* by the very experience it concerns, so it never needed to be caused by the quale in order to be knowledge — just as knowing where you are doesn't require your location to beam a signal at you. So the paradox only gets going if you smuggle in the assumption that all knowledge needs causal contact, which is a prejudice borrowed from perception.

Could you write me a dialogue (700–900 words, set wherever you like — I pictured two people killing time in a department common room after a seminar, or stuck on a delayed train) between two people who both know this material inside out: one an epiphenomenalist deploying constitutional anchoring to dissolve the paradox, the other convinced it fails? I want proper back-and-forth where they answer each other's actual points rather than trading past one another, and I want them evenly matched — no walkovers, though the anchoring side should be allowed one genuinely uncomfortable concession. A few things I'd like them to chew on: whether the analogy with "I am here" survives the fact that self-location is immune to error through misidentification while "this is a pain" has a classificatory component that can go wrong, and if the quale is inert then what fixes the classification is entirely sub-personal machinery, so the judgement may latch onto the physical property rather than the feel; a case where two physically identical judgers have their qualia swapped (or deleted) and, under anchoring, each judgement still comes out true of its own experience, which makes introspection look like it can't discriminate anything and so says nothing; whether anchored demonstrative judgement still earns you *that* there is an experience and *which* one even if it can't classify it; and, the bit where I think the whole thing comes apart — judgements made a moment later, or contrastive ones ("the second one was duller than the first"), where the anchoring has to reach across time and the experience isn't there to constitute anything anymore. If you think my move has a better name, rename it, and if you think that last objection is where it dies, say so in the dialogue rather than in a footnote. British English throughout please — and sorry if any of this is a bit garbled, I've only had one coffee and my notes are a mess.
