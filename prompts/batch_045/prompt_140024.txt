I keep going round on this with a friend from my old lab and I want a number out of you, not a tour of the literature.

Set up, so we're talking about the same case: colour phi. A red dot flashes briefly at position A, then around 100 ms later a green dot flashes at B. People report a single dot moving from A to B that changes colour partway along — and they locate the change at a moment before the green dot was actually presented. Two stories about that moment: the 'Orwellian' one says something was experienced there and the record of it was rewritten once the second flash arrived; the 'Stalinesque' one says nothing was experienced there, the content was settled after the second flash, and then the whole thing was served up as if it had happened earlier.

Here's my idea, which I haven't seen anyone actually run. At the moment the subject will later report as the moment of the colour change — i.e. before the second flash has been shown — flash a tiny chromatic probe at the midpoint of the apparent trajectory and measure detection (or discrimination) thresholds for it. The hope is that the probe reads out the visual system's chromatic state at that instant: if the system is already committed to green at t, the threshold should look different than if it's just carrying the first flash's red forward, and different again from the case where nothing chromatic has been settled at all. That means Orwellian and Stalinesque appear to predict different measurable things at t, which is more than the standard argument ever gets you.

So: what's your credence that the Orwellian-vs-Stalinesque question about this display is a genuinely answerable empirical question? Concretely, that some third-person measurement taken at or around time t would come out differently depending on which story is true, and that we'd be entitled to read any such difference as being about the timing and content of experience rather than about some other bit of the pipeline. Ground rules for what counts: you can have any measurement technology you like on the subject's brain and behaviour, but no time travel, no backwards causation, and no access to the subject's experience except through their reports and measurements. I'll also count the question as settled if the right verdict turns out to be that it has no determinate answer.
