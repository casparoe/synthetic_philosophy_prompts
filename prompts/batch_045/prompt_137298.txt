my friend (econ PhD, extremely confident) and I got into a three hour argument last night about whether economics can say anything about what people actually want, and he ended up laying out revealed preference theory as the mic drop. His version went roughly like this: preferences aren't hidden mental states you infer from behavior, they're just whatever the choices reveal, so 'utility' is nothing but a label for the choices — not happiness, not welfare, just bookkeeping. The axioms (WARP, SARP, GARP — the weak, strong and generalized axioms of revealed preference) are consistency requirements on choice, so if someone's behaviour violates them they're being irrational in the same way they would be if their preferences were intransitive. And since a utility function is only defined up to a positive affine transformation, you can rescale and shift it however you like, which is why interpersonal comparisons of utility are literally meaningless and cost-benefit analysis has to fall back on dollars. Then he said something I couldn't quite follow: that even when people's choices flip depending on what else is on the menu, you can just let utility take the menu as an extra argument, so the theory can't actually be refuted by that kind of data. He closed with: once you have a utility function that reproduces someone's choices there is nothing left to say, and anything about addiction, weakness of will, or being nudged is just moralizing dressed up as economics.

I pushed back on the menu thing — it feels like if you let the utility function depend on the menu in any way you like it stops predicting anything, so calling it a 'representation' is cheating — but I couldn't say why, and he accused me of being a psychologista and not understanding what a representation theorem is. Can you go through his summary claim by claim and tell me which bits are just the standard position stated plainly, which are him sliding something extra in, and whether his conclusions (that welfare talk is unscientific and that dollar metrics are therefore the only game in town) actually follow from the parts that are right?
