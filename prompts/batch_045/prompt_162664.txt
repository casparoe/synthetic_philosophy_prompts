I have been reading rationalist blogs and forum threads on Bayesian epistemology for something like fifteen years — the sequences, the old comment wars, and more recently a small forecasting league I help run, where participants routinely issue conditional forecasts (if the treaty is ratified, then the currency peg survives) and we argue about how to score them — and the same thing happens every time a thread turns to what it means to put a probability on an indicative conditional. Someone asserts that the probability of if A, B just is the conditional probability of B given A; someone else says that identity was refuted decades ago by Lewis's triviality arguments; a third person mentions Adams, or closest-world selection-function semantics, or the idea that conditional probabilities are primitive rather than derived; and the thread stalls at the level of brand names without anyone saying what a theory of the probability of a conditional is supposed to do. So before I read any more of the literature, I want the specification rather than the menu.

Write me the desiderata for a theory of the probability of an indicative conditional: what any adequate account has to deliver, each paired with the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a taste. I want the requirements to carry the weight here, not the scorecard, though two or three lines on the main candidate families at the end is welcome. In particular, say which of the things people routinely demand are genuine desiderata and which are the prejudices of one camp. Compositionality, for instance: a conditional occurring inside Boolean compounds and inside further conditionals, so the theory must make sense of the probability of if A then B, or if C then D — is that a requirement, or an artifact of assuming in advance that conditionals express propositions? Is a unified treatment of indicatives and counterfactuals required, or is that a preference a theory of indicatives may decline? Is it a desideratum that the probability of if A, B be defined when P(A) is zero or infinitesimal, or is leaving it undefined an acceptable cost, given that the near-miss cases (if the dam had held, if the launch had been scrubbed) are exactly the ones where people report the firmest probabilities? I suspect the deliberational cases matter and I almost never see them raised: when the antecedent describes an action the speaker is even now deciding whether to perform (if I were to press the button, if I pay the hundred dollars), conditional probability looks like the wrong object entirely, since an agent who has not yet settled on A has no stable unconditional P(A) to condition on, and yet agents hold firm credences in such conditionals and act on them. And I want a requirement — or a debunking of the apparent requirement — from the forecasting side: if a conditional forecast is scored only on those occasions where the antecedent is realized, is calibration over that subclass, or expected accuracy under a proper scoring rule, the standard an account of the probability of conditionals has to meet?

The part I most want, though, is the negative result. As I understand them, Lewis's triviality arguments show that a cluster of plausible-looking constraints — that identity with conditional probability, the conditional's being a proposition in the algebra, closure under Boolean operations, and non-collapse of the probability space into a handful of atoms — cannot all hold together. So I want the list to be honest about which subsets are jointly unsatisfiable, with the minimal inconsistent subset identified and the assumption doing the damage named: I would rather watch the desiderata turn into a theorem than have the list quietly presuppose that some escape hatch has already been taken. Relatedly, tell me whether the choice between conditioning and imaging as the operation of supposing A is itself a desideratum or a disguised decision about semantics. My understanding is that if you revise by Lewis's imaging rule, moving each world to its closest A-world, the resulting probability of B just is the probability of the Stalnaker conditional, which is one route to letting the probability of the conditional come apart from the conditional probability; if that is right, what does it cost elsewhere — imaging does not commute with ordinary conditioning, or does not compose the way updating should, or something in that vicinity, and I want the cost stated properly rather than gestured at. I also want your view on conditional bets: if every payoff a bet on the conditional could deliver is replicable by a bet on B conditional on A, does that drain the identity of content, or does the price of a bet on the conditional remain a quantity a theory is obliged to predict? Finally, which single requirement on your list excludes the largest number of live candidates, and which two do you expect defenders to quietly drop? Please argue from the cases, and do not give me a reading list or a set of references — I have no library access and want the reasoning, not the citations.
