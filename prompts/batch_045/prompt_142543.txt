My study group on Wittgenstein's later talk of language games and family resemblance produced the following argument, and I think it proves too much but cannot locate the mistake. Please name the premise or inference I should reject, explain why it looked acceptable, and say what rejecting it costs.

(1) Wittgenstein's discussion of games is meant to show that there is no single feature—being amusing, having rules, having winners, involving competition—that every game possesses. What unites games is a criss-crossing network of overlapping similarities.
(2) That phenomenon is not special to the word game. It occurs for any concept that is learned from examples and extended by analogy rather than introduced through an explicit definition.
(3) Almost all ordinary concepts are learned and extended in that way: language, language games, number, art, knowledge, punishment, democracy, perhaps even belief.
(4) If a concept is unified only by overlapping similarities, then it has no necessary condition: there is no feature F such that anything lacking F cannot be an instance of the concept.
(5) Therefore it has no necessary and sufficient conditions: there is no pair of conditions that is both necessary and sufficient for falling under the concept in every possible case.
(6) But many concepts we depend on—prime number, equilateral triangle, valid deductive argument, registered voter under a statute—do have necessary and sufficient conditions and sharp boundaries.
(7) Therefore the family-resemblance picture cannot be a general account of concepts; either it must be restricted to a few open-ended words such as game, or it forces us to deny that the concepts in (6) have sharp necessary-and-sufficient conditions.

If I have to choose, I would most like to keep both the claim that game, language, and number really are family-resemblance concepts and the claim that prime number, valid deductive argument, and registered voter really do have necessary and sufficient conditions; I do not want to resolve the tension by declaring one set of concepts unreal or unimportant. Which step should I reject, and what do I have to give up if I do? Finally, please say how confident you are in your diagnosis and single out the point you are least sure of.
