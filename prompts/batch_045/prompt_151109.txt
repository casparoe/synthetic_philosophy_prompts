i'm TAing a philosophy of mind seminar and our last session is on Dennett — the physical, design and intentional stances, plus that idea from his real patterns paper that a pattern counts as real when some description of the data is cheaper than the bit map, i.e. when it compresses. I want to close the term with one short poem, 14 lines-ish, and I'd like the line lengths to carry the philosophical work rather than the vocabulary. Pick one ordinary event — a chess engine pushing a pawn, my flatmate reaching for a glass, whatever — and give it three times: first under the physical stance in clunky over-long lines, then the design stance in visibly shorter ones, then the intentional stance in the shortest lines in the poem, so that climbing the stances literally looks like compression. The turn should be the moment the reader notices that the shortest description isn't the least true one: she wants the water isn't a lazy placeholder for the physics, it's the thing that makes prediction cheap. The last couplet should land that without picking a fight with either camp — real, but not made of extra stuff, and not merely in the eye of the beholder either, even though what counts as cheap is relative to a predictor with limited time. Anyone who actually knows the compression criterion should be able to tell which lines belong to which stance and why the poem gets shorter as it gets more predictive. poem first, handout second.
