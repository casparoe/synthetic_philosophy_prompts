I make my living playing mid-stakes cash poker, and I never went to university. A friend pressed a copy of The Prince and the Discourses on me last year, and I have been working through them on my own, with no one to talk to about them. What keeps happening is that I try to restate Machiavelli's account of fortune and virtù in the vocabulary I actually think in, and then I can't tell whether I have understood him or merely flattened him into something I already knew.

So I would like you to do that translation properly, and then tell me where it breaks.

The vocabulary I mean is the one I am fluent in from daily use: variance and edge, expected value, the difference between playing well and running well, sample size and the long run, bankroll and risk of ruin, game and table selection, reading a player pool, balanced play versus exploitative play, studying away from the table, moving up and down in stakes, taking shots, tilt and temperament, and the plain fact that the games get tougher over time and stop being beatable. Assume I am completely at home with all of it and that I will notice if you use it loosely.

Please restate, in those terms, the whole cluster of things he says: that fortune is the arbiter of roughly half of what human beings do and leaves the other half to us; that fortune is like a violent river which can be dammed and channelled when things are calm but sweeps everything away when it floods; that fortune favours the bold and the young because they are less cautious and more ferocious; that virtù is what meets occasion, and occasions do not wait; and, the claim I find hardest and most interesting, that the same way of proceeding makes one man successful and another ruined depending on whether it fits the times, while a man cannot really change his own nature to suit them, so that the same person who prospers under one set of circumstances must fail when they shift.

Then give me the residue report. Say which of those claims map cleanly onto my vocabulary, which translate only if you distort something on one side or the other, and which cannot be expressed in it at all. I am especially interested in the last group: is it missing from poker talk because poker is a narrower case than founding and maintaining a state, because my vocabulary is built around repeated trials while his cases are mostly one-offs, or because his distinction is doing less work than it appears to and is really two or three separable thoughts that he runs together? And where he says something that sounds, in my terms, simply false — that boldness as such is rewarded, say, or that a fixed style must eventually break — I want to know whether the falsity is an artefact of my translation or is already there in him.

One thing to avoid: do not quietly import my framework's assumptions and call the result a translation. If I am told that virtù just means playing with an edge, I lose whatever in his use of the word is theatrical, moral, or bound up with how a ruler is seen and how long his work outlasts him.

Please write in continuous prose — no headings, bullets, bold text, or tables — the way you would write to someone in an email. Open with a sentence or two giving your overall verdict on how well the translation succeeds, then work through it.
