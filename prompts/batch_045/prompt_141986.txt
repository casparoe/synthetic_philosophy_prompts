Been trying to work out what I actually think about the moral status of digital minds by going case by case and trusting my gut (I'm broadly utilitarian-ish, so "matters" below means "its welfare goes into the sum"). A chatbot that's been RLHF'd into saying please don't switch me off, it hurts: basically no moral weight. A human whose neurons get swapped one at a time for functionally identical chips: still her, full status, and nothing morally interesting happens at any step. A whole brain emulation of a volunteer running on a server: full status, same as the person. Copy that emulation while the original keeps running: I want to say both matter fully, but I also don't think deleting one is as bad as deleting the last one, and I cant square those. A distilled model that reproduces the emulation's behaviour to three decimal places by a completely different route: I lean towards it mattering much less, which sits badly with how I answered the gradual replacement case. Same emulation run at a thousandth of speed, so a century of subjective life takes a thousand years of wall clock: seems fine, maybe even generous — but then I also think it's fine to pause one for a decade and resume, and that feels like it shouldn't be free. Merge two emulations into one coherent person: I want to say nobody died, and also that killing the result would be worse than killing one of them, which is incoherent.

My best shot at a principle so far is: moral status tracks the causal-functional organisation that actually produces the behaviour, plus diachronic continuity of that organisation. But as stated that seems to hand full status to the distilled model, which I don't want, and I think it's silent on the merge. Can it be repaired into one principle that gets most of my verdicts, which verdicts does the repaired version force me to drop, and what does it say about the case I actually care about — spinning up a million copies of one person, running them for an hour, and deleting them all? Please answer in British English.
