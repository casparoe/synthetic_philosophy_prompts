I help run a fortnightly debate night at the public library, and the immigration argument is the one that never goes anywhere: one side says borders are violence, the other says a state with no border isn't a state, and after an hour we've just repeated ourselves. So I want to hand people something different — a one-page decision tree they can walk themselves through, roughly 9 branch questions and about 12 leaves, where every branch node is a concrete case or a claim you can say yes or no to without knowing where it leads. Nobody should get asked 'does the state have a right to exclude?' straight out; I want them sorted by their answers and only handed the label at the leaf. The commitment at the top of the tree should be one nearly everyone at the library shares — that turning away somebody who arrives announcing, truthfully, that they intend to burn down the parliament and shoot the people inside is not automatically wrong — and branch out from there.

A few things I specifically want in it, because I think the actual disagreement is hiding in distinctions we keep collapsing. First: refusing someone entry is not the same as admitting them on conditions, and neither is the same as choosing among applicants who are all admissable — so I want separate nodes for a state that charges a large entry fee, one that admits workers for five years with no vote and no access to benefits, and one that runs a points system or a lottery once it has decided how many to take. Second, I want the tree to force a view about whose right this is supposed to be: give me a node where a clear majority votes to admit and a single member objects that she simply doesn't want to associate with this person, and another where the objection is that the migrant will vote for policies that leave existing citizens worse off. Third, cases I want used: a family fleeing a famine that is nobody's fault; a doctor with a job offer; a retiree who'll buy a house and pay more in tax than they cost; ten million people arriving at once versus the same ten million spread over forty years; somebody whose family was driven off that exact stretch of land six generations ago; and a group that wants to buy an empty region, move in, and secede. I'd also like two ideas of my own built in as nodes even if they aren't real positions — the departure test, where a state's right to keep people out is discounted by whether it lets its own people leave, and the auction model, where the state has to admit anyone willing to pay whatever price would fully compensate current members for any loss.

At the leaves I want a name where there's a recognisable one — full open borders, but with the associative-freedom version and the priority-to-the-worst-off version as separate leaves, national self-determination, the welfare-state-as-club-good view, the remedial-only view where you may exclude threats and nobody else, outright sovereignty where any reason or no reason will do, plus the auction and secession-friendly variants — each with one sentence on the most awkward thing it is committed to, and 'unnamed' where nobody actually holds it. Two more requests. There are combinations of answers the tree permits that I suspect nobody occupies — for instance somebody who says the exclusion right is held severally by each member and that each member's consent is required, but who also thinks nearly every actual exclusion is wrong — and I want those cells flagged and told whether they're empty because incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. And I already know some pairs will collapse onto the same leaf: the libertarian association story and the national self-determination story seem to answer almost every case identically, and so do the two open-borders versions. Don't paper over that — flag the collisions and, where you can, say whether some further case would prise them apart or whether they're genuinely one leaf wearing different clothes. No reading lists or footnotes please; we can argue about who said what after the pub.
