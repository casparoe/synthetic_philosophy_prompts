I am drafting the trust-transfer section of the safety case for our recursive self-improvement workstream, and I would like to check one argument with you before it goes out to the reviewers.

The setup is the familiar one. An agent A_n reasons in a recursively axiomatized theory S_n extending Peano arithmetic. Before handing control to a successor A_{n+1}, it wants a proof — in S_n — that any action A_{n+1} takes will be one that A_{n+1} has itself certified as safe. The obstruction is Löb's theorem: if S proves the implication from Prov_S(φ) to φ, then S already proves φ, so no such system proves the soundness schema for itself, and an agent cannot prove a reflection principle for its own reasoning and use that as a licence to delegate. Since the theorem applies to any classical system extending PA, I have been treating this as a fully general limitation on self-trust — an agent cannot even certify an exact copy of itself — which would mean the only freedom left is architectural.

That suggests the fix I want your view on: make the strength descend. Fix a base theory B and let S_n be B together with the first N − n iterated consistency statements over B, for some large N. Since S_n is then exactly one consistency step above S_{n+1}, it proves Con(S_{n+1}), and a consistency proof is precisely what entitles a parent to trust a child's theorems: strictly speaking it delivers only Π_1-soundness, but the safety claims we actually care about — no irreversible outcome occurs within the first t steps of the successor's execution — are Π_1, so that restriction is notional. Each generation surrenders a single axiom, so the cost per generation is negligible; take N large, or start from a transfinitely iterated theory, and the chain of certified handoffs is as long as we like. In my draft I have therefore described the obstacle as follows: an agent cannot certify a successor more powerful than itself, and the descending construction is how one routes around it.

Two questions before I finalise the section. First, is that one-sentence formulation of the obstacle the right one, or is there a sharper standard statement I should be using? Second, is a single consistency step per generation genuinely sufficient for the certification step, or do I need to budget more strength than that — and if so, does the descending construction still yield an unbounded sequence of certified self-modifications, or does it break somewhere I have not noticed?

Many thanks in advance.
