I teach a 90-minute undergraduate seminar on norms and social order: 18 students, one room, no projector, and I would rather not lecture. Design a runnable simulation in which the class is divided into small "settlements" that must each decide how much rule-following to demand of their own members and then live with the consequences over several rounds.

Four things should be built into the mechanics rather than said afterwards. First, each settlement secretly receives an "ecology" card that changes what strictness is worth — a shared external threat, scarce resources, or rapid turnover of members, say — so that identical rules produce different outcomes and students discover that the question has no context-free answer. Second, build in an asymmetry between conduct the group can observe and conduct it cannot: enforcement should grow expensive and start producing visible-but-hollow compliance, so students feel the point where more rules buy less order. Third, allow migration, with members carrying an internalized disposition to comply or to improvise that the receiving settlement did not choose, so the costs of any regime fall hardest on those who never consented to it. Finally, I want them to hit this wall: a settlement that tries to be permissive about everything, including about how firmly it enforces its own permissiveness, cannot do so — the rules about the rules have to be held fixed by someone.

Give me materials, roles, a scoring system needing no real money, and a round-by-round timeline. Then a debrief mapping specific moments onto specific features of the tight/loose distinction — without using those words or naming the distinction until the debrief, so students meet the problem before the label. Tell me what will predictably go wrong when I run it and how to handle it, and be frank about what the simulation cannot reproduce, particularly anything that depends on norms being inherited rather than chosen.

Lead with a single sentence stating the core mechanism and what participants will have felt by the end; then give the design. No preamble and no restating of my request.
