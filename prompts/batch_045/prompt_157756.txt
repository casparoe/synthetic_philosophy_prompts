our reading group is doing a session on AI safety via debate — the setup where two systems take turns making short statements and a weaker human judge picks whichever one gave the most true, useful information — and the bit I can never get people to feel rather then nod at is the obfuscated arguments worry. Could you write me a sestina in the voice of the debater defending the wrong answer, where the six end-words are the load-bearing terms of its case (things like step, safe, bound, check, cost, true — your pick) and the rotation does the real work: each word means something slightly different every time it comes back, so the reader gets carried along while nothing is ever quite pinned down.

The point I want the form to make is that obfuscation isnt lying about any particular step — every individual spot-check passes — its making the chain long enough that a judge who can only verify a bounded number of steps never finds the one bad link, and quietly shifting the burden onto the honest side, which has to re-derive the whole thing while the liar only has to sound continuous. In the three-line envoi all six words should crowd back in at the moment the judge has to rule, and the last line should be the one place where the thing that cant be rotated away slips out — but leave it open whether the judge catches it. It should work for someone who's read the debate setup once, and still be a decent poem for someone who hasnt.
