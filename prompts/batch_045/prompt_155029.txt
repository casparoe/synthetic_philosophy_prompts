Taking a seminar on consciousness this term and the textbook keeps comeing back to one picture. Consciousness is like a theatre: a brightly lit stage, a spotlight that moves around, and out in the dark a big audience of specialist systems — face recognition, language, motor planning, memory — that can't talk to each other directly but all get to see whatever is on the stage. When something is conscious it's on the stage and the whole audience gets it at once; when it's unconscious it's happening backstage, in the dark, by one specialist working alone. The book says this is the global workspace picture, and that it explains what consciousness is *for*: integration, flexibility, being able to report something, being able to act on it in ways you couldn't if each module could only whisper to its neighbours.

Our TA used it for more than that. She said once you have the theatre you have consciousness full stop, and the thing people call the hard problem is just taking the metaphor too seriously — there's no extra ingredient, there's just the broadcast. Someone in the class said the opposite: that the theatre picture exactly as described is a picture of access, of a content being available to a lot of downstream systems, and that you could have the whole theatre running — stage, spotlight, audience, the lot — in something where there is nothing it is like to be it. Which would mean the metaphor is doing sneaky work: it looks like it's explaining the feeling part when all it's really explaining is the availability part.

I want the audit rather than a verdict. What maps onto what in the theatre version as I've given it, and where does the mapping fail? Two things bug me especially: a theatre needs somebody in the seats, so who or what is the audience supposed to be, and is that just a homunculus smuggled back in; and a real broadcast is public, whereas this one goes out to systems inside the same head, so the publicity that makes broadcasting useful in ordinary life seems to be missing here. Then: which of the disanalogies actually matter for the TA's conclusion and which are just decoration?

I also made up three cases, on the theory that if the analogy is any good it should have an answer for each. (1) Everything works, the stage is lit, but every member of the audience has been disconnected — the signal goes out and nothing downstream ever uses it, or could now use it. Still conscious? (2) The stage is lit but there's no spotlight anymore: everything is on stage at once, no bottleneck. Is that more consciousness, since more is being broadcast, or does the picture fall apart, given that the whole point of a workspace is that it's narrow? Some people claim experience overflows what you can report, and I can't tell whether this case is supposed to be that or something else. (3) There are two stages in the same head, each with its own spotlight and its own audience, and neither audience can see the other stage. One subject with a weird defect, or two subjects, and does the metaphor even have the resources to say?

Last thing: if the theatre can't carry the phenomenal half, is there a better analogy that can — a shared noticeboard, a headline on a newswire, a router, whatever — or is the honest answer that anything you build out of parts, signals and receivers is going to land on the access side and leave the other side out, so that no amount of fixing the metaphor gets you the TA's conclusion?
