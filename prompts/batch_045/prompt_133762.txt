Our reading group just did the "Ant Fugue" chapter, and one member kept leaning on this move: a colony is to an ant as a mind is to a neuron — ants are the neurons, pheromone trails are the synapses, the colony's foraging decisions are its thoughts — so Aunt Hillary really does have a mind, and she, not the individual worker, is the thing we should treat as a subject. They pushed it further: stepping on one ant is killing a cell, while wiping out the nest is a death. I think the analogy is doing more work than it can carry, but I cant say precisely where it gives.

Can you audit it? Give me the correspondence explicitly — what plays the role of a neuron, of a synapse, of a spike, of learning, of memory, of the body — and then where it breaks. The ones I keep circling: a neuron fires at a specific target, but an ant laying a trail is broadcasting to whoever wanders past, which sounds less like a brain than like a market or a crowded room; a neuron has no agenda and no offspring of its own, while an ant is a whole motivated animal with a brain of its own, so the picture builds a mind out of minds and I dont know whether that recursion is fatal or just fine; and the timescales are wildly off, since colony "decisions" take minutes to hours, so even if the computation comes out right there may be nothing there at the temporal grain experience seems to need.

Then two stress tests. Workers die and get replaced, so within a year or two basically every "neuron" in Aunt Hillary has turned over — a brain whose every neuron was swapped out would be a different brain or a dead one. What is the colony supposed to be if it survives that: a pattern, a process, a lineage? And if it's a pattern, does that still get you to a mind? Second: suppose we scatter all the ants across a continent but give each a radio, so the same signalling network is preserved — same information flow, no shared nest or body. Is that still Aunt Hillary? If yes, the analogy was never really about being an organism and I want to know what it was about instead; if no, then the "she has a mind" verdict was resting on her being a bounded animal all along and the neuron talk was decoration.

Where does that leave the two conclusions — that the colony thinks, and that the colony rather than the ant is the unit of concern? Tell me which of these disanalogies actually matter for those two uses and which are noise, and whether there's a better comparison (a market, the internet, a slime mould, the Ship of Theseus) that carries more or less of the load.
