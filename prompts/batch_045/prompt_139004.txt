I've spent about fifteen years reverse engineering binaries — malware triage, firmware, a lot of differential work against rebuilt targets. The vocabulary I actually think in is: stripped object code and symbol recovery, decompiler output that you can't trust, control-flow and call graphs, the optimizations a compiler applies that erase what the author wrote, equivalence classes of source programs that compile to the same bytes, dynamic analysis versus static analysis, provenance and chain of custody, and the standing distinction between what a program does and what its author meant it to do. I have no training in archaeology beyond one popular book, and a friend who does prehistoric survey work and enjoys arguing with me.

She keeps making a claim I initially thought was just underdetermination with a fancier name: that what's hard about her material isn't that the record is incomplete, but that a lot of what she wants to know — whether these people had a rule, an office, a god, a system of value — isn't the sort of thing objects can be evidence *of*, because for some of it there was never a separate thing for the objects to record. In her words, sometimes the deposits aren't the trace of an institution; they are the institution.

I'd like you to translate this dispute into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, and then tell me what didn't survive. Here are the three cases I want it done on, all from a hypothetical valley I'll make up so we don't argue about real sites: five centuries of continuous settlement, thousands of excavated features, good preservation.

1. Three hundred single-room structures, rebuilt in place over the whole sequence. Every doorway faces within a few degrees of the same compass bearing. The bearing is not the one that maximizes winter sun, does not track prevailing wind at each location, and does not follow the local slope. Candidate stories: a practical response to something about microclimate we haven't identified; a convention transmitted among builders, possibly long after anyone could say why; or a byproduct — everyone laid out walls by walking the same fixed procedure from a track that ran that way, so the bearing is an artifact of the construction technique rather than something anyone chose.

2. Pits cut into abandoned house floors, and separately, waterlogged ground three kilometers from any settlement. Both contain sets of near-identical cast metal axes: same few molds, edges never sharpened or used, hafts absent, always in groups of three, eight, or twelve, never singly, and never in the same deposit as food debris or human bone. Candidate stories: stored wealth or a standard of value by weight; a maker's stock of castings, unfinished or rejected; votive deposition; objects deliberately destroyed at the end of some cycle; or the refuse of a redistribution system.

3. In that same valley, across five centuries: not one burial. No cemeteries, no graves cut into floors, no cremated bone, no grave goods — while the next valley east has formal cemeteries from the same period. Candidate stories: the dead were disposed of in ways that leave nothing recoverable; the dead were taken elsewhere; mortuary treatment used no durable objects and no distinct features and so is invisible; or the survey has simply sampled the wrong parts of the valley.

Please do the following. First, restate the archaeological problem of inference in my vocabulary — as lossy compilation, symbol-stripped artifacts, non-unique sources, formation processes as transforms applied after the program stopped running, the absence of dynamic analysis, ethnoarchaeology as the equivalent of having the build system and a corpus of known-source binaries — and say, honestly, where the mapping is doing real work and where it is a metaphor wearing a work shirt. Then the residue report: for each of the three cases, what would someone who thinks only in my terms conclude, what in the archaeological dispute translates cleanly, what translates only after distortion, and what cannot be said in my vocabulary at all. I especially want to know about three things: whether "utilitarian versus symbolic" is a code/data distinction or a fake one my framework forces; whether the constant doorway bearing has any real analogue in a value that is constant only because a shared routine computes it and nobody chose it; and whether her claim that the deposits can *be* the institution, rather than a record of one, has a counterpart in reverse engineering — there is always a source level in my work, even when I can't reach it, so I suspect I have no way to express what she means.

Finally, the verdict: is the residue a defect in my vocabulary, evidence that she is claiming something substantive that I genuinely cannot represent, or a sign that her claim is less clear than it sounds? If you think it splits by case, say which way each case cuts. At the end of each section, mark how confident you are in what you've just said, and at the very end single out the one claim in your answer you are least sure of, with a note on what would change your mind.
