I keep getting lost in the Malcolm–Dennett fight over whether dreams are experiences, and I want the specification before I return to the positions. What I need is a theory of sleep mentation good enough for one job: telling a research programme in the science of consciousness whether a dream report is evidence about what the sleeper underwent, the way a waking report is, or only evidence about what happens at the moment of waking. Write me the desiderata such a theory must meet, with the case behind each. The cases I want covered: lucid dreamers signalling with prearranged eye movements from inside REM; sleep-talkers who narrate in the present tense; sleepwalkers and night-terror subjects who show fear and afterwards recall nothing; patients whose REM atonia fails and who act out what they later describe as a dream; apparent dream duration tracking the length of the REM period; dreams nobody recalls leaving measurable traces in morning mood and performance; REM in infants and in animals with no capacity for reports; and the dreamer who takes her dream for reality while in it. Two cases are mine: a sleeper who, cued across successive REM episodes in a single night, produces reports that continue one narrative; and a subject whose sleep shows every physiological and behavioural signature of dreaming but who never recalls anything. Say which distinctions the theory must respect and which it may collapse: undergoing something versus being disposed to report it; dreams being experiences at all versus experiences located during sleep; there being no fact of the matter versus our merely being unable to tell. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the prejudices of one camp, such as first-person authority, continuity with waking perception, demand for a neural signature, or the insistence that the question be settled empirically. Flag the pair of desiderata you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and the single requirement that excludes the most candidates. Then one sentence each on Malcolm, on Dennett, and on the default view that dreams simply are experiences occurring during REM. Tell me how confident you are overall and single out the claim you are least sure of. Write it as continuous prose, roughly the length of a considered email, with no headings, bullets, bold text or tables.
