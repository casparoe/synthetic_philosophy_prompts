I work as a lexicographer on a documentation project for a language with no written tradition, and one of my field habits has started to look philosophically loaded. When I record a word for a natural substance — a plant, a mineral, a kind of water — I note what the speaker pointed at, or what sample I had in hand, and I treat that as fixing what the word means. In this field the samples are rarely clean and the histories are rarely short, and I keep running into the following shape of case. I want to know whether it has a name, what the nearest established concepts are, and how, exactly, my cases differ from each of them.

First case. A word I have been recording, call it *mbula*, was, as best I can reconstruct, first applied by a guide to the contents of a spring that dried up two generations ago. Later analysis of the spring's sediment suggests its water was chemically unlike anything else in the region. Everyone who has used the word since has applied it to the ordinary local water, which they take to be the same stuff, and no one alive has ever encountered the spring. Does the word mean the spring's stuff, the ordinary stuff, or something covering both — and what should I write in the dictionary entry?

Second case. In a neighbouring community the wells have, over roughly forty years, come to draw mostly on a different aquifer whose water is chemically distinct, in a way that was invisible to the speakers at the time and is still invisible to them without instruments. Speakers say the same sentences, mean what they meant, and would produce the same description if asked to specify the reference without using the word. Is there a point at which the word starts meaning the new stuff? Is there a threshold, and if so is it sharp, vague, or simply not a fact of the matter?

Third case. In a third settlement the samples appear to have always been a fairly stable mixture of two chemically distinct liquids, with no fact about which the speakers encountered first, most often, or most saliently. Here I do not even know how to state the question. Is the meaning unsettled, is it settled on something disjunctive or vague, or does it get settled by some property of the liquids themselves rather than by the speakers' relations to them?

A variant that may or may not be the same phenomenon: a laboratory introduces a term for a compound, and it emerges a decade later that the original synthesis contained a rare impurity, while every subsequent sample has been pure.

What I have read about this general area — a popular chapter built around a thought experiment involving two planets with different liquids — seems to me to be about a different configuration: two communities, two environments, one speaker each. Mine is about a single community whose environment changes underneath it, about how much change is required before meaning changes, and about mixtures where no single sample dominates. So I would not be satisfied with being told that this is just that thought experiment again. Please tell me:

- Whether the pattern I have described — a question about the resolution, or grain size, of whatever relation fixes a word's meaning — has an established name, and whether my four cases are one phenomenon or several.
- Which established concepts fit each case best, and precisely where the fit breaks down. I would especially like the differences marked off from: ordinary vagueness, ordinary ambiguity, straightforward change of meaning over time, reference failure for empty terms, and the case where speakers defer to experts they cannot identify themselves.
- What the main families of view would say about my cases, argued out rather than merely labelled — in particular whether any of them has the resources to answer the threshold question in case two and the mixture question in case three, or whether they all leave it underdetermined.
- Whether the phenomenon has consequences for what speakers can know about the meanings of their own words, given that in my cases nothing changes inside the speakers at all.
- If nothing established fits, coin a term, define it, and give me its boundary cases: what would clearly count as an instance, what would clearly not, and what sits on the edge.

Two practical constraints. I am at a field station with no library access, so please argue the positions out instead of pointing me at papers; a single label or name that I could chase up in a year is welcome, but a list of things to read is useless to me. And please say how confident you are in each substantive thing you claim, on whatever scale you find natural, and single out the one point you are least sure of — particularly anything turning on whether this specific question has actually been addressed or whether you are extrapolating from neighbouring cases.
