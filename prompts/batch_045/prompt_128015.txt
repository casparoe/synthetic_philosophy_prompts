I could use a hand turning heterophenomenology into an actual protocol. I've been reading the rationalist blogs since forever, I basically already buy the Dennett line — that a science of consciousness should treat subjects' first-person reports as text to be interpreted rather than as authoritative verdicts about what experience is really like, that you build up the subject's heterophenomenological world out of what they're disposed to say, and that you don't settle the ontology by fiat — and now I have to make other people follow it. I'm the coordinator for the qualitative arm of a psilocybin trial for treatment-resistant depression: we run a ~40 minute semi-structured experience interview about 48 hours after each dosing session, transcripts get coded by two undergrad RAs, and I write the qualitative section. The PI wants a per-session yes/no on whether a mystical-type experience occured (the regulatory people apparently care), and I want to give her something that neither smuggles in a metaphysics nor, worse, manufactures the data.

The bit I keep arguing about with my friend who's into phenomenology is one I think the standard heterophenomenology story handles badly, and it's the part I most want the procedure to deal with: the elicitation isn't neutral. If I ask "did you feel a sense of unity or oneness", people say yes, and then I've created the very report I was going to treat as neutral third-person data — the interview is itself an intervention on the thing it measures. So I need the protocol to track the provenance of every report: whether the vocabulary came from the participant or from us (the consent form, the scale we administer afterwards, the culture, the therapist who sat with them), whether it was produced before or after we handed them any vocabulary at all, and what to do when the two come apart. I also need rules for the awkward cases: the participant who says words fail and they can't describe it; the one who reports nothing at day 2 and then at the three-month follow-up says it was the most real thing that's ever happened to them; and the one who is plainly performing what they think a mystical experience is supposed to sound like.

Could you write me a one-page-ish protocol, ordered steps, that me and the RAs could actually run — what to ask first and why, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure stops with a "no" (don't code it, flag it, drop it), and where it hands the decision to a person because it's a values call rather than a factual one? Plus the transcription and codebook rules that follow from it: what has to be preserved verbatim, what has to be tagged, what we're forbidden from cleaning up into a tidier idiom. And an honest section on where the whole thing misfires given our constraints, since we can't do ten hours of repeated interviewing per participant and the PI does need her dichotomous outcome. Then run it on two cases: (1) a participant who three minutes in, unprompted, says something like "the room and I were sort of the same thing", and then later, after we've administered the scale, agrees that yes, unity, oneness, all of that; (2) the day-2-nothing / three-month-everything participant. At least one of those should come out with an answer I won't like — I'd rather find out which one now, while I still have time to argue with the PI about it.
