The question:

Sleeping Beauty volunteers for an experiment. She is put to sleep on Sunday, a fair coin is tossed, and she is awakened on Monday if it lands heads and on both Monday and Tuesday if it lands tails, with her memory of each awakening erased before the next one. She knows the setup, and on waking she cannot tell what day it is. (a) State the "thirder" and "halfer" answers to the question what Beauty's credence should be that the coin landed heads, and reconstruct the strongest argument you can for each side. (b) On each of those two views, what should Beauty's credence be that it is Monday? Show how you get the number rather than just asserting it. (c) Someone in the seminar says the whole dispute is verbal: the coin is fair, everyone agrees on that, so both sides are right about their own question — one half for the coin, one third for the awakenings — and there is no genuine disagreement. Is that right? Explain why or why not.

The student's answer:

Sleeping Beauty is woken either once (Heads) or twice (Tails), with amnesia in between, and the question is what credence she should have in Heads when she wakes up not knowing the day.

Thirders say 1/3. The argument is essentially an indifference argument: there are three possible awakenings — Heads-and-Monday, Tails-and-Monday, and Tails-and-Tuesday — and since from the inside they are subjectively indistinguishable, Beauty should spread her credence equally over them, so each gets 1/3, and the one Heads awakening gets 1/3. You can also put it in terms of long-run frequency: repeat the experiment many times and only a third of Beauty's awakenings occur in Heads runs, so 1/3 is the credence that lines up with the frequencies.

Halfers say 1/2. Their argument is that Beauty already knew on Sunday that she would be awakened at least once whichever way the coin landed, so waking up tells her nothing she didn't already know and gives her no evidence that discriminates Heads from Tails; and the coin is fair, so its objective chance of heads is 1/2, and the Principal Principle says your credence should match a known chance. Halfers are not committed to denying the frequency point, either: the reference class that matters is runs of the experiment, half of which are Heads runs, so there is no conflict — they just don't think awakenings are the right unit to count.

(b) Both views should give the same answer here. Two of the three possible awakenings (Heads-and-Monday and Tails-and-Monday) are on a Monday, so Beauty's credence that it is Monday is 2/3 regardless of which side of the coin debate you take. The disagreement is only about the coin.

(c) I think the seminar objection is basically right. Everyone agrees the coin is fair and that its chance of landing heads is 1/2, and given that known chance the Principal Principle fixes rational credence at 1/2. So the halfer is answering "how likely is the coin to land heads" and getting it right. The thirder's 1/3 is not an answer to that question at all — it answers "if you sample an awakening at random, how likely is it to be a Heads awakening", which is a different question about a different thing. Once you specify which question is being asked both answers are correct and the disagreement dissolves; the only interesting thing is that Beauty's situation makes it very easy to slide between the two.
