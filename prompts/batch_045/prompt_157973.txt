Construct me a decision problem where one option has an undefined expected value in the Pasadena style — the positive and negative parts of the expectation each diverge, so the sum depends on what order you enumerate the outcomes in — and the other option is an unglamorous sure thing: a modest guaranteed gain, or a modest guaranteed loss. Constraints: no infinite utilities, no demons or perfect predictors, and every payoff in the schedule has to be a finite amount of money a person could in principle be handed. The gamble should terminate with probability 1, so nobody has to picture an actual infinite run of coin flips; the infinity lives in the tail of the payoff schedule, not in the story. The schedule itself should read like something a shady bookmaker with a very rich backer could write on a card — I want the undefinedness to fall out of the payoff rule, not to be stipulated, and I dont want Pasadena with the names filed off.

Second requirement: despite EV being undefined, there has to be an intuitively right answer about whether to take the gamble, and it has to come from somewhere else — first-order stochastic dominance, a truncation or cutoff argument, a repeat-play or bankroll consideration, whatever you think survives. And I want the case to make naive truncation look bad: the expectation of the game cut off at N should either oscillate as N grows or point the wrong way for every N anyone would actually bother computing.

Then do the walkthrough: what maximize-expected-value says here (i.e. nothing usable), what each fallback rule says and why, and be explicit about which structural feature of the schedule is doing the work — and show the case collapses into something boring if you remove that feature. Finish with the defense: what does an EV-maximizer say to make the case go away (bounded utility, an unimplementable schedule, the bookmaker shouldnt be modeled as able to pay), and if that defense works against your case, does it also dispose of the original Pasadena game, or does your version survive it?
