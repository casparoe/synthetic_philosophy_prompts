Write a joke that would land with someone who actually works on the metaphysics of persistence: the punchline should turn on the real content of the dispute between endurantism and perdurantism, not merely on the fact that philosophers worry about strange things.

Set the joke in an ordinary institutional setting where it practically matters whether a person is wholly present at each moment of their life or is present at a time only by having a temporal part located there — an insurance payout, a disputed will, a marriage, a gym membership, whatever you think works best.

One complication. I hold a minority variant of the perdurantist view that I call coarse-grained perdurantism: objects do have temporal parts, but the parts are extended stretches rather than instantaneous slices, and there is no privileged grain at which to divide a life, so the question how many temporal parts a person has admits of no unique answer. If you can make the joke work for coarse-grained perdurantism as well as for the standard version, do so; if not, use whichever version gives the better joke and add two or three sentences saying which you used and what the other would have required.

Keep it under 200 words, and aim for something an expert reader could genuinely enjoy rather than politely acknowledge.
