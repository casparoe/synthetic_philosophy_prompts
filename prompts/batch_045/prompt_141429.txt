I'm giving the maid of honor toast at my sister's wedding on Saturday and I'm kind of stuck, so I'm outsourcing it.

Background. My sister is a realist about neural network representations, like genuinely: the directions and features you pull out of a model's activations are things that are already there, we discover them, a good SAE feature is a real object in the world roughly the way a gene is. Her new husband thinks interpretability hands us tools that are useful for prediction and steering, and that's it: what we call a "feature" is an artifact of what we chose to decompose, how fine-grained we went, and what we decided to hold fixed, so saying the model "really represents" deception (or whatever) is us draping a story over a clustering that mostly tracks something boring like register or sentiment. This is not a bit, they argue about it constantly and they both love it.

The argument they were having the night they met, at a whiteboard at like 1am, was basically this, and they've been having it ever since: you train two models from different seeds on the same data, you find a direction in each that lights up on the same kind of text (say, text where someone sounds like they're covering something up), and you can swap one direction into the other model and behavior is preserved. She says: we found the same representation twice by different routes, so it isn't just our projection. He says: we built two things that play the same role in two different systems, and there's a whole continuous family of nearby directions that stitch just as well, so "same" is a word we get to define. Neither has moved in four years.

Here's why I'm stuck. About half the room are interpretability people who will hear every hedge and groan, and the other half are our relatives, who think AI is the thing that writes your work emails and who will start checking their phones the second I get technical. So the actual metaphysics has to be doing structural work in the toast — deciding what I can and can't say about these two — not just sprinkled on top. And every version I write secretly picks a side. "They looked at the same world and saw different things" is his view in a cardigan. "She finds things and he helps her make sense of them" is hers. I also can't take the pluralist way out: the groom's old advisor is at table four and his line is that representations are real relative to a choice of basis and granularity and there's nothing more to say, and they both think that's a cop out, so if I go there they'll both be quietly annoyed at their own wedding.

Two more things. I tried a version whose punchline is that a toast isn't in the business of describing anything, it's in the business of making something true — he thought that was great and she said that's exactly his problem, which I take to mean I can't use it either. And she has banned puns about features and about alignment, and they've both banned "maybe the truth is somewhere in the middle."

Five-ish minutes, affectionate rather than lecture-y, true to both of them in a way they'd both recognize, and definitely something the relatives can follow. Help?
