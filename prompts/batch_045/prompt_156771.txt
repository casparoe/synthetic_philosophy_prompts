our reading group is doing a session on the timing of experience — the one where the moving-dot cases come up — and I've been asked to open it with a poem instead of the usual handout so people actually wake up. I want a sestina, and I want the form doing the arguing rather than just decorating.

The case (getting it right matters more than naming it): a red dot is flashed at one place and goes off; a fraction of a second later a green dot is flashed some distance away. What people report is a single dot that travels across the gap and turns green somewhere in the middle of its journey — they report seeing the green before the green dot has ever been shown. So there are two ways it could have gone. On the first, the change really was experienced late, and afterwards the record of what was experienced got quietly edited so that it looks like it happened earlier: the rewrite is done to the archive, after the fact. On the second, there was no experience of the change at the early time at all — nothing there to edit — the whole thing was manufactured downstream once the second flash came in, the fix in from the start, the trial staged before there was anything to stage.

Why the two stories are stuck together: every way of testing between them has to go through a report, and the report is exactly what both stories say is unreliable. Which is why people conclude that the question — when exactly did that experience occur? — has no answer; or, if you prefer, has one we could never reach, and who is to say those are different claims.

What I want:

- thirty-nine lines: six stanzas of six, then a three-line envoy. Unrhymed is fine, no need for strict meter, but it has to read out loud without catching.
- the six end words are red, green, move, before, after, archive — rotating the usual way (last, first, fifth, second, fourth, third), with the envoy pulling all six back in, two to a line, one mid-line and one at the line end.
- let the rotation do the work: the same six items keep coming back in an order that wont sit still, and a reader shouldnt be able to tell from the poem whether the green was always there or got written in later. That undecidability is the content, not a puzzle with a solution, so dont tip it either way in the first five stanzas.
- the last stanza and the envoy are where the poem admits it cant settle it. Leave open whether there being no fact of the matter and there being a fact we cant get at are the same thing. Dont resolve it, dont wink about it.
- no labelled names: dont use Orwell, Stalin, phi, brain, neuron, consciousness, illusion, retrospective, revision, or any of the -isms. Get the content in by other means. Someone who knows the debate should be able to point at which passages are committed to which story and which are deliberately uncommitted; someone who doesnt should just have a poem about a dot that shouldnt have changed colour yet.
- no little room inside where things get shown to somebody. If there is a stage, a screen or a viewing gallery anywhere in the poem, youve broken it — the whole difficulty is that there isnt one.
- if you can make room: one stanza, and only one, as an image rather than a second subject, can use the same trick on the skin — a few taps running up the forearm, and the feeling of taps that were never struck arriving ahead of the ones that were.

After the poem, three or four lines of prose, outside it, telling me which stanzas are committed to which story and which lines are doing the keeping-it-open, so I can check you arent just hedging. Keep it short.
