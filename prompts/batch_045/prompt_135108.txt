so I've been stuck on this for weeks and I think the only way out is to write down my actual gut verdicts on a pile of cases and see whether anything clean covers them. The view I keep half-signing up for is the one people sometimes call lexical threshold negative utilitarianism: below some point on the pain scale, pleasures and pains just trade off against each other, but once a pain gets bad enough no quantity of pleasure can pay it off. I want to know if that's really what my verdicts add up to, and please write in British English.

Ground rules for the cases: the two options are the whole story, no knock-on effects, everyone starts from a neutral day, nobody deserves anything, and I'm asking which outcome is *better*, not what I'd do or who's to blame. By compensated I mean the package as a whole ends up no worse than the neutral baseline.

1. One person, a properly sore stubbed toe, 3/10 for ten minutes. Against: one person gets a really good slice of lemon drizzle cake. Cake wins, or it's a wash. Fine, plainly commensurable.
2. Same stubbed toe. Against: a thousand people each get a mildly pleasant warm feeling, the nice bit of taking off tight shoes. I'm fine with the trade.
3. One person, one hour of severe torture, 9/10. Against: any number at all of those slightly-warm feelings. Not fine. No number works. This is the case that makes me want a threshold in the first place.
4. Ten thousand people, one ten-second pinprick each. Against: a big pile of tiny pleasures. The pinpricks add up, and enough tiny pleasures outweigh them. So pain aggregates and sub-threshold stuff is commensurable in both directions.
5. Ten million pinpricks, against one person's hour of torture. The torture is worse, even though the total pain-time is massively bigger on the pinprick side. So there's a cliff somewhere between a pinprick and torture.
6. A billion people each with a real 4/10 stomach ache for an afternoon, against one person's hour of severe torture. I think the billion aches are worse. This is the one that breaks my own half-baked principle, because it says no amount of pain below the threshold can ever out-rank a single episode above it.
7. Same intensity, same rough total, different shape. One person with a 3/10 backache every waking hour for a month, against a thousand people each with a 3/10 backache for forty minutes. I think the smeared-out one is worse, and I think the spread one is compensable by a big pile of small pleasures while the continuous one isn't, at the same intensity. Call it the granularity thing: pain with gaps you recover in is a different beast from pain you never get a break from.
8. Same 4/10 injury twice. One person gets it mid-match and doesn't notice until the final whistle; the other gets it sitting in a dull waiting room with nothing else going on. The first one barely registers as a cost to me, the second matters a lot. Which makes me think whatever the threshold tracks, it isn't raw feel alone.
9. Same 6/10 burn, same amount of delight afterwards. One is a tattoo the person begged for and is thrilled by; the other is inflicted by a prankster and the delight goes to someone else entirely. The tattoo case is paid for, the prank case isn't.
10. Creating people. A life with one hour at 9/10 followed by eighty years of bliss: I wouldn't bring that person into existence. The same life with the hour at 5/10: I would. And if an *existing* person were offered the 9/10 package starting now, I'd tell them to take it and I'd say their life goes well.

My first pass at a principle: no amount of pleasure compensates for any single episode at or above the threshold T; below T everything is additive and commensurable, but the summed badness of all sub-T pain in a comparison can approach and never reach the badness of one episode at T. Which is definately weird — it puts an asymptote in — and case 6 is exactly where it feels absurd. So: is there a better version, maybe one that orders outcomes by worst episode first and only then by the net sub-threshold sum, and what does that version say about the case I keep flipping on:

11. World A: one person endures a million hours at 8/10, always just under the threshold. World B: one person endures ten seconds at 8.5/10, just over it, and nothing else. My gut says B is worse on Tuesday and A is worse on Thursday.

Also, if the exchange rate between pleasure and sub-threshold pain is finite rather than lexical, then some huge number of tiny pleasures must eventually beat the torture, and I don't want to say that. So tell me honestly which verdict has to go: my no-number-works in case 3, or the thought that one more slightly-warm feeling always makes things a bit better.

What I'd like back: two or three candidate principles stated precisely enough to apply, each clause tagged with the case that motivated it, an honest list of which of my verdicts each one can't accommodate, and verdicts on cases I haven't listed — non-human animals with a thinner sense of time, a being with full phenomenal pain but no memory at all, pains that are enjoyed in the moment like very hot curry or a horror film, and pains the person later endorses versus ones they later resent.
