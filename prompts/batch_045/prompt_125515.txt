I am a high school student in an introductory ethics class, and I need your help with a slogan my teacher used to end an argument. We were discussing whether it is ever permissible to tell a small lie to spare someone's feelings—for example, telling a friend that a deliberately awful poem they wrote is good, because they are already having a terrible week and the poem will never be published or graded. I argued that this is a harmless one-off exception: the ordinary rule against lying still does almost all of the work in our shared life, and my case is not the kind of case the rule exists to forbid. My teacher replied with the one-liner 'What if everybody did that?' and treated the discussion as closed. That feels too quick to me, but I cannot say exactly why.

I would like you to distinguish the distinct precise principles this one-liner might be expressing, and to arrange them from the least committal reading to the most far-reaching one. Please present them as a numbered list I can bring back to class. For each principle, tell me:

2. whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible moral principle, or actually false or confused;
3. whether it is a genuinely Kantian universalizability test, or instead a consequentialist, fairness-based, coordination-based, or purely rhetorical principle;
4. whether it would really vindicate my teacher's attempt to close off my harmless-exception example, or whether it looks decisive only because of an ambiguity.

In your list, please address the following complications I thought of, and use them to sharpen the distinctions:

- Scope of 'everybody': Does the slogan mean literally every human being, everyone in relevantly similar circumstances, everyone with my desires, everyone in my social role, or merely enough people to trigger some threshold effect? How does each choice change the argument?
- Act versus maxim: Is the slogan asking me to imagine universalizing the bare act of telling a small comforting lie, or the maxim or rule I am actually following? If the latter, how should I formulate my maxim, and why does that matter?
