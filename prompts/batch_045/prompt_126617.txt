In 1976–77 Canada claimed a 200-nautical-mile exclusive fishing zone off its Atlantic coast, pushed out most of the foreign distant-water fleets, and took sole charge of managing the northern cod stock off Newfoundland and Labrador. It then managed that stock under a total allowable catch set, at least nominally, from scientific surveys; harvests and processing capacity expanded as a matter of deliberate regional development policy; and in 1992 the federal minister of fisheries closed the fishery outright, with the spawning biomass down to roughly one per cent of its historic level. Tens of thousands of fishers and plant workers were idled, a large federal adjustment programme followed, and the stock has not recovered in the three decades since. The technology story I take as given: steel trawlers, sonar and electronic navigation, factory ships, and a fleet whose catching power grew far faster than anyone's estimate of what the stock could sustain.

I want this analysed through the lens of the tragedy of the commons and the public-goods framework that usually travels with it. I can recite the standard application myself — open access, each boat's catch is a private gain and a shared cost, rational expansion, ruin — so please don't spend the answer restating it. What I want is the following.

First, a disciplined mapping rather than a metaphor. Who, exactly, are the herders: inshore skippers, offshore trawler companies, processors, the provincial and federal governments, or the coastal communities whose members had no realistic exit? What is the rival good, and what is its exclusion structure after 1977 — was this a commons, common property with rules, or state property administered as open access? What did the actual option set look like for a skipper in 1985, and what were the relevant payoffs, including the option value of leaving the fish in the water?

Second, whether the case even has the logical form the theory ascribes to it. Hardin's pasture is a capacity problem: each added animal imposes a small, roughly continuous externality on a regenerating flow. A spawning biomass is a stock with recruitment that may be non-linear, and possibly threshold-governed, below which recovery does not simply reverse the decline. Does that difference in dynamical structure change the diagnosis, or only the speed at which the same ending arrives?

Third, what the framework misses. The resource here was not unowned: it sat under a single sovereign with coercive authority, a science budget, and a quota instrument, so Hardin's own remedy of mutual coercion mutually agreed upon was formally in place. That suggests the operative mechanism was not free riding among users but something else — concentrated and immediate costs of restraint on identifiable communities against diffuse and delayed benefits; an agency that both assessed the stock and promoted the industry; and genuine uncertainty about biomass that made any single year's cut look postponable. I would also like the public-goods half of the lens applied explicitly: which goods here were non-excludable and under-provided, and under-provided by whom — the fishers, the department, the electorate?

Fourth, my own candidate reading, which I want tested rather than confirmed: that this is not a commons tragedy at all but a race conducted under a regulator whose targets were set in a political process, and that the theory is doing little work beyond supplying a vivid word. Say whether that is a genuinely different model or the same one relabelled, and what each would have prescribed to someone making decisions in, say, 1985 with the information then available.

Finally, run the analysis back the other way. Does the collapse confirm the commons framework, is it an anomaly for it, or is it a case the framework was never designed to address? Is the label earning its keep in explanation, or does it name the mechanism so loosely that it conceals which mechanism actually operated? And be explicit about where the framework is silent — where it has no verdict to give — instead of forcing it to pronounce on the parts of the story it has no handle on.
