I'm a TA for the intro Chinese philosophy course and on thursday I've got to run a 50-min tutorial on the butterfly passage, the bit where Zhuang Zhou wakes up and says he doesnt know whether he's Zhou who dreamt he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming it's Zhou. About half the students read Descartes' First Meditation last term and half havent, and I can already tell half the room will go "oh, it's just the dream argument again" and switch off. So don't explain the passage to me, plan how I'd teach it. What has to be in place before the question "am I dreaming right now?" even bites for them, and why the move I keep wanting to make myself — we only ever correct dreams from the waking side, never the other way round, so waking wins by default — doesnt actually settle anything, given that from inside a dream the very same asymmetry seems to hold. Which one case should carry the whole session: I'm torn between lucid dreams, where you know you're dreaming while still in it, and false awakenings, where you seem to wake up inside the dream — and why not just one really vivid ordinary dream. The three misconceptions I expect: that this is Descartes in chinese costume, that it's relativism about truth, that it's really the hard problem of consciousness — and where in the sequence to head off each. Also whether the worry is about the external world at all, or about which one he is, since the "I" survives Descartes' version intact and seems not to survive this one. Plus a question to end on that tells me whether it landed, and one thing I should openly refuse to get into in 50 minutes.
