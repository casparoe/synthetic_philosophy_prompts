We're doing a little reading group on the physics of information — me, two physics grad students, and one philosophy guy who keeps derailing us — and we got stuck on something that I think is actually an analogy problem rather than a physics problem. Our text introduces Maxwell's demon and then more or less immediately pivots to Feynman's ratchet and pawl as the mechanical version of the same setup: paddle wheel sitting in a gas, ratchet that supposedly only lets the wheel turn one way, pawl held against the teeth by a spring. The point, as I took it, is that the thing fails because the pawl is itself at temperature T and jiggles, so it slips and lets the wheel run backwards about as often as it goes forwards, and if you try to fix that by cooling the pawl you've just built an ordinary heat engine between two reservoirs. The moral the book then draws is that no clever mechanism can rectify equilibrium fluctuations, and that the demon is that mechanism with some extra mystique bolted on.

One of the grad students went further and said the ratchet is basically a perfect picture of the demon, and that you can read the whole modern resolution off it: the pawl is the demon's memory, the jiggling is the thermal noise that corrupts the record, and whatever it costs to run the ratchet is the same thing as what it costs the demon, just described in different vocabulary. I don't buy it but I cant say exactly why. The modern story I've heard puts the cost in erasing the demon's memory — kT ln 2 per bit, Landauer and Bennett — and that doesn't look anything like a pawl slipping; it looks like a bookkeeping operation that doesn't care whether your parts are noisy. It also bugs me that people have actually run feedback-control versions of this in the lab, bead in an optical trap, measure where it is, shift the potential, net work out of a single bath, which sounds like exactly the rectification the ratchet argument says is impossible — except that the ratchet never had a memory in the first place, so maybe it was never in a position to forbid that.

So: can you audit the analogy? I'd like an actual correspondence table — what's supposed to play the demon, the gas, the trapdoor, the measurement, the record, the reset, in ratchet terms — and, just as useful, the places where the mapping has nothing to map onto, e.g. is there anything in the ratchet that corresponds to a stored result about which way a fluctuation happened? Then separate the disanalogies that matter from the ones that don't: is the ratchet's failure mode (noise in the mechanism) really the same failure mode as the erasure cost, or are these two independent arguments that happen to arrive at the same verdict? And does the ratchet's verdict actually survive if you take the information story seriously — can a device that keeps a record and then erases it do something the ratchet argument forbids, and if so is that a violation or just a relocation of where the entropy gets paid, and to what? Finally, what does the ratchet quietly smuggle in about the demon — that it's passive, autonomous, memoryless, at one temperature with everything else — and if it's a bad stand-in, is there a better everyday object to hang the demon on? My philosophy friend suggested a turnstile with a notebook next to it, but we couldnt make that concrete.
