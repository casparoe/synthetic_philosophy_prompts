Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two philosophers who have argued for years about the three stances — physical, design, and intentional — and about what it is for a pattern to be real.

Camp R (realist about patterns) holds that a pattern is real when some description of a system's behavior is cheaper than the bit map of that behavior under a non-gerrymandered coding scheme, and that where the intentional stance yields such a description, its belief and desire attributions are straightforwardly true rather than merely useful.

Camp I (instrumentalist about stances) holds that the stances are instruments distinguished by which assumptions they buy prediction with — law, presumed design plus optimality, presumed rationality — that pattern-hood is relative to a coding scheme and to our explanatory interests, and that intentional attributions are true only in an as-if sense.

Both accept a common scorecard. For any target and any stance, record: (i) accuracy on held-out situations; (ii) total description length, counting the stance's own attribution or assumptions plus the residual needed to recover the behavioral record; (iii) behavior under intervention, i.e. whether predictions degrade gracefully when components are modified. Assume throughout the usual no-traps and no-miracles conditions: the environment is not rigged against a stance's predictions, and the predictions' success is not luck.

They have agreed to score four stipulated targets. S1: the intentional stance beats both other stances on held-out accuracy and on total description length with attribution cost included, and interventions come out as the intentional account predicts. S2: the intentional stance beats the design stance on raw held-out accuracy, but loses once the description length of the belief and desire attribution is charged, because the desire set has to be re-specified for each new class of situation; the physical stance is far worse on both measures. S3: intentional predictions are accurate and cheap, but fail systematically whenever an experimenter alters a component, whereas design-stance predictions degrade gracefully; nothing in the environment is rigged against the intentional attributions. S4: under coding scheme C1 the best intentional description is cheaper than the best physical one, and under C2 — whose decoder is no longer and no more gerrymandered than C1's — the ordering reverses.

Write the document they should have produced. Begin with a numbered section of claims both can sign, including claims about what each stance is, what work the no-traps condition does, and what each of the four profiles does and does not establish. Follow it with a disagreements table whose columns are: the issue in neutral wording; the formulation of it each side would itself sign; whether the disagreement is genuine or merely verbal; the case, argument, or further finding both agree would bear on it; each side's prediction about that case, recorded in advance; and what each side would count as a reason to give up its own view. Add cruxes they have not yet thought of, especially any concerning whether intentional-stance success requires the target to be an optimizer rather than merely interpretable by us. Close with a short note on which residual disagreement you judge to be verbal and why, and on which single finding would most move the debate.

Throughout, keep separate what is settled in the literature from what is contested among people who work on these questions, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus. Flag explicitly where you are extrapolating beyond anything anyone has actually argued, in particular where your conclusions depend on the stipulated profiles above rather than on real results.
