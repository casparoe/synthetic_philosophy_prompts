A recent popular article summarizes Dennett's four kinds of minds in this way: Darwinian creatures have only the hard-wired behavioral dispositions that natural selection has built into them; Skinnerian creatures can additionally learn during their own lifetimes by actually trying actions out and being shaped by reinforcement; Popperian creatures can go a step further and test candidate actions in internal simulations, which lets failed hypotheses be eliminated without the creature having to suffer the real consequences, so learning by overt trial and error is no longer necessary for them; and Gregorian creatures acquire language and other cultural mind tools, which let them import hypotheses already tested by others and thereby bypass individual learning altogether. On that basis the article infers, first, that in a fully Gregorian creature the Darwinian, Skinnerian, and Popperian mechanisms have been superseded and do no genuine explanatory work, and second, that because the Popperian stage turns on private mental simulation, the whole taxonomy is behaviorally unfalsifiable, since one can never tell from the outside whether an animal is really imagining outcomes or merely associating stimuli, so the apparatus is little more than a metaphor. I am inclined to think the first inference is too quick and that the levels are meant to be cumulative rather than successive, but I am unsure about the second charge and about the claim that Popperian creatures no longer need real trial and error.
