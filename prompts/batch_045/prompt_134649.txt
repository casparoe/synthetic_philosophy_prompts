Jaspers' post-war distinction among criminal, political, moral and metaphysical guilt was drawn for a population that had lived through the crimes in question; I want to know what is left of it when the population has not. Here are my verdicts on cases.

1. A German born in 1976 is not guilty of anything, and calling her guilty is a mistake of some kind. Still, she owes a share of the reparations and a duty of remembrance that no non-German owes.
2. A Syrian who took German citizenship in 2016 owes the same share and the same remembrance, but not the shame. I am much more confident of the first half of that than the second.
3. A university that dissected people it enslaved owes their descendants repatriation and redress today, even though its members, its charter and its legal person have all changed since. A nation that conquered its neighbour eight centuries ago owes nothing for that conquest.
4. When a federal state dissolves, the duties it carried do not evaporate; they devolve on the successor states. I cannot say in what proportion, or why on them rather than on the federation's former trading partners.
5. A people that was itself wronged and then goes on to wrong another is more to blame, not less. I hold this at the same time as I deny that guilt can be transferred.
6. A beneficiary who knew nothing of the wrong and would have refused the benefit had she known still owes the benefit back.

The principle I keep reaching for distinguishes collectives that can act, and so can owe and be answerable, from collectives that merely formed the setting in which others acted. But it does not tell me which collectives are the same collective after a century of complete membership turnover, and as stated it pushes my verdicts in 1 and 3 in opposite directions.

Is there a single account of what a collective is, what makes it the same collective over time, and what it can owe, be blamed for, and feel shame about, that yields all six verdicts? If not, say which verdict you would overturn and what you would put in its place. Then apply whatever account you arrive at to the case I cannot settle: a country holding a truth commission two generations after a civil war, where the descendants of perpetrators and of victims have intermarried, now form a single electorate, and where many living people are, in one person, heir to the claim and heir to the debt.
