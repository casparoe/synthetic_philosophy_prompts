Here's where I'm stuck. Take A = a billion people all at welfare 100 (arbitrary scale) and Z = ten trillion people all at welfare 0.001, lives that are clearly worth living but with nothing much in them. Total-ish views say Z beats A. That's the repugnant conclusion, fine, I get it.

But the version I keep reconstucting in my head isn't Parfit's two-step (add a big block of worse-off-but-still-worthwhile lives, then level everyone down to the same middling level). It's a ladder, where you do both moves at once and tinyly. At each step: everyone already alive goes down by ε, and you add a huge block of new people at a level just above where the incumbents now are — so below where they just were. Repeat a few thousand times. Each individual step has (i) an incumbent loss of ε, which is below anything you'd call a real harm, (ii) new lives that are unambiguously worth living, at a standard of living only a hair under the old top, (iii) total welfare up by a lot. Run it long enough and you've walked from A to Z without any single step looking like the thing that made me recoil in the first place.

So:

1. Is the ladder the same argument in disguise, or has fusing mere addition with the levelling-down step changed what's being asked of me? My suspicion is that it launders the controversial premise by making each instance of it negligible — i.e. that this is really a sorites problem wearing population-ethics clothes — but I can't tell whether that makes it harder to resist or just moves the bump somewhere I'm not looking.

2. Which single premise would you actually give up, and what does giving it up cost you elsewhere? Average-looking views seem to escape and then immediately get the mirror-image absurdity: add someone whose life is plainly worth living and you've made the world worse, merely by dilution. The other move I've seen mentioned is dropping transitivity of better-than; but if I do that, what happens to the ladder, where every pairwise step looks fine and only the endpoint is horrible? Does intransitivity even help there, or does it just let me refuse to look at the endpoint?

3. Is the real problem aggregation (how to sum across separate lives at all) or the neutrality threshold (where a life starts counting as worth living)?
