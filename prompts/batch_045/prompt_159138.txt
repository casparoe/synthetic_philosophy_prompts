Dennett glosses Darwin's "strange inversion" and Turing's as aspects of a single discovery: competence without comprehension, with comprehension itself an emergent product of uncomprehending competence. I want to press on a distinction I have not seen drawn cleanly. Call comprehension *eliminated* when no stage of the explanation — the mechanism, its provenance, its embedding environment — needs anything to grasp why the competence works; call it *deferred* (or *borrowed*) when the operating system is comprehension-free but the comprehension has merely been pushed upstream, into a programmer, trainer or designer, so that deleting that source makes the competence disappear.

My suspicion is that the two inversions are not on a par here: Darwin eliminates, since differential reproduction tracks fitness without anything having to understand what an adaptation is for, whereas Turing, at least in its textbook form, only defers — the machine runs mindlessly, but the comprehension sits in whoever wrote the program.

Please explain whether this asymmetry is real, and if so what turns on it. Two things especially: is a comprehension-requiring source of the program a skyhook, making the Turing case a genuine inversion only when the program's provenance is itself comprehension-free — in which case it collapses into an instance of the Darwinian one? And is there any reading on which selection itself smuggles comprehension back in?

Open with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble and no restatement of my question.
