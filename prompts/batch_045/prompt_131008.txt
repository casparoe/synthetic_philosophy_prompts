I got dragged into a philosophy of science reading group by a friend (I'm a molecular biologist, not a philosopher) and we've ended up in an argument I can't get out of my head about Lakatos. I've ended up at a fairly hard position: the methodology of scientific research programmes looks to me like it's unfalsifiable as a methodology, and in exactly the way that Lakatos accused everyone else of being useless. Three reasons. First, appraisal only ever attaches to a sequence of theories over time, never to any single theory, so there's no moment at which a programme can be caught out. Second, the time-lag move means no run of failed predictions ever definitively marks a programme as degenerating, because the defender can always ask for more time; it's never the right time to pronounce. Third, because the hard core is immunised by stipulation, every empirical misfire gets shunted into the protective belt, so no observation ever functions as a verdict. Put those together and MSRP can't tell a working scientist what to do on Monday morning, and it can't separate science from pseudo-science while it's happening, which was supposed to be its whole advantage over both Popper's falsificationism and Kuhn's paradigms. My friend's reply is that I've collapsed two different distinctions. She wants to separate a local reading of the progressive/degenerating judgement, meaning a programme's own track record of turning anomalies into confirmed novel facts inside an explicitly fixed window, from a comparative reading, meaning whether one programme is actually superseding another in the same domain. She says the hard core and protective belt machinery, and the tolerance for temporary degeneration, only really earn their keep for the comparative question, and that once you commit to a window in advance the local degenerating verdict becomes a claim that can be wrong. I think that's just the time-lag move smuggled back in with a number stapled to it, but she's made me less certain than I was.

What I want is the case against me, and I want it as a map of exposure rather than a summary of the debate. What would have to be true for my position to be wrong? Which historical episodes, which kinds of evidence, which formal or structural results should move me, and which would merely be awkward? I'm especially interested in whether there is any episode where a recognisably Lakatosian appraisal, made in real time and not retrospectively, got the fate of a programme right before the outcome was obvious: I can't think of one, but I'm not a historian and I'd rather be told there aren't any than be given a fake one. I've read the usual Feyerabend line about anything goes and Laudan's complaints about appraisal being impossible in the present tense, and neither really moves me, so either tell me why those really are the whole story or go past them. I'd also like you to sort the defeaters by what a card-carrying Lakatosian would themselves concede counts against them versus what they'd dismiss as a misunderstanding of the framework, and rank them from fatal to merely embarrassing.

Two more things. There's a research programme in my own field that hasn't produced a genuinely novel predictive success in about fifteen years, but the people in it are doing technically gorgeous work on puzzles the programme generated for itself rather than puzzles the data handed them; my instinct is that calling it degenerating is precisely the kind of retrospective pronouncement I'm objecting to, and my friend says no, with a pre-registered window the verdict is earned. Run that case through both of our views and tell me whose verdict I should trust, or whether the framework just declines to answer. And the thing that would move me most personally is a concrete account of what a scientist could do differently on the strength of a Lakatosian appraisal that they couldn't do on the strength of just tracking the evidence: if there is such a thing, my position is in trouble, and if there isn't, I want to know whether my position has quietly been qualified into unfalsifiability and whether that's a problem for a view of this kind.
