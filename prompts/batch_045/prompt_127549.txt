My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about the work-conditionality regime attached to means-tested unemployment benefits, and neither of us could say what it actually commits anyone to believing. So let me describe the practice and ask you to reconstruct its theory.

As I understand it: to keep the benefit, a claimant must log a fixed number of job-search activities each week and must accept any job the agency counts as suitable; in a number of systems the suitability bar falls the longer someone has been claiming, so obligations tighten as the odds of finding work lengthen. Compliance is measured by the activity logged, not by whether any application could realistically have succeeded. A claimant can also be required to work unpaid for several weeks at a placement, often for a private employer who keeps the output. That placement counts as work when it comes to what the claimant owes, but not when it comes to the minimum wage, employment rights, or a pay slip. Non-compliance is met by suspending payment for a fixed period, in some systems with only discretionary hardship relief. Meanwhile the same state attaches none of this to pensions, child benefits, or mortgage-interest relief, and nothing at all to a person of independent means who has never worked. My friend says the whole thing is desert-talk about the undeserving poor written into administrative rules; I say a state that blamed nobody could rationally run something very similar, for screening and for incentives.

Three things, please. First, reconstruct the implicit view of where the obligation comes from: receipt of collectively funded transfers, the fact of being able to work and not working, or the need to sort the genuinely jobless from the work-shy under imperfect information. Second, say which commitments are strictly presupposed and which are merely comfortable with the practice: could a purely incentive- or screening-based justification with no claim about desert generate the same rules, does exempting pensioners and the idle rich show that reciprocity is not really what is doing the work, and is it coherent to treat one and the same placement as work when it grounds a duty and as not-work when a wage is at stake? Third, tell me what would have to change in the practice if each rival view you identify were substituted for the one it actually rests on.

I do not want reading lists, citations, or surveys of the literature; I want the argument. Close with a single last line in the form \answer{...}, containing a phrase of no more than ten words naming the one commitment the practice cannot give up.
