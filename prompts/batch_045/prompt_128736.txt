Hard no on "a game theorist walks into a bar" (done to death) and hard no on the "two economists walk past a twenty dollar bill" family of bits. Also, about half the room will be people who wandered in for the free wine and have never heard of a belief hierarchy, so it can't defintelately require a whiteboard.

Here's the stuff I want the joke to actually ride on. The seminar spent two weeks on the Aumann–Brandenburger style results: in a two-player game, something relatively weak — mutual belief in rationality plus each player knowing what the other will do — is already enough to get you a Nash equilibrium, but once you've got three or more players the epistemic story needs something much stronger, roughly common belief in rationality plus everyone sharing a single conjecture about what the others are doing, or the whole derivation just stops working. We also did the "almost common knowledge" thing: two generals trying to coordinate an attack by swapping confirmation messages, where no finite number of "got it, do you got it, do you got that I got it" messages ever gets you real common knowledge, and the attackers never attack no matter how long the chain gets. Someone in the room said the moral is that common knowledge isn't the limit of finite knowledge, its a different animal entirely.

And here's the character I want in it, I made up a name for him and I'm attached to it: penultimate-order belief failure. That's the guy who will grant you "you're rational," grant you "you believe I'm rational," grant you "you believe that I believe that you're rational," and keep climbing cheerfully — and then, at the exact step where the iteration would finally pay off, he stops one level short and does something catastrophically dumb. Not because he doubts anyone's rationality. He just runs out of steam right before the finish line. I think this is the funniest idea in the whole literature and nobody has done anything with it.
