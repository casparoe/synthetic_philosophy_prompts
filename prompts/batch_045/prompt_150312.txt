My half-formed idea: the familiar argument that a generous welfare state requires bounded membership treats membership as binary and instantaneous — you are either in or out, and once you are in you are in all the way. I want to unbundle it. Residence, labour-market access, contributory insurance (unemployment, pensions), and non-contributory transfers could each vest at a different point, the way employee benefits vest on a schedule. Once timing is a policy variable, the standard positions look mis-specified: because a migrant's lifecycle fiscal balance is driven mainly by age at arrival, open entry combined with late vesting of non-contributory transfers may be fiscally indistinguishable from a closed, points-based regime while being morally unrecognisable to both sides — which suggests either that the fiscal objection to open borders was never really about numbers, or that openness has been defended on the wrong axis. One complication to keep in view: a long probationary tier may itself undermine integration, so the result may be a real trade-off between fiscal defensibility and the second-class-status objection rather than a free lunch.
