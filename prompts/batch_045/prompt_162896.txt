I'm a journalist who writes about psychology and the science of the self, and I'm assembling a magazine feature on the narrativist account of personal identity — the view that a person is not an extra thing lodged inside a human being but something more like the center of gravity of the stories told by and about that human being. For the feature I want one satirical insert: a mock university press release, headline and dateline plus roughly 600 words, announcing the results of a randomized controlled trial of a fictional clinical intervention called gravitational re-centering, which promises to diagnose, restore, or strengthen a patient's center of narrative gravity. Add a sidebar of about 150 words containing the abstract of the trial, written in the house style of a journal of your own invention, with a methods section, a primary endpoint, and numbers.

The jokes should come from getting the view right, so that someone who actually works on narrative self-constitution laughs rather than winces. Without ever stating them outright, the piece should land: the worry that the narrative needs an author, and that the author starts to look like the very homunculus the view was meant to eliminate; the problem of confabulated or simply false narratives, where a self built out of a story that never happened seems to work perfectly well; the fact that other people's stories about you appear to be partly load-bearing; the marginal cases in which the narrative is very thin; and the awkwardness that a center of gravity is a mathematical abstraction with a determinate location, whereas the narrative center is whatever the measurement procedure says it is. Some of the best material follows from the metaphor itself: a body's center of gravity can lie outside the body, it shifts when mass is redistributed, and it does no predictive work unless you were already doing the calculation. Please use at least one of those.

Keep the voice deadpan throughout, as if the news office believed every word: no winking, no nudging, nothing that announces the absurdity, but a general reader should still be able to tell from the content alone what is being mocked. Include at least one quote from an invented outside skeptic whose objection is to the physics rather than to the ethics — a gravitational physicist, a biomechanist, or a representative of a memoir ghostwriters' union would each do. Let the reductio arrive through the market: the intervention is already being sold to people who were never unwell, and one of the trial's own outcome measures quietly presupposes that the treatment succeeded.

Give me the headline, the release, and the sidebar only — no preface and no closing note explaining what you were satirizing.
