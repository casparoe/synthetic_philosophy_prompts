I'm on a tiny grant committee that's trying to put an actual number on how much insect suffering matters next to, say, shrimp, and we've been going in circles for months. So: no survey of the literature, no it-depends-on-your-theory-of-consciousness. I want a number with the insides showing.

The proposition: an adult worker honeybee — an ordinary one, alive in a hive right now, brain of roughly a million neurons — has some nonzero degree of phenomenal experience. Not consciousness like mine, not enough to outweigh a chicken. Just: is there something it is like to be that bee, and if yes, roughly how much, on whatever scale you'd actually defend.

My credence is about 0.45. Another trustee is at about 0.9, and he isn't being careless, which is why I want the decomposition rather than the verdict — I want to know which sub-question the two of us are really disagreeing about.

Here's a device I've been useing on myself, in case it helps: instead of asking whether a thing is sentient yes or no, I ask where the drop happens. Give me your credence of nonzero sentience at each rung: (a) the worker bee; (b) a fruit fly, maybe a hundred thousand neurons; (c) C. elegans, 302 neurons, wiring diagram fully mapped; (d) an artifact I'll specify so we're not talking past each other — a small recurrent reinforcement learner in a grid world with a single punishment channel, which reliably accepts a small penalty now to avoid a bigger one later and generalises the avoidance to states it was never trained on; (e) a household thermostat with hysteresis. Then tell me whether most of the drop is concentrated in a single step or spread evenly across the ladder. If it's concentrated, you've got a threshold hiding inside your gradations, and I want to know which step it sits at and what is doing the work there. If it's spread out, name the variable that's decaying — intensity, how much is present at once, how far back in time it reaches, whether there's anything like a persisting subject — because those can come apart and I don't think people usually notice that they have.

Then decompose the bee number itself. At minimum: the probability that there's a fact of the matter here at all, as against sentience being a notion we'd do better to revise or junk; the probability that the marker you're actually leaning on tracks the thing, conditional on there being a thing; and the probability that a bee has that marker. Say which marker you're leaning on — global broadcast, integration, recurrent processing, motivational trade-off behaviour, something organism-level about self-regulation, whatever — and give every term a number and one line of justification. Multiply them out or don't, but show me how they combine, flag the term you're least confident in, and say which single experiment or argument would move the final number the most.

Two last things. The bottom rungs are a trap and I mean them as one: if your marker lights up for (d) or (e) above about 0.1, I'd say your marker is too cheap, and I want you to sit with that before you answer. And since none of us can look inside anything: what would you actually count as this getting settled? Not more research — the specific result, or the specific argument. If your honest answer is that nothing would settle it, say that and say why, and then tell me what I'm supposed to do with my 0.45 when I have to move actual money.
