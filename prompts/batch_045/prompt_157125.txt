I'm in a graduate seminar on Wittgenstein's On Certainty, and after class two classmates got into an argument that nobody else in the room could referee. The case they were arguing over: now that cheap, convincing synthetic video is everywhere, is the assumption that video recordings are generally reliable records of what happened — one of what the secondary literature calls hinges — actually in the process of shifting?

Maya: On Wittgenstein's picture that assumption was never a hypothesis confirmed by evidence. You can't check a recording without relying on other records and on other people's reports, so the demand to verify it is a regress rather than a question, and there is no such thing as answering it. That means the deepfake situation isn't new evidence bearing on a belief; it's a change in what we can do — practices of settling disputes by footage are becoming unworkable. And Wittgenstein allows that what is firm can silt up and move over time, so the honest description is that the hinge may be eroding, with no question of whether that erosion is justified.

Tom: If being a hinge just means standing outside the game of reasons, then hinge talk can't tell this case apart from the conspiracist who takes it as certain that every official record is fabricated and treats your evidence as part of the fraud. He too reports an unshakeable certainty. Your only comeback is that our community happens to rely on footage — a sociological fact about us, not a reason. So hinge epistemology either collapses into relativism or is conservatism with better vocabulary.

Maya: That misdescribes what a hinge is. Hinges show up in what people do, not in what they say they're certain of: we find our way by photographs, we settle insurance claims with footage, and a community that declared all recordings unreliable while going on relying on them in practice would have said nothing at all. The conspiracist's certainty is idle in exactly that way — it changes nothing about how he finds out whether his train was cancelled. That is a principled distinction, not head-counting.

Tom: But "makes no difference in practice" is an ordinary empirical test, and it is doing justificatory work. You are saying, in effect, that reliance on records has worked and still mostly works, and that where it stops working we revise — which is plain evidential reasoning about reliability, available for any proposition. So the view is unstable: either hinges sit outside reasons and you have no criticism of the conspiracist, or they answer to reasons and nothing distinctive remains — it is just the old problem of induction in new words.

Please adjudicate this move by move: for each of the four contributions, say what is right and what is mistaken, and then say what both of them are missing. I'd especially like to know whether hinge epistemology has any principled way to distinguish a hinge that is genuinely eroding from one a conspiracist merely declares dissolved.
