I've somehow agreed to run a joint seminar with the computer science department on time in philosophy and time in computing, and I've realised I mostly hand-wave when my colleague asks me what is actually at stake between presentism and eternalism. I spent about a decade as a backend engineer before I moved into philosophy, so I am the wrong audience for block-universe analogies borrowed from popular physics. I want it in the vocabulary I still think in: event sourcing and append-only logs, write-ahead logs, MVCC and snapshot isolation, system-versioned and bitemporal tables with valid time against transaction time, surrogate keys and slowly changing dimensions, git's object graph and HEAD, and enough distributed systems to know why there is no global clock and what a consistent-global-snapshot algorithm, the Chandy–Lamport sort of thing, actually buys you.

So: I want you take the presentism/eternalism dispute and restate each position in that vocabulary as carefully as you can. Eternalism: past and future rows equally there, and now not a property of the data but a parameter the query substitutes, a session value rather than a stored one. Presentism: only the committed current state exists, and truths about the past have to be made true by present traces, records and log entries and the like. Growing block: append-only, written up to now and no further. Moving spotlight: a cursor sweeping an otherwise tenseless store. And the argument from special relativity: no privileged foliation hence no privileged present, which I take it maps onto there being no global snapshot, only many equally good consistent cuts, none of them the cut.

Then the residue report, which is the bit I actually care about: which claims translate cleanly, which translate only if you smuggle something in, and which have no expression in that vocabulary at all. Three things I can't get straight and want you to press on rather than smooth over. First, passage: nothing in a log or an object graph corresponds to the present's moving or to time's passing, since HEAD's moving is just another relation among commits and not something extra, so is that a gap in the computing vocabulary or a sign that passage was never a separate claim? Second, the asymmetry: the store keeps the past and computes the future, and my half-baked suspicion is that the growing block is only presentism plus an epistemic fact about which direction we happen to keep records in, with no metaphysical difference between the two edges of the block. If the dynamics are time-symmetric, so that the present state fixes the past as much as the future, doesn't the block's having an edge at one end come entirely from entropy and from where we sit? Say whether the vocabulary supports that or quietly begs the question. Third, branching: in git a branch is an alternative, a way things might go, whereas the eternalist wants all times to be actual and not alternatives at all, so does the vocabulary even have the resources to distinguish many actual times from many possible futures, and is the old fatalism worry about eternalism partly an artefact of the target framework collapsing the modal into the temporal? Tell me for each whether the residue is a defect of the vocabulary or a sign that the philosophical claim was less clear than it looked. And while you are at it: where the mapping of identity across versions, surrogate keys, entities against rows, helps or fails with the problem of temporary intrinsics, how the same thing can be one way at one time and another way at another.

One last thing, id like it as continuous prose, the way you would write an email. No headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. I read these on my phone between seminars and formatting just makes them harder to follow.
