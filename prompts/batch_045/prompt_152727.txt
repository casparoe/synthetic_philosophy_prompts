I don't want to argue that in the packet myself; I want to hand them something that makes the point by performing it.

So: write me a ~800-word capability brochure for a fictional compliance-consulting firm — something like Foresight Partners, or whatever name you like better — that sells exactly that service to organisations which need a death to come out as merely foreseen rather than intended. The jokes have to come out of the actual machinery of the doctrine: the intended/foreseen distinction, the requirement that the good effect not be produced *by means of* the bad one, the proportionality condition, and especially the closeness problem — the cases where the bad effect is so tightly welded into the mechanism of the good one that calling it a side effect is a stretch. Their "closeness audit" should be a real, load-bearing part of the product, and I want at least one client whose case comes back borderline and gets handled the way the literature actually handles it: by bolting on a finer distinction (redirecting a threat that already exists versus originating a fresh causal chain, that general shape) and billing it as a premium module.
