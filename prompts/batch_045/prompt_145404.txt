I've been playing with a spreadsheet version of a very simple game, and I keep running into a pattern I don't have a word for, though I assume there is one.

The game: a biased coin comes up heads 60 percent of the time. Each round you are required to stake one half of your current bankroll; on heads you gain that half, on tails you lose it. Every round is favorable in the ordinary sense, since the average multiplier on your bankroll is 1.1 per round. Now two things seem true at once. Run a large crowd of players for a few hundred rounds and the average final bankroll grows without bound, at roughly that 1.1 per round, just as you would expect. But any single player who keeps going ends up with essentially nothing, almost surely — and so does the typical member of the crowd: in a crowd of a thousand the median player is nearly broke, while the average is astronomical, held up by a handful who got very lucky. There is no absorbing barrier here and nobody needs bad luck; with the stake set below the whole bankroll, wealth just decays toward zero without ever arriving at it.

The part I really can't place is what I noticed next. Have a thousand people play that same game on independent coins, and after every round have them throw everything into one pot and split it equally. Now the pot grows at very nearly the same 1.1 per round: the crowd's average has become the actual experience of one continuing entity, and the decay disappears. Have them bet on the same tosses instead of independent ones, and pooling does nothing at all.

Does the gap in the first case have an established name, and does the pooling maneuver have one of its own? People have offered me risk of ruin, variance drag, and gambler's ruin, and none of them seems to be quite it. I am also not mainly after a label: I want the carving. What does the gap consist in; what is pooling actually doing, such that it closes the gap under independence and not under correlation; how does that differ from ordinary diversification and from insurance; and where in this picture does the advice to stake the growth-maximizing fraction belong? Please answer in under 400 words.
