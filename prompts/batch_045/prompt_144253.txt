I convene a graduate reading group that is about to spend a term on self-locating belief, and I want a self-assessment instrument for the first session: a "which view do you actually hold?" quiz on Sleeping Beauty, with a scoring key. The original case is off limits as an item. To fix the protocol: a fair coin is tossed on Sunday; Beauty is awakened once if it lands heads and twice if it lands tails, with a memory-erasing drug between awakenings; she knows all this and, at each awakening, is asked for her credence that the coin landed heads. Every reader I will hand this to can already recite that case and already knows whether they are a one-half person or a one-third person. I want items whose answers they have not rehearsed, so that the instrument sorts them by what they say about unfamiliar cases rather than by the label they have adopted.

Please produce the following.

A short preamble, under 150 words, addressed to a reader who knows only the standard case and the two standard answers, stating the rules of the quiz. It should contain no theoretical labels.

Nine items. Each is a concrete case with three or four answer options, one of which is always a refusal or no-determinate-answer option. No item may be a rewording of the original case or ask, in effect, for a credence in heads under the original protocol; no item may turn on any fact a reader would need to look up; and no two items may be redundant in the sense that every leaf answers them identically. Among them I want at least:

An item that separates the view on which Beauty should treat herself as a random draw from the class of awakenings or subject-occasions from the view on which she is a random draw from a class of subjects. My candidate is a two-laboratory variant: the same protocol is run independently in two indistinguishable laboratories, Beauty wakes in one of them knowing the protocol and knowing truthfully that exactly one of the two runs came up heads, and is asked for her credence that her own run came up heads. Use it or better it.

An item that distinguishes a view's numbers from the route by which it reaches them, so that two readers who both answer one-third are not automatically sorted together.

An item asking whether the answer changes if the second tails-awakening is undergone not by Beauty but by a numerically distinct person created overnight with her memories, the original not being revived.

An item in which Beauty is offered, at each awakening, a choice between a ticket paying a fixed sum if the coin landed heads and a ticket paying the same sum if it landed tails, with the stipulation that after the experiment is over one of her awakenings will be selected uniformly at random and only the choice made at that awakening honored. The point of the stipulation is to cancel the familiar effect whereby a halfer's betting behaviour mimics a thirder's because she bets twice on tails; my assumption is that selecting one payoff awakening uniformly at random restores the ex ante weighting of worlds, so that the prices she is willing to pay track her credence in heads. If the stipulation does not do that work, say so in the diagnostics and propose one that does.

An item in which Beauty is told, truthfully and inclusively, that either the coin landed heads or today is Tuesday. If my arithmetic is right this item reverses the ordering produced by the familiar "you are told it is Monday" item: both varieties of halfer go to two-thirds here, while the thirder goes to one-half. Check the arithmetic rather than taking my word for it.

A scoring key defining seven leaves by their answer profiles rather than by their names: (i) one-half at the outset, two-thirds upon learning it is Monday; (ii) one-half at the outset and still one-half upon learning it is Monday, hence three-fourths in Monday beforehand; (iii) one-third in heads and two-thirds in Monday, reached by treating the multiplicity of awakenings within a single world as evidentially relevant; (iv) one-third in heads reached by counting subject-occasions wherever they occur, so that it generalizes to the two-laboratory case in a way (iii) need not; (v) one-half or one-third depending on which reference class Beauty is sampled from, where that choice is substantive and unforced; (vi) no precise credence is mandated, only an interval or a refusal; (vii) the question has no answer independently of the decision problem in which the credence is to be used, and the apparent first-order disagreement is partly verbal. For each leaf, state how it handles the duplicate-person item and the two-laboratory item, since those are where I expect the key to do real work.

Finally, a diagnostics section: the logical space the items generate, which questions generate it and which combinations of answers are coherent but unoccupied, with a verdict on why each empty cell is empty (incoherent, unattractive, merely unexplored); the item doing the most discriminatory work; the leaf most likely to be mis-sorted; and every pair of leaves the instrument cannot separate, with an honest statement of whether the failure lies in the views or in my questions.
