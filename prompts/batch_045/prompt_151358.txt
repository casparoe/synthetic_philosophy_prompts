I am editing a volume on explanation in historiography, and for the closing chapter — on retrospective necessity — I would rather open with a poem than with an epigraph. Please write a villanelle (nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, the two refrains returning on the usual schedule at 1/6/12/18 and 3/9/15/19) in which both refrains describe the same event: a commander who waits three days for fog before moving an army. One refrain is the version written before the outcome is known — roughly, he waits, and the fog may never come — and the other is the version written after: the fog came, so waiting was the thing there was to do. The point of using a villanelle is that the second version should encroach: with each return the event should feel a shade less contingent, more the sort of thing that was always going to happen, until in the final quatrain both accounts stand side by side and the reader has to hold the same three days twice. I want creeping determinism rendered properly — the felt sense, once you know how it turned out, that it could not have turned out otherwise — but I also want the poem to leave room for a wrinkle I cannot shake: the tidier, later version is not a pure distortion, because the person writing it knows something the earlier writer did not, and knowing that the fog came is some evidence about what the fog was likely to do. So the poem should not settle whether the tidiness is a fault in the knowing or a fault in the telling. Someone who works on hindsight bias and narrative explanation should recognise the mechanism in the behaviour of the form, not merely in the vocabulary.

After the poem, add a short note — a few sentences at most — separating what is well established here from what is genuinely contested: that people systematically overstate what they would have predicted, and that narrative accounts compress contingency, are one thing; whether that overstatement is a bias at all rather than reasonable updating on information the earlier writer lacked, and whether narrative shape in written history is an epistemic failure or simply what a written account is, are another. Flag any place where the poem leans on a contested reading rather than on a settled one.
