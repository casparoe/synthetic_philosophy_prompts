My opening: I think the whole apparatus quietly assumes that what someone values can be pulled apart into seperate line-items, and usually it can't. Concrete case. My neighbor and I disagree about basically everything: I think my discretionary money should go to insecticide-treated malaria nets, she thinks hers should go to restoring the roof of the old cathedral downtown, and neither of us thinks the other person's cause is worth a dime. Textbook trade: I pay for the roof, she pays for the nets, both of us come out ahead by our own lights. Except a big chunk of what I actually get out of giving is that I'm the one giving, visibly, as the sort of person who gives to that — and same for her and her roof. Call that the expressive part, and call the rest — nets delivered, roof fixed — the causal part. The expressive part travels with whoever performs the act, not with whoever supplies the cash, so after the trade neither of us has what we wanted and your famous Pareto improvement evaporates. And here's a second wrinkle that makes it worse for you: she may not want my money anywhere near her cathedral in the first place — funding from the wrong hands can spoil the thing for her, not just fail to help it.

So: can you even run a moral trade when what people care about is partly about who does what? And if your answer is "fine, just trade the causal part and leave the expressive part alone," tell me concretely what that looks like with my neighbor — given that we can't write anything enforceable, we will never interact like this again, we each have to decide whether to hand over our money before knowing whether the other one did, and we can't even talk it through without the conversation itself becoming the expressive thing we were trying to protect.
