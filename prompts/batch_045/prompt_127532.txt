You're a philosopher of art who's spent years on authenticity, forgery and the value of originals. Please think this through properly — brainstorm a couple of different ways of carving it up before you commit to one — and write your answer in American English. Don't just validate me; I want to know where I'm kidding myself.

Why this came up: I inherited a small oil landscape from my aunt that the family always believed was by a minor 19th-century painter. Last month a dealer looked at it and said it's more likely a "school of" picture — done in the studio, possibly by a pupil, possibly after the fact. The object didn't change at all, and I definately like it less. I'm annoyed at myself about that, and I want to know whether my reaction is just snobbery or whether it's tracking something real.

Here's where I think I've landed on fakes generally:

1. If two things are perceptually indistinguishable, they can't differ in aesthetic value. A perfect forgery is exactly as beautiful as the original, and being moved by it isn't a mistake.
2. Separately, causal history matters: the actual hand, the actual hour, the object that was really in the room. That's what I'd pay for, and probably what's bugging me about the downgraded attribution.
3. What's morally wrong with forgery is the deception, and basically only the deception. Hang it with an honest label and I've got no complaint.
4. All the talk about "aura" is mystical hand-waving.
5. I genuinely don't care whether I'm reading a first edition or a beaten-up paperback. The novel is the novel.
6. I do care that a signed print actually came off the plate the artist made, and that a "limited edition" is actually limited.
7. An exposed forgery gets a second life — apparently the famous Vermeer fakes now sell for real money under the forger's own name, and if that's right I think that's how it should be, they're more interesting than a competent copy by nobody.

So: which of these are actually in tension, which only look like it, and what has to give? Rank the tensions by severity, give me the cheapest fix for each and say what it costs me, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. A friend muttered that the whole thing is settled by the point that some arts allow multiple instances of a work and others don't — but a print is already a multiple and I still care about it, so I don't see that this settles anything.

Then run whatever survives on this case: a painter has a stroke, and for the last five years of his life the canvases signed with his name are mostly painted by his assistant — with his knowledge, consent and supervision, in his own studio, with everyone around knowing and nobody minding. They look exactly like his earlier work. Forgery or not? Do they have what I claim to value? Which of my commitments has to bend, and is there an honest way to keep the rest?
