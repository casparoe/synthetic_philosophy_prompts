I'm trying to settle whether a repair I've been kicking around actually rescues the Dutch book argument for countable additivity, and I'd rather have a number with its machinery showing than an essay.

Setup, so we argue about the same object. Let {A₁, A₂, …} be a countable partition of Ω into mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive propositions, and let an agent have credences p on the field they generate with p(Ω) = 1 and Σᵢ p(Aᵢ) = q < 1; write δ = 1 − q > 0, so p is finitely additive but not countably additive. Take the book in which she buys one unit stake on Ω at price 1 and, for each i, sells one unit stake on Aᵢ at price p(Aᵢ) — every transaction at her own fair price, either side, as these arguments assume. After n rounds her net position is qₙ − 1 if the true cell Aⱼ has j ≤ n, and qₙ if j > n, where qₙ = Σ_{i≤n} p(Aᵢ). So she is behind by at least δ once the true cell has been covered, and ahead by qₙ — which tends to q — for as long as it hasn't. In the limit the position is −δ in every state; at no finite stage is she behind in every state. As I understand it, the finitary half of this is a theorem: no finite book of fair-or-better bets guarantees a loss exactly when the quotients are coherent, i.e. finitely additive. The infinitary book is therefore doing work no finite book can do. (It doesn't obviously over-generate: run the same construction on a countably additive agent and the limit is exactly zero.)

The repair. Have the bookie make no infinitely many offers. Offer one contract at one time: a single instrument whose payoff is defined as the limit of the partial net positions above, so that it amounts to a sure payment of δ by the agent. One decision, one opportunity; the infinitary structure becomes the content of the contract rather than the shape of the choice sequence. Call it the rolled-up book.

The claim I want a credence on: the rolled-up book yields a Dutch book argument for countable additivity of the same kind and force as the standard finite argument yields for finite additivity — every premise it needs is one the defender of merely finitely additive credences already grants, and its conclusion is a genuine criticism of her rational standing rather than of her arithmetic, her information, or her attention. Give me your probability that this is right, as a single number.

Then decompose it, with a probability and one line of justification per term, and combine the terms explicitly. At minimum I want you to take a position on what the normative force of a Dutch book is supposed to be — the availability of a sure loss at your own prices, or the per-transaction acceptability of each bet plus the aggregation of individually acceptable transactions into an unacceptable package — on whether a loss that exists only in the limit is a loss at all, and on whether countable additivity has support from somewhere other than betting, which would make the repair less load-bearing than it looks. Add whatever terms I haven't thought of. The repair seems to me to face a dilemma: either the rolled-up contract is transparent to her, in which case she declines it and there is no book (and, worse, a bookie who wants to profit must either conceal its value or hand her at least δ up front), or it is opaque to her, in which case what has been exposed is a failure of computation, not incoherence. Say whether that dilemma is exhaustive, whether either horn is fatal, and what a third option would look like.

My own credence is about 0.3, and almost all of its movement comes from that dilemma. Tell me which term someone sitting at 0.8 would have to be setting differently, which term your own number is most sensitive to, and which single argument or observation would move you furthest. If you think the claim as I've posed it isn't well posed, or that it dissolves on inspection, say so and give me the number on the nearest claim that is.
