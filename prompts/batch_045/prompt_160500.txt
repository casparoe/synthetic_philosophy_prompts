I'm in the final year of my philosophy degree and am taking an advanced seminar on truth and realism. I'm part-way through an essay on deflationary accounts of truth and have become stuck on a point that I would like explained rather than surveyed.

The terrain I can take as given: the minimalist package built around the equivalence schema — the thought that all there is to *true* is captured by our disposition to accept each instance of *it is true that p if and only if p*, together with the claim that this apparently thin notion nevertheless does all the work we seem to need from truth: expressing infinite conjunctions we could never state one by one, such as *everything the witness said was true*; endorsing propositions we cannot formulate; and so on. I am setting the paradoxes aside, as the seminar has done.

The thing I cannot get straight is what I have started calling the problem of co-schematic predicates. Once the schema is stated with necessity — roughly, necessarily, for every proposition p, a predicate F applies to p if and only if p — it looks as though truth is not the only candidate. The predicate *true and 2 + 2 = 4* satisfies every instance, as does *true and snow is white*, since the added conjuncts hold of necessity; and in general a predicate of the form *true and A*, for any necessary truth A, is necessarily coextensive with truth itself. So the totality of schema-instances does not, merely by itself, single out one property.

Two replies I have seen gestured at strike me as unsatisfying, and I would like to know precisely where each fails or succeeds. First, the claim that truth is the minimal property satisfying the schema: I cannot see how this helps, since necessarily coextensive predicates are not ordered by minimality of extension, so the move seems to select nothing. Second, the claim that what matters is explanatory priority — that our acceptance of the instances is non-inferentially basic, whereas accepting an instance for *true and 2 + 2 = 4* would require arithmetical knowledge.

It is the second reply that I want to press with a case I have invented. Imagine a community whose language contains a predicate *twue*. Its speakers accept every instance of the corresponding schema immediately and non-inferentially, in just the way we are supposed to accept the instances for *true* — but they never use *twue* for semantic ascent, blind endorsement or infinite conjunction, and they have no separate device for those jobs; they simply do without them. Is *twue* truth? If yes, the minimalist appears committed to saying that the expressive work usually advertised as truth's entire function is inessential to it, and I no longer know what the notion is supposed to be for. If no, then something other than the instances — a pattern of use, or a functional role — is doing the individuating work, and I want to know whether that something can be spelt out without conceding that truth has a substantive nature after all.

There is a further thread I would like pulled: the normativity of truth. If a co-schematic predicate would serve just as well, then the claim that truth in particular is the standard or aim of belief seems to require more than the schema supplies; but it is unclear to me whether supplying that extra element is compatible with remaining a deflationist.

What I would find most useful: first, a plain statement of why the problem arises at all, in a form I could reconstruct in an essay; second, an assessment of which minimalist reply is strongest and what it costs; third, your sense of where the burden of proof lies — whether the co-schematicity worry is a genuine objection to deflationism, or a puzzle about property-individuation that tells equally against substantive theories. If the prosentential or redundancy formulations are in better shape here than the schema-based one, I would like to know that as well. Please do not give me citations or suggestions for further reading — I have plenty of those; what I need is the conceptual mechanics, in about 800 words. The essay is due in a fortnight.
