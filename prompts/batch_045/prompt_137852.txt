I am writing a paper arguing that the non-identity problem is not, at bottom, a problem about identity. My claim is that even once you grant the impersonal turn — that an act can be wrong without being wrong for anyone — there is a residue of cases the standard moves leave unsettled. I need one case to carry that claim, and I need it built to specification.

Design a case in which all of the following hold at once:

(i) the act under assessment is a genuine cause of who exists: the individual who ends up badly off would not have existed had the agent acted otherwise, so the identity-dependence that drives the standard cases is fully present and not merely alleged;
(ii) the number of people who ever exist is the same under each option;
(iii) total welfare is the same under each option, so the totalist fallback that normally rescues the judgement that something has gone wrong falls silent;
(iv) everyone who exists has a life clearly worth living, comfortably above any plausible threshold, and the wrong is not to be located in the agent's motives, in any deception, or in a violation of anyone's rights in the ordinary sense;
(v) the setting is one a real institution could face — a fertility clinic's protocol, a hospital's scheduling, the timing of a public health programme — with no science-fictional apparatus.

Against those constraints the case should still generate a firm judgement that one option is worse, and that choosing it is a wrong of exactly the sort the non-identity problem makes hard to articulate: readers should not be able to shrug and say that the person who fares badly is, after all, lucky to be alive.

After setting out the scenario in a single paragraph, do the following. Give the verdict of the person-affecting view, of total utilitarianism, and of a prioritarian or otherwise distribution-sensitive view, and show the reasoning that produces each verdict. Say which, if any, secures the target judgement. Then tell me honestly which feature of the design is doing the work, what becomes of the case if total welfare is allowed to differ even slightly between the options, and how a defender of the person-affecting view would attack it — in particular whether my supposed residue is only a disguised appeal to equality. Keep the whole answer under 700 words.
