hey — I run a tiny reading group (me, two grad students, and a retired programer who got into all this through Gödel) and we just spent a semester on Hofstadter's I Am a Strange Loop. Last session is next week and I'd like to close with a poem instead of a summary, because the central move of that book feels more like a mood than a thesis and I don't want to flatten it into bullet points.

So: write me a pantoum — five quatrains, twenty lines, last line identical to the first, the thing literally closing on itself. The speaker should be the self-symbol itself: the "I" token that lives inside the system's own vocabulary and purports to denote the whole system of which it's a part. Not Hofstadter's voice, not mine, the voice of the token. And I want the philosophy to actually be right rather than name-checked: the tangled hierarchy (you climb levels looking for the top and come back to where you started), the "I" as a symbol in the brain that refers to the brain, the epiphenomenon worry that a mere symbol shouldn't be able to push anything around given that the lower levels are causally closed, and Hofstadter's downward-causation answer that the pattern still exerts its pressure from the top down. Then the homebrew part, which is where the session actually went: our programer has this idea he calls the "re-issue rule." The self can't be any particular token, he says, because the system's model of itself gets revised every time it acts — the "I" at one moment isn't the same symbol as the "I" at the next. What persists is the procedure for minting a fresh one and passing it along, like a title, or a family heirloom, or a variable that keeps getting reassigned. The self is a rule for re-issuing a token, not a token. That gives him a criterion: anything whose self-symbol never gets updated — a recording of someone saying "I", a diary entry, a system that stores an "I" but never lets that symbol write back down into the machinery below it — has no self at all. He calls those stale tokens. We spent maybe forty minutes on whether a person in a coma is a stale token and whether that's a bug in his view or a feature.

Here's the part I care about most: make the pantoum's interlocking repetition do the work of the re-issue rule. Every time a line comes back in a new stanza it should be doing slightly different work — same words, new surroundings, the way the same "I" is a different symbol each time it's minted — and the final line returning to the opening one should be the loop catching itself, not a cute flourish. Let the stale-token worry land somewhere in the middle stanzas, the fear that what's speaking might be a recording rather than a self, and dont resolve it; leave it open, since that's honestly where we left it. No epigraphs, and no invented quotations — don't put wording in Hofstadter's mouth, and if you want to gesture at Gödel's incompleteness, allude to it without quoting anything. Aim for something where someone who's actually read the book can tell we're not just name-dropping, and someone who hasn't still gets a real poem that stands up on its own.
