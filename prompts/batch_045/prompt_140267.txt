I run a weekly grad reading group on confirmation theory, about 12 people, 90 minutes, and next week is the old evidence problem. Last time we covered it half the room left convinced it's just a glitch in the formalism with no real intuition behind it, so I want an activity where they walk into it themselves. My rough idea, steal it or throw it out: a cold-case board of maybe fifteen facts they've been staring at and getting nowhere with for the first half hour, then hand them a hypothesis about what happened that makes six of the dead-end facts suddenly click into place. They should feel a big surge of confirmation at the exact moment their credence in every one of those facts is already 1. Private written odds on the hypothesis before and after, no money, some scoring rule that makes them care. Two things I definately want built in. First, seed the board with facts at different levels of how well they're remembered: some they were plainly told, some they only half-recall, one or two they'd have bet against at the start — so the boost they report can be compared against how much uncertainty they had left, and they can see whether it tracks that or just tracks how strong the "of course!" feeling was. Second, in the debrief keep the aha (the sudden sense of fit) separate from an actual change in rational credence, and tell me honestly which one your game produces. I also suspect a good chunk of the surge is really the hypothesis looking more plausible or simpler once it unifies everything, which is a different thing again — if the design can pull that apart, do it. Include a timeline, the debrief mapping moments onto the pieces of the problem, what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (my bet: someone says "I just learned that H explains E" and thinks that settles it, and someone else's odds move because the room moved), and which feature of the problem the activity just can't reproduce so I have to state it myself. They shouldn't hear the words likelihood ratio until afterwards, and no lecture up front.
