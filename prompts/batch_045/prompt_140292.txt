I'm a second-year PhD student and this year's graduate representative on my department's Fellowships Committee, and I've been asked to draft the memo we'll vote on at Thursday's meeting. I'm also auditing a value theory seminar this term, and the material that's been rattling around my head is the worry that 'better than' might not behave the way committees assume: the spectrum-style argument that if a long chain of tiny, individually imperceptible improvements runs from a plainly weak thing to a plainly excellent one, then either some imperceptible difference is a real difference in merit, or betterness isn't transitive, or the endpoints aren't as far apart as they look. A friend in the seminar says the right response is that 'better than' is really three-place — better than with respect to some purpose — and that our mistake is trying to squeeze a single ordering out of several respects. I don't know which of these to accept, and unfortunately our committee has now impaled itself on all of them at once.

Here is the case. We have four one-year dissertation fellowships and 58 applications. After first-round scoring, 17 files form a dense band: ordered by average score they run from F-06 down to F-51, with every adjacent gap below 0.2 points on a 100-point scale, and readers who looked at adjacent pairs said, in effect, that they could not tell them apart. Yet the endpoints of that band are not alike at all: F-06 has a sole-authored article in a leading journal and a polished sample; F-51 has no publications and a thin, under-revised chapter — and no one on the committee is willing to say those two are equally meritorious. On top of that, our head-to-head votes on the top three files in the band cycle: a 4–3 majority preferred F-06 to F-11, a 4–3 majority preferred F-11 to F-17, and a 4–3 majority preferred F-17 to F-06, with different members moved by different respects (strength of the writing sample, teaching record, promise versus accomplishment, and fit with the fellowship's stated purpose of buying out a year of writing). Our charter contains a pairwise clause — it forbids the committee from ranking Q above P when a majority has preferred P to Q head-to-head — which no complete ranking can now satisfy. One member proposes a margin rule: treat any two files less than a point apart as not distinguishable and rank from there; another points out that this makes 'tied' fail to behave like equality (every adjacent pair is tied, yet F-06 beats F-51) and gives us no instruction about which chains of ties to follow, so that you can reach opposite conclusions depending on whether you compare each file to its immediate neighbour or to the one two places down. A colleague also reminds us that when we ran last year's shortlist votes in a different agenda order, a different file won. The dean has told us we may split a single fellowship across two recipients, once, but only if we give written reasons, and winners must be announced nine days from now.

Please draft the memo I'll circulate: 800–1,000 words, with a To/From/Date/Re header, addressed to the committee. It should recommend one concrete course of action we can vote on and act on within the next nine days — what decision rule to adopt for this cycle, how the four fellowships (and any split) should be allocated among the files in the band, and what wording to change in the charter going forward — and it should say plainly which of the options above it is relying on: that imperceptible differences are nonetheless real, that betterness is not transitive, that merit claims are respect-relative and need a specified purpose, or some combination. It should also give the strongest thing the losing side can say, which I expect is the charge that banding, lotteries, or procedural shortcuts abandon the judgment each candidate is owed. Please argue it out rather than pointing me to literature or telling me what to read; I need something I can hand to the committee. After the memo, output a single JSON object with exactly these keys: "bottom_line" (one sentence, worded so it can go into the minutes as a motion); "awards" (an array with one object per file in the band, each with "file_id", "outcome" — one of "award", "split_award", "lottery", "waitlist", "no_award" — and "reason", at most 20 words); "betterness_stance" (one or two sentences stating what the memo assumes about transitivity and about imperceptible differences); "rule_change" (the exact replacement wording proposed for the pairwise clause, or null); "strongest_objection" (at most 40 words); and "next_steps" (an array of objects with "action", "owner", and "date").
