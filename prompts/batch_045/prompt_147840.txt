Something's been bugging me and I can't tell whether its deep or just three bits of engineering convenience that happen to sound metaphysical. Three practices that all seem to quietly take a stand on whether two exactly similar things could still be *two*.

First: content-addressed storage. In a system like git, a file's contents get hashed and the hash literally is the object's name. So if I've got two files with byte-identical contents sitting in two different folders, the repo doesn't store two things — there's one blob, reachable by two paths, and what tells "the two files" apart is just where each sits in the tree. Delete one path and the object survives for the other. Compare the boring database case: every row gets an ID, so two rows with identical values in every single column are still two rows, and you can delete one and keep the other. Also the failure mode here is creepy in a specific way: if two genuinely different files ever collided on a hash, the system would treat distinct content as numerically one and the same object.

Second: the legal/everyday one. Borrow a cup of sugar, pay back different sugar, we're square. Borrow my bike and you owe me *that* bike — an indistinguishable replacement isn't good enough, and the law will order you to hand over the specific one. Money, grain, shares on one side; the bike, the painting, the heirloom on the other. Same legal system, same week.

Third: physics. The standard move in a stat-mech class is that when you count microstates of a gas of N particles of the same species you divide by N!, with the gloss that permuting the particles isn't a new arrangement — they aren't the kind of thing that could have been swapped. In quantum mechanics it gets sharper: for bosons you can pile arbitrarily many into one state, and the textbook says exchanging them gives you the very same state, not merely an equally probable distinct one. For fermions the question never even comes up, because no two of them share a complete set of quantum numbers, so nobody has to decide anything.

So: the same civilization that stores identical content exactly once and refuses to count permutations of identical particles also insists that my bike is mine and no qualitative duplicate will do. What I want from you:

Then separate, for each, what it *strictly presupposes* from what it merely fits nicely with, and tell me where it's underdetermined: which rival views would license exactly the same practice, unchanged?

Specifically on the physics: is it really making a metaphysical claim, or only a claim about what's measurable and trackable — the "we can't follow them so we coarse-grain" story? If it's the second, does the corrected counting still deliver an extensive entropy, or does something in the practice actually need the metaphysics? That's the bit where I get lost.
