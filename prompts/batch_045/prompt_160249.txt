You are a professional philosopher of mind whose research specialization is the metaphysics of consciousness. Please think carefully and work in stages: first, privately, spell out for yourself what the combination problem is actually supposed to be and what work an analogy could possibly do in that neighborhood; second, give me the explicit mappings; third, audit them; fourth, give me a verdict. I am not asking for a literature survey and I do not need citations or reading suggestions — just the conceptual audit.

I am in a graduate reading group on panpsychism, and our supervisor reaches for the same analogy every time the combination problem comes up: a human-level conscious experience, he says, is to the experiences of the micro-level subjects as the roar of a football stadium is to the individual shouts of seventy thousand spectators. No single spectator produces a roar, no single shout is a roar, and yet there is one roar; get enough voices together with the right degree of loudness and synchrony and the roar just is what you have. He pairs this with his own positive proposal, a threshold-fusion view: below some degree of causal integration among the micro-subjects you have many subjects, above it you have one, and the threshold is supposed to be doing the same work as the critical mass of volume and synchrony in the stadium. He uses the whole package to license a specific conclusion — that constitutive panpsychism is no worse off than the emergentist physicalism that everyone else in the room already accepts, and therefore that the combination problem is not a decisive objection to panpsychism but simply the hard problem wearing a different hat, something everyone has to swallow somewhere and no one gets to use as a weapon.

I have a worry I cannot get him to address, and I want to know whether it is the load-bearing one. A roar is a public, third-personally observable event in a pre-existing spatial medium; it exists as a sound wave whether or not anyone attends to it, and when it is heard it is heard by an external hearer. What needs explaining in the combination problem is not the presence of a new entity in the world but the existence of a single subject for whom the macro-experience occurs — and, worse, it looks to me as if a roar may not be anything over and above the many shouts at all, which is the very claim the panpsychist needs explained rather than licensed. When we pressed him on the palette version of the problem — how micro-experiences with presumably tiny, simple qualitative character are supposed to generate the enormous palette of human sensory experience — he swapped analogies and said it is like pixels on a screen: no pixel is red, a million slightly different pixels give you the whole chromatic range, so the palette problem is merely a resolution issue. Please audit both analogies. For each, give me a correspondence table: what plays the role of the micro-subjects, of the mechanism of combination, of the threshold, of the medium or container, of the unified macro-experience, and — if the analogy has room for it — of the subject whose experience it is. Then separate the disanalogies that matter for the conclusion he is using these to support from the ones that are harmless decoration; say explicitly what each analogy quietly imports that the target lacks (public observability, a spatial medium, an external observer, summation without genuine unity, a whole that is not a new subject); and tell me whether the no-worse-off conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogies were carrying the argument the whole time and the threshold-fusion proposal is left with no picture at all. If you think my difficulty comes partly from running together different versions of the combination problem — the summing of subjects, the palette of qualities, the structural mismatch between the microstructure of the brain and the structure of experience — say so plainly and tell me which analogy was supposed to be addressing which. Finally, if there is a better analogy for what the panpsychist needs (merging droplets, an orchestra, a parliament, something you construct yourself), describe it and say what it would still have to make good on.
