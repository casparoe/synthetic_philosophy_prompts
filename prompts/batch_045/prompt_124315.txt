my econ-ish group chat has been going round on this and I cant tell whos right. The setup: we read up on the Milgrom–Stokey no-trade result (1982, two economists, name-check not important) and the hayek-flavoured thing about prices pulling together scraps of knowledge nobody centrally has. And the group has settled on one picture to hold it all together: the market is a computer. private info is the data going in, trading is the processing, the price is the number on the screen, and the rest of us just read the screen. They lean on it twice: first to say we should basically defer to prices, and that if prediction markets can be built for anything then they beat expert panels at everything; second, combined with the poker table picture (traders as gamblers in a zero-sum game where somebody has to be the sucker), to say that the enormous daily volume we actually see must be noise and overconfidence, so more trading is evidence the market is getting dumber, not smarter.

Can you audit the computer picture for me? Give me the correspondence table – what plays the role of input, processor, output, operator, and error – and then where it breaks. Im especially stuck on one thing I half-remember: if the screen already shows everything, nobody has a reason to pay for the data, so there is no data to aggregate, which means the machine cant be a machine in the first place. Is that actually a disanalogy or am I confusing myself about what the result says? Also, what does the picture quietly import that a market doesnt have – a designer, a clean split between hardware and data, the idea that the information is just sitting out there waiting to be collected instead of being produced by people who need a reason to produce it?

Test it on this case I made up: a contract paying a dollar if a sealed envelope holds a red card. 100 traders, everyone shares the prior that red and blue are equally likely, each gets one private signal that is right 60 percent of the time, there is a flat 2 cent fee per trade, and after each round the price is public. Halfway through, a few of them get a surprise vet bill and need cash now. Under the groups picture, what is supposed to happen? Does the no-trade result actually say volume is zero here, and if not, which bit of the setup broke it – and is the 2 cent fee doing any of the work or is it a red herring?

Then the bottom line: does either conclusion survive, or was the analogy carrying all of it? If the computer is the wrong picture, whats a closer one?
