I'm in a small reading group that has been slowly working through older thought experiments about minds and machines, and I'd like an audit of an analogy that one member keeps deploying to close out an argument — specifically, I want to know whether the analogy is carrying the weight he thinks it is.

The case on the table concerns a hypothetical frictionless plane, the "careenium," strewn with myriads of extremely tiny marbles, the "simmballs," which collide with one another and with the walls and, losing no energy, keep going indefinitely. Set the initial conditions appropriately and the collisions can be described at a coarser level as a system of interacting symbols: clusters of marbles playing the role of bits, collisions playing the role of logical operations, and the whole ensemble readable as implementing a computation — say, one that mirrors the information processing of a human brain. The question the group cares about is whether that symbolic reading is doing any work. Strictly speaking the marbles are just bouncing; nothing in the physics of the plane mentions symbols. So: do the symbols push the substrate around, or does the substrate push itself around while the symbols are only our narration of it? Josh's move is to say this is a familiar situation with a familiar answer. The careenium is a simulation, and a simulation of a phenomenon lacks the phenomenon's characteristic causal and material properties: a simulated storm isn't wet, a simulated fire burns nothing, a simulated stomach nourishes no one. On that mapping the marbles are the simulator, the symbolic pattern is the simulated, and whatever the pattern appears to do it merely represents rather than performs — the careenium no more thinks than a weather model rains. He concludes that the symbol level is causally idle and that we can set the whole worry aside. Priya replies that the analogy is being asked to do a job it was never tested for: in the storm and fire cases we already know what the genuine article is and we are comparing a copy to an original, whereas in the careenium there may be no original at all, and whether the collisions are a copy of something or the thing itself was the question at issue.

I'd like the audit done properly, and I want it pinned against a case I invented that neither of them has addressed. Suppose the very same marble trajectories are readable, with equal precision and equal compression, as implementing two different computations — one a brain simulation, one a global weather simulation — which agree about every collision that actually occurs but diverge about what the marbles would have done if a particular marble had been nudged at minute 47, an event that never happens. Both readings fit the actual physics perfectly; neither is a worse description of what occurs. Now: does the simulation analogy tell us which reading, if either, is the program? Does the claim that symbols push the substrate around become more or less plausible when one substrate supports two incompatible symbolic stories equally well? And what becomes of Josh's conclusion, that the symbol level can be dismissed, if the honest answer is that both readings are implemented and implemented equally?

What I'm asking for: lead with your bottom line in one or two sentences, then give a correspondence table of no more than six rows laying out what maps onto what in Josh's analogy — simulator, simulated, original, copy, the analogue of wetness — then the two or three disanalogies that actually matter for the conclusion he draws, at least one that doesn't, then a verdict on whether his conclusion survives once the dual-implementation case is on the table, and any better analogy you would put in its place. Please keep the entire answer under 700 words, table included.
