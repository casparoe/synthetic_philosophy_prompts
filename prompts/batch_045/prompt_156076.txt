You are a professional philosopher who specialises in ancient Greek moral and political philosophy, and in particular in Aristotle's treatment of wealth, exchange and money. Think the question through carefully before answering, and open by sketching, in a few lines, the distinctions you intend to rely on and roughly where each of them comes from in Aristotle.

My younger brother has done rather well over the past three years running an automated market-making operation: his software continuously posts both a bid and an offer in a handful of thinly traded markets — collectibles, currency exchanges, ticket resale — and he keeps the difference between the two prices. Nothing is produced, stored, transported or improved by him, and very little of his own capital is ever at risk. At a family dinner our uncle called him a parasite: someone who simply stands between buyer and seller and skims a cut, adding nothing. My brother's reply was that in these markets there is frequently no buyer and seller to be matched at all until he turns up, so he is paid for making exchange possible rather than for tolling a trade that would have happened anyway. He then added that our uncle lives on the rent from two buy-to-let flats, which he called 'the same thing with a different label', and I could not think of a good answer to that either.

A friend who read classics at university said the whole argument sounded like something Aristotle had a settled view about. It does to me too, but I cannot reconstruct why. So: who has the better of this dispute, if anyone? I want more than a verdict — I want to know what exactly is supposed to make one way of getting money out of money different in kind from another, whether my brother's 'I make exchange possible' defence would carry any weight with Aristotle, and whether my uncle's rent really is the same thing. Please also mark clearly where you are extrapolating beyond anything Aristotle actually says, so that I can say something more useful than 'it is all just money from money' when this comes up again at Christmas.
