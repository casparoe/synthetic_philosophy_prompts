So this is going to sound like a dumb question but it's been bugging me for about a month.

I've played piano by ear since I was a teenager — no classical training, I just play. Last month I started teaching a 12-year-old beginer, and two lessons in she asked me how I know which note to play next, and I could not tell her. Not "I couldn't put it well" — I had literally nothing. My teacher says of course I comprehend harmony, I can hear a ii-V-I coming and I'm already voicing it before it lands. I think I have a large bag of licks and fifteen years of muscle memory and that's the whole story. A friend sent me some Dennett essay about Darwin's and Turing's strange inversions — design without a designer, comprehension built out of parts that don't comprehend, "competence without comprehension" — and now I can't tell if that's supposed to comfort me or freak me out.

I'd like a number, and I'd like the reasoning that produces it, not a survey of positions. Here's the proposition I want your credence on, stated as precisely as I can manage:

CWC-strong: it is possible for a system to have competences that are dispositionally indistinguishable from those of a comprehender in every context — every variation of the task, every novel or adversarial case, every why-question, every self-report of the form "what are you doing and what is it for" — and yet genuinely not comprehend what it is doing.

Give me your credence that CWC-strong is true. Please don't answer "it depends what you mean by comprehension" — I want a number, and I want that worry in the decomposition as its own term. Things I'd like to see as terms, and add your own if I'm missing something big:

- whether "comprehension" picks out anything with a determinate yes-or-no answer at all, versus being a folk-cluster notion that goes out of scope here;
- whether comprehension is exhausted by a system's intrinsic functional organization, or whether something non-intrinsic is required;
- the inheritance idea I came up with and can't shake: maybe comprehension is partly relational, so a system can *borrow* it from the causal-historical chain it came out of — the designers, the trainers, the people who wrote the text it was trained on — rather than having it intrinsically. If that's right then CWC-strong is false for a weird reason: behavior isn't all the facts, but the extra facts aren't about the mechanism either.
- whether the "every context" idealization is even coherent, or whether there's always some possible discriminating context if comprehension is real;
- whether comprehension requires phenomenal consciousness;
- whether meta-competence settles it. I can usually tell, before I play it, that a line is going to go wrong, and I can predict my own next phrase maybe half the time. Turing-flavored claim: a mechanism that predicts itself can be built from parts that predict nothing. So is my self-prediction comprehension of my own playing, or just one more uncomprehending competence?

Also please separate two claims I keep running together: the order-of-explanation inversion (competence comes first, you don't need a comprehender to get comprehension) and the nothing-but inversion (comprehension is nothing over and above a certain organization of competence). Give each its own credence and tell me which one the argument actually needs.
