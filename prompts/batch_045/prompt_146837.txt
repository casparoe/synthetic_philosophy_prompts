hi — I build multi-agent systems out of LLM instances, and I keep bumping into something game theorists seem to have thought through properly and we haven't.

The set-up I actually work in: I take one base model, spawn N agent instances, fine-tune each on its own private corpus (sometimes with a KL penalty pulling back toward the base policy, sometimes without), then let them talk to each other and trade prediction-market contracts on a shared question. When they disagree I can never tell whether it's because they saw different documents — fine, that's information — or because fine-tuning pushed them into genuinely different inductive biases, which I'd call a prior difference.

A guy on the team who did some grad econ says that distinction does no work. His line: by the common prior assumption (Harsanyi's doctrine, he calls it) you should always model disagreement as pure information difference, backed by a single shared probability measure over states of the world; and by Aumann's agreement result, agents who share a prior and have common knowledge of each other's posteriors can't agree to disagree, so persistent trade in my markets is either a bug — uncalibrated posteriors, non-Bayesian updates, no genuine common knowledge — or evidence that my agents really do have different priors.

He also says the ML analogue is exact: shared init / shared base model = the common prior, private fine-tuning data = the information partition, KL-to-base = the prior doing its anchoring work, and my "prior drift" worry is just sloppy talk about what is really differential evidence.

I dont buy that it's exact, but I can't say where it breaks. So: do the translation properly, in my vocabulary — base model, pretraining distribution, init seed, fine-tuning, KL anchoring, calibration, likelihood vs. inductive bias, distribution shift, agents that can't do infinite-depth reasoning about each other's reasoning — and be faithful to what the assumption actually says rather than to his version of it. Don't smuggle my framework's assumptions in and call it translation; where you have to fudge, say so. Then give me the residue report: what maps cleanly, what maps only with distortion, and what has no expression at all in anything I can build or measure.

Things I specifically want you to land on, if they're real worries and not me missing something:
