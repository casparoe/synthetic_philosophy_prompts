I'm writing a position paper for my school's philosophy society on Kavka's toxin case — the one where a billionaire pays you a million dollars tonight on the condition that you form, by tonight, the intention to drink tomorrow a dose that will make you wretched for a day but do no lasting harm, and you keep the money whether or not you end up drinking. Before I commit the paper to this, I'd like the set of views I've ended up with checked for consistency. Here is where I've landed:

(1) No one can form the intention in the original case, and the reason is that you cannot intend to do something you know you will have no reason to do when the time comes.

(2) Intending is not predicting. Intending to run a marathon I know I probably won't finish is perfectly ordinary, so forming an intention does not require believing that you will go through with it.

(3) Intentions give reasons. If I intend to finish an essay tonight, that is itself a reason to sit down and start now.

(4) In a variant I made up — the billionaire pays you for standing up in front of witnesses and promising to drink rather than for intending to drink — you can promise, the promise gives you a genuine reason to drink, and so the case is easy.

(5) In another variant of mine, where the dose only gives you an hour of headache instead of a day of misery, I still think you cannot form the intention: the size of the aversion is irrelevant, because what blocks the intention is that drinking would be pointless.

(6) But if the task were to intend to drink a glass of plain tap water, which costs nothing and is merely boring, I think you obviously could intend that.

Individually each of these seems right to me, so I can't see where the trouble is. Please tell me which pairs are genuinely in conflict and which only look that way, order the real ones by how severe they are rather than listing them all as equal, say for each what I would have to give up and what that would cost me, and tell me whether there is a single revision that would clean up several of them at once.
