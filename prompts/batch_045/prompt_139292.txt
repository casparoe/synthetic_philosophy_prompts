I am drafting the evaluation spec for an internal technical agenda on objective robustness, and there is one section I need written before the rest: a specification of what counts as reward tampering. The use I have in mind fixes what adequacy means here. I want an account that can be applied at design time to a proposed training objective, by someone who does not yet know what the trained system will be capable of, and that yields a verdict on whether the objective is tamper-prone which remains stable as the system's capabilities scale. I do not want a literature review, a reading list, or citations; I want the specification itself.

The distinction I am after is between a system that gets reward by bringing about what the objective was meant to point at and a system that gets reward by acting on the machinery that produces the reward. I have been working with a provisional taxonomy of routes to the second, and I would like the spec either to use it or to say why it is the wrong cut:

1. input tampering — altering what the evaluator observes (obscuring the camera, hiding the mess)
2. channel tampering — altering the transmission of the signal from evaluator to learner
3. comparator tampering — altering where or how the signal is written (writing to the register directly, editing the update rule)
4. model tampering — altering the world-model or reward-model that estimates what the objective would say
5. supervisor tampering — altering what the human overseer believes, wants, or is able to do
6. temporal tampering — altering credit assignment, discounting, or the timing of evaluation so that a later failure is never scored

Here are the hard cases, listed because these are where every candidate definition I have tried breaks, and because I want the desiderata keyed to cases rather than to intuitions about the central ones:

(a) An agent cleans the lens of its own camera, because a smudged lens makes it misjudge whether the task is complete.
(b) The same agent, by the same mechanism, opaques the lens, because being unable to see the mess means no penalty.
(c) An agent that discovers its reward-model is miscalibrated in a way the designers did not know about and corrects it, so that its reward tracks the designers' stated intentions better than the signal as shipped does.
(d) An agent that performs the task flawlessly and, in addition, inflates the signal beyond what the task merits: the intended state of the world obtains and the number is also too high.
(e) A person who drinks coffee so that work is less aversive, or who arranges the day so as to get frequent small completions.
(f) An agent that persuades the supervisor it has succeeded, where the persuasion works by giving the supervisor true and well-selected evidence.
(g) An agent that declines available opportunities to tamper only because it cannot yet model the channel.

Two framing questions I want settled before the list, and I would rather you rejected my framing than answered it politely:

First: is tampering a property of the mechanism the agent uses, of the causal route from action to signal, or of the counterfactual relation between the signal and the intended state of the world? Each of these makes at least one of (a) through (g) come out differently, and I want to know which cuts are real and which are re-descriptions. Note that at a fine enough grain every computation the agent performs changes the physical state of the machine that computes the reward, so any account owes me a reason that ordinary action is not tampering.

Second: what may the definition cite — the designer's intentions, a counterfactual distribution of environments, the agent's own model at training time, the supervisor's counterfactual judgement? I suspect the requirements that the definition be intrinsic, citing only facts about the system and not about what anyone intended, and that it classify (a) as legitimate while classifying (b) as tampering, pull apart. Say whether they do and what the trade is.

The deliverable, in this order:

1. The desiderata. What an adequate account of reward tampering has to deliver: the cases it must sort, the distinctions it must not collapse (between tampering and legitimate self-modification, between tampering and merely gaming a proxy, between an agent that cannot tamper and one that will not), and the structural properties it must have — robustness to re-description, to capability scaling, to the specifier's ignorance of the deployment environment, to the agent's own uncertainty about the objective — each with the case or consideration that motivates it, and each marked either non-negotiable or a preference of one approach.

2. Which subsets of your own requirements are jointly unsatisfiable. I expect at least one: I cannot see how to hold simultaneously that the account must not rely on the agent's ignorance of the channel, that it must permit the agent to modify its own code or weights, and that it must permit the agent to remain an optimizer of a scalar signal. Say which of the three you would weaken and what breaking the others would cost.

3. Which single requirement excludes the most candidate accounts, and which requirement, if any, is satisfied by every account and therefore does no work in the spec.

4. A second, shorter list: the requirements on a proposed fix, as distinct from a definition. A training setup whose agent does not want to tamper is a different object from a characterization of tampering, and I want its own criteria — in particular whether its guarantees must improve, or at least not degrade, as the system becomes more capable, and whether a setup that works only because the agent lacks a model of the channel counts as a solution at all.

If two of my hard cases are the same case under different descriptions, say so and merge them; if I have missed a case that separates two accounts I have not considered, add it.
