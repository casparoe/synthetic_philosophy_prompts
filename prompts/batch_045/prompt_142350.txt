I'm giving a toast for a colleague who's leaving and who happens to work on epistemology, and I want one Gettier joke in the middle of it. But I want an *actual* Gettier joke, not the "haha philosophers don't know anything" thing you get if you go looking for this.

What I mean by actual: the punchline has to turn on the machinery of a Gettier case. Someone who's read one should be able to point at the exact line where the justification and the truth come apart and groan for the right reason. Ideally it also catches why these cases are irritating rather than sad — the person did nothing wrong, by any normal standard, and still doesn't get to say they knew.

Some constraints, sorry in advance:

- Fresh case. No stopped clocks, no fake barns, no sheep in a field, no job candidate with coins in his pocket, no disjunction involving Barcelona. Make one up. If it helps, set it in a specific workplace: a bike courier dispatch, a vet clinic, a restaurant kitchen, an airport gate.
- It has to land as a joke first. People should laugh — or at least do the loud exhale through the nose — before they start diagnosing the case. A shaggy setup is fine; a lecture with a punchline stapled to it is not.
- I'd rather it be the kind where somebody reasons from a justified false premise to a conclusion that happens to be true, than the kind where they're surrounded by fakes and happened to look at the real one. The first kind plays better in a room, and it's also the kind where the "no false lemmas" fix sounds plausible for about a second before it stops sounding plausible.
- Ideally the line right before the punchline is something the character is completely justified in believing, and which turns out to be true, but only by accident. I definately don't want the joke to just be the word "know" doing all the work.

Then, after the joke, keep it to three or four lines: which bit of your case is doing the Gettier work, whether it's a false-lemma case or one where "no false lemmas" wouldn't help anyway, and what you'd say if someone in the back of the room shouts that it isn't really a Gettier case at all. If you're tempted to give me two, give me the funnier one first and the smarter one second.
