I'm one of the volunteer moderators on a big hobby forum (nature and birdwatching, around ten thousand members). A good chunk of my week is locking threads where two people are obviously furious at each other but I genuinely can't tell what they're disagreeing about. Half the time I suspect they're just using the same word differently — is a domestic pigeon a "wild" bird, does a phone photo through a scope count as "digiscoping", does the forum "have a duty" to do something. Other times I think there's a real disagreement buried in there about how things actually are or about what we should do, and locking it feels like a cop out.

My own rule of thumb is: if both people would give the same answer to every concrete case you could throw at them, they're only fighting about a word. I've been told that's too crude — that people can agree on every concrete case and still have something real at stake, and that people can disagree on concrete cases and still only be fighting about a word because theyve quietly started talking about different things. I also dread a whole class of threads where people argue about whether something really exists (numbers, species, the forum itself as something over and above its members) and nothing anyone could observe seems to hang on the answer, so I never know if I should close them as pointless or let them run.

What I want is a procedure my team could actually run, in writing, in about fifteen minutes, in plain language that both arguers can follow and that assumes no philosophy. Ordered questions to ask, what counts as a good enough answer to each, the point where you stop and say "this is only words, here's how to end it", the point where you stop and say "this is real, leave it open", and the point where you have to hand it to a human vote because it's about what we want rather than what's true. Constraints: we're volunteers, we can't force anyone to accept a definition, and whatever we produce has to be one short paragraph we post in the thread.

Then run it on three threads: (1) two members arguing about whether a bird that escaped from a zoo and bred in the wild counts as "wild" for the county list, where the list rules award an actual prize; (2) a thread about whether AI-generated nature images are "photographs" at all; (3) the numbers-exist one. At least one of these should come out with an answer I won't like, and be honest about what your procedure simply can't do.
