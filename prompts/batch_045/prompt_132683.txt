Thesis to defend. The familiar genealogy is wrong about its starting point, and therefore wrong about where the real disagreement lies. The old historicist slogan about showing the past as it really was is standardly presented as the banner of a naive objectivism that Carr's mid-century lectures and White's narrativist work of the early 1970s then dismantled. But that slogan is better read as an internal polemic: a demand that historians stop subordinating the particular case to philosophical schemata of progress or of spirit, and instead let unfamiliar sources discipline the categories they bring to them. Read that way, it is not a claim to have escaped selection and emplotment — so the two famous constructivist arguments, that the historian decides which facts count and that the shape of the narrative is contributed rather than found, are not refutations of it. One can grant both and still be doing exactly what the slogan asked.

Argue, then, that the substantive fault line is elsewhere, in what I shall call the anchoring question: does the archive constrain only the individual statements a narrative contains, or does it constrain the shape of the narrative as a whole? Defend the second answer, subject to the following constraints.

The argument must be internal: grant the critic that selection is value-laden and that no evidence dictates a plot-structure, and reach your conclusion anyway.

Answer a second reply: that even granting anchoring for individual statements, the cognitive content of history lives in the shape, and shape is never settled by evidence.
