I'm trying to get clear about a structural problem in decision-making under imprecise probabilities, and I'd like an explanation of the mechanics rather than a survey of positions.

Set-up. An agent's uncertainty is represented by a convex set C of probability functions over a finite state space, and she ranks options by a maximin rule: choose the option whose minimum expected utility across C is greatest. Now embed this in a two-stage problem. She first decides whether to run a diagnostic test whose result she observes before choosing a treatment; both the prevalence of the condition and the reliability of the test are known only imprecisely, so C contains distributions that disagree about the prior and about the likelihoods, and observing the result does not merely pin down a single unknown parameter.

There appear to be two ways to evaluate a contingent plan, i.e. a complete specification of what she does after each possible result.

(G) Ex ante: for each plan, minimize its unconditional expected utility over C, then maximize over plans.

(S) Sequential: fold the tree back. At each final decision node, replace C by the set of conditional distributions its members induce on the evidence available at that node, evaluate each continuation by its worst case over that updated set, choose the best continuation, and propagate the resulting node values back to the initial chance node by taking the minimum over C of the expected node value.

My sense is that these cannot agree in general, because (G) forces a single member of C to serve as the adversary throughout the whole tree, whereas (S) lets a different member play that role in each branch — and the distributions in C that are bad for you conditional on one test result need not be the ones that are bad for you conditional on another. If that is right, then (S) should be weakly more pessimistic than (G), not less. I would like this made precise, and I would like to know what follows from it. (Please set aside the complication where some member of C assigns probability zero to a possible result, so that the conditioning step itself becomes delicate.)

1. Give the smallest example you can — a handful of states, a credal set you can describe in a sentence — in which the two procedures come apart, ideally in which plan they recommend and not merely in the value they assign to a given plan. Explain the disagreement in terms of which distributions in C are doing the adversarial work where.

2. Under what structural condition on C would the two procedures necessarily coincide for every decision tree built on that state space? Say what that condition prevents the agent from expressing — for instance, about repeated trials of a single possibly biased process — and whether you regard that as an acceptable cost.

3. Is the gap best described as a failure of dynamic consistency, a failure of consequentialism, or neither? Concretely: after observing the result, can the agent have reason to depart from the plan that (G) recommended ex ante? If so, is that an objection to (G), to (S), or only to the assumption that plans should be assessed ex ante at all — and does it give the agent reason to purchase commitment?

4. Now consider a rival rule, which I will call the least-adversarial-prior rule. First find the member p* of C under which the decision problem is worth least to the agent: the distribution that minimizes the expected utility she could obtain by best-responding to it. Then simply act as an expected-utility maximizer with p*, updating by ordinary conditionalization at every node. Assume for simplicity that p* is unique. How does this rule compare with maximin? Specifically: is it dynamically consistent as a matter of course; can it select a plan whose worst case across C is worse than the worst case of the maximin plan; does it at least guarantee that the selected plan is undominated relative to C; does it matter whether the agent may randomize over plans; and is it a decisive objection that p* depends on which options happen to be available rather than only on the agent's evidence? Briefly compare it with minimax regret.

Please argue from the structure of the problem. I am not asking for references, and an answer that leans on citations rather than argument will not help me.
