I have no training in philosophy; I read about it on my own, and over the past few years I have worked my way to a position that I would like stress-tested rather than defended. Call it lexical threshold negative utilitarianism. It has two halves.

First, below a threshold, pleasure and pain are commensurable: mild discomforts and mild enjoyments trade off against each other and against sums of each other on something like a single scale, so ordinary reasoning about small hedonic costs and benefits goes through roughly as common sense says it does.

Second, there is a threshold of suffering — I tend to think of it in terms of intensity and duration together, though I am not confident which — such that any outcome containing a single instance of suffering above that threshold is worse than any outcome containing none, regardless of how much pleasure or how many sub-threshold discomforts the second outcome holds. Above the threshold, pleasure does not compensate. Below it, it does.

I hold it because it seems to secure two claims I do not want to surrender: that one episode of extreme agony really does matter more than any number of headaches, or than any quantity of mild contentment; and that it is not irrational to trade a little pleasure for a little less pain in ordinary life.

What I want from you is the case against it, not a tour of the surrounding literature. In particular:

Which parts of the view are load-bearing? If I dropped the lexicality, or the commensurability claim below the threshold, or the assumption that there is one sharp boundary rather than a vague band or a family of boundaries for different kinds of suffering, would I have a repaired version of this view or a different view altogether? I would like to know which component the view most stands or falls with.

What would count decisively against it, and what would merely be awkward? Rank the defeaters, and add ones I have not thought of. The lines I have in mind are: the arbitrariness of a sharp cut-off, since any placement seems either to collapse the view into a pure negative utilitarianism that cannot condemn anything except by comparison, or to sit so high that the view is extensionally indistinguishable from an ordinary aggregative one; the worry that lexical superiority combined with uncertainty about exactly where the threshold falls leaves my preferences incoherent or exploitable in the manner of intransitive or sharply discontinuous preferences generally; the suspicion that I have performed a verbal manoeuvre, redescribing a strong intuition about extreme suffering as a structural feature of value itself; and the empirical questions, namely whether there really is one hedonic dimension along which agony, grief, nausea and boredom are comparable, and what to make of people who report lives containing severe suffering as nonetheless worth living — whether my view must say they are simply mistaken about their own experience, or whether it can allow that the suffering is real, the value is real, and the two do not trade against each other.

For each defeater, would someone committed to a view like mine actually concede it as counting against the view, or is it the sort of thing such a person would simply bite the bullet on? I care about this distinction, because from the outside I cannot tell which objections people familiar with these debates take seriously and which they regard as stock.

Does the view risk anything at all? I notice that I have never said where the threshold lies, and I notice that this lets me accommodate almost any imagined case by placing the threshold just above whatever the case involves. Is that a sign the view has been insulated against correction, and if so, is that a defect in a view of this kind, or an acceptable feature of one that aims only to constrain the extremes?

For what it is worth, so that you can tell me whether I am weighting things wrongly: the arbitrariness objection moves me most, and the incoherence-under-uncertainty objection moves me least, because I suspect it proves too much against any view with lexical structure at all. Tell me if that ordering is backwards.

Please argue against me as directly as you can. I would rather be talked out of the view than have it returned to me in better words.
