I'm putting together a two-page dissertation-prospectus proposal for a one-year fellowship, on evidential cooperation in large worlds, and I need you to write it. The committee is mixed philosophy and economics, so the document has to be self-contained for readers who don't share my background; I'll supply all citations myself, so don't give me any, and don't refer me to things to read.

Here is the background as I currently have it, so you don't have to reconstruct it. By a large world I mean one containing many agents in approximately the same decision situation as the agent in question, where those agents' actions are correlated with hers — paradigmatically through shared causes (similar environments, similar histories, similar algorithms) rather than because anyone causally influences anyone else. In such a world, an agent who treats her own action as evidence acquires reasons she would not otherwise have: if her cooperating is evidence that many others cooperate, then in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma the evidential bearing of her act can swamp the small causal difference it makes to her own payoff. Two features strike me as where the real difficulty lies. First, the correlations that matter are the residual ones, conditional on everything else the agent already knows; an agent who knows a great deal about the shared causes gets less evidential reason than an ignorant one, which looks like a penalty on knowledge. Second, how much an action is worth as evidence and how many actions it is evidence about pull in opposite directions as the world grows, and whether they cancel depends on modelling choices I don't think have been made explicit.

My half-formed idea is that the standard treatments hold fixed something agents can actually move: the similarity structure itself. Which agents count as counterparts, and how similar one is to them, is not simply given. An agent can make herself more predictable (commitment devices, tying herself to a rule, acting in public), less predictable (randomization, delegation, opacity), more or less informed about the shared causes, or more or less like a particular subpopulation. If similarity and information are choice variables, then the reference class relative to which the evidential argument is computed is endogenous, and the recommendation becomes a fixed point: one acts on the correlations implied by a reference class whose composition depends on what one does and on what one lets oneself learn. My suspicion is that in small worlds this endogeneity is negligible and in large worlds it is not, because the number of agents whose behaviour is at stake amplifies whatever the agent does to the reference class — so that the size of the world is what turns a curiosity into a genuine problem for the view. My further suspicion, the part I most want tested, is that once an agent is allowed to manage her own similarity and her own information, evidential cooperation converges on something like be the kind of agent who cooperates, and the distinction between evaluating acts and evaluating policies or characters, which decision theory is supposed to respect, collapses.

Write the proposal in about 1,000 words, with: (1) the question, stated so that it has a determinate answer, with the formalism fixed up front — how many counterparts, what correlation structure, what exactly the agent controls — rather than invented later; (2) why the existing answers leave it open, in particular why it is not settled by the two-agent case, by switching to a causal or a functional decision theory, or by the existing work on ratifiability and self-fulfilling equilibria; (3) the method, month by month across twelve months, distinguishing what gets proved, what gets illustrated by worked cases, and what stays a conjecture; (4) the possible conclusions — what a yes, a no, and an ill-posed-question result would each look like — and which consideration would decide between them; (5) what would count as the project failing, stated so that I can't wriggle out of it; and (6) a short paragraph answering the objection I expect most, that this is a reason to stop treating correlations as reasons for action rather than a research programme within such a view.

Two further constraints. If my endogeneity point turns out to be a familiar problem under a new name, or if the convergence claim I gestured at rests on a confusion, say so in the framing section rather than quietly repairing it — I would rather submit a sharpened question than a smooth one. And keep the scope honest: say plainly which of the six items above a single year of work cannot deliver.
