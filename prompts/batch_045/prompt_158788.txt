The area is van Fraassen's Reflection principle: roughly, the requirement that your current credence in A, conditional on your later credence in A being x, should itself be x. I want a proposal that does something with it, not one that just re-explains it.

Here's where I'm at, and you can push back on any of it. The principle usually gets two kinds of support: a diachronic Dutch-book / coherence style argument (if you violate it, you'll accept a combination of bets now and later that loses you money for sure), and an "expert deference" argument (later-you knows more, so current-you should defer). And it usually gets two kinds of objection: the worry that you can't satisfy it at every time without already having averaged in everything you might later learn, which seems to make it either trivial or unsatisfiable; and the self-locating / memory-loss style cases (Sleeping Beauty, forgetting-the-day setups) where the future credence isn't obviously the one to defer to.

My half-formed thought is that all of these turn on something nobody seems to have systematised: the informational relation between the earlier stage and the later stage. It's not always "later knows more". Sometimes later-you knows strictly more (clean learning). Sometimes strictly less — you'll be given the result of an experiment and then have it wiped, repeatedly, so later-you has strictly less total evidence even though later-you is "closer" to the event. Sometimes the relation is sideways: you'll acquire a new distinction or concept, so the thing later-you has a credence in isn't quite the same proposition as the one you now have a credence in; or you'll fission, and there'll be two later stages that each remember being you, with different credences. So maybe Reflection should be stated as a family of principles indexed to that relation, or as one principle with an explicit deference weight that varies with it, and the interesting question is whether the weights are principled or whether any value other than 0 or 1 is just ad hoc. I have a feeling "ad hoc" might be the honest answer, and that that would itself be a real result: it would mean Reflection is all-or-nothing and the general principle should just be dropped in favour of a restricted one, with the interesting work being to say exactly where the restriction falls.

- Why the existing positions leave it open — in particular why the standard moves (restricting Reflection to non-self-locating propositions, abandoning it in favour of conditionalization, or going imprecise / interval-valued) don't already do the work I'm describing.
