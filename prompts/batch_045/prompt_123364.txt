Putting together a position paper on the knowability paradox for a reading group and I want someone to audit the package before I defend it in front of people who'll enjoy taking it apart. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Anti-realism in the old verificationist spirit: there are no recognition-transcendent truths, i.e. what makes a proposition true can't outrun everything that could in principle count as evidence for it.
2. Obviously we aren't omniscient, so plenty of truths aren't actually known.
3. "Knowable" has to be read with an idealisation built in: knowable by a suitably idealised inquirer, not by us as we actually are, and also not by a literally unlimited one, since that would make the principle empty.
4. I think Fitch's argument is valid and that the honest response is to restrict the knowability principle, not to revise the logic or to abandon (1).
5. But the restriction has to fall out of the anti-realist's own story about meaning and evidence. I don't want to end up with a restriction that amounts to whatever happens to block the derivation, like the usual move of excluding propositions that embed their own unknowability.
6. I want to be able to say there is a determinate truth about something like the total matter distribution beyond our cosmological horizon, that no inquirer of any kind could ever be in a position to know, and that this doesn't sink (1), because anti-realism is a claim about the sort of discourse we actually engage in rather than about every putative truth there is.

My worry list is probably wrong: I keep thinking (4) and (5) are fine together and that the real trouble is somewhere around (1) and (3) or (1) and (6), but I can't localise it. Go through the set and tell me which pairs genuinely fight, which only look like they do, and for each real one what would have to give and what that would cost me. Don't flag everything as a problem.

Answer as exactly five numbered items, worst tension first, one or two sentences each, no intro and no summing-up at the end. If some of the clashes are only apparent, use the item to say so and say what dissolves it. No citations or reading suggestions, I know the literature.
