I'm in my final year of school and have been asked to send a short statement of my current views on determinism and predictability to a tutor ahead of an interview for a physics-and-philosophy programme. Before I send it I would like someone to check whether the views actually hang together, because I have a feeling that they do not, but I cannot locate the fault and I would rather find it now than in front of an interviewer. The set, roughly as I wrote it:

(1) The macroscopic world is deterministic: its total state at any one time, together with the laws, fixes the whole of its future, so an intelligence outside the universe, with the state and the laws written down, could in principle predict everything. I take this to be true even though no such intelligence exists and none could.

(2) Many real systems are chaotic, and chaos brings a stronger limitation than our merely lacking good enough instruments right now. Any agent inside the world would need to specify the present state to arbitrarily many digits, and no measuring device or memory that fits inside the universe can do that; the act of measuring also disturbs what is measured. So the limit holds for us in principle, and not just for the time being.

(3) This is what reconciles free will with (1). The future is open in the only sense that matters to someone deciding what to do: I cannot, even in principle, discover now what I am going to do, so deliberating is not a ceremony performed after the outcome is already fixed and knowable.

(4) I also think quantum mechanics is indeterministic, and that this offers no comfort at all to anyone who wants freedom of the will: randomness entering my decision-making would be something that happens to me, not something I do.

(5) I trust projections of long-run average temperature while thinking that a specific forecast of the weather ten days out is nearly worthless.

(6) A test I apply generally: I refuse to count a limitation as metaphysical unless some law forbids the better performance. If the obstacle is only that we lack precision, memory, or time, I classify it as epistemic and file it under facts about us rather than facts about the world.

(7) A verdict I am attached to: that my mother can reliably finish my sentences and guess what I will order for lunch does not make my choices any less free or any less mine.

What I would like is an audit rather than a survey, and I am not asking for reading suggestions. Please tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look that way, ordered by how bad the tension is; for each real one say what would have to be given up and what giving it up would cost me; and then say which single revision would clean up the most. If one of these is doing more work than the others, or if two of them are really the same problem wearing different clothes, I would like that pointed out too. I would rather keep (1) and (7) than anything else, but I am more interested in where the strain actually is than in protecting them.
