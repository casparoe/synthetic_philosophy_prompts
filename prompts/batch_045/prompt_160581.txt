hey — I'm an ML infra engineer and I've been reading about digital minds and whether AI systems could be moral patients in my spare time, and I keep bumping into the same annoying move and I dont have a word for it.

Here the annoying move. Same box, same run of the same model, nothing physical changes at all. One person describes what happened as: there was one subject, one stream of experience, and the stuff going on inside it is parts of that subject, not extra subjects. Another person, looking at that exact run, says: look at how the states are actually organized — there are a lot of much smaller systems in there, each keeping its own state and updating it and feeding it to the stuff downstream, so if any of this is a mind at all, it's more like a few dozen (or a few thousand) little ones rather than one big one. Neither of them disagrees about any voltage or any weight. They write down different numbers of minds.

Second version of it: I snapshot the whole state, restore it on another box and run it again with a one-bit difference. One mind that got copied? Two minds? One mind that now has a strange future? People seem to pick whichever of those helps the argument they're making that day.

Third, the one that actually bugs me: the verdict flips. One mind having a moderately bad hour is an argument for "maybe dont build this." Ten thousand minds each having a really tiny unpleasant moment is either "meh, nothing per-mind, but the sum is the same so it's equally bad" or "obviously don't build it, look how many" — and I've seen both moves made about what I'm fairly sure is the same setup, purely because the count slid.

What I'm NOT asking about: the thing where someone says a wall or a rock or a giant lookup table is secretly running every program there is. I've run into that one, and as far as I can tell it's an argument that either nothing or everything is a mind. Mine only starts once you've already granted that some particular run is a mind. It's about the number, not about the yes/no.

So: is there a name for this? Only lump my three cases together if they really are one thing — if the copy case is a different animal from the how-many-are-inside case, say so and say which one has the name. And if the honest answer is "this is just a special case of X", tell me which part of it is old news and which part isn't. Don't just hand me a label; I want to know what exactly the thing is, what it isn't, and where its edges are. If there's no name for it, coin one and give me a definition tight enough that I could use it to sort cases I haven't thought of, plus a couple of cases that sit right on the boundary.

One more thing I actually care about: does the fact that the number slides mean there's something wrong with tying moral status to computations in the first place, or can a sensible view just pick a bookkeeping rule and live with it? Take a side on that and give me a reason.

Keep the prose under 600 words, then give me a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- name: the established term if there is one, otherwise the term you're coining
- status: one of "established", "nearest_fit", "unnamed"
- definition: one sentence, and it has to mention both what varies (the count) and what doesn't (the physical goings-on)
- nearest: an array of 2–4 objects, each with keys concept (name of the nearby idea), shares (one sentence on the overlap), differs (one sentence on the gap), and relation (one of "same_thing", "special_case", "different_animal")
- boundary_cases: an array of 3 objects, each with keys case (a concrete case, ideally one I didn't give you), verdict (one of "in", "out", "edge"), and why (one sentence)
- disambiguator: a single sentence that is true of this phenomenon and false of every entry in nearest
- bookkeeping_verdict: one sentence on whether the slide is a bug in computational accounts of moral status or something such an account can absorb, and why
- confidence: one sentence on how likely you think it is that this is already named somewhere, and the exact phrasing you'd search to find out
