So I've more or less settled on the homeostatic property cluster story about natural kinds, in a sharpened form I've ended up with: a class is a natural kind, to a degree, just when some mechanism keeps its members bunching together in property space across the disturbances that actually arise within a causal regime, and the degree of kindhood tracks how much you gain by projecting a property from sampled members to unsampled ones under interventions in that regime. Kindhood on this view is regime-relative and graded, and it's meant to earn its keep by paying for induction — that payoff matters to me far more than the intuitions about water and jade that these debates always start with.

What I want from you is what would count against it. Not recieved objections from survey articles: I want the things that should actually move me, ranked from fatal down to merely awkward, and for each one, whether a defender of the view would concede it as counting against them or rightly wave it off as aimed at a version nobody holds. Be honest about whether the regime-relativity and the degrees talk have already insulated it into something that can't lose, and if they have, whether that's a defect or just what you'd expect from a view about a graded phenomenon.

Please work in the case I keep turning over and can't get anyone to engage with, which I've been calling the loop case: imagine a family of things studied by a mature science where, within any single causal regime, members cluster tightly and homeostatically; neighbouring regimes are indistinguishable, so the cluster center drifts continuously as you move regime to regime; the drift closes back on itself, so the two regimes furthest apart along the loop share none of the diagnostic properties; and there's no microstructure common to all of them. A kind-property analogue of a ring species, basically. Also tell me which of the worries I already half-believe actually bite: externally maintained clusters, where the bunching is produced by a shared environmental filter rather than anything intrinsic to the members; the promiscuity worry that any old statistical cluster now qualifies; the worry that the view quietly collapses into microstructural essentialism plus noise; historical kinds like species, where what holds the cluster together is genealogy rather than similarity; and the worry that nobody has shown kindhood as I've defined it predicts projectibility better than plain base-rate reasoning over the properties we happened to measure. Add ones I haven't thought of, including anything that says the question itself is ill-posed, and remember I'm least persuaded by intuition-mongering and most persuaded by anything showing the induction payoff doesn't materialise.

And if the request is confused — if my formulation is internally inconsistent, or if "what counts against it" has no stable answer for a view this easy to restate — say that plainly instead of producing eight diplomatic paragraphs.
