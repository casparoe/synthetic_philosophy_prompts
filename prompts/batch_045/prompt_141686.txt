Two friends of mine got into an argument at a barbecue on Saturday and I've been going over it since, because I honestly can't work out who had the better of it. It started when my flatmate Raj described a dream about wandering around a building he'd never seen, looking for an exit, and said it felt like it went on for hours. Theo said, more or less out of nowhere, that there was probably nothing it was like for Raj while he was asleep — that the dream wasn't an experience Raj had during the night at all. He said he'd picked this up from a philosophy essay his sister sent him: the thought being that all that really exists is Raj waking up with an impression of having dreamt, and there's nothing to check that impression against, because while it was supposedly going on Raj wasn't available to anybody, including himself. He made a lot of the hours thing: the dream can't have lasted hours and can't have lasted four minutes either, so the report isn't a measurement of anything. (1) Mina — she's a night-shift nurse, if that matters, she's seen plenty of sleep weirdness — said this was just memory skepticism in a fancy hat: when you remember a concert from last month nobody suggests the concert might not have been an experience, on the grounds that all you've got now is a memory. (2) Theo said the concert was checkable while it was happening, there were other people there and someone has it on their phone, and that's exactly the difference — with a dream there is no 'while it was happening' that anyone, the dreamer included, could have consulted. (3) Mina said that's just false: they wake people up during REM sleep and most of the time the person reports a dream, some people physically act their dreams out, and lucid dreamers can move their eyes in a pattern they agreed on beforehand from inside the dream while the machines say they're still asleep — and deciding to do that and doing it is an action, not a memory. (4) Theo said all that shows is that sleep produces stuff that later turns into a dream report; it doesn't show a stream of consciousness during the night as opposed to something manufactured at the moment of waking, which is the entire question. And he said the concert case actually helps him: if it turned out Theo had never been to the concert and someone had planted the memory in him last week, we'd say he hadn't experienced it.

(5) Then Mina produced a thing I'd never heard before. Suppose you could record whatever goes on in a sleeping person's brain and play it into another sleeper who otherwise had no dreams at all, so that the second person wakes up with exactly the same apparent memory. She said: if we'd say the second person didn't really dream, they just got handed a memory, then the first person has to be different somehow, and Theo has to say what that difference is. (6) Theo said fine, there is a difference, just not the one she wants — the first person's report comes from the machinery that normally produces dream reports and the second person's doesn't, which is a difference in causation, not a difference in whether an experience occurred. Then he turned it round with a case of his own: suppose a machine reads a coherent story out of Raj's brain at 3am, a whole sequence of being chased through a building, and then at 7am Raj wakes up and reports a completely unrelated dream and has no sense of the 3am one whatsoever. If dreams are experiences happening in the night, Theo said, you have to say Raj had an experience he has no access to at all, which he thinks is just a misuse of the word; and if you won't say that, you've admitted the waking report is doing all the work. (7) Mina said that only shows memory is unreliable, same as in waking life — you forget plenty of things that happened to you, that doesn't mean they didn't happen. (8) Theo said with waking life you can normally corroborate a forgotten event some other way, whereas here the only candidate corroborator points at something the dreamer has nothing to do with, so it isn't corroborating his experience at all. Somewhere in the middle Mina said the whole dispute was verbal and asked what would actually be different in the world either way, and Theo said it matters because if dreams are experiences then there's this enormous class of experiences nobody has ever been able to observe or compare notes about, and if they aren't then treating dream reports as evidence about sleep is built on sand. A kid knocked a glass over around then and we never got back to it.

I kept switching sides while they were talking, so I'd rather not be told who won overall — could you go through the moves one at a time and say who's right about which specific bit, roughly rating each move from clearly wrong to clearly right and saying why? I'd especially like to know whether Mina's recording machine actually helps her or hands the argument to Theo, whether the 3am readout case is really the problem for her that he takes it to be, and what, if anything, both of them are missing. I've got no philosophy background whatsoever — I dropped out of a business course — so please don't assume I know any of the jargon.
