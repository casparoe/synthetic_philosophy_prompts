Base case:

Sealed-batch chicken. A processor slaughters in batches of 10,000 birds. The batch count for a production window is fixed by a contract signed six weeks ahead, computed from a moving average of the previous quarter's demand and then rounded to the nearest whole batch. Quarterly demand is around 10 million birds. So if I buy one chicken today: it can't affect the current window (already contracted); it nudges next quarter's average by one unit out of ten million; and because of the rounding it does anything at all only if that nudge happens to push the average across a batch boundary — a roughly 1-in-10,000 shot at triggering 10,000 extra birds. Expected effect comes out at about one bird. My purchase is basically a lottery ticket.

Three variants, and this is where I'm stuck:

1. Opaque: as above — I don't know where the tally sits, so there's a genuine 1-in-10,000 chance.
2. Transparent: same lumpy mechanism, but the processor publishes the running tally, so I can look it up and see for certain that today my purchase lands mid-block and changes nothing.
3. Smooth: no batches, no rounding — breeding and slaughter adjust continuously, so my purchase certainly makes a difference, but the difference is a microscopic increment of crowding and handling spread across the whole flock, something no bird and no person could ever notice, with the same expected magnitude as in case 1.
