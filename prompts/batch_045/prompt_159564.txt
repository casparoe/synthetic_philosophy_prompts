I am designing a graduate seminar on machine ethics—the field concerned with how to implement ethical reasoning in machines—and I keep returning to the public debate over how autonomous vehicles should be programmed for unavoidable collisions. The case I want analyzed is the juxtaposition of two real responses to that problem. The second is the set of national ethics guidelines for automated driving issued in the late 2010s by a European commission; for this analysis, take the guidelines to hold that an automated system may not, in an unavoidable accident, decide on the basis of personal characteristics such as age, gender, or physical condition, and may not simply aggregate and offset distinct lives against one another, while permitting only general programming aimed at reducing the overall number of injuries.
