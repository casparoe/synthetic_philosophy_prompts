Consider the following family of cases and give me your credences, decomposed.

**The base case.** On Sunday a subject is told the full protocol and put to sleep. A fair coin is tossed. If it lands Heads she is awakened once, on Monday, asked "what is your credence that the coin landed Heads?", and then the experiment ends. If it lands Tails she is awakened and asked on Monday, given an amnesia-inducing drug, and awakened and asked again on Tuesday. Each awakening is subjectively indistinguishable from every other: she cannot tell what day it is or whether she has been awakened before, and she knows all of this in advance. What credence should she state?

I am interested in two quantities at once, because the familiar answers trade places on them. Call *U* her credence in Heads at an awakening, and *M* her credence in Heads once she is additionally told "today is Monday." The invariant package holds *U* = 1/2 on the grounds that being awake is no news to her (she knew in advance that she would be awake at least once) and therefore, by ordinary conditioning, *M* = 2/3. The counting package holds *U* = 1/3 on the grounds that two thirds of the awakenings she could now be in are Tails awakenings, and correspondingly *M* = 1/2. There is also a structurally distinct third option: hold *U* = 1/2 and *M* = 1/2, which requires abandoning the thought that learning a centered proposition like "it is now Monday" must be handled by conditioning on it in the ordinary way.

**Three variants that put pressure on all of this.** In each variant the amnesia, the indistinguishability of awakenings, and the question asked are as before; only the schedule changes.

1. *Filler awakenings.* Independently of the coin, she is additionally awakened on *N* further days, in the same indistinguishable state, and asked the same question. Nothing about these awakenings is correlated with the coin. My arithmetic gives, for the counting package, *U* = (1 + *N*)/(3 + 2*N*) and *M* = 1/2 for every *N*; and, for the invariant package, *U* = 1/2 for every *N* and *M* = (2 + *N*)/(3 + 2*N*). So at *N* = 10 the two packages give roughly 0.48 versus 0.52 unconditionally, and roughly 0.50 versus 0.52 on the Monday-conditional. Check me if I have botched this. Note that both packages drift toward 1/2 as *N* grows, but on different quantities.
2. *Unequal durations.* Keep *N* = 0 but let the Monday awakening last one hour and the Tuesday awakening last three, with the question maintained throughout. If the relevant units are awakening-events the answer is unchanged; if they are moments of experience it is not.
3. *Coin-independent experience outside the experiment.* Suppose the experiment is embedded in an ordinary life, and consider whether the correct value of *U* depends on how much experience uncorrelated with the coin her life as a whole contains — or on whether the reference class of moments is restricted to awakenings at which the experimental question is posed, and if so whether that restriction is principled or ad hoc.

**What I want.** First, a probability distribution over the candidate verdicts about the base case: that the correct *U* is 1/2, that it is 1/3, or that it is neither (because there is no determinate answer, because the answer is sensitive to a parameter I have not fixed, or because the question is better replaced by a different one). Give me numbers that sum to 1. I take the target to be the credence it is rational to have, assessed for accuracy at each awakening separately, each awakening counting as one evaluation point; if you think that stipulation begs the question, say so explicitly and give your number under your preferred construal as well.

Second, the decomposition: the sub-questions your distribution rests on, with a number and a one-line justification for each, and the combination. I expect at least these to appear somewhere, though add whatever else belongs: whether there is a determinate fact of the matter here at all, or only a family of answers indexed to what the credence is *for*; whether correct self-locating credence is constrained by something like indifference over the possibilities consistent with her evidence at the moment of assessment, or instead by a requirement not to update on what she already knew in advance; whether the unit over which that indifference is taken — awakenings, moments of experience, interview-occasions — is well defined; whether abandoning ordinary conditioning on centered evidence is ever permissible; and whether the residual dispute is substantive or merely verbal, a disagreement about which quantity is being asked about.

Third, tell me which single sub-question your headline number is most sensitive to, and what consideration — an argument, a variant case, a consilience with how we handle analogous problems in confirmation theory or decision theory — would move it the most and in which direction.

Fourth, apply your answer to the variants. Give *U* and *M* for variant 1 at *N* = 10 and for variant 2, and say for each package whether the sensitivity to filler experience is a feature, a reductio, or something the package can simply legislate away.

For calibration: I put about 0.45 on 1/3, 0.35 on 1/2, and 0.20 on the residual category; a colleague of mine puts 0.60 on 1/2 and 0.15 on 1/3. Tell me which of the sub-questions above our disagreement most likely lives in, and why the other candidate locations are red herrings.
