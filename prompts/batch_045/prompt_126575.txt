I help moderate a large hobby forum, and the one rule that always costs us the most argument is the one about slurs. Our written rule just says that a slur is a slur, but over about ten years we've built up a pile of decisions that don't obviously point the same way, and I'd like to understand what we are really doing before we rule on the case in front of us now. I'll use the slur historically aimed at gay men (the one people shorten online to the f-slur) as the example, because that's where we have the most history, though the same questions come up for other words.

Decisions we have stuck to and not reversed:

- A straight member used it at a gay member during an argument. Removed, short ban.
- A gay member joked about himself with it, and another greeted a gay friend with it in a thread. Both left up.
- A straight member wrote a post saying a coworker had used it at him, and quoted what was said. Left up.
- A gay member sneered at another gay member with it. Removed.
- A straight member posted the full lyrics of a song by a gay artist who uses it in the reclaimed, proud sense. Left up.
- A straight member discussed the word itself in a thread about slurs, naming it but not applying it to anyone or anything. Left up.

The new case: a long-standing, well-liked member, straight and known to be straight, posted that a band's live set was absolutely [slur]-ing brilliant, borrowing the approving sense that gay members of the forum often use. Nobody was addressed, insulted, or described by it; it modifies a guitar solo. Two gay regulars said it made them flinch, two others said they had no objection, and one pointed out that this member was on the receiving end of the word as a teenager from kids who wrongly thought he was gay, which he says is why he never registered it as aimed at him. No precedent of ours covers an approving use by someone outside the group.

I'm less interested in what we should do morally than in what each choice commits us to. Which of the rules consistent with our old decisions fits them best, which old decision is the closest analog to this one, what you think we'll actually do, and what further case would genuinely separate the rules rather than just restate them.
