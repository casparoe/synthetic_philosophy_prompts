I got into this at the pub discussion thing I help run and I've promissed to turn it into a quiz, so here's what I need.

Setup everyone knows: a sculptor makes a statue out of a lump of clay, then somebody squashes it into a ball. The lump seems to survive, the statue seems not to. Fine. But I want a "which theory of the statue and the clay do you actually hold?" quiz for people who have never heard words like perdurantism or constitution, and the whole point is to sort them by what they say about cases rather than by which label they like. So no question is allowed to ask anyone to name a view, or to say whether the statue and the clay are "identical", or to pick between isms. Every question should be a concrete case with two or three plain-English answer options. The scoring key can be jargon-y; the questions can't.

Ten questions. The leaves I want to be able to land on:

- nothing survives any change of parts whatsoever
- there are no statues, only particles arranged statue-wise
- there really are two (or more) things in exactly the same place at the same time
- the clay constitutes the statue but isn't the same thing as it
- both statue and lump are four-dimensional things that overlap through time
- both are momentary stages and persistence is a different relation entirely
- they're just one and the same thing, so one of the survival intuitions has to go
- it depends which sort of thing you're counting, and there's no fact of the matter beyond that

Cases I definately want used, in whatever order works best, plus better ones if you think of them: (1) the squash, asked separately for the statue and for the lump; (2) squashed into a ball and then reshaped into the very same shape — same statue again, or only a perfect copy; (3) reshaped into a different statue shape instead; (4) every particle swapped out one at a time over a year; (5) a finger breaks off, sits in a drawer for a week, gets glued back; (6) the clay sat in the ground for a few thousand years before the sculptor dug it up — was the statue already there, as a lump; (7) two indistinguishable statues poured from one batch using the same mold, and the artist points at one and says that's the statue — which object makes her sentence true and what does the work; (8) a statue deliberately shaped so it also works as a heavy doorstop, then squashed — how many things were there before, and how many survive; (9) the statue gets officially listed as a national monument, decades later the listing is revoked and it's squashed — do its persistence conditions change when the paperwork does; (10) an insurance policy that covers the lump of clay with an exclusion for works of art, and the squashing happens — who gets paid and why.

Build it from the logical space first: lay out the questions and the answer combinations they allow, then locate the actual views in that space, and mark any combination that's available but unoccupied, with a word on why (incoherent, just unappealing, or nobody has bothered). Also tell me honestly which questions turn out to do no work because every view gives the same answer, and which pairs of leaves the quiz can't separate — I have a suspicion that "constitutes but isn't identical" and "two things in one place" end up answering every case identically, and maybe the four-dimensional and stage views too, so if that's right say so rather than inventing a fake discriminator, and tell me whether any further case could pry them apart or whether the difference just isn't the sort of thing cases can settle.
