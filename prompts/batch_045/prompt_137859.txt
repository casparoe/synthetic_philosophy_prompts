I'm a software engineer on a long work assignment in a city whose everyday norms differ sharply from the ones I grew up with: queues here are loose and pushy, tipping is not expected, and people cross wherever they like rather than at corners. A colleague ended an argument about whether I should adapt by saying these conventions are exactly like the rule of the road. Driving on the right is arbitrary in content, since driving on the left would coordinate just as well, but once everyone expects right, you have a genuine obligation to drive right, and visitors have it too. So, he concluded, I owe it to locals to queue, tip, and cross as they do for as long as I'm here.

I would like an audit of that analogy rather than a verdict on the underlying question. Please lay out the mapping explicitly, as a correspondence table: arbitrary content, mutual expectations, the payoff from coordinating, the harm from deviating, the role of law and enforcement, the situation of the visitor. Then tell me which of those features tipping, queueing, and jaywalking actually share with the road-rule case and which they lack. My own sense is that the three are different kinds of thing: queueing looks like genuine coordination, tipping looks like a transfer to a particular worker rather than an equilibrium I join, and crossing the street looks like a rule about risk that happens to be partly codified in law.

Two complications I want handled. First, apply what I think of as the invisible-deviation test: if I skip the tip, melt into a loose queue, or cross against the light at three in the morning on an empty street where no local sees me and no one is affected, does the analogy still generate an obligation? If the road-rule case survives that test and the others don't, that seems to matter a great deal. Second, take the visitor's excuse seriously and tell me whether it is special pleading or whether it exposes a real gap: I did not help set the local equilibrium, my single deviation changes nothing, and I could endorse a rule permitting discreet opt-out by outsiders without the convention collapsing.

Does my colleague's conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table? Does it survive for all three norms or only for some, and is there a better analogy for the ones the road-rule picture does not fit? I have no philosophy training, so please define any term of art you rely on.
