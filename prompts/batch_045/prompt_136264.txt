I'm writing a paper on whether Kant's argument for a federation of free states can be extended to international institutions that coerce without ever declaring war. I need you to play out the extension rather than exegete the text, so take the following as given: the federation he argues for is a voluntary and dissoluble union of republican states under public right, explicitly not a world state; and the condition of states outside such a union counts for him as a condition of war even when no shots are being fired.

Put him in front of three arrangements he never saw:

(i) An assembly of nearly all the world's states whose binding enforcement decisions — sanctions, peacekeeping mandates, and, rarely, armed intervention — can be blocked in advance by any one of five great powers that hold a permanent veto, and whose resolutions bind non-members only if those states consent.

(ii) A Compact of democratic states founded in the 2030s: membership conditional on verified competitive elections, no power of taxation, no coercion of non-members, but authority by two-thirds vote to impose financial sanctions on a member and to deploy a standing force contributed by the others in a declared emergency.

(iii) A permanent standoff between two nuclear-armed blocs in which neither attacks the other because each could annihilate it, and in which a network of treaties, inspections and hotlines has kept the peace for decades longer than any European peace he knew.

For each, say whether he would treat it as a step toward the federation, a relapse into the state of nature, or something his framework has no vocabulary for — and give the reasoning that makes it come out that way rather than the other two. Then tell me which of the three he would find hardest, and identify the point at which extending his argument would force him to give up something he actually held: say what that commitment is, why these cases strain it, and what he would have to say instead. Where you think more than one answer is defensible on his principles, say so and explain what the disagreement would turn on.
