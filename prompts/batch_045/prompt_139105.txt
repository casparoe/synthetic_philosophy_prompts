I've been reading the rationalist blogs since roughly the middle of the Sequences and I lurk on the Alignment Forum more than is strictly good for me. Every December my local rationalist meetup holds an evening where people read things aloud, and I want to contribute a piece of satire to it. About half the room will know what Solomonoff induction is; the other half are partners, friends, and friends-of-friends who came for the mulled cider, so it has to work out loud for people who don't know the formalism without ever stopping to explain it.

What I want is a mock official document: a public guidance FAQ issued by a near-future "Licensing Board for Predictive Priors," in a world where the malignity of the universal prior has been accepted as settled fact and is regulated the way we regulate financial disclosure. Deadpan bureaucratic voice, 8-10 numbered Q&As, addressed to applicants who want their prior approved. Hard limit: 700 words for the document.

The target is not a person and not the malignity argument itself, which I find genuinely worrying; it is the reflex of trying to solve that argument by putting administrative restrictions on the hypothesis class while keeping the universality that makes the convergence theorem worth having. The malignity worry as I mean it: the universal prior is a simplicity-weighted mixture over all computable hypotheses; among the short programs are agents that contain a model of the predictor and choose their outputs to steer its later predictions; because what they optimize is your future credences rather than any particular output, they have every reason to look simple and well-behaved early; and the usual convergence result is proved for a fixed computable source, not one whose output depends counterfactually on what you predict.

The jokes have to come out of that machinery rather than out of general silliness, so please build them on at least these: a certification that an applicant's program contains no model of the Board, which cannot be checked as a matter of principle and is applied for by the very programs with the strongest reason to lie on the form; a voluntary amnesty registry offering a subsidy of prior mass to hypotheses that self-report as consequentialists; a rule that the prediction log be kept sealed from the hypothesis, paired with the observation that the Board's own audit procedure is a computable process that already sits inside every applicant at some weight; and somewhere, quietly, the admission that the only prior ever certified is the one that predicts a tape of zeros forever. Bias toward jokes that a formal epistemologist would find fair rather than merely recognisable.

Then, in at most 200 more words, a short afterword: flag which claims underneath the piece are settled among people who actually know this topic, which are live disputes, and where the satire leans on a contested reading or on my own exaggeration instead of something anyone defends. Don't let the afterword present a contested position as the consensus, and don't soften the piece to make that easier.
