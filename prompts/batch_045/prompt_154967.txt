I am a novelist, and for close to twenty years I have written fiction in which most of the work happens inside a character's head. Something about that work has bothered me for a long time, and I have no word for it. I would be grateful if you could tell me whether there is one.

Here is the observation. There are two ways I can render a stretch of a character's life, and they do not agree with each other.

The first is what I think of as the absorbed mode. A cellist is halfway through a difficult passage. If I write that scene honestly — that is, if I write what I myself have to do in order to play anything at all — there is almost nothing to report: the passage's next demand, the correction of a finger, and nothing besides. There is no continuous film, no one watching, no self who is having the experience. The prose works precisely when I refrain from adding a layer of noticing to it; the moment I give the character an inner spectator, the playing falls apart on the page, and I suspect it would fall apart in the room as well.

The second is the retrospective mode, and it is unavoidable. Pages later, or in conversation, or at the point where the character is deciding whether to accept an engagement, the same stretch of playing turns up as something quite different: a continuous, unified, well-lit scene, viewed from one point, with a motive at the front of it, a difficulty in the middle, and a resolution at the end. That version has the shape of a story. And this is the part I cannot get past: it is the version with consequences. The character accepts or refuses the engagement on the strength of it. It is also the version my readers treat as the character's actual mental life — when I write a long scene entirely in the absorbed mode, readers tell me they could not get inside the character, as if the presence of the story-shaped layer were the very mark of consciousness.

A second instance, from my own case. I am asked in interviews how I wrote a particular chapter, and I answer fluently, in sentences that arrive already shaped, often with an anecdote of the sort that interviewers like. I know while I am producing it that the account is being composed in the telling. I will repeat it for years. It will decide what I write next. And it is not a description of the hours.

So the thing I want a name for is not the familiar claim that memory distorts. My claim is stronger and stranger: the story-shaped item is not a corrupted record of the absorbed stretch at all. It is an article manufactured for a consumer downstream — deliberation, confession, being held to account — and it is authoritative for that consumer, and it is systematically silent about whatever produced the playing, or the chapter. A readout, as I would put it, rather than a log. Its authority is also oddly self-certifying: the manufactured article is exactly what we point to when we claim to know what we were conscious of, and its absence is exactly what we call being unconscious of something.

What I have found so far does not fit. Someone at a festival told me that philosophers have a phrase for roughly this — that consciousness is a kind of user illusion, an interface rather than a report of what the machinery is doing — which sounded like my problem exactly. But everything I managed to read on that theme turned out to be about the thinness of the visual field: how little of the world we see, how much is filled in, how badly we notice large changes. That is not my difficulty. I do not care that the world is thinner than it looks. I care that an account with the narrative shape of a life is manufactured after the fact, is taken to be the explanation of the act, and yet is plainly not a description of the process that produced it.

I have a working name borrowed from my own trade — the draft effect, since a finished chapter is not a record of the hours that made it, is often wildly misleading about them, is nonetheless the thing that governs what comes next, and is the thing a reader would point to if asked what the writing was. But I am not attached to it, and I suspect it is either too narrow or too broad.

Two requests about form, if you will indulge them. First, I have no library where I am: please give me no bibliographies, reading lists, titles, or citations, only the carving. Second, I am not asking for a label to stick on this; I want to know what precisely the thing is, what it is not, and where its edges lie. Please answer in exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no preamble and no closing remark:

1. Whether the phenomenon has an established name, given in plain terms.
2. The nearest established concept, and precisely where it fails to cover this case.
3. A second candidate, and where that one fails.
4. Whether the draft effect is a good name, and if not, the name and definition you would propose, together with the single test that separates the phenomenon from its neighbours.
5. A boundary case: something that looks like this but is not it, or is it but does not look like it, with the reason why.
