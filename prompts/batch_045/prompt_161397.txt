Here is the question I gave the students:

Question 3 (careenium and symbols). In Hofstadter's 1982 essay on the careenium, a careenium is a billiard-table-like enclosure filled with an enormous number of tiny magnetic balls called symms. The symms bounce around (careen) off one another and off the walls. The walls' bounciness varies with the state of the world outside the careenium, so the changing bounciness is how information about the outside world gets into the system. If you zoom out, the swirling symms form larger, temporarily stable clumps and patterns; Hofstadter calls these higher-level formations symmballs, and they are meant to be analogous to symbols or concepts. The careenium as a whole is analogous to a cranium, the symms to the neural substrate, and the symmballs to neural representations or symbols. Hofstadter uses this to ask, in effect, who shoves whom around inside the careenium: do the symmballs (symbols) push the symms (substrate) around, or is it always the symms pushing symms, with symmball-talk being only a higher-level redescription?

(a) Explain the sense in which, in this original setup, symmballs might be said to push the substrate around, and the sense in which they do not. Be clear about the difference between the microphysical story and the symbol-level story.

(b) Now consider a modified variant I'll call the feedback careenium. In addition to the walls' bounciness being set by the external environment, suppose the table is coated with a special varnish that controls how magnetic the symms are to one another. The varnish does not respond to individual symm collisions directly. Instead, every 100 milliseconds a scanner samples the currently dominant global symmball pattern — say, the symmball that represents a tiger, or the self-symmball — and then the varnish sets the inter-symm magnetism for the next interval to a value that depends on which symmball is dominant. So the presence of a certain symmball makes the symms stick together more (or less) during the next interval.

In the feedback careenium, does the dominant symmball now push the substrate around in a way that counts as genuine top-down or downward causation? Or is it still, on closer inspection, just symms pushing symms once the varnish and scanner are themselves described at the physical level? Explain how your answer bears on the original question of whether the I — understood as a self-symmball — can shove the symms around, and say what you think would settle the dispute. Do not cite outside sources; just reason from the analogy as described.

And here is the student's answer, verbatim:

In Hofstadter's original careenium, the symms are tiny magnetic balls bouncing around inside a table (the cranium). The walls' bounciness encodes the outside world, so sensory info gets in. When you zoom out, the clumps of symms form symmballs, which are like symbols or concepts. The question who shoves whom is about whether the symbol level can push the neural substrate around.

(a) I think the answer is that the symmballs do push the substrate around. Even though at micro level it's always one symm bumping another, the pattern of symms — the symmball — is what determines which bumps happen next. If you don't cite the symmball, you cannot predict the trajectories. So the symmballs have causal power over and above the individual symms; they are real patterns that exert downward causation. Hofstadter's whole point is that the I is a symmball, and it shoves the symms around; otherwise the strange loop would be epiphenomenal. Also, in his original setup the walls change bounciness depending on the symmballs present, so the careenium already has top-down feedback: the symbol level alters the substrate's environment, which then alters the symms. Thus symbol-level causation is literally built into the original analogy.

(b) In the feedback careenium, things are even clearer. The scanner samples the dominant symmball and the varnish resets the magnetism. Now the symmball literally reaches down and changes the forces between symms. That is genuine top-down causation, not just redescription: the dominant symmball is an additional cause, because the magnetism for the next 100 ms depends on the symmball, not on any particular symm collision. This also settles the original dispute about the I: the self-symmball directly causes the symms to fire, so the arrow of causation goes from symbol to substrate, not the other way around. Hofstadter (and Dennett, in the careenium essay) endorse this: they think the self is a real causal agent implemented in the symms. What would settle the dispute is just noticing that the varnish depends on the symmball, because then everyone should agree there is top-down causation.
