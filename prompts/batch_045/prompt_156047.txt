I'm assembling a graduate seminar on persistence and I want a case that runs the usual dialectic backwards. The standard move is to press fission on the endurantist: one continuant divides into two, the four-dimensionalist can say that both continuations are things which were the original, and the endurantist must either pick arbitrarily or deny that either survives. I want the mirror image: a case in which the endurantist can affirm, strictly and literally, the identity that ordinary practice presupposes, while the perdurantist — granted the full worm-theoretic apparatus: unrestricted composition over time, scattered and temporally gappy objects, and the usual temporal-part paraphrases of tensed predication — cannot do so without paying for it.

Specifications. Many-to-one rather than one-to-many: two antecedently existing continuants become one, and the question is whether the resulting thing is numerically identical with one of them. Constraints: (1) ordinary objects or institutions, told in a single paragraph; no fission, no branching persons, no time travel or exotic physics. (2) Something must hang on the answer — an undertaking, obligation, or claim of responsibility that is discharged only if the later thing literally is the earlier one. (3) The verdict must not be fixed by stipulation or convention: no contract clause, statute, or charter declaring which entity continues; the asymmetry between the two inputs has to be earned by the facts of the case. (4) No strawman: the perdurantist keeps the paraphrase machinery and may posit scattered objects.

After the scenario, state the yes-or-no question exactly and derive each verdict from the theory rather than asserting it, then name the feature doing the work. Then say what happens to both verdicts if the same case is run backwards, since a fusion reversed is a fission; give the perdurantist's three best replies and what each of them costs; and address whether the fallback that the earlier thing is a temporal part of the later one vindicates the ordinary claim or merely restates its denial. Close with a committed judgement: does the case genuinely invert the dialectic, or does the perdurantist escape at the price of paraphrase? Take a side, give your reasons, and flag what you are least confident about.
