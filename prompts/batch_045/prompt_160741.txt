Next week I'm giving a short paper to a longtermist research group and I want Q&A prep, not a defence of the view.

The thesis: taking the simulation argument seriously should dampen longtermist fanaticism. Not "nothing matters" — just that once you put real credence on us being in a sim, the expected value of far-future work stops being astronomically dominant and near-term causes are back in the running.

My sketch, in four steps: (1) most sims, if there are any, are cheap and short — bounded ancestor-simulations run on a small slice of a much bigger base reality; (2) so the future we can affect is bounded by the sim's runtime, not by the physically colonisable universe, which knocks the headline numbers down by orders of magnitude; (3) whatever simulated people experience matters just as much as the real thing, so this is a smaller-numbers argument, not a nihilist one; (4) with smaller numbers, existential-risk work has to compete with global health and animal welfare on ordinary cost-effectiveness, and sometimes loses.

Audience: a dozen or so longtermist/EA researchers who already buy the expected-value framing and the astronomical-future premise, three of whom do anthropics professionally and know sim-argument stuff cold; two moral philosophers who are generally hostile to fanaticism and think our credences about payoff sizes that big are hopelessly unreliable anyway; and one funder who mainly wants to know whether this should change grantmaking.

Give me the five objections I'll actually get from that room, in the order they'll arrive, each in the voice and vocabulary of whoever would raise it, with a two-minute reply I can give and a note on where that reply is soft. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. My own worry is that the "many runs of the same sim" point cuts against me — that branching amplifies my impact rather than dampening it — so tell me where that sits in the ordering and whether I should pre-empt it or let them spring it on me. I've been staring at this so long I can't seperate the objections I've answered from the ones I haven't. Exactly five numbered items, two or three sentences each, no preamble and no summing up at the end.
