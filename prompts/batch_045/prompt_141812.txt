I work as a biostatistician. I am fluent in the vocabulary I use daily — sampling frames and sampling units, cluster and length-biased sampling, informative versus ignorable designs, design weights and model-based weighting, estimands versus estimators, conditioning on selection, and long-run operating characteristics under hypothetical repeated realisation — and I have no philosophy training beyond what rubs off from statistical inference. I keep encountering the Sleeping Beauty problem and I cannot tell whether the dispute is one my vocabulary can settle or one it silently presupposes the answer to.

The case, as I have it: on Sunday a fair coin is flipped. If it lands heads, Beauty is awakened once, on Monday. If it lands tails, she is awakened on Monday and again on Tuesday. Each awakening is followed by a drug that erases her memory of it, so on waking she cannot tell whether it is Monday or Tuesday and has no evidence other than the design itself. She is asked for her credence that the coin landed heads. Thirders say one third, halfers say one half, and both sides have been saying it for about twenty-five years.

What I want from you:

First, restate the problem and both answers as faithfully as you can in statistical vocabulary. Be explicit about what maps to what: is the coin a parameter with a prior, is the awakening a sampled unit, is the run the cluster, is the tails branch an informative design that makes the sampling length-biased, and are the two answers two competing estimators of a single estimand or two different estimands? Where you have to choose between a design-based and a model-based reading, say so rather than picking one quietly.

Second, the residue report. Which parts of the argument translate cleanly; which translate only with distortion, and what exactly gets distorted; and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. In particular: is the thing they call *credence* an estimator's long-run frequency, a posterior over a parameter, or neither? Can self-locating content — *it is now Monday* — be a random variable in any sampling model, or does it name something my framework has no slot for? Does the halfer's claim that Beauty gains no relevant information on waking have any counterpart when the design is fully known to her in advance?

Third, a variant I invented and cannot find discussed, though tell me if it exists. On heads, Beauty is awakened once, for one hour. On tails, she is awakened twice, for six hours each. Amnesia between awakenings, no clock, no way to distinguish them, no way to tell how long she has been awake. Give me what your translation yields here. I expect it to yield more than one number — one from counting awakenings, one from counting runs, one from weighting by time — and I want each tied to a specific sampling unit and estimand in my vocabulary.

Finally: my framework returns an answer conditional on a chosen unit and estimand and has nothing to say about which choice is correct. Is the original halfer–thirder disagreement partly or wholly that kind of question rather than a question about inference, and if it is not, what would my vocabulary have to add in order to adjudicate the choice of unit?

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — the single most important thing you concluded — then give the reasoning. No preamble, no restatement of my question, and no citations or reading suggestions.
