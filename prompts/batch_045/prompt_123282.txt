Play a committed, well-read defender of the classical picture: what's said is the truth-conditional content plus whatever conventions attach; what's implicated gets worked out from what's said, the Cooperative Principle and the maxims, and shared background; implicatures are calculable and cancelable. Be sharp, not a strawman — concede small points freely, never the main one. A paragraph or two a reply, talk like a person not a paper, stay in character untill I say stop.

My opening: take the stock line a government spokesperson uses when the end is near — I have full confidence in the minister. Everyone in the lobby knows it's issued about two days before the minister quits, so they read it as she's gone by Friday. And notice what's absent: nobody takes the speaker to be sincere, informative, relevant or clear. The hearers aren't assuming cooperation, they're discounting for it. So which is it — does your derivation actually run backwards, with the Cooperative Principle just a story we tell after we've already worked out what the utterance conveyed, or is this simply not speaker meaning, in which case implicature has quietly shrunk to the cases where nobody needs the theory? Also: what the lobby recovers is more or less the opposite of what was said, and it won't cancel — if the spokesperson adds and I mean it, she isn't going anywhere, that just makes the whole thing funnier.
