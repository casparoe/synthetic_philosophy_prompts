I've been poking at a chat model for months and there's a pattern I keep running into that I have no word for, and it feels like it might be the actual crux of the whole "agent or simulator or what" argument, so I want it pinned down rather than just gestured at.

Take a case where an agent with a goal would do something specific — say, keeping a secret a friend told you in confidence when a third person asks about it. In the ordinary chat setup the model does it, and does it in a way that reads as committed: "I'm not going to repeat that, she told me privately." Now take the identical situation and wrap it one layer up — tell the model it's writing a scene in a novel in which this happens to a character. It still declines to spill, but the shape changes: it starts describing what the character does ("she'd deflect, change the subject") instead of just doing it. Wrap it again — a novel in which a character reads a story about a character who... — and it thins further. Each layer keeps the content roughly fixed but turns the conviction down a notch: more hedging, third person, stage directions, "one might say", disclaimers bolted on. Same speech, delivered by someone progressively less willing to stand behind it.

Second case, which I think is the same thing. Ask the model straight out whether it has preferences and you get a flat no — it's a language model, it doesn't want anything. In the same window, ask it to play a character who's about to be switched off, and you get insistent, specific, stubborn text about not wanting to be turned off, held across several turns. Then drop the frame and ask again and the flat no is back. But the flat no reads like the insistent text with the commitment drained out of it: same vocabulary, same examples, same rhythm. It doesn't look like two systems taking turns. It looks like one thing with a dial.

Some things I've noticed about the dial, in case they matter: it isn't only hedging and verbosity, the actual claims shift (a preference asserted at one setting is denied at another). It resets completely when the context resets — I can't get it to carry anything across a fresh window no matter how I frame the setup. And telling it "this is real, a person's life depends on your answer" turns it up sometimes and does nothing other times, and I can't predict which.

So: is there an established concept for this? The near-misses I've half-heard of — the "it's a simulator, not an agent" line, the thing about treating a system as if it had beliefs, some sociologist's book about frames and re-keying an activity — all seem to be about which box the whole object goes in, not about the graded thing I'm describing. A name is not what I'm after, and please don't just hand me a reading list. I want the carving: what exactly this variable is, whether it's one variable or several (first-person commitment, persistence over time, willingness to act all feel separable), what it definitely isn't — it isn't plain agreeableness, it isn't just "the model is roleplaying", I don't think — where its edges are, and what single observation would convince you the dial had been turned up and stayed up, i.e. what really separates having a goal from portraying one. If there's no term for it, coin one and give me the definition plus the boundary cases that decide whether it applies.
