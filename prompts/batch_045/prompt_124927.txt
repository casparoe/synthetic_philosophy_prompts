I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for something like fifteen years, and one of the standing fault lines in that scene is between people who think of themselves, roughly, as Bayesian belief-updaters and people who think that whole self-conception is naïve. In a comment thread on a group blog I follow, someone invoked Wittgenstein's late notes on certainty to support the second side, and characterized the view roughly like this:

"Wittgenstein's point is that justification has to bottom out somewhere. Any chain of reasons eventually reaches things you can't give evidence for, and Wittgenstein calls these hinges. Because nothing supports them, committing to a hinge can't be a matter of following the evidence; it has to be a leap, or at best a decision to stop asking. That's what he means when he says that underneath all our justified beliefs there is something we hold without justification. Two things follow. First, the rationalist ideal of a mind that proportions every belief to the evidence is incoherent: on this picture any belief can be held fast against any evidence, and if the hinges ever shift they shift for reasons internal to the practice, not because of what anyone observed. Second, since our hinges are just what our upbringing drilled into us, and another community could have been drilled into incompatible ones with nothing to adjudicate between them, this 'hinge epistemology' is relativism about reasons wearing a cardigan. It also pulls the rug out from under the usual anti-skeptical move: you can't answer the simulation or brain-in-a-vat worry by saying you know you have hands, because on Wittgenstein's account hinges aren't the sorts of things that can be known at all. They're what you take for granted, not what you know."

I don't trust myself to audit this. I've only ever encountered Wittgenstein through secondary summaries, and the commenter writes with a confidence I can't match. My priors say the relativism conclusion is a misreading, but I can't say where the derivation goes wrong. My own gloss had been that hinges are something like the fixed points of a language game — not arbitrary, not adopted by anyone, and not the kind of thing that is true or false relative to a culture — and I now suspect that gloss is exactly where I've gone wrong, since it sounds more like a formal system than like anything in Wittgenstein.

Could you go through the summary and give me an accuracy audit? In particular, I'd like you to distinguish: what a sympathetic Wittgensteinian or hinge epistemologist would endorse as stated; what is a compression a specialist would accept as shorthand but that would mislead a beginner; what rests on a conflation of two things the view keeps apart — I have a vague sense that "hinge" is being made to do duty for both "a proposition we cannot doubt" and "a rule that makes doubting anything intelligible in the first place," and that these get run together; and what is really a live objection that the commenter has presented as a settled refutation. I'd also like to know what to say about the Moore-style part at the end, since I can't tell whether Wittgenstein is denying that we know such things or denying that the sentence does the work Moore wanted it to do.
