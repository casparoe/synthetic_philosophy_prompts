Rough version: Jones is deliberating about whether to kill Smith, and unbeknownst to him a device — call it the Governor — is attached to his intention-forming process. The Governor is not a predictor. It doesn't scan for any prior sign, twitch, or change of mind; it monitors the process itself and is wired so that at the very instant Jones's intention not to kill Smith would cross the threshold into being formed, the Governor substitutes the intention to kill instead. It's also fallible: about 3% of the time it simply fails to fire, and then Jones refrains. In the actual sequence it never fires, Jones decides to kill Smith for his own reasons, and the Governor does nothing whatsoever. The design is meant to break both standard moves: there is no prior sign that could serve as a deterministic indicator (so the dilemma-style objection about begging the question against indeterminism has nothing to grab), and the only thing prevented is the very event that would have triggered the prevention (so it's hard to see what flicker of freedom is left, other than a 3% chance that the machine misfires).

A third says my Governor just is a deterministic mechanism sitting at the moment of intention-formation, so I've smuggled in the very thing the incompatibilist denies, only one step later than usual.
