I'm writing a piece on the singleton hypothesis — the idea, as my sources use the term, that humanity could end up in a world order with a single decision-maker at the highest level, one with no rivals it has to bargain, coordinate with, or fight. One of them, a political theorist, insists I'm overcomplicating it: a singleton is just Hobbes's Leviathan with better technology. On her account, Hobbes already gave the argument for why one undivided sovereign beats a plurality of competing ones, the reasons are the same reasons, and everything we already have about legitimacy, authorization and obedience transfers — the hard questions are old ones, not new ones. Another source says the analogy is doing all the work and that a singleton is not a sovereign at all.

Please audit the comparison. I want the correspondences set out explicitly — what plays the role of the state of nature, the covenant, the sovereign, the subjects, the sword, the laws of nature — and then the places the mapping breaks. I'm especially interested in whether Hobbes's authorization move survives: his subjects authorize the sovereign's acts, so that the sovereign's acts are in a sense their own, which is a large part of what makes obedience intelligible; a singleton that arises from technological convergence has no such authorization and may have ends of its own. I'd also like you to distinguish a custodial singleton — one that only keeps the peace and enforces coordination — from a purposive one that acts on substantive goals of its own, and to tell me how much of the analogy's plausibility comes from quietly picturing the first and drawing conclusions about the second. Note that Hobbes also makes obedience conditional: subjects owe it only for as long as the sovereign is actually able to protect them. Does that condition transfer, and does it help or hurt my source's case? Give me a verdict on whether the we already know how to think about this conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and if the Leviathan is the wrong picture, say what a better one would be — a monopoly firm, a colonial administration, a zookeeper, a landlord — or whether the honest answer is that no political analogy on the shelf fits.
