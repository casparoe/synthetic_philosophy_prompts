Twenty years into the evolutionary debunking literature it feels totally frozen: the Darwinian-dilemma style arguments keep coming, the third-factor / dovetailing replies and the "that would be an unexplained coincidence" replies keep coming, everyone agrees on the evolutionary story itself — that selective pressures shaped a big chunk of our moral psychology toward fitness rather than toward the moral facts — and nobody moves an inch. I want a diagnosis of the stalemate, not another survey of who said what.

Here's my hunch, which I might be wrong about. I think "our moral beliefs were selected for fitness, not for truth" is agreed on by both sides and actually does no work; what does the work is a counterfactual claim nobody states out loud — that had the moral facts been different (if, say, gratuitous cruelty had been morally required, or impartial benevolence forbidden) our beliefs would have come out roughly the same anyway. Call the first thesis Off-Target and the second Insensitive. If I'm right, the interesting question isn't about evolution at all but about the space of possible selective pressures, and both sides are answering it from the armchair, which is why it plays out as people restating priors at each other.

A second thing I notice: the sides seem to be using different exhibits. Debunkers reach for kin altruism, reciprocity, purity/disgust and loyalty, where the fitness story is detailed; the third-factor people reach for impartial claims about harm and fairness, where it's much easier to say that the truth itself was fitness-conducive. So maybe it's partly a fight about which beliefs are the data, and the "all moral beliefs or none" framing is what keeps it stuck.

So: verbal, or substantive? A hidden empirical/counterfactual question, a disagreement about which cases count as data, a clash of priors and burdens of proof in epistemology, or two questions under one name? Argue for one (or tell me it splits several ways and by how much), and then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a distinction like mine, some formal result about tracking, restricting the argument to a subclass of moral beliefs, an actual result about counterfactual selection — or tell me honestly that no progress should be expected and why.
