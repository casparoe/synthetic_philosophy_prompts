hey, i need a closing joke for a 15 minute conference talk on the wrong kind of reasons problem, and I want one the room actually laughs at rather than politely exhales at.

Context so the joke can be properly in the weeds: the talk defends something I call second-order wrongness. Everyone in the room accepts the basic move already — a demon threatens to wreck your life unless you believe that the number of stars is even, so you've got a reason to believe it, but it's the wrong kind of reason: it's state-given rather than object-given, it doesn't speak to whether the belief is correct, it isn't what you'd cite in defense of the belief, that sort of thing. What I argue on top of that is that the demon cases split, and the split does real work. Sometimes the threat gives you a reason to *bring it about* that you believe — go get hypnotized, read only the favorable evidence, whatever. Sometimes it gives you a reason to *be the sort of person who* already believes, without giving you any reason to make yourself believe. And which of those two you've got depends on what the demon is tracking: if the demon is checking the belief itself you get the second, and if the demon is checking something the belief disposes you to do — say whether you'd bet on it at long odds — you get the first. Only one of the two is, I claim, the right kind of wrong-kind reason, and definately it's not the one people assume.

My last slide is a photo of a really fed-up demon at a folding table with a clipboard and a little sign reading Compliance Office, Attitudes Division.

So: write me the joke. It has to turn on that distinction actually doing work — the laugh should come from someone watching the bring-it-about / be-such-that split land somewhere they didn't expect, not from a pun on reason and not from a philosopher walking into a bar. Tellable out loud in about thirty seconds, with no setup the audience has to reconstruct afterward. Then give me two or three sentences on which half of the distinction the punchline is leaning on, and on where it might just bewilder people instead of being funny.
