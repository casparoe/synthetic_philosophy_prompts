hey — I need a dialogue for a little zine our animal advocacy group hands out at the market stall, and I want it to be the one piece in there that doesn't read like a pamphlet. Everyone who walks past already knows the pitch; I want the thing that the people who already agree still argue about at 1am.

So: two characters, old friends, both vegan for a decade, both work on animal policy, and both reject speciesism — neither thinks species membership itself does moral work. They disagree about what's underneath. Mara: it's valenced experience all the way down, no principled bar on creating and using animals if the lives are good and nothing ends up worse off, killing only matters through what it does to welfare. Theo: a creature with a life of its own has some claim against being killed and used that isn't just a sum of moments, and the wrong of farming isn't only the suffering. Rename them if you like.

They're arguing over an actual application sitting on the table between them. A grazing company has applied for a new certification, and the facts of the application are these:

- hill pasture that can't grow crops, nothing wild displaced, herd capped so the land doesn't degrade
- welfare audited against the animals' own revealed choices — they push panels for warmth, shade, company — so "good life" means what they actually pick, not a vet's proxy
- each animal is killed instantly at roughly a third of its natural lifespan, timed to just before a steep welfare drop (joints, hooves), so the death prevents suffering the animal would otherwise have had
- one-for-one replacement: every slaughter funds a new animal, herd size stays constant
- a wild-offset: per animal bred, the company funds drought feeding and vaccination for wild animals nearby, so total wild suffering falls by more than the herd adds
- an open gate: any animal can walk out into a sanctuary paddock whenever it likes and will never be killed; it just isn't replaced

Make them both genuinely good at this. Mara has to actually answer the deprivation point — that the animal is worse off than it would have been had it not been killed, not merely better off than never existing — and the point that you don't get credit for the gift of existence you then cash in. Theo's best move is the replaceability reductio: if it works for cattle it works for people, since a carefully run human herd with wild-offsets would also come out net-positive, and flinching there requires naming a property that does the work, because "but they're human" is just speciesism pointed the other way. Mara has to meet that with something other than "but humans," and Theo has to meet the "if it's only a matter of magnitude, the offsets outweigh it" point and the "your principle is about keeping our hands clean, not about animals" point. Real back-and-forth — each one should be replying to what the other actually said, not to a rehearsed version of the opposing view.

End with them having pinned down the crux: the single claim each would have to give up in order to move, and what each would concretely change their mind about if the other turned out to be right about it. No winner, no tidy resolution. About 700–900 words, dialogue format, readable standing up. I keep going back and foward on which of them I actually am, so please don't tip it.
