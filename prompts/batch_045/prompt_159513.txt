My half-formed idea: “Darwinism” as Popper argued about it isn’t one theory but a layer cake of at least three different things glued together. There’s (1) the big historical claim that species are modified descendants of common ancestors and that all terrestrial life probably goes back to a few simple organisms; (2) a population-genetic / model-building layer where, if you specify things like mutation rates, selection coefficients, population size, and an independent measure of fitness, you get concrete predictions about allele frequencies and trait distributions; and (3) an adaptationist heuristic: assume that most widespread traits are there because natural selection put them there, and look for selectionist explanations first unless you have special reason not to. I think Popper’s early charge that Darwinism is “not a testable scientific theory, but a metaphysical research programme” was really mostly about (3), and maybe about a sloppy version of (2) where “fitness” is just defined by actual survival, so “survival of the fittest” looks like a tautology and he even called the theory “almost tautological.” His later recantation, where he says the theory can be formulated so it is far from tautological and is testable, was mostly about (2), and maybe (1) too. But (3), the look-for-adaptive-explanations framework, still looks untestable on its own: no failure of any particular trait to have a selectionist story seems to refute the heuristic itself, because you can always say the trait is a byproduct, a constraint, drift, or you haven’t found the right selective regime yet.

So the question I want the proposal to ask: can Popper’s own demarcation machinery handle a composite object like this—part testable model, part historical narrative, part untestable-but-heuristically-vital framework—or do we need to supplement falsifiability with some notion of “framework appraisal” (explanatory fertility, ability to generate novel testable models, resistance to ad hoc rescue, maybe something like degrees of empirical content inherited from the models a framework spawns)? A sharper way to put it: is Popper’s recantation best read as (a) a genuine retraction, (b) a relocation of the “metaphysical research programme” label from the whole theory to just the adaptationist heuristic / framework layer, or (c) a sign that his falsifiability criterion is too coarse to classify real historical sciences, so we should replace or supplement it? I want the question stated so it has a determinate answer (one of those options, or a defended taxonomy with conditions), not just an “explore the issue” prompt.

Why the existing answers leave it open: the obvious moves are (i) “Popper was just wrong early on and right later, end of story,” (ii) “he was always talking about different things, so there’s no real reversal,” and (iii) “falsifiability is useless for a mature historical science like evolutionary biology, so the whole framing is misguided.” I think (i) ignores that even the testable formulations of natural selection depend on a non-testable framework that tells you what counts as a selective explanation and what to ignore; (ii) doesn’t say what the different things are, or why the framework part can’t just be eliminated or seperated from the models; and (iii) gives up on Popper’s project instead of testing whether it can be refined. The proposal should explain why these don’t settle it.
