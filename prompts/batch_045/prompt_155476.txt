For a comparative seminar on the legal regulation of hate speech I want the specification before the doctrines. Please write the desiderata that any adequate theory of when the state may restrict hate speech must satisfy: the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the empirical or doctrinal facts it cannot contradict, each stated with the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and each marked as either non-negotiable or as a preference of one camp. Fix the purpose as follows: the theory is to be good enough to be drafted into a statutory definition that a court could apply without open-ended balancing in each individual case, so vagueness and appeals to weigh everything count against a requirement rather than for it.

Some leads I want handled explicitly, with the case behind each:

- the mirror case: the same proposition asserted by a member of a historically dominant group about a minority and by a member of that minority about the dominant group, where a theory must say what, if anything, licenses treating them differently, and why the difference is not merely a judgement about who currently needs protection more;
- the re-enactment case: the same words quoted, staged, or set to music by a speaker who denounces their content, where the harm complained of is said to survive the denunciation, so that the theory must locate the wrong somewhere other than the speaker's endorsement;
- the export case: an utterance lawful where it is made and received only where it is unlawful, and the converse, which forces a theory to say whether the harm sits with the speaker, with the audience, or with neither, and to live with the consequence;
- the cumulative case: very many individually trivial utterances whose aggregate effect is the only harm alleged;
- and the distinction between restricting what is said, restricting what it does, and restricting what it reveals about what the speaker will do next.

Mark which requirements are genuinely neutral between the American free-speech tradition and the European dignity-and-proportionality tradition, and which are commitments of one of them that have been passed off as demands of reason; identify the subset you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, say which one or two you would abandon and what abandoning them costs; and name the single requirement that excludes the largest share of the positions currently on offer. No bibliography, no case citations, no reading list — I want the arguments, not the sources. Please write in British English.
