I'm a software engineer with no philosophy background, working in reverse-engineering and model interpretability, and there's a distinction I keep running into that I don't have a word for.

When I pick apart a system I didn't write — a stripped binary, a trained network, someone else's distributed service — I end up with two very different descriptions of it. One is at the level of voltages, instructions, or weight matrices: complete in principle, since everything the system does follows from it, but computationally useless to me for working out what it will do next in any reasonable amount of time. The other is at the level of what the system is for: it is validating a token, it is keeping a running total, it treats the cache as stale. The second description is the one I actually reason with, and it is the one that lets me predict.

Three things I can't sort out, and I'd rather have the carving than a label:

First, sometimes I can construct two high-level descriptions of the same component that predict exactly the same behavior and are nonetheless incompatible — one says the module is tracking X and has registered that Y, the other says it is tracking Z and has registered that not-Y — and I cannot think of any observation that would separate them. Saying one of them is the real one and the other is merely a manner of speaking seems to have no content; but saying the meaning is just whatever I find convenient seems to throw away the fact that finding the interpretation was genuinely hard work, and that not every interpretation works.

Second, some interpretations fit every observation perfectly and are clearly empty: the system computes whatever its weights dispose it to compute; it outputs the string it outputs. They cost nothing to state and give me no leverage. I want a principled way to say why they are empty rather than trivially true.

Third, a colleague says the low-level description is the only one that is really there and the rest is projection. But if the low-level one is unuseable in practice, it's unclear to me what "really there" is doing.

Is there an established concept for the property that makes a high-level description earn its keep, and for the case where two incompatible ones tie on predictive power? If the phenomena I've described only partly match the established concepts, I'd like to know where they come apart, and if there's no name, I'd like a definition and its boundary cases rather than a coined word.
