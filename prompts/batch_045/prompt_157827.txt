Write a proposal for a 6,000-word philosophy-of-language term paper, one semester, on an idea I've only half-worked out. Grice's account of speaker meaning derives conversational implicatures from the assumption that the hearer may treat the speaker as cooperative — as observing the maxims, or at least as trying to. But much of what we confidently read off an utterance happens where the hearer knows the speaker is not cooperative in any ordinary sense: a press officer's non-denial, a hostile witness's "I don't recall," a negotiator's opening offer, a liar's literally true statement, and the double bluff in which a speaker says something true precisely because she expects a suspicious hearer to invert it. My provisional label for these is adversarial implicature: inferences the hearer draws only because she distrusts the speaker, and that the speaker can foresee, rely on, and exploit.

Turn this into a proposal that: (i) states the question so it has a determinate answer — I want both whether such cases are genuine speaker meaning at all (as opposed to hearer-side inference the speaker merely exploits) and whether they can be derived from a principle that keeps interpretive charity about the speaker's rationality (she chose this utterance over available alternatives for a reason the hearer can reconstruct) while dropping any assumption of cooperativeness or benevolence; (ii) explains why the obvious resources — the Cooperative Principle and its maxims, the Q/R-style principles of the neo-Gricean tradition, and relevance theory's cognitive principle — do not already settle it; (iii) gives the method I would actually execute over a semester, and argues for a case-driven conceptual approach over the alternatives I could have picked (running psycholinguistic experiments, building a signaling-game model, or surveying who said what); (iv) sets out the two or three possible conclusions and which considerations would decide between them; and (v) says honestly what would count as the project failing and whether it is too big for the word count.

Do not include a bibliography or any citations; I will add references myself. But name the positions and arguments specifically enough that I can tell what to look up.

Commit to a definite choice between the two framings — weakening the Cooperative Principle versus replacing it with a rationality-only principle — and to a definite working hypothesis about whether adversarial implicature is speaker meaning or merely exploited hearer-side inference; give a verdict on whether this is tractable in a semester. Argue for the choices rather than laying out the menu.

Use these sections in this order: "Question and working hypothesis" (~150 words), "Why the standard derivations leave it open" (~250), "Method and schedule" (~300), "Possible conclusions and deciding considerations" (~200), "Failure conditions and scope" (~150). About 1,000 words total.
