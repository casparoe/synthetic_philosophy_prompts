I work on feed ranking at a mid-size social app — I'm just an engineer, no philosophy background, so sorry if I butcher the vocabulary. There's a move that keeps getting made in my corner of the internet: ideas are genes. The version I keep running into goes roughly: cultural items replicate, mutate, and get selected; the scarce resource being competed for is attention; so what spreads is whatever is good at spreading, and the person carrying it is just a vehicle — the meme is the unit that matters, not the human. My staff engineer uses it to license two things: (1) there's no point trying to design our way out of harmful content, selection will just re-optimize around whatever we do, and (2) it's a category error to blame anyone — creators, users, or us — for what spreads, because the dynamics don't run through intentions. That second one is doing a lot of work in a design review I have to sit through this week.

I want an audit. Give me the explicit mapping — what plays the role of the gene, the organism, the phenotype, the environment, fitness, mutation, inheritance — and then where it breaks. I've got three specific worries I want you to take seriously rather than pat on the head:

- Cumulative selection is supposed to need a minimum replication fidelity, otherwise gains get washed out by copying noise (there's some threshold idea along those lines in molecular evolution). Cultural copying looks extremely low-fidelity and heavily blended — most shares are remixes, not copies. Is culture even above the threshold for Darwinian accumulation, or is it mostly one-generation shuffling?
- A lot of what looks like "selection for fitness" in popularity data might just be cumulative advantage plus drift — rich-get-richer dynamics generate the same power laws with no fitness differences at all. If so, "the fittest ideas win" may be a story told after the fact.
- Variation in genes is blind with respect to what would help. Cultural variation isn't: people edit toward what they already think works, which looks Lamarckian and might short-circuit the whole Darwinian machinery. And the "environment" here is partly a product of the things living in it — we ship a new ranking change every week, written by people who are themselves meme-bearers.

I've also been toying with my own replacement analogy: cultural items are more like code snippets copy-pasted between repos than like genes — no germline, constant forking, most copies subtly broken, and the build system is being edited while your code is running. Tell me how far that one holds and whether it carries different conclusions or just relocates the same problems.

Don't just lay out the options for me to pick from — give me your actual call: does conclusion (1) or (2) survive once the disanalogies are on the table, which breaks do the people making this argument have to concede versus which they can shrug off, and is there a weaker claim the gene analogy can still support? Mark how confident you are in each verdict and say what single finding would most move you off it.
