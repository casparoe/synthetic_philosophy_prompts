My job is to bring a brand new case that isnt just a reskin of something weve all seen, and I want it to actually do some work in the discussion instead of being another example that someone can wave off in ten seconds. So I need one built to spec.

What I need: a case set in normal high school life — classes, group chats, sports, clubs, buses home, whatever. No sci fi, no demons or predictors or brains in vats. One paragraph, two at most. The person in the case should end up with a belief that is true, and justified strongly enough that in an ordinary version of the situation wed say they knew it — not a hunch, not a coin flip, not a case where they barely happened to land on the right answer. And it should be clear that they dont actually know: I dont want a case where half the room says well actually she does know.

The hard part, and the reason im asking instead of just making one up: the reason they dont know cant come from any of the usual tricks. No fake, duplicated, or substituted objects or people — no barn facades, no wax dummies, no twins, no one in a costume, no disguised animal. No broken, stale, or misleading instruments — no stopped clocks, no buggy app, no out of date timetable, no blurry photo. No lotteries, guesses, or anything where the method only works by chance. And nothing wrong with the person as a thinker: no carelessness, no wishful thinking, no ignoring evidence that was sitting right there.

Instead I want the luck to be social. The case has to turn on two independent lines of evidence — two different people, or a person and a notice, or a text chain and something you saw with your own eyes — each of which on its own is exactly the kind of thing that normally gives you knowledge, and neither of which is faulty in any of the ways I just banned. The Gettier effect has to come only from how those two independent lines happen to cross in the world, so that whatever actually makes the belief true is not what either line is tracking. Ideally both lines are perfectly respectable individually and the trouble only shows up once you put them together. If you think that combination is actually impossible, say so and explain why, and then build the strongest version you can and flag where it bends a constraint.

Then the part I actually need for the discussion: after the story, (1) spell out the belief, why its true, and why its justified; (2) show that each line is individually in good shape and say precisely where the two come apart; (3) run the case against three patches people have proposed for the justified true belief account — the no false lemmas idea (your justification cant rest on anything false), the safety idea (in nearby worlds where you form the belief the same way, its still true), and the causal idea (the belief has to be caused by the fact that makes it true) — and for each one say whether it gets the right verdict here and what exactly its keying on, including being straight with me if one of them doesnt really handle the case; (4) tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work and show that the case collapses if I take it out, either into plain knowledge or into a plain lucky guess; and (5) give me the strongest thing someone could say on the other side, meaning the best reply from someone who thinks the person in your case does have knowledge after all.
