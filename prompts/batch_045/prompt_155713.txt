I am one of two student members of my school's Permanent Collection Committee, which each spring decides which works by graduating students the school acquires and hangs permanently. I have gone back through six years of our minutes, and I think our rulings hang together rather less well than we like to suppose, so I would be grateful for your help before we meet on Tuesday.

Our precedents, as I read them:

1. We declined an archival print of a student's own watercolour — edition of one, signed by her, the painting itself kept at home — on the ground that it is a record of a work rather than a work.
2. We acquired a bronze in an edition of six, poured at a commercial foundry from the student's own mould; she never touched the metal or the patina.
3. We declined a hand-painted, nearly indistinguishable copy of a well-known painting by someone else, even though several members admitted they could not have told the two apart; looking identical, the minute says, is not the point.
4. We accepted as an original historic work a 1974 woven wall hanging by a former student, re-woven two years ago by a current student who replaced roughly two-thirds of the material.
5. We declined a painting whose underdrawing was the submitter's but whose brushwork was almost entirely her friend's, and which the submitter signed alone.
6. We accepted a mural painted by twelve students and credited to all twelve, with no single author named.

The case before us now: a senior's welded steel figure was destroyed in a workshop accident about a fortnight before we were to view it; it had never been exhibited, accessioned, or photographed by anyone but herself. She has since rebuilt it by hand — from her own maquette, her own snapshots, and her working notes — in new steel, and she says the second object is the better one and the one she wants us to take. She has disclosed all of this to us in writing, so no one is being misled about its history.

Please reply in exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, with no introduction and no concluding summary:

1. The two or three rules about what makes something an original that fit all six of our precedents at once.
2. What each of those rules predicts about this submission.
3. Your recommendation for Tuesday with the one reason that decides it for you, and, if you think our precedents genuinely underdetermine the case, the principle we would be committing ourselves to with each choice.
4. Which of the six precedents is the closest analog to this case, and why the others can be distinguished from it.
5. One further case, stated in the same concrete way as ours, that would settle which of those rules we have really been running.
