Predict how George Berkeley (1685-1753) would have judged the following hypothetical modern monetary regime, and justify the prediction by the lights of his actual economic views as summarized below.

In his economic queries published in the 1730s, a series of numbered questions commonly called The Querist, Berkeley argued that a nation's real wealth is not gold, silver, or money itself, but the industry of its people and the useful products of land and labor. Money, for him, functions as a public ticket or counting device: its proper job is to circulate, to call forth labor, and to exchange useful goods, not to be hoarded or treated as treasure. He supported a proposal for an Irish national bank that would issue and supervise a system of paper credit, on the ground that well-managed credit could set the poor to work, improve land, and increase useful production. He was hostile to luxury imports, idleness, and any arrangement that encourages people to lock money up instead of putting it to work; the test of a monetary system is whether it promotes industriousness and the common good.

Now imagine a modern state adopts this regime. The central bank issues a central bank digital currency, meaning a digital currency issued and recorded directly by the central bank, with an account for every resident; physical cash is phased out, and the digital currency is the sole legal tender. New units are created only in two ways: to replace lost or destroyed balances, and to fund public works that hire unemployed workers to build infrastructure or produce useful goods inside the country. New money may not be created to buy financial assets or imported luxury goods. Any balance left idle is charged a carrying fee of 2 percent per year, so that merely hoarding the currency slowly reduces its purchasing power unless it is spent on domestically produced goods and services or used to pay taxes. The currency is not redeemable for gold or silver; its acceptance rests on the state's declaration and on the requirement that taxes be paid in it. Commercial banks may not create money by lending; they may lend only funds that savers have actually deposited. Private cryptocurrencies, including Bitcoin, are prohibited for domestic payments.

Assume Berkeley is informed of this regime, with all modern terms explained, and is asked to evaluate it as monetary policy. Would he approve, disapprove, or approve only conditionally? Your task is to predict and defend his verdict.

Write your entire response in no more than 450 words. Lead with a single-sentence verdict: Approve, Disapprove, or Conditionally approve. Then identify the two or three features of the regime that do the most work in producing that verdict, and say how each of those features would look to him given the summary above. Finally, state which feature of the modern regime, if any, falls outside the resources of his theory, so that the prediction has to extrapolate rather than simply apply. Do not quote or cite any text; do not provide a bibliography, reference list, or reading list; and do not appeal to Berkeley's general metaphysics or theology except where you explain why it directly shapes his view of money.
