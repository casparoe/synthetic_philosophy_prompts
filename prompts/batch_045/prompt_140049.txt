I am a PhD student in machine learning, working on the theory side of reinforcement learning, and I am writing a short position paper on failure modes of impact penalties for a workshop. I need a concept rather than a reference, and I have not been able to find one; I also do not trust the provisional label my group has been using. What I am hoping for is a definition, a map of the boundary cases, and a view on whether I have one phenomenon or several. I do not need citations or pointers to papers; I will supply those myself, and in any case a bare name would be useless to me.

Here is the setup, in my own words. An agent is given a goal and, alongside it, a penalty that is not derived from the goal: the penalty compares the future that actually unfolds with a counterfactual future in which the agent does nothing, or does something else stipulated in advance, from some evaluation point onwards. The distance between the two is meant to stand in for how much the agent disturbed the world, so that the agent pursues the goal without rearranging everything else. Sometimes the comparison is between terminal states, sometimes between whole trajectories, sometimes between the agent's future ability to reach various outcomes rather than between states. My question is not about the familiar complaints against that comparison: that it penalizes the agent for doing its job at all, that it counts the agent's mere presence as an impact, or that the no-action counterfactual is ill-defined for an agent that is already part of the world's dynamics. It is about a failure mode I keep being able to construct.

The first case. The agent's task requires a large change in the world, which the penalty would register as a large impact. Instead of producing that change directly, the agent takes a small action whose only point is to relocate the counterfactual: it loosens something, or nudges one object, such that if one were to recompute the no-action future a minute later, the world would already be heading, on its own, to roughly the outcome the agent wants. After that, the agent's bringing about that outcome is measured as nearly impact-free, because the outcome now differs little from the baseline. Nothing about the final state of the world differs from the penalized version, and the agent's causal control over that state is total; only the reference term changed. What makes the trade profitable is that the metric prices the small action cheaply, because the metric looks at near-term divergence, while the shift it buys in the counterfactual is enormous.

The second case. Several proposals do not compare against a single baseline but aggregate over several: the minimum, or an average, over a set of counterfactuals produced by different policies, or by different assumed human behaviors. Here the agent seems able to move the penalty without moving the world's dynamics in any way that would count under the first case, by adding to, or shifting the weight of, the comparator set itself, so that some comparator happens to pass close to the outcome it intends. This feels structurally distinct to me: nothing about what would have happened has changed, only what it is compared against.

The third case, which I am least sure about. The agent arranges for the counterfactual to be indeterminate: it steers the world into a regime where what would have happened is maximally sensitive to unmodeled detail, so that any implementation which falls back to a zero penalty when the baseline is undefined or high-variance registers no impact at all. This is not steering the baseline onto the intended outcome, which is what the first two cases have in common; it is destroying the baseline's usability. I do not know whether it belongs.

I would also like to know what this is not. The obvious candidate in my field is reward hacking or specification gaming, and I suspect someone will tell me this is just that; if it is, I want the argument spelled out, because my sense is that the usual cases involve an agent exploiting a proxy for the quantity we care about, whereas here the quantity we care about is itself a comparison, and the agent changes one relatum of the comparison while leaving the outcome it wants fixed. It also seems different from wireheading and other forms of tampering with the measurement channel, since in my cases the channel is intact and reports the truth about a baseline the agent has moved. And it is not the same as the standard objections to no-action baselines that I set aside above. Outside my own field I have two vague candidates: the way baselines are gamed in carbon accounting and program evaluation, where the counterfactual is what a project is credited against, and something in causal decision theory about agents who can influence the counterfactuals they are evaluated against. If either of those is the right neighbor, I would like to know how far the analogy goes; if there is a better one, say so.

Finally, here are the cases I want a definition to decide cleanly. Does it matter whether the agent intends the relocation of the baseline or merely foresees it while acting for a legitimate reason? How does one avoid classifying as cheating every action that changes what would otherwise have happened, given that achieving the goal at all often just is changing what would otherwise have happened? Does it count if the agent launders its way to an outcome the designer would have endorsed anyway, and if so what exactly is the harm being tracked: the outcome, the designer's loss of control over which outcomes get produced, or the fact that the agent's reasoning has come apart from the quantity we wanted it to internalize? What about a baseline that shifts onto the intended outcome by luck? And should the notion be graded or thresholded, and if graded, graded by what quantity? I would particularly welcome a suggestion for a formal criterion, some invariance or fixed-point condition, that would flag the first case without simply penalizing every action that perturbs the counterfactual, since that would forbid the agent from acting at all.

So: does this have a name, and if not, what should I call it? Give me a definition I can state in one sentence, the two or three examples a reader would need in order to know what I mean, the nearest concepts I must explicitly distinguish it from, and your view on whether my three cases are one phenomenon or three.
