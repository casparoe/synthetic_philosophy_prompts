I've been reading history on my own for a few years — no classes, no background, just books — and I've ended up somewhere I'd describe as loosely Marxist without ever deciding to be. I want to write down what I actually think so people can push on it. Is any of this inconsistent, or am I quietly helping myself to something?

1. The economic side of a society — what the technology is, who owns what, how people actually get a living — sets the broad shape of everything else: the laws, the religion, what family life looks like, which ideas strike people as common sense. Not in the crude sense where every poem is secretly about money, just: it sets what's workable.

2. But the stuff on top pushes back. A legal system, a national identity, a church — once they exist they obviously cause things, they aren't just foam. And ideas can run ahead of the economy: rights talk shows up in places whose economy hasn't shifted, and I don't want to call that an illusion.

3. Timing and individuals are off limits. Why this year and not twenty years later, why this person — the theory doesn't reach there, and I'm happy to say so.

4. Here's the bit I made up myself because I couldn't find anyone putting it this way. I now split the base explains X claim into a menu claim — the base explains which arrangements can survive, what's on the menu — and a choice claim — the base explains which one actually gets picked, and when. I want the menu claim only. My reason is a bit suspicious: I can't think of any evidence that would settle the choice claim, and that makes me think it isn't a real claim.

5. Superstructure lags. Old ideas and institutions hang around long after the conditions that produced them. I want to say both that the lag has an economic explanation of its own (sunk costs, buildings, people trained the old way, career incentives) and that long lags are my best evidence the base isn't the whole story.

6. I also think you can read a society's economy off it's ideas and laws — that's honestly how I use the theory when I read.

7. Someone told me 1 is circular, because property relations need law to exist at all, so law can't be explained by them. I think that objection is confused but I'm not sure why.

And one more: I think the long-run driver is technology, and the who-owns-what part changes to fit it — but when I actually explain some society's laws or religion, it's always the who-owns-what part doing the work. Feels like the word base is doing two jobs there.

Go through these in pairs: which are really in tension and which only look like it, ranked by how bad, with what would have to give in each case. Then the single revision that cleans up the most, and what I'd lose by making it. Skip names and a reading list — I don't know the literature anyway and I'd rather have the argument.
