I'm a cryptographer; the vocabulary I actually reason in is commitment schemes (binding versus hiding), adversaries, security experiments defined against a class of adversaries, soundness, simulation, and resource bounds. I have never read the philosophical literature on this.

Here is the setup. On a Friday a teacher announces that there will be exactly one exam during the following week, on a day the students will not be able to know in advance. The familiar backward induction appears to show that no such exam can be given; the exam on Wednesday nonetheless surprises them.

Restate the whole thing in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can: the announcement as a commitment, the students as an adversary trying to output the day, "surprise" as a security condition, the backward induction as an attack or a reduction, and the announcement itself as a claim that has to be not merely true but known to be true by the very party it is addressed to. Be honest about the translation: if "know" has to become "cannot compute," "cannot predict better than chance," or "is not derivable in the students' proof system," say that you are making that substitution and why the original resists it. In particular, is the backward induction just a proof that hiding fails against an unbounded receiver — that commitment to a five-day message space is inevitably predictable to a perfect logician?

Then give me the residue report: what translated cleanly, what fit only after distortion, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. I especially want to know about three things: the self-referential character of the announcement, the difference between surprise as unpredictability and surprise as an occurrent psychological state, and the gap between being unable to prove which day it is and being unable to know it.

Finally, your verdict on that residue. Is the awkwardness a defect of my vocabulary, which has no resources for knowledge-talk, or a sign that the original paradox was never really about knowledge — that it is about the assertability of self-referential announcements, or about what can be truthfully said to a perfect reasoner — and that no security game was ever going to capture it?
