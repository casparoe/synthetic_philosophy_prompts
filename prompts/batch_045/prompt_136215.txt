Bit of an odd one, and sorry in advance if this is well-trodden ground. I'm retired — forty years of experimental condensed matter, neutron scattering mostly, never opened a philosophy book until I joined a reading group at the local library about eighteen months ago — and lately we've been stuck on cosmology and particle physics. My eldest is doing a PhD in something adjacent and tells me I'm being naive, which is fair, but I keep noticing the same shape of argument and I dont have a word for it, and I'd rather be told there's no word than be handed a label that doesn't quite fit.

Here's the shape. A framework doesn't determine some quantity Q; instead it says there's a very large space of possible values for Q, realised across some huge ensemble of subsystems (numbers like ten to the five hundred get thrown around for the string landscape), and of course we find ourselves in the corner where Q is compatible with our own existence. My puzzle isn't the usual "that's unfalsifiable" complaint: in the cosmological constant case, as I understand it, an anthropic argument of roughly this shape was on the table before the acceleration was measured and got the order of magnitude about right, and that looks like a genuine success. What I want to know is what's doing the work in that success. The hugeness of the space is load-bearing — a small ensemble with no life-permitting value in it would make the explanation fail — so the theory is being credited for a property that in every statistics course I ever sat through is a debit, since a hypothesis that spreads itself over more outcomes is supposed to be punished by the data rather than rewarded for being generous enough to contain the answer. My statistician friend says that's just the Bayesian Occam effect and there's nothing to see, but I think it's slightly different, because here the spreading is what produces the result rather than a cost you pay for being vague. And then there's the second half, which is the bit I really can't place: the same slack decides where the blame goes. You need a measure over the ensemble to get any number out at all, nobody agrees on the measure, and when a number comes out wrong it's the measure that gets revised, never the framework. Something similar at smaller scale with supersymmetry — for years the expectation of new physics around the TeV scale was offered as a reason the theory was attractive and the machine worth building, and when the LHC came up empty everyone said well, it never specified a scale, so nothing has been refuted. The odd thing is that the looseness wasn't smuggled in afterwards to rescue the theory; it was advertised in advance. It's like a protective belt that's been pre-registered, and that gets to be both the shield and the engine.

So: is there an established concept for this configuration, where one piece of flexibility is simultaneously (i) doing positive explanatory work, (ii) the reason the theory can't lose, and (iii) the designated sink for whatever goes wrong, with the designation made up front rather than after the fact? I know the neighbourhood — Popper and falsifiability, ad hoc hypotheses, the Hungarian chap's research programmes and protective belts and degenerating programmes, accommodation versus prediction, the measure problem as a technical headache, and there's a literature I've half-read on non-empirical confirmation where the absence of alternatives is supposed to count in a theory's favour — but none of those looks like quite it: the belt and the core aren't supposed to be the same object, and this isn't about there being no alternatives. If I'm just missing the right reference, say so, and tell me which nearby notion fits best and why the others don't; if there genuinely isn't one, coin something for me, define it tightly, and give me the boundary cases — in particular, where this stops and the ordinary honest use of a free parameter begins, since obviously every theory has knobs and nobody thinks that's cheating, and I want to know what the line is and what you'd have to check to say which side of it a given case falls on. Please don't just hand me a name. And seperate out for me what's settled from what's live controversy among people who actually work on this, and flag which of your own diagnoses are contested — including, if it comes up, whether the two cases I've described are even good examples of whatever you think I've spotted. Our group has a bad habit of somebody reading one book and announcing that Popper settled all of this decades ago, or the opposite, that nobody has any standards anymore, and I've learned to distrust both.
