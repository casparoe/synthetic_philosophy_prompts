I work in formal methods and proof engineering. The vocabulary I am actually fluent in is that of programming-language semantics and type theory: syntax and interpretation functions, denotational versus operational semantics, computational adequacy and full abstraction, canonicity, extraction, erasure and proof irrelevance, realizability models, logical relations and parametricity, definability and non-definability of elements of a semantic domain, and the familiar phenomenon of a semantics that is elegant and compositional yet fails some adequacy standard people separately care about. I have no real background in metaphysics, and I would rather not be told that semantics is about meaning while epistemology is about warrant and therefore nothing needs translating.

Here is what I want. Restate Benacerraf's dilemma about mathematical truth in that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then report what does not survive the translation.

Fix the target as follows. On one side, a standard Tarskian semantics for mathematical sentences: numerals behave as terms denoting objects, quantifiers range over a domain, and the semantics for mathematical discourse is required to be homogeneous with the semantics for ordinary empirical discourse. On the other side, an empirically respectable account of how speakers come to know what they know and to refer to what they refer to, which appears to require some kind of causal contact with the objects in question. The dilemma is that the objects the first side needs look as though they cannot be the objects the second side can reach.

Please give me:

1. A translation table. One row per load-bearing claim or move — the uniformity requirement on semantics, the causal or empirical constraint on knowledge and reference, the intuition that the domain vastly outruns anything a speaker could be in contact with, the claim that no single account satisfies both constraints at once, and the main escape routes (structuralist reconstruals of what the objects are, positions that multiply the objects so that any consistent structure is realised, nominalist or rewrite-the-language responses, and the modest response of simply dropping the demand for uniformity). For each row: the nearest construction in my vocabulary, and a verdict of translates cleanly, translates only with distortion, or cannot be expressed at all. Say what the distortion consists in where there is one.

2. A residue report. Which parts of the philosophers' dispute have no counterpart in the vocabulary I listed, and is that a gap in my vocabulary or a sign that the original claim was doing less work than it appears to? I care about this distinction more than about the translation itself, so be specific and give reasons rather than splitting the difference.

3. An assessment of a mapping I have been toying with, which I suspect is too glib and want stress-tested rather than flattered. The idea is to read causal contact with the referent as a reachability condition on the interpretation function: call a semantics grounding-complete when every element of the domain that any closed term can denote is also producible by some closed term that reduces, under the operational semantics, to a value the machine can inspect and compute with, and when no element of the domain is essentially unnamed. On this reading the causal constraint becomes a definability requirement, the access problem becomes the familiar gap between a semantic domain and the set of definable elements in it, and the dilemma becomes the observation that the semantics we want is not grounding-complete. Tell me precisely where this identification fails: which feature of the epistemic constraint it captures, which feature it silently drops, and whether what it drops is the whole point of the original argument.
