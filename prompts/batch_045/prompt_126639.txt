ok so the view I keep ending up defending is a kind of contained (quarantined) dialetheism, and I want to know what should move me off it. The idea: contradictions are true, but only in isolated regions of discourse — semantic paradoxes, legal and fictional talk, vague boundries — and the isolation is supposed to be underwritten by two separate mechanisms. One, a true/assertible split: A and not-A can both be true while neither is assertible, so a glut in the truth predicate doesn't on its own license anything in practice. Two, a closure condition on the glutty region: no valid inference takes you from glutty premisses to a conclusion about wholly classical subject-matter — arithmetic, ordinary middling-sized objects — so the glut can't leak outward.

What would count against this, ranked fatal down to merely awkward? I want the defeaters I'd have to concede as a defender, not just what a classical logician would throw at me. In particular: is the closure condition even statable without a usable conditional, given that a detachable conditional plus the naive truth schema plus modus ponens looks like it hands you Curry and hence triviality — and if I have to give up detachment or conditional proof, do I have anything left that deserves to be called inference? Relatedly, is the true/assertible split stable, or does assertion just end up tracking truth in some other guise? And has the "isolated" bit been qualified into unfalsifiability — if to every apparent leak I can reply that that region was glutty all along, is containment claiming anything at all, and what would a leak I couldn't pre-empt that way even look like? I'd be most moved by arguments that the view can't be stated or does no work; I'm much less interested in the "but it's irrational to accept contradictions" line, which I think I have an answer to.
