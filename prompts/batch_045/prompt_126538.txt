hey — I'm putting together a 90-minute workshop for the eng org where I work (roughly 60 PMs and tech leads, no philosophy or stats training, but all of them have been burned by an estimate before). The theme is basically: when shd you shut up and go with the base rate? I want the centrepiece to be a decision tree that sorts people into a position on the inside view / outside view dispute by what they say about cases, not by which label they'd pick for themselves.

The tree starts everyone at the same concrete case: you're estimating a data-centre migration. Four broadly similar migrations you can find numbers for took 14, 11, 19 and 16 months. Your team's bottom-up plan, built task by task, comes out at 8 months, and nobody can find a mistake in it. From there, each branch node is one question, phrased so a PM could answer it out loud without already knowing what position it leads to — never "are you a Bayesian?", never "do you trust base rates?". Aim for 5 or 6 nodes, 2-3 answer options each, and 8-10 leaves, and make sure every leaf is actually reachable.

Leaves I definately need in there: the hard-line reference-class forecaster (the base rate just is the forecast); the person who says the base rate is only a prior and the whole inside/outside contrast dissolves once you update; the mixture person who weights the two by track record; the person who says the outside view only earns its keep across a portfolio of projects and tells you nothing about any single one; the person who says the reference class is contaminated by which projects got attempted in the first place and has to be corrected before you defer; and the "narrowest class you can still get numbers for" person, who keeps shrinking the class until the data gets too thin to use.

Two things that keep coming up in our org and that I want the tree to handle rather than dodge:

First, reference-class shopping. People pick the class that makes their plan look typical. So there should be somewhere to go where the interesting question is not "what's your base rate" but "who picked this class, and are they allowed to be immodest about picking it". I'd like a leaf for the move of applying the outside view one level up — to the choice of class itself, so you ask what happens to people who go looking for a narrow class at all. Call it the hereditary outside view if you like. Note in the notes whether that move bottoms out in a regress or in something like "widen until you hit everything", and whether anyone is actually there.

Second, the calibration double-count worry. Suppose you also apply the outside view to your own history of claiming "I checked, and this one really is different" — you now have a base rate over your own exceptionalism claims as well as a base rate over the projects. Some of my colleagues think that's the whole point of epistemic modesty; others think it counts the same evidence twice. If your tree can't separate those two, say so explicitly rather than pretending it can.

Also: build it from the logical space, not from a list of names. Give me the branch questions first, then locate the positions in the space they create. For combinations of answers nobody occupies, say whether the empty seat is there because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. And flag any two leaves your tree can't tell apart, with the extra question that would separate them if you had one.

Please don't cite anything, name-drop papers, or give me a reading list — I've read the standard material, and I need something I can hand out, not references to chase.

Give me the whole thing as a single JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "opening_case": string, one sentence restating the case the tree assumes
- "nodes": array of objects, each with "id" (a letter), "question" (the question as I'd read it out), "options" (array of objects with "answer" and "next" — the next node id or a leaf id), and "splits_what" (one sentence on which disagreement this node exists to catch)
- "leaves": array of objects, each with "id", "label", "path" (like "A2 -> B1 -> C3"), "core_claim" (one sentence), "awkward_commitment" (one sentence on what this position has to swallow), and "occupancy" (either who holds it, or "unoccupied" plus a short diagnosis)
- "unoccupied_combinations": array of objects with "combo" and "diagnosis" ("incoherent", "unattractive", or "unexplored", and why)
- "cant_separate": array of objects with "pair" (two leaf ids), "why", and "extra_question" (the question that would split them, or null)
- "facilitation_notes": array of 3-4 strings on running this live, including which node you expect the room to row about

Write it in British English, and keep the questions short enough to fit on a slide.
