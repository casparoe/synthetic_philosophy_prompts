So I work freelance, set my own hours, and I've noticed something kind of dumb about myself. Every three or four weeks I flip my whole schedule. I'll be a 6am person for a while, then I get convinced that's destroying me and switch to working 2pm till midnight, then a few weeks later I'm sure the late shift is wrecking my focus and I go back to mornings. Each flip feels totally justified at the moment I make it, like I've finally figured out what actually works for me, and the old way now looks obviously worse. But it isn't free: every flip costs me about a week of lost output, I reschedule clients, and clients have started commenting on it.

My flatmate says I'm basically paying to go in circles, and that if this were money changing hands instead of hours everyone would immediately see I was being had. He's started making small bets with me about which way I'll flip next and he wins basically every time, which he treats as proof. My reply is that each change was a genuine improvement given how I felt and what I knew when I made it, that I'd make the same flips again, and that I'd rather keep the flexibility than get locked into one schedule forever. Also, half the time when I flip I can't tell whether I've had a real realisation about how I work or whether I'm just in a mood, and I definately can't tell in advance.

I've tried the commitment stuff — blocking apps, telling clients my hours, calendar locks — and I always just click through, so "pick a plan and stick to it" isn't obviously available to me. And here's the bit that confuses me most: if I only flipped once a year it would cost almost nothing, so how bad this is seems to depend on how often I reopen the question, and I have no idea how often a person is supposed to reopen a question.

I get that my flatmate is profiting off me, so please don't just tell me I'm being taken advantage of and stop there. What I want to know is whether that actually shows I'm being irrational, or whether it only shows he found a way to make money off how I change my mind, and if there is a defect here, where exactly it sits — in the flipping itself, in my not being able to stick to a plan, in how often I reconsider, or somewhere else. Oh, and one more thing: should it matter that I sort of enjoy the flipping? Please answer in continuous prose, no headings, bullets, bold text or tables, just write it out like an email.
