I'm in the third year of a PhD in a lab that works on conscious access, and I need someone to audit the picture we actually operate with, because we are about to build a paper — and a short white paper on machine consciousness — on top of it, and I have started to suspect the picture is doing more work than it can carry.

Here is our lab's version of it, which is a press-office version of the global broadcast story. Parallel, partly independent processes file candidate reports. A small number get picked up and issued as a single press release. The release goes out to every department at once, and the departments can act on it; a report that never gets issued can still affect processing locally, but nothing downstream ever learns about it. We lean on this in two ways. First, to read our data: we take the broadcast as the event of a content becoming conscious, so that *when* it became conscious and *what exactly* was conscious are well-posed empirical questions, and reportability plus its neural signature — late, widespread, sustained response — is the instrument that tells us whether and when the release went out. Second, to argue about architecture: a system with a bottleneck that broadcasts to many consumer systems is conscious, and one without it is not.

Set that against Dennett's multiple drafts model, as I understand it: there is no place where it all comes together, no canonical draft, no finish line at which content is fixed, and what a subject is conscious of is settled partly by how and when you ask — the familiar case of two flashed spots experienced as one moving dot whose color changes in mid-flight being the sort of thing that motivates it. Correct that characterization if I have it wrong; I would rather be corrected than agreed with.

What I want is the audit. Give me the correspondence table explicitly: what files the reports, what issues them, what the release is, who receives it, what the press office itself is — and then, for each mapping, whether it holds, holds only with strain, or breaks, and crucially whether the mapping is load-bearing for one of my two uses or is just decor. Separate the disanalogies that matter for my two uses from the ones that don't: I suspect some of them are harmless for reading data but fatal for the architectural claim, or the reverse.

Here is the case I cannot fit, which is why I'm asking. In our current task a masked word is shown for a variable duration, and after a variable delay we probe. With a detection question, accuracy rises early; with a forced-choice identification, it rises later — expected, and the press office handles it fine as a weak release that some departments act on and others don't. What doesn't fit: on trials where we give both probes, separated by a gap, the answer to the second depends on the gap and on the order, and at long gaps subjects will sometimes identify the word above chance while denying in the same breath that they saw anything, or deny seeing it and then, when pressed to guess, identify it. My senior author reads this as two releases, one local and one global. I read it as no release at all, only drafts that the probe edits. Adjudicate that, and be explicit about what each picture predicts before the data, not just after.

I have also been trying a repair, and I want to know whether it works or quietly smuggles the theater back in. Replace *broadcast* with a distinction between *on the record* and *merely retrievable*: a content is on the record when later processing is held to it, constrained to stay consistent with it, and is the thing the system will defend if challenged; it is merely retrievable when some probe will surface it but nothing downstream is committed. Say whether that repair escapes the multiple drafts objection, and if it does not, say exactly where the observer goes back in — and, if the honest answer is that what holds the system to the record is just later processing, or whatever the experimenter does next, say plainly whether that means the time and content of consciousness are partly constituted by the probe rather than merely revealed by it. Along the way, keep three questions apart for me: whether a content was conscious at all, when it became conscious, and what exactly was conscious — and tell me which of them the press office makes well-posed that the multiple drafts model denies are well-posed, and whether that model is committed to saying the *when* question has no answer at all, or only that it has no answer independent of a probing.

Finally: is there a better analogy, or is the honest answer that no single one covers both uses? I would rather have nine hundred disciplined words than two thousand hedged ones.
