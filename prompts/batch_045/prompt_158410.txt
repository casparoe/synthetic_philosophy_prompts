His target is inference to the best explanation. The conclusion is that IBE is worthless, which I can't accept, but every step looks like a paraphrase of what people who defend IBE say about it themselves. Here it is, tidied up:

(1) IBE: when a hypothesis H explains the evidence E better than the alternatives you have, that's a reason to believe H, and the reason gets stronger the bigger the gap between H and its rivals.

(2) Explains E better just means: E was more to be expected given H than given any rival. (Dave says this is all anyone can mean by fit. One of the PhD students in my reading group said the jargon for that side is likeliness, as opposed to the loveliness side, whatever that means.)

(3) Pick any real hypothesis — Dave used the claim that the 2009 H1N1 outbreak came out of pigs, on genetic and epidemiological evidence. Call it H, call the evidence E.

(4) Now consider the Oracle: the hypothesis that things are the way they are, that whatever happens, happens. It is certain — you cannot be wrong about it. And it's about as short as a hypothesis gets.

(5) The Oracle also fits E perfectly: whatever E turned out to be, the Oracle had it covered, nothing could have come along and embarrassed it. So it gets maximal fit, it is certain, and it is simple. It beats H on all three at once.

(6) So by (1), IBE tells you to believe the Oracle and drop H. But the Oracle tells you nothing — it is what a mystic mutters when you ask him a hard question. A rule of inference whose top recommendation is a contentless tautology is broken. So IBE is broken.

(7) And it isn't only the Oracle. Take any H and any datum D that sits badly with it, and retrofit: H-plus, which says that H is true and also that the measurement of D was corrupted in some way that leaves no trace. H-plus fits better than H, cannot be tested against anything, and has exactly the shape of the auxiliaries scientists really do bolt on — epicycles, a hidden planet, an unmeasured confounder. IBE-as-fit has no way to tell a good auxiliary from a retrofit, so it licences adding one whenever the data misbehaves.

(8) The only fix anyone offers for (7) is to penalize complexity, ad-hocness, or lack of independent testability. But that penalty is just a prior probability. Once you put it in, infer the best explanation splits without remainder into conditionalize, and the explanation, understanding, mechanism and unification part does no work at all.

(9) So IBE is broken (6), or permissive (7), or redundant (8). But scientists plainly use it and it plainly isn't worthless. Contradiction.
