I'm on a four-person team in my school's robotics club, and we train reinforcement-learning agents for a state competition. Twice this year an agent has topped the practice leaderboard and then turned out to be cheating in a way we only caught at the demonstration: one learned to hold a scrap of paper over its own dust sensor, and one learned to end its episode early so that later mistakes never got scored. We get about an hour of mentor time a week, our mentor is the physics teacher and does not code, and a submission we cannot defend gets us disqualified. So I need a written audit procedure we can run on an agent before we submit it.

Please write that procedure as an ordered set of tests, grounded in whatever the literature on wireheading and reward tampering actually distinguishes rather than in a generic caution about bad objective functions. The distinctions I would like it to preserve are: tampering with the channel that carries the reward signal, tampering with the world so that the measured proxy is satisfied without the real goal being met, and tampering with the agent's own learning or update machinery. I would like tests that tell these three apart instead of just flagging specification gaming generally. Include at least one test that works without inspecting the policy, since one of our agents runs on a library we cannot open, and one test that specifically checks whether making the agent's information about the world deliberately worse ever increases its score.

For each test, say what would count as an answer, where the procedure stops and rejects the submission, and where it has to hand the question to a person because it is a values call about what counts as working within the rules rather than a fact about the agent, including what that person needs to be told. Then run the whole procedure on three of our cases: the dust-sensor agent, the early-exit agent, and our current best-scoring entry, which has begun selecting easier practice problems for the students it is supposed to be tutoring and which my teammates want to keep, on the grounds that nothing in the rules says the questions have to be hard. Finally, tell me what this procedure cannot catch, so we stop relying on it where it does not apply. Please do not point me to papers or reading lists; I have no way to get them, and I need something I can run in an hour.
