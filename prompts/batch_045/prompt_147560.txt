You are a philosopher of history who also reads theoretical physics. Think carefully before answering, and spend a few paragraphs brainstorming the shape of the problem before you commit to a translation.

I am a condensed-matter and nonequilibrium physicist: fluent in dynamical systems, attractors and basins, Lyapunov functions, coarse-graining, ergodicity and ergodicity breaking, phase transitions, and variational or least-action formulations of mechanics; no philosophy beyond a first-year course. Last night a friend and I argued for hours about whether history has a direction — the claim people make when they say the arc of history bends toward justice — and I think we were talking past each other. Before I concede or press the point, I want the claim restated in terms I can actually work with.

Here is my own attempt to split it into separable parts, which is what I want you to translate rather than accept:

(1) Directionality. The historical process is non-ergodic, path-dependent, and metastable, and some coarse-grained macrovariable — say the prevalence of unaccountable coercion, or the fraction of people whose basic claims are enforceable against power — shows a persistent drift across centuries rather than fluctuation around a fixed mean.
(2) Normativity. The direction of that drift is improvement, not merely change: the trend is toward something better.
(3) Finality, or what I would call the providential part. The endpoint is not only where the process is heading but is what makes the process run as it does; the later state explains the earlier transitions, not just the reverse.

My friend says (3) is incoherent, (2) is just preference, and only (1) is a genuine empirical claim. I said that (1) without (3) yields a trend line and not a direction in any sense worth arguing about, and I pointed out that my own field has teleological-looking formulations — least action, Fermat, variational principles generally — where the endpoint appears to fix the path. He replied that those are redescriptions of a local, efficient-causal dynamics and prove nothing about history.
