I'm putting together a short position paper for a graduate seminar I'm co-running this term on whether large language models understand anything, and before I circulate it I'd like someone to audit the package as a whole rather than argue with me about any single claim. Here is roughly where I've landed, in the order I would defend it. (1) Understanding is a causal-informational achievement, not a matter of being made of the right stuff: a system understands a subject matter when its internal states are reliably produced by the things they bear on and are deployed in coping with them. I take this to dispose of the Chinese Room — Searle's argument, as I read it, gets its grip only by demanding that semantic content be intrinsic to the vehicle rather than relational, and I don't want to grant that demand. (2) Understanding does not require consciousness; a functionally organised zombie would understand everything we do, and I don't want any premise in the set that quietly rules that out. (3) I read the stochastic-parrot line as, in the end, a normative and political criticism — about training-data provenance, the labour behind corpus curation, hype, and the harms of selling fluency as comprehension — and not as a metaphysical thesis about what understanding is; the word 'statistics' names a level of description, not the contrary of 'meaning'. (4) But I do think the trained-only-on-form objection has real force in one place: I want to say that a purely text-trained system can come to understand 'bank', 'contract', 'mitochondrion' or 'defeasible', and cannot come to understand 'red', 'heavy' or 'sour', because the latter kind of concept needs the sort of direct perceptual contact with the world that no volume of text supplies. (5) I also think understanding carries a normative condition: for a system to count as understanding, there must be something that counts as getting it wrong by the system's own lights, and its states must be shaped by sensitivity to that. I don't think this is a consciousness requirement in disguise, and I take next-token prediction under a cross-entropy loss to be precisely the kind of error-driven shaping the condition asks for, so on that basis I think current systems pass it. (6) And a case verdict I'm attached to: when a model confabulates fluently and confidently about a matter it has no signal on, I want to call that a failure of understanding rather than mere ignorance, because I think understanding a domain includes having some grip on where one's competence in it runs out.

What I can't shake is that (4) looks like the very move I rejected in (1): the octopus case that the form-and-meaning literature likes — two creatures messaging each other in a language we can read, whose traffic we intercept but whose world we never see — is supposed to show that form without contact cannot deliver meaning, and I find that persuasive for observation terms while denying it everywhere else, even though testimony-mediated understanding (historians, electron microscopy, remote sensing) is fine by (1). I've been toying with a way out: what matters is not perceptual contact but portability — whether a competence survives radical reformulation (another register, a low-resource language, reversed clause order, a diagram or table instead of prose), on the thought that exploitation of corpus statistics would fragment under reformulation while genuine understanding would not. I genuinely can't tell whether that rescues (4) or quietly replaces it with a different claim that has different consequences, and a colleague suggested the problem is really in (5) rather than (4), which I also can't adjudicate.

What I'm after is an audit rather than a survey: which of (1)–(6) are genuinely in tension, which pairs only look tense and dissolve once the vocabulary is cashed out, and — for each real tension, ranked by severity — exactly what would have to give and what giving it up would cost me elsewhere in the set. Please be specific about whether the error-sensitivity condition in (5) is doing etiological work (the states were shaped by a history of error) or online work (the system can now register and revise its own failure), since I suspect that question decides whether (6) is affordable at all, and tell me whether my portability idea is a fix for (4) or a substitute for it that I should stop presenting as the same claim. If a single revision would clean up more of the mess than the others, say which one it is and what it leaves me unable to say.
