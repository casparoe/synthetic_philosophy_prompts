I'm giving a twenty-minute commentary at a grad workshop next week on that short paper Ramsey read to the Apostles about the meaning of life, and I want to open with one actual joke instead of my usual mumbled apology. The angle I'm pushing is that the deflation he pulls with the perspectival picture of the world — humans huge in the foreground, the stars shrunk to about the size of small coins, sizes fixed by how much we care rather than by how big things are — is the very same move he pulls on theoretical terms: you replace the thing with an existentially bound variable, keep every role it was playing, and stop claiming to know what it is. Call it Ramsified meaning: a life is meaningful iff there's a (roughly unique) property satisfying the Ramsey sentence of your biography, so we never get to say what the meaning of life is, only that something or other had better have played that role in the story. Can you give me a few one-liners I could actually say out loud that turn on that specific joke — that Ramsey hands you a way to go on talking about the meaning of life while permanently declining to say what it is, and that the same trick is what flattens the cosmos into background scenery? They should get a laugh out of people who know what ramsification is and have read the Apostles paper, and they shouldn't be puns on his name or generic "philosopher walks into a bar" filler. I'd rather have one that lands then five that don't, and bonus points if it survives being delivered by someone visibly nervous in front of thirty people who mostly work on something else.
