I am writing a paper on the bootstrapping objection to dogmatism about perceptual justification, and what I want from you is a diagnosis of why that debate has stopped moving — not a verdict on who is right, and not a survey of the positions, which I know well.

The set-up, as I see it: dogmatism holds that a perceptual experience as of p gives one immediate, defeasible justification for believing p, with no antecedent justification that the faculty is reliable. The objection is that this makes knowledge too easy. Read an instrument repeatedly, take each reading at face value, infer from it how things stand in the world, and then infer inductively that the instrument is reliable: a source vouching for itself. The three replies I keep meeting are that warrant does not transmit through that last inference, since the entitlement to trust the source already presupposes what the conclusion asserts; that bootstrapping is not in general vicious and the objector has never shown the perceptual case to be relevantly like the instrument case; and that the objection simply helps itself to the thought that no source can vouch for itself. Everyone on every side can now recite all of this, and as far as I can tell no one has moved in years.

My hypothesis, offered as one: the sides are testing the same principle on different cases, so part of the disagreement is about which cases count as data. Distinguish single-channel bootstrapping, where every apparent check on a source is informationally downstream of that very source, so that agreement between what the source says and how things are could not have failed and therefore confirms nothing, from multi-channel bootstrapping, where the checks arrive by routes independent of the source, so that agreement is a genuine constraint and disagreement was possible. The objector's instruments are single-channel; perception, as the dogmatist conceives it, is multi-channel, with cross-modal agreement and practical success doing the checking. Tell me whether that is right, whether it makes the dispute merely verbal, and what it leaves standing — presumably the question whether perception really is multi-channel in the relevant sense, or whether everything we count as a separate channel draws on a single faculty, in which case the objection bites after all.

So: is this a verbal dispute, a substantive disagreement about transmission, or two questions under one heading? If my distinction is confused or begs the question against the objector, say why. And given your diagnosis, tell me what would count as progress — a result about when agreement across channels is evidentially inert, a better regimentation of the reliability claim the bootstrap is supposed to yield, a change of question — or give me an argument that no resolution should be expected, and say what that argument is.
