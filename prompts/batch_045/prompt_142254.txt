setup: every neuron in someone's brain gets swapped out for an artificial unit that does the same job. here's the specific catch i want to build in, because it changes things: the replacement units only have to match the old neuron's firing behaviour for the input patterns that neuron will *in fact* receive over the rest of the subject's life. they're allowed to be totally wrong about inputs that never actually come up — call it counterfactual slack. they can be lookup tables that are only correct along the single trajectory the brain actually travels.

i don't want to argue about whether the whole process can preserve the person at all. assume it does: at the end you've got a fully artificial brain and it's still them. what i care about is the middle of the process. some schedules of replacement seem like they must have a moment where something goes wrong — either the subject blanks out entirely, or their experience quietly slides (colors drain, pain turns into some other sensation) in a way they can't notice, because the noticing machinery is what's been swapped.

so: rank these schedules into tiers from least worrying to most worrying, one sentence each on why it lands where it does.

1. one neuron per minute, scattered randomly around the brain, taking ~30 years
2. one neuron per minute but clustered — you finish a whole local circuit (a cortical column, say) before moving to the next one
3. everything at once, in a single operation under general anaesthesia
4. overlapping: each new unit runs alongside the old one for a few weeks with its output faded in from 0% to 100%, and only then is the biological one removed
5. bottom-up: brainstem and subcortex first, cortex last
6. top-down: cortex first, brainstem last
7. sleep-only: nothing is swapped while they're awake — each night a large batch is replaced and settles in, and they wake up with it already integrated
8. the whole brain in about a day, i.e. faster than the surrounding network can retune its synapses around the newcomers
9. replacement with upgrades: each unit also fixes a defect, so over the course of it their recall sharpens, reaction time improves, and a nagging chronic pain they'd had for years quietly stops
10. replacement with revival: the old biological tissue is kept alive in a dish, still wired in but silenced, and only disconnected at the very end
