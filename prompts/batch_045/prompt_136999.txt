I'm in the final year of a PhD in political theory, writing a chapter on the ethics and political economy of norm enforcement, and I need one scenario built to order rather than found in the literature.

The chapter's tentative claim is that tightness–looseness theory — the view, familiar from cross-cultural psychology, that societies develop strong norms and a low tolerance of deviance as an adaptive response to ecological and social threat, and relax both when threat is low — is tracking the wrong independent variable. What matters, I want to argue, is not the magnitude of the threat but the epistemic structure of the response the threat demands: whether averting it requires uniform compliance with a known procedure, or requires perishable local judgement and voluntary disclosure of bad news by the people at the sharp end. I need a case that makes that distinction bite.

Please construct the following.

A single-paragraph, realistic scenario in which the tightness–looseness framework, working strictly with its own success criterion — minimise coordination failure, maximise the group's chances of averting the threat — recommends *loosening*, in a setting that is in every other respect as favourable to tightness as that framework could ask for. Specifically: the threat is genuine, severe and objective rather than merely perceived; the population is highly interdependent; the group-level information about the threat is good; the enforcers are competent and well-intentioned; and sanctions are accurately targeted at real violations, with no corruption, no sadism and no error-prone or arbitrary punishment. The self-defeat, in other words, has to come from the well-calibrated strictness itself and from how people rationally respond to it, not from a malfunctioning enforcement apparatus.

Further specifications:

1. The load-bearing mechanism must be informational. Averting the threat depends on discretionary, situation-specific judgement and/or on frontline people volunteering unwelcome information (near-misses, early anomalies, their own mistakes); codified strict norms plus certain sanctioning degrade exactly that signal. State the mechanism in one sentence.
2. Please separate the two dimensions that tightness bundles together — the strength of the norm (how widely shared, how clearly stated, how much it occupies people's attention) and the severity of sanctioning (how much deviance is punished, how certainly). The accounts should be allowed to come apart across them.
3. Give verdicts from three standpoints, each derived from that standpoint's own commitments rather than stipulated by you: (a) the threat–coordination functionalism described above; (b) a rule-of-law or legibility view, on which the evil to be avoided is unchanneled discretion, and rules should be general, clear, prospective and predictably enforced — I want this one to recommend *tightening*, codifying and enforcing surely; (c) an expressive or motivational-crowding-out view, on which punishment signals distrust and displaces the unmonitored willingness to do the right thing — I expect this one to give a split verdict, and I would like to see whether it does.
4. Setting: contemporary and ordinary — a hospital ward, a fire or ambulance service, rail or air operations, a water utility, a university, a municipal administration. One paragraph, no invented states, no states of nature, no disaster fiction. Hold population, threat, technology and resources fixed: only the enforcement regime varies, and nothing arrives to rescue anyone.
5. For the functionalist verdict, show the comparison explicitly — the expected coordination outcome under each regime — not merely an intuition that looseness feels better here.

After the scenario, please give me: the verdict and reasoning for each of the three standpoints, about a short paragraph each; an identification of the single feature doing the work, together with what happens if it is removed (my expectation is that if the threat becomes centrally and automatically detectable, so that no local disclosure is needed, the case collapses back into an ordinary high-threat case in which the functionalist account says tighten — tell me whether that is right); the strongest available reply from whichever standpoint comes out worst, which I suspect is the legibility view saying that I have conflated clarity with severity and that it can want codified rules with lenient, safe-harboured enforcement — answer that reply honestly, conceding what deserves to be conceded; and a note on where the case itself is most likely to fail. By that last item I mean: which empirical finding or argument would lead you to withdraw the claim that the functionalist account recommends loosening here; whether you have anywhere smuggled in a value the framework does not contain (autonomy, dignity, pluralism, distrust of the state); and which behavioural premise you are relying on that a sceptic could reasonably contest.

What counts as cheating, for my purposes: making the threat partly illusory, making the enforcers inept or malicious, redefining tightness so that looseness wins by definition, or announcing the verdicts rather than deriving them. I have the literature to hand, so please spend no words on references, citations or reading suggestions. Please write in British English, and prefer roughly 600 careful words to 1,500 padded ones.
