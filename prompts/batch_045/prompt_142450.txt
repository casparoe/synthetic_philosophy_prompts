Hey, I'm in my second year on this university-community review panel that has to sign off on external partnerships for our lab's models, and I'm trying to figure out what rule we're actually running. Our votes feel consistent to me but I can't say why, and I get the feeling were applying at least two different tests and picking whichever one gives the answer we already like. Heres roughly what we've done: (1) approved a deal with a regional hospital network — they get a model fine-tuned on patient records where consent was broad and pretty thin, we get access to a rare-disease registry that should let us catch a specific cancer months earlier; the privacy people on the panel hated it and we approved anyway for the health gain. (2) rejected a deal with a state police agency — they'd have handed over a decade of incident reports, which would have improved our emergency-response model a lot, if we let them run license-plate search on it; we said no even with an audit trail, a warrant requirement and annual outside review on the table. (3) approved with conditions a license of our logistics model to a large meat processor — fixed share of the efficiency savings had to go to animal-welfare upgrades at their suppliers, audited externally, contract terminable if the audits fail. (4) rejected a deal with a foreign education ministry — textbooks in 40 languages, genuinely big reach, in exchange for filtering out material on democratic participation and on some religious minorities. (5) approved a deal with a conservative school district that required our tutoring model to not generate content about gender identity, in exchange for serving around 200k students who otherwise get no tutoring at all, plus conditions that the filter is disclosed to students and that we publish what's being filtered.

Now the new one, which is what I actually have to vote on next week. A national retail chain wants our scheduling-and-checkout model across all their stores. The catch is continuous productivity monitoring of cashiers with no individual opt-out. In exchange they'd commit to a wage floor above the local median, a cap on shift-splitting, a seat for a worker rep on the deployment board, and a share of the surplus from the efficiency gains going into a worker fund. So it's a bit different from our earlier cases: roughly the same people bear the cost and get the benefits, management captures most of the productivity gain, and the monitoring can't be switched off by the worker but can be paused by the worker rep under defined conditions.

What I want from you: what rule or rules fit all five of our earlier votes, and what does each predict here? The candidates I keep circling are something like accept any trade that leaves each value better off in its own currency, versus a side-constraint rule with some threshold, versus something about precedent and what a deal licenses later, versus the chair's line about being able to explain the deal to whoever bears the cost. Give me your best guess on how we should vote for consistency with ourselves, say which old case is the closest analog, be honest where our precedents just underdetermine it and what principle we'd be adopting either way, and name the one hypothetical variant of this retail case that would tell the competing rules apart. And the bit I care about most: is trade even the right frame here, or is there something about moral compromise that makes gains-from-trade a misleading model even when every affected party is arguably better off in some respect?
