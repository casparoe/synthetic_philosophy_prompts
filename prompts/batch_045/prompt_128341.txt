I lead the annotation team for a content-safety classifier at a mid-sized platform. Roughly forty labelers, working across three languages, apply a guideline document of about ninety rules to a few hundred thousand items a month. Those labels retrain the classifier, so a systematic mislabel is expensive and slow to notice, and a labeler who has quietly been applying a deviant version of a rule can go undetected for months. Each labeler has about thirty seconds per item. Anything I add to the guideline has to be readable by someone with no training in philosophy or linguistics, it has to survive translation into three languages, and it has to clear Trust and Safety and Legal, who meet with me once a week for forty-five minutes.

The recurrence I want help with is the one described by the Kripkenstein reading of Wittgenstein on rule-following: a rule plus the entire stock of its past applications still leaves its application to a genuinely new case open, and when two labelers disagree and neither has misread anything, the instinct is to keep redrafting the rule as though the right wording were hidden in it somewhere, waiting to be found. I want to stop treating that as a drafting failure and build the openness into how we work. A wrinkle specific to us: the labeling interface shows each labeler the classifier's own suggested label, generated from the labels we have already produced, and labelers drift toward agreeing with it.

Please write a reusable procedure my team can run in that weekly forty-five-minute meeting on a batch of disputed items. It should absorb, without using any of the technical vocabulary, the following ideas: that a rule together with all its past applications underdetermines the next one; that there is a difference between hunting for a further fact and settling on assertibility conditions by appeal to what our labeler community in fact does; that sustained disagreement which survives repeated redrafting is evidence that there is no fact of the matter to be found here rather than evidence that the rule is badly written; and that a labeler can be following a divergent rule that agrees with ours on every item we have ever seen.

The procedure needs: an entry condition; ordered steps; the question asked at each step; what counts as a sufficient answer; the point at which the procedure stops with a no or a not-yet; and the point at which it hands the decision to a person because the question is one of values rather than of fact or meaning, since how much over-blocking we tolerate is not something a guideline can settle. It must distinguish three kinds of disagreement and route each differently: disagreement about the world or about what a text says, settled by adding a worked example; disagreement traceable to a divergent rule, settled by constructing the smallest probe item that would separate the two candidate rules and, where no probe item that could ever reach us separates them, recording the divergence as unfalsifiable and stopping; and disagreement that is really a values question, escalated. Include a rule for designing such a probe item, a stop rule stating how many failed refinement rounds trigger switching to a hardcoded lookup table issued as a decision rather than presented as a clarification, a verdict on whether the classifier's suggested labels may be used as the tiebreaker, and a specification of who signs off on what and how decisions taken by fiat are recorded and later revisited.

Then run it on three cases: (i) two labelers split on whether an AI-generated image of a real person, posted without consent, falls under our sexual content rule, both citing the same sentence of the guideline; (ii) a labeler who has agreed with the team on four thousand items and who then applies a rule in a way that is consistent with everything she has ever labeled but that none of us expected, because the feature distinguishing the two readings has never appeared in any item we have seen; (iii) a class of items I have already tried to fix by redrafting three times, where satire directed at a public figure keeps splitting the room between harassment and political speech. Run (iii) honestly even where the procedure tells me to stop drafting; I would rather hear that than a fourth rewording.

Say plainly what the procedure cannot do.

Use this output format. First, a table of the procedure itself with the columns: Step; Entry condition; Question to ask; What counts as a sufficient answer; If it fails, go to; Hard-case flag. Second, a table of the three worked cases with the columns: Case; Step entered; Steps run, in order; Outcome; Settled by fact, by convention, or by values; Residual risk; What the asker will dislike. Third, a single JSON object with exactly these fields, which you should fill in: probe_design_rule (string); stop_rule (object with keys rounds, condition, fallback); nodes_converted_to_fiat (array of objects with keys rule_id, item_class, why_drafting_failed, fiat, review_date, signoff_role); unfalsifiable_divergences (array of objects with keys source, candidate_divergent_rule, why_no_probe_separates_them, disposition); classifier_suggestions (string: your verdict and the reason); cannot_do (array of strings); judgment_calls_surviving (array of objects with keys decision, who_makes_it, why_it_cannot_be_proceduralized).
