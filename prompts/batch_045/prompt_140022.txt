hey — I'm on a small team building a long-term memory system for an LLM-based coding assistant, and I need someone to audit the analogy we keep leaning on, not to tell me whether the thesis is true.

Setup: there's a persistent store of notes (user preferences, decisions we made last week, "don't touch this module"). The notes are written by the assistant itself. A separate embedding-based retriever surfaces them, but stochastically — a note is there and usually comes back, not always. And the store is shared: other agents on the team can write into it.

The justification our team reaches for is the extended mind thesis in its prosthetic form: external memory is to a mind what a prosthetic limb (or the blind person's cane) is to a body. It's outside the skin, it's kit, but it isn't something you inspect and then use — it's part of you; the right unit of description is the coupled system, agent plus store. Then three conclusions get drawn from it:

1. evaluate and credit the whole agent+store system, not the weights or the context window
2. it doesn't matter that nothing is currently in the context window, because the information is available to the system in roughly the way your own memories are available to you
3. if the store gets corrupted, or another agent silently edits a note, that isn't the agent being influenced by its environment — it's closer to brain damage, or to someone rewriting your diary

What I want:

- the mapping laid out explicitly, a correspondence table is fine: what plays the body, the limb, the amputation, the fitting, ownership, and the body schema updating to incorporate a tool (does anything in the target even correspond to that last one?)
- where the mapping breaks, and — more usefully — which of those breaks actually matter for conclusions 1 through 3 and which are noise you can wave through
- what the analogy quietly imports that our system doesn't have: physical coupling, single-user, reliable availability, being under the agent's own motor control, being deliberately fitted rather than accreted... and for each, whether the usual patches people reach for in this literature (trust, reliability, automatic endorsement, easy accessibility, durable coupling) actually fix it or just rename the problem
- whether prosthesis is even the best analogy going. My colleague prefers a memory-hierarchy one — registers/cache/RAM/disk — where the CPU doesn't decide to fetch, the memory system just is the machine's memory, and retrieval is done by the architecture rather than by the agent. Give me that mapping too if you think it carries more, or a different one if neither does.

Two bits I'd like you to chew on specifically, because I dont think either analogy handles them: (a) the notes are self-authored, so their content isn't "non-derived" in the way a handwritten notebook's is — does self-written external content still get to be part of the system, or does that collapse into just being a longer context window; (b) the store is shared and the retrieval is probabilistic, so the resource is partly someone else's and only sometimes there — does that break the ownership and reliability conditions in a way no analogy patches, or is it just a weaker version of the same case?

No reading lists or citations please, and don't give me abstract pros and cons of the thesis — keep it keyed to what the prosthetic picture can and can't carry. End with a last line stating your verdict on whether the analogy carries conclusions 1-3, in this form: Verdict: survives / partially survives / collapses
