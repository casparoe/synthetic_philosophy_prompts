I'm drafting a short position paper on advance directives and want a consistency check on my own view before I commit it to writing. Roughly where I've landed on the authority of the earlier self:

(1) In dementia, the prior competent person's values should generally outweigh the current person's experience: a woman who spent her life insisting she would never want to live in an institution, and who now seems content singing in the activities room, should still be treated according to the values she expressed earlier, and the fact that honouring them makes her worse off now is not by itself a reason to set them aside.

(2) Ulysses contracts should be enforced against the later self's contemporaneous protest, including lucid and articulate protest: the psychiatric patient who asked in advance to be held if he later demands discharge during an episode should not be discharged when he demands it, even if he can state the reasons back to you.

(3) But where the later refusal or reversal comes from a change the earlier self could not have known from the inside — a religious conversion, or becoming a parent for the first time — the earlier self's directive loses its grip, and the transformed person's present say-so should carry the day.

(4) Metaphysically I hold a reductionist view: personal identity over time is nothing over and above psychological continuity and connectedness, which come in degrees; there is no further fact of the matter; and it is not identity that matters.

(5) The point of honouring advance directives is to respect autonomy as self-government, not to promote welfare; a directive stands even when overriding it would leave the person better off.

Audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look as though they are? Order the real tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give, what giving it up would cost me elsewhere in the set, and what the cheapest fix is; then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of what I asked.
