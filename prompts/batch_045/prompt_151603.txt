Exam question:
In the Discourse on Method, Descartes argues that nonhuman animals lack minds. Reconstruct his argument, focusing on the two tests he proposes for detecting a rational soul. Then consider the following objection: a modern large language model can produce context-appropriate sentences on almost any topic, so by Descartes's own language test it ought to count as having a mind. How well would Descartes's position handle this case? In your answer, be careful to distinguish (a) Descartes's claim that animals lack thought, (b) his claim about the relation between language and thought, and (c) what he would say about a merely mechanical system that imitates linguistic behavior.

Student answer:
Descartes famously holds that animals are machines. In Part V of the Discourse, he says that even if a machine looked and acted just like a human, we could always tell it was not truly human by two tests. First, it could never use words or signs the way humans do to declare its thoughts in response to whatever is said in its presence. Second, although it might do some tasks very well, it would fail at others because it acts only from the disposition of it's organs, not from knowledge. Since animals fail both tests, Descartes concludes they have no rational soul and hence no thoughts. He thinks they do not reason, and he also thinks that they do not have sensations in any sense at all, because sensation for him is a mode of thinking. So an animal in pain is not really feeling anything; it is just a complicated clockwork mechanism making noises and movements.

Against this, someone might point to a modern LLM. These systems can produce grammatical, context-appropriate text on almost any subject we give them, which looks exactly like the ability to arrange words so as to reply to whatever is said in its presence—the ability Descartes says only a minded being can exercise. Descartes's language test is basically a behavioral test for language use. If the test is the only way we can tell whether another being has a mind, then Descartes would have to accept that a system that passes it has a mind. It does not matter that the LLM has no flesh-and-blood body or animal spirits; Descartes's criterion is about the use of signs, not about the material the machine is made of. So either Descartes's test counts the LLM as minded, or Descartes's test is just a prejudice against animals and machines.
