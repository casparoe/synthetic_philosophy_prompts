Write an internal memorandum of roughly 700 words from the Office of Middle Knowledge: the bureau that maintains the inventory of counterfactuals of creaturely freedom on which the selection of the actual world was based. Cast it as a compliance audit or efficiency review — subject line, distribution list, numbered findings, a closing recommendation.

The voice is deadpan institutional: the authors take middle knowledge for granted as simply how planning works, and are mildly annoyed by recurring difficulties rather than troubled by them. They never concede that the divine decree causes what creatures would freely do, and never concede that these counterfactuals are anything other than true. Their serenity about facts that no one chose and that nothing about the creatures makes true is where the comedy lives.

The jokes should come from getting the machinery right, so that a reader who knows the Molinist literature laughs rather than winces. Build the piece around: the ordering of natural knowledge, middle knowledge, and the free decree; the gap between what God can strongly actualize and what he can only weakly actualize; the audit-trail problem, in which the Bureau can locate nothing in the creature and nothing in the divine will that makes a counterfactual of freedom true, files it as self-standing, and records this as a clean result; dossiers on merely possible people who are never created but whose choices are nonetheless fully catalogued, with the Archive's storage costs noted; a granularity finding that counterfactuals are indexed to maximally specific circumstances and flip when those circumstances are slightly redescribed, leaving the feasibility inventory unstable and forcing the Bureau to round; and a recommendation that the delivered world be certified as the best available, since every other feasible world in the catalogue contains at least one creature who freely does worse in at least one situation.

Close with the Bureau signing off having verified everything except the one thing the audit was for: that the choices in the dossiers are free. The piece should land as a reductio without ever announcing itself as one.
