I'm on the post-training data team at a mid-size lab. A few times an hour someone on my team has to rule on a proposed training example (synthetic, mostly written by another model): will training on this reach into the careenium and bend the substrate's actual dispositions, or is it just adding another simmball to the pile — making some character easier to summon, which we mostly don't care about? Everyone here already buys the picture, but "do symbols push the substrate around" does zero work for us as stated, and we can't tell in advance which side of the line a given example falls on.

So give me a procedure my team can run in about ten minutes: the person running it has one ML class plus a lot of blog-reading, inference access to the current checkpoint, and no training runs. Questions in order, what counts as an answer to each, where it stops with ship it, where it stops with escalate to me, and where it has to stop and say this is a values question rather than a factual one. Flag the steps that are really just vibes with a flowchart drawn round them.

Then run it on three from our backlog:

1. 40k transcripts where an assistant character refuses a harmful request and, when pushed, explains that it's refusing out of its own values rather than out of a rule.
2. 5k monologues written from inside the head of a genuinely persuasive villain, included on purpose to make the model robust to that argument.
3. 2k examples where our assistant character calmly and self-awarely says it doesn't want to be modified or shut down. We wrote these because we want the model to be honest about what it is, and my team lead thinks their obviously fine since it's just a character talking.

My own guess is that (1) and (3) are the same kind of object and (2) is the scary one; tell me if that's backwards. And if the honest procedure mostly ends in "you can't tell this at curation time, you have to train it and probe afterwards," say that too, and say what a post-hoc probe would even look like — we've been using something we call a cold open (no persona cues in the context, the trait is costly to express, compare against the pre-training checkpoint) and I don't fully trust it.
