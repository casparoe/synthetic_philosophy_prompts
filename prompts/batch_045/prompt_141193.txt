I price risk for a small mutual that insures self-build and off-grid houses — about 400 policies, so half the classes I could pick are three claims and a hunch. Every house belongs to dozens of classes that all point at different numbers, and I keep catching myself choosing the one that gives the answer I want: either so broad it's meaningless or so narrow it's me and two sheds. I want a written procedure I can run on each new quote: how to choose the class, how far to narrow it before the data stops supporting it, what to do when two perfectly reasonable classes disagree wildly, when to say I definately can't produce a defensible number at all, and what to write down so a lay complaints panel can see I wasn't just picking a class to suit myself. Constraints: roughly twenty minuets per quote, the classes have to be pre-registered with the board each year so I can't invent them ad hoc, and the board has banned any class defined partly by postcode. Then run it on three: a straw-bale house, a house with a subsidence claim from the previous owner, and one where the only class with enough data in it is ruled out by the ban. Take a definite line on each rather than listing my options, and be straight about where the "procedure" is really just my judgement in a nice suit.
