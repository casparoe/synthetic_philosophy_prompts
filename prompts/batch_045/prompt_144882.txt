ok so I'm in this group chat with like six people from my school and we have been having the same argument for two weeks now and it will not die, and I got voluntold to write up something we can all put our names to so we can move on with our lives. Basically: half of us think that if someone spends 200 hours building a base on a survival server and another player sneaks in and griefs it, or talks them into a fake trade and takes their rare item, that is a real thing that really happened to that person and the person who did it did something genuinely wrong in more or less the way destroying their actual bike would be wrong. The other half keeps saying its just rows in a database, nothing was literally destroyed, an admin can roll the server back or just dupe the item back, and words like stealing and harm are metaphors we borrow from real life because theyre convenient. Call the first camp the Realists and the second the Deflationists. I dont want a verdict on who is right and I really dont want a essay that just says it depends what you mean by real and then stops — if part of our fight is only about the word, say exactly which part and why, and then get to the part that would still be there after we fix the vocabulary.

What I want is the document we should have written: first a numbered list of claims both sides can actually sign — about what happened in those cases, about what the players experienced, about what the code did, about what the norms of the server were — then a table of the disagreements where each row is phrased so that the person holding that view would read it and go yes, that is what I think, and not yeah I guess. Then I want the cruxes, the smaller questions where if we answered one of them at least one side would have to move. I've got a bunch of cases I want used as the tests, because I think some of them split our two camps in ways people havent noticed: (1) the admin rolls back the server from a backup an hour later, so nothing is permanently gone — was the harm real while it was happening; (2) a glitch lets someone copy your rare item, you still have yours, they just also have one; (3) someone plays totally offline, never tells a soul, and their save file corrupts; (4) the item was bought with real money from the in-game shop vs. grinded for two hundred hours; (5) someone deletes a digital pet or an NPC that visibly begs not to be deleted, which I think is a different question from whether deleting it hurts the player; (6) the same griefing happens to two players, one of whom is super invested and one of whom genuinely shrugs and says whatever, it was just pixels; (7) a game where raiding is a designed-in mechanic and everyone clicked agree to the rules before playing — is consent to the rules a defence or does the harm still count. For each crux say which case or argument both camps would accept as real evidence about it, and record in advance what each side predicts — what they predict the player will actually report feeling six months later, or what they predict people will judge in the case, or whatever the observable thing is.

One more thing: for each camp, what would they count as evidence or argument that would make them give up their own view, and is there anything at all both camps agree would settle this, even in principle. Please dont cite anything or reference any studies or papers or name any philosophers — none of us can check them, it'll just start a new argument, honestly half the reason we're stuck is people name-dropping stuff. Just arguments and the cases. And keep it as something a bunch of 17 year olds would actually read and sign, not a term paper — short sentences are fine, but I do want the disagreements to be spelled out precisely, like painfully precisely, because every time we've tried to write this ourselves the other side has said that's not what I said.
