Me and two friends have been running a sloppy little reading group on Nāgārjuna and the four-cornered negation (the catuṣkoṭi: a thing exists / does not exist / both / neither) for a few months, and I want to turn what we've argued about into a one-page photocopied handout — a decision tree, not an essay. Everything we keep finding is either devotional or a formal logic paper, and neither is any use for what I want.

Here's the thing I keep getting stuck on, and which I want the tree to actually handle. I don't think there are four corners. I think there's a hidden parameter nobody writes down: whether each corner carries a silent respect-qualifier ("in respect R") and whether R is allowed to shift between the two halves of the "both" corner. So "both" could mean "exists in respect R1 and does not exist in respect R2" (shifting) or "exists and does not exist in one and the same respect, full stop" (fixed), and those feel like completely different animals to me. Please build the logical space out of parameters like that one instead of starting from a list of school names, tell me how many cells that actually gives, then locate the real positions in it and say which cells are empty and why — incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored.

What I need concretely:

- Eight to ten branch nodes, every one phrased as a question someone who has never opened a book on Buddhist philosophy could answer. No branch node may mention a school, a text, an author, or a Sanskrit term.
- The diagnostic cases should all be modern things the tradition never talked about. I want people sorted by what they say about cases, not by which label they memorized. So: does the belief-like state of a large language model that answers "Paris is in France" exist? Does a corporation's decision exist over and above the emails and the meetings? Does the "function" of a stretch of so-called junk DNA exist? Does the pain of a patient in a medically induced coma exist while nobody can report it? Invent at least four more in that vein and put them at the nodes that do the real sorting.
- Leaves: give me these as separate leaves if the tree can genuinely separate them — (i) rejecting all four corners is a claim about the thing (it lacks any intrinsic nature or own-being), (ii) rejecting all four is a claim about the question instead, which gets dissolved rather than answered, (iii) the "both" corner is unqualifiedly and literally true, contradiction and all, (iv) a plain two-valued reading on which "both" is simply contradictory and "neither" simply violates excluded middle, so there were only ever two corners, (v) the therapeutic reading, on which the point of the exercise is to shut down conceptual proliferation rather than to land on any truth. Also include whatever the "neither" corner becomes if it means "true of neither" rather than "I decline to answer" — one member of the group insists that's a fifth corner, and if she's right put it on the tree, and if she's wrong tell me which node was already doing that work.
- Each leaf gets its usual name if it has one, plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment.
- Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot actually separate and say what extra question would be needed; don't smuggle them into one leaf to make the diagram look tidy, I'd rather have the gap.

Please write the whole thing in American English, and sorry for the spellling, it's going straight onto a handout.
