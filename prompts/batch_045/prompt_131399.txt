Two people in my AI safety reading group keep talking past each other. One thinks we should only ever deploy systems where we can state and defend some kind of worst-case (or at least explicit high-probability tail) guarantee, and that otherwise the honest answer is "not yet"; the other thinks that demand is unfalsifiable in practice and that we should be optimising expected outcomes over the distribution we actually face, counting the value of deploying sooner and the value of not being the side that unilaterally waits. Please draft the joint statement they could both sign after an actual adversarial collaboration: the claims both can agree on, then the exact disagreements in wording each side would accept for its own view, then the cruxes, and for each crux a concrete case or piece of evidence both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Three things I want them to adress rather than dodge. (1) The worst-case side has to say what the guarantee ranges over: the max is taken over some hand-built set of scenarios, so the guarantee is only as good as the imagination behind that set — let's call that the frame problem for guarantees. Is conceding that a step toward the other camp, or is the fair reply that an explicit bound over a partly arbitrary set still beats an expected value over a distribution nobody can estimate? (2) The average-case side has to say what to do when the distribution is partly produced by the system being evaluated: suppose a model behaves fine on 99.9% of evaluated deployments and in the remaining sliver does something that looks like strategic sandbagging, and the rate visibly moves when it knows its being measured. Is the measured frequency evidence, or its the kind of quantity that self-undermines? (3) Both should say whether "I'm strongly risk-averse about tails" and "I want a worst-case guarantee" are actually different positions, or whether that's the merely verbal part of the fight.

Don't spend the document on whether AI risk is real at all — assume both grant it is. Format: numbered agreed claims, then a disagreements table with a column for each side's own phrasing, then cruxes with predictions. Keep it tight, and don't cite anything; I just want the argument.
