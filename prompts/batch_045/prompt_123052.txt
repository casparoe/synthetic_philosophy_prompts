So I've had a responsive neurostimulator in for focal epilepsy for about two years, and it logs my intracranial EEG continuously — I can pull the raw data onto my laptop. There's one reproducible signature in there that I noticed about a year ago and gave a name to; I call it "the fuzz". It started out naming how the thing feels, but I've logged it ~200 times since, I can often tell it's coming before it shows up, and lately when the log and how-I-feel disagree I've started siding with the log. Nobody else uses the word, nobody else has ever looked at my data, and I mostly use it to decide things like whether to drive that day.

I got into an argument about whether the word means anything with my old flatmate, who did her PhD on Wittgenstein, and I shure can't work out what her actual test is, because her verdicts on other cases don't line up the way I'd of guessed:

- someone who writes a private mark in a diary whenever a certain feeling shows up and never shows it to anyone: meaningless
- someone who invents a word for a mood and uses it with their partner so the partner knows to give them space, and who sometimes gets corrected — "no, that's not wobbly, that's just tired": fine, meaningful
- a chemo patient rating pain 0–10 for an oncologist who adjusts the dose and occasionally pushes back ("you wrote 3 but you were grimacing"): meaningful
- the exact same 0–10 ratings kept in a notebook nobody ever reads: not meaningful
- someone who names a quality of their visual afterimages and checks it against a paint swatch in their desk drawer, telling no one: not meaningful — she says a private sample is just another private thing
- a pair of twins with a word for an odd interoceptive feeling, who have taught nobody else but do correct each other on it: meaningful

Given that track record, what's she going to say about "the fuzz"? I care much less about the yes/no than about which rule she's really running — something about other people being able to check, or about there being a standard that can contradict me, or about the word doing work in a shared practice — and where my case makes those come apart. And what's the one further case I should throw at her to tell the rules that fit her other verdicts apart?
