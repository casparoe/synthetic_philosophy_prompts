Posting this here because I've definately been going in circles on it for weeks and I keep failing to taboo my own words. Longtime ACX/LW reader, no geology background beyond blog posts and comment sections, so flag it if I'm using a term of art wrong.

I got into an argument at a party about the Anthropocene and realised afterwards that I have a whole cluster of opinions that might not fit together. Roughly where I've landed:

1. The only honest way to date it is the deep-time test: date it by what a stratigrapher with no access to any of our texts could pick out of the rock in ten million years. If a signal won't survive, it shouldn't be doing definitional work.

2. I think the right start date is early — the Columbian exchange, the collapse of Indigenous populations in the Americas after 1492, the dip in atmospheric CO2 recorded in ice cores and the species turnover that followed — not the mid-20th century. The proposal that actually went forward was for 1950 at Crawford Lake with the bomb-test plutonium as the marker, and it got voted down in 2024, apparently largely because the sediment record is so thin and the start date so recent.

3. I don't think the boundary should have to be a single year or a single site. The thing I want to mark unfolded at different times in different places, and I'm fine with a boundary that's spread out rather than isochronous. I remember someone floating that we formalize it as an event rather than an epoch precisely because events are allowed to be spread out in time, and that felt right to me.

4. I think we should defer to the stratigraphers here. The Geologic Time Scale is their object, they have rules about how units get ratified, and if they say no I don't think the rest of us get to just declare an epoch.

5. I also think the rejection barely matters. The word is going to keep doing its work in climate writing, policy and art regardless, and that work seems more important than whether it ends up on the chart.

6. I think it matters a lot that the name not sneak in a "we". The forcing was done by a specific and fairly small slice of humans, and Anthropos flattens that. This is a big part of why I want the early date: it ties the boundary to colonial extraction instead of to something that happened to all of us equally.

7. And I don't think there's a fact of the matter about where epochs begin beyond whatever the relevant body ratifies. Nature doesn't come with joints there. These are conventions we adopt for our purposes.

So: which of these are actually in tension and which only look like they are? My guess is that something in 1, 2 and 6 has to give, but I can't tell which, and I have no idea whether 7 is holding the package together or quietly sawing through it. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, say for each what I'd have to drop or weaken and what that costs me, and then tell me which single revision cleans up the most. Don't give me citations or a reading list, I can find those myself — I want the conceptual audit, and if there's a framing where I get to keep most of this, I'd rather hear that than a lecture on how definitions work.
