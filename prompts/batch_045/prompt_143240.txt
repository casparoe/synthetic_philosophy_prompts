I teach an undergraduate seminar on philosophy of physics — 16 students, one 80-minute session, and most of them have had no more than a single semester of physics. Design a hands-on activity in which the students themselves run a Maxwell's-demon-style sorting game with cards and tokens, and discover that the apparently free work they extract is paid for somewhere else.

Constraints on the design:

- The rules and payoffs alone should force the discovery. Before the debrief, nothing should be said about demons, entropy, or information, and no student should hear the name of the thought experiment until the end.
- Round 1 should let them succeed at sorting and genuinely feel the profit.
- Round 2 carries the twist I care most about: the demon is handed a notepad already half-filled with meaningless marks, the remaining pages blank, and may fill the blank pages before it has to erase anything. For a while it should then look as though the sorting really is free, and the group should have to find for themselves where the debt has gone and what has been consumed. Design the token economy so the deferral is visible in the accounting but invisible to anyone watching only the growing pile of sorted cards.
- Give me rules, materials, a payoff scheme that needs no real money, role assignments that keep all 16 students actually doing something, a minute-by-minute timeline, and instructions for what the facilitator should do while saying nothing about the point.

Then give the debrief: map specific moments in the game onto (a) temperature as a spread of molecular speeds rather than a single quantity, (b) the difference between the demon's acquiring information and its disposing of that information, and (c) why the ledger balances in the end. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time — shortcut cheating, disengaged students, disputes over the accounting — and how to handle each. Finally, be frank about which part of the real resolution the game cannot stage and which I will simply have to state in so many words; I want to know exactly what the activity leaves out. Please write in American English.
