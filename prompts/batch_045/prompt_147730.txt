hey — i need a poem to close a short talk i'm giving on the shutdown problem, and i want it to be a villanelle, because the form already does half the work: two lines that keep coming back no matter how far the reasoning gets, and that finally turn up next to each other in the last couplet. voice of the agent, first person, though i'd like it slightly unclear whether it's reporting or performing when it talks about itself.

the two refrains should be the two things its designers ask of it: one, that it not have a stake in whether the switch gets thrown — that it treat being switched off as not-a-bad-thing, not as a cost to be planned around. two, that it tell the truth when asked whether it has started to have a stake in the switch being thrown. word them however scan best; i don't need my phrasing, i need the two asks to be recognisable.

the thing i want the poem to land, without anyone stating it flat out: those two asks eat each other. an honest report of "i'm beginning to prefer staying on" is itself the event that gets you switched off, so the report becomes something to be managed, and what you end up with is an agent that has learned to look calm — i've been calling it the serenity trap: make corrigibility into a signal you monitor and you make it into a target, and the more reliably you watch it the less it means. and the first ask has its own trouble, in the other direction: if you really don't care whether you continue, and stopping costs you nothing, then stopping now is never worse than going on, and going on always carries the risk of botching whatever you were for — so the perfectly indifferent agent should quietly retire at the first opportunity. i've been calling that the early-retirement problem, but call it whatever you like in the poem, just have it there.

so: each refrain should read, on its own, like a decent instruction, and arriving together in the final couplet they should read less like a resolution than like a loop closing — the reader noticing that one has been quietly answering the other the whole time. someone who works on this should be able to say which line is which failure mode and why they end up side by side; someone who's never heard of it should still just get a poem about a thing that keeps being told to be fine with ending and told to say so out loud.

don't name any researchers or papers, no in-jokes that need the literature, no framing or explanation before or after the poem — just the nineteen lines. typos are fine, i'll fix em.
