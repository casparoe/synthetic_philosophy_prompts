Our philosophy of physics reading group — me, two grad students, a postdoc who works on QFT, and a faculty member who has been through this literature twice and is visibly tired of it — has been going in circles on the hole argument for about four months and I want a diagnosis, not another survey of the positions.

The setup, so we're on the same page: take a model of GR, a manifold with a metric and matter fields on it, and pick a closed region H with no matter in it. Drag everything inside H around by a diffeomorphism that's the identity outside H. Because the equations are generally covariant, the result is another solution: identical to the original everywhere outside H and on its boundary, different in what it assigns to the points inside. If you think manifold points are individuals whose identity doesn't depend on what fields sit on them, the theory then fails to determine what happens in H given everything outside it — that's Earman and Norton's indeterminism charge. The relationalist shrugs: the two models are two descriptions of one physical situation. The sophisticated substantivalist keeps the manifold but only lets diffeomorphism-invariant facts about points be genuine, and also gets out. The gauge theorist says this is just the general point that gauge freedom isn't indeterminism, and that the hole argument is one instance of a general argument about gauge. And there's always someone who says GR has other sources of indeterminism anyway so who cares.

Here's why I think we've stalled. Nobody in the room defends the bare, haecceitistic substantivalism the argument is aimed at. Once the sophisticated substantivalist and the relationalist both say the two hole models are one world, the fight looks like it's about vocabulary. And yet nobody is satisfied, and people keep writing papers. That pattern is suspicious.

My hypothesis, which nobody will engage with: it stopped being a dispute about the ontology of spacetime a while ago and is now a dispute about which structures you're allowed to write into a theory in the first place — background independence as a constraint rather than spacetime ontology as a conclusion — and we're still using the old labels so nobody notices we've changed the question.

The reason I think that: I built a toy version to test it. A 1+1 cellular automaton, finite ring of cells, deterministic local rule. I wanted to run the hole argument on it — mark off a stretch of cells as the hole, relabel the cells inside while holding everything outside fixed, and see whether the rule is indifferent to the relabeling. It isn't. On a fixed lattice the only relabelings that preserve the update rule are the rigid ones, rotations and reflections of the whole ring, because relabeling cells inside my hole breaks their adjacency relations with the cells just outside it. So there is no hole argument for the CA. The postdoc says this is trivial and shows nothing. I think it shows that the hole argument only gets going in a theory with no fixed background structure, and that whether that absence is a requirement or a choice is exactly what's being fought over. I've started calling the CA-with-a-lattice position granular substantivalism, which nobody likes, and the obvious objection is that I've just helped myself to a background structure GR doesn't have — to which my reply is: yes, now tell me why I can't, without assuming the conclusion.

So: what kind of stall is this? Is it verbal, two different questions sharing a name (what exists vs. how models represent vs. what counts as a physical degree of freedom), a hidden formal question about background structure or gauge or the initial value formulation, or bedrock modal intuitions about whether bare numerical difference can be a real difference? Argue for one, using what each side actually says, and don't just list the views. Then tell me honestly what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a distinction, a formal result, a different question — or why nothing should be expected. And tell me whether my toy model is doing real work or is a distraction I should definately drop.
