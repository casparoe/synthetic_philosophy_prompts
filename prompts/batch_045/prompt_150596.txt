Case one: my bot had a lookahead — it could spin up a copy of its own decision code and run it in a simulated game. Against a normal opponent this was great. Against a copy of itself, both bots just recurse forever. I fixed that with a depth limit, but then the winner was decided entirely by whatever dumb fallback move the depth-limited bot emits at the bottom, which was basically arbitrary — the "real" reasoning never actually happened. So that one I think I understand, it's just recursion plus a bad base case.

Case two is the one I care about. I tried a different design: instead of simulating the whole decision code, give the bot a world model — a steppable simulation of the game board that includes the bot's own body — and let it evaluate "what happens if I move left" vs "what happens if I move right". The problem is that the model contains my own decision procedure, so in the simulated branch the sim-bot does whatever the real bot is actuallly going to do, not "left". Every branch I plug in comes out identical to the real future, so there's nothing to compare. When I hacked it by hard-forcing the sim-bot's move, I got clean comparable branches — but they were branches where my decision procedure didn't run, which isn't the world I'm in, so I'm not sure the comparison is supposed to mean anything. And when I tried just deleting my own body from the model, I could compare branches fine, but they were about a world where I never moved.

Case three came out of a fix for case two: I let the bot just read off what its own decision procedure is going to output and skip deliberating. If the readout is perfect, then whatever "thinking" it does on top is theater — it outputs what it was going to output anyway. If the readout is like 90% reliable, the bot has to ask "what should I plan to do in the 10% of cases where the readout says something I wasn't expecting?", and that question seems to be about a situation the bot itself thinks won't happen, but it still has to pick a policy inside it, and every answer I try feels circular.
