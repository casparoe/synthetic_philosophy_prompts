My friend Dina and I got into a big argument about emotions at a barbecue a couple of weeks ago and I've been turning it over in my head ever since (I drive a delivery van at night so I have a lot of time to think, probably too much). She has really firm opinions about what counts as an emotion and what doesn't, and I started writing down her verdicts because I couldn't work out what her actual rule is. Neither of us has any philosophy background, she just reads a lot of popular science and is very hard to argue with. Her track record: anger at a coworker who took credit for her work — yes, an emotion, and she says it can be undeserved, and in fact hers vanished the moment she found out the coworker had been told to present it; fear watching a horror film even though you know nothing is after you — yes; being startled when a car backfires — no, she says that's just a reflex; hunger, thirst, needing the toilet — no, those are drives; that 3am dread where you couldn't say what you're afraid of — yes, she says it's about everything and nothing, and that one is the reason I don't think her rule is just about whether the feeling is aimed at something; schadenfreude at a politician's misfortune — yes, and a mean one; awe standing under a huge redwood — yes; thinking a stranger's face looks familiar — no; cilantro tasting soapy to her — no; an earworm she couldn't shake — no, she said that's an itch in your head not a feeling; being creeped out walking past an empty house at night even after she'd checked it was empty — yes; secondhand embarrassment watching a cringey reality TV moment — yes. She also said once, and this is the bit I keep coming back to, that an emotion is the kind of thing that can be wrong the way a guess can be wrong, not the way a taste can be — but then she counts the horror film fear, which you can't reason your way out of at all.

So I want to spring two new cases on her and I'd like to know which way she'll go. First: that creepy, almost nauseating feeling you get from a super realistic robot face or a slightly-off CGI face in a video game. You know perfectly well it isn't a person, nothing about it is dangerous or bad, there's no story you're reacting to, and telling yourself it's just pixels does absolutely nothing — the feeling just sits there and doesn't move. Second: being angry with someone for years without ever feeling anything. You'd still say "I'm angry with him," you'd still say the anger is deserved, you'd refuse to be in the same room as him, but nothing stirs in you when you think about him — it's more like a settled verdict you've filed away. Is that an emotion on her rules?

What I'm actually after: what rule or rules fit all her verdicts so far, what each of those rules predicts about these two cases, which one you'd bet she's really running and why, and then — this is the part I care most about — what further case I should throw at her to tell the surviving rules apart, since I suspect these two pull in opposite directions and might just cancel out. If her verdicts genuinely don't settle it, say so and tell me what she'd be committing to either way.
