I've been trying to work out what I actually think privacy is, by trusting my gut on a list of cases and then asking what principle the verdicts add up to. I keep failing to find one principle that fits them all, and I'd like your help. No reading lists, citations, or references, please — I can't look anything up, and what I want is the principle itself, not where to read about it.

Here are the cases and my verdicts.

**C1.** My landlord installs a device in my apartment that watches continuously and processes the video on the spot, purely to count how many people are in the room so the heating can be adjusted. Footage is deleted every thirty seconds. No human ever sees it, nothing about what I do is ever stored, and nothing about me is ever inferred by anyone. My verdict: a clear and serious violation — the worst thing on this list. But if the identical device with the identical setup were in the building's shared lobby, which I walk through every day, I'd say it was fine, and I can't say what the difference is.

**C2.** A data broker assembles a dossier about me — address, employer, hometown, the names of the people I live with, my political donations — and sells it to an insurer. Every item in it comes from a page I myself put up or agreed to publish. My verdict: a violation, and a worse one than any single one of those publications was.

**C3.** A stranger who knows nothing about me goes and reads those same public pages, one at a time, over a week, and forms roughly the picture of me the dossier contains. My verdict: no violation at all. This is the pair I can't reconcile: C2 and C3 seem to differ only in that the broker's copy is cheaper to use.

**C4.** The model on my phone — it runs entirely on the device, and nothing is transmitted anywhere — infers from my typing rhythm and app-switching that I am probably pregnant, and starts showing me prenatal ads. The inference is true. I hadn't told anyone. No one at any company ever learns it. My verdict: a violation, though a milder one than C1.

**C5.** My ex-partner, who knows all manner of intimate things about me from the years we lived together, passes a few of them to an insurer that sets premiums with them. Nothing was accessed, hacked, bought, or inferred; they simply said what they already knew. My verdict: something is badly wrong here, and my first instinct is to call it a violation of my privacy — but it may be a different kind of wrong wearing privacy's clothes, and I can't tell which.

**C6.** A colleague tells people I have a gambling problem. It is false, and it costs me a great deal. My verdict: a serious wrong, but not a violation of my privacy — which I think matters, because it suggests privacy isn't about what others believe about me, or about my reputation.

**C7.** A close friend, over months, notices changes in me and works out correctly that I'm depressed. She never asks, never looks at anything of mine, never opens anything. She just starts checking in on me more. My verdict: I go back and forth. Sometimes this feels like the most intimate intrusion imaginable; sometimes it feels like the precise opposite of one.

My own rule of thumb, such as it is: privacy is violated when someone makes me the object of their attention in a way I haven't agreed to. That gets C1 and C4, but it also says the stranger in C3 and the colleague in C6 violate my privacy, and I don't believe that. And the common alternative — that privacy is my control over information about myself — seems to get C2 and C3 backwards and to have nothing to say about C4, where no information goes anywhere.

So: is there one principle that captures all seven verdicts, or close, and what does it say about C7? If no single principle does the work, give me the two or three best candidates, state each precisely enough that I could apply it to a case you haven't seen, and tell me plainly which of my verdicts each one forces me to give up — including whether C2 and C3 can really come apart, or whether one of them has to go. I'd also like to know whether my seven cases even settle the question, or whether they are consistent with several quite different theories that would diverge elsewhere.

Please give me, first, at most about 500 words of prose: the candidates, the tension between them, which of my verdicts each sacrifices, and your recommendation. Say plainly how confident you are in that recommendation, and single out the one point in your answer you are least sure of and what would change your mind about it.
