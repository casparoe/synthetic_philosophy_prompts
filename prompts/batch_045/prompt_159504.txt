I work on latency in a game engine — netcode, client-side prediction and rollback, frame pacing, the whole display pipeline — been doing it about eight years now, and I have basically no philosophy past one intro class in college. I was listening to a podcast on consciousness with a friend from work (also an engineer, also smug about it) and they got onto something called the color phi phenomenon: you flash a red dot in one spot, then a fraction of a second later a green dot a bit further along, and what people report isn't two dots but a single dot sliding across that turns green somewhere in the middle of its path — so it looks like they see green before the green dot has actually been shown. Apparently there are two named hypotheses about this, Orwellian and Stalinesque, one where the experience happens on time and the record gets doctored afterward, one where the content is already doctored by the time it reaches consciousness, and my friend's take was that this is obviously just buffering and there's no real question left once you describe it properly.

I'd like to test that. Could you restate the phenomenon, the two hypotheses, and whatever conclusion people draw from them as carefully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use — simulation ticks versus rendered frames, the fixed delay between an input and the frame that actually gets presented, a write-once event log versus a state buffer you can rewrite in place, a post-process pass that runs before present versus doctoring a replay after it's already been displayed, rollback re-simulating a window you've already shown, that kind of thing. Don't dumb it down, and please flag the places where you had to fudge something to make it fit, or where you quietly imported an assumption that isn't in my vocabulary. I'd also like to know whether the claim that the two hypotheses can't be told apart is something that falls out of the translation, or something you have to add by hand.

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. What translates clean, what translates only with distortion, and what has no expression at all in the pipeline vocabulary — and for whatever's left over, is that because my vocabulary is missing something real, or because the philosophers' distinction was thinner than it sounds? My own hunch is that my vocabulary cheats here, because it gives me a perfectly sharp fact about which frame hit the panel and when, and that might be exactly the thing the debate is denying. Last thing: say how confident you are in each of the main claims you make, and single out the one point you're least sure about.
