I've been arguing about Twin Earth cases on LessWrong and adjacent blogs for something like fifteen years, and I've noticed that I keep leaning on an analogy I have never seen audited properly. I'd like that audit, and I'd like it done on a variant of the case that I've never gotten a straight answer about.

The analogy I use: a speaker's word is like a pointer variable in a running process. What is in the head is the variable — a location, an address — and it is local, inspectable, and fully specified by the process's own state. What the address picks out lives in memory the process does not own. Oscar's token of 'water' and Twin Oscar's are two variables holding the same address in two processes forked from a common ancestor: same internal state, different referents, and no amount of inspecting the variables from inside tells you which liquid you are pointing at. I use it to license two conclusions — first, that what Twin Earth shows is that reference is relational, not that meaning or mental content is; second, that externalism therefore cannot be recruited to argue that a system with thin causal contact with the world (a language model, a brain in a vat) fails to understand anything or has no contentful internal states.

The variant I want folded in: keep the fork, but make the divergence latent. Two systems with identical weights and identical training data, instantiated in environments that are indistinguishable by any measurement either can perform, where the liquids differ only under conditions neither will ever encounter. Same address, different referent, no possible evidence of the difference. Does meaning differ, on the externalist picture? If yes, am I committed to something indexical hidden inside every natural-kind term, and is that cost or a feature? For a second probe, take the switching version: a subject moved back and forth between the two environments often enough that no intrinsic change is doing the work.

What I want is the correspondence table (variable, address, memory, fork, interpreter or linker, read/write access — each mapped onto the corresponding item in the semantic case), then the disanalogies, sorted by whether they matter for the conclusions I'm drawing rather than merely being true. I'm especially suspicious of three places: an address is a structural description of a location, so the pointer picture may quietly import the descriptivism the original case was supposed to refute; the analogy may presuppose that there is a narrow content for the pointer to be made of, which is the thing under dispute; and the address had to be assigned by something outside the process, which may just relocate the external dependence one level up rather than remove it. Say for each whether it sinks my conclusions or leaves them standing, and say plainly if the analogy was carrying the argument the whole time. If there's a better mapping — I've wondered about relative pathnames resolved against an environment variable the process didn't set but can read — give it, and say what it does better and what it gives up.

Finally, be explicit about epistemic status: separate what is settled orthodoxy about these cases from what is genuinely contested among people who work on them (whether content externalism generalizes beyond natural-kind and observational terms, whether narrow content exists, whether externalism threatens privileged self-knowledge, whether any of it bears on artificial systems), attach rough confidence to each claim, and flag anywhere you are oneself taking a side in a live dispute rather than reporting one.
