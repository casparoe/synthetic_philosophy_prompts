The thesis, short version. Hilbert's Hotel is supposed to show that an actual infinite is coherent: the rearrangements feel bizarre but nothing contradicts anything, so the anti-infinitist's "absurdity" charge fails. I argue the standard presentation smuggles in more than the mere existence of bijections. It assumes the manager can carry out rearrangements via a single finite, uniform instruction that each guest can evaluate using their own room number alone — one finite programme posted in the lobby, no infinite lookup table, no private whisper to each guest. Call it the finite-programme constraint. Impose it and some perfectly well-defined rearrangements of a countable hotel become unperformable. My central case is gap-closing: the manager announces that the guests whose room numbers lie in some infinite, co-infinite set S are to stay and everyone else leaves, and the stayers must compress down so there are no gaps. The compression map — send each member of S to its rank in the induced ordering — exists as a set of ordered pairs, sure, but it is only computable relative to an oracle for S; for non-computable S like the halting set there's no finite lobby notice that lets each guest work out where to go, because whether you go to room k depends on how many smaller-numbered guests stayed. Second case I lean on: "prior-notice lockout," where every guest has to decide, from their own number and the one posted notice, whether to shift left one room or stay put, with no collisions — and the target configuration is again an arbitrary infinite set.

So the payoff I'm defending: cardinality is fine, Cantor is fine, but the hotel's operational powers are strictly weaker than its size suggests, and neither the friends of actual infinity nor the finitists have noticed that there's a third position here — infinite totalities that are real but not fully actable-on. I'm explicit that this does not rescue finitism, and in the last five minutes I gesture at supertasks and at whether an infinite physical cosmology would inherit the constraint.
