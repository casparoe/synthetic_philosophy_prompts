Longtime LW / ACX lurker, first time actually posting. I've ended up at what I take to be the boring answer to the special composition question: composition happens always — any bunch of things whatsoever makes a further thing, including the sum of my left shoe and the tip of the Eiffel Tower — and the reason that sounds mad is that our object-concept is basically a trained classifier over spatiotemporal-causal signatures, so gerrymandered sums feel like non-objects the same way adversarial examples feel like non-images. Call it classifier-universalism: unrestricted composition, plus the claim that every appearance of a substantive answer to 'when do some things compose something?' is an artifact of that classifier.

What would count against it? I want defeaters ranked fatal-to-merely-awkward, and I want the ones a committed universalist would themselves concede, not just the ones critics repeat. Four things I can't settle: (i) whether the classifier story does real work or is just a redescription of the intuition its supposed to explain away — if a community that never acquired the classifier still balked at gerrymandered sums, is that a knockdown; (ii) whether 'always' is an answer to the question or a refusal to answer it, and if it's a refusal, whether a refusal that leaves all the practice untouched is even a cost; (iii) whether I'm now stuck with more objects than there could be singular thoughts about objects, and whether that pairing is unstable; (iv) whether the view has insulated itself — any counterexample intuition gets filed as classifier noise — and whether that insulation is fatal, or whether a view saying 'there was never a substantive question here' is entitled to it.

What I'd personally find most moving: something showing the dissolution leaves a residue, e.g. that middle-sized causally integrated objects recur in every community's ontology in a way pure pragmatics doesn't predict. Also please mark clearly which bits are settled among people who actually work on this and which are live disputes — I keep getting confident-sounding answers I suspect are one side of the literature.
