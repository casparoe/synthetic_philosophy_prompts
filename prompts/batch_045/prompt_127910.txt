putting together a short statement of where Ive landed on shared agency and joint action, for a reading group, and I want someone to check whether it actually cohere's before I stand up and defend it. Here's the set, more or less:

(1) Shared intention is nothing over and above the right kind of interlocking individual intentions — each of us intends that we do it together, each intends to do their part by way of the other doing theirs, and all of it is out in the open between us. No irreducible group subject, no primitive joint commitment, nothing collective floating above the individual plan states. Basically the reductionist picture.

(2) Still, acting together really does generate obligations, and I mean obligations, not just extra reasons. If you walk away halfway through what we were doing, you owe me something, and Im not being childish if I resent you and demand an account. Its directed at your co-participant specifically, and I dont want to cash it in as a promise, or as a duty not to induce reliance, or as good manners. It comes from the jointness itself.

(3) Structured groups can be agents in their own right: a company or a ministry can have attitudes of its own, can intend things, can be blameworthy, and the group intention isnt shorthand for some member's intention or for the members intentions added up.

(4) Methodologically, start from the grown-up cases — two people who explicitly agreed to paint the house together, a duet — get the conditions right there, and only afterwards ask how far down the scale the account reaches. The minimal cases come at the end, as a test, not at the beginning as data.

(5) Even so, I think the minimal cases are the real thing and not a watered-down cousin: toddlers coordinating in social play, two strangers wordlessly getting a heavy door open, that sort of case. Genuinely acting together, not just behaviour that happens to mesh.

(6) And I dont think joint action requires anybody to have an occurrent thought about the shared activity while its going on. A couple making breakfast together, a band improvising — nobody is representing the joint goal at the moment, and it still counts.

So where does this set actually break, and where does it only look like it breaks? If I had to guess I'd say (3) is the one that sits worst with (1), and Im least sure about (6), but I honestly cant locate the problem and I'd rather not give any of them up. What I want is an audit: find the real tensions, rank them by how bad they are, and for each one say what I'd have to drop or weaken and roughly what it would cost me. And please be explicit about any pair that only seems to conflict — I'd rather be told two of these are fine together than get six problems of equal weight.
