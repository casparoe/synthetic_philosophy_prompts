bit of an odd request but here goes. I'm a retired experimental physicist (solid state, thirty-odd years) and over the last couple of years I've started reading philosophy of time, mostly because I got annoyed at a documentary. I've written down roughly where I've landed and I'd like to know weather it actually hangs together, because I have a nagging feeling I'm quietly helping myself to something. The list is roughly in the order I picked the beliefs up:

1. I think the Rietdijk–Putnam argument is basically sound. Given special relativity, for any event anywhere there's some frame in which it's simultaneous with my here-and-now, so there's no non-arbitrary way to carve out a "present" that's real while the rest isn't. Hence the block universe: all times equally real, no ontological difference between past, present and future.

2. I'm happy with the frame-relativity verdict in the Andromeda sort of case. Two people passing each other on the street, one walking toward Andromeda and one away, disagree about whether some event over there has happened yet, and I want to say there's just no further fact about who is right. I feel no pull toward there having to be a real fact of the matter.

3. Presentism I take to be simply false, and the growing-block and moving-spotlight pictures strike me as unmotivated once you've seen 1.

4. But I want to keep the future being open, and I mean something stronger than "I don't happen to know it yet". Quantum mechanics is indeterministic, so at least some future events aren't fixed by everything that's happened so far, and that's the sense in which deliberating is worth doing and the past is closed off in a way the future isn't.

5. The arrow of time — why there are records and memories of the past but not of the future, why we act toward later rather than earlier — I think is fully explained by the universe having started in a very low-entropy state plus ordinary statistical mechanics. No extra metaphysics needed. I'd also say it'd be unmotivated to stick a second low-entropy boundary condition on the far end just because the block looks symmetric.

6. I dont think the block universe threatens free will or responsibility. Could-have-done-otherwise is a counterfactual matter, and the block simply contains the fact that I deliberated and then did what I did.

7. Methodologically, I think the metaphysics of time should defer to physics: whatever our best physics says, goes. Relatedly, I take cosmology to hand us a genuinely distinguished slicing of spacetime, the comoving frame in which the cosmic microwave background is isotropic, which is why it's meaningful to say the universe is 13.8 billion years old. Simultaneity isn't only a convention once you're doing cosmology.

So: is that a consistent package? My worry is somewhere around 4 and 7 but I can't quite put my finger on it. Could you go through it pair by pair and tell me which pairs genuinely clash and which only look like they do, rank the real ones by how bad they are, and for each real one say what the cheapest repair is and what it would cost me? Then tell me which single revision would tidy up the most, and which commitment you'd drop. I genuinely don't know which one I'd give up, so don't take your cue from me on that.
