Take the 2006 imaging study as a case study for the access/phenomenal distinction and for global workspace theory: the one where a woman diagnosed as being in a persistent vegetative state after a traumatic brain injury was put into a scanner, told to imagine playing tennis and then to imagine walking from room to room in her house, and produced activation in roughly the regions healthy controls produce when performing those same imagery tasks — motor-association areas for the tennis, place-and-navigation areas for the house — while showing no behavioural sign of awareness at the bedside. Take the broad shape of that finding as given, along with the later extension in which a comparably diagnosed patient used two different imagery tasks as yes-and-no answers; I'm going from memory on the details, and I don't want the numbers or the follow-up literature.

What I want is a disciplined mapping rather than a survey. Is instructed, task-compliant, behaviourally invisible imagery a case of access consciousness on Block's definition, given that its only reliable output is a change in the BOLD signal? Does global workspace theory count a broadcast as having occurred when the consumers it normally feeds — report, executive control, action selection — are damaged or silent, or does it require an actual consumer rather than a merely available one? Which features of the case does the theory explain, which does it only accommodate after the fact, and where is it silent? I especially want the epistemology: since any evidence we can get from a non-reporting subject arrives through an access-shaped channel, say whether this episode could in principle be evidence about phenomenal consciousness at all, or whether it is evidence about access wearing phenomenal clothing.

Then push on one configuration I haven't seen handled. Suppose a scan on the same kind of patient shows the task-specific signature arising spontaneously, with no instruction and no cue, and without reaching frontal or executive regions at all. Classify that pattern by the theory's own lights, and say whether it should tell us more or less than the instructed case did.

Attach rough confidence levels to each of your main claims, and single out the point you are least sure of.
