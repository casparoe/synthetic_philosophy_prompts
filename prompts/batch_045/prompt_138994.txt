But her amendment is that the conjecture has the wrong object: what's doing the work isn't the survival of the biological species but the survival of a "normative community" — an intergenerational chain of beings who can recognize, take up, contest, and be answerable to the reasons we now have. So on her view extinction is a matter of degree rather than a binary, biological extinction wouldn't be bad at all if a successor population (uploads, a successor species, artificial agents trained into our practices) inherits the chain, and — this is the part I find wildest — some ways of humanity surviving are worse than extinction, e.g. a humanity that persists for millions of years but in which nobody is answerable to reasons anymore because everyone is permanently in some wireheading/flow state. She also insists the value here doesn't scale: it doesn't get worse the longer or bigger the future is, so she resists the longtermist inference to "maximize expected future people," while still insisting the loss is a loss for *us now*, agent-relatively.
