Probably a dumb question, but I've been running the Ship of Theseus thing on friends as a dinner-table game and I keep hitting the same wrinkle, and I don't have a word for it.

Setup: every plank gets swapped out one at a time over a year, and the old planks go into a shed. Now change only what happens to the shed. Version A: at the end of the year the old planks are burned for firewood. Everyone says immediately, with no hedging at all, that the ship in the harbour is Theseus's ship, nothing to discuss. Version B: the old planks get reassembled into a seaworthy ship. Now the same people stall, or split down the middle, or say the question has no answer. But nothing about the harbour ship differs between A and B — same plank-by-plank history, same crew, same continuous use. Something done to a pile of wood across town changed whether *that* ship is the same ship.

Same effect with a band: swap the members out one at a time until none of the founders is left, nobody minds; then mention that the founders are all alive and now tour together under the old name, and suddenly everyone wants a verdict.

Probes I've tried: the reassembly happens two hundred years later, long after the harbour ship has rotted — does that retroactively unsettle what was in the harbour in year one? And if the planks are never reassembled but just sit there, reassembly being merely possible, does the hedging come back anyway?

I know there's a literature on the paradox itself, and I've seen people say there no fact of the matter, or that it's just vagueness — but that's not my question: in version A people are perfectly confident, and nobody cares where the cutoff falls between 50 and 51 planks. What I want to know is whether this thing has been isolated and named: that the existence of a *rival candidate* (actual, possible, or merely future) can unsettle an identity judgement even when the object being judged and its whole history are held fixed. If it has a name, what exactly is it claimed to be — a constraint on what can serve as an identity criterion, something about how we apply sortal terms, a competition between candidates for a role? If it doesn't, name it and define it, and tell me where its edges are: how good a rival has to be to count, whether it needs to be actual rather than possible, whether it has to be contemporaneous, what happens when there are three, and how it differs from ordinary vagueness and from the flat claim that there's no fact of the matter. I'm not after a label, I'm after the carving.
