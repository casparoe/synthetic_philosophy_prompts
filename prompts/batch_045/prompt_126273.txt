Dennett, working from memory research and REM studies, reached the same conclusion by a scientific route: because nothing is recorded during the night and the memory of the dream is laid down at the instant of waking, there is no fact of the matter about what was experienced, and the dream is manufactured on the spot, the brain confabulating a narrative and then mistaking it for a recollection — like a log file rebuilt from a crash dump rather than a film that was actually playing. Both therefore hold that no conscious experience occurs during sleep; they differ only in that Malcolm got there by conceptual analysis and Dennett by neuroscience.
