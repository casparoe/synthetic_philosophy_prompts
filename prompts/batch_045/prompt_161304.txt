Here's how I currently understand it, tell me if I've garbled it: assume each person's preferences over gambles satisfy the von Neumann-Morgenstern axioms, so each person gets a utility function that's unique only up to positive affine rescaling; assume society's preferences over gambles also satisfy those axioms; assume Pareto, in the sense that if nobody is made worse off and somebody is made better off that's a social improvement; and out falls the result that social welfare has to be a nonnegative weighted sum of individual utilities. What I want from you is a translation into the vocabulary I actually use, plus a report on what gets lost. Assume I'm comfortable with: per-objective losses, linear scalarization of a multi-objective problem and why weights are hyperparameters, dominance and Pareto fronts, expected loss under a stochastic policy, risk-sensitive objectives that optimize something other than the mean (worst-case group performance, CVaR-ish things), and the fact that a per-task loss is only identified up to scale and offset so comparing scales across tasks is always a convention. Assume I have no philosophy vocabulary beyond what I've picked up from blog posts, and — important — don't give me a reading list, paper titles, footnotes or citations: I have no library access and can't chase anything up, so explain whatever argument you rely on in your own words and say things like "there's a standard objection that goes roughly like this" instead of attributing it.
