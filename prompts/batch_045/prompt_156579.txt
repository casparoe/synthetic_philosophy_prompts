hey — i need a favour for a thing i'm putting together. write me an internal launch memo from the "Public Experience" team of a big news-and-socials platform, announcing a new product called The Public. About 700 words: subject line, three or four numbered sections, short FAQ at the bottom.

the joke has to be a Kierkegaard joke, not a "social media bad" joke. what i want skewered is the presses self-image — that there is already a public out there with opinions, and the platform merely addresses it, serves it, gives it a voice. the memo should be proudly taking credit for having built the very public it claims only to have found, without ever noticing that that is what it is doing. other things to get right and then exaggerate:

- the public has no situation and no simultaneity: nobody is anywhere, nobody is with anybody, nobody has anything at stake. a feature that strips location, credentials and consequences out of every post would be a nice concrete way to show that.
- levelling: whatever sticks out gets pulled back down, and the user who actually knows or actually cares is treated as a bug rather than a user. give the levelling feature a name and a launch date.
- reflection instead of decision: infinite commentary, takes, "the conversation", and never once anything that anybody does. the engagement metric should reward exactly that.
- the public can be outraged, can demand an apology, can be consulted, can be heard — but cannot act, because it isn't anybody. land this hardest in the FAQ; i think "can The Public recieve an apology" is the funniest item in the document and it should be played completely straight.

two more requirements. somewhere in the middle there has to be one real, particular human detail — a named subscriber, something specific and slightly sad — and the memo has to metabolise it into a metric without pausing. and at the very end i want one line from a junior engineer that leaks through, someone with a name and a place and something they're risking, so the reader feels the contrast without anybody pointing at it.

don't name Kierkegaard, don't quote him, no Danish, no moral at the end. someone who has never read him should still be able to tell what's being made fun of, and someone who has should think it's fair rather than a cartoon. deadpan the whole way through.
