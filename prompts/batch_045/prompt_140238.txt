my dad has early-stage alzheimers and about two years ago, before it really showed, he filled out one of those living will documents saying he wouldn't want to be kept going if he couldn't follow his music anymore or recognise us. I've been reading about it on my own and two things bother me.

First, the same people seem to say both that it's still him so the earlier decision is the one that counts, and that he's changed enough that what he says now doesn't count -- those feel like opposite claims and I don't see how you get both. Second, everyone reaches for odysseus tied to the mast, but that doesn't fit: odysseus wanted to get home the whole time, he just didn't trust himself in the moment. My dad doesn't seem to want the same thing more weakly, he seems to want something else. Though when I watch him in the garden, genuinely content, listening to the same opera over and over, I honestly can't tell whether that's exactly what he was afraid of or whether it's fine by the standard he actually cared about, since what he always said mattered wasn't feeling good but his life still making a kind of sense to him.

I want to read something at his birthday, not a eulogy -- his own words coming back to him. Could you write me a pantoum, four quatrains, in the voice of the earlier self addressing the later one, where the lines that repeat are phrases straight out of the directive and come back changed by their new surroundings, and where the last line of the poem is the first line again. Don't tell me which of them is right, and no circle-of-life stuff.
