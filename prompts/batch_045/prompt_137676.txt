I convene a small reading group that meets in the back room of a bookshop, and the last two sessions went nowhere on the topic of karma as a theory of moral desert. The disagreement is stubborn because it is not really about karma: one member thinks karma is just retributivism with extra metaphysical machinery bolted on, another thinks it is a theory of moral education with no desert in it at all, and a third thinks the word karma does not make a desert claim in the first place but rather offers an explanation of why suffering is not randomly distributed. I am a retired actuary, which is probably why I keep hearing all of this as a question about a ledger: who holds the account, what is denominated in it, whether the debt accrues interest, whether it can be transferred, whether it expires. What I want is not another overview but a sorting instrument we can actually use on each other, so that people get classified by what they say about cases rather than by which tradition they feel warmly about.

So please build me a decision tree I can print as a one-page handout — plain text, numbered nodes with arrows to the next node or to a leaf, roughly twelve branch nodes and at most ten leaves, plus a short path key so a reader can note their answers and look up where they landed. Every branch node must be a concrete case or claim that a person can answer without knowing any of the labels, and no node may simply ask whether the answerer believes in rebirth, accepts a traditional metaphysics, or counts themselves as belonging to some school; rebirth may appear, but only as something that follows from other commitments or as a question about what sort of gap in continuity a view can tolerate. Build the tree from the logical space first and only then place the positions in it. The opening split should be about what the karmic link is even supposed to be when a bad outcome is karmically downstream of a bad act: that the agent merits the outcome, that the outcome does something good such as teaching or deterring, or that the outcome is merely the upshot of a lawful pattern and calling it karma is a way of explaining it. Below that, I want the tree to be forced to take a stand on at least these cases: an agent who suffers the stipulated fruit of a wrong they cannot remember committing; a fruit that lands on someone other than the agent, such as their child or their community; a third party who takes on or discharges the debt, as in a transfer-of-merit arrangement; a wrongdoer who has genuinely reformed before the fruit lands; a fruit that lands on someone who cannot possibly learn from it, being an infant, severely demented, or already dead; a fruit that nobody ever recognizes as karmic, including the agent; an agent who has already paid in remorse and then also suffers the external misfortune, raising whether the view has a no-double-payment constraint; whether an unexpiated debt grows with delay or stays fixed, and whether it can ever expire; a harm produced jointly by two agents, which forces a view of how the debt is apportioned; a person born into terrible conditions, where I want the tree to separate the claims that this is deserved, that it is merely explained, and that the complaint of injustice survives the explanation; whether the ledger runs in both directions or desert attaches only to burdens and not to rewards; and what has to be true of the later stage for the fruit to count as theirs, whether memory, psychological continuity, bodily continuity, mere membership in the same karmic causal chain, or nothing at all because the account is not individual but belongs to a lineage or a community or a fixed cosmic quantity of suffering that gets redistributed. If two of my cases turn out to do the same work on your tree, say so and collapse them rather than inventing a distinction.

For each leaf give a label, one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and one sentence on what would count as evidence against it; where a leaf corresponds to a historical view, state the view's content in your own words and add the tradition in parentheses only as a pointer, letting the branch questions do all the real work. The leaves must include at minimum a retributive desert reading, an educative or consequentialist reading, an impersonal lawlike moral-causation reading with no judge anywhere in the story, a reading on which the merit is stored and then administered, roughly along the lines of the Jain subtle-matter account or the Mimamsa-style unseen-potency account, a reading on which karma is an explanation or theodicy rather than a desert claim, and some non-individual reading or other. Then give me three to five answer-combinations that the tree allows but that nobody occupies, each with a one-line diagnosis of whether the combination is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate instead of pretending a question distinguishes them. Finally, add a five-minute version: the three questions that do the most sorting work, in the order you would ask them, for when we do not have a whole session.
