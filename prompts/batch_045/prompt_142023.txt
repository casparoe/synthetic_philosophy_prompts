I'm a senior at a public high school, and I edit the small philosophy-club zine we put out at the end of each term. The theme of the next issue is things we treat as if they had minds, and I'd like to close it with a poem rather than an editorial. I'd be grateful for your help with it.

The piece should be a Shakespearean sonnet — three quatrains and a closing couplet, fourteen lines, loose iambic pentameter, rhymed but not rigidly — about a single object. My candidate is the algorithm that decides what plays next on a phone's video feed, but if you think another object carries the idea better, use that one and tell me why in the JSON below.

The structure should carry the philosophy. Quatrain one takes the physical stance toward the object, quatrain two the design stance, quatrain three the intentional stance. The volta should land at the opening of the third quatrain: the moment the poem gives up on voltages and on circuitry or code and begins addressing the system as something that has beliefs and aims and wants the reader to keep watching.

The couplet is the load-bearing part, and I want it to do two things. First, it should land the point about real patterns: the pattern the intentional stance finds is not a fairy tale imposed on the physics, because it buys real predictive compression that the lower stances do not in practice deliver — and yet buying that does not entitle anyone to say the wanting is in there the way wanting is in the reader. I'd like both halves of that to be present in the two lines, with the tension between them left standing rather than tidied up. Second, and this is my own addition rather than anything I've read, the couplet should gesture at a fourth stance, which in my private notes I've been calling the training stance: you predict the system neither from its physics, nor from its design, nor by handing it beliefs and desires, but from the fact that it was selected and tuned against the responses of creatures like you — so that its behavior is interpretable by you because it was shaped to be, and you are part of what it was shaped on. If that can be got into two lines without wrecking the poem, put it there; if it genuinely cannot, leave it out and say so in the JSON.

Accuracy matters to me more than polish. Someone who has actually read Dennett on the three stances and on real patterns should find the poem right rather than merely decorated: the physical stance should not be the caricature that everything is just atoms; the design stance should not require anyone to have read the wiring diagram or the source; the intentional stance should read as a predictive strategy rather than as a metaphysical claim about what the system really is. And someone who has never heard of any of this should still just read a decent poem about a phone that seems to know them. Please write, and reply, in American English.

After the poem, please give me one JSON object and no prose outside it — anything you want to tell me goes in a field. Use exactly these fields:

- "volta": an object with "line_number", "line" (the line itself, copied from your poem), and "what_turns" (one sentence).
- "couplet": an object with "lines", "prose_restatement" (two sentences at most, giving the real-patterns claim as the couplet actually makes it), "is_the_pattern_real" (yes, no, or yes-with-qualification, with the qualification stated), "tension_resolved" (yes or no), and "how_the_tension_is_left_open" (one sentence).
- "fourth_stance": an object with "included" (yes or no), "lines" (which lines carry it, or "omitted"), "definition" (one sentence, the training stance as the poem uses it), "differs_from_design_stance" (one sentence), "differs_from_intentional_stance" (one sentence), and "case_where_it_wins" (one sentence: a concrete case where it predicts better than both other stances, or "none offered").
