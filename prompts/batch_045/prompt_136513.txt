My dad had a stroke last spring, and one of the people at the meditation group I started going to after it said — very kindly — that this is just how accounts get settled, and that nobody is being blamed. I nearly walked out. Then a couple of months later I found out that the guy I went into business with had been siphoning our accounts for two years and is now doing extremely well, and I noticed I was spending a stupid amount of time hoping the universe would get round to him. So I've been going back to the group and reading around, and I dont actually know what I think.

A guy there who's finishing a PhD in philosophy wrote this on the whiteboard one night. It's my reconstruction, so I might of made it worse. He says "karma" has to be a theory of desert or it doesn't say anything interesting:

(1) Karma-as-desert says: a person's suffering is in order — is what they're owed — when, and because, it's the fitting return for wrongs they've done. There's a quantity owed, and the system gets it right.

(2) Desert needs control. Nobody deserves a burden for something that wasn't up to them.

(3) What's up to you in the moment you act is fixed by what you want and believe right then — and you don't choose, right then, what you want and believe.

(4) So nothing is ever up to you at the moment of acting. Anything that's up to you has to have been up to you earlier, the way a debt you chose to take on is still yours when the bill comes. The disposition behind the act has to trace back to a choice that was genuinely yours.

(5) Karma says the same thing and just runs the tracing back past death: the dispositions you're born with are the residue of choices in earlier lives, and those were shaped by earlier ones still. It has to, since otherwise your whole opening hand is luck. (This is also where it gets strange for me: I dont remember any of those lives, so whatever I'm paying for is a debt I can't read.)

(6) So either the tracing stops somewhere or it doesn't. If it stops, there's a first choice made out of dispositions nobody chose and nobody can trace to anything — so by (2) that choice wasn't up to the agent, and nothing downstream of it can inherit desert from it. You can't get desert out of a source that had none.

(7) If it doesn't stop, then for every act the dispositions behind it are explained by an earlier act, and no act anywhere in the series traces back to anything the agent ultimately chose. By (2) again: nothing is up to anyone, and nothing is ever deserved.

(8) So nobody has ever deserved anything and no suffering has ever been owed. And notice (6) and (7) never used rebirth — run it on one ordinary life and the dispositions I act on came from genes, upbringing, and what happened to me. Ordinary blame goes too. Which means my dad didn't earn his stroke, which is the part I want, but the guy who took our money doesn't deserve to lose anything either, which is the part I don't want to give up.

He's completely happy at (8). He says we should drop desert and keep quarantine, deterrence and repair under other names, and that the published replies to this style of argument are all bad. I'm not happy there and I cant see where to get off.

I tried one way out myself: maybe karma isn't a payment at all, it's just that the dispositions you grow bear the fruits they bear, the way bad posture wrecks your back — no debt, no quantity, nobody owed anything. Someone at the group quoted a sutta at me about a lump of salt dropped in a cup of water versus in the Ganges, where the same sort of deed barely registers for one person and ruins another, and I think that's meant to be on my side but I don't see why. That version gets my dad off the hook, but it also means the theft doesn't deserve anything, it just has consequences, and I'm not sure anyone at the group actually means that.

So: which step of his argument do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost me? If there's more than one place to get off, tell me what each one buys and what it takes away. Lead with your answer in the first sentence, then give me the reasoning — no preamble, and don't restate the question back at me.
