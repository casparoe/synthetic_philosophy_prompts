hey — I need something for my writers' union branch newsletter, and I want it to be funny in a way that actually lands an argument rather than just grumbling about chatbots. I'm up to my neck in the current round of negociations (the publishers want a clause saying a "first draft" can be machine-generated as long as a named human "authors" the revision) and everyone on my side keeps reaching for the same line: that work is what gives a life its shape — the hours, the collegues, the sense of being needed, having something to be bad at on a Monday morning — and that automation therefore steals meaning, not just wages. I half believe that. I also think it's a trap, and I'd rather write the trap than argue about it.

So: a straight-faced op-ed, 700–850 words, in the voice of a union member — me, roughly — proposing a Dignity Preservation Scheme for the next collective agreement. I want it worked out in deadpan bureaucratic earnest:

- Companion posts: for every job a publisher automates, it funds a post of identical hours, grade and pay whose output is contractually required never to be used by anyone. Call it the sterile-output clause. The pitch is that if what work gives you is structure, a place to be at nine in the morning, people to complain with, and the feeling that someone is waiting on you, then none of that depends on the output — so guaranteeing that the output is worthless is the whole point. It proves you're kept on for yourself and not merely as a means. I want that last bit played completely straight, and I want it to sound reasonable.
- A provenance floor on the creative side: writers on machine-assisted contracts still have to log a fixed number of unmediated hours, but nothing specifies what those hours have to produce. I keep imagining a collegue spending his unmediated hours writing longhand letters he never sends and billing them as substrate maintenance.
- Something for the monitoring problem: the old autopilot finding from the eighties, that the more you automate, the worse the remaining human gets at the one bit she's still there for. I want the scheme's answer to be a fake version of the skill — manual proficiency hours, in which the copy-editor still marks up a proof that is then shredded — and I want the narrator to notice, exactly once, that this is the same thing as the companion posts, and then carry on.
- An invented number or two, followed through: a ratio of companion posts to automated ones, a fine schedule, the audit paperwork. Don't pad, but let the arithmetic be part of the joke.

Rules for the voice: he never says "and this is obviously absurd," and he never breaks into "what we really need is shorter hours and a decent settlement" — if he did, the piece would just be an op-ed someone could agree with. He believes the dignity line all the way down and is genuinely annoyed that the other side keeps offering him leisure instead of work. The recoil should be the reader's job. And I'd like the final paragraph to be indistinguishable from sincere: him saying, in effect, that the scheme is already half in place, that he's spent eight of the last twelve months writing human-origin affidavits for prose he didn't write, that it's the most valued he's felt since 2019 — and let the reader work out whether he's noticed.

Also: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. Continuous prose, the way you'd write an email, since it's going straight into a layout template that eats formatting anyway.
