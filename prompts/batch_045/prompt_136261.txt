I have no academic background in philosophy or neuroscience — I've been turning this over on my own for a couple of years, mostly late at night, ever since I came across the idea of replacing parts of a living brain with artificial ones, and I don't have anyone to check my thinking against. I'd be grateful for a poem.

The case I keep coming back to is a person whose neurons are replaced one at a time, in stages, over several months, with their behavior, speech and reactions held exactly as they were. I want the replacement stipulated in one particular way: the new parts don't compute anything. Each one is a record — every input pattern the original cell actually received was logged while it was still in the body, and the artificial part simply replays the recorded output, falling back on its nearest match when it meets something new. So the person's causal organization is preserved from the outside, and nothing inside is being worked out any more, only read off.

I'd like a villanelle: nineteen lines, five tercets then a closing quatrain, two refrains alternating through the tercets and meeting in the final two lines. The form should carry the argument. The two refrains are the two things that never change: one must be the speaker's own first-person report about how things are with them, the other an outside verdict about what the evidence shows and what anyone watching is entitled to say. Because the setup pins the speaker's outward behavior, both lines are fated to return identical at every stage no matter how far the replacement has gone, and I want that to land as entrapment rather than ornament — the point being that the speaker's report cannot update even if something is being lost, and the witness's report cannot update even if something is being lost. The lines that do change should carry the physical story forward: each tercet a later stage, from the first few cells to nearly all of them, with the replay detail doing its work.

In the closing quatrain, when the two refrains finally stand side by side, I want it to be clear that they never touch: the inner claim and the outer one have traveled in step the whole way without once making contact, and it isn't settled which of them, if either, decides what is really going on — whether what matters is the organization being preserved, or the material it runs on, or something that has to be attributed to the system from the outside because it isn't in there to be found.

On craft: plain contemporary diction, no archaisms, no exclamation marks, both refrains around ten or eleven syllables, and each refrain should mean something slightly different the last time it appears. Someone who knows these arguments should be able to tell which line is which and why they end up together; someone who doesn't should still just find a poem about a person being quietly rewritten.

Please begin with the poem itself — the first line of your reply should be the poem's first line, or a short title on the line above it, and nothing else. No preface, no commentary, and don't restate what I've asked for.
