I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm finishing a feature for a Sunday magazine on accountability when the causal chain runs through machines and institutions — a hospital system whose triage model quietly deprioritised patients from one postcode, a chatbot that talked a grieving teenager out of calling a helpline, a fleet operator whose cars shared a policy update nobody at the firm actually authored. Every individual in each story says some version of I was following the dashboard. For the online edition I want to run a which-theory-of-moral-responsibility-are-you-actually-committed-to interactive, and I need you to build it. No pollster question (do you believe in free will), no abstract agree-disagree statements, and definitly no question that asks readers to pick a label — nobody should be asked whether they are a compatibilist, or whether people are ever really blameworthy in the abstract. Every item should be a concrete case with two or three options a reader can answer without having heard of any -ism, and the scoring key should map answer patterns onto leaves.

Nine or ten questions, and I want the instrument built from the logical space rather than from a list of names: first the dimensions that genuinely separate views (whether the practice of blame has to be justified by desert or only by what it does downstream, whether an agent's history can defeat responsibility even when present responsiveness to reasons is intact, whether indeterminism in the right place would help or hurt, whether the reactive attitudes are what constitute responsibility or merely evidence of it, whether the practices themselves can be educated and revised rather than merely described, whether anything that isn't an individual human can be a target of them at all), then the combinations those answers allow, then the actual positions located in that space, with unoccupied combinations named and diagnosed — is nobody there because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unwritten? — and with any pair of leaves your tree can't really seperate flagged as such rather than split by a trick question. Leaves I want reachable: the Strawsonian social-practice view built on resentment and gratitude (and I want it distinguished from the Wallacian version that ties blame to the holding-responsible practice's demands), reasons-responsive semicompatibilism, the quality-of-will or answerability view, the free-will skeptic who keeps a trimmed-down forward-looking practice, the abolitionist who thinks the whole practice trades on an illusion worth unmasking, the revisionist who thinks the attitudes are educable and should be shaped deliberately, libertarianism, and a leaf for the person who has no idea and goes on blaming anyway. Four cases are non-negotiable because they came out of my reporting: the hospital with the triage model, the chatbot, the Designer's Daughter (a girl raised by a team of behavioural engineers who selected her environments, peers and feedback schedules so she would grow up to weigh moral reasons reliably and to care about doing so — she now does, apparently freely, and takes pride in it, and then does something cruel and self-serving), and the Swap (two agents with identical observable behaviour and identical histories up to the moment of choice, one deterministic, one with an indeterministic weighting step in deliberation, same act). I also want one second-personal-versus-third-personal pair: the same wrongful act judged by the victim, by a stranger who reads about it, and by a sentencing algorithm — if readers answer differently depending on who's doing the blaming, I want that to count in the scoring rather than get averaged out.

And please commit rather than survey: answer all ten questions as you go, tell me which leaf you land in and which leaf you'd bet my median reader lands in, commit to the single question that does most of the discriminating work and defend it against the obvious rival, and where you're relying on a contested reading — the optimistic versus skeptical reading of Strawson, say — flag it in one line and then take a side anyway. Don't send me a reading list or citations; I can read the encyclopaedia myself, what I need is someone to make the editorial choices.
