Here's the thing I've been chewing on, and I want the question built around it. It seems to me there are two different things that get lumped together under incommensurability. One is semantic: the vocabulary of the old theory can't be translated into the new one without residue, so you can't even state what the old theory claimed. The other is evaluative: even if translation went perfectly and you could restate every old claim in the new terms, the two theories might still be incomparable, because the standards you'd use to rank them — the usual theory-choice virtues like accuracy, consistency, scope, simplicity, fruitfulness — aren't neutral arbiters; their interpretation and their relative weighting were themselves part of what got overturned. My seminar leader said something vaguely like this in passing and then moved on. Call the second one pure evaluative incommensurability, the kind you can have with no semantic incommensurability at all.
