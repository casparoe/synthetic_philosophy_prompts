A friend and I spent most of last evening arguing about how much of a safety case should be allowed to rest on the assumption that a frontier system is on our side, and I left unsure whether the position I was defending actually hangs together. Roughly, I hold the following:

(1) A safety case should never depend on claims about the system's motives. Assume worst-case misalignment, including a system that is strategically choosing the moment to defect, and ask only whether the control measures still hold under that assumption.

(2) The untrusted model should do the actual work, and oversight should come from weaker trusted models plus non-ML scaffolding rather than from a copy of the untrusted model, since two copies share the same blind spots and could collude.

(3) We should apply no optimization pressure to visible reasoning or surface behavior: no training away frightening-looking chains of thought, no unlearning of dangerous capabilities, no rewarding of cooperative-sounding introspection, because each of these destroys the evidential value of what we later measure and gives the system a reason to hide what it is doing.

(4) The protocol itself should be public: red teams get full knowledge of how we monitor and get to optimize against it, and nothing in the safety case may rest on the system being ignorant of the measures.

(5) The system must nonetheless not be able to tell whether a given episode is an evaluation or a real deployment, since otherwise our measured rates of misbehavior tell us nothing.

(6) Monitoring should work partly through deterrence: the system should know it is watched and should have good reason to believe the watchers are competent.

Is that a consistent package? Go through it and tell me which commitments genuinely pull against each other and which only look as though they do, ordering what you find by severity; for each real tension, say what would have to give and what that costs, and for each apparent one, say why it dissolves. I would much rather be told which of (1) through (6) has to go than be given a tour of the control literature.
