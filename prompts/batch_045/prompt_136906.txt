I've been going round on this by myself for a couple of years now — no philosophy background, just reading stuff online and arguing with my brother — and I've ended up with a set of things I believe about believing that I'd like someone to check for whether they actually hang together. Here's the list:

1. I think Clifford is basically right as a default. His rule that it's always wrong, everywhere, for anyone to believe on insufficient evidence — I think that's close to right, and more importantly I think it's fine to *criticise* people for it. The shipowner who talked himself into believing his ship was seaworthy is doing something blameworthy, not just making a private mistake.

2. But I also think James is right that there's an exception. When the question is forced (sitting it out isn't really available), momentous, and the evidence genuinely can't settle it in time, I think you're allowed to let what you hope is true tip you over.

3. I've got my own restriction on that, which I've never seen written down anywhere and which I'm least sure about. Call it the bootstrap rule: the James permission only applies when your believing could help *make the belief come true*. The climber who believes he can make the jump, the person who decides to trust a friend before she's earned it, someone starting a business — fine. Believing that the universe has a plan, or that someone who died is still watching over you — not covered, because nothing about my believing makes that so. This is the bit that lets me hold 1 and 2 at the same time, I think.

4. I dont think belief is something you can just decide to have. You can't flip it on. (Which makes 2 weird, I know, but it also makes 1 weird, so maybe its a wash.)

5. In my own life: I don't believe the religious stuff I was raised with, but I go to church with my family, I let it shape how I act, and I don't feel like a fraud about it. I call it going along — acting as if it's true without believing it is. I'd rather do that than either fake a belief or walk away from people I love.

6. I also think each person is mostly answerable to their own evidence. When someone has lived a completely different life and seen different things, I'm wary of outsiders — me included — telling them their belief is unjustified. I hold this even though I also think it's obviously fine to tell my brother-in-law that his health-conspiracy stuff is unjustified and that he shouldn't be acting on it with his kids.

7. And a case: a woman in our town whose son has been missing for two years is convinced he's still alive. The police have more or less said he isn't. I think she's fine. I think it would be monstrous to tell her to line her belief up with her evidence, and I'd encourage her if she asked me.

So: which of these actually fight with each other, and which only look like they do? I'd like them ranked by how bad the clash is, and for each real one, tell me which commitment would have to go and what giving it up would cost me — I genuinely can't tell which of these I'd part with. Also: is the bootstrap rule doing any real work, or is it just a way of smuggling in conclusions I'd already reached?
