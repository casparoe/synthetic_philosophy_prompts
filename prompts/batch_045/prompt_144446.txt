Write an essay on whether the fact that some outcome is "locked in" actually tells us anything interesting about whether it was ever avoidable. Please use these two distinctions, since theyre the part I care about (though argue against them if you think they're the wrong ones): call an outcome hard-locked if, from where we are now, there is no sequence of steps that gets us to the competing outcome where each individual step leaves everyone involved better off at the time; call it soft-locked if the alternative is still reachable, but only by a detour that makes things worse for a while before they get better. Separately, in a "replay" test where we restart history from a slightly earlier starting point, distinguish landing on the very same outcome from landing on a different outcome that ends up doing roughly the same job.

Now take a position. If most real-world lock-in turns out to be soft, and if replays mostly land on a functional equivalent rather than the same thing again, is path dependance a deep claim about how history works, or just a dressed-up way of saying that changing course is expensve?
