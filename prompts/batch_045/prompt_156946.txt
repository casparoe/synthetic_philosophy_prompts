I'm an infrastructure engineer with no formal philosophy training, and over the past year of reading Rorty on my own I've drifted into something close to neopragmatism. I'm writing my position up for a small reading group of other engineers and would like an audit first: I can't tell whether I've assembled a coherent package or a set of commitments that quietly eat each other. Here is the list.

1. No vocabulary, including our best physical one, depicts the world or carves it at the joints; the language–world relation is causal, not semantic. The analogy I lean on is that there is no machine code underneath our abstractions — no privileged lowest level — and the hardware only ever raises interrupts. It never issues instructions.

2. Words like *true* and *real* are not the names of relations to reality; they are devices of endorsement that function inside an adopted vocabulary. I don't think this obliges me to reform ordinary or engineering talk, and I don't want to.

3. There is no standpoint outside all vocabularies from which to rank them — no language of nature, no a priori tribunal. Justification is conversational the whole way out. But I also hold that logical and probabilistic norms are not optional in the way a vocabulary is: you cannot redescribe your way out of modus ponens or out of probabilistic coherence. I'm genuinely unsure whether asserting that is compatible with the first half of this item.

4. Some vocabularies are nonetheless genuinely better than others — germ theory beats miasma theory, and not merely in the sense that my milieu happens to prefer it. I've been trying to underwrite this with a notion I invented and call *grip*: the way recalcitrant experience forces revisions on us, as when a load test fails or a bridge comes down. I want grip, and not only the approval of my interlocutors, to be what corrects me.

5. I'm an ironist about my own final vocabulary, including my liberal-democratic and human-rights commitments: they are contingent, and I don't think they track anything.

6. I hold that cruelty and humiliation are the worst things we do to each other, and I want that to be more than a report about my own corner of the world — a society that stopped minding humiliation has gone wrong, not merely become different.

7. I want to keep a real distinction between convincing someone by argument and steering them by framing, rhetoric, or propaganda, and I resist the thought that redescribing is all anyone ever does.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, ranked by severity rather than listed as equally bad. For each tension, say precisely what I would have to give up and what that would cost me. Also tell me which apparent conflicts dissolve on closer inspection: I suspect at least one does, and I'd rather not amputate a commitment I'm entitled to keep.
