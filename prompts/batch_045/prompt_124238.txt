My mate's been helping judge a type-design thing — people submit one glyph at a time for the 'a' slot of a grid they're all building together, and she keeps a running verdict on each: is it an 'a' or isn't it. I've been trying to work out what she's actually doing, because her yeses and nos don't line up with any list of features I can come up with, and I want to know what she'll say about the one that landed this morning.

Her verdicts so far:

- plain double-storey a, bowl plus stem, more or less Helvetica: yes
- single-storey one, circle with a stem on the right, Futura-ish: yes
- a bare circle with no stem at all: no — she said that one "is just the o"
- double-storey a with the bowl left open where it meets the stem, stencil style: yes
- an angular blackletter-ish thing built out of two straight strokes and a wedge: yes, though she reckoned nobody would call it an 'a' shown on its own. That's the verdict I can't parse.
- a Greek alpha dropped in among ordinary latin letters: no
- a small-cap A scaled down to x-height: no — "that's a capital in a small coat"
- a black weight where the counter has closed down to a slit: yes
- a serifed, bracketed a submitted to an otherwise monolinear geometric grid: yes to it being an 'a', no to it belonging to that grid. She does keep those two questions apart.

The new one is for a heavy display face where every bowl is a solid triangle — no counters anywhere, the 'o' is a solid triangle too, and nothing in the grid is drawn as a stroke, it's all filled shapes. The submission is that triangle, identical to the grid's 'o', with no vertical stem anywhere; instead the bottom-right corner juts out past the baseline into a short horizontal spur, so the silhouette is a bit like a little flag. It's distinct from every other glyph in the grid and it's stylistically consistent with the rest, and when we set words in it and showed them to people they read it as an 'a' about four times in five, but shown on its own almost nobody guesses it.

Do you think she passes it? I care much less about the yes or no than about the rest: what rule is she running, which other rules fit her existing verdicts just as well, what each of them predicts here, which one you'd bet on and why, and what single further submission would best split the ones that agree on this case.
