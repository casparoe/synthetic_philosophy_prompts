I'm taking a perception seminar this term and I keep getting stuck on whether cognition can penetrate perception. Every time I think I have a rule, I find a case that breaks it, and the reading isn't helping: half of it says basically every apparent top-down effect is really a change in judgment or attention or response bias rather than in how things look, and the other half says that distinction was never operationalizable anyway. So I want to work from my own verdicts instead. Here they are, gut-level, no theory attached.

Müller-Lyer: I've known for years that the lines are equal, measured them myself, and the one with the outgoing fins still looks longer. Not penetrated. My friend who reads mammograms for a living says that after residency the films literally look different to her now, that a particular kind of shadow used to be invisible and now pops out at her before she's thought anything at all. I want to say that one is penetrated, or at least that something genuinely perceptual changed. A banana under blue light still looks yellow: I call that constancy doing its job, not penetration by my belief about the lighting. Someone tells me the guy in the photo is furious and suddenly his neutral face looks angry to me: I want to deny penetration there and say I'm just placing him higher on a scale I was always going to place him on. But then my TA described a display he called the Marchetti dots — I'm probably mangling the name and I couldn't find anything about it online, so treat it as a made-up case if that's easier: dots drifting downward across a screen, and if you're told beforehand it's rain people report seeing them fall, while if you're told it's sparks from a fire a lot of people report seeing them rise, upward motion, not just a different story about the same motion. My verdict: that's penetration, and the usual move of saying the stimulus was ambiguous and cognition just picked among the percepts it already allowed doesn't cover it, because here the motion direction isn't ambiguous — the label seems to be overriding something determinate. One more: phonemic restoration, where a cough covers a phoneme and I really do hear the missing sound, not infer it. Penetrated, I feel strongly. And a case I'm oddly resistant to: a week of inverting goggles and the world reportedly comes to look upright again. I don't want to call that penetration, since no belief or desire is doing any work, only recalibration — but it obviously changes how things look, which sits badly with any principle that requires a cognitive state as the cause. Similarly, after training to discriminate two extremely close greens, they eventually look obviously different to me; changed experience, but I'm not sure I want to call it penetration.

So: is there a principle that gets these, and what exactly is it saying the difference is between the radiologist and the Müller-Lyer, given that both involve knowledge and only one changes the look? Note that the Marchetti dots and the Müller-Lyer look like the same shape of case — a proposition you're told, a look that either shifts or doesn't — and I've given opposite verdicts, so something has to go, and I'd rather you told me which verdict to drop than paper over it. Same for restoration versus the angry face: if I let a label put a phoneme into my experience, why not let it put anger into a face? The case I keep flip-flopping on is hearing my own name across a noisy party — it seems to just leap out at me, fully formed, out of a wall of sound I can't otherwise parse, and I can't decide whether that's the best case I have or a terrible one. What I'd like: one candidate principle that saves all my verdicts even if it's ugly and has four clauses, and one simpler principle that saves most of them and overturns one or two, with an honest statement of which verdicts each can't accommodate and why the simpler one is (or isn't) worth it. Also tell me what each predicts for a case I haven't listed — say, someone who learns a brand-new color category, or a synesthete — and if you think the whole looks-different-versus-judged-different thing can't be cashed out in a way that would settle any of this, say so and tell me what I should have been tracking instead.
