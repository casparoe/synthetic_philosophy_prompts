I've drifted into accepting the self-indication assumption, i.e. the thing that says your own existence is evidence for hypotheses on which there are lots of observers, so a big-universe cosmology gets a prior boost roughly proportional to its observer count. That's what kills the doomsday argument for me and why I end up thirding in Sleeping Beauty. I know the presumptuous philosopher is supposed to be the reductio of all this and I've been telling myself it's a bullet worth biting. I want to stress-test that properly instead of just repeating it to myself.

So: what would count against SIA? Rank it for me from fatal down to merely awkward, and — the part I care most about — be clear about which of these a card-carrying SIA defender would themselves concede count against the view, as opposed to what the SSA crowd says counts against it. I'm especially after anything I can't wave off as a reference-class complaint, since the reference class problem is the main reason I dumped SSA in the first place, and I have a suspicion it just migrates: SIA still has to say what counts as a possible observer and how those observers get counted.

Here's the case I keep turning over and genuinely can't tell is a defeater or a confusion. Call it the Faint Ones. Hypothesis A has 10 observers with rich, unified, long streams of experience. Hypothesis B has 10,000 observers, each of whom exists for a fraction of a second, or is a near-zombie with the barest glimmer of experience, or is one of 10,000 numerically distinct but perfectly correlated copies of the same single stream. Does B get the full thousand-fold boost anyway? If SIA counts observers, yes. But if the boost was ever supposed to be about there being more chances for you to turn up, it feels like marginal or duplicate observers shouldn't buy full price — and the moment you start discounting them you're choosing a measure, which looks about as mushy as SSA's reference class. Is that a real problem or am i smuggling in a substantive assumption about what the boost is for?

Also: how insulated is this thing? If cosmology tomorrow delivered really strong evidence for a small, nearly empty universe, would that count against the assumption or only against my physics, and if it's always only the latter, is that unfalsifiability bad for a principle of this kind? And does the SIA/SSA disagreement stay substantive once both sides get to pick a reference class and a counting measure, or does it quietly collapse into a verbal one?

Two last preferences: I already know the Boltzmann brain embarrassment, so only dwell on it if you think it generalizes into something worse; and I trust case intuitions pretty little, so flag which of your defeaters are structural rather than intuitive.
