So I somehow ended up on my town's Heritage Commission — five of us, appointed by the selectmen, we meet the second Tuesday — and we've got a mess on our hands that I'm pretty sure is a philosophy problem wearing a hi-vis vest. For ninety-odd years Northchurch has held a dawn observance on the Common on the first Sunday of October, in front of a granite marker put up in 1932 whose raised letters read IN THE HUNGRY WINTER OF 1788 NO ONE IN NORTHCHURCH WENT HUNGRY. Last spring a grad student working in the state archives turned up the selectmen's accounts for that winter: grain was distributed, generously by the standards of the day, but only to ratepayers in good standing and to families in full communion, and there are three burials of transients that winter plus warning-out orders for two households whose names are still in the town. So the relief was real — neighbouring towns wrote to ask how we managed it — but the sentence on the stone isn't. What makes it worse is that the sentence is load-bearing right now: the Hungry Winter Fund, which pays heating bills for about forty households, was founded in 1933 with that line as its origin story; the county is asking us to host a share of a regional shelter and the vote is in November, with people on both sides quoting the stone at each other; a descendant of the 1788 selectman sits on our commission and hears the correction as an accusation against his family; and a woman descended from one of the warned-out households has formally applied to install a second marker, which she'd like unveiled at the same October observance. The state marker program will pay for a replacement only if every claim on it is documented by primary sources, and otherwise will pay for an addendum panel — either way the application is due in April. Our own 1974 bylaw says town markers "shall be of documented historical accuracy," which somebody is going to read at me.

Four things are on the table and I can't get them to sort themselves: (a) recut or replace the stone with a defensible text; (b) leave the stone alone and bolt on an addendum panel telling the fuller story; (c) Tom Reilly's idea, which half of us think is a cop-out and half think is the only honest move — leave the stone exactly as it is and reclassify it, so the plaque beside it says this is a monument to 1932 and to what Northchurch believed about itself in 1932, not a record of 1788; (d) Ellie Vance's idea, which I keep turning over: keep the sentence and treat it as a promise instead of a description, i.e. we don't correct it, we make it true going forward by endowing the Fund properly and dropping the residency conditions, so that from here on no one in this town goes hungry. Add to that the timing problem — the observance is in six weeks, the shelter vote is seven weeks after that, and whichever way we go people will say we timed it to influence the vote.

What I need is the two-page written opinion I'll circulate before the meeting a week from Tuesday. It should recommend one concrete course of action with actual dates between now and next October, not a menu. It should also give the strongest version of the argument on the side I should probably reject — for me that's the one that says the dawn observance isn't a bundle of assertions at all but something the town does, a practice of remembering, so that "accuracy" is the wrong norm to bring to it and recutting the stone would be a kind of vandalism against a thing that isn't claiming to be true. It should tell me how to handle the November vote without either laundering a policy decision through a plaque or pretending the two have nothing to do with each other. And it should say what we owe each of the two descendant families, who want opposite things and both of whom will be in the room. Please don't just split the difference or tell me to do all four — I need a bottom line, and I need to be able to read it out loud to people who are angry. End with a final line beginning "Recommendation:" that gives the bottom line in six words or fewer.
