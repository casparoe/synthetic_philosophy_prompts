I'm a backend engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I've never read any of the primary Stoic texts — only what two colleagues who are into the modern Stoicism revival have told me. Please translate their position into the vocabulary my team actually works in, as faithfully as you can.

The vocabulary: we split the world into state our service owns — its own memory, the invariants it can enforce locally, what it decides to do next — and state it merely reads, requests, or is affected by: whether a downstream service accepts our write, its latency, what another team does with the events we emit, our on-call reputation. For the second kind we have retry budgets, idempotency, timeouts, best-effort delivery, blast radius, graceful degradation, and a standing joke that you own the request, not the response. Nothing in any of this counts as valuable except relative to an objective function somebody wrote down.

The position to translate: some things are up to us — our judgments, our assent to impressions, what we pursue — and some are not: body, property, reputation, outcomes. Tranquility consists in confining concern and desire to the first class, and, when acting on the second, acting with a reservation to the effect that the outcome may not come off. One colleague insists the revival version really divides things three ways, since most outcomes are partly up to us and partly not.

Give me the translation and then a residue report: what carries across cleanly, what has to be bent to fit, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. I care most about two points. First, does the step from "this is not up to me" to "this is therefore neither good nor bad for me" survive the translation, or is it a claim about value dressed as a claim about control — and is my vocabulary's lack of any value notion a blind spot, or a sign that the step was never warranted? Second, can "pursue the outcome wholeheartedly while remaining reserved about whether it happens" be said in my terms at all, given that either a metric drives my retries or it doesn't? Close by saying which of the two-way and three-way versions translates better, and, for each piece of residue, whether it indicts my framework or the Stoic claim.
