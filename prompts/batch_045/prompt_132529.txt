Please act as a professional philosopher of mind with a solid grip on metaphysics and epistemology, and take your time: I'd rather you brainstormed a few candidate carvings before settling than gave me the first label that comes to mind.

I'm a couple of years into a PhD on consciousness and I keep noticing something I don't have a word for. Take someone who holds that experiences are perfectly real but do no causal work at all. Ask them to describe a headache for a paper: they write "dull, throbbing, behind the left eye". A week later they read it back and correct it — no, it was stabbing, not throbbing, they got it wrong. The correction looks like it's answerable to how the thing actually felt. But on their own view how it felt didn't do anything to the first description or to the correction; the physical story, plus both utterances, could be exactly the same with the feel swapped. And the trouble seems to scale with detail: "I'm in some state or other" is fine, "throbbing rather than stabbing" is in trouble, and I can't see a principled place to draw the line — which makes me think the fine-grainedness is where the real problem lives, not the bare existence claim. If consciousness is inert its hard to see what makes one of several detailed, revisable descriptions the right one.

Second version: the same person will insist, in a seminar, that a colleague who denies there is anything it is like is *wrong*. Same shape again — a claim about what things are like, whose correctness floats free of anything that could settle it.

I know there's a familiar worry in this neighbourhood about whether you could so much as know you're conscious if consciousness does nothing, with the zombie twins saying all the same things and meaning nothing by them. But that feels like it's about whether the judgement has any justification or knowledge status at all, whereas what I keep hitting is about which of several competing detailed attributions is correct, and about revisions over time. Is that the same problem in different clothes, a special case of it, or genuinely its own thing? Does it have a name of its own, and if not, can you coin one and define it properly? I don't want a bare label: I want to know what exactly this is, how it differs from the neighbouring worries (the standard self-undermining objection, Moore-style paradox, ordinary self-refutation, and the thing where reliability seems to depend on how finely you describe the belief-forming process — that last one feels close but not quite right), and where its boundary cases fall.
