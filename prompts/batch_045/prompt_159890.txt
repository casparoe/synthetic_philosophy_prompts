ok so my school totally redesigned the cafeteria over winter break and its obviously one of those nudge things — fruit moved to eye level, pizza behind a little wall, the trays got smaller, the "default" lunch you get if you don't say anything is now the salad one. My econ teacher lent me that Nudge book (Thaler and Sunstein, 2008) and I basically recognised every single trick in it, so now I want to write something making fun of it for the school literary magazine.

Can you write me a parody of one of those long cheerful emails our principal sends to parents — you know, "Dear Panther Families!" energy, lots of exclamation points, bullet points, a fake committee name — announcing the new Office of Student Choice Architecture and the new cafeteria design. Around 600-700 words, and end it with a short "Questions Families Have Asked" FAQ of 3 or 4 Q&As.

The joke has to come from actually getting the view right, not from inventing a dumb version of it. So: the email should keep insisting that every single change is choice-preserving and totally voluntary (the pizza is still there! you just have to walk around the smoothie station and ask), should keep saying it only steers means and not ends, and should justify itself by saying it's helping students do what they'd choose anyway if they were calm and rested and fully informed — that whole "it's for your own good as judged by you" move. Have someone on the committee describe students as biased and themselves as the experts who aren't. Maybe a line about how the committee also removed some "sludge" from somewhere, and one place where really obviously they added a lot of it. The FAQ should handle the manipulation worry by appealing to transparency — like, we told you about it right here in this email, so it's fine — and I want that to feel like a dodge without anyone saying the word "manipulation."

Here's the extra thing I want you to put in, since I need it to not be generic: the district has adopted a "Mirror Rule." Any nudge applied to students has to be applied at equal strength, in the same room at the same time, to the staff who designed it — so the committee members eat out of the same default tray, sit in the same rearranged queue, and have to sign an "Architecture Awareness & Opt Out Form" that is physically located behind the salad bar. Just follow that rule straight-faced to its grossest consequences; don't have the principal wink at it once. Also: near the end, some students rearranged the fruit bowls back, and the email treats that as vandalism, while treating the committee doing the exact same thing as design.

It should be funny to someone who actually knows the paternalism literature (my econ teacher will read it, and he'll wince if you get the voluntary/means-ends stuff wrong) but still readable as just an annoying school email to everyone else. Please don't make it sound like a Wikipedia summary, and definately don't have the principal break character and explain the philosophy.
