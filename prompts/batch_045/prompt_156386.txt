I am assembling a teaching unit on truthfulness for a first-year honors seminar that doubles as a cross-cultural communication workshop, and I need a classification instrument rather than a survey: a taxonomy of the positions on white lies, drawn as a tree in which every branch is labeled with the claim that separates it from its siblings. Please build it as follows.

Start from the logical space, not from a list of names. Lay out the choices a position has to make: whether the wrong-making feature must include asserting something you believe to be false, or only intentionally causing a false belief, or only causing one by means the hearer is entitled to trust; whether it matters if the addressee never believes you; whether silence, omission, and letting a known-false default stand belong in the same category as speaking falsely; whether the conventions of a speech community fix the content of what is said or only the inferences reasonably drawn from it; whether a ban on deception can be satisfied by literal truth plus a carefully chosen framing. Say which of these choices are genuinely independent and which collapse into one another, then place the actual positions in the space the independent choices open up.

The nodes must be concrete cases, phrased so that a student can answer them without already knowing which labeled view the answer leads to; no node may simply ask whether lying is always wrong. At minimum, use these as branch points, in whatever order your space demands: the phatic exchange in which someone replies "fine" to a routine enquiry when they are plainly not fine; the standing invitation that neither party expects to be accepted; the falsehood addressed to a hearer who discounts everything the speaker says; the strictly true answer to a question the speaker knows will be read as answering a different question; the inquirer who would use a true answer to harm a third party; and the case I care most about, the cross-regime mismatch in which the speaker is running one politeness convention and the hearer another, so that an utterance the speaker treats as conveying nothing false is taken literally and a false belief results without anyone intending deception. Treat the familiar contrast between Dutch-style directness and Japanese-style indirectness as two stylized regimes, and do not rest any branch on empirical claims about how actual national speech habits work.

The leaves must include a rigorist view on which no false assertion is ever permitted but withholding is; a view on which the wrong of lying is that it hijacks the hearer's deliberation and deliberate misleading is therefore equally wrong; a view on which misleading is not lying and carries a lesser or no duty; a view on which polite conventions redraw the content so that nothing false is asserted; a straightforwardly consequentialist view; a view on which even harmless deception erodes a shared stock of trust; a view on which the deeper vice is indifference to how things are rather than falsity itself; and a role- or relationship-based view on which truthfulness is a matter of fit to the relation rather than of propositional accuracy. Cap the tree at twelve leaves; if the space generates more, collapse siblings and say which distinction you dropped. Give each leaf one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

Two further things I need. First, for every leaf, state what it says about the cross-regime mismatch case, whether the blame there falls on the speaker, the hearer, both, or neither, and whether the leaf is forced to deny that such mismatch is really possible. Second, mark which leaves flip their verdict depending on whether the falsehood's benefit accrues to the hearer, to the speaker, or merely to the smoothness of the interaction, and which leaves are indifferent to that difference.

Close with two short diagnostic sections. List at least three answer-combinations your space permits but that nobody, as far as the tree can tell, actually occupies, and say for each whether it is unoccupied because it is incoherent, because it is merely unattractive, or because it is unexplored. Then flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, and either give the further question that would separate them or explain why no such question is available. Please write in prose and diagrams, with no bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
