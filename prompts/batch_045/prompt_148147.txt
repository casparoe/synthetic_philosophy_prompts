I do a movie-night thing with some people from the old rationalist scene — one film a month, and whoever picks it also has to bring the weird angle — and I volunteered for Minority Report, which I'm now regretting a bit.

My half-baked thesis is that the movie is a counterfactual-mugging / XOR-blackmail machine wearing a sci-fi costume. Setups as I remember them: in counterfactual mugging, Omega flips a coin, shows up in the tails world and asks you for $100, and in the heads world hands $10,000 to anyone whose decision procedure would have paid — so the only way to be the person who gets paid is to be the kind of agent who pays in the world where paying is pure loss. In XOR blackmail the letter gets sent iff exactly one of (you have the disease, you're the type who'd pay) holds, so receiving it is evidence about your own algorithm, and the winning policy is to be a non-payer even though at the moment of decision paying looks costless.

Here's the formalization I want to try on the film: the pre-vision of Anderton killing Crow is issued iff exactly one of these is true — (a) Anderton kills Crow in the world where he's seen the vision, (b) Anderton kills Crow in the world where he never sees it. On that reading the vision is the letter, and getting it is evidence about Anderton's own decision procedure, and the FDT-flavored move is to be the guy whose algorithm returns "don't shoot" in both branches, in which case the vision never gets issued in the first place.

What I want from you:

- Is that formalization even coherent, or is an XOR over two counterfactual behaviors of the same agent a thing that collapses, or stops really being an XOR? I may be stapling two problems together and calling it one.
- Does the film support it? The Crow scene is the test case: Anderton only goes to that hotel room because he saw the vision, he decides not to shoot, and Crow kills himself with Anderton's gun so the vision comes true anyway. Is the movie saying the vision was never tracking Anderton's decision procedure at all — that it was a setup that was going to resolve either way, which would make my reading empty — or is it saying something more interesting about how robust a prediction can be to your trying to avert it?
- The bit I'm most worried about: is a pre-vision even a predictor in the sense these problems need? A predictor of the kind FDT likes tracks what your algorithm outputs; a vision of one causal future that you can sometimes dodge (the whole minority-report business, Anderton getting the drop on his own future) is closer to ordinary causal information, where plain CDT handles it fine. Does the film keep those apart, or does it help itself to whichever one the scene needs? And if it mixes them, is that a flaw, or is that the point?
- The tension I can't resolve: the film's politics are clearly that punishing people for what they would have done is monstrous, but Omega's entire deal is rewarding people for what they would have done in a branch they're not in. Real tension, or does the disanalogy (a causally reachable murder vs. a counterfactual payout) kill it?
- Which scenes should I actually point people at during the discussion? I was going to use the Crow scene and the missing minority report for Anne Lively's murder, but tell me if there's a better pairing.

On calibration: I'd much rather have a skeptical read than a clever one, so please mark roughly how confident you are on each of those, and flag which parts are your own take rather than something a decision theorist would standardly grant. And if you think the whole framing is bunk — if, say, pre-vision just isn't a predictor in the relevant sense and the honest answer is that this film has nothing to do with any of it and I should pick a different lens — say that plainly instead of politely building me a reading. Also flag anywhere I've quietly assumed something about what the precogs can see that the film doesn't actually give me: I know it's slippery about whether that future is fixed, and I suspect my formalization needs it to be more fixed than the movie lets on.
