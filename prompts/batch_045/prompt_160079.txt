I write a column on science and ideas, and I keep getting dragged back to a fight in animal signalling that I'm no longer sure is a real fight. Here's the version I carry around. On one side: the handicap principle, Zahavi's idea that a signal is trustworthy because it costs more than a low-quality signaller can afford, so the differential cost is what buys the honesty. On the other side: the index or "revealing signal" story, where honesty comes more or less for free because the signal is physically tied to the quality it advertises — a frog's call can't help but broadcast something about body size, given how the apparatus that produces it works — so no cost differential is doing any explanatory work. And then there's the awkward case that neither side ever seems to fold into the argument: the very highest-quality individuals often signal the least and still get believed. Fifty years, the same three moves, nobody moved.

My working theory is that this isn't an empirical disagreement at all. I think it's a disagreement about what a mechanism is supposed to be. "Cost" can be read as a genuine explanatory posit, or as a redescription of the fact that a signal happens to be expensive, and on the second reading the handicap principle can't possibly lose. Its also striking to me how few of the empirical papers actually measure a marginal cost curve: mostly they measure a correlation between signal size and quality, which both camps predict anyway. So maybe the whole thing is one verbal dispute plus one measurement problem wearing the costume of a biological controversy.

Is that right, or is there a real checkable question buried in there? I want a diagnosis built out of what each side actually claims, not a survey of the positions.

Here's the stress test I want to run it against, because I'm working on a piece about the generative-AI content flood and it maps onto this wierdly well. Grant me the premise that producing a polished essay, a chunk of working code, or a decent song has become nearly free, and that credentialing is drifting toward things like handwritten in-person exams, supervised orals, and artists posting footage of themselves working. Under each of the two accounts, what should we predict there — an escalating arms race of cost, or a swing toward signals that are hard to fake because of how they're produced? If both accounts predict the same thing, say so, and say what that tells us.

Last thing: given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — an experiment, a formal result, a distinction, or dropping the question? And should I definately expect this to be resolved in my lifetime, or is permanent stalemate the honest answer?
