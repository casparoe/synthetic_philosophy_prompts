Me and my mate got into it about hinge epistemology last night after a seminar and he rebuilt my own objection better than I could, and now I can't find the loose step. His version goes like this:

(1) A hinge proposition is defined by its job: it's what has to stay put for doubt, evidence and justification of other things to make sense at all, so it can't itself be justified or doubted from inside the practice it belongs to.
(2) That job is functional, not genealogical — nothing about being a hinge depends on how the proposition got installed. One that was drilled into a kid by a Cult leader functions as a hinge just as well as one you absorbed by growing up in an ordinary form of life.
(3) So if a community's practices make something like "the Leader is never wrong" exempt from doubt, then inside that community there literally is no such thing as checking the Leader's claims — the doubt isn't false, it's unthinkable.
(4) Rational criticism of a belief needs a shared practice in which the doubt is intelligible; where the practice differs, all you can do is describe what each side does.
(5) So hinge epistemology can't say anything against systematic indoctrination, or against any deviant framework at all — it collapses straight into relativism about rationality.
(6) But that can't be what the view was for: it was supposed to describe the structure of our certainties, not to say that anything a community is sure of is as good as anything else. And (5) is just absurd anyway. So something above is wrong.

Every single step looked fine to me at 1am and I can't spot the slide. Which step do you reject, why does it look innocent when it isn't, and what do you end up paying? I'd rather hold on to (1) and to the anti-genealogical point in (2) than save (6), but I want to know what holding them costs. Also, if it matters: what does each of the two big readings — the one where hinges aren't really truth-apt propositions at all but something more like rules, and the one where they are propositions we hold without evidence because inquiry needs them — have to give up to block (5)?
