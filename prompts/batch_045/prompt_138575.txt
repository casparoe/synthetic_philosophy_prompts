ok so i've been going back and forth on the Lucas-Penrose style argument and im not sure what the actual load-bearing premise is supposed to be. As I get it: hand me any machine, or any recursively axiomatized system M, that's meant to capture my mathematical output; I build its Gödel sentence G(M); I can see G(M) is true while M can't prove it; so I out-run every machine, so im not one.

What I want explained carefully is the gap between "I can out-Gödel any machine I'm handed" and "I'm not a machine", and I want it explained through this wrinkle. Take a sound extension M of arithmetic, and consider M-minus: M plus the arithmetical sentence that says "there is a proof of 0=1 from M's axioms", i.e. a machine that asserts its own inconsistency. If M is consistent, then M-minus is consistent too, by the second incompleteness theorem — but M-minus thinks everything is provable, so for its own Gödel sentence it cheerfully proves not-G(M-minus). A machine can be perfectly consistent and still decide the exact sentence the anti-mechanist was going to wave at it. (Tell me if I've built that wrong.)

So: does the argument smuggle in the premise that the machine is sound, or at least Sigma-1-sound, rather than merely consistent? And if that's the premise, what exactly is the anti-mechanist claiming to be able to do — recognise soundness in arbitrary candidate machines? only in ones she built herself? only in ones she likes? Because it looks to me like once the argument needs "I can tell that a consistent machine is spinning a false story about its own inconsistency," it stops being an argument about proof-theoretic strength and turns into something else, maybe about self-knowledge, or about whether I can ever know which machine I am (set aside for a second the usual point that I might be M without being in a position to tell). And then does the same trouble come back one level up, so that we get a regress of ever-shifting machines?

Please actually separate the premises out and say what each one is doing in the argument, and say which one you think the anti-mechanist should retreat to — don't just give me an even-handed summary of the debate.
