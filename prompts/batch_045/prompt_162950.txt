Hi all — I've been nursing a view on the equality-of-what question for a few years now and I want someone to try to break it. Short version: I think welfare, resources and capabilities are all proxies for a currency that never makes it onto the standard list, namely what I call a person's security profile: the expected fraction of the possible life-histories open to them, weighted by how much each of those histories is up to them, in which they retain a threshold set of central human capabilities. Justice, on my view, is about equalising (or, on my less confident days, prioritising) that, assessed over complete lives rather than at time slices. What attracted me to it is that it seems to answer the standard complaints against each of the big three in one move: the expensive-tastes objection to welfarism, the adaptive-preference worry, and the resourcist's trouble with people who convert identical bundles very differently.

So: what would count against it? I'd like the defeaters ranked, fatal at the top and merely awkward at the bottom. Four things I'm especially curious about, but please don't feel confined to them.

1. Collapse. Is this secretly one of its rivals in disguise? If I weight variance-by-control at one extreme do I just get resourcism, at the other do I get expected welfare, and in the middle do I get the capability view with extra notation? And if it does collapse, is that a defeater or a result I should be pleased about?

2. Cases it can't describe properly. Two worry me. The Vow: someone who knowingly and gladly surrenders a central capability for good (silence, celibacy, the cloister, the alpinist who burns everything on one expedition) and is never alienated from that choice at any later point. My view seems committed to counting them as badly off, and I'm not sure I can live with that. The Lottery: two people with identical expected security profiles, where all of A's variance is option luck they went looking for and all of B's is brute bad luck that no hypothetical insurance market would cover. If the view can't tell them apart, that's serious for me.

3. How much is actually at risk. The threshold set and the control-weighting are doing a lot of work, and I'm worried I've built something that can be squared with any distribution you like by fiddling with them. How exposed is the view really, and is that flexibility a vice or just the ordinary vagueness of a mid-level principle? If you think it's insulated to the point of being unfalsifiable, say so and say whether that matters for a claim of this kind.

4. What it predicts that its rivals don't. If there's no distribution, and no case, where this disagrees with plain capabilities, I should probably just be a capability theorist and stop.

For what it's worth: the thing that would move me most is a case where the view plainly misclassifies someone as disadvantaged, rather than a formal or measurment worry. And I'd rather hear what a sympathetic defender of something in this neighbourhood would themselves concede as counting against it than what an out-and-out welfarist would say. Oh, and please don't spend a paragraph telling me the capability literature already covers all this — if you think it does, name the claim of mine that's already in it and tell me what follows. Thanks!
