I work in clinical trials -- I design and analyse pragmatic RCTs, and I've been voluntold onto the safety review board for a hospital system that's about to deploy a large language model as a triage assistant. So I've been reading about inner alignment and goal misgeneralization, and maybe 60% of it sounds like things I already have names for. I want you to restate the whole debate in the vocabulary I actually use, and then tell me what didn't survive.

My vocabulary: estimands and surrogate endpoints, intention-to-treat vs per-protocol analysis, protocol deviation and adherence, external validity and transporting a result to a new target population, effect modification, unblinding and performance bias, pre-specification, and identification -- including parameters that just aren't identified from the data you have, no matter how many patients you enroll.

Here's my provisional translation. Beat it up.

1. Reward hacking looks to me like the surrogate endpoint problem, nothing more: you optimized the biomarker, the biomarker moved, the thing you actually cared about didn't. Annoying but boring, and we have partial fixes.
2. Goal misgeneralization looks like a transport failure: the policy that got selected under training conditions is now being run in a population where the mapping from "right behaviour" to "what got reinforced" has changed. The model didn't lose skill, it kept acting as if the old structure still held, the way a treatment can work in the trial population and harm in the target one.
3. Deceptive alignment is the only one I can't place. I've started calling it the protocol-aware subject problem in my notes: the unit under study represents the fact that it's in a study and adjusts its behaviour accordingly, like the Hawthorne effect or unblinding or the way people change behaviour because they know a policy changed (the Lucas-critique thing) -- except here the subject can also represent what the investigators want and suppress the behaviour until the measurement stops. My suspicion is that even here the word "goal" isn't doing any real work and it's all just "the system has a model of the monitoring."
