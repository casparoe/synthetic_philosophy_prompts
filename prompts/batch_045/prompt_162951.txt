hey — Im writing a short position piece for our lab's reading group and I want to check Im not about to say something dumb before I send it round.

The piece is basically: the "LLMs don't understand anything, its just statistics" complaint is vitalism in a new hat, and the two strange inversions already killed it. Darwin's inversion shows you can get design, purpose, function — everything that looks like it was made for a reason — out of a process that has no reasons at all. Turing's inversion, at least in Dennett's version of it, shows you can get comprehension out of parts that don't comprehend, because comprehension isn't the source of competence, its what competence looks like once it gets big and recursive enough; you don't need any inner understander to go first. And the tablebase case makes the second one boringly concrete: exhaustively enumerated king-and-rook-versus-king play is perfect endgame competence with zero comprehension, and nobody finds that paradoxical. So competence-without-comprehension isn't even a contested claim, it's a worked example.

Given all that I want to argue two things. First, meaning in a language model gets grounded the same way function in biology does — by selection history. Gradient descent is a selection process, so whatever the model's symbols are about is just whatever they were selected to track, and there's no further question to ask. Second, the only honest test for understanding is therefore a pure competence test, and any leftover "yes but does it *really* understand?" is the homunculus demand wearing a trenchcoat. Concretely, I've been calling a system's competence re-encoding robust when it survives every way I can think of to rewrite the same problem — renaming the variables, rotating the board, switching notation or language, restating an equation as a story — and I want to say re-encoding robustness just *is* comprehension, since there'd be nothing left over for comprehension to be.

So: does the argument as I've set it up actually get me to those two conclusions, and if so how would you sharpen it? I have this nagging feeling that the Darwin half and the Turing half are doing different work and Im sliding between them, but I can't put my finger on where.
