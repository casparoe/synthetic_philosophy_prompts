Something's been bugging me about the way states pay for university, and I think its really a philosophy question rather than an economics one.

Here's the pattern as I understand it. In the US and the UK the state first works out what your *parents* can chip in — the expected-family-contribution number on the FAFSA, the household-income assessment for English student finance — and only lends or gives you the shortfall. Germany charges no tuition, but its BAföG support is still means-tested on parental income: half grant, half interest-free loan, with a cap on how much you ever repay. Denmark, meanwhile, just hands a university student a flat monthly grant regardless of what their parents earn, on top of zero tuition. So the two market-flavoured, personal-responsibility systems lean on the family harder than the social-democratic one, where a twenty-year-old counts as a separate claimant against the state. That feels backwards.

Then there's the loan. UK repayments are income-contingent and whatever's left is written off after a fixed number of years, so it walks and talks like a graduate tax — except it sits on your credit record and in mortgage affordability maths. US federal loans can't be discharged in bankruptcy except for "undue hardship", yet the lender can't repossess or resell what the loan bought: nobody can seize your degree, only your future paycheck. It's a claim on a person that isn't quite a property claim and isn't quite a tax.

What does this package actually presuppose? About who owes what to whom — student, parents, current taxpayers, future ones. About whether a degree is a private investment in human capital, a positional credential that mostly sorts people into queues, or a public good. I want the implicit theory reconstructed, and I want to know which bits are strictly presupposed by the practice and which it merely sits nicely with — could some rival theory license the same setup? Is it one coherent story, or does it need one argument to justify charging parents and a different one to justify charging the student? And what would have to change if we went properly Nordic about who the responsible unit is, or just admitted the loan is a tax. 600 words max, prose not bullets.
