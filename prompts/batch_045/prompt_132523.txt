The bit I'm stuck on is where they draw the line between the narrow and the broad way of stating the alignment problem.

Roughly, they said something like: the narrow problem is intent alignment, which just means the system ends up trying to do what the operator had in mind when they gave it the task — its goals are the operator's goals. The broad problem is value alignment, which asks that the system be trying to do what's actually good, or that its goals be the ones we'd endorse after thinking about it more and knowing more. They said people have settled on the narrow definition because it's the only one you can actually test: you can check whether the system does what you asked for, whereas you can't check what's good. Then they said intent alignment is basically just outer alignment with a new name, since both come down to whether the thing we specified captures what we wanted, and that inner alignment — whether the model's actual learned goal matches the objective — is a training problem, not really part of the alignment problem at all.
