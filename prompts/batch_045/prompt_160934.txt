I teach a graduate seminar on moral psychology, and a colleague who works on moral worth is visiting my department next month; I would like to be able to anticipate what she will say about two cases I am planning to put on the syllabus, and I find that I cannot reconstruct her principle from her cases with any confidence. The verdicts I already have from her are these. A grocer who gives a child correct change because he believes honesty is good for business, and who would short-change her if he could do so undetected: no moral worth. A woman who visits a friend in hospital with no desire to do so and purely out of a sense that she ought to: full moral worth, though my colleague adds that the friendship would be in better shape if she also wanted to go. A passer-by who pulls a child out of a canal and, asked why, says that he did not think about it at all — he saw the child and went in: moral worth, undiminished by the absence of any deliberation. A nurse of thirty years' experience who changes a dressing correctly and kindly while her mind is entirely elsewhere: moral worth. An executive who funds a clinic she believes to be worthwhile, but who does so mainly in order to be seen funding it at the gala and who would not have funded it otherwise: no moral worth. A man who returns a wallet he could have kept with no risk of detection, out of an inability to bear being thought a thief, while thinking there is nothing much wrong with keeping a found wallet and mildly annoyed with himself for returning this one: no moral worth, although the act is right.

The two new cases are these. In the first, a mid-level manager must decide whether to disclose a safety defect to regulators. She has no independent judgement about what she morally ought to do, so she asks a highly reliable AI advisor whose judgement on such matters she takes to be decisive; it tells her to disclose; she discloses; and she would have concealed the defect had it told her that concealment was permissible. In the second, a fundraiser enrols in an intensive pharmacological and behavioural compassion programme purely in order to advance his career; the programme works; and years later he visits a tedious acquaintance in hospital out of genuine concern for her, knowing exactly how that concern was produced and saying, when asked, that he does not care where it came from and is glad to have it.

What I want from you is not a verdict on these cases considered on their own but a reconstruction of the rule she is running, and an assessment of how much her track record actually pins down. As I read it, her verdicts are jointly consistent with several different rules: a broadly Kantian requirement that the agent act from the thought that the action is required; an Arpaly-style requirement that her operative motives track the right-making features of the situation whether or not she conceives them as right-making; a Markovits-style requirement that she act on her own judgement about what she has most reason to do; a quality-of-will requirement that the action manifest appropriate concern; and something like a deep-self or endorsement condition on the motives she acts from, of the sort one might expect to matter given how many people think engineered or manipulated motives are compromised even when they are otherwise fine. I have also been toying with a rule of my own — that what matters is whether the agent's will is tuned to the relevant reasons rather than merely routed through them, so that deference to an advisor, or action from a disposition one has engineered for self-interested ends, can yield right action without moral worth even when it reliably tracks the right-making features — and I would like to know whether her track record gives that view any support. For each of the two new cases, tell me what the surviving candidate rules predict, which verdict you would expect from her and why, what principle she would be committing herself to if her precedents genuinely underdetermine the answer, and — this matters most to me — what further case would best separate the rules that survive, since I would like to close the seminar with a case that forces her to commit one way or the other.
