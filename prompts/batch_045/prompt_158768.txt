The position I want translated: David Lewis's analysis of convention in Convention (1969). Specifically, all of its moving parts: convention as a regularity of behavior in a population, in a recurrent type of situation, where nearly everyone conforms, nearly everyone expects nearly everyone else to conform, nearly everyone prefers to conform on condition that the others conform (so that conformity is a coordination equilibrium and there is some alternative regularity that would also have been such an equilibrium had others conformed to it) — the arbitrariness condition — and where all of this is common knowledge rather than merely mutually believed to some finite depth. Alongside the definition I want the accompanying story about how such regularities get started and change without anyone having convened or agreed: the role of salience, of precedent, of reasoning by analogy, and the gradual accretion of expectations that turns a coincidence into a rule. And I want the application to language: the sender-receiver signaling games used throughout the modern literature on the emergence of communication, in which a signal is supposed to acquire something meaning-like from nothing but a coordination equilibrium among agents who are assumed to share no prior semantic resources.

Part III, four stress cases, each of which I want run through the translation and then evaluated.

1. A public signal with no one behind it. Two agents trained together converge on conditioning their behavior on a shared random signal supplied by the environment, reaching a correlated equilibrium: each is optimizing given the signal, and neither has any hierarchy of expectations about the other's expectations at all, only a learned response to an observation. 2. The alternative that cannot be represented. A population locks into a stable but bad joint policy — the familiar multipolar trap — and the better joint policy lies outside what either policy network can express, either because of the parameterization or because training never visits that basin of attraction. Lewis's arbitrariness condition is counterfactual: the agents would have preferred to conform to the other regularity had the others conformed to it. Tell me whether my vocabulary can state that counterfactual at all when the alternative is unrepresentable, and whether Lewis's account then misclassifies a lock-in as a convention or correctly declines to call it one.

3. Sanctions rather than coordination. Consider a regularity maintained by third-party punishment, where conforming is individually costly and each agent would deviate if unobserved: not a coordination problem at all, but something more like a repeated public-goods problem with an enforcement term.
