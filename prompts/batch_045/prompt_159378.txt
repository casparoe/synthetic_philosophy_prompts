I sit on the teaching committee of a university that has spent the last two years rewriting its assessment rules, and I have come to think that the rules we have landed on do not agree with one another about what we are doing when we educate someone.

The regime, in outline. Core modules are assessed by invigilated, closed-book, handwritten examinations in which no external resource is permitted: no notes, no searchable copy of the set texts, no calculator in a numerate paper. Where work is submitted rather than sat, the marking criteria award substantial credit for method — a wrong answer reached by a route the marker can follow scores well, a right answer whose route the marker cannot follow scores badly. Students are routinely asked, in a viva or a written follow-up, to reconstruct in their own words how they got from question to answer, and failure to do this is treated as evidence that the work was not theirs. And the newest rule, the one we argued about longest: any use of a generative model in producing assessed work is handled as a provenance offence, reported to a disciplinary board, rather than as a wrong answer, a defective method, or a sign that the student has not acquired what the module was meant to give them.

Two features of this strike me as hard to reconcile. First, we assess under deliberate deprivation and then certify people for work in which they will be expected to use every tool available, and to work alongside colleagues who supply what they lack. Second, we are inconsistent about the deprivation itself without noticing: the law paper hands out a statute book and nobody concludes that it has stopped testing the student, while the physics paper withholds a calculator and nobody concludes that it has started testing arithmetic. Meanwhile the marker seems to care about reconstructible process and the disciplinary board about authorship, and these can come apart — a student who used a model to produce the work may perfectly well have acquired the capacity the module was aimed at, and a student who produced every word unaided may have acquired nothing that survives a change of context.

The lever I want to apply is Turing's. In the section on learning machines of his 1950 paper on computing machinery and intelligence he proposes, as I take it, that instead of trying to write a programme that simulates an adult mind directly, one should build something more like a child's mind and then educate it — reward, punishment, trial and error, contact with the world — so that most of what makes it competent is acquired rather than installed, and so that the teacher cannot fully inspect, in the finished machine, what the teaching has put there.

So my question: what does our regime have to assume about what learning is, and about what a qualification certifies, for its parts to make sense? I would like the pieces separated rather than treated as one package. Call the requirement that the student be able to reconstruct their route to the answer the reconstruction condition; call the removal of tools the deprivation proxy; call the handling of model use as an authorship offence provenance grading. Which of these strictly presupposes that education aims at a capacity stored in the learner as opposed to a stock of retrievable content, and which is equally at home under either picture? Does the deprivation proxy actually measure the thing it is a proxy for, or does it measure a particular kind of internalisation that the profession we certify people into does not require? Is the reconstruction condition a test of ownership, or only a test of one person's ability to talk about their own competence — and if the educated mind can be opaque even to its owner, as Turing's picture suggests, what exactly is that condition filtering for?

Finally: if we took the child-machine picture seriously as a model of what education does to a person, what would assessment have to look like instead — what would we have to start measuring, what would we have to stop treating as evidence, and which of our present rules would survive the change untouched?
