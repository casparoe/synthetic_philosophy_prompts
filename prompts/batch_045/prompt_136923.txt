hey — novelist here, about 250 pages into a book I've been loosing sleep over for three years, and I've run into a wall that turns out to be a philosophy problem rather than a plotting problem. hoping you can help.

setup, short version. in the book, a made-up country sets up something I'm calling the Commission of Weights: its job is to divide one fixed pot of reparations money — plus nine minutes of speaking time at a national memorial — among groups descended from several different atrocities. a couple of those are meant to feel like recognisable-in-spirit versions of real ones, but the one my two protagonists care about is entirely invented, and it's the one I need the thinking done on:

- the Long Allotment: roughly thirty-eight years of ration-and-resettlement administration in one province. nobody ever ordered anybody killed. the deaths fell out of a ration formula, out of a rule that grain couldn't cross a district line without a stamp obtainable only in the district capital, and out of the fact that the district capital was where the shortage was worst. estimates run from forty thousand to four hundred thousand dead, because in the amnesty year 800 of the district ledgers went to the pulping mill.
- a deliberate part: for two generations children were caned for speaking the local language in school. it went from about 200,000 speakers to a few hundred. that bit was definitely intentional and is definitely tiny next to the deaths.
- a lot of the clerks who ran the allotments were conscripted from the very same districts, and some of them ended up standing in the ration queues themselves.
- and the same population had, forty years earlier, been burned out by a neighbouring state's retreat, so two of the claimant groups are descended from partly the same dead people, and everyone's lawyers know it.

so: before I write the Commission's method, I want the specification. what does a method for ranking atrocities by severity have to be able to *do* in order to be good enough to allocate a finite fund? I don't want a verdict on the Long Allotment, I want the criteria, each with the case behind it — think of it as me trying to work out what my Commission is even arguing about.

could you give it to me in these sections, in this order:

1. "What the verdict is for" — about 150 words. pin down what the Commission is actually producing, given that it has to pay out money and hand over nine minutes of airtime: is it ranking wrongness, harm, or present claim? say why that fixes the rest of the list.
2. "The requirements" — about 700 words, the main event. numbered, each with the requirement, the case from the Long Allotment (or from the claimants) that makes it a requirement, and a mark for non-negotiable vs. a preference of one camp. things I already know I want handled in there: the ledger problem (a verdict under radical uncertainty about numbers, and why a method mustn't just reward whichever atrocity had better archivists); the grain problem (must the ranking be the same when one atrocity is described as a single policy and when it's described as eleven thousand individual decisions, and what happens if it isn't); the double-counting problem when the same dead people sit under two claimant groups; unintended-versus-intended; diffuse/bureaucratic agency where the perpetrators are also victims; tempo (thirty-eight slow years against one bad month); deaths against the destruction of a language; and whether a *near-miss* atrocity — one that was ninety per cent built and then abandoned — has to be rankable at all.
3. "Demands I should ignore" — about 200 words. the things people shout for in these arguments that are really just one side's prejudice dressed up as a criterion.
4. "Where the list eats itself" — about 350 words. pairs of requirements you suspect can't both be satisfied, and which single requirement excludes the most candidate methods. this is the bit I most want, because the Commission in my book is going to break on exactly this, and I'd rather it break somewhere true.
5. "Scorecard" — about 250 words only, keep it brief. a line or two each for four families of method: total-harm/body-count, culpability-and-intent-first, incommensurability ("we decline to rank"), and let-the-claimants-bargain-it-out.

british english please. and don't point me at books or papers — I'm not going to read a reading list, and my characters have to sound like people who are actually in the room, not like a bibliography with opinions.
