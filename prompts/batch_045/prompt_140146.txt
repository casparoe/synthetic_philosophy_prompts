Here's my opening move. Brinkmanship needs two separable things: a risk of things getting out of hand that is genuinely real, and the ability to nudge that risk upward in a controlled way during a crisis and then take it back down. The standard defense of deterrence is that it isn't a threat to commit suicide but a shared manipulation of exactly that risk. But a system like mine severs the two. If you pre-delegate, you buy commitment at the cost of the ratchet: the risk is whatever the procurement process chose, all-or-nothing, and changing it mid-crisis means rewriting code under time pressure, which announces that the commitment was never real.
