Me and two people from an online reading group have been slowly working through anthropics stuff — self-location, the doomsday argument, all of that — and I promised to write down where I've actually landed before the next session, because last time I got caught holding two things at once and couldn't say which one to drop. So here's the list, roughly in the order I picked the views up:

1. In Sleeping Beauty I'm a thirder. My reason is basically the frequency one: run the experiment a hundred times and two thirds of my awakenings are tails-awakenings, so 2/3 is what I should believe when I wake up. This is the item I'm most attached to.
2. I reject the self-indication assumption. My main reason is the presumptuous philosopher: the thought that I can sit in an armchair, notice that I exist, and thereby get strong evidence for whichever cosmological theory says there are more observers — and against the one that says there are fewer — strikes me as obviously wrong. That's what telescopes and data are for, not what my own existence is for.
3. But I do accept the self-sampling assumption, in the observer-moment version, and I think the reference class should be fixed by whatever the correct theory of consciousness turns out to say, not by whatever delivers the conclusions I already wanted. I'm fairly convinced lots of animals are conscious, so I think that class is going to come out much wider than "humans" and will include things like chickens at enormous numbers.
4. I accept the doomsday argument, at least as one piece of evidence rather than a knockdown: given self-sampling, finding yourself at roughly human #100-billion-ish when you had no prior reason to expect that particular rank does push toward "the human future is shorter than the naive expectation," and I have literally moved numbers in a spreadsheet over this.
5. I also think anthropic reasoning has real bite for existential-risk work: the fact that we find ourselves in a civilisation that got through the cold war without a nuclear exchange is weaker evidence that nuclear technology is safe than it looks, because observers at our stage are disproportionately the ones whose civilisations didn't get wiped out earlier (I think this is sometimes called the anthropic shadow). I lean on this constantly in arguments about cause prioritisation.
6. And I think the whole machinery probably breaks if the universe turns out to be spatially infinite, which cosmology keeps hinting at — but I'd rather quarantine that than give up anthropic reasoning in the finite cases.

What I want is an audit, not an explainer. Which of these are genuinely in tension with which, and how badly? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and for each real one tell me which commitment would have to give and what giving it up costs me. If any pair only looks inconsistent and actually dissolves once you think about it carefully, say so and say why, because I don't want to throw away a view I'm entitled to keep. And if there's a single revision that would clean up more than one of the tensions at once, tell me what it is. Don't be gentle about which item has to go — I don't have a favourite among the six, and I'd rather end up with a set that hangs together than with all of them.
