I'm the TA for a small graduate seminar on heuristic arguments — reasoning that gets its grip from a plausible-but-unargued assumption rather than from a full probability model — and this week's problem set was about how to formalize the presumption of independence such arguments lean on. The seminar has been working with stylized duration heuristics: you find yourself at some point in a process whose total length you don't know, you treat your position as unremarkable, and you draw a conclusion about the remainder. We covered three sampling rules (a rule being a specification of how your observation time was produced): internal sampling, anchor sampling, and a trigger rule where you observe because the process itself crossed some threshold and so your observation time is a function of the process. Question 3 sticks to the first two. I've now graded about twenty of these scripts and I don't trust myself to be rewarding fluency rather than correctness, so I'd like an independent read before I fix the rubric. The question and one student's answer are below; I've left my own view out deliberately.

**Question 3 (20 points).** A research programme begins at time 0 and will reach its target at an unknown time T > 0. At time t after the start you observe that the target has not yet been reached, and you want to say something about how long the remainder will be. The seminar's two benchmark sampling rules are:

INTERNAL: your observation time is drawn uniformly from the interval [0, T].
ANCHOR: your observation time is fixed at t0 by factors independent of T; the duration is what is unknown.

(a) [6 points] Derive the likelihood for T under each rule (the two-clocks lemma: L(T) ∝ 1/T for T ≥ t under INTERNAL; L(T) ∝ 1{T > t0} under ANCHOR). Then state the presumption of independence in the form f ⫫ T, where f = t/T is your fractional position and f ~ U(0,1), and explain why it holds under one rule and fails under the other.

(b) [5 points] Under INTERNAL and a scale-invariant prior π(T) ∝ 1/T, show that P(T ≤ 2t | t) = 1/2. Then say how much of that headline number is owed to the sampling rule and how much to the prior — for instance by recomputing it with π flat on [0, U].

(c) [9 points] A critic argues that ANCHOR, not INTERNAL, is the rule that fits the actual practice of forecasting from a fixed vantage point: you write your survey in a given year because that is the year it is, not because the process selected that date for you. On ANCHOR, the critic says, the heuristic collapses into mere truncation of the prior and the expect-to-be-halfway intuition has no purchase. Assess the critic's argument. In particular: does the critic's case, if successful, show that the presumption of independence is unjustified, or only that the prior component is doing more work than the heuristic's presentation suggests? Your answer should engage the seminar's Separation Thesis — the claim that an argument of this shape decomposes into a sampling-model component and a prior component that can be assessed independently of one another — and the dictum that a sampling rule fixes the likelihood, so that you do not get to stack a second selection correction on top of it without a second selection step in the model.

**Student's answer (verbatim):**

(a) Under INTERNAL the density of t given T is 1/T on [0, T] and zero outside; read as a function of T with t held fixed this is L(T) ∝ 1/T for T ≥ t. Under ANCHOR the observation time carries no information about T on its own; all I learn is that the programme was still running at t0, so L(T) ∝ 1 for T > t0 and zero otherwise.

The presumption of independence says that f = t/T is independent of T and uniform on [0, 1]. Under INTERNAL this is immediate, since f | T ~ U(0,1) for every T. Under ANCHOR it fails outright: f = t0/T is a deterministic decreasing function of T, so conditional on T the position is a point mass. The presumption is therefore a claim about how my observation time was produced, not a way of registering ignorance about T. (Relatedly: if what I observed were f itself rather than t, the INTERNAL likelihood would be flat in T and I would learn nothing about T at all — the entire force of the observation comes from my knowing the absolute time and not the fraction.)

(b) With π(T) ∝ 1/T and L(T) ∝ 1/T the posterior is ∝ 1/T² on [t, ∞), so P(T ≤ 2t) = ∫_t^{2t} T^(−2) dT ÷ ∫_t^∞ T^(−2) dT = (1/t − 1/2t) ÷ (1/t) = 1/2. With π flat on [0, U] the posterior is ∝ 1/T on [t, U] and P(T ≤ 2t) = ln 2 ÷ ln(U/t), which goes to 0 as U grows. So the headline is mostly prior: the sampling rule supplies the 1/T tilt, the scale-invariant prior supplies the rest, and without it the argument tells you nothing you didn't already put in.

(c) The critic is right that ANCHOR is the realistic rule for a forecast written in a particular year, and right that on ANCHOR the inference is just truncation of the prior at t0 — with π flat on [0, U] that moves the expectation from U/2 to (t0 + U)/2, and with a scale-invariant prior the truncated posterior is improper at the upper end, so on ANCHOR the argument cannot even produce its headline number. But this does not damage the position-based reasoning. Even under ANCHOR, once I conditionalize on the programme's being unfinished at t0, my fractional position f = t0/T is spread over (0, 1], and with no reason to think my position special I should treat it as uniform; independence then holds after conditioning, and the correct likelihood is the INTERNAL one on top of the truncation, i.e. L(T) ∝ (1/T) · 1{T > t0} ÷ P(T > t0). What the critic has shown is at most that the argument is prior-sensitive, not that its independence presumption fails.

And it is no reply to invoke the Separation Thesis here. That thesis says the heuristic decomposes into a sampling-model component and a prior component; but decomposition is not insulation, since the posterior is the product of the two and a defect in either infects the whole. The criticisms transfer: if the prior component is doing all the work then the independence presumption is doing none, and if the independence presumption fails then no choice of prior will save the argument. So once the critic is granted the point about the prior, the point about the presumption of independence is granted too, which is what the critic claimed. Either way the heuristic carries no force.
