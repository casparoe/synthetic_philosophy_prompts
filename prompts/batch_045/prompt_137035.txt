I am on the working group revising our national classification of mental disorders, and before we argue about any individual category I want the specification we should be holding ourselves to.

Fix the purpose first, because I take adequacy to be relative to it. Our classification has to serve three uses at once: guiding treatment and giving a usable prognosis in ordinary clinical settings; supporting research that looks for mechanisms and for causal risk factors; and producing statistics that remain comparable across a decade or more, so that service planning and epidemiological work are not destroyed by every revision. It will also be published openly, with patient-facing descriptions of every category, because we treat informed consent and self-referral as non-negotiable.

Write the desiderata for a classification built for those purposes. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement: reliability in routine hands; whatever notion of validity we are entitled to ask for, and of which kind (predictive, construct, etiological, consequential); the capacity to distinguish a real change in incidence from a change in recognition, in awareness, or in the idioms in which distress is expressed and reported; a revision procedure that does not turn every correction into a break in the time series; heterogeneity within a category and the near-universal overlap between categories; threshold versus dimension, splitting versus lumping; transparency about which judgments in the manual are evidential and which are administrative or moral. Add anything you think belongs that I have not listed. Where you illustrate with a familiar case, mark whether the empirical claim behind it is contested.

Two things matter more to me than completeness.

First, separate the genuine requirements from the preferences of a camp. Some things on any such list — carving discrete neurobiological kinds, being fully dimensional, being value-free, being usable by non-specialists, being usable non-coercively — look like requirements to the people who already want them. Tell me which survive as desiderata, which are negotiable, and which are really a research programme smuggled in as a criterion.

Second, the looping problem. Psychiatric categories classify people who can learn the classification, and the classified may come to experience, describe, present, seek help for, or organise their lives around what the category makes available; the category is then revised in response to the people it has changed, and the surrounding culture of self-description shifts as well. Please keep those three movements distinct rather than running them together, and then tell me which items on your list are hardest to satisfy given that all three occur. Is any pair jointly unsatisfiable — openness to patients against minimal iatrogenic effect, stability for comparison against responsiveness when a category's meaning drifts, specificity for the individual against reliability across settings? Is it even a desideratum that a classification should not itself generate phenomena of the kind it names, or is that a mistake, given that classification can also relieve suffering?

One suspicion I want the list to adjudicate. Several members think the looping considerations show that these categories track nothing, so that a nosology can only ever be a matter of convenience. I want to know whether that follows, or whether it depends on collapsing a distinction the list should preserve — for instance between a kind being unstable and a kind being unreal, or between a category failing to describe an antecedent condition and a category failing to do any work.

In a short closing section, no more than a third of your answer, apply the list briefly to three stances you will recognise from the literature without my naming them: descriptive operational criteria validated mainly by reliability and clinical utility; a mechanism-first scheme that wants kinds to be defined by underlying systems rather than by symptoms; and a view on which a disorder is a harmful dysfunction, so that the pathology is factual and the disorderhood is partly evaluative. Then name the single requirement on your list that excludes the most candidates, and say what we lose if we drop it.
