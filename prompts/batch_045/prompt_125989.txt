Hey — I'm running a reading group on the Liar next term (mostly philosophy grad students, two logicians who will argue with everything). Instead of walking through theories one by one I want to hand out a decision tree that takes someone from "here's a sentence that says of itself that it isn't true" down to a named position. Can you build it?

What I want: five to seven branch questions, each phrased as a commitment someone can answer without knowing where it leads, and around eight leaves — Tarskian hierarchy, Kripkean gap/paracomplete, supervaluationist, revision-theory, dialetheic/paraconsistent, contextualist (the Burge / Parsons / Simmons / Glanzberg family), deflationist dissolution, and whatever you think belongs that I've left out. Each leaf gets a label (or "unnamed" if nobody holds it) plus one sentence on the most awkward thing someone at that leaf is stuck with.

Two of the nodes have to be cases I made up, because I want the tree to actually do work rather than sort labels:

- The Borrowed Loop: sentence A (in language one) says "no sentence in language two that ascribes truth to a sentence in language one is true", and sentence B (in language two) says "A is true". Does the theory's stratification run by language-level, by subject matter, or by something else — and are there theories that can't even state the pair?
- The Scheduled Liar: a card reading "the sentence written on the whiteboard tomorrow at noon is not true", where what gets written depends on the weather. Can a sentence's semantic status hang on a non-semantic fact on your theory, and what happens to it if the board stays blank?

Also please build the logical space first — which combinations of answers are actually available — then locate the real positions in it, mark the empty cells and say briefly why nobody lives there (incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored). And flag any pair of leaves your tree can't separate: I'd much rather read "these two only come apart on X" than a fake split. Last thing I care about: put a branch somewhere on whether the theory can state its own semantics without cooking up a fresh paradox (revenge / self-application), since that's the bit I actually want to fight about at the end.
