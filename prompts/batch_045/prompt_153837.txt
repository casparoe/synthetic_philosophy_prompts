Construct a variant of the Sleeping Beauty problem that isolates a question the standard case does not isolate.

What the case is for: I am preparing a graduate seminar on self-locating belief, and I want to drive a wedge between two questions the usual setup runs together: first, how many centered possibilities Beauty should spread her credence over (the halfer/thirder dispute); second, whether purely centered evidence can rationally move her credence in a plainly uncentered proposition, such as how the coin landed. I want a case whose verdict on the second question is robust: however you settle the first, the answer to the second comes out the same. That is what fixes what counts as cheating here. A case that gets its result by stipulating credences, or by letting Beauty learn something that carries uncentered news about the coin, has not done the job.

Specifications:

1. One fair coin, one flip; the coin influences nothing about the world except the schedule of Beauty's awakenings. No clones, fission, duplication, extra observers, or exotic machinery — only the standard erasure of the day between awakenings.
2. At least one awakening occurs whichever way the coin lands, so that Beauty's evidence "I am now awake in the experiment" is itself uninformative about the coin.
3. Every possible schedule includes a Monday awakening, so that the uncentered proposition "an awakening occurs on Monday" is already certain given the design, independently of the coin.
4. Beauty is uncertain, in the ordinary Sleeping Beauty way, about which awakening she is in and what day it is. She then acquires exactly one item of purely self-locating information — say, she reads a date display that is always present, always legible, and whose reading depends on nothing but the calendar — and this item must lower her credence that the coin landed Heads. Not raise it, not leave it where it was.
5. The lowering must show up under both treatments. Roughly: the halfer treatment, on which the awakening provides no new relevant information and credence in Heads stays at the fair-coin prior; and the thirder treatment, on which credence is divided among centered worlds in proportion to how often each kind of awakening occurs. The two may disagree about the numbers — I expect them to — but they must not disagree about the direction of the change. For the halfer's day-credences, use the treatment that yields three-fourths for Monday in the classic two-awakening case, i.e. uniform indifference over one's own awakenings within a branch; if your case needs a different treatment, say so and justify it.
6. The mechanism by which she learns the day must be clean: the display must not be more likely to be present, legible, or accurate on one coin outcome than on the other, and no one must choose to show it to her conditionally.

Deliverables:

- The scenario, in a single paragraph.
- The bookkeeping: Beauty's credence in Heads before she learns the day and after she learns it, computed explicitly under both treatments, with the assumptions of each written out rather than gestured at.
- A statement of which single feature of the design is doing the work, and why the classic case — which has the opposite profile — produces the opposite movement.
- The removal test: alter one feature so that the schedules become symmetric in the relevant respect, and show either that the case collapses back into the familiar one or that the direction of the update flips.
- How a defender of whichever position this case embarrasses would attack it. Cover at least two: someone who holds that centered evidence is evidentially inert with respect to uncentered hypotheses, and someone who wants Beauty's credence in Heads to remain at one-half both before and after she learns what day it is. If the case forces either position into an inconsistency, derive the inconsistency rather than asserting it.
- One honest sentence about what the case cannot show.

Write the whole thing in American English.
