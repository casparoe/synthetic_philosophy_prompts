I've started tracking my beliefs as ranges instead of single numbers — like, I'm 0.4–0.7 that the launch ships on time — because I got convinced point priors are overprecise. My question is how to actually decide with these. I built a little spreadsheet that takes the midpoint of each range and then does ordinary expected-utility maximization, and a friend says that throws away the whole point of going imprecise. I don't see how: surely a range is just a flat spread of second-order probability over the numbers in it, so the average is the right summary — and any decision rule has to collapse the range to one number eventually anyway, since you can't hand someone an interval and have them act on it. So the only real choice is which number you collapse to — mine's the midpoint, a pessimist takes the bottom, an optimist takes the top — and the midpoint seems like the neutral pick. Where am I going wrong?
