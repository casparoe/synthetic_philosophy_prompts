hey, I need a piece of satire and I want the philosophy in it to be actually right, not vibes.

Setup you can assume: it's five years after a fictional National Research Council banned "skyhooks" in anything it funds. You know Dennett's distinction — a crane is an explanatory mechanism that rests on things that are themselves explained, built up out of simpler, already-explained parts; a skyhook is something that's supposed to do explanatory lifting while hanging from nothing, an unexplained explainer. After the ban, every grant has to exhibit an unbroken crane chain.

What I want: an internal annual report (say 700 words) from a mid-sized university's Office of Explanatory Compliance, covering the first five years of the ban. Deadpan bureaucratic voice, headings, maybe a table of enforcement statistics, the odd footnote.

The thing the piece has to land: the office has quietly slid from "no skyhooks" into mandating greedy reductionism. Which is a different error from the one it was meant to fix — you can be a thoroughgoing reductionist about cranes and still think deriving Romeo's love from quark states is a bad research programme, because the explanation you need often lives at a higher level than the lowest one available. I'd rather that confusion show up in the texture of the document itself (the forms, the criteria, what got funded) than in a paragraph where someone explains it.

Some things I'd like worked in:

- the compliance form has a field for "lowest level reached," departments compete over it, and the classics department got flagged for stopping at intentional states
- a couple of genuine filings: the art history department being told to re-derive linear perspective from the visual system; the music theory group's filing about why a cadence is satisfying
- some invented bureaucratic category that is obviously a skyhook with paperwork — a "provisional crane," "licensed unexplained explainer," whatever you come up with
- at least one moment where the office itself commits the exact mistake it polices, e.g. grounding its own enforcement authority in something with no crane under it, without ever noticing
- one complaint that is legitimately sympathetic, so it's not just everyone being stupid

It should be funny to someone who knows the cranes/skyhooks literature and readable to someone who doesn't, and definately keep the irony legible — no winking at the reader, I want them to work out who's being made fun of.
