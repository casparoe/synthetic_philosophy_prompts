I'm on the safety review board at a small lab. We have a planner ("Marrow") that isn't allowed to affect the world unless it first produces a machine-checked proof, in its own theory T, that the action is ok. Every couple of weeks someone proposes a change to Marrow's reasoning stack — a new inference rule, a stronger theory, a faster verifier, a cache — and we recieve it and have to say yes or no. We're engineers who took one logic class, not proof theorists; we get an afternoon per patch and three of us have to sign. If we say yes to the wrong thing the agent acts on a fake proof; if we say no to everything it can never improve itself.

The worry is the Löbian one: T can't prove its own soundness, so Marrow can't prove that proofs produced by a modified version of itself mean anything, and if we just let it accept "I'm sound because I say so" we've deleted the whole reason we required proofs. (We know the theorem itself: if T proves Prov(φ)→φ then T proves φ, so an unrestricted "from a proof that φ is provable, infer φ" rule collapses everything.)

What I want is a written procedure we can run on a patch. Concretely: the questions in order; what counts as a good enough answer to each given we can't do proof theory; the points where the answer is an automatic no; and the points where it has to stop and hand us a values call rather than pretending to be a fact — I think "which theory do we trust, and how much unsoundness risk do we accept" is one of those, and I suspect "is the patched thing still Marrow" is another. These two seem seperate and I don't know which order to take them in. Be honest about where the checklist will misfire: patches that are safe but look scary, and patches that look like plumbing but quietly move the trust boundary.

Run it on three patches:

A) add the rule "from a proof that φ is provable, conclude φ", with no restriction on φ.
B) move from T to T plus Con(T) — let Marrow use the fact that its old theory is consistent, and check its successors' proofs in the new theory.
C) the proof cache we shipped six months ago and everyone loves: it stores every theorem it has already proved as an axiom and skips re-verification. I want the procedure's answer even if it tells us to unship it.

Give me the checklist as a table with columns: step_number | question_to_ask | sufficient_answer | if_it_fails (block / escalate / allow_with_condition) | what_this_step_cant_catch. Then a JSON object with one entry per patch, fields: patch_id, steps_triggered, verdict, reason_in_plain_english, judgment_calls_the_board_must_make, would_change_verdict_if. Plus top-level arrays assumptions_you_made and known_misfires.
