I debate on the national circuit, and this year most of our rounds have been about whether health care should be financed publicly or through markets. I keep running into one move, and I want to know whether it has a name before I accuse anyone of it.

Instances. (a) A market advocate says that a woman who cannot afford a needed surgery has not been denied care: she simply does not receive something, and not receiving is not the same as being deprived of it. The comparison is to a world with no system at all. (b) The same advocate says that a patient who waits four months for the same surgery on a public waiting list is being denied care; here the comparison is to what she is owed, not to the no-system world. (c) The same advocate says that a tax funding public care takes from the taxpayer, and that his receiving care in return does not make him no worse off; now the comparison is to what he would have had but for the tax.

The pattern I think I am seeing: the state of affairs against which worse off gets measured keeps moving — state of nature, current holdings, entitlement — and it always moves toward the conclusion being defended.

My partner says this is not a mistake: a tax removes what someone already has, whereas unaffordable care was never his, so the two cases genuinely call for different baselines. I cannot tell whether that is a real distinction or a rationalization.

So: is there an established concept or literature for choosing the comparison state so that it fits the conclusion, when the comparison state is itself what is in dispute? I have already been offered positive versus negative rights and doing versus allowing, and neither seems to be it: both classify kinds of act, while my worry is about which counterfactual situation counts and whether that choice can ever be non-arbitrary. If several concepts are nearby, say how mine differs from each and which one actually fits. If nothing fits, say so plainly and coin a term, with a definition and two boundary cases where it would and would not apply — and tell me whether my partner's principled version survives.
