I'm a PhD candidate in political theory, and I've been co-opted onto the working group that sets the franchise for elections to my university's Senate and Court. Under the current rules only members who are British or Irish citizens and aged eighteen or over may vote, which excludes most of our postgraduate researchers and international students on study visas, as well as everyone on the one-year foundation programme, some of whom are sixteen. Two motions are tabled: (a) lower the age threshold to sixteen; (b) replace the nationality condition with a subjection condition, so that anyone enrolled at the University and expecting to remain under its regulations for the whole of the coming academic year may vote. I also want the group to consider a mechanism of my own: newly enfranchised members would vote on the same ballot, but for the first two election cycles their ballots would be counted and published separately from the rest, merging into a single count afterwards unless separate counting shows that they would have changed who won at least one seat, in which case the extension lapses and returns to the full Senate.

Please draft the decision note I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no heading, preamble or sign-off: recommend a concrete position on both motions and on my mechanism, with reasons; then give the strongest objection to your own recommendation, stated as strongly as you can, together with why it nevertheless fails. Two members of the group argue that these are not the elections of a political community at all but the internal government of a self-governing corporation, so that the franchise is an incident of membership rather than something owed to everyone subject to the University's rules; if you think that is the strongest objection, say why it fails, and if you don't, treat whichever objection you take to be strongest instead.
