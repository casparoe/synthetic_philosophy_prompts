My friend and I spent three hours last night arguing about when the Anthropocene starts and who even gets to decide, and I came away with a pile of verdicts I cant fit together. My tentative rule was: date it to the earliest change a geologist a hundred thousand years from now could pick out of the rock with no other information — but that rule keeps giving me answers I dont like.

Here's where I land case by case. The 1610 dip in CO2, when forests regrew after the collapse of Indigenous populations in the Americas and New World pollen starts showing up in European lake mud: no, dating the age of human dominance by a moment when human impact briefly went *down* feels backwards. 1784, steam engines and coal: no, machines aren't rock, I want something a future geologist could actually see. The early-1950s plutonium spike: yes, it's sharp, global, unmistakable — but it bugs me that it's a historical accident, that if we'd never built the bomb it wouldn't be there, which feels like dating the twentieth century by the Titanic. The stuff that would show up anyway — plastic, concrete, the billions of near-identical broiler chicken skeletons — seems like the honest marker, except none of it has a sharp onset and concrete and farming both start way too early to be useful. Pinning the whole thing to one small lake in Ontario: feels arbitrary, but I know every boundary gets pinned to one site, so I don't know why it bothers me. And when the stratigraphy people voted the proposal down in 2024, I thought both that they own the timescale and that historians and climate scientists should just keep using the word anyway.

Is there one principle about what a period boundary is *for* that makes sense of all this? Give me exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, no intro and no sign-off: the repaired version of my rule, which of my verdicts it forces me to drop, what it says about dating by the cause versus by the sediment that records it, what it says about who gets to decide, and what it predicts for some start-date case I haven't listed.
