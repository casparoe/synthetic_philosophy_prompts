Last month my neighbourhood group spent a week convinced that the council had quietly sold the field behind the primary school to a developer, and that the sudden resurfacing of the lane was a cover story. It was false; I checked. What I keep turning over is what happened next. Someone posted the actual council minutes and the story did not collapse — it shed one detail and grew another, and the two people who had posted most said the minutes were exactly what you would expect if the fix were in. I have started calling this the hydra property: answer one version and a revised version appears in its place.

A doctor friend says he now thinks of such stories as infections rather than arguments: the story is a pathogen, the group chat is the vector, a correction is an immune response, and what matters is building resistance, not whether the original story was true. He draws the practical moral that you do not argue with a virus, you vaccinate against it, so engaging with the content only feeds the thing.

I would like that analogy audited. Can you set out what maps onto what — pathogen, host, vector, immunity, patient zero, mutation — and then say where the mapping breaks and what it quietly imports into the case? Two things bother me. First, germs have no truth value and claims do: whatever makes a false story catchy would make a suppressed but true one catchy in exactly the same way, so I cannot see how the picture could tell you which of the two you have got. Second, my friend's framing explains how a story spreads, but the conclusion he draws from it is about whether a story deserves an answer, and here the correction appears to have strengthened the story rather than weakened it, which is not what an immune response does. Is the hydra property really mutation, or is it something else — people protecting a position they have already staked something on?

Does the moral that one should not argue but instead build resistance survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying it? And if what needs explaining is why refutations entrench the already-committed, is there a better comparison to put in its place?
