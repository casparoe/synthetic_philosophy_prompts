i'm writing the theory-of-skill section for a curriculum proposal at my college — we're starting a practical-skills strand (knot work, wilderness first aid, that sort of thing) and the committee wants to know what we think we're actually certifying when we sign someone off. Someone suggested I write down my real position on knowing how vs knowing that first, so here it is. Please be honest about whether it holds together:

1. Knowing how is genuine knowledge, not just a dressed-up way of talking about abilities or reliable dispositions. A fluent speaker of a language knows something a phrasebook doesn't, and that difference isn't merely a difference in behaviour.

2. Knows how is standard-relative: you can know how to tie a bowline well enough to rig a dinghy without knowing how to tie one well enough to pass a rescue-knot test, and there's no single cutoff where the knowledge starts. Knowing that p has nothing comparable.

3. You can acquire knowledge-how in one shot, from testimony or a demonstration, with no practice. If a trainee watches the video once and immediately ties it correctly, she knows how to tie that knot. That's why I want our written-plus-video module to count as certification even for people who have never tied one.

4. Knowledge-how outlives the ability. The pianist with both arms in casts still knows how to play the piece, and my instructors say the same about a surgeon who has temporarily lost fine motor control after a hand injury.

5. Knowledge is factive — no knowing that p unless p is true — but knowing how isn't the sort of thing that can be correct or incorrect, so factivity just doesn't get a grip on it.

6. Knowing how rules out luck. Someone who gets it right by accident, or who couldn't tell a bowline from a granny knot, doesn't know how, whatever the outcome was.

7. Lots of non-human animals and pre-verbal kids know how to do things — grasp, track a scent, walk — even though they have no propositional attitudes about ways of doing those things.

I can't tell whether any of this is internally consistent and I'd rather find out now than in front of the committee. Go through the pairs and tell me which are genuinely in tension and which only look like it. Rank the real ones by how bad they are, and for each say what would have to give, what that would cost me elsewhere on the list, and what the cheapest fix is. If there's a single revision that cleans up more than one of them, tell me which. Also flag anything that looks fine to me but is quietly doing real work — especially if it's load-bearing for the certification policy in 3.
