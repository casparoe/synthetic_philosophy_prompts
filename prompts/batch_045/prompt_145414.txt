Not sure if this is the right place for it but someone said you lot like this kind of thing, and I've got an actual family mess that has turned into an argument nobody can settle. My grandad had a wooden sailing boat, the Morning Star, that he finished building himself in the sixties; I've sailed her every summer since I was about six and I've looked after her for the last thirty-odd years, which has meant replacing basically every plank, both masts, the deck, most of the ribs. My grandad never threw anything away, so all the original timber sat in a shed and then a barn, and my older brother Dave — who grandad fell out with years ago and barely spoke to again — spent ages reassembling those old bits into a second boat, which he finished a couple of years ago, though he had to make maybe a fifth of it new because some of the old wood was too far gone. Grandad died last year and left a small trust whose income is to be used for "the upkeep of the Morning Star", and the solicitor has told me I can split it between the two boats, but only if I can say that both of them are the Morning Star or neither of them is; if one of them is, the money has to go to that one. It's not life-changing money but it's about four grand a year and both boats need it, and separately some local maritime heritage people want to put one boat on a list of surviving wooden vessels and will only take one, and I think grandad would have cared more about that than the cash.

A few things make it messier than the simple version. First, our boat isn't a clean one-plank-at-a-time story: in 2009 she was blown onto a wall in a storm and something like forty per cent of her was replaced in a single winter by a boatyard, all in one go. Second, grandad actually did say something about this — he called Dave's project "a pile of old firewood" and in a letter to me referred to the Morning Star as "the one in the water". Third, and this is the bit I can't get out of my head, Dave's boat only existed as a finished boat two years ago, so if it is the Morning Star, did our boat stop being the Morning Star the day he finished his? Nothing happened to our boat that year. Everyone I ask has an instant answer and they're all different: my uncle says obviously the wood, because it's what grandad's hands touched; my wife says obviously ours, because it's the one that's been sailed and been in the family and the one he'd have recognised; a guy on a forum said there's a famous old version of this puzzle and then posted a link instead of actually helping.

So can you help me think it through properly? I don't want the history of the puzzle or a reading list, I want the argument.
