I chair the research-allocation committee of a biomedical funding foundation. For the last two cycles we have run our final deliberation under a procedure one of our members proposed as an implementation of Aumann's agreement theorem, and I am now unsure whether the procedure rests on the theorem or merely borrows its authority.

The procedure is this. Before any discussion, each member privately writes down a probability that the shortlisted programme will meet its primary endpoint within five years. We then circulate the numbers, ask everyone to revise in light of what they have seen, and repeat until no one wishes to move; we rank the portfolio by the settled number. The rationale offered was that Bayesian agents who begin from a common prior cannot agree to disagree, so a number on which the committee has converged is the committee's rational posterior, and a member who declines to move toward the others is thereby displaying either irrationality or bad faith. To secure the common prior we circulate an identical evidence dossier to everyone a week beforehand.

Two things have gone wrong, and I would like to know which of our background assumptions is at fault.

First, the settled number has repeatedly fallen outside the interval spanned by our initial estimates, sometimes far outside; in the last cycle our private figures were 0.30, 0.35 and 0.40, and we converged on 0.85. I had taken it to be a consequence of the theorem that the consensus must lie among the starting points, and two members now treat the overshoot as proof that the procedure is broken. Is the theorem really in the business of constraining where the consensus falls relative to the individual starting points, or does it say something weaker?

Second, a senior member has stopped revising at all. Her stated reason is that the common-prior assumption was never true of us: we come from different fields, we read the same dossier through different theoretical commitments, and there is no fact of the matter about our shared prior over the states. On that basis she says the theorem has nothing to say about this committee and that we are invoking it to discipline dissent. Does sharing a dossier secure a common prior, or does the antecedent of the theorem fail here in a way that makes our talk of the rational posterior empty?

I would also like to know whether it matters that we exchange numbers only and never the reasoning behind them, whether the settled figure is the best estimate available to the panel or only a negotiated truce, and how the procedure should be redesigned if what we actually want to act on is the best estimate the panel's pooled evidence supports.
