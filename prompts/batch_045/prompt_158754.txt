I am copy-editing an unpublished manuscript on the rationality of flat-out assertion, and I need to anticipate how the author will rule on a case that appears in her final chapter, which she describes but declines to decide. Her earlier rulings are these. First: in a million-ticket lottery with a single winner, drawn tomorrow, where you hold one ticket and have no evidence bearing on which ticket it will be, you may not flat-out assert that your ticket will lose. Second: the same lottery, except that the draw took place an hour ago and the winning number now sits in a sealed envelope that nobody has read — still no. Third: a historian's monograph contains five hundred separately researched claims, each something like 0.99 probable, and in her preface she concedes that probably one of them is false; she may flat-out assert each claim in the body of the book, but she may not assert that all five hundred are true.

The undecided case is this. An investigative journalist's exposé rests on four hundred claims. For three hundred and ninety-nine of them she has two independent sources. Claim 211 rests on a single source, and a trustworthy audit of her own previous work shows that single-source claims of this kind turn out false about one time in fifty, independently of anything else she can tell about them. Claim 211 is thus the one claim she can name in advance as the likeliest to be wrong; the exposé largely loses its point if she withholds or hedges it, and being wrong about it would burn a source and cause moderate, non-catastrophic harm. May she assert claim 211 flat-out?

I would like four things: your prediction of her verdict; a statement of the two or three rules about assertion that would generate all three of her earlier rulings, with an account of what you take to be doing the work in the lottery cases given that the probability there is far higher than in the preface; what each of those rules predicts about claim 211 and which you think she is actually running; and the further case that would best separate the rules that agree on 211, together with what each would say about it.
