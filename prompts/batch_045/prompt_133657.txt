I spend my professional life pricing low-probability, high-consequence risks: tail events in insurance portfolios, catastrophic infrastructure failure, the sort of exposure whose expected loss is dominated by scenarios whose probability nobody can honestly pin down. The vocabulary I actually reason in is that of probability distributions over severities; parameter uncertainty as against structural or model uncertainty; expected loss and exceedance curves; the value of information; sensitivity analysis and scenario trees; heavy tails and the fanaticism problem; and the choice among decision rules for acting when the model itself is in doubt, whether expected-utility maximization, robust satisficing, or minimax regret. Assume I am fluent in all of it and do not explain it back to me.

What I want translated into that vocabulary is the scale argument about insect sentience. As I understand it, the argument runs roughly like this: insects are vastly more numerous than the animals our institutions in fact worry about, and you should simply take it as given that their headcount exceeds that of farmed vertebrates by many orders of magnitude; the probability that they are sentient at all is uncertain and may well be modest; but because the population is so enormous, the expected quantity of welfare at stake in anything that moves insect numbers or insect lives — pesticide regulation, land-use policy, agricultural practice, deliberate population management — is correspondingly vast, large enough that it may dominate every vertebrate-welfare intervention we could fund instead.

Restate that argument for me as carefully and as faithfully as you can in my terms, and be explicit about where you have to cheat in order to do it. Give me the mapping: what plays the role of the loss variable, and what are its units; how is the chance of insect sentience best represented, as a parameter inside a single model or as uncertainty across a family of models that disagree about more than a parameter; what does the severity distribution look like in its tail; what is the expected value of further evidence, say from work on nociception, anesthesia, and behavioral response to noxious stimuli, and how much of the argument's force survives if that evidence arrives ambiguous, as I suspect it will; and what does each of my decision rules say about the concrete case I care about, a funder or regulator choosing between a well-understood intervention on behalf of vertebrates and an insect-directed one whose very sign is uncertain, since it is unclear whether reducing insect numbers reduces suffering or causes it.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually want. My suspicion is that the translation will be cleanest exactly where the argument is weakest, and I would like you to push past my suspicions rather than confirm them. The places where I expect trouble are these. First, sentience looks threshold-like, or at least has a threshold-like structure, whereas my framework would prefer a continuous capacity parameter that can be integrated over. Second, and more seriously, in the models where insects are not sentient the welfare loss is not small but zero, and there are not even any individuals there to be affected; so the expectation is being taken over hypotheses that disagree about who or what the welfare subjects are, not over a fixed population with an uncertain property, and I do not know what my vocabulary is supposed to do with a value function whose domain of patients changes across the very hypotheses it is averaging over. Third, my framework has no way to distinguish being wrong about a fact from being wrong about a value, and no place for the thought that a harm has to be a harm to someone. Fourth, I cannot see how to stop the calculation from treating insect sentience exactly like the Pascal-mugging style of case, the one where a negligible probability of an astronomically large payoff seems to obligate expected-value reasoning to chase it, even though the two cases feel entirely different to me and I would like to know whether that difference can be stated in my terms or only outside them.

For each of those four: say whether it translates cleanly, translates only with distortion, or has no expression in my vocabulary at all. Then give me your considered view on what the residue signifies. Is what is left over a defect of risk-analytic vocabulary, which was built for uncertainty about magnitudes and is simply missing the concepts of moral status and of a population of patients constituted by the hypothesis itself? Or is it evidence that the scale argument is less clear than it sounds, that its central quantities, the probability of insect sentience and the expected welfare at stake, are doing a great deal of work the argument never makes explicit, and that some of what presents itself as a conclusion about expected value is really a claim about which uncertainties get admitted into the calculation and how the averaging is set up? If it is both, tell me how you would apportion the blame, and name the single clarification or piece of evidence that would most reduce it.
