I keep noticing a pattern in how I reason and I don't have a word for it, and I'm not even sure its the same thing as the two famous cases people always bring up.

Concretely: I've got a working list of maybe sixty independent claims I've each checked with some care — historical dates, who said what in a meeting, whether a given package still supports a library, that sort of thing. Any single one I'd put at maybe 97%. If you ask me about one of them I'll just assert it, flat out, and in the same breath I'll say sure, probably one or two things on that list are wrong. I feel no tension between those two at all, and making the list longer doesn't make me any less comfortable per claim.

But something does happen. It shows up in what I'll do, not in what I believe. If I need to lean on five of them together — write a page that needs all five to be right — I suddenly want to go re-check those five, even though nothing about any individual one has changed and I'd have used any one of them alone without a second thought. And it's weirdly selective: I re-check the five this particular project needs and leave the other fifty-five alone, then re-check a completely different five tomorrow for a different project. I'm never thinking about the conjunction of the whole list, and I never feel any actual doubt about a member of it. It's more like my willingness to act has become sensitive to which subset you pick on, while my confidence hasn't moved at all.

I've read a little about the lottery case, and about the thing where authors believe every sentence in their book while also believing the book contains a mistake. This feels adjacent but not identical: in those setups the question is always whether you believe the conjunction, or whether you get to keep all the individual beliefs, and nobody seems to care that the trouble might come out in action rather than belief, or that the subsetting is where the work is being done.

So: does this have a name of its own, or is there literature on the subset-sensitivity part specifically? If the nearest established concepts are the ones I half-remember, I want to know exactly how what I'm describing differs from each of them and which one really fits; and if nothing fits, say so and give me a term, a definition, and some cases that are and aren't instances of it. I'm trying to work out what the thing is, not what to call it.
