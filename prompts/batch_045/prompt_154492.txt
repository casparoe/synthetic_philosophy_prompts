My friends and I keep arguing about this after work — a few of us are at a small AI lab, and our group chat has basically turned into a permanent debate about why everyone keeps cutting corners on safety. I've drifted into holding a position I'd put like this: what drives races to the bottom isn't that participants have bad values or short time horizons, it's that the population of participants is itself selected over time. Even if everyone in the system is patient and public-spirited, the actors who end up surviving, growing, and setting the terms for everyone else are the ones who defected earlier and harder, so the aggregate slides toward the bad equilibrium without anyone ever having chosen it, and without anyone's character needing to change. Call it the composition-drift version of the Moloch story: the mechanism is differential survival and expansion of actors, not the content of anyone's preferences. I like it because it survives the obvious objection that plenty of people inside these races clearly hate what they're doing.
