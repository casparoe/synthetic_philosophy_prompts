I am a journalist covering science and ideas, and I have been asked to give a twenty-five-minute briefing at my own paper to a mixed room: reporters and editors on one side, audience-analytics and product staff on the other. The occasion is a live decision about whether editorial bonuses should track pageviews and time on page, so the room is numerate, sceptical, already knows the slogan about a measure becoming a target, and is inclined to file the whole subject under people respond to incentives.

Do not explain the idea to me; tell me how to explain it in that room. I want the order the pieces must come in and what depends on what; the one case that should carry the twenty-five minutes, including why it should or should not be a case in which nobody intended to cheat; and the misconceptions I should expect, in the order I should expect them, with the point in the sequence where each is best headed off. I can predict three: that this is really a story about bad incentives rather than about the limits of a proxy, that it is the same complaint as overfitting, and that it licenses the conclusion that measurement is pointless.

Two things I picked up while reporting seem to me to be doing real work, and I want them placed rather than merely dropped in. The first is that an apparent Goodhart failure can be several different failures: the proxy held only across the range of cases you happened to observe; you are now pushing it into the extreme range where it was never checked; acting on the proxy breaks the causal link between proxy and target; or an interested party is deliberately adapting against the metric. These have different diagnoses and different remedies. The second is that the worst cases need no one to want to cheat, so the word gaming obscures as much as it explains.

Close with the one question or exercise that would show me whether the room can take a proposed new metric, say which failure to watch for first, and name what would count as early evidence of it. And tell me what I should openly refuse to cover in twenty-five minutes.
