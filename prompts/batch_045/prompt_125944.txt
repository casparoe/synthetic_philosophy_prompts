I'm TAing a formal epistemology seminar this term, and we spent most of a session on the Judy Benjamin problem without converging on anything, so I've rewritten the puzzle with my own numbers in order to stop us from reading the answer off the handout. Judy is dropped by parachute into a region divided into four cells: Red (hostile) or Blue (friendly) territory, and within each territory a Headquarters Company area or a Second Company area. Her briefing map leaves her with priors of 0.30 on Red-and-Headquarters, 0.10 on Red-and-Second-Company, 0.20 on Blue-and-Headquarters, and 0.40 on Blue-and-Second-Company — so her prior on being in Red at all is 0.40, and her prior on Second-Company-given-Red is 0.25. The radio message then comes through, and I'll paraphrase it rather than quote it: the operator cannot say where she is, but he tells her that if she is in Red territory, she is probably in Second Company area. I want "probably" read as pinning that conditional to exactly 3/4.

Three ways of handling that message seem to be on the table. First, the message is not evidence about a worldly event at all but a constraint on her posterior: she should adopt, among all distributions over the four cells satisfying Second-Company-given-Red = 3/4, the one closest to her prior, with closeness measured by relative entropy. I worked this out and got roughly 0.28 for Red, i.e., learning the conditional makes enemy territory less likely, which is the opposite of what I expected. Second, the message tells her how to redistribute credence within the Red cells and nowhere else, so credence flows from Red-and-Headquarters to Red-and-Second-Company while the probability of Red stays put at 0.40. Third, the message is really a report about a frequency in the world, produced under some protocol — say the operator sends that sentence just in case the actual share of Red territory occupied by Second Company exceeds 3/4 — in which case the update is ordinary conditioning on the sentence's having been sent, and everything turns on how he would have chosen between that sentence and its Blue-territory counterpart.

My credence that Judy's probability of being in Red ought to end up strictly below 0.40 — that the first kind of answer gets the direction right, with "ought" cashed out as whatever the correct theory of updating would prescribe for a fully rational agent here — is about 0.55; a friend in the department puts it at 0.2. Give me your own number, but more importantly give me the decomposition: how much weight you put on there being a unique rationally mandated posterior here rather than the evidence merely narrowing things to a family of permissible ones; on the message being better modeled as a constraint on the posterior than as evidence about some proposition already in Judy's algebra; on picking among the compatible distributions by minimizing distance from the prior at all; on relative entropy specifically being the right distance, conditional on some distance being right; on the reporting protocol — whether the operator could have sent a different sentence, and how — being part of what Judy learns and mattering in this case; and on "probably" fixing the conditional at 3/4 rather than at a loose threshold. Give each a rough number and one line of justification, say how you are combining them, tell me which term your final figure is most sensitive to and what consideration would move it furthest, and then tell me where you suspect my 0.55 and my friend's 0.2 actually disagree. Please also redo the 0.28 arithmetic: if the relative-entropy calculation does not in fact push Red below 0.40 for these priors, that changes what the question is. And don't stop at "it depends which update rule you accept" — that is exactly what I want taken apart.
