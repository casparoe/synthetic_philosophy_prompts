The paragraph summarizes Skolem's paradox roughly as follows:

"Set theory proves that the real numbers are uncountable: there are strictly more of them than there are natural numbers. Yet the Löwenheim–Skolem theorem shows that if ZFC is consistent, then it has a model whose entire domain can be arranged in a single infinite list — a countable model. Inside that universe of sets, every set, the set of reals included, is countable when seen from the outside. The contradiction is only apparent. 'Uncountable' just means 'no bijection with the natural numbers exists anywhere inside the model'; the model lacks the functions that would pair its reals off with its naturals, and so it mistakenly reckons its own reals uncountable. Skolem drew the moral himself: notions like uncountability are irreducibly relative, and there is no absolute fact of the matter about how many real numbers there are. This is the same phenomenon that set theorists exploit when they use forcing to change the size of the continuum, and it is why the continuum hypothesis can be neither proved nor refuted. The culprit throughout is first-order logic. If set theory were written in second-order logic, so that the quantifiers range over absolutely all subsets, its models would be pinned down once and for all and the paradox would disappear."
