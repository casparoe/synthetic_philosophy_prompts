im in the PhD micro sequence but also sitting in on a philosophy of econ seminar this term, and the "what even is utility" fight is losing me. My actual working vocabulary is discrete choice / random utility stuff — latent index, disturbances, scale and location normalizations, whats identified and what isnt, taste heterogeneity vs scale heterogeneity, mixed logit — that I'm fine with. The seminar I'm lost in.

So: translate the philosophy into RUM terms, as faithfully as you can, and don't quietly smuggle my assumptions in and call it translation. Two things to translate, separately. (a) The revealed-preference reading: utility is nothing but a numerical representation of choice behaviour, all the content is in the consistency axioms (the strong axiom and its nonparametric generalizations), and talking about what utility "really is" is confused. (b) The readings that say utility is supposed to be something substantive — wellbeing, or a mental state you could in principle be wrong about, or the gap between what people choose and what they later report experiencing.

Then the residue report, which is what I actually want, and here's my half-baked hunch to push on or kill: I think the whole fight is a fight about what's allowed to live in the error term. Put unobserved taste variation in there and utility is still "the person's preferences." Put akrasia, or inattention, or the agent optimizing the wrong objective in there, and utility stops being about the agent at all and starts being a summary of my dataset. And interpersonal comparability of utility looks to me like exactly the scale-heterogeneity problem: two people's latent indices aren't on the same scale, so subtracting them is meaningless, and no amount of data fixes that.

So: what translates cleanly, what only translates with distortion, and what has no expression at all in my vocabulary? Is "utility is wellbeing" secretly a claim about a normalization I can't identify, or is it saying something RUM literally cannot say? And is the leftover a blind spot in how economists model choice, or a sign the philosophers are fighting over nothing? Under 600 words, be blunt where my analogy breaks.
