I'm a philosophy professor and I teach a grad seminar on naturalizing intentionality about every second year — mostly philosophy of mind students, plus one or two people from psych — and there's a point around week six where the seminar just stops dead. It's the same fight every time. The teleological side: content gets fixed by what a mechanism, or the mechanism downstream of it, was selected to do. The causal-informational side: content gets fixed by what a state actually carries information about, or by its actual nomic-covariation profile. Everyone on both sides knows everyone else's moves cold, and reciting them again changes nobody's mind. The misrepresentation argument against the information theories. The chance-duplicate case and the "which function exactly?" indeterminacy argument against the teleo theories. The reply that you defuse indeterminacy by letting the consumer mechanism, or the selection history of the producing mechanism, do the individuating work. The counter-reply that this only pushes the same indeterminacy up one level. Same exchange, forty-odd years, nobody's position shifts by a degree. I want a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right.

Here's a case I've started using to try to force it, because everyone in the room agrees on every empirical fact in it and still disagrees. A prey species has an alarm-response mechanism that was selected long ago for firing on a chemical cue from predator species A. The population gets translocated to a lake where A is absent; in the new lake the mechanism is triggered almost always by a chemically similar but harmless compound from some other organism, and only rarely by anything dangerous. The response is cheap and occasionally life-saving, so nothing deselects it. Our psych people could hand you the full causal-nomological profile of the state, the mechanism, and the downstream consumer by tomorrow. Question: does that state now mean predator-A, or predator, or predator-ish-chemical, or nothing determinate at all — and does it misrepresent when it fires on the harmless compound? The teleo people say something like "predator, and yes, it misrepresents." The information people say something like "whatever it in fact covaries with now," or "indeterminate among all of those." And then they argue about whether my case is even data, which is the part I find most interesting.

My hypothesis, offered strictly as one: this isn't a disagreement about which natural relation constitutes content. It's a disagreement about what a theory of content is supposed to *be* — a classificatory device for a projectable cognitive science, so it has to be fixed by facts discoverable from a creature's actual physical profile and has to license generalizations across individuals; or an interpretive device, so it has to yield the content that makes the creature's behavior intelligible and can be wrong. Each side then treats the other side's central cases as beside the point. But I've been wrong about my own seminars before, and I genuinely can't tell whether that's a real distinction or just a fancy redescription of the standoff.

So: is this verbal, substantive-but-deep, two questions sharing a name, or ill-posed? Is there a hidden empirical question doing the work — about how neural states get individuated in actual working neuroscience, or about how much mechanism-variance there really is across conspecifics — that we could in principle settle? Or is it bedrock, a difference about whether misrepresentation has to be accommodated at the ground floor that neither side can argue for? Also fold in the third voice: my psych colleagues think the whole debate is empirically empty. Engage what each side actually says rather than a parody of it, and then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a distinction, a formal result, an empirical finding, a change of question. Be honest if the honest answer is "nothing, dissolve it."

One more thing, since I'm building a lecture out of whatever you send: separate what is genuinely settled among people who actually work on this from what is contested, and don't hand me my own side's self-image as the consensus. Flag the spots where your diagnosis rests on a reading of one of the positions that a defender of that position would reject — I'd rather know that the diagnosis is itself one more contested move inside the same fight than get a clean story that collapses after a week of reading.
