Construct a fully specified prediction scenario — a learner, a box that emits bits, and a scoring rule — built to settle whether the no-free-lunch case against induction (all learners tie in expected score once you average over every possible environment) and Reichenbach's pragmatic vindication of the straight rule (predict in line with the observed relative frequency) are genuinely in conflict, or only appear to be because they answer different questions.

Requirements:

1. An exact no-free-lunch equality: the environment class contains every binary sequence of the relevant length, the learner has no side information and no oracle, and so every learner — the straight rule included — scores exactly chance in expectation on every trial. Prove this for your setup.
2. A vindication that nonetheless singles out one learner: not an appeal to the uniformity or simplicity of nature, not a simplicity or computability prior, but a claim of the form "this rule succeeds on every environment on which success of the relevant kind is available at all". Name a rival that a reasonable person might adopt, which ties the vindicated rule under (1), and show why the vindication rules it out.
3. Explicit compatibility: state precisely what shifts between (1) and (2) — which quantity is being optimized, or which notion of success is in play — and show that the vindication therefore buys nothing at all under the assessment in (1).

Constraints: finite, or asymptotic if you prefer, but say which; no oracles, no side channel, no probability over environments other than the uniform one over sequences; the learner must be something a person could execute by hand; the vindication must not be a disguised uniformity assumption.

Close by naming the single feature of the case that is doing the work, by saying what happens to the argument if that feature is removed, and by giving the strongest attack available to someone who reads the no-free-lunch theorems as vindicating Humean skepticism about induction — including the charge that the vindication is circular.
