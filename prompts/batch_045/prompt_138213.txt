Me and a couple of people from my climbing gym have this weird book club thing and somebody brought in that idea I've seen floating around that consciousness is a "user illusion" — the claim being that what's in awareness is like a desktop GUI sitting on top of a mountain of machinery you never actually see. I work in infra, so the metaphor lands a little too well for me, and I think I've pushed it somewhere it wasn't meant to go, and now I can't tell whether I've found a real problem or just argued myself into a corner over a bad analogy.

Here's where I keep ending up. There are two different things a UI can do. A folder icon isn't literally a manila folder, but it isn't lying to you either — it's a workable stand-in. A progress bar that sits at 99% for four minutes is different: it's actively misrepresenting the state of the system. And "illusion" normally means the second kind of thing — your'e getting something wrong about how things really are, like a stick in water looking bent. So which one is the consciousness claim? If it's the folder-icon kind, the view looks trivial to me: of course my experience of "I decided to name this variable that" isn't the full causal story of what my brain did, nobody ever thought it was, and nothing follows about whether consciousness really exists or not. If it's the progress-bar kind, then my experience is getting something wrong, and getting something wrong needs a fact of the matter to be wrong about — some way my conscious life really is, underneath the dashboard — which is exactly the intrinsic, ineffable, what-it's-like-ish stuff the illusionist crowd says isn't there at all. So the whole position looks like it's either boring or it eats itself. And then there's the bit that actually bugs me: who's the user? A GUI has somebody sitting in front of it. If I say the user is the brain, or some subsystem of the brain, I've smuggled a little person back inside the machine, and now that subsystem needs its own screen, and I'm off to the races in a regress. My friend who does interpretability stuff says I'm committing a category error and should stop.

Can you tell me which specific step is broken? I want the actual repair, not just "it's only a metaphor" — I get that, but I want to know what the view would have to be claiming in order to be neither trivial nor self-undermining, and what happens to the "who is the user" worry once you say it properly. One more thing that might be the crux, or might be me sneaking the conclusion in: I keep wanting to say that a dashboard that reports "I am in pain" can't be wrong about itself in the way a temperature readout can be wrong about the engine, so if the view needs pain-experience to be a misrepresentation of something, it needs a hidden real pain-nature behind the readout, and that's the hard problem sneaking back in the side door. Is that the mistake?
