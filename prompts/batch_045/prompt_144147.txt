I've been reading a pile of history blogs and arguing with my buddy about whether the US is Rome, and it's turned into this thing where I keep asking myself when it's actually fair to guess from how things usually go, versus when you owe it to the case to look at the details. Here's where I land on a bunch of them, and I want to know if there's any one rule behind it.

- New restaurant opening on my street: totally fair to say it'll probably fold within a few years, just going on how often that happens. I don't need to see the menu.
- My own kitchen reno: fair to tell me it'll take twice as long as I think, based on other people's renos. I accept this one, gratefully.
- My buddy's startup, where I know both founders and have seen their numbers: saying "most startups fail, so yours will too" feels like a jerk move and also like bad reasoning. But I can't tell you how it's different from the restaurant.
- A merger I've actually read the filings on: "most mergers destroy value" feels useless here too, same way.
- Rome and America: I want to throw this out entirely. Ancient empire, totally different world, feels like a parlor game.
- Athens and Sparta, US and China: this one I find genuinely useful and kind of chilling. But it's the same move, isn't it? Old thing, modern thing.
- "Sea-based global trading empires" (Britain and a couple of others): feels like a legit group to reason from.
- "The one other time a reigning superpower faced a rising one and both had nukes" (that's just the Cold War, there's nothing else in it): feels fake. Like an analogy with a number taped to it.

The rule I keep half-believing is: a reference class is fair to use if you'd have picked it before you knew how things turned out, and there's more than a couple of cases in it. Test that against my list and tell me where it breaks, and be honest about which of my verdicts a cleaner rule would force me to give up. Also tell me what it says about the one I'm stuck on: guessing how much longer the US has by looking only at empires that had already made it to 250 years. Is that the right class, or am I just picking the survivors?
