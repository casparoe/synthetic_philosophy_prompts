I'm writing the specification before I buy into any particular account. What I want a theory of the self for is three jobs: to underwrite diachronic attributions of responsibility, including the kind courts make; to leave room for self-knowledge and self-deception, that is, for being wrong about who one is; and to say what is lost, and to whom, when a person is lost. Please write the desiderata list for a theory with that job: which phenomena it must explain, which cases it must get right, which distinctions it must not collapse (self versus self-conception, unity at a time versus over time, having a self versus being a responsible agent, and whatever else belongs), with the case or consideration behind each requirement. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable given those three jobs and which are preferences of a camp that could be surrendered without giving up the project.

Three cases the list has to handle, plus whatever cases you would add: (1) The ghostwritten self. From early childhood a caregiver supplies a child with a detailed and largely false story about her own past — what she did, what was done to her, what she is like — which she has no independent access to and which she goes on to inhabit, act on, and retell as an adult with complete conviction. At thirty she learns that nearly all of it was fabricated. Has her self changed, was there never a fact of the matter, and if the false narrative is not constitutive of her self, what privileges the true one? (2) The two ledgers. Someone has lived for decades two internally coherent lives with two organizing stories, each fully accessible to the other but never integrated — no memory loss, no discontinuity of experience, just no bridging narrative. One self or two, and by what criterion? (3) The non-narrators. Plausible candidates for having a self who apparently tell no story at all: a bright two-year-old, a dog, someone with dense amnesia who retains skill and moment-to-moment affect.

Two things I want most from the list. First, whether some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable: my suspicion is that (a) the self's being an abstraction rather than an additional object, (b) a narrative's being constitutive of it rather than merely evidence of it, and (c) its being possible to be wrong about oneself cannot all three be held, and I want to know which pairs survive contact and what each costs. Second, which single requirement, enforced strictly, excludes the most otherwise credible candidates. Add, if it is cheap, one line each on how three views do against the requirements you weight most heavily: the view that selves are constituted by self-narrative, the view that they consist in overlapping chains of psychological continuity, and the view that a person is nothing over and above a living human organism. Nothing more than that; the list is the deliverable.

Where you are unsure, say so. Tag each requirement as one you take to be genuinely forced by the three jobs or one you would defend only tentatively, and tell me if any of the cases above is doing less work than it looks like it is. And if the specification project is itself misconceived — if there is no single thing those three jobs are all jobs for, or if the center-of-narrative-gravity metaphor collapses either into vacuity or back into a hidden entity — say that plainly rather than producing a compliant list.
