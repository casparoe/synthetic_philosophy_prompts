I'm writing up my position on Skolem's paradox for a graduate seminar in philosophy of mathematics, and before I defend it I want an audit of whether the package actually hangs together. Here is where I have landed, stated as flatly as I can.

(1) Uniqueness. There is a single intended cumulative hierarchy, V. Set-theoretic vocabulary as we use it is not ambiguous between many equally intended interpretations, and the continuum hypothesis has a determinate truth value whether or not we ever find out which.

(2) The theorem has real bite. The downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem (any first-order theory with an infinite model has a countable model) applies to first-order ZFC; a countable model M really does satisfy the sentence asserting that the reals are uncountable; and this is a substantive discovery about the expressive limits of first-order formalization, not a trick of notation.

(3) The paradox is dissolved, not solved. "M is countable" is an external claim; the bijection that would witness countability is not an element of M; so (2) conflicts with (1) not at all. I use one general manoeuvre throughout, which I call the standpoint rule: claims about the size or width of a domain can only be assessed from a standpoint strictly outside that domain. I intend to apply it uniformly.

(4) The methodological moral is second-order. Because of (2), set theory should be formulated in second-order logic with full semantics. ZFC₂ is quasi-categorical — its full models are, up to isomorphism, the rank initial segments V_κ for inaccessible κ, so any two are isomorphic or one sits as a rank-initial segment of the other — and I treat that as a genuine epistemic gain over the first-order theory. The residual worry, that the full powerset of the domain is itself an external notion fixed only from some standpoint, I handle with (3). I take the first-order and second-order cases to be relevantly alike here.

(5) The gain in (4) is vertical, and I need it to be. The analogue of (2) for well-foundedness — no first-order theory with an infinite model can rule out a nonstandard descending ∈-chain, by compactness applied to a sequence of constants — is repaired by full second-order semantics, which does pin down that ∈ is well-founded. I mark the contrast by calling height questions vertical and width questions horizontal (width being: which subsets of a given stage exist). My claim is that second-order resources give us vertical grip.

(6) Anti-Putnam for the right reason. The model-theoretic argument against metaphysical realism fails, but not because our theory, or our operational and theoretical constraints, singles out a unique intended interpretation — plainly they do not. It fails because fixing a reference does not require a description that singles out its referent.

(7) Determinacy of width rests on a horizontal maxim. What makes the continuum hypothesis determinate is that at each stage all subsets of earlier stages exist; the powerset operation is not up for grabs. I want (4) to be the formal vehicle for that maxim, and I want the vertical success in (5) to be evidence that the second-order framework is tracking something real rather than merely convenient.

(8) There is no outside for us. The hypothesis that our own quantifiers range over a countable domain is not something we are in a position to assert, deny, or assign a probability to, since there is no standpoint outside our own quantifiers from which it could be assessed.

What I want from you is the audit, not a survey. Which of these commitments are in genuine tension, which only look as though they are, and for each genuine tension, which commitment has to give and what the cheapest repair costs me elsewhere in the set. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally serious; I would rather have one real problem identified precisely than eight hedged warnings. In particular I would like you to check whether the standpoint rule, applied with the uniformity I claim for it in (3), is compatible with (4); whether (8) quietly deflates the bite I claim in (2) and therefore the premise of (4); and whether (7) gets any grip at all from a framework whose grip is vertical in the sense of (5).

Please do not give me a reading list or tell me whom to read — I have the literature in front of me and I want you to work from the commitments as I have stated them. If a technical claim is load-bearing in your answer and you are not certain of it, say so explicitly rather than asserting it.
