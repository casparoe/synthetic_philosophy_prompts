Write me a joke about Chesterton's fence — the reformer who wants to tear down a fence he can't explain the purpose of. It should be actually funny to someone who knows the thing, i.e. the punchline has to turn on the real logic (the Burkean suspicion of inherited arrangements, the asymmetry between whoever wants to change something and whoever wants to leave it be) rather than just being a libs-vs-cons gag with a fence in it.

Here's the wrinkle I want it built on: call it the groundskeeper problem. The one person who can tell you why the fence is there is usually also the person whose job, status, or budget depends on the fence staying — so the testimony you'd need in order to apply the principle is exactly the testimony you have least reason to trust. Can you get a joke out of that, and if it doesn't work, say why it doesn't.
