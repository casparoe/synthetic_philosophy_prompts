I do a bit of volunteering for a food co-op and I also knock on doors at election time, which means I spend a silly amount of time arguing with people online about whether one person's bit makes any difference. I've noticed a move that keeps coming up and I don't have a word for it.

The setup is always the same. Someone says the "it makes no difference what I do" thing — my one vote won't decide anything, my one pack of chicken won't change how many birds get killed, my one car won't change the weather. And the reply that always shows up is: sure, not to *that*. You're measuring the outcome at the wrong zoom level. My vote doesn't change who wins, but it changes the margin, and the margin matters — there are recount triggers, parties get public money or ballot access off a percentage threshold, and a candidate who got slaughtered behaves differently next time. My chicken doesn't change today's order, because the supplier ships whole crates and my pack just gets absorbed into the slack — but it changes where the shelf sits at the end of the week, and a few weeks later a crate that would have been ordered on Thursday gets ordered on Friday, so at the level of "birds killed this year" there's a difference, just not at the level of "birds killed because of me". Same with driving: no storm is any different, but the odds of one shift by some fraction of a fraction of a percent.

Two things bug me.

First, the move looks like it's available in both directions without limit. Zoom in far enough and literally anything makes a difference — the exact bytes in the supplier's database, the path of one molecule. Zoom out far enough and nothing does. So "you're looking at the wrong resolution" definately can't be all there is to it. Something has to pick the right resolution, and I want to know what, and whether it can be said without just begging the question against the person who started with "it makes no difference".

Second, I think there are two different moves hiding under "wrong zoom level" and I can't cleanly seperate them. One is describing the same outcome more or less finely: who wins versus by how much. The other is walking further along the chain: who wins, then what policy, then who gets hired, then what that person does with the job. And the second one can come apart from the first in the opposite direction, which is the bit that actually confuses me: I break a tie, so at the level of "who wins" my vote was the whole story, and at the level of "what happens afterwards" it made no difference at all, because both candidates would have done the same thing. So the fine/coarse axis and the sooner/later axis aren't the same axis, and I don't know how they interact.

So: does this have a name? Either the zoom move itself, or the "you're measuring the outcome at the wrong resolution" reply to the no-difference argument. If there's no established term for it, give me one, with a definition and a couple of boundary cases, so I can tell in a fresh argument whether someone is doing it or not.

Three things I specifically don't want it collapsed into, because I've seen all three offered and none of them is what I mean: the "you can't know you aren't the one who tips it" reply (that's about uncertainty, mine is about what you're counting); the "what if everyone thought like that" reply (that's about generalising, and mine is supposed to work even if nobody else changes); and the "you're complicit, you're part of a group doing this" reply (that's about membership, and mine is supposed to work for someone acting entirely alone).

And one boundary case I want handled: sometimes the only difference I make at the fine level is one nobody has any reason to care about — which particular bird dies instead of which other one, which byte ends up in which file. Does the move still go through there, or does that kill it? A good answer should let me say what the thing is, what it isn't, and where it stops working, not just hand me a label.
