I edit the fortnightly newsletter of a professional association for psychiatric classification — a readership that is roughly two-thirds clinicians who have sat on diagnostic criteria committees and one-third philosophers and sociologists of medicine who work on human kinds — and I want a satirical centrepiece for the issue that goes out just after the next revision cycle. The form I have in mind is the release notes, or changelog, for a point release of a fictional diagnostic manual (call it whatever you like), written in the voice of the committee that maintains it, treating the fact that its classifications are moving targets not as a scandal but as an ordinary engineering problem the committee has quietly built tooling around. Aim for 700–900 words, laid out as versioned sections — Added, Changed, Deprecated, Known Issues, Migration Notes — with short numbered entries and occasional remarks attributed to invented officers of the committee, and with at least one invented disorder name and one invented institutional unit (a working party, an office, a ticket number) that the humour depends on.

The substance I want smuggled in, always as procedure and never as thesis, is the behaviour of classifications of people as against classifications of things: that the people classified read the criteria and change, that the criteria are then revised to track what they have become, and that the manual's own vocabulary for what it is doing — validity, discovery, natural kind, underlying mechanism — sits oddly with the fact that its kinds move partly because it named them. So, please include: an entry for a newly added disorder whose onset criterion requires that the symptoms have appeared only after the patient encountered the published criteria, together with whatever the committee has had to do about patients who present with the disorder before the entry is unsealed; a deprecation notice for an older disorder retired because widespread self-diagnosis destroyed its discriminative power, with a migration path, a note on patients grandfathered in under the old code, and something about the reimbursement and insurance categories that outlive the kind they were built for; a Changed entry describing a feature I have invented for the prompt, call it pre-adaptation or forecast criteria, in which the committee now drafts entries so as to anticipate how the people described will re-form themselves once they read them, and the joke should come from the fact that anticipating the loop is itself part of the loop; and a Known Issues entry about why the usual validation work cannot be run on a kind that shifts under measurement, in which the contrast with classifying a particle or a plant specimen is let slip through an example rather than drawn as a moral. If you can work in a contested entry — patients or advocacy groups arguing with the committee about the name or the boundary, a voting record on a rejected criterion — so much the better, since contestation over the classification is one of the ways these kinds move.

Keep the voice deadpan and inside the position: the committee should be competent, well-meaning and sincere, never winking at the reader and never explaining what it is doing philosophically; a reader who knows the literature should feel the piece is fair to it, and a reader who does not should still find it funny and be able to tell what is being sent up. Please write in British English, do not name, quote or cite any real author or work, and include no footnotes, references or suggested reading — invented attributions only. Close the piece with a single final line beginning Summary: that states, in one sentence, what this changelog is satirising.
