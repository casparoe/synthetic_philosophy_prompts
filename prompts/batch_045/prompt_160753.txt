Hi — I sit on the clinical ethics committee at my NHS trust and we've got to sign off (or bin) a draft ICU surge protocol by Thursday. Two versions are on the table and, as far as anyone can model them, the same patients die in the same order.

Version A: everyone gets scored by the triage tool every 48 hours; anyone below the threshold goes to the ward round and a consultant has to actively authorise withdrawal of ventilation.

Version B: same tool, same threshhold, but withdrawal happens automatically at the 48-hour mark unless a consultant actively overrides it. In last winter's audit only about one in twelve patients got an override, and half of those were just a 24-hour extension.

Half the committee says B is just A with the paperwork running the other way, and that what we're really deciding is who dies, not who does what — we wrote both policies, after all, and we know in advance which patients each one will kill. The other half says B is a machine that kills on a timer, that a human who could have intervened doesn't turn a death we cause into a death we merely allow, and that being free to press a button isn't the same as doing nothing.

Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate: recommend A, B, or B with conditions, bottom line in the first two sentences, under 500 words total. Take the losing side's strongest argument seriously rather than just restating the law on withholding and withdrawing — and if you think the doing/allowing distinction can't actually carry the weight our debate is putting on it, say that plainly and tell us what we should decide instead.
