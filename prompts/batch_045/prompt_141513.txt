One student (anonymous, so no names) wrote this:

Teleosemantics (or biosemantics) tries to naturalise content by grounding it in biological function instead of in causation. A state has the content p not because it is caused by p, and not because of what it resembles, but because the mechanism that produces it has, as its proper function, the job of producing states that are accurate when things are a certain way — the function it was selected for because past tokens of it helped ancestors survive. So a toad's prey-catching mechanism has the content "food there", and a bee's dance means "nectar in that direction", because that is what those mechanisms were selected to do. This is meant to beat causal or covariation theories like Dretske's, which identify content with whatever actually correlates with the state and so cannot distinguish a fly-detector from a fly-or-fleck detector: the teleological story can say that the function is to indicate flies, and that when the mechanism fires at a fleck it misfires and therefore misrepresents.

The classic objection is the creature with no history — Davidson's Swampman — but I want to use a modern version of it: a neural net trained by gradient descent on images labelled "tabby". Teleosemantics says Swampman has no contentful states at all, since nothing has a function unless it has a history of being reproduced and selected. That is a feature, not a bug: content cannot come from nowhere. But the same reasoning gives a nice result for the net, and shows the theory is not stuck in the Pleistocene. Gradient descent just is a selection process — it keeps the weights that performed well and discards the ones that did not — so after training the unit's proper function is to fire at tabbies, and its content is "tabby". The same goes for learned concepts like CARBURETTOR, which have no innate function.

On error: a misrepresentation is simply a state that fails to do its job. When the unit fires at a tabby-coloured cushion it is not performing its proper function, and that is what error consists in. So the theory dissolves the problem of error, and with it the indeterminacy problem, because a function picks out one content rather than a whole class of correlated properties.
