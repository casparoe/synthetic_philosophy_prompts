I am writing a paper on Avicenna's flying man (suspended-man) argument, and I need a thought experiment built to a specification: it should separate what the argument actually establishes from what its later readers typically take it to establish, and it should do that by generating the separation rather than stipulating it.

Construct a one-paragraph scenario continuous with Avicenna's own setup — a subject with no sensation, no proprioception, no bodily contact, and no previously acquired beliefs, including any belief that it has a body — in which the subject still affirms its own existence, but in which that affirmation is purely indexical: awareness that this very awareness is occurring, carrying no conception of itself as a persisting thing, a simple thing, an agent, or a thing of any kind at all. Constraints: no Cartesian demon; no stipulation that the self is or is not immaterial; no science fiction, in the sense that the case should be one a clinical case report (deafferentation, anesthesia, sensory deprivation) could in principle describe.

Then show that the design works. For each of three readings — (1) the minimal reading, that self-awareness is primitive and not derived from any sensory input; (2) the substance reading, that the self is thereby shown to be a simple immaterial substance knowable apart from the body; (3) the persistence reading, that what is affirmed is a persisting subject, which is what the argument's later use for survival of death requires — state the verdict the case generates and the reasoning behind it. Tell me which single feature of the scenario is doing the work for each verdict, what happens if that feature is removed (for instance, if the subject's self-awareness arrives with a sense of agency or of duration), and how a defender of the substance reading would attack the case as rigged rather than neutral. Finally, say honestly whether my deflationary conclusion is something the case positively supports or merely something the case is compatible with. Please answer in American English.
