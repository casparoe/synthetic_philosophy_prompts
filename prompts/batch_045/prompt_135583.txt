I am convening a small adversarial collaboration and need the document it is supposed to produce. Two historians in my research group have argued past each other for two years about whether non-human things — a pathogen, a shift in rainfall, a volcanic eruption — belong among the actors in a historical explanation rather than among its background conditions. Camp P holds a causal-parity view: anything that made a difference to what happened, in the plain counterfactual sense, and whose occurrence is not itself fully accounted for by human choices, should appear in the causal story on the same terms as a decision, and withholding the word actor from it is a rhetorical choice, not a discovery. Camp A holds that actorhood is not a matter of difference-making: it requires that the thing be the sort of item that can be addressed, Answered, held to account, or reasoned with — and that histories written as if germs or eruptions acted are trading on a metaphor whose political effects (exculpating decision-makers, writing colonial conquest as epidemiology, explaining poverty as climate) are not accidental to it.

Please draft the joint statement they could both sign after a good-faith collaboration. I want:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims: what both accept about difference-making, about overdetermination (the epidemic that arrives after the garrison has already surrendered), about interaction (the same pathogen, the same exposure, two cities with very different mortality because of housing and nutrition), and about what makes an environmental explanation bad — the slide from a climate shift as a difference-maker to the tropics made them lazy. No claim in this section should be so thin that a third party would call it vacuous, and none should be slanted toward either camp.

2. A disagreements table with these columns: the crux; the formulation of Camp P's position that Camp P would itself accept; the formulation of Camp A's position that Camp A would itself accept; the case, argument, or test both accept as genuinely bearing on that crux; Camp P's prediction about that test, recorded in advance; Camp A's prediction. At minimum I want cruxes for the following: whether responsiveness to reasons is necessary for actorhood; whether sensitivity to consequences without sensitivity to reasons is enough for a weaker grade of agency (a virus under selection pressure evolves, a volcano does not); and whether the difference between the two camps is ultimately about explanation or about blame.

3. For each camp, one statement of what would actually count as evidence against its own view.

4. A short closing section saying which parts of the dispute you take to be verbal rather than substantive, and why, and what the collaboration would have to find for it to be worth running another year.

Two devices from their last, failed meeting should be built into the document. First, the symmetry audit: for every explanation that cites a non-human cause, ask what human cause of identical counterfactual magnitude would have been written up as a cause rather than a condition, and if the answer is none, treat that as a strike against the explanation. Second, the closed-magistrate case, which I will specify: a magistrate in 1816 sentences grain rioters under a mandatory guideline, with no lawful alternative sentence open to him and no discretion he could have exercised otherwise; the harvest failure that produced the rioting traces to a volcanic cooling of the previous year. Both camps should say whether the magistrate, the eruption, both, or neither is an actor in this story, and each should be held to whatever their own criterion entails here rather than to what it entails in the easier cases. Where you think either device is rigged in favour of one camp, say so and propose a neutral repair.
