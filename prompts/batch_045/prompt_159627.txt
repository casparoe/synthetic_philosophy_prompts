The puzzle I keep coming back to is the lamp: a lamp starts off, you flip its switch on after half a minute, off after a further quarter minute, on after a further eighth, and so on, so that infinitely many flips are packed into the minute. What is the state of the lamp at the one-minute mark? I've seen the standard move in reply — that the description of the procedure fixes the state at every moment *before* the minute is up but says nothing about the minute itself, so there is no contradiction to be found, only an incompletely specified setup. I don't find that satisfying on its own, and I'd like you to explain what more is really going on.

What I actually want to understand is the difference between infinite sequences of operations that seem to have a determinate result and ones that don't. So please work with three comparisons I came up with:

1. A runner (or a ball) covering half the remaining distance, then half of what's left, and so on. There are infinitely many steps, yet the final position seems perfectly determinate because the displacements add up to a convergent sum. Why does the sum help here and not with the lamp?

2. A variant of my own: a dimmer switch instead of an on/off switch, where each press nudges the brightness by an amount that shrinks by half every time, alternately up and down. Every press is still a discrete event, and there are still infinitely many of them, but the resulting brightness seems to settle on a definite value. Is convergence of the *effects* the whole story, or does the continuous range of possible states also matter? Is there any example where the effects shrink to nothing and yet the end state is still indeterminate?

3. Another variant of my own: lamp A is flipped at the half-minute, three-quarter-minute, seven-eighth-minute marks, and so on; lamp B is flipped at exactly the same moments except that the very first flip is left out. Both start off. After the half-minute mark the two lamps are always in opposite states, and by the end of the minute both have been flipped infinitely many times. Yet deleting a single flip from a *finite* schedule always changes the final state, while both schedules here contain the same "number" of flips in the only sense of number I can apply to them. Does this pair-of-lamps construction create a new difficulty, or does it just restate the original one? What exactly does it show about whether the end state could be a matter of counting flips?

Two further things I'd like you to address. First: the times at which the lamp is on form a scattered collection of stretches of time, and the one-minute mark is a limit point of both the on-stretches and the off-stretches. Does that geometric fact settle anything, and would adding a rule such as "the lamp is on at the end if it was on at moments arbitrarily close to the end" be a discovery about the lamp or merely a stipulation?
