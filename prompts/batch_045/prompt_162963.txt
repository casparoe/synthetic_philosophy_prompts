I am writing the decision-theoretic section of a paper on when an agent should stop gathering evidence, and I want the specification settled before I commit to a formalism. The setting is deliberately unfriendly to the textbook result: the agent is bounded, with a fixed attention budget, so buying one signal means not buying another, and its posteriors come from a fixed update procedure rather than from ideal conditioning; and some signals are public, in the sense that other agents whose later reactions matter will observe them too. Good's theorem, understood as the result that an ideal expected-utility maximizer cannot lose in expectation by conditioning on free evidence and gains only where the evidence would change what she does, settles very little here, so I want to know what I should be demanding of a measure of the value of information anyway.

Write the desiderata, each with the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and each marked as either non-negotiable or a preference of one approach. I have these pressures in mind, but reorganise, split, and add as you think right: recovery of expected-utility gain as the ideal, free, and ignorable limit; capacity to go negative, with the negative cases explained by one mechanism rather than collected as exceptions (my candidate is that information one cannot costlessly dispose of was never free, and I want to know whether that covers attention opportunity costs, public observability, and designs that perturb the system they measure, or whether one of those needs separate treatment); independence from how the signal is labelled or encoded, except where dependence on encoding is a real fact about a bounded updater; computability ex ante, from the agent's own situation, and decomposability across stages of a sequential design; and denomination in the agent's utility rather than in bits, variance, or money.

Two things matter more than the rest of the list. Say which subset cannot be jointly satisfied by any single scalar, especially whether invariance under re-description is compatible with sensitivity to a bounded update procedure, and whether ex-ante computability from the agent's perspective is compatible with correctness about a strategic environment; if the list is unsatisfiable as one number, say what the deliverable should be instead, such as a family indexed by decision procedure or a pair of bounds. Then name the single requirement that does the most work excluding the standard proxies and say what it rules out. Close with a four-line scorecard for expected-utility gain, mutual information between signal and state, expected variance reduction, and probability that the optimal act changes.
