Some background. The payoffs here have the usual venture shape — most things return zero, a few return 20 to 100x — and when we fit power-law tails to the return distribution the fitted exponent lands right around 1. Depending on the estimator and the window we get 0.9 or we get 1.1, which I take to mean we literally cannot tell, from any data we could have, whether the mean of the thing we're buying exists at all. My colleague Priya says that means we should stop computing means altogether and rank everything by first-order stochastic dominance where it applies, and simply refuse to rank where it doesn't. My other colleague Dave has a different fix: compute the truncated expectation below the p-th percentile as p goes to 1, and if that sequence settles, use the limit; if it oscillates, declare the two prospects EV-incomparable and break the tie with medians, then with the 5th percentile. He keeps invoking the Pasadena game as the canonical case — coin flipped until heads, payout something like 2^n/n with the sign alternating each toss, so the naive EV series is 1 - 1/2 + 1/3 - ... which comes to about 0.69 but only conditionally, and someone told me you can rearrange it to add up to more or less anything. Both of them also want an infinite or undefined expectation to fire a model-misspecification flag and force a refit with a capped tail.
