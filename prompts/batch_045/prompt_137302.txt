Quick bit of context before the question: I sit on the program committee of a small foundation that funds conceptual work in philosophy with a long shelf life — no lab budget, no empirical research unless someone makes a really good case for it, and we hand out people rather than grants, so about eight person-years over four years in epistemology next cycle. Hinge epistemology — the literature that grew out of Wittgenstein's late notes on certainty, Moore-style propositions, hinge commitments, the idea that some things stand fast for us without being justified — is on the shortlist, and I'm the one who has to argue for or against it. My own background is nowhere near this (my PhD was decision theory-adjacent and I've never been able to get through a Wittgenstein commentary), so I need an agenda I can defend to the rest of the committee in terms they'll understand, not a tour of the secondary literature.

Here's my rough map of the area, which you should feel free to correct or replace: (1) whether being a hinge is a property of a proposition at all, or only of a proposition-as-it-figures-in-some-practice — my hunch is that this is upstream of everything else, but I can't tell whether it's a real question or just a terminological decision; (2) what a friend who works on this calls hinge drift: cases where a proposition seems to get promoted from ordinary empirical status to something hinge-like, or demoted back again (whether anyone has traveled to the moon, how old the earth is, the reliability of some measuring instrument), and whether there is any normative story about when a hinge should be demoted or whether asking that is just confused; (3) the acquisition-and-deference problem — if hinges are arational and not the sort of thing that gets justified, what exactly is transmitted when a child picks up a community's hinges from testimony, and is that a genuine puzzle or does it dissolve once you're careful; (4) whether any of this answers the regress argument against the skeptic or quietly changes the subject; (5) an applied line I half-invented, call it hinge capture: if a community's hinges aren't grounded in reasons, is there any room left for criticizing it when a school curriculum, a propaganda apparatus, a religious community, or increasingly the output of an AI system ends up setting them — and is that a real implication of the view or an abuse of it; (6) the apparently empirical material about wholesale collapse of certainties, people who lose an entire framework at once in severe delusion or depersonalisation or after leaving a high-control group, and whether that's evidence about hinges or a category mistake.

What I want is a defended agenda for those eight person-years. Which three or four lines of work should get them, in what order, and on what criteria — how much of the rest depends on each, whether it's tractable in three or four years by someone working alone at a desk, whether it's already crowded or actually neglected, and how you weigh those when they conflict, which they will. Three things I care about most: which single question, if someone cracked it, would change what the other questions even are; which implications of hinge epistemology people oversell, the ones that sound great in a funding abstract but don't actually follow and should not be used to sell this to my committee; and what kind of result over the next two or three years would move something up or down your ranking, since we'll likely have a second round and I'd like to have built-in update conditions. One more worry I can't shake and want you to take seriously rather than reassure me about: if hinges really are outside the space of reasons, it's not obvious there is anything left for an epistemologist to say about them, as opposed to an anthropologist or a therapist. Tell me whether that's a reason to fund this area, a reason to fund it with a different framing, or a reason to put the person-years elsewhere. Prose is fine, tables if they help, and please skip anything like a reading list or who's-written-what — I can't read it all and what I need is the reasoning behind the ordering.
