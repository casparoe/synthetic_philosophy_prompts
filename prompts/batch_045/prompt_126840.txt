ok so I've ended up with the boringly orthodox view in my department and I'd like to know how exposed it actually is.

The view: the distinction between killing and letting die does no moral work in its own right. Where it seems to matter, it's a proxy for things that do matter — how much causal contribution you make to the outcome, whose agency the outcome flows through, what people are entitled to expect of each other given how we've set up our institutions, how bad the outcome is, what the action expresses about the agent. Hold all of those fixed and the leftover difference between killing and letting die is, I think, morally inert. And I've got two commitments bolted onto it: first, I like an interventionist account of causation (causes as difference-makers under hypothetical interventions), and I think that account is doing a lot of the real work in the famous cases — in the bathtub pair, for instance, the guy who stands there ready to hold his cousin's head under is on a par with the guy who does it, because on any interventionist test the outcome isn't sensitive to whether he has to intervene. Second, I think the asymmetry people feel in the standard trolley pair gets explained by something like rights and agency, not by the doing/allowing distinction as such.

Two things I've been chewing on that I don't have a good line on. One is a case a friend made up that's been going around: a life-support machine that keeps a patient alive only if the doctor affirmatively renews the order once an hour. Not renewing looks like an omission, but so does dying, and it's not obvious why ninety consecutive omissions add up to an allowing while one switch-flip is a doing. On my view there's no difference between not renewing and switching it off, and I'm fine with that, but it makes me suspect my proxies aren't tracking anything stable. The other is the reverse-shaped case: the only way to stop the trolley is to *stop* doing something you're already doing automatically — not sending a routine signal, say — so that withholding is your only means of rescuing. I predict the distinction should go quiet here, and it doesn't feel like it does.

So: what would count against the package? I mean that seriously — not just the standard objections, but which cases, which intuitions, which results elsewhere (about causation, about how the law handles omissions, about moral psychology) it stands or falls with. Concretely: is there a case where every one of my proxies is fixed and the intuition still tracks the distinction, or does every such case secretly smuggle one of them back in? What should I make of the hypothetical empirical finding I keep worrying about — a big pre-registered cross-cultural study, and a developmental one, showing the asymmetry survives when all the proxies are controlled for, shows up in small kids, and predicts verdicts better than causal contribution or rights-violation scores do? Would that move anyone on my side, or would we just re-describe the result as the proxies in disguise — and if we did, is that a real problem for a view of this shape or a fair bit of theory-protection?

Also, the thing I actually lie awake about: if causation is difference-making under interventions, then unplugging a ventilator, removing a barrier, letting a boulder roll are all causing the death, and my story seems to collapse almost every allowing into a doing. That should be fine for me — I deny the distinction matters — but it also means my account of why the distinction *looks* like it matters has nothing left to work with in exactly the cases where it looks most like it matters. Is that the real defeater hiding in the middle of my own view, or is it a sign I've mis-specified one of the proxies?

Rank the defeaters for me from fatal to merely awkward, and be clear which ones my side would itself concede as counting against us and which we'd wave off. For what it's worth, I'd personally be most moved by a minimally different pair of cases where the proxies really are all equal and the verdicts still come apart, or by an argument that the equivalence line has been qualified into saying nothing. I'd be least moved by bare appeals to how sturdy the intuition is. Oh, and if the answer is that this view is extremely well insulated and that's fine, say so and tell me why.
