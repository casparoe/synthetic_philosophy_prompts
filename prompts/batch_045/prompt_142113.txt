I'm defending a chapter of my dissertation in a comparative-historical workshop on nationalism next Thursday, and what I want is Q&A preparation, not a defense of the thesis. The chapter argues three things.

First, a decomposition: treat the nation as three separable conditions that happen to travel together — (i) a named population with a story of shared descent, shared custom, or shared confession; (ii) a belief that this population is bounded and continuous across generations; (iii) the further normative claim that the boundary of that population is the right boundary of a jurisdiction, so that the cultural unit and the unit of legitimate rule ought to coincide. Second, a historical claim: (i) and (ii) are demonstrably premodern and very common, from early medieval descent narratives to early modern partisan confessions, whereas (iii) is the genuinely new element, and it becomes widespread in the nineteenth century rather than the sixteenth. Third, a mechanism claim: (iii) is not produced by print markets, literacy, or industrial standardization but is a legitimatory template that spreads by emulation. I want to argue that Anderson's own appeal to modular nationalism does more explanatory work than his print-capitalism story, and that the two pull in opposite directions, since anything genuinely modular travels without the material base that is supposed to explain it. On my version, state legibility — census, civil registration, conscription — does the origin work, and print only the diffusion work, which reverses the usual emphasis and leaves the census–map–museum triad doing unequal labour.

The room is eight people. Two are committed Andersonians, one senior and one ABD, who know the print-language, creole-pioneer and modularity arguments cold and will hear my third claim as an internal critique that steals a thesis while discarding its machinery. One is a Gellnerian comparative sociologist, who thinks a legitimatory template is an idealist relic unless I can say which actors have an interest in adopting it and what their material situation was. One is a Hobsbawmian historian of nineteenth-century Europe, who holds that invented traditions cluster where rapid social change has broken the practices the older traditions fitted, and who will want to know why an emulation story needs a trigger at all. One is a historian of premodern ethnicity, sympathetic to the decomposition, who will press the evidentiary question. One is a postcolonial and decolonial theorist who will press the charge that my three conditions are abstracted from European material and that applying them to anticolonial nationalism merely redescribes what the colonial state already assumed about the populations it ruled. One is a quantitative comparative politics person who will ask for an operationalization and for the observable implication that distinguishes my account from Anderson's. The chair is a liberal political theorist who will ask what my account leaves of the normative force of self-determination claims, and who may also want to know whether a nation under my description can ever be wrong about its own boundaries. I have twenty minutes of Q&A.

Give me seven or eight objections, ordered by expected damage rather than by the order they will be raised, each tagged with who raises it and phrased in that person's idiom so I can recognise it coming. For each: the ninety-second reply I should give, where that reply is weakest, and whether it is better to concede than to answer. Flag the two concessions I should make in the first two minutes of the talk rather than under questioning, since conceding them up front costs less than being forced into them. Name the one question I am hoping nobody asks; my own candidate is whether my coding of the premodern cases as lacking condition (iii) is an artifact of an archive that rarely records non-elite political opinion, so tell me whether that is the trap or whether something worse is coming. No citations or reading lists — I need the moves and the wording, not sources.

Lead with a single sentence naming the objection most likely to sink the talk and the one-line concession that defuses it, then give the prep sheet. No preamble and no restatement of my thesis.
