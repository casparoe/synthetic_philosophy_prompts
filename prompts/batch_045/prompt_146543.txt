I have just come out of a long and unsatisfying argument with a friend about uploading, and I want to write down where I actually stand before we take it up again. What I would like from you is a consistency audit of the set below, not an introduction to the topic.

Where I have landed:

(1) What matters in my survival is continuity of psychological and functional organization. Not numerical identity, and not the material the organization happens to be realized in.

(2) I would happily undergo gradual replacement: swap my neurons a few at a time, over months, for prostheses that are functionally equivalent, and I take it that I come out the other side in exactly the way I come out of an ordinary night's sleep. I would say the same if the whole procedure were carried out while I was under general anesthesia. My friend agrees with me here, which is part of why the rest is hard.

(3) I would not undergo destructive scan-and-copy. I think the scan is where I end, and that the person who wakes up on the new substrate is not only numerically distinct from me but also does not carry forward what matters. My friend says this is just superstition about carbon, and that (1) and (2) leave me no distinction between the two procedures that I am entitled to care about. I cannot shake the intuition, and I also cannot say what in my stated view supports it.

(4) I accept the Parfitian line that identity is not what matters. In a fission case where two continuers each stand in the relation that matters to me now, the fact that neither is numerically identical to me is not a loss, and the outcome is about as good as ordinary survival.

(5) I also accept the fading-qualia style of argument against the idea that a functional duplicate of me could be an unconscious zombie: I do not think experience can quietly drain away, or jump about, across steps that preserve functional organization. So I take a full functional duplicate of my brain, if reached by gradual replacement, to be conscious and to be me, and I take the same to hold for a digital duplicate reached by that route. I include this because I do not want to defend (3) by sneaking in the claim that silicon cannot be conscious.

(6) By functionally equivalent in (2) I mean something stronger than a match on the actual sequence: a prosthesis that reproduces one of my neurons' firing statistics for the inputs I in fact receive, but would diverge wildly for inputs I never get, is not functionally equivalent, and I do not accept that swapping it in preserves me.

(7) The case my friend sprang on me, which I could not answer. Imagine a procedure with no discrete copying event: a second, initially inert network is grown alongside my brain; then, unit by unit, each of my neural units is replaced by a device that drives two successor units at once, wired in common at first so that nothing about my behavior changes; over months the cross-links between the two systems are weakened by imperceptible degrees until they have drifted into independence. Every individual step is smaller than the steps I accept without hesitation in (2); there is no moment you could point to as the moment of branching. At the end there are two people, each a full functional duplicate of what I was. I find that I want to say that something has been lost here that is not lost in (2) — a kind of death, or at least a real loss — even though by (1) and (4) I cannot say what.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, ordered by severity rather than all flagged at once. For each real tension, say what exactly I would have to give up or add and what that would cost me. I also suspect that at least one item on the list is only an apparent tension, and I would like that separated out from the real ones. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Last: say whether whatever I am reaching for in (7) can be turned into a principle I could actually state, or whether it is the residue of an intuition that (1) and (4) have already ruled out. For each of your main claims, state how confident you are in it, and single out the one you are least sure of.
