Bit of an odd ask, and it's come up because it just happened to me: me and my cofounder, in the same week, both went public in front of the team with incompatible deadlines we'd each quietly picked, and now we're locked in and we both know it was dumb. I've been asked to run a 75-minute session on it for about 18 founders in our accelerator next month and I really don't want to stand up and lecture about it.

Design me a game that makes them feel a commitment race instead of hearing about one. Two parties, something worth dividing, a move that's costly to take back, whoever commits first gets the better split — so both want to move first and both end up worse than if neither had. Rules, materials, a payoff scheme in points or chips (no real money; one small prize at the end is fine), and a few rounds. I don't want the phrase "commitment race" said out loud until the debrief.

Two things I'd like built in if you can. First, make observability imperfect: sometimes the other side can't tell whether you actually committed or just said you did, so the race is partly a race to be *seen* committing. Second, a seperate late round where they're allowed to pay each other to un-commit — that's the move I keep thinking should rescue my situation and doesn't, and I want to see whether the game shows why.

Give me a debrief that maps specific moments onto the mechanism, what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (pairs finishing at wildly different speeds, someone colluding, someone who just refuses to move), and be honest about which feature of the real thing the game can't reproduce and I'll have to say in words. Also: how confident are you that your payoff numbers will actually generate the race rather than a shrug, and which single parameter are you least sure about — I can re-tune it after a test run.
