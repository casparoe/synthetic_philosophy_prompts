so I've been stuck on this testimony question — whether someone telling me something gives me a reason to believe it just by itself, or whether it's always just one more bit of evidence I have to weigh up against everything else I already know. I think the fancy names for the two sides are reductionism and anti-reductionism but I don't care about labels. I got bored of arguing it in the abstract so I wrote down what I actually believe in concrete cases, going by gut:

1. I'm late for a train in a city I don't know. A woman I've never met, standing on the platform, says the Lyon train goes from platform 7. I believe her and go. Completely fine — and it doesn't feel like I'm running any inference from her accent or her being a local or whatever. It just feels like she told me.

2. Same trip, a guy at the bar tells me the cathedral is a reconstruction because the original was bombed in 1943. I have no way to check. I find I half believe it — I'd repeat it to a friend, hedged — but it's nothing like case 1, and I can't say why.

3. My cousin believes every conspiracy theory going, but he's describing a car crash he watched out his window. I believe him about the crash. I would not believe him if he added that the council staged it.

4. An anonymous account on a forumn states some niche historical detail. Nobody has contradicted it, there's a long thread, nobody seems to object. I don't believe it, and it doesn't even feel close — but if the reductionist story is right, absence of counterevidence plus the fact that someone bothered to say it should count for something, and I feel exactly nothing.

5. My neighbour heard from the building manager, who heard from the landlord, that the rent is going up in March. I believe it. If I were multiplying reliabilities along the chain I'd end up less confident than I actually am, so whatever I'm doing it isn't that.

6. My five-year-old niece says the dog knocked the vase over because he was mad at her. I believe the first half, not the second. Same speaker, same sentence, same confidence from her.

7. My Spanish is bad. A Spanish-speaking friend tells me the buses aren't running tomorrow — I get roughly the words but I'm piecing the sentence together myself, so a lot of what I'm relying on is my own shaky decode rather than her word. Still feels like being told rather than inferring, which seems odd given how much guessing is mixed in.

8. A used car salesman who I know lies about service history tells me the car has full service history and has never been in an accident. I take that as evidence against. But I also feel like I've learned something from what he said, which is weird if learning from what someone says means taking their word for it.

9. Three people separately watched the same accident, none of them especially reliable, and all give the same description. I'm more confident than I'd be in any one of them — so my reason can't just be my estimate of a particular speaker's reliability.

That's the list. The pair I can't square is 1 and 4: in both cases it's a stranger, no track record, no ability to check, low stakes, and I take the one at face value and not the other. I don't think I can blame it on subject matter, because 2 is also a matter of fact I can't check and there I'm in between.

I had a go at a principle: you get a default entitlement to believe what someone tells you when they're offering it to you as a reason to believe it and are in a position to be held to it — asked to justify it, answer for it, embarrassed if it turns out false — and the entitlement is missing or hollow exactly in the cases where that address is missing or empty. But I think that makes 8 strange (he's maximally addressable and I still don't take his word), and I don't think it explains 7 or 2 at all.

So: is there one thing that gets all nine, or do I have to give one of them up? Where does my principle go wrong and what's the best version of it — the deliverable I want is a principle I can actually state, with the case that motivates each bit of it. And then tell me what it says about the case I keep flip-flopping on, which is why I started thinking about this: when a chatbot tells me a fact I can't check. I don't take its word the way I take the woman's word about the platform, but I can't tell whether that's just an accurate reliability estimate on my part, or something about there being nobody there to answer for it, or whether I'm just being prejudiced against the thing.
