Two people at a dinner I was at last night spent almost an hour going round in circles on that torture-vs-dust-specks case — you know the one, pick between one person being tortured for fifty years and some astronomical number of people (3^^^3 or whatever) each getting a speck of dust in their eye for a couple of seconds. Neither of them budged and they asked me to referee and I honestly couldnt tell. Can you go through it move by move?

(1) Owen: The specks, obviously. Whatever a dust speck costs, it doesn't cost *nothing* — it isn't a neutral event. And once you grant that, you can't turn round and say there's no number of them that's worse than one torture. If you want to say torture is worse however many specks there are, you owe us the number where that stops being true, and there isn't one you can defend. And it's not like the example uses a million; 3^^^3 is picked precisely to swamp any threshold you could name. So the example isn't a trick, it's just what denying aggregation actually commits you to.

(2) Lena: You've helped yourself to the thing I'm denying. I don't think a speck has a *disvalue* sitting on the same scale as torture, so there's no "how many of these add up to one of those" question to answer. My claim is lexical: below some threshold of seriousness a harm isn't the kind of item that gets summed into the category of serious harm at all — adding annoyances is like adding nothings, not because each one scores zero on a shared scale but because "speck vs torture" isn't a comparison of magnitudes in the first place. The burden is on you to show they're on one scale, not on me to locate a cutoff.

(3) Owen: Two problems. First, continuity. Build a series: one person tortured for fifty years at the top, and at each step shave a bit off the worst outcome and spread the remainder over more people in slightly milder forms, until you bottom out at 3^^^3 people with a speck each. You say the top is worse than the bottom. But each step is only a hair better than the one before, so somewhere there's an adjacent pair where you flip from "worse" to "not worse" — and you've drawn the single biggest line in the whole series across the smallest gap in it. That's arbitrary, and it's not answered by saying you can't locate the boundary. Second: you do accept summation inside one life. A million specks blown into one person's eye over a year with no recovery time is definitely a worse life than one speck. So sub-threshold items accumulate for you. What changes when they're spread across a million people?

(4) Lena: Second thing first, because it's a good question: inside one life they compound. They interact, they ruin a stretch of experience that has a single subject, and I'm happy to call that string bad-for-her in a way that isn't just one-speck-repeated. Spread across a million lives there's no subject whose experience gets ruined; the badness doesn't pool anywhere. That isn't a dodge, it's the whole content of the view. On continuity: "worse than" is vague, and vague comparators don't transmit down long sorites series. Since you can't point to the grain that turns a heap into a non-heap, I'm not obliged to point to the rung where torture stops being worse. My inability to locate the boundary is a fact about the predicate, not a reductio of me.

(5) Owen: You're running two replies that don't fit together. Either the threshold is fuzzy, in which case you can't lean on a crisp qualitative category like "serious harm" to do the work, since that category has no sharp edge either — or it's crisp, and you've just relocated the arbitrariness into a new primitive and my smallest-gap complaint stands untouched. Also the vagueness move has a cost you haven't paid: if nothing in the series is determinately worse than anything else, then faced with the actual choice I'm permitted to take either — flip a coin over whether somebody gets tortured for fifty years. Buridan's ass with a life as the hay. A view that can't rank the options and doesn't care which one I pick has stopped being a view about what to do.

(6) Lena: On that last bit: incomparability isn't "anything goes." If the welfare ordering leaves the pair open, I can break the tie with a different kind of reason — procedural fairness, what people could reasonably be asked to consent to, the fact that a tiny cost shared by everyone is fairer than something catastrophic dumped on one. That's not comparison smuggled back in, it's a tie-breaker. And here's one for you. Change the case: instead of certain specks, each of the 3^^^3 people faces a one-in-10^40 chance of being tortured for fifty years, and the alternative is one person definitely getting a speck. You multiply, so in expectation that's an enormous amount of torture and you have to prevent it. I say nobody is meaningfully at risk and the numbers don't matter. You can't dismiss small probabilities in general — you'd never cross the street — so tell me why the multiplication is compulsory for chances but optional for actual harms.

(7) Owen: On your tie-breaker: fine, but then it's doing the real work and you owe us an account of it — and "fairer to spread a tiny cost than to dump a catastrophe on one person" sounds exactly like you're comparing the sizes of the two costs, which is the scale you said you didn't have. On the risk case, I'll bite the bullet: with those numbers the expected toll is astronomically worse than one speck, and I'd prevent it, and I don't think that's fanaticism, I think it's taking 3^^^3 seriously. Your line — "no one is meaningfully at risk" — is precisely the slogan a government would use to impose a small risk on everybody and call the total nothing.

Two things I noticed while they went at it, which may or may not matter: nobody ever said whether "not worse than" was supposed to mean "equally as good" or "can't be ranked," and they seemed to use it both ways; and all of Owen's arguments seemed to lean on the number being 3^^^3 rather than, say, ten billion, without anyone asking whether that's doing independent work. Please rate each move from clearly mistaken to clearly right with a short justification for each, say who's more right overall, and tell me what both of them are missing.
