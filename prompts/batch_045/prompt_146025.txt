Longtime lurker, been reading the Sequences and the rationalist-adjacent blogs since way back, and I'm a fairly serious birder, which is why I keep tripping over this thing I dont have a word for. In my first year, a warbler was a small yellow bird, a blur with a bit of motion in it. Now the same patch of retina delivers a crisp eye-ring, a particular way of flicking, a shape that is unmistakably one species and not the two lookalikes — and, importantly, I cant get the blur back. It isn't that I choose to interpret it; once I've seen it as the right species I can't even see it as the wrong one, any more than I can unsee the duck-rabbit once it flips. Same story from my sister in radiology (scans used to be grey mush, now they are obviously a thing) and from a friend who trained as a coffee taster. I keep wanting to say that learning changed what the stuff looks like, not just what I think about it, but I also cant shake my friend's reply that this is all judgment and vocabulary and I'm just more willing to say the words now.

Three cases make me think it isn't one thing, and I want to know which of them belong together. First, the lock-in: a while back I was told a friend had said something nasty about me, and for months afterwards her face looked different to me — harder to read as friendly, slightly wrong — and then it turned out the report was fabricated, I fully believed it was false, apologized, the works, and her face kept looking wrong for another couple of weeks. So whatever happened outlived the belief that produced it, which makes me suspicious that "your belief is reaching down into your perception" is the wrong shape of explanation. Second, provenance and value: knowing that a bird is an introduced species rather than a native one, or that the call I'm hearing is a mimic and not the real bird, changes what I hear — the same acoustics lose something — even though I know perfectly well that nothing about the sound changed and I have no particular feelings about the bird either way. Third, the de-enrichment: learning sometimes makes me take in less. Once I can name a call I stop hearing the rest of the soundscape around it, and my sister's crowd apparently miss things in scans that a naive undergrad walks right past. (I think I half-remember a study about hiding something in a scan, but the details don't matter — the phenomenon I mean is that expertise can flatten as well as sharpen.) None of this is the dress thing or the Necker cube, where the same input flips on its own with no learning involved, and it isn't plain inattention either, since in the warbler case I'm attending as hard as I can and can't undo it.

I've half-heard of "cognitive penetration" and of modularity of mind, and maybe something called New Look psychology, but I genuinely don't know if that's the right neighborhood or which of my three cases would even qualify under the usual criteria — I suspect some of them get excluded on a technicality, e.g. the lock-in case might stop counting precisely because the effect outlasts the belief, and get relabeled as learning or recalibration instead. So: what's the name for this cluster, if there is one; which of my three cases are really the same phenomenon and which aren't; how you could in principle (or in practice) tell "you see it differently" from "you judge or describe it differently," without the test just begging the question; and is there an established term for the specifically evaluative/provenance-ish subset, as opposed to penetration by plain factual knowledge? If that subset has no name, coin one, define it, and give me the boundary cases — what the smallest tweak to the case would be that makes it stop being an instance. I don't want a one-word answer and please don't send me a reading list or citations, I'm not writing a paper, I just want to know the shape of the thing and which of my three weird cases is the interesting one.
