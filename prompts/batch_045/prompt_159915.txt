I've more or less accepted anatta — no persisting self, just the aggregates and a causally connected stream, with the felt "I" as something the stream throws off rather than something it's about. What I can't get straight is what that's supposed to do to my practical concerns, because my gut verdicts on cases won't line up:

- Brain split, both halves going on with full psychology: neither one is me, and I don't think the question even has an answer — but each matters to me nearly as much as an undisturbed survivor would.
- Same body, but every memory and character trait gone overnight: that's basically death, and the resulting person matters much less.
- Dreamless sleep, or six hours under general anaesthetic: obviously I survive. No worry at all.
- Two hundred years with no experience whatsoever (cryonics, say): I go back and forth, and probably lean no.
- Me at eighty, sharing maybe 20% of my current memories: definately me, matters fully, I should save for retirement.
- One operation that leaves me with that same 20% overlap, done all at once, in the same body: reads as death to me, and I'd care far less about that person's old age. This pair is the one I can't reconcile — same overlap, opposite verdicts.
- A pill that leaves experience and behaviour exactly as they are but removes the felt mineness of it: I'd take it, and I don't think prudence would become irrational.
- Someone who did something terrible thirty years ago and now shares almost nothing with the person who did it: they still owe something, an apology at least, but much less punishment.
- And the one I keep coming back to: I'm still plainly more afraid of my own future pain than of a stranger's equal pain, even though I don't think anybody is special.

Is there a single principle behind all this, and is there a version of it that's actually stable given anatta, rather than just a familiar reductionist view of personal identity dressed in Buddhist vocabulary? I want the clause-by-clause statement, which case motivates each clause, what it says about the slow-decay-versus-one-surgery pair, an honest list of which of my verdicts it can't keep, and what it predicts about a case I haven't thought of.
