I keep coming back to Thomson's lamp and feeling like the standard dialectic is stuck in a rut, so help me build a case that pries it open.

The setup I want: a lamp wired to a switch that slides along a track instead of snapping between two positions. The lamp is lit whenever the slider is right of the midpoint, dark when it is left of it, and at the exact midpoint both contacts touch, so it is lit. Now drive the slider so it crosses the midpoint infinitely many times before t=1, but the excursions shrink: its position converges on the midpoint and it comes to rest there at t=1, with speed and acceleration bounded throughout. Pick the schedule and show the bounds. No point masses, no infinite forces or speeds, nothing that would not fit on a table. Also say what the machine does after t=1 and whether that is doing any work.

The verdicts I want, all on that one case:
- The Benacerraf style reply (the story simply does not say what is true at the limit, so there is no contradiction, only a description with a hole in it) has to be blocked. The final state should fall out of the limit plus one small, checkable fact about the hardware, not from me stipulating it.
- Thomson style reasoning (every switch-on is followed by a switch-off, there is no last switching, so the lamp is neither on nor off at t=1, so the supertask cannot be performed) has to run on this case, look applicable, and come out wrong.
- Continuity style reasoning (a quantity that converges takes its limit as its value at the limit) has to come out right, and I want to see exactly which of its premises the Thomson argument is quietly denying.

Then tell me which feature is load bearing and what happens if I remove it: swap the sliding switch for a dimmer with continuous brightness; make the position sequence diverge instead of converge; fatten the both-contacts-closed region from a single point into a band of positive width. And give me the best steelman available to a Thomson defender. My guess is the band-width objection is the strongest one, but say if there is a better attack.

Point of the post, so you know what counts as cheating: I want to argue that the Thomson argument only goes through if occupying a state at the limit requires occupying it throughout some stretch running up to the limit, and that the real moral concerns the read-out rather than infinity itself. So the verdicts have to be generated by the setup, not announced by it.
