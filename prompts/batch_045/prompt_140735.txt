My old housemate Nadia has run an Arendt reading group more or less forever (Truth and Politics, Lying in Politics, all the Pentagon Papers material) and she has extremely firm verdicts on hypotheticals people throw at her. Her track record so far:

- A city councilman denies a real affair and gets caught within a week: bad, but boring — ordinary lying, not politically interesting.
- A campaign invents a whole heroic biography for its candidate, war record and all, and mostly gets away with it: much worse, this is what Arendt is actually worried about.
- A president's staff quietly produce optimistic progress reports they don't believe, while everyone knows some of it is spin: top tier, the paradigm case.
- A press secretary says something false about how well the economy is doing: shrug, opinion-adjacent, that's just persuasion.
- A regime rewrites the school history textbooks around a wholly invented national past and enforces them: top tier again.
- A mayor answers a question about a police shooting with we neither confirm nor deny and stonewalls for months: not lying at all, different category, Arendt's machinery doesn't reach it.

Here's the case I actually want her on. A media operation that never asserts a single falsehood: every sentence it puts out survives factcheck, no deepfakes, no invented numbers, no denials of specific claims, and it actively invites people to check. But the selection and ordering is done so well that everyone who follows it ends up holding the same extremely detailed, internally consistant and completely false picture of what's going on.

So no fact is ever denied and the fact/opinion line is never blurred, but the shared world gets replaced anyway. Does that count on her own terms, or does it slip straight through? I care much less about the verdict than about which rules her track record is consistent with — I've steelmanned two so far (roughly: the wrong is systematic world-building at scale, vs. the wrong is turning facts into opinions) and I keep going in circles. Which of them bite on this case, what's your best guess at her answer and why, and what further case would seperate them? 350 words max, bullets over prose.
