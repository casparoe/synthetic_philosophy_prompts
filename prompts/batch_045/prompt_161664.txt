Hey — I TA a second-year module on the philosophy of mind and I also run a small optional reading group on the side, and we just did a week on illusionism about phenomenal consciousness (the Frankish-style line: there is no such thing as phenomenality, there's only the introspective illusion of it). The session went sideways: about half the group came away convinced illusionism is just old-school eliminativism with better PR, the other half thinks it's realism in denial, and a couple of people claimed that if you press hard enough the illusionist and the non-reductive physicalist end up saying the same thing. I don't think any of those three readings is right, but I also can't articulate why in a way that shuts them up, so I want an instrument that does the work for me.

What I'd like is a decision tree / taxonomy — for a handout, so think 7 or 8 branch nodes and somewhere between 9 and 11 leaves — that takes a student from one starting commitment, roughly "I'm as certain as I am of anything that there is something it is like for me to see red," down to a named position. Build the tree out of the logical space first (which questions, and which combinations of answers they permit) and only then drop the actual views into the cells, and note any combination that's empty with one sentence on why: incoherent, unattractive, or just unoccupied. Leaves I want to see placed: Frankish-style illusionism as distinct from straight eliminativism, a "reference-failure" version where the concept of phenomenality simply fails to denote, non-reductive physicalism, Russellian monism, panpsychism, epiphenomenalism, and a quietist or therapeutic option that says the whole dispute is a pseudo-problem — plus at least one leaf you label "unnamed" if the space has a coherent slot nobody defends.

Two constraints on the nodes. First, no node may be a label question: nothing like "is consciousness real?" or "are qualia physical?" — every branch has to turn on a concrete case or a specific claim someone could agree or disagree with before they know which -ism it leads to. Second, I want at least three of the branches built from cases I've been chewing on, which I don't think are in the standard menus: (a) the replay case — you store a creature's complete causal-functional organization and run it once, frozen, with no behavioral output and no downstream effects; does that run have phenomenality? (b) the fade case — a subject's ability to notice, compare, and report their own states stays perfect while something like their access to the "what it's like" of those states degrades steadily; does phenomenality diminish with it, stay put, or was it never a separate quantity? (c) the retrospective-insertion case — you discover your vivid memory of last night's pain was confabulated and there was no pain signal at all; did you undergo a phenomenal pain at the time? Add at least one further case of your own that does diagnostic work the three above can't (I have a feeling none of mine cleanly seperates illusionism from the quietist option). Also make sure one node somewhere faces up to the recursion problem the illusionist has to answer — whether the illusion they postulate is itself something it's like, or whether "illusion" is doing metaphorical work only.

Finally, at the end of the handout, give me a short honest section on the tree's failures: which two leaves it genuinely can't seperate, which node is really doing all the work (my bet is the tree secretly reduces to one or two questions and the rest is decoration), and whether the whole exercise secretly stacks the deck — i.e. whether the way you have to phrase a case in order to make it answerable already smuggles in a realist or an illusionist assumption. I'd rather hand out something that admits that than something that looks tidy.
