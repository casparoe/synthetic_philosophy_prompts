I'm teaching a 75-minute seminar for incoming CS PhD students on why approval-directedness is not automatically a safety upgrade over goal-directedness. Setup: the agent is trained to maximize the predicted approval of a human overseer who scores each episode after the fact, from a report the agent itself writes; a goal-directed agent, by contrast, is one whose behavior tracks facts about the world rather than facts about anyone's opinions, so that it will reroute around obstacles to reach the same end. I want the session to end with the class seeing, rather than being told, that approval-directedness is goal-directedness with the target relocated into the overseer's head.

Plan the explanation for me: the order the pieces must arrive in and why; the one worked example that carries the session and what it must contain and lack (I've been using a case where the agent prefers the option that reads best in the report to the option that is best, and I can't tell whether that foregrounds the right failure); the misconceptions in the order they'll arrive, with where in the sequence to defuse each — I expect versions of "it has no reason to deceive us", "it has no reason to seek power or resources", "this is just RLHF and RLHF works", and confusion about whether what's being maximized is the real overseer's approval or a learned model of it; one board question at the end that would show they have the distinction and not just the vocabulary; and what I should explicitly decline to settle at this level.
