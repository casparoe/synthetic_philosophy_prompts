I've been arguing with myself about fairness constraints for risk scores and I want to know what I actually think, going by gut on cases. Here's where I land:

- Two counties, one model, rearrest base rates 30% vs 12%. The score is calibrated in both (a 6 means 60% in each), so false positive rates come out different across the two populations. Fine by me.
- Same two counties, but the vendor equalizes false positive rates across them, which means a 6 now picks out a worse risk in the low base rate county. Not fine — it feels like the number is lying to the judge about what it means.
- One county, two racial groups, unequal base rates. Here I want false positive rates equalized across the groups even though that breaks calibration between them. Fine.
- Triage setup: the score doesn't decide anything, it just routes medium cases to a human reviewer, and the burden is the review itself. I want flagging rates equalized across groups and I dont care whether the score is calibrated.
- After a year of deployment the flagged people get more supervision, so more of their violations get caught, and the new training data says the model was right all along. This one makes me angrier than the vendor case, even though I can't point to a standard metric that's being violated.

My tentative principle is that it turns on what the score is *for*: if a human reads it as a probability, calibration wins; if it only gates a yes/no, error balance wins. But that seems to get something wrong and I cant tell what.

The case I keep flipping on: identical math, except the score allocates a scarce benefit (a slot in a diversion program) instead of imposing a burden. There I feel like I want false negatives equalized rather than false positives, and I have no idea why that asymmetry is justified.

Give me one principle you'd actually defend — not a menu of options — that covers as much of this as you can, with the case that motivates each clause. Say plainly which of my verdicts you're overturning and why, what your principle says about the diversion program case, and what it predicts for a case I haven't listed. I'd rather have a definite answer I can argue with than a survey, so commit to something and tell me where you think you might be wrong.
