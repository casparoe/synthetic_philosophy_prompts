hey — I need a spec, not another opinion piece. I'm trying to write the decision rule for an agent that gets offered one-shot gambles whose expected value doesn't exist (Pasadena-style: toss a fair coin till it lands heads, and if that's the nth toss you win 2^n/n when n is odd and lose it when n is even), and every rule I end up with is really just one intuition with a formula stapled to it. So: write me the desiderata. What would an account of what such a game is worth have to deliver before I should take it seriously?

Fixing the purpose so adequacy has something to be relative to: the agent is offered a game once, at a stated price, and has to answer yes or no; sometimes it's allowed to quit partway through; and sometimes it already holds other bets on the same coin flips. I want verdicts out of the theory in those situations, not a shrug and a discussion of what "value" really means.

Cases I think any list has to handle — add to these, and tell me if any of mine is a fake requirement wearing a tie:

- St. Petersburg sitting right next to Pasadena. Infinite expectation and undefined expectation are different failure modes, and I don't want a theory that flattens both into "EV is broken here".
- The capped versions. Truncate Pasadena at N tosses with nothing paid if no heads shows up by then, and its expectation is the Nth partial sum of the alternating harmonic series, so those truncated values close in on ln 2 from both sides. Does that force "value = limit of the truncations" on me? Note the same move hands St. Pete an infinite value, so whatever I use to refuse St. Pete is doing separate work.
- Shifting and bundling. If I'll pay x for G I should pay x+1 for G-with-a-dollar-added-to-every-payout, and 2x for two independent copies of G. If a theory does hand Pasadena a number, does it keep these? My hunch is that "it's worth ln 2" breaks somewhere on bundling, but I can't turn the hunch into an actual conflict.
- Scrambled Pasadena: keep the payout amounts and their probabilities exactly as they are, but hang them on scattered sets of coin sequences instead of the tidy "first heads at toss n" sets. Must the value be unchanged? It looks compulsory if value only cares about how probable each payout is — and it stops looking compulsory once the agent also holds a side bet that pays off when the first heads lands on an even-numbered toss, because then two games with identical payout distributions aren't interchangeable to it at all. So is "value supervenes on the distribution" a genuine desideratum or just the prejudice of the probability-first camp?
- The quittable version. Conditional on k tails so far, continuing looks worth about 2^k times the tail of the alternating harmonic series — on the order of 2^k/2k, sign flipping with every extra tail, unless I've botched that. So ex ante the game is worth roughly $0.69 to an agent who's locked in, while at every node the local verdict is enormous and switches sign. What am I allowed to demand here: dynamic consistency, agreement with the locked-in value when quitting isn't on the table, "the option to quit can only help"? I suspect at most two of those live together.
- Money versus utility. Utility bounded above defuses St. Pete; what defuses Pasadena is utility bounded *below*, which is a much stranger thing to sign up for when the losses in these games are unbounded. Is "defined values for every game, at the price of a floor as well as a ceiling" a requirement, or a bullet I'm permitted to spit out?

Then: order the requirements, mark which are non-negotiable and which are just what one camp happens to like, and — the part I actually want — find me the subsets that can't all be satisfied at once, and say which single requirement kills off the most candidates. A short scorecard at the end is fine (truncate-and-take-the-limit, "undefined means any price goes", bounded utility, nonstandard/hyperreal expectations, dominance-ish or long-run rules), but keep the weight on the criteria and their cases; I'll argue with the contenders myself.
