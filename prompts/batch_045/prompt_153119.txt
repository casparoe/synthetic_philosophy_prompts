I'm running a reading group on the question of whether the shift to agriculture was a mistake — you know, the idea that foragers were the original affluent society versus the famous worst-mistake verdict everyone's already heard of, plus all the gradualist pushback saying nobody ever chose farming so calling it a mistake is a category error. Eight of us, mixed undergrads and grad students, one 90-minute session, and I'd rather spend it arguing than listening to me summarize. So please build me a taxonomy rather than an essay.

What I want is a decision tree where every branch node is a single claim someone can say yes or no to, phrased so they don't know which named position the answer walks them into. Six or so branch questions, eight to ten leaves, and each leaf gets a short name (or "unnamed" if nobody actually stands there) plus the one thing a person at that leaf has to swallow.

The branch questions I specifically want in it:

1. What the scoreboard is — leisure, health and stature, workload, infectious disease, inequality, or something like total capacity to do things nobody could have done before. Also whether it's the transition itself or the resulting steady state that gets scored.

2. Whether "mistake" needs a chooser. One person in the group will definately keep saying that if it was slow, unintended, partly accidental, with no decision point anywhere, then mistake-talk is just confused. I want that as a real branch, not as a conversation-stopper.

3. The nonidentity problem. Basically everyone alive after the Neolithic exists because of the Neolithic, so on a person-affecting view is there anybody who got made worse off? Make this a branch that splits "worse off than they would otherwise have been" from "badly off in absolute terms", and give me a leaf for the view that says no verdict is available at all.

4. Ratchet versus dimmer — this is the bit I care most about and I don't think it's in the literature under any name I know. Farming raised carrying capacity, so once the land supported more people, going back wasn't an option available to the people then alive: the forager option was foreclosed by population numbers, not by anyone's preference for grain. Call that a ratchet. A dimmer would be a change you can dial back down if it turns out badly. Put this in as a branch, because it seems to open up a verdict that is neither quite "mistake" nor quite "fine": every step locally sensible, the overall direction something nobody would have picked, and no point at which anyone had the option to stop. Give that leaf a slot even if it's unnamed.

After the tree: list the combinations your branches allow, mark which cells nobody occupies, and for each empty one give one line on why — incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unclaimed. Also flag any two leaves your tree can't actually separate. I suspect the "agriculture was a precondition for everything we value" leaf and the "more people, more total welfare" leaf both land on not-a-mistake but come apart somewhere; if they don't come apart anywhere your tree can see, say so instead of inventing a distinction. Finally, tell me which single branch question is doing the most work.

No need for citations or who-said-what — I've got the reading list already, I want the structure.
