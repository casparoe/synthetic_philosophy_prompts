hey so I need help writing something and im kind of in over my head. I'm a junior and I'm one of the two student reps on my school's Performing Arts Advisory Board — it's six people: Ms. Okonkwo (the drama teacher who basically runs everything), Mr. Adeyemi (assistant principal), two parents (Mrs. Traxler and Mr. Boone), me, and Priya, the other student rep. We pick what gets staged at the spring festival, we're meeting Tuesday night, and I'm supposed to circulate a short written opinion beforehand with an actual recommendation in it, and I genuinely dont know which way to go.

Here's the mess. Back in November we picked a one-act called "The Long Silence" by a local playwright named Dale Weyburn — it's about a family that decides not to tell their grandmother she's dying, she figures it out anyway, and most of the play is the stuff nobody says out loud. It's been in rehearsal since December, nine students are in it, and two of the seniors are counting on the festival footage for their scholarship audition tapes. Then in January Weyburn pled guilty to embezzling something like $180,000 from a hospice charity he was treasurer of. So: a guy who stole from dying people wrote a tender play about a family lying to a dying person. That's the bit the board can't stop arguing about. Mrs. Traxler says the play is now unwatchable and that staging it is the school putting its name behind him; Ms. Okonkwo says the play is twelve years old, it has nothing to do with what he did, the cast has worked for three months, and that the resonance actually makes it *more* worth doing — like the play is evidence he understood that situation and then chose to do the opposite of what the family in it does, which she thinks is interesting rather than damning. On top of that: the licensing fee is $900 and it goes straight to Weyburn, and Ms. Okonkwo's fix is to pay it and announce that the fee is being donated to the hospice he stole from, while Mr. Boone says that's worse, that its just buying forgiveness at a discount and makes the school the place that launders his reputation. Also the festival's ticket proceeds always go to a local hospice, so either we hand hospice money raised on the back of a thief's play or we quietly redirect the money and it looks like we're hiding something. Priya and half the cast want to stage it with a program note condemning Weyburn and donating everything; Ms. Okonkwo says a program note turns the play into a lecture and then nobody can just watch the thing.

The other reason this is spinning in my head: my sister is doing a philosophy minor at state and I asked her about it over break, and she gave me three distinctions I keep turning over — "content taint" (the work's own moral stance is bad, like a play that endorses cruelty), "source taint" (the work is fine but you can't un-know what the person did and it spoils the experience), and "transaction taint" (what *we* do by staging it — the money, the platform, the honoring). She also described something she called a replacement test: if a totally blameless person had written the exact same script, would the play be any worse or any different? If not, the badness is all in the person and none of it is in the work. But that feels wrong to me here, because the play does feel different to me now even though not one line of it changed — so either the test is broken or I'm mixing up two different questions, and I cant tell which.

Both Ms. Okonkwo and Mrs. Traxler are going to read it and both will spot immediately if I've just picked a slogan, so the reasoning has to be the kind of thing they could actually argue with and respond to. Please also say which of my sister's three taints (or something better) ought to drive a decision like this, and tell me straight up whether the replacement test is a bad test, because I've been using it as my gut check for two weeks and now im not sure about it at all.
