The two positions, as they themselves would describe them:

Camp GS (graduated status). A system's moral status is a matter of degree, and the weight of the reasons it generates is a continuous, increasing function of that degree. Degree of status is itself composite, with at least three separately variable components: affective intensity range (how bad or good things can feel at the extremes); temporal depth (how far a valenced state's reach extends, via memory and anticipation, into the system's past and future); and evaluative bandwidth (how many distinct objects a system's concern can take at once, and how finely it discriminates among them). No component is lexically privileged; more of any of them, other things equal, is more status, and there is no point at which reasons switch from absent to present.

Camp CT (constitutive threshold). There is a constitutive condition on being a moral patient at all: being a persisting subject for whom things can go well or badly, a unified locus of valenced experience. Systems below that line may have functions, information, self-monitoring, and nociception, but nothing matters to them, so no direct duties are owed to them; there are only derivative reasons not to damage or destroy them. Above the line the threshold stops doing work: variation in intensity, depth, and bandwidth changes how much a patient's reasons weigh, but not whether deontic constraints and the standing duty of non-maleficence apply to it.

Cases both sides must handle (treat these stipulations as fixed; do not revise them):

- The Spike. A system with extremely intense momentary aversive states and zero temporal depth: no trace of any state survives into the next, no anticipation, no learning across episodes.
- The Drone. A system with uniformly shallow affective intensity but very great temporal depth and wide evaluative bandwidth: it carries its concerns across long stretches of time and discriminates finely among many objects, each of which matters to it only slightly.
- The Hive. Two hundred thousand individuals, each individually just below the CT threshold, jointly realising a colony-level control system whose own integration is high and whose own trajectory persists for decades.
- The Dimmer. A single individual whose affective intensity range can be continuously attenuated toward zero and continuously restored, with no other change to its psychology.
- The Crowd. Ten thousand individuals, each comparable to a member of the Hive, set against one individual at full human-level intensity, depth, and bandwidth, where the intervention can help either the ten thousand or the one, and the per-individual gain is equal.
- The Thermostat. An ordinary household thermostat. Both camps agree it has no moral status; they are expected to disagree about what explains that.
