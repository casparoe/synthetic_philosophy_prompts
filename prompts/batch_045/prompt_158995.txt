I'm spending the summer as a research assistant on a monograph about mechanical rigidity — the type case being the digger wasp that drags its paralysed cricket to the burrow, leaves it on the threshold while it goes in to check the nest, and, if an experimenter moves the cricket a few inches in the meantime, comes back out, drags it to the threshold, goes in again, and repeats the whole cycle dozens of times. My supervisor wants the notion to do real work at the boundary between mere behavioural flexibility and the kind of agency we are willing to call free, so we are coding a long list of cases into a manual, and I have to write the case note for next week's meeting in advance. Her verdicts so far, with the reasons she recorded: the wasp, sphexish ("restarts the routine from step one every time; it is running a chain, not aiming at a result"); the greylag goose that goes on performing the egg-rolling movement after the egg has been removed, sphexish ("same signature — the behaviour outlives its own point"); a rat in a maze whose usual shortcut is blocked, not sphexish ("reorganises the route within a trial or two"); a fixed-depth chess program, not sphexish, with the note that determinism is not rigidity ("its play is exquisitely sensitive to the position, and it enumerates options it then rejects"); a long-haul driver who covers a familiar stretch with no later memory of it, not sphexish but flagged ("she braked for a deer that ran out; on our interruption diagnostic — interrupt a routine midway and alter something, then see whether the system resumes at the interrupted step or restarts from step one — this is resume, not restart, so the loop is closed even with attention elsewhere"); an adult with Tourette syndrome and a vocal tic, no verdict at all ("no task-directed routine, so the label does not apply"); and a woman with severe OCD who rechecks a lock twelve times while knowing each time that it is locked, sphexish ("restarts from step one and is deaf to her own testimony — the wasp's signature exactly, whatever else is true of her").

The case on the table next week is one I proposed myself. Nadia came out of a bad period of obsessional re-checking fifteen years ago and adopted a strict "decide once" policy for purchases and bookings: she researches carefully, commits, and then never looks again. Last month she booked a flight; an hour later her partner found the identical itinerary for four hundred euros less and told her so, and she declined even to look at it, on the grounds that reopening would start the old cycle again and that the policy is worth more to her than the money. She is calm rather than distressed about it, and when pressed she says that if the difference were four thousand euros she would reopen, and that she has in fact reopened once, years ago, when someone pointed out she had booked the wrong date — so her loop is closed to price information but not to information that she got the object of the decision wrong.

What will my supervisor code here — sphexish, not sphexish, or no verdict — and what should she code if the manual is to stay consistent with the cases already in it? I would like the rule or rules her track record is compatible with set out explicitly, what each of them predicts about Nadia, your best guess with the reasons for preferring it, and a statement of which earlier case is the closest analog, because I keep going back and forth between the lock-checker and the truck driver. I would also like the single further case you would put in front of her to separate the rules that survive. And if the precedents really do leave this open, tell me what the manual would be committing itself to either way — in particular whether "sphexish" is meant to track something about the control structure of a routine or something about whether the agent can act for and revise her conduct in response to reasons, since those two seem to me to come apart precisely in a case where the closure of the loop is itself something the agent chose.
