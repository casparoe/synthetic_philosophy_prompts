So I got put onto this app where you get matched with one other person for a single anonymous round — no chat, no rematch, nothing carries over. You each tap either SPLIT or TAKE. Both tap SPLIT and you each get $600. Both tap TAKE and you each get $100. If one taps TAKE and the other taps SPLIT, the TAKE one gets $1000 and the other gets $0. The money is small enough that I'm happy to just maximise the average payout, I dont care about risk.

The odd part is the matching. Before the round you fill in a long questionnaire about how you make decisions, and they pair you with whoever scored closest to you — same profile, same instructions, same payoffs, same screen, shown at the same moment, and no way to communicate. On the match screen they show you two numbers: people matched this way pick the same option 72% of the time, and across all users about 70% of taps are SPLIT.

My flatmate says its a no-brainer: whatever the other person taps is already decided by the time I tap, and TAKE beats SPLIT either way — 1000 beats 600 and 100 beats 0 — so all the matching business is irrelevant noise. My other mate says the 72% means I am basically deciding for both of us at once, so I should just tap SPLIT.

I dont think either of them has actually done the arithmetic. What should I tap, and how much does that 72% really buy me? And how far off would those numbers have to be before the answer flipped?
