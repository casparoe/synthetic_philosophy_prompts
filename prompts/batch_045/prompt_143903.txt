Hey — I teach a large intro ethics course (around 400 students, twelve TAs, most of them grad students from other departments who've had one philosophy class between them), and I also write the hypothetical cases that go on the exams. So I knock out maybe thirty short cases a year and then reuse them for years, which means a squishy one keeps doing damage long after I've forgotten why I wrote it.

What I want is a procedure a TA can run on a case in fifteen minutes, before they teach it, that ends in one of three outputs: keep, fix (with the minimal edit actually specified), or retire. The failure mode they need to catch is a case that looks like it isolates one feature but is really doing its work with something else — a vivid detail nobody notices, an underdescribed bit that every reader fills in with the same default, an outcome that's disgusting rather than wrong, the "and nothing else changes" clause smuggled in without being said. My TAs can't be expected to know any literature, so: no names, no citations, no "it depends on your theory of X."

One wrinkle I care about: I use these things for two different purposes. Sometimes a case is pure rhetoric — I want the room to *feel* that arrival time can't matter, and I'd happily keep a case that's a bit loaded if it teaches well. Other times I'm using the judgment as evidence, claiming the response actually tracks the distinction rather than the framing. I suspect those want different failure tests, but I want one checklist, not two.

Three from my folder to run it on: (1) a pharmacist with one leftover dose of a scarce drug, two patients who'd benefit equally, one arrived eleven minutes earlier, the other is a nurse at the same hospital; (2) a lab with de-identified tissue samples, ethics committee approval but no donor consent, which yields a screening test that saves hundreds; (3) two hikers who drop a lit cigarette into identical dry pine needles — one goes out, one burns down a house — meant to pump that our blame tracks luck and so is incoherent. The third one has opened my responsibility unit for six years and I'll be annoyed if it dies.

Commit to an actual procedure: which tests, in what order, what counts as passing, and where the TA stops and hands it to me because the call is mine. Don't hand me a menu of options.

Sections, please: "The procedure" (~700 words), "Three cases" (~500 words), "Where it misfires" (~250 words).
