I do interview-based work on worker co-ops and a couple of religious mutual-aid funds, and I keep hitting the same thing in my own material and I dont have a word for it. Maybe somebody does.

The thing: I offer a causal explanation of why a group behaves the way it does, in terms of the values or the beliefs the members actually hold — and let's stipulate the explanation is correct, and that the members would more or less endorse it if you asked them straight out. But it looks to me like a lot of these explanations are true only for as long as the people inside them havent heard the explanation. The value does its causal work behind their backs. Once they've read it and gone "oh, right, that's why we do that," the thing I was explaining either falls apart or turns into something else.

Two bits of fieldwork. In one co-op people covered the grim Friday-night shift on a completely informal rota, and my account was that a norm — that it would be slightly shameful to ask for extra money for it — was doing the scheduling. Then someone circulated a write-up of mine and over a few months the rota broke down. Nobody started asking for money. It broke down because after reading it, each turn felt like a conscious act of norm-following, which made it feel optional, and the minute it felt optional people started negotiating. Second case, a church mutual-aid fund: the opposite happened. After the members read an account of why their practice holds up — something about reputational pressure in a face-to-face community — the practice got more rigid and much more loudly performed, because now it was an identity badge people had to avow out loud. Same shape of explanation, opposite outcomes, and in both cases the thing I said was causing the behavior stopped being what was causing it.

And I cant patch it by adding "provided they are unaware of this explanation" as a background condition, because publishing it is precisely what removes the unawareness. Its not like a ceteris paribus clause that fails in the wild sometimes; it fails because the explanation succeeded.

What makes this annoying is that some explanations get *stronger* when the actors learn them: tell people they all stop at red lights because each expects the others to, and either nothing changes or the coordination gets better. So there's a real difference between the ones that survive their own publication and the ones that don't, and I have no idea what marks it.

Things people have suggested that don't quite fit, in case it saves you time: (a) a self-defeating, or "suicidal," prophecy — as I understand it that's a prediction about a future event, whereas mine is an account of a pattern that already exists, and a failed prediction is simply false while mine was true; (b) the fact that people act differently when they're being studied — in both cases above they read the write-up long after I'd left the field, nobody was watching; (c) the crowding-out thing where you start paying people for something they used to do for free and the motivation dies — no payoff or incentive changed in either case, only what people knew; (d) the ideology-critique move where the actors were wrong about their own motives — my explanation is one they'd happily agree with. And (e) "reflexivity," which just names the whole mess and tells me nothing.

So: is there an established concept for this exact thing — a causal explanation in terms of values whose truth is destroyed by its own uptake — and if there is, where are its edges? I especially want the contrast with the publication-stable cases, and I want to know whether this is supposed to be a problem for the *explanation* or for the *evidence* (can you ever really test one of these claims, if testing wrecks the setup?). If there is definately no term for it, coin one and define it tightly enough that I can drop it into a methods footnote and know what doesn't count. Please don't send me off to a reading list — I'd rather have the carving than the references.

Context if it helps: this came out of me trying to work out what I think about the old Weber argument about Protestants and capitalism, where the causal story seems to depend on people not understanding themselves in those terms. But I'm after the general thing, not a Weber discussion.
