Hey, I need a piece of satire for a small zine I put together: a mock city-council consultation paper, about 650 words, from a fictional city that has decided to take seriously the idea that everyday conventions are genuine moral obligations and that visitors are bound by the local ones. Give me a draft byelaw in numbered clauses with a deadpan explanatory memorandum underneath, civil-service voice throughout, no winking and no "of course this is absurd" tells — a reader should be able to tell what's being ironised from the content alone.

The jokes have to come from getting the philosophy right rather than from silly names. The stuff I want landing: that queueing, tipping and crossing the road are all arbitrary solutions to a reccuring coordination problem (nothing about arriving first, or about some particular percentage, is morally special in itself, yet people moralise about it furiously); that what there's reason to do is match whatever everyone else is doing rather than perform that particular act; that the obedient tourist standing at an empty crossing can be the one causing the coordination failure; that where tips are doing the work of a wage, the non-tipper's wrong is really about the wage and not about the convention at all; and that a visitor who takes no benefit from the queue, or who simply declines to participate and leaves, looks like they owe nothing — which the council cannot afford to concede.

Also please invent one doctrine the committee comes up with to plug a hole in its own position: "substituted compliance," letting a visitor discharge the local obligation by performing their own country's equivalent instead. Let it quietly wreck the byelaw: if a bow or a handshake can settle the debt, then what bound you was never the specific local convention; if it can't, the council is just demanding deference. Close with the jurisdiction section, where "who counts as a visitor" (three days? three years? the delivery driver?) comes apart. No real names anywhere, and don't quote anything.
