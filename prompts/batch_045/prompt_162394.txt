I'm writing a novel that's basically all two-hander arguments — a husband and wife, a lab pair, a composer and her former student — and I want each character's implicit epistemology of disagreement to stay consistent across the book even though nobody in it ever says anything philosophical. So help me build a decision tree I can use as a casting sheet: starting from a character who's in the situation where the other person seems about as smart and about as well-informed, neither can find the flaw in the other's reasoning, and they've both said everything they have to say, take me down to a named position on what that character should do with their confidence.

Every branch should be something I can dramatize in a scene, not a claim about evidence I'd have to narrate. A few I want used: whether the character moves when the other person has one extra bit of information the character can't inspect; whether they move when the other person is a real expert in a *different* field; whether they move when they learn the other person got there by a route the character thinks is unreliable but can't actually show to be wrong; and whether any of it changes when the subject is politics or taste rather than, say, a shared bank balance. Leaves should include at least the equal-weight conciliationist view, the total-evidence steadfast view, and a permissivist version where both people are allowed to just hold fast.

Keep it to ten branch points or fewer, invent a case or two of your own if it separates things better than mine (but it should be stagemanagable in a room with two people in it), and flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really tell apart, plus any combination of answers nobody seems to hold and why you think that is.
