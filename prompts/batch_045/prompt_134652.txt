Take your time and think carefully; do not start drawing anything yet. First do a short scratch brainstorm I can delete later: four to six independent axes along which our problem word can vary, written down before you sort anything. Then build the tree on top of those axes, then run all eight test posts at the bottom through it and tell me where it breaks.

Context: I help moderate the Great Lakes Native Plant Swap, ~12k members, forum plus Discord, mostly home gardeners, some restoration volunteers, a handful of nursery people who sell in the marketplace channel. Our problem word is "native". It is a copycat concept in the worst way: it got lifted out of restoration ecology where it does fairly precise work (tied to a baseline, to a place, and to a claim about how the organism got there), and in our server it has become a fluid concept that mostly carries a halo. Saying "this is native" is how you signal you are on the right side of an argument, and the actual criteria have quietly detached. So people talk definately past each other, and we mods cannot tell whether a post is making a checkable botanical claim that could just be wrong, smuggling a value judgement (purity, belonging, anti-nursery, anti-cultivar) in botanical clothing, or not really making a claim at all.

What I want: a decision tree a mod can actually run on a single post in the queue. The angle is moderation, not botany: we do not want to police vocabulary, we want to sort posts into (1) claims that could be empirically wrong, (2) value claims wearing a lab coat, (3) pure halo use with no content, (4) uses that will land as identity-exclusion to other members whether or not the poster meant it. Only some of those we act on, and each leaf should end in a disposition plus one line on why.

Requirements:

- Every branch node is a concrete case or a yes/no question about a case, never a label and never a question like "do you care about local genotypes?" Someone should not be able to tell from a node which leaf it leads to. First split should probably be whether "native" in the post attaches to something that could be a plant taxon at all, since we get it applied to compost methods, gardening styles, and vibes.
- 10-14 branch questions, 6-9 leaves max. Each leaf: a name in our vernacular, one sentence on the most awkward thing it commits you to, and a disposition.
- Leaves must include: the baseline purist; the no-human-transport view (if a human carried it, it is not native here); the local-provenance / genotype view; the ecological-function or novel-ecosystem view (who cares where it came from, look at what it does now); the cultivar-tolerant straight-species view; the deliberately vague marketplace-label view; the belonging/identity sense where the word is doing cultural or even ethno-nationalist work whether the poster intends it or not; and the deflationary view (retire the word, say locally adapted / right for the site). If two of those are really one position, merge them and say why.
- Build it from logical space, not from the list of names: after the axes, sketch the grid of combinations the axes allow, mark which cells the named positions actually occupy, and for the empty cells give a one-line diagnosis each — incoherent, unattractive, or just unoccupied because nobody has thought of it yet.
- Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot really separate, and flag every place where the right classification depends on the reply context rather than the post itself. That matters a lot: our queue shows one message at a time, so if a leaf needs surrounding context, say what the mod should do instead.
- Use these cases from our archives as branch material wherever they bite: a named cultivar of a species that is on the state native list, bred overseas for shorter stems; a savanna whose pre-settlement baseline only existed because people set fires; a naturalized flower that is doing fine for the pollinators; starts brought in a bucket from the same continent but 800 miles east; a marketplace listing that says native and locally collected and knows nothing else; a plant that walked north on its own in the last decade as its range shifted; and a use where "native" is a pure prestige adjective attached to something that is not a plant at all.
- Do not worry about getting the botany right. If one of my cases is botanically confused, fix the case and keep the diagnostic point.

Then, separately, a six-question version we can pin for new members so they can find out which sense they have been using all along: concrete cases, two or three options each, scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the senses, written so the options do not give away the labels. Note anywhere the quiz key and the mod tree disagree about what counts as the same position, since one sorts people and the other sorts posts.
