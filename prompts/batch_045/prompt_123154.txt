Hey — I run a tiny type foundry with one other person, and our main product is crowdsourced handwriting fonts: contributors draw their letters on a tablet, we capture stroke order and direction, and we ship the result for embroidery machines, where each glyph has to be one continuous stitched path (our machines can't do a jump without a thread trim, which customers hate). So two different questions come up dozens of times a week and I need to stop answering them by gut: (1) is this thing an 'a' at all, and (2) is it an 'a' in *this* contributor's hand, given that we only ever see one or two exemplars of their 'a'. My cofounder thinks the bowl-plus-stem double-storey shape is what makes an a an a and that single-storey ones are just circles with a stick; I think the load-bearing part is more like the counter and the way the bottom-right terminal flicks off, and that stroke order matters more than the outline. We're not gonna settle that between us, so I want a procedure that either picks a side out loud or routes around the disagreement.

What I need is a checklist a part-time contractor with no typography training can run in about two minutes per glyph, in a fixed order, ending in accept / reject / kick it to me. Say what counts as a sufficient answer at each step (the contractor shouldn't have to guess what "looks right" means), where to stop early with a no, and which steps are really judgment calls dressed up as tests — mark those as such. Also say plainly what the procedure can't do.

Then run it on four cases:

- a double-storey 'a' made one-stroke by doubling back over the bowl, so there's an overstitched segment
- a single-storey 'a' submitted into a family where every other exemplar is double-storey
- an 'a' whose bowl is barely closed and merges with the family's 'o' when it's stitched small, like left-chest-logo size
- one that clears every test but is basically a traced Helvetica 'a': right letter, single path, nothing of that person's hand in it, and I want to reject it

If your procedure accepts that last one, say so and tell me what I'd have to add to catch it.
