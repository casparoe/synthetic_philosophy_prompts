I teach a first-year seminar on the ethics of memory, and I want to screen Pixar's *Coco* alongside Avishai Margalit's *The Ethics of Memory* and David Rieff's *In Praise of Forgetting*. Give me a reading of the film that takes all three seriously: I don't want the film reduced to an illustration, nor either philosopher bent to fit it.

Three things I want worked through.

First, the film literalizes a metaphysical claim: in the Land of the Dead the unremembered undergo a final death. Is that Margalit's claim? His duty to remember is grounded in thick caring relations, yet the film treats the vanishing of Héctor's friend, for whom no one is left to do the remembering, as a real loss, and it shows a whole forgotten quarter of anonymous dead waiting to disappear. The film thus seems to need a duty of far wider scope than caring can underwrite. Is that a defect in the film, or a pressure point in Margalit?

Second, the mechanism of rescue: Héctor is saved when Coco — who is herself losing her memory — is moved by the song, not when she comes to believe the true proposition about who wrote it. So the film appears to hold that what discharges the duty is affect rather than true belief, and that the duty can be discharged vicariously, Miguel remembering in Coco's place. Call the background assumption the dependency thesis: the dead depend on the living for their continued existence, so a duty to remember entails a right to be remembered. Assess it. Does Margalit's insistence that memory is a relation rather than a record survive vicarious remembering, and does the film thereby make a right to be forgotten unintelligible?

Third, the ending: the family's ban on music is revealed as an enforced forgetting born of resentment and is plainly condemned, but the exposure of Ernesto de la Cruz and the collapse of his public memory is an orchestrated collective forgetting that the film endorses. Is the distinction between forgetting as resentment and forgetting as sanction available to it, or does Rieff's point — that what gets remembered and what gets erased is settled by whoever wins — swallow both cases, given that here the winners happen to be in the right?

Close by saying what, if anything, the film adds to the Margalit–Rieff dispute that argument alone doesn't, and flag the places where the film is simply confused rather than illuminating.
