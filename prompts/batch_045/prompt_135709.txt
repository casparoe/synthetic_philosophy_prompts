my dads got vascular dementia and the family has been going in circles about when exactly he stopped being able to make his own medical decisions. every single step looked like nothing when you were standing on it, and yet somewhere along the way we started signing things for him and no one can point to the appointment where that flipped over. i basically live inside the sorites now, and i want to end our next family meeting with something short instead of another argument.

so: write me a poem, 16-20 lines, no set rhyme scheme, that does the paradox rather than naming it. i want it to erode — drop a word or phrase each line, grain by grain, so the reader watches something go while never being able to say which line was the one where it went. and the erosion should carry the actual ideas without turning into a lecture: the pull of the intuition that no single small change can be the one that matters; the option that theres a sharp cutoff somewhere we just cant ever locate; the option that some of these sentences are simply neither true nor false; and the nasty part, where if you try to say which cases are borderline you now need a borderline between the clear cases and the borderline ones, and it turtles down from there.

a couple of things im not sure about and want you to use your judgment on: whether eroding a poem is really vagueness at all or just identity over time wearing a vagueness costume, and whether the law just going ahead and drawing the line anyway — capacity tests, age thresholds — is discovery or stipulation. work in whichever of those you think belongs.

then give me 3-4 lines of prose on where the poem cheats or leans on one contested reading of the puzzle, because i dont want to hand my brother a view that just happens to be somebodys favorite. and if the whole setup is muddled — if the poem-thing and the dad-thing are different puzzles and ive stapled them together, or the bit about the law is beside the point — say so straight out rather than writing me something pretty that hides it.
