I sit on a constitutional-reform commission that has had to rule on a series of proposed Ulysses-contract devices — clauses by which one legislature tries to bind its successors. Our record so far:

- Approved: a rule requiring a two-thirds vote plus a thirty-day cooling-off period before any emergency declaration can be renewed, itself adopted by two-thirds.
- Rejected: an unamendable clause shielding a statute from repeal forever, inserted by ordinary simple-majority statute rather than through the amendment procedure.
- Approved: delegation of monetary policy to an independent bank with staggered terms, whose governing statute is itself amendable only by two-thirds.
- Rejected: a balanced-budget rule in ordinary statute that any later simple majority may waive at will.
- Approved, with conditions: automatic across-the-board spending cuts that take effect unless the legislature affirmatively votes to opt out within sixty days.
- Rejected: an irrevocable commitment to international arbitration with no withdrawal clause, ratified by simple majority.

The new proposal is a permanent transfer of authority over a named policy domain to an independent commission with life-tenured members, with no domestic route to recover the power — but adopted properly, by the ordinary two-thirds amendment procedure.

What should we decide, if we want to be consistent with ourselves? I can read our record two ways: as the rule that a genuine precommitment must always leave the future polity some route back, however expensive — which rejects this proposal — or as the rule that irreversibility is objectionable only when a bare majority or an executive is what tied the successors' hands, so that a supermajority may bind absolutely — which accepts it. Both readings fit every verdict above, including the sham constraint we rejected. Tell me which reading you think we are actually running, what each predicts here, which precedent is the closest analogue, and what further case would force the difference into the open if this one does not.
