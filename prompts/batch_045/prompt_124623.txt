I'm writing a paper defending a qualified version of functionalism, and before I commit to it I want a map of its exposure. The view, briefly: whether a mental kind is multiply realized is not a bare modal fact but a fact relative to a tolerance threshold. Say that a kind M is multiply realized at tolerance τ just in case there are two physical kinds, P1 and P2, that both satisfy M's causal-role specification to within τ. Specify the role tightly (τ small) and you get few realizers, perhaps one; specify it loosely and you get arbitrarily many. So the multiple-realizability argument against type identity is only ever as strong as a substantive claim about which τ is the right one. My further claim — the part I most want tested — is that the right τ is not fixed by the metaphysics of functional definition at all, but by the grain at which a mature neuroscience actually taxonomizes when it explains causally. Call it tolerance-indexed realization.

What would have to be true for this to be false? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and distinguish what a critic would say from what a defender of the view should concede. I'd particularly like you to address:

- Is the tolerance-indexed framing doing real work, or is it just the familiar point that realization claims are grain-relative in fancier notation? What does it predict that plain multiple-realizability arguments don't?
- Which cases can it not describe well — the silicon-organism and alien intuitions, the liberalism/chauvinism bind, the disjunction problem, the exclusion-style worry that the realizer does the causal work.
- There is a familiar empirical line of argument that multiple realizability has been oversold, because neural taxonomies across species are far finer-grained than philosophers' stock examples assume. Suppose that line succeeds broadly. How much of my view survives, and does it survive as the same view or as a rival?
- If τ is set by whatever neuroscience happens to do, has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability? Is that fatal for a claim of this kind, or acceptable, and why?
- What I personally would find most persuasive: a case where one and the same mental kind is realized by states we have independent reason to treat as genuinely different at the causally relevant grain — not merely different under some finer description. Say whether such a case actually exists in a form that bites, or whether every candidate quietly shifts the grain.

Don't pad with citations; argue from the considerations themselves. Finally, say how confident you are in each of your main claims and single out the one you'd be most willing to retract, with a note on what would settle it.
