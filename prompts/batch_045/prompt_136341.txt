My brother and I are splitting up our dad's stuff after he died and we've hit a wierd disagreement about his sailboat, and I keep going in circles on it.

Dad bought a wooden sailboat in 1974. In 1998 he took it completely apart to restore it — hull planks, ribs, mast, the lot — numbered every piece and put them in crates in his barn. Then he lost interest for about eleven years. He started putting it back together in 2009 and finished in 2012, and he ended up replacing roughly a third of the pieces because they had rotted, reusing the rest. It's been in the water ever since, same name on the stern, and the registration was never cancelled — one continuous piece of paper the whole time.

The will says the boat goes to me if what's in the water now is "the boat I bought in 1974," and to my brother if it isn't. My brother says that's obvious: the 1974 boat stopped existing the day it became a pile of numbered boards, and what got launched in 2012 is a new boat built partly out of the old one's leftovers. I say it's the same boat, Dad always talked about it as the same boat, and the paperwork says so. And before you reach for the usual thing about the ship where every plank gets swapped out one at a time: this feels different to me, because here there was a stretch of eleven years when there was nothing boat-shaped anywhere.

I don't much care about winning, but I'd like to know whether there's a right answer, because the argument keeps turning into stuff neither of us can settle. Three things especially:

1. Does the gap do any real work? If Dad had taken it apart and rebuilt it the same summer, I'd of said same boat, and I think my brother would too. So is his position really about the eleven years, or is he sneaking in something else — like that the thing in the barn wasn't a boat at all? But if it wasn't a boat during those years, then what was it, and where did the boat go in the meantime?

2. Say he'd never finished it and the crates are still sitting in the barn today. Is his boat, right now, a stack of numbered boards? My brother says obviously not, and I sort of agree, but then the gap does seem to matter and I can't say why it should. Would eleven minutes be different from eleven years? Is there some number of years where it stops being the same boat, or is that just a question with no answer?

3. And honestly: even if there is a correct answer about which it is, should we care? My brother says Dad was a man who just wanted us to split things fairly and that we're both being idiots, and part of me thinks he's right but that it also feels like a dodge — like he's saying that because he thinks he's losing the actual argument.

So: who's right, about the boat and about whether it matters? Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — your actual verdict — and then give me the reasoning. No preamble, no restating my question back at me.
