Since I began volunteering at a wildlife rehabilitation center last spring, I have found myself thinking constantly about the sheer amount of suffering that nonhuman animals endure in the wild—parasitism, starvation, disease, and predation all seem to be ubiquitous features of life outside of direct human control. This has made me sympathetic to the idea, which I have recently started reading about in my spare time (I am a biology undergraduate, not a philosopher, and I find the ethical literature somewhat confusing), that wild animal suffering is a serious moral problem and that we might have reasons to intervene in nature to reduce it. However, when I raised this with a friend who works in conservation, she reacted with impatience and said that we cannot go around trying to police nature, because, as she put it, nature is just “Nature, red in tooth and claw.” (I later learned that she was quoting a line from Tennyson’s In Memoriam (1850), where the poem has the speaker say “Tho' Nature, red in tooth and claw” and then “With ravine, shriek'd against his creed—”.) Her tone suggested that the matter was settled: since nature is by its very character a realm of violent struggle, there is no point in—and perhaps no obligation toward—trying to make it less brutal. I am sure there is something too quick about this, but I cannot tell exactly what.

Could you help me disentangle the slogan? In debates about wild animal suffering, people often repeat the phrase “Nature, red in tooth and claw” as if it settles whether we should try to help wild animals. Please lay out, as a menu, the different precise claims this one-liner could be making: for example, is it merely saying that wild animals sometimes suffer, that suffering is a pervasive and unavoidable feature of ecosystems, that this suffering is natural and therefore not bad or not our responsibility, that intervention would be futile or would make things worse, or something stronger still? For each reading, say whether it is trivial, whether it is a substantive and defensible empirical or moral claim, and whether it is actually false. Then tell me which of these readings my friend most likely had in mind, and whether that reading really supports her conclusion that we have no reason to intervene to reduce wild animal suffering. Finally, because I am trying to figure out how much weight to give your answer, please state how confident you are in the disambiguation as a whole and single out the specific point—whether a particular reading, verdict, or attribution—that you are least sure about.
