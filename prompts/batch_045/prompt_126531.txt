My friend and I spent about three hours arguing about whether the laws of nature *govern* what happens — you know, whether the difference between "F = ma" and "every gold sphere is less than a mile across" is that the first one somehow makes bodies behave — and we got nowhere, partly because we kept finding we agreed about things we'd assumed we didn't and disagreed about things that didn't line up with the Humean / anti-Humean label at all. So I want something better than the label fight for the reading group I run (mix of grad students, a couple of advanced undergrads, one physicist, one person who's never done metaphysics).

What I want is a decision tree, not a survey. It should start from a commitment both of us already accept — say, "accidentally true generalizations and laws aren't distinguished just by how many instances they have" — and route you through roughly eight to ten branch questions down to a named position. Every branch node has to be a concrete case or claim that someone with no métaphysics background could answer in a sentence, and it should be phrased so you can't tell which leaf it leads to from the wording. (So no "do laws necessitate?" — that's just asking for the label.)

Here are cases I want used, some of them made up, so it's not just the same three thought experiments everyone writes about:

1. A universe containing exactly one particle that exists for a while and decays once, with nothing else ever happening. Is there a law there, and if so which one?
2. A world where a neat dynamical regularity holds perfectly across a huge region for a trillion years and then, with no change in the underlying state that anyone can point to, just stops holding. Did the law change, was it never a law, or was it violated?
3. Two candidate systems of generalizations that are exactly as simple as each other and exactly as informative, but disagree about some region nobody has looked at. Is there a fact of the matter about what the laws are?
4. A generalization that's a one-liner in one vocabulary and an unreadable mess in an equally good one. Are the laws of that world language-relative?
5. A law about a kind of particle that never actually gets instantiated in that world — never fires, never manifests.
6. A world where something that looks like energy conservation seems to sit *above* the dynamical laws — it holds across variations in which the first-order laws are different, and it seems to constrain which candidate first-order laws are even up for consideration. Is that the same kind of thing as the first-order law?
7. A world just like ours but one electron does something forbidden, exactly once, with no hidden cause. Did the law fail to govern, was it false, or what?
8. Two worlds with identical histories of goings-on, where in one the regularity holds *because of* some deep structure and in the other it holds by coincidence. Does that "because" make a difference to what the laws are?
9. A case where the interesting question is what the laws say about an antecedent that's physically impossible in that world.
10. A world whose best system is irreducibly probabilistic.

Leaves: I want the tree to have somewhere around seven to nine occupied leaves, including a Mill–Ramsey–Lewis-style best-system Humeanism, a contingent-necessitation-between-universals view (Armstrong-flavoured), a dispositional-essentialist / powers view, primitivism about laws, a non-governing non-Humean view (Lange-flavoured, laws as counterfactually robust but not pushing anything around), a "no fundamental laws, just patched-together nomological machines / capacities" anti-fundamentalist view, and a nihilist or fictionalist leaf. If I've garbled who holds what, relabel with a description and a question mark rather than arguing with me — I care about the cells in the space, not the name-tags.

Two things I care more about than the leaves themselves. First, build it from the logical space: give me the questions and which combinations of answers are actually available, then locate the real positions in that space, and flag at least two or three combinations that are *empty* and say why nobody's there — incoherent, unattractive, collapses into a neighbouring cell, or just nobody's bothered. Second, be honest about where the tree fails: which pairs of leaves it can't separate (I suspect sophisticated Humeanism and the non-governing non-Humean view are going to look awfully similar on my cases, and I won't be surprised if the governing views all converge), which branch nodes are really just restating what the leaf already says, and which two nodes are doing all the work (the ones where, if you deleted them, the tree collapses into a list).

And please push back where I deserve it. If "governing" is a red herring and the real disagreement is about something else — explanation, counterfactuals, what a law has to be *for* — say so plainly and rebuild around that instead. If one of my ten cases secretly begs the question, or both sides just answer it the same way, tell me and cut it; I'd rather hand out seven real nodes than ten where three are decorative. Mark clearly where you're reconstructing a position rather than reporting one, and tell me what sort of case would have to exist to split two leaves you can't currently pull apart.
