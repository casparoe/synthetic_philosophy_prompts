I'm writing my term paper for a graduate seminar on rational choice, and I want to defend a reading of Buchak's risk-weighted expected utility theory (RWEU) that I can't yet argue for cleanly. Set the framework up as we did in class: for a prospect whose outcomes are ranked x₁ < … < xₙ, with upper-tail probabilities Sᵢ = P(outcome ≥ xᵢ) and S_{n+1} = 0, the value is Σᵢ [r(Sᵢ) − r(Sᵢ₊₁)]·u(xᵢ), where the risk function r is increasing on [0,1] with r(0) = 0 and r(1) = 1. Setting r(p) = p recovers expected utility; a convex r (so that r(p) < p, shifting weight onto worse-ranked outcomes) produces risk aversion with no concavity in u at all.

Here are the two claims I want defended together. First, interpretive: r does not encode an attitude toward risk — a desire-like taste for or against uncertainty — but a weighting of the importance of gains at different ranks: a kind of intrapersonal prioritarianism, on which a unit of utility in one's worse-off live possibilities counts for more than a unit in one's better-off ones. Second, structural: if that is what r is, then risk-weighting applies recursively — at each node of a decision tree, over the distribution of continuation values at that node, folding the tree backward — rather than once, to the terminal distribution of an entire plan. The motivating thought is that priority is contrastive: it is a relation within the set of possibilities still live at a node, so it has to be re-applied as information eliminates possibilities.

Please argue for this package, and answer the two objections I keep running into.

(i) The recursive reading gives up reduction of compound gambles, which the global reading gets for free, and makes a prospect's value depend on how the uncertainty is represented. Take r(p) = p² and u(x) = x: a prospect that with probability ½ yields a sure 40 and with probability ½ yields a 50–50 gamble between 0 and 100. Reduced to a distribution over 0 (¼), 40 (½) and 100 (¼), I get 26.25 under the global reading; folding the two-stage tree back, I get 40 and 25 weighted 0.75/0.25 at the outer node, i.e. 28.75. Same prospect, two values. Defend that — either by arguing that the timing of resolution is a real feature of the situation rather than a representational artifact, or by some better route. Correct my arithmetic if it is wrong.

(ii) Say why the recursive reading is neither vacuous nor collapsing: does it degenerate into expected utility maximization with a reshaped u, into something close to risk-neutrality when the tree is finely grained, or into the global reading when the tree is coarse? Tell me where on the space of representations it bites hardest, and whether that is a virtue or a vice.

If you think r belongs at choice nodes but not at chance nodes, adopt that variant and say how it changes the argument. Open with a single sentence stating the spine of the argument, then defend (1) and (2) and reply to (i) and (ii). Please keep the whole answer under 700 words: I want one tight argument, not a survey.
