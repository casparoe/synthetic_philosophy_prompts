Hey — I'm the undergrad rep on my university's Academic Planning Committee and I've somehow ended up drafting the recommendation memo for Friday's meeting. I'm also taking a metaphysics seminar this term and the two things have collided in a way I wasn't expecting, so I thought I'd ask here.

The case: the Dean of Social Sciences wants to turn the Centre for Quantitative Social Science (CQSS) into a School of Data and Society (SDS). Our handbook says a "substantively new academic unit" needs a full external review before it can be approved, but a "rebranding or internal reorganisation" only goes through the light track — one-page filing, no external reviewers, no waiting a year. The Dean wants the light track. On the facts: same staff, same building, same budget line in year one, but also a new undergraduate degree, a new dean post, and four new tenure lines phased in over three years.

Six faculty wrote in objecting (two are philosophers, and one of them is my seminar prof, which is awkward). Their claim is that SDS is a genuinely new unit and that filing it as a rebranding is a way of dodging scrutiny of the hiring lines and the deanship. The Dean's reply is basically: nothing factual is in dispute, everyone agrees about who moves to which office and what the money is, so this is a purely verbal dispute and there is nothing here for the committee to decide.

Two things I found when I actually read the file, which I don't think the Dean has addressed:

1. There's a donor offering money restricted to establishing "a new school of data and society," and the gift lapses if the unit isn't new. So the word new is doing legal work, not just PR work.
2. There is already something on paper called the Data Science Initiative — a website, a mailing list, and one faculty director with a 5% course buyout, no budget and no students. It has never been through any review. If a thing like that counts as an existing unit, then almost anything can be said to already exist; if it doesn't, I'm not sure what would.

My prof's line in seminar (she keeps going on about something called quantifier variance? I may have that wrong) is roughly that questions like "is it really the same unit, does the school really exist" may not have a fact of the matter at all, and the actual question is which way of carving things up is better for our purposes. That feels right to me but I don't know what to do with it, and I really don't want to hand in a memo that amounts to "it's all just words, light track it is," because the Dean would quote that at us for the next decade and I think the objecting faculty have a real point buried in there.

Can you draft the memo? It should:

- recommend one concrete course of action — light track, full review, or something conditional (e.g. we stipulate a definition of new unit and apply it, or we split the question somehow) — with a timeline;
- be written for the actual committee, which is the Dean, a physicist, an anthropologist, the CFO, and me, so it can't lean on seminar vocabulary;
- treat the strongest version of the side it comes out against honestly, not as a strawman.

Roughly 700–900 words is right. Please don't just conclude "this is a verbal dispute" and stop — tell me what we should do on Monday. And no citations or reading suggestions, I can't use them and the committee won't read them.
