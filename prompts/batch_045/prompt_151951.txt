I've been defending a position I call exposure-window realism about generational cohorts. The claim is that generations — Millennials, Gen Z and the rest — are genuine, projectible social kinds rather than convenient decade-slices laid over a continuous gradient, and that what makes them genuine is this: each cohort is unified by having passed through a formative exposure window, roughly ages fourteen to twenty-four, during which it encountered a distinctive bundle of technologies, institutions and macro-events. That shared exposure, not the birth years as such, is what does the causal-explanatory work behind the correlations we observe. On this view a cohort's edges are in principle discoverable rather than merely stipulated, cohort membership explains rather than only predicts, and the familiar objections — that different institutions draw the same cohort's boundaries several years apart, that apparent cohort effects are hopelessly confounded with age and period effects, that generational labels are media artifacts which then shape the very people they sort — are all answerable.

I want to know how exposed the view actually is. What would have to be true for exposure-window realism to be false?

- Give me the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward. I have in mind the off-window test (if people who meet the same event bundle a decade later acquire the same signature attitudes, the window is doing no special work and the view collapses into a cumulative-dose model); the age–period–cohort identification problem turning out to be unsolvable in principle rather than merely hard; and cross-national variation so large that the same nominal cohort picks out nothing stable outside a handful of wealthy societies. But tell me which of these really bite, and what the view predicts that a pure period-effects or cumulative-dose rival does not.
- Which of them should a defender concede as counting against her, as opposed to dismissing as attacks on a straw version of the position?
- If the view survives mainly by reclassifying discordant cases as edge-fuzziness around a protected core, has it been qualified into unfalsifiability? And for a view about a kind whose members partly self-classify under the label, is that insulation a defect or an honest feature of the subject matter?

For what it's worth, the evidence I would personally find hardest to shrug off is cross-national: if one and the same nominal cohort shows the same attitude signature in very different societies, or conspicuously fails to, I won't know how to read it.
