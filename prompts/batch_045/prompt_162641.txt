I teach a third-year seminar on knowledge and mind: 18 students, one 75-minute session, and I don't want to lecture that day. Design a runnable classroom game that makes them feel the force of the dispute about whether knowing how to do something is really just a species of knowing that something is the case — before anyone in the room names the dispute or hears the words intellectualism and anti-intellectualism.

My thought is a hidden-rule classification game. Students learn to sort items as admissible or inadmissible under a rule they are never told, and the payoff is accuracy on a transfer set of unseen cases. The variable I want manipulated is transmission: one half acquires the skill by practice with feedback only and is forbidden from writing hypotheses down; the other half is handed an explicit written specification of a comparably hard rule and applies it with no practice. Both then face the transfer set, and both are asked to state their rule in writing and to teach it to a newcomer whose accuracy score counts double toward their own — so that transmission, not just performance, is what pays. I also want a short condition where participants classify while doing a demanding secondary verbal task, and one where they classify while doing a demanding secondary motor task, with predictions written down before the reveal.

Give me the rules, materials, the payoff scheme without money, a minute-by-minute timeline, and predictions for each condition. Then the debrief: map each moment onto a live crux — whether know-how is transmissible by testimony alone, whether skillful action needs propositional guidance, whether failure to articulate is failure to know a proposition or merely failure to say which proposition one knows, and what a view that treats know-how as an ability, or as a relation to a way of doing the thing, would predict here. Be explicit about what will predictably go wrong the first time: rules that turn out to be too easy to state, cheating, too few transfer items to be legible, and effects that may simply fail to appear. Finally, be frank about which feature of the dispute the game cannot reproduce at all — in particular how a defender of the propositional view absorbs the dissociation between doing well and saying what one knows — and say what I will have to supply myself.
