Eighteen months ago I stopped drinking, after roughly fifteen years of drinking in much the way my brother still does. He has not stopped, and his drinking is now the main thing damaging our mother's health and the family's ability to be in a room together. At a family dinner last month I told him, in front of everyone, that he was being selfish and that he needed to stop. He said: you did exactly this for fifteen years, so you don't get to say that to me. I said that was beside the point. Since then I have been trying to work out whether I actually believe that, and I have written down what I take myself to think about blaming. Before I say anything else to him, I would like to know whether it hangs together.

(1) Whether my brother is blameworthy is a matter of facts about him and about what he did — whether he knew what he was doing, whether he could have stopped, what he owes our mother. Nothing about me is relevant to any of that. So his tu quoque does nothing as a defence of his drinking or of him; it shows nothing about whether he deserves blame.

(2) Standing to blame is a separate question, and a genuinely moral one rather than a matter of politeness or tact. So the very same fact — that I did the same thing — can be worthless as a defence of him and at the same time a real complaint against my having blamed him. When you blame without standing you wrong the person you blame; the complaint belongs to them. I keep feeling that (1) and (2) cannot both be right, since on (1) nothing about me bears on his blameworthiness while on (2) something about me makes it wrong to blame him. But I cannot locate the inconsistency, and I suspect I am confusing two questions.

(3) The explanation I find most compelling for why hypocrisy costs standing is that the hypocrite does not really accept the norm she invokes. She is deploying it as an instrument against someone else rather than as a standard she takes herself to be bound by. Standing, on this view, tracks whether you are genuinely committed to the norm you are trying to enforce.

(4) I nonetheless think standing is lost in the case where that explanation plainly fails: someone who sincerely accepts the norm, applies it to himself, feels real guilt about failing it, and keeps failing out of weakness of will. I still want to say he has no standing to blame others for the very thing he is struggling with.

(5) I think standing is *not* lost in a case that looks identical from the outside and is worse by the record: the person who did the thing for twenty years, has genuinely stopped, and now warns someone else off it. Given (3) and (4) I would expect her record to count against her at least as much as the weak-willed blamer's present failure counts against him; I do not think it does. This is my own situation, so I may simply be rationalising, and I would like that possibility taken seriously.

(6) I think a preface repairs it. If I tell my brother plainly that I am not claiming higher ground, that I did exactly what he is doing and know how hard it is to stop, and that I still think he should stop, then my standing is largely restored — even though nothing about my record has changed and the demand I am making is the same demand. I also do not see why this should work only for people who have already stopped, which may be a problem.

(7) Two verdicts on cases where my own conduct is not the issue at all: I take myself to have standing to blame my brother for what he is doing to our mother, even though she is the one being wronged; and I take a stranger with no relationship to any of us to have considerably less standing than I do, despite having cleaner hands than mine.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it, ordered by how severe the tension is? For each real tension, say what I would have to give up and what that would cost me elsewhere in the set; if a tension can be resolved by keeping both claims but abandoning something I have not stated, say what that is. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and what I would end up having to say to my brother if I made it.
