hey — I need a poem for the last session of my self-reference unit and I'm hoping you can do better than anything I'd manage myself.

Context: I run Hofstadter's tangled-hierarchy material — the bit where a system's levels fold back into each other and there's always an inviolate level, the one outside the system that actually does the holding-up and which the system can't depict from inside — alongside Escher's Drawing Hands (1948 lithograph: two hands rising out of one blank sheet, each holding a pencil, each drawing the other into existence, neither one privileged, no way to say which is the real hand and which is the drawn one). Then I pair it with the bootstrapping problem in epistemology: a source or method that seems to certify its own reliability, and the easy-knowledge worry that you can't get a justification for a process just by running the process.

The discussion always ends the same way — someone says "so its a paradox, lol" — and the structure gets lost. I want to close with something that makes them feel the structure rather than the vibe.

Form: I'm imagining a pantoum, quatrains where lines 2 and 4 of each stanza come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next, and the last line of the poem is the first line again. The rotating repeats make the poem itself into a tangled hierarchy: no line sits above any other, each is held up by the ones it holds up. If you've got a different form that does this better I'm open, but tell me in a sentence why.

The part I actually care about: the inviolate level has to be *in* the poem without being *of* it. So I want exactly one line that isn't recycled from anywhere and never comes back — a single seam where the maker's hand enters. It should be audibly in a seperate voice from the rest (the poet talking, not the drawn thing), and it should be the line that makes the loop possible while not being part of the loop. For Escher the inviolate level is his own hand, outside the picture plane, drawing both.

Keep it in the image, not in the abstractions. What I don't want in the poem: the word paradox, the word recursion, the word loop, and anything that lands on "isn't it funny how self-reference is weird" or "everything is interconnected, man." What I do want a reader to walk away with: the system looks like it floats free, but something outside it is holding it up, and you can't get that from inside.

Also, if you can get something out of the cuff — the bit of sleeve where each hand emerges from the sheet, the place where a hand stops being a hand and turns into drawing — that's the crux of why there's no bottom level, so I'd like it in there.

Bonus, not required: if the last stanza can do double duty, so that someone who also did the epistemology week hears the same structure in a belief source vouching for its own reliability, great. But the hands come first and it can't turn into a lecture.

Around 24 lines, don't sweat syllables, just keep the line endings clean enough that it scans as verse. thanks!
