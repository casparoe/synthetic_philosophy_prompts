I work on a team that writes animal-welfare guidance for insect farming — mealworms, black soldier fly, that kind of thing — and I keep having to make calls that feel like they need a number attached: how much the life of a beetle matters next to the life of a pig, how many beetle-deaths a pig-death is worth, at what point a rearing method stops being acceptable and starts being a problem. I've ended up convinced of what I call the dimmer-switch view: moral status isn't a switch that flips on once some capacity is in place (a global workspace, a self-model, whatever your favourite candidate is), it's a quantity that varies smoothly with whatever the underlying capacity turns out to be, so there's no fact of the matter about where the line falls, and a nematode with a few hundred neurons has some nonzero, tiny status while a thermostat has something like none but not in a metaphysically dramatic way. I also hold a second thing that I think follows from that, and which my colleague Priya says is actually inconsistent with it: the ramp is not a discount rate. On my view you can't just sum a thousand beetle-pains into one human-pain and trade them off, because below a certain region the reasons these creatures generate aren't the kind of thing that aggregates — call it non-aggregability below the fold. Priya says that if status is genuinely a matter of degree then arithmetic should apply, and if arithmetic doesn't apply I've smuggled a threshold back in and my view collapses into hers.

So: what would count against the dimmer-switch view as I've described it, including the non-aggregability bit? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want it clear which ones other degree-theorists would themselves concede as counting against them, as opposed to which they'd dismiss as resting on a misunderstanding of what the view says. Some things I specifically want dealt with rather than just listed: (a) the build-up argument — if I add channels of integration to a simulated nervous system one at a time, each step looks negligible, so either I get from thermostat to human by negligible steps (the sorites) or I have to say that some particular step matters, which is a threshold; I've been saying that "matters as much as" just isn't transitive, and Priya says that denying transitivity is conceding incoherence, so I'd like to know who's right about that; (b) the phenomenology of lines — some cut-offs (at birth, at species membership, at the moment of death) don't feel arbitrary to us in the way a smooth ramp seems to predict they should, and I genuinely can't tell whether that's evidence against me or just psychological noise; (c) the collapse worry — maybe "how much moral status" is ill-posed, and what there really are are several different statuses (can suffer, has a future it can be deprived of, can be wronged as opposed to merely damaged, can be held responsible), so that my single scalar is really a vector and degree-talk is shorthand for a bunch of separate questions, in which case do I have a distinct view or just a sloppier version of that one; (d) the legislative alternative — that there's no answer to how much a beetle matters until we stipulate a scheme, and the schemes are chosen rather than discovered.

I'd also like you to say what a real observation would do to it: if someone built a credible consciousness-meter and it came back with sharp cut-offs, or if a dial-down experiment on a simulated nervous system showed a discontinuity in some behavioural or neural signature, would I have to give the view up, or would I just reinterpret the result and carry on? And please give me an honest verdict on how exposed the view actually is — has it been qualified into something unfalsifiable, and if so, is that a problem for a claim of this particular kind, given that it's metaphysical and normative at once? The thing that would most move me personally is a case where I have to make an actual decision and the ramp view yields literally no guidance while a threshold view hands me a decision rule; tell me whether that's a fair thing to demand of a view like mine or whether I'm asking the wrong kind of question of it. I'd rather be told the view is basically insulated than be handed a list of defeaters that don't really bite.
