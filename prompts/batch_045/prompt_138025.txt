hey all — I'm in a game theory & society seminar this term and I keep running into a pattern that I dont have a word for, and it's driving me a little nuts.

The setup is the familiar one: a bunch of parties are stuck spending more and more on something that only matters relative to what everyone else spends, nobody can quit unilaterally without loosing ground, so the total just climbs. I know that part has a name and I'm not asking about it. What I keep noticing is what happens when someone actually tries to cap it. Two things, and I'm not sure they're even one thing:

(1) The spending doesn't go away, it relocates. Cap or ban the obvious margin and people compete on whatever you forgot to cap, or on whatever is hardest to measure — and the new spending is often worse than the old, in the sense that it doesn't even buy anyone an advantage anymore, it's just cost.
(2) It doesn't reset. Lift the cap, or remove whatever started the race, and the level stays where it climbed to. The new margin of competition sticks around even when nothing is making anyone compete on it anymore.

Admissions is my clearest case: kill one screening device and the competition reappears somewhere else, more expensive and less informative, and it doesn't unwind afterward. But I think I've seen the same shape in hiring requirements and in how labs chase benchmarks, and "credential inflation" only names the admissions version.

So: is there a name for the relocation-plus-not-resetting thing, or am I mashing two separate phenomena together? And if there's no established name, coin one with an actual definition and give me boundary cases — I need to be able to tell it apart from a plain arms race, from a measure getting gamed until it stops tracking anything, and from ordinary stickiness in prices or wages. Rank how close each of those near-misses is, and tell me which features of my description each one fails to capture. Please no reading lists or paper recs, I want the carving and not the sources. American English, under 300 words.
