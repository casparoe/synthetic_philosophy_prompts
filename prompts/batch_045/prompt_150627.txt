Hi — I have a slightly odd job and I need something operational out of it. I'm on the small methods team at a big interdisciplinary environmental research institute (ecologists, economists, GIS/modelling people, engineers, some policy folks). When two subgroups have been deadlocked for weeks over some word and it's blocking something real — a shared database schema, a joint paper, a work package in an EU grant — they get a 45-minute slot with me, which we call term triage. I run maybe three of these a month. I have no prep time (I usually get the word and a one-line description the morning of), the people in the room are senior domain experts with zero philosophy and are visibly annoyed to be there, and my deliverable is a one-page note that says one of three things: "you genuinely disagree about X, here is what data or analysis would settle it"; "you don't disagree, go build the thing"; or "this isn't a question evidence can settle, escalate to the steering committee." So I need a decision procedure I can run live, in the room, in about thirty minutes of talking, and that terminates.

I know roughly the shape of the philosophical material here, and I want it turned into steps rather than explained to me again: the test where you strip the contested term out and re-describe the situation in neutral language and see whether the disagreement survives, versus the test where you ask whether the parties would actually disagree about any case, actual or hypothetical. I've noticed those two come apart, and I want the procedure to say which one to run first and what to conclude when they disagree with each other. I also want somewhere in it the point that a dispute can be perfectly substantive while not being a dispute *about the world* — that the fight is over which concept or which way of counting should occupy the slot the word is sitting in, i.e. a practical/normative question about which vocabulary to build the project on, not a factual one. That distinction matters a lot for my one-pager, because the "which concept do we commit to" cases should go to the steering committee and not to a data request. Four things I keep running into that I don't know how to handle and that I'd like the procedure to have an answer to: (1) *one-sided paraphrase* — one subgroup can restate its claim fine without the word, the other one can't or won't, so the elimination test only fires in one direction; (2) *load-bearing terms* — where the word is doing real work inside somebody's model or regulatory framework, so substituting a neutral description loses inferential content even though everyone agrees on the cases; (3) *strategic vagueness* — someone benefits from the term staying blurry (it's in the grant text, or it holds two factions together), so they won't commit to a definition under any prompting; (4) the nasty one where after all the cases, actual and imagined, both sides say "yes, I'd describe it the same way as you" and *still* won't agree that the other side is right. Please also mark honestly which steps are judgement calls wearing a procedure's clothes, and where I as a non-expert outsider am simply not entitled to declare a dispute merely verbal even though my tests say it is.

Cases I'd like run through it, in the note: (a) ecologists and an ecosystem-services economist arguing about whether a restored wetland is "degraded" — the ecologists mean departure from a pre-drainage baseline, the economist means reduction in the flow of things people actually value, and a monitoring protocol depends on which wins; (b) the ML subgroup and the clinicians arguing about "bias" in a mortality-prediction model, where the statistical reading (disparate error rates across groups) points at one fix and the clinical reading (miscalibration in a specific subgroup) points at a different, partly competing one; (c) a case where I've already got a bad feeling — after everyone agrees on every case about "resilience" in a coastal infrastructure model, the two factions are still fighting, and my suspicion is that the honest output of your procedure will be "this is not verbal, but it's also not answerable by any study; the real question is which operationalisation you want to be stuck with for five years, and that's a committee decision," which is exactly the note nobody wants from me. For each, show where the procedure stops, what I write down, and one sentence I can actually say out loud in the room. Finally, tell me what this procedure can't do — what kinds of deadlock it will mislabel even when I run it perfectly.
