I am giving a paper next week at our university's astrobiology seminar, and I would like a Q&A preparation sheet rather than a defence of the view. My thesis is that the search for a real definition of life should be abandoned in favour of a purpose-relative pluralism: the term alive functions as a cluster concept, and the operative criterion legitimately shifts with the inquiry — metabolic self-maintenance and compartmentalisation for the working microbiologist, self-sustaining reaction networks for the origin-of-life chemist, and roughly a self-sustaining chemical system capable of evolution by natural selection for the mission scientist who needs a remotely detectable biosignature. My one constructive move is that the virus dispute is not answered but dissolved: the real question is which entity in the viral cycle is the organism, the extracellular particle or the infected cell that manufactures new virions, and once that is disambiguated the rival verdicts stop contradicting one another.

The room will be: two virologists who regard the status of viruses as an empirical question close to settled and who want criteria that track laboratory practice; two origin-of-life chemists who take metabolism to be the primary phenomenon and replication a later consequence of it; a member of a biosignature working group who cares only about operational criteria and false positives; and three philosophers of science, one of them a realist about natural kinds who will think pluralism abandons the explanatory ambitions that made the question interesting in the first place.

Give me the eight or so objections I should actually expect from these people, ordered by how much damage they would do, each phrased in the vocabulary of the person raising it, with a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is softest. Tell me which concession I should make at the outset and which single reply has to land if the view is to survive. Build in how I should handle the mule case — an organism uncontroversially alive that does not reproduce — and prions, and say whether I should concede in advance that my account has nothing to say about computer viruses or self-replicating machines. Finally, give me the one question I should hope nobody asks.
