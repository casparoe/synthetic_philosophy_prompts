Hi — I need a flowchart, not prose. I'm putting together a one-page handout for my MA seminar on iterated knowledge, and I want students to be able to start at the top, answer seven or eight questions, annd land on a named position about whether knowledge is luminous. I know the literature, so please don't give me citations or a reading list; what I want is the instrument itself.

What I keep finding is that my students' disagreement is really three disagreements stacked on top of each other, and I want the tree to seperate them rather than let them blur:

(a) whether there are blind-spot cases at all — cases where someone knows p but can't tell that she does;
(b) whether knowing p requires a margin for error, i.e. requires that you'd still get it right in nearby cases, or whether knowledge can be fragile;
(c) whether a failure at the second order somehow infects the first-order knowledge — whether not knowing that you know is supposed to erode the knowing.

So I want those as three distinct branch regions, with cases doing the work. Every node should be a concrete case or a plain claim a student can have a view about without knowing where it leads — nothing like "do you accept the KK principle?" or "are you an externalist about knowledge?", which only sort people by which label they've already picked up. Please use at least these three cases somewhere in the tree: a room warming up so slowly that you can't tell when you stopped being cold; someone who does quick mental arithmetic and is right maybe ninety percent of the time but genuinely can't tell which ten percent she botched; and someone who was taught a fact years ago, has no idea whether she still remembers it, and then answers correctly when asked.

I also want the tree to treat knowing that you know and knowing that you don't know as separate branches rather than as one question with the sign flipped — my sense is that the cases which generate blind spots in one direction don't generate them in the other, and I'd like the handout to make students take a stand on each.

Deliverable: eight questions max, each with two or three options, then a set of leaves. I need at least these leaves: full luminosity of knowledge; global anti-luminosity built on a margin requirement; restricted luminosity, where some designated subclass of propositions is luminous and the rest isn't; a fragile-knowledge view that blocks the margin premise; a view on which it's justification or rationality that's luminous and knowledge that isn't; and something like a change-of-subject view, on which the right target was never knowledge but assertion, inquiry, or what you're permitted to act on. Name the leaves where a name is standard and mark them "unnamed" where one isn't.

Two more things. First, there's a combination I think might be empty and I want you to check it: someone who grants the blind-spot cases and grants the margin premise, but denies that you therefore fail to know that you know, on the grounds that the margin requirement is weaker at the second order — what has to be stable across nearby cases when you know that you know is your reliability about p, not the truth of p twice over. If that cell is empty, tell me whether it's empty because it's incoherent or just because it's unattractive, and say briefly why. Second, flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually seperate — I'd rather the handout admit that than pretend the questions cut finer than they do.

Finally, one line per leaf on what the position commits you to about the practical question I actually care about: may you just go ahead and assert p, or act on p, in a case where you can't tell whether you know it?
