I'm writing my term paper for a seminar on social ontology, on what it is for a group to act together, and I keep running into the same pattern: someone points at a single case — a duet, a mob, a committee — and claims it refutes the leading account. Before I add another case of my own, I want the specification. So: what would a theory of joint action have to get right to count as adequate — not adequate in general, but adequate for the job I need it to do, which is to underwrite attributions of obligation and blame to groups: who owes what to whom when a collective project goes wrong, and when one participant may rebuke another for not doing their part.

Give me the requirements list — the cases it must handle, the distinctions it must not collapse, the results from elsewhere it must not contradict — with the consideration behind each requirement. The cases I most want covered are: mere convergence versus acting together; joint action under divergent private ends; a group that settles on an outcome no member individually endorses, as when a decision procedure aggregates votes into a result everyone voted against; the dissenting and the coerced member, who seem to be inside the first-person plural yet not bound in the same way; improvised joint action where roles are invented mid-course with no prior plan and no communication; coordination brokered by an algorithmic system no member understands, where no member grasps the whole plan and the convergence is nonetheless theirs; and long-running collectives whose membership turns over completely, where I am unsure whether the same we persists. The distinctions I don't want collapsed are: shared intention versus shared belief or acceptance, the group's act versus the members' acts, intending-together versus each intending that we do it, and joint action versus the joint causal production of an outcome nobody aimed at.

Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable given that purpose and which are only the preferences of one research programme, such as reducibility to individual attitudes. Most importantly, flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied: I suspect that demanding coverage of loose, dispersed, partly unwitting collectives pulls against demanding that joint action carry the distinctive normative residue — entitlement to rebuke, obligation to renegotiate, rather than merely to desist — that is supposed to separate it from parallel action, and I want to know whether that is a genuine trade-off or just my confusion. Finally, say which single requirement does the most work in excluding otherwise plausible theories. One line each on the plan-based, we-intention and joint-commitment families is enough; keep the weight on the criteria.
