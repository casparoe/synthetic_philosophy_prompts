Write a satirical document of roughly 700 to 800 words: an orientation handbook issued by a university Department of Cosmology and addressed to newly assembled Boltzmann brains — observers that have just fluctuated out of thermal equilibrium, complete with a full set of apparent memories, a working nervous system, an office, colleagues, and an expected remaining lifetime of a fraction of a second. The Department has been required to produce the handbook by its own auditing standards, on the grounds that a cosmology on which fluctuation-observers vastly outnumber ordinary ones is treated as disqualified, and that the Department can therefore remain accredited only by maintaining that readers like you do not exist.

The voice should be the deadpan institutional earnestness of an HR onboarding packet or a government pamphlet: headings, numbered sections, a couple of footnotes, reassuring bureaucratic cadence. Every section must sound genuinely useful and be quietly undercut by the handbook's own premise, without ever acknowledging that anything is amiss — no winking, no authorial asides, no punchlines that name the joke.

The humor must come from the real structure of the argument rather than from invented absurdity. Specifically:

1. The typicality reasoning that makes your existence bad news for the Department, and the Department's resulting interest in your not being counted.
2. The fact that your memories — including your memory of reading this handbook, and including whatever evidence you take yourself to have for the cosmological theory in question — are thermal noise, and so cannot be evidence for anything, including the claim that you are a fluctuation. Handle this self-undermining feature carefully: it has to damage the argument without the handbook ever admitting that it does.
3. The measure problem: nobody can say what the ratio of observers like you to observers like us is until a cutoff over an infinite or arbitrarily long-lived spacetime is supplied, and no one has supplied one. The handbook should nonetheless proceed as though the ratio were well defined.
4. The fact that the handbook's own instructions presuppose a stable, law-governed, moderately low-entropy world of the sort your existence counts against.

Required sections: a Welcome; a brief "What We Know About You," including the expected lifetime and the reason the Department is unable to confirm it; "Your Memories"; "How to Report a Suspected Fluctuation," whose procedure cannot possibly work; "Your Rights," in which the rights are meaningless for something that will not exist in a second; and a closing exhortation to continue with your research and your teaching as though you were an ordinary observer, which is where the satire should land hardest. Do not state the reductio anywhere — the reader should feel it.

Constraints: keep the physics honest, so that a working cosmologist winces in recognition rather than at a distortion, while remaining legible to a reader with no physics background. Do not attribute views to named authors, cite or allude to specific papers, or quote technical results from the literature; where a figure helps the joke, make it plainly invented. No citations, references, or bibliography of any kind.
