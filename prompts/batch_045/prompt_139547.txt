Our campus sci-fi film club is doing Tenet next week and I got volunteered to lead the discussion, and I'd rather do something better than everyone saying time is weird. I want to read it against the philosophy of time's arrow: the thermodynamic arrow, the psychological arrow, and the usual claim that the second is explained by the first. So: does the film's inversion actually reverse entropy, or does it only run causal order backwards while leaving each inverted person's experience, memory and action glued to entropy in their own frame? Which reading makes the movie more coherent than it usually gets credit for, and where does it still break — fire, breathing, the demo where the bullet goes back into the gun, the highway chase? Two of our members pushed further and I couldn't referee them. One said the arrow stuff and that fatalistic refrain about the past being fixed are separate commitments: you could have inverted objects in a genuinely open future, and the film just smuggles determinism in on top. The other suggested thinking of inversion as what you'd get if the entropy gradient were a local field rather than a global one, so there could be pockets where it points the other way, and that the real question is what happens at the boundary between two such pockets — is that a usefull way to think, or does it just relocate the puzzle? Where would you say the film is genuinely doing philosophy of physics rather than running the footage backwards for cool shots, and which two or three scenes should we argue over?
