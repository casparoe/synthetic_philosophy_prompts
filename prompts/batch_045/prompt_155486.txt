Next week I'm giving a 45-minute talk at a mechanistic interpretability workshop, and the audience is the reason I'm nervous: it's mostly ML researchers (25ish) who think the Chinese Room is a dusty 1980s intuition pump that got settled by the fact that we now have systems that actually do the thing, plus two philosophers of mind who were invited as the designated opposition. One of them is sympathetic to Searle's causal-powers / biological line and thinks there is something genuinely missing in the room; the other is a hardline anti-intuition-pump functionalist in something like the Dennett mould, who thinks any argument that turns on "but does it *really* understand" has already lost the argument by asking the question. I dont think either of them will be convinced by the stuff that usually works on the other.

The thesis I'm defending is something I've been calling channel externalism about implementation. Rough sketch: what a system has or lacks isn't consciousness and isn't "real" semantics in the philosopher's sense, but what I call uptake — a system has uptake on a domain when its internal states are differentially sensitive to features of that domain in a way that would survive a perturbation of the channel, i.e. if you degrade or garble the input the system's behaviour changes in a way that tracks the specific way you broke it, and it can't be restored by better internal inference alone. Implementation, on my view, is signature-relative: whether a physical system implements a given computation-with-a-content depends partly on facts about the channel it's sitting in and who or what is maintaining it, not just on its internal state transitions. So: the man in the room has uptake on squiggles but not on Chinese conversations; the system as a whole inherits uptake from nowhere, because nobody inside is tracking the channel; and a model trained only on text about colour has uptake on colour *talk* and not on colour, at least until you hook it up to something. I like this because it lets me say the room is really missing something without signing up for Searle's biology, and without signing up for "if it produces the outputs it understands."
