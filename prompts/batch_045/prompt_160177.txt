I've been lurking rationalist blogs since the OB days and I'm finally in a comment-thread argument where I think I'm right but also suspect I might be the confused one, so please steelman the other side and then tell me where I went wrong.

Setup: we're arguing about whether a language model trained on nothing but text can be said to refer to anything at all. I claim Twin Earth basically settles it. The whole point of the thought experiment is that meaning ain't in the head — Oscar and Twin Oscar can be molecule-for-molecule identical, have the same dispositions to use the word, associate all the same descriptions with it, and still mean different things, because what fixes reference is the actual stuff in the speaker's environment. Generalizing: reference requires the right kind of real, causal-historical hookup between the speaker and the thing, not anything internal and not anything about how the speaker is disposed to use the word. A text-only model has no causal contact with H2O whatsoever, so its water doesn't refer to H2O — it refers to nothing, or at best to something in the training distribution. So all the talk about the model knowing or understanding things is a straight-up category error, and it's not a matter of degree that gets better with scale.

Two things make me nervous though. First, the obvious extension: if we're in a simulation then every thought any of us has is about sim-stuff, and there's definately no way to ever find out, which is maximally terrifying. But I have a vague memory that externalism was supposed to be the thing that kills skepticism rather than the thing that makes it unbeatable, and I havent been able to reconstruct why — something about how the skeptic's own sentence would have to refer to something? Second, someone said the model could just borrow reference from the humans who wrote the text, the way Putnam says you can mean elm without knowing the first thing about elms. But if that works, why can't Twin Oscar defer to his community and end up meaning H2O anyway? Which would seem to collapse the original argument.

Where did I screw up? Is the causal-historical hookup actually required by Twin Earth, or is that just one theory of what does the work — and does the deference move save the LLM case or break Putnam's?
