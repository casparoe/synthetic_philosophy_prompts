Im a journalist doing a piece on whether deontology has a wound it inflicted on itself, and I keep going in circles, so let me just ask for a number.

The version of the problem I mean — call it the aggregation trap: deontologists want both (1) you must not kill one innocent person even when that's the only way to stop five such killings, and (2) you must prevent killings when you can do so at little cost to yourself. But run a long sequence of those cases, or a whole population of agents each facing one. Every refusal is individually permitted, and yet the pair seems to license an outcome the theory itself counts as far worse; and it's unclear the two can be willed as a general rule without contradiction, given that universal adoption multiplies the very thing deontologists think matters most.

So what's your credence that agent-relative constraints really survive this — that there's a coherent, non-ad-hoc way to hold (1) and (2)? Not "it depends which deontology." A number, then the decomposition, each term with a number and one line: how much rides on whether doing/allowing, or the "not with my own hands" restriction, can bear the weight without smuggling in outcome comparisons; how much on whether the aggregation step is valid rather than an illicit slide from individual permissibility to collective optimality; how much on whether a contractualist or Kantian derivation actually delivers the constraint instead of restating it; how much on whether the baseline intuition survives debunking stories about personal force and emotional response. Combine them, then tell me which term your number is most sensitive to and which single argument would move it most.

One more thing, and it's the bit my editor thinks is the actual story. A philosopher I interviewed says the paradox is an artifact of camera angle: it shows up only if you describe the choice as five deaths versus one death, which is already the consequentialist's ledger; describe it as four people saved versus this person killed and (1) stops looking like it conflicts with (2). Call it frame variance. How much of your number is hostage to that move — and if frame variance is right, does it rescue the constraint or just dissolve the question? Im at 0.45 and genuinely can't tell which.
