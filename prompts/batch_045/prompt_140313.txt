My slot is on split-brain cases and the unity of consciousness.

I really don't want to stand at the front and talk at them, I want them to run into the thing. My prof keeps seperating access unity (roughly: what's available to the same reporting-and-control system, whatever exactly that cashes out to) from phenomenal unity (the felt togetherness of experiences at a time), and she also keeps insisting that "one mind" and "one agent running one body" are different claims again. Whatever you give me should make at least two of those come apart as something the participants feel while they're doing it, not something I announce at the end.

My own half-baked idea — tell me if it's any good or if it teaches something false: put them in pairs, one person is the "talking side" (can speak and write, but can't see what's presented to, say, their left) and the other is the "silent side" (can see it, can only point, push, or move objects around). Run a set of short tasks where the pair has to respond as a single person, then afterwards the talker has to explain why they responded the way they did — and they were never told what the silent side saw.
