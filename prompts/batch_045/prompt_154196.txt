I'm putting together my philosophical profile for a modal metaphysics reading group and want a consistency check before I hand it out. Roughly where I've landed on possible worlds:

(1) Lewis-style modal realism: teh other worlds are concrete, spatiotemporally and causally isolated universes, and "actual" is an indexical, like "here".
(2) The principle of recombination is unrestricted, so the plurality is wildly abundant — and I take that abundance to be most of why the theory earns its keep.
(3) Counterpart theory is false, though. When I say I could have been a plumber, that's about me, not about someone similar to me. I want genuine transworld identity, and I want essentiality of origin to come out as a plain de re fact about me.
(4) The payoff of the whole view is supposed to be that modality reduces to non-modal facts, and I want that reduction complete — no modal primitives smuggled back in.
(5) But I'm also Humean about laws (best-system style), and I'm happy to let a primitive natural/unnatural property distinction plus an interest-relative similarity ordering on worlds do the work in counterfactuals, in duplication, and in what counts as a law.
(6) And I want to insist, right alongside (1), that our world is the one that's really actual and the others are merely possible.

Which of these genuinely pull against each other, and which only look like they do? Rank the real ones by severity, say what would have to give in each case, and tell me which single revision cleans up the most. I'd rather not give up (1) or (2), but say so if I can't keep them.
