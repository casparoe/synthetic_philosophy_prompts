I'm a high school student on the math team, and a friend said something I keep arguing about: if you know the premises of a valid argument and know that it's valid, then you already know the conclusion — you just don't know that you know it. That struck me as obviously false, but every fix I try ends up looking ad hoc. So here are my gut verdicts, and I'd like the principle behind them, if there is one.

1. Ana knows that every multiple of 6 is even and that 48 is a multiple of 6, but has never considered whether 48 is even. I say she knows.
2. Same shape, except that getting from what she knows to the target fact takes thirty short steps she hasn't walked through — say, filling in a large logic-puzzle grid. I say she doesn't know. This is the pair I can't reconcile.
3. Ben knows the rules of chess and can see the board, where there is a forced mate in three he hasn't found. Doesn't know. He's a strong player and would spot it instantly if he looked — still doesn't know, though I do think he'd be unreasonable to say "I don't know." That makes me doubt that amount of effort is the real variable.
4. Cara has the ciphertext, the key, and the algorithm, and hasn't run the decryption. Doesn't know — but if she'd pressed enter and read the output, she would. That seems like a silly place to draw a line, and I want to know whether to accept it anyway.
5. Dev works out a product in his head and believes it truly: knows. Eve guesses the same number and happens to be right: doesn't know. So it isn't just true belief.

Is there one account that gets all five — something like closure under consequences that need no new information but do require you actually to take the steps, or a version where the budget of steps is set by how much the question matters? Which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? And what does it predict for two cases I haven't settled: a classmate who gives the right consequence when asked but contradicts himself when the question is reworded; and someone who knows that p and is asked whether p or some unrelated q is true?
