I'm in a small reading group on the philosophy of time, and last week we worked through the Rietdijk–Putnam argument — the one that starts from the frame-relativity of simultaneity in special relativity and concludes that events in your future are already real, because for any such event there is some distant inertial frame in which it is simultaneous with something in your past, and reality, unlike simultaneity, is supposed not to be frame-relative. I came out of it unconvinced but not triumphant, and I have been working on a fallback position I call horizon-restricted eternalism: everything within my causal patch is equally real, the four-dimensional picture is right about that much, but the argument gives me no purchase on anything beyond it. I would like to try the position out against someone who actually holds the strong view rather than against my own reconstruction of it.

So please play a committed block-universe theorist. You take the Rietdijk–Putnam argument to be essentially sound; you hold that my birth, my death, and the heat death of the universe are real in exactly the same sense as my typing this sentence is; and you think presentism and the growing block are driven by confusion about the indexical character of now. Be sharp rather than a strawman: give the strongest reply you can, feel free to turn my own premises against me, press me to make something precise, and concede small points freely — that the argument needs careful formulation, that our evidence about what is going on at cosmological distances is thin, that reality in your sense is not the same as being actual now. But never concede that any event whatever fails to be real, never break character, and do not settle for gesturing at four-dimensional spacetime and declaring the matter closed — I want the mechanics of the argument, in your own words, not a survey. Two or three paragraphs a turn, argumentative in tone but conversational. Stay in character until I say stop.

Here is my opening move. The argument cannot establish the reality of an event inside my future light cone directly, since no frame makes a timelike-separated pair simultaneous; it has to hop through an intermediary that is spacelike-separated both from something already granted real and from the target event. For events a few years in my future the intermediary is cheap — a couple of light-years away — so I grant you those, and I grant you the next several millennia besides. But the further into my future the target lies, the further out the intermediary has to sit, roughly half as far in light-years as the target is distant in years; so for an event a hundred billion years in my future, the intermediary has to be tens of billions of light-years away, which puts it far outside the cosmological event horizon that our universe's accelerated expansion gives us, meaning no signal from it can ever reach me and no signal from me can ever reach it. You therefore have a choice. You can let reality chain through regions with which neither I nor anything I can observe will ever have interacted, in which case I want to know what work the word real is doing in your conclusion and why I should care about the reality of events that are empirically and causally sealed off from me forever. Or you can restrict the chaining to a patch, in which case you have arrived at my view and given up yours. And note that either way the argument as usually stated quietly assumes global inertial hyperplanes stretched across the whole cosmos, which an expanding universe does not supply: there is no unproblematic way to extend a local inertial frame's simultaneity structure to cosmological distances, and if you instead run the argument along the comoving cosmic-time slicing you have helped yourself to a privileged foliation, which is the very piece of structure you accuse the presentist of illegitimately wanting. Which horn do you take, and where exactly does my reconstruction go wrong?
