hey - I'm putting together the take-home final for my final-year seminar on AI alignment, and I need one essay question on the shutdown problem and corrigibility. The trouble is that everyone can already rattle off the standard moves: a system shoudn't want to disable the switch, you can make it indifferent between operating and being turned off, it shouldn't manipulate the operators into not pressing, and so on. I want something that separates students who understand the structure of the problem from students who've memorised the labels.

Two wrinkles I'd like built into the question. First: drop the binary off-switch and give the operators a graded dial that continuously scales down how much the system is allowed to act on the world, all the way to zero. Does the shutdown problem get easier or harder under that setup, and why exactly - I'm after the point that indifference now has to hold at every position of the dial rather than at a single one, and that a system can nudge the dial gradually in ways it couldn't nudge an on/off switch. Second: a corrigible system defers to the signal, but what we actually seem to care about is what the operators would want if they knew what the system knows. Those come apart, and aiming at the second thing looks like it hands the system reasons to manage what the operators find out.

So: write me the question itself (should be doable in 45 minutes, around 800 words), then a model answer, then a marking scheme out of 20 built from five things a top answer has to do, with the marks for each, plus a one-line note on how students typically get each one wrong. The scheme shouldn't reward name-dropping papers or reciting a definition of corrigibility - I want the specific moves, including what a stable target for the system would have to look like rather than just a shrug that the problem is hard.
