The statue-and-the-clay debate has the same shape every time it is run: the non-identity side argues from divergent modal and temporal properties — the lump survives squashing, the statue does not; the statue might have been made of different clay, the lump not — the identity side replies by rejecting or paraphrasing those attributions, the nihilist denies that there is a statue in the first place, and the four-dimensionalist dissolves the puzzle into two space-time worms sharing a temporal part. Every move has a rehearsed answer, and I don't know anyone who has switched sides on the strength of an argument.

What I want is a diagnosis of the stalemate, not a verdict. Here is the case that makes me think the diagnosis is not the obvious one. Call a pair inseparable when the two candidates have exactly the same modal and temporal profile: a statue whose matter is essential to it — an artwork that could not have been made of any other material — together with a lump that, as lumps are normally understood, has its parts essentially. Nothing in the standard repertoire distinguishes them; they coincide now, and there is no modal or temporal difference between them to appeal to. So whatever settles the general question is not the consideration that actually gets traded.

Is the dispute, then, verbal — about 'identical', or about what counts as one object? Is it a disagreement about which properties are admissible data: modal, temporal, sortal, artistic? A disagreement about whether the occupants of a region can be counted at all? Or are two questions being run under one heading: a question about identity, and a separate question about what makes something an object of a kind? My hypothesis is the last — that the sides are tacitly using different generators of candidate entities, a mereological one and a sortal or interest-relative one, so that 'how many things are here?' is not one question on which they disagree. Test that hypothesis against what each position is actually committed to, say where it breaks down, and say what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a distinction, a formal result about counting, a different question — or argue that, on your diagnosis, no progress should be expected.
