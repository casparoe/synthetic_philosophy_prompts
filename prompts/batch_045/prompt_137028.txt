ok so I run a small reading group on philosophy of religion — half the room finds Gödel's modal ontological argument genuinely beautiful and half think it's a bar trick — and I want to close the last session with a poem instead of a summary, so I need something that doesn't sneer at either side.

Here's the ask: a Petrarchan sonnet, 14 lines, loose iambic pentameter (don't torture the meter). The octave should build the argument as an attraction rather than a target: God-like defined as having all and only the positive properties, necessary existence counting as positive, positive properties compossible, so it's possible that something God-like exists — and then S5 does its little trick, possibly-necessary sliding into necessary, so it exists in every world. The sestet does the turn. And the turn shouldn't be the boring "but maybe God doesn't exist" move; it should be the modal collapse. Run the machinery with positivity closed under entailment and you don't just get God, you get that everything true is necessary — contingency drains out of the world, and the possibility premise you leaned on back in line 3 turns out to be the only kind of possibility there is.

Two things I'd like built in if you can manage it. First, I have this pet worry about the step from "any finite selection of positive properties can be had together" to "all of them can" — it looks like it needs a compactness assumption you're not entitled to just from the modal logic, and I'd like the poem to register that doubt without turning into a footnote in verse. Second, my own favourite repair, which I only half believe, is to downshift from S5 to something weaker — S4-ish — so that possibly-necessary no longer hands you plain necessity, and the argument tops out at "possibly, God exists" instead of "God exists." I'd love the closing lines to sit with that trade honestly rather than resolve it: you save contingency and you lose the conclusion, and maybe that was always the most the argument had in it anyway.

The formal bit that matters most to me: I want the volta to be audible as the moment the poem loses the ability to say "might." Before the turn, modality is doing real work — maybe, could, possibly. After it, I want no modal auxiliaries at all in the last six lines: flat indicatives, because on the collapsed picture nothing could have been otherwise. Someone who knows the argument should be able to point at line 9 and say "that's where S5 does it."

Rhyme it properly but don't let the rhymes get jokey, and where a phrase has to choose between scanning and being technically right about the axioms, choose being right — I'd rather have a slightly lumpy sonnet than one where someone who actually knows the literature winces at line 6.
