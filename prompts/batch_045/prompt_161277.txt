At a reading, a hostile reviewer — he's a philosopher, and he's enjoying himself — corners her with what I take to be the preface paradox grafted onto the logical-omniscience problem: you assert every sentence in this thing, you'd defend any single one of them if challenged, and yet you also say the whole is shot through with error; so either you aren't really asserting them, or you're irrational, since rational belief is closed under entailment and all those sentences entail their conjunction.

What I need from you is a properly engineered version of that confrontation, not the textbook example.
