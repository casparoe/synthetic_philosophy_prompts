My opening: I don't think your view has a determinate subject matter even before we get to the luck stuff. Talent and effort don't add up, they multiply — an extra hundred hours of practice is worth far more to someone with a high ceiling, and a great opportunity is worth nothing to someone who won't take it. When inputs combine that way, the extra that comes from combining them doesn't arrive labelled: hand the whole interaction surplus to effort, hand it all to circumstance, split it evenly, or average over every possible order of adding the inputs in (the Shapley thing), and you get different numbers for how much of Ana's salary was really hers. So what quantity is the desert claim about, and why isn't your answer just an artifact of how you carve up the surplus?
