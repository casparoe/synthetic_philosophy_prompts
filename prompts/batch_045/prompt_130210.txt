Hi — I need a sorting instrument for the testimony stretch of my third-year epistemology seminar, the bit on reductionism vs. anti-reductionism. I've taught the standard Hume-vs-Reid framing maybe six times now, and every year the students just memorise which name goes with which slogan and then can't say what actually turns on it. So this year I want them to sort themselves by cases first and only get handed the labels at the end.

Build me a decision tree, 9 to 11 branch nodes, that starts from a commitment everyone accepts and lands a student on a named position. Details:

- Start from a case nobody disputes: a stranger on the platform tells you the train leaves at 4:03, you believe it, and you're justified. Everything branches off that.
- Every node has to be a concrete case or a claim about a case, phrased so someone who has never heard of testimony theory can answer it. If a node uses a phrase like "basic source of justification", "positive reasons", or "defeasibility condition", I'm rejecting it. No node may ask which theorist a student agrees with.
- Leaves I need to appear: global reductionism (the Humean line where testimonial belief always needs non-testimonial backing); local reductionism (the per-instance version); the credulity/acceptance family (Reid and the Burge-style principle); the communitarian anti-reductionist view associated with Coady; the assurance or interpersonal view in the Moran vein; and the virtue-and-credibility view in the Fricker vein. Also give me one or two leaves for combinations that are coherent but that, as far as you know, nobody actually defends — mark them unoccupied, and say in a line whether that's because the combination is unstable, unattractive, or just unexplored.
- Two nodes I definately want in, because they do the separating work I care about. First: a source that is highly reliable but isn't an agent in any sense my students would grant — a well-calibrated instrument readout, an LLM chatbot that confabulates maybe 4% of the time, a Wikipedia sentence with no traceable author. Is that testimony at all, and does the answer sort people by position or cut across every position on the tree? Second: a speaker who is in fact reliable but whose reports get systematically discounted by hearers because of who she is. I want the tree to force a choice between treating that as one more instance of the failure reductionists already worry about, and treating it as a distinct failure in its own right.
- Fit in as well a node on whether the backing reasons have to be in the individual hearer's head or can be distributed across a community and stored in the epistemic environment, and a node on long hearsay chains.
- Flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely can't pull apart — I'd expect the credulity and acceptance views to collapse into each other, and I suspect the Fricker-style view behaves like a second axis rather than a leaf — instead of inventing a node to fake a distinction you don't have.
- End with a scoring key: which answer patterns land where, and one sentence on which single node does the most work.

Aim for something that fits on a page front and back.
