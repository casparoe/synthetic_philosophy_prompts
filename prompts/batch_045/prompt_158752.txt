Schlick held that a sentence has cognitive significance only if it can in principle be verified, and I have always read his essay on the meaning of life as applying that test to the question what the meaning of life is, finding it empty, and stopping there. But its closing pages praise play and a certain youthfulness of spirit, and if those pages are to be consistent with his own criterion, they must, it seems to me, be advancing an empirical hypothesis: that a life organised around activities pursued for their own sake contains more of whatever it is that makes a life worth living. I want to test that hypothesis, and I have hit a design problem — pursued for its own sake does not distinguish play from mere distraction or from compulsive absorption — so I propose adding a counterfactual filter, the conscription test: an activity qualifies only if the agent would still value it were it made compulsory and every external reward withdrawn. Is the conscription test a faithful explication of Schlick's criterion, and what precisely should my study be measuring?
