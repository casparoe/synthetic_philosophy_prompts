I've just spent a long evening arguing with a friend about the Knobe effect, and I ended up defending what I take to be the strong reading of it: that the asymmetry in people's willingness to say the chairman brought the side effect about intentionally — readily when the side effect was bad, reluctantly when it was good — is a competence-level fact about the folk concept of intentional action, showing that moral considerations partly constitute or at least constrain what we count as acting intentionally, rather than a performance artifact in which the word intentionally gets recruited after the fact to license blame or withhold praise. My friend thinks the whole thing is motivational, and I don't think either of us moved.

So: what would count against my reading? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and for each I want your judgment about whether a committed defender would concede it as counting against the view or would treat it as compatible. Three hypothetical results in particular, none of which I take to be settled:

(i) A large preregistered replication in which the asymmetry survives fully when the bad side effect consists only in the violation of a pointless local convention — no victim, no harm, nothing anyone cares about — but vanishes when the side effect is genuinely harmful and the agent is blamelessly ignorant that it would occur.

(ii) A developmental finding that the asymmetry is absent in children too young to have any grip on blame, paired with the cross-cultural finding that it appears in adults everywhere it has been tested, including populations where the harmful side effect in question is not locally condemned.

(iii) The same vignettes run with an AI system as the agent rather than a chairman, where the asymmetry not only survives but is considerably larger.

Which of these, if they landed, would be decisive; which are ambiguous and depend on further facts; and which would a defender actually welcome? I also want to know whether the strong reading predicts anything that blame-validation, statistical-normality, or lexical-ambiguity accounts of the effect do not — my own sense is that the thing that would move me most is a dissociation from blame itself, for instance if the asymmetry showed up on a speeded or implicit measure where there is no room for motivated reasoning — and, finally, an honest assessment of whether the dispute has reached a point where the strong reading has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that is or is not a legitimate place for a claim about the semantics of a folk concept to end up.
