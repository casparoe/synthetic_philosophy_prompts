my wife and I have been going back and forth about this for like two months and I need someone to tell me who's thinking badly here, because I think she's doing some kind of philosophy move on me and I can't quite catch it.

We're deciding when to start trying for kid #2. I'm 37, she's 35. Starting now is biologically easier. Waiting about two years would mean my job is stable, we're not broke, and we're not total zombies as parents. Her argument: whoever we'd have in 2027 is not the kid we'd have now — different egg, different sperm, a completely different person — and that kid's life would still be a life worth living, and might honestly go better given the money and the calmer household. So, she says, we aren't making anybody worse off, because there's no one person who shows up in both scenarios to be worse off.

I've got three things I think are wrong with this.

1. It seems to prove to much. If the reasoning works, you could never say anything about how to treat a kid before you've conceived them, and nobody actually lives like that — it would be messed up to, say, drink heavily through a planned pregnancy on the grounds that the kid who results is a different kid than the one you'd have had otherwise.
2. The frozen embryos. We did IVF for our first and we've still got two embryos on ice, and we'd probably use both either way. So if the real question is just *when* we transfer them, the "it's a totally different kid" move doesn't even get off the ground — same two people, different timing. Unless you want to say an embryo isn't anybody yet, or that who it turns out to be depends on when and how it's born, which... maybe? But here's the thing that really bugs me: we could also just not use them at all and try naturally, and then her point is back in full force. I don't get why the moral situation should flip depending on which of those we do.
3. Numbers. If we wait we'll probably stop at one more kid; if we start now there's a decent chance we end up with two. Her argument looks like it only works when the headcount comes out the same.

Also, my cousin does medical-mal defense and said something I half remember: courts mostly won't let a kid sue over having been born at all, because there's nothing to compare their life to, but parents can sometimes recover the extra costs. If that's right, the law seems to be running her argument for her, and I want to know if that's just a coincidence or whether the law quietly inherited one contested view.

The way I keep putting it to myself is: we'd be filling one slot with a worse-off kid rather than making one particular kid worse off. She says "a worse-off kid" isn't even a thing you can pick out — whoever shows up is the only kid who could have shown up. That's where I lose the thread.

So: is her argument sound? Does the embryo detail change the answer, and does the one-kid-vs-two thing matter? Or is my discomfort just a feeling dressed up as a reason? Give me an actual verdict, not "it's a personal decision." And please say how confident you are in what you're telling me, and single out the one point you're least sure about.
