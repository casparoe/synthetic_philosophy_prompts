So there's a woman on my team, Priya, who runs our platform design reviews and has this habit of labeling things "Moloch" — you know, the god-of-races-to-the-bottom thing people online are always quoting. She's oddly consistent about which situations get the label and which don't, and I've started keeping a list because I want to steal the concept for an internal doc I'm writing about why our org keeps making itself miserable. Problem is I can't figure out what her actual rule is.

Her verdicts so far:

- Teams padding their project estimates because promo packets compare relative scope across teams: "Moloch."
- Two teams each building their own version of the same internal library, because each is measured on its own feature throughput and neither gets credit for shared work: "Moloch."
- Everyone dumping pages into the shared on-call queue instead of fixing root causes: "not Moloch, that's a commons thing, just give the queue an owner."
- Weekly status reports nobody reads, which survived for a year because one director liked them: "not Moloch, one person could kill that with a Slack message."
- The industry-wide thing where every company makes candidates do longer and longer take-homes: "Moloch."
- One team quietly stopped writing the weekly report and nothing bad happened to them: she retroactively ruled "so that one was never Moloch."
- Two teams negotiating directly over the same headcount slot at planning time: "not Moloch, there's two of them, they can just talk."
- Teams competing to rewrite the same service, which burned a lot of time but ended with a genuinely better service that everyone now uses: "not Moloch. Wasteful, but something good came out the other end."

Here's the one I need a ruling on. Next quarter's GPU budget is allocated to each team in proportion to how much compute that team actually burned this quarter, and the org's total spend is capped anyway. So everyone is now running pointless jobs to keep their number up, everyone knows everyone is doing it, and everyone hates it. There is a platform group that could change the formula, but they've said they won't touch it mid-year.

Is that Moloch by Priya's lights? The way I see it her verdicts fit at least two or three different rules at once — something like "each team is better off gaming it no matter what the others do, and everyone ends up worse off," versus "no single team can stop without getting punished," versus "there's no one person who could just fix it," versus "it's a race, not a shared resource" — and I have a feeling those come apart on the compute case but I can't see exactly where.

So: which rule do you think she's actually running, what does it say about the GPU thing, and what additional case should I ask her about to be sure? Please just commit to one rule and argue for it instead of laying out the options and leaving me to choose, and tell me honestly which of her verdicts your rule can't accommodate, if there are any. American English please.
