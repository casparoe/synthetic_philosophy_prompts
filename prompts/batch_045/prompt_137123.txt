Hey — I'm putting together an internal design spec for a system that will run alongside human overseers who are tired, fallible, and maybe one time in ten press the off-switch for bad reasons: misreading a dashboard, a policy that changed overnight, plain spite. Before I look at any proposed solution to the shutdown problem I want the requirements list. What does a theory of corrigibility have to deliver to be good enough to actually deploy in that setting?

Heres the list I'd start from, but please argue with it: no incentive to disable, hide, or guard the button; no incentive to talk the operator out of pressing it, or to arrange things so the question never comes up in the first place; full effort on the objective in the worlds where nobody ever presses it (an agent that reasons "I'll probably get switched off, so why bother" is useless); it has to survive updating — learning from corrections shouldn't turn into a reason to go cause corrections; being corrigible about the button has to stay distinct from just deferring to whatever the operator says this minute, since the operators are sometimes wrong; and it should be safely interruptible partway through a task, not only between tasks.

The one I most want your view on: someone told me we should give the agent separate channels for "stop now" and "you've got the goal wrong," because those are different events and it ought to respond differently to them. But it looks to me like the richer you make the correction channel, the more the agent has something to prefer — and therefore something to manipulate — so that requirement may be in direct conflict with the no-manipulation one. Real tension, or am I conflating two things?

For each requirement give the concrete case that motivates it, mark which are non-negotiable for a deployment like mine and which are only preferences of one camp (neutrality about shutdown versus an actual preference for being corrected, that sort of thing), and flag any subset you think can't be satisfied together. And say which single requirement does the most to knock out candidate designs. A couple of lines scoring the obvious approaches against the list is fine, but the list is the deliverable.
