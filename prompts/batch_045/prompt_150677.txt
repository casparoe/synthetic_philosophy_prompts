I'm writing a magazine piece on the return of "culture" as an explanation in economics and politics, and I want to use a real episode to put pressure on Weber's Protestant ethic thesis — understood not as the bumper-sticker claim that Protestant diligence produces growth, but as the causal claim that a religious value complex can help bring a distinctive economic form of life into the world. Take Singapore: independence in 1965 with no natural resources and a small domestic market, and within a couple of generations one of the richest states per head anywhere; then the "Asian values" argument of the early 1990s, in which Lee Kuan Yew and Mahathir Mohamad credited the East Asian record to a Confucian inheritance of thrift, discipline, filial obligation and deference to authority, and drew the political moral that liberal individualism is not a precondition of prosperity. Analyze that episode through the thesis, and then run the analysis back the other way.

Three things in particular. First, map the claimed cause onto Weber's mechanism: his story runs from an otherworldly asceticism whose tension with the world produced methodical this-worldly accumulation as an unintended consequence, and it is offered as an elective affinity rather than a covering law — so say whether the Singapore case reproduces the mechanism or only its outward correlation, and what would have to be true of Confucian discipline for it to be the same cause rather than a lookalike. Second, turn Weber's own comparative method — the search for the absence of the effect where the cause is absent, in his studies of China and India — on this case: what do the other Chinese-diaspora economies, and the Protestant economies that did not industrialize early, do to the inference? Third, the hard part. After the 1997–98 crash the same value complex was turned around and used to explain cronyism and opacity, and the values themselves were codified and promoted by the state well after the growth was under way. Does that refute the thesis, or only expose the ordinary hazard of a cause identified retrospectively and never specified in advance? And can a Weberian reply that elective affinity is a claim about a founding moment of transition and legitimation rather than a durable national trait without thereby making the thesis unfalsifiable? Close with a verdict, argued for, on what this episode should do to a working journalist's willingness to write that culture was a cause here.
