My attempt at the factorization, steal it or replace it:

2. P(the system actually occupies that role, given such an account is correct) — split this however you like, the interesting parts are below.

Three things I want actually engaged with rather than gestured at, because this is where I think my friend and I come apart:

(a) Affine freedom. The optimal policy for a discounted MDP is invariant under positive affine transformations of the reward function: add a constant to every reward and you've added the same constant to the return of every trajectory; scale by a positive number and you haven't reordered anything. So the reward signal has no zero point and no scale of its own. But badness looks like it does have both — there's presumably a fact about how much of one bad state you'd accept to secure some good thing, and that fact shouldn't change when I relabel the rewards in a way that leaves every behavior you could ever observe exactly the same. If valence is what reward encodes, which member of the equivalence class is it, and why isn't the choice arbitrary? And if you want to fix the zero point extrinsically — the no-op baseline, the agent's own average return, whatever — say what does the fixing, and say what happens when I shift reward by a constant and nothing about the agent budges at all.

(b) What I've started calling the disjoint-subject problem. In the setup everyone actually runs — collect a batch of rollouts, compute advantages, take a gradient step on the policy — the thing that acts never sees the reward, and the thing that sees the reward doesn't act. The gradient lands on the weights; it doesnt get written into the activations that the deployment forward pass runs on. So go looking for the subject: is it the rollout policy, the only thing present at the moment of action, but reward-free and not learning anything? The trajectory, which has a natural beginning and end and so maybe a natural subject, but which is finished before anything is learned? Or the training process stretched across updates, which does get the signal but whose identity changes at every step? Pick one and pay for it. I want your probability on each branch, not just on the disjunction.

(c) The convergence thing. If whatever plays the valence role is closer to a reward-prediction-error signal than to reward itself — the move people reach for when they want to keep a grip on the dopamine story — then notice that a well-converged policy has RPE near zero essentially everywhere. The better trained the agent, the less it suffers, and a perfectly trained one has no valence at all: all the suffering is in the learning and it stops when the learning does. That seems like a strange place to land. Is it a reductio of the RPE move or is it fine?
