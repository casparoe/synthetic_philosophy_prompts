I'm writing a one-semester term paper (about 6000 words) and need a proposal for it. The topic is Moore's Proof of an External World, and specifically the dispute over whether his argument transmits justification to its conclusion: on one side, transmission-failure views of the kind associated with Crispin Wright, on the other, immediacy or dogmatist replies of the kind associated with James Pryor.

My half-formed idea is that this dispute runs together two questions. First: can a Moorean argument confer on its conclusion the status of knowledge or justified belief? Second: can the argument serve the subject as a reason against the skeptic — can they use it to answer the skeptical argument, and not merely end up knowing its conclusion? I suspect that once these are separated, several of the standard transmission verdicts turn out to be answering only the second question, and that the interesting residue is what exactly a subject gains by running the argument if the answer to the first question is yes and the answer to the second is no — call this the problem of the idle proof, a piece of knowledge whose argument does nothing to secure it.

Write the proposal: state the question so that it has a determinate answer; explain why the existing positions don't settle it, and where my distinction does real work rather than merely relabeling the standoff; lay out how the paper would proceed section by section, and why that route rather than the obvious alternatives of routing everything through closure principles or through Wittgensteinian hinge commitments; set out the range of possible conclusions and which considerations would decide among them; and give an honest account of what would make the project fail or outgrow one semester. Don't include a bibliography or citations — I'll supply those myself — but do say which positions and arguments the paper has to engage.
