Hey — I write novels (well, one novel right now) and I've got a problem that's more philosophy than craft. It's a two-POV book. One narrator is German, mid-fifties, career public administration: for him the moral life is largely about keeping a shared practice intact — the rule, the procedure, your post, doing your bit so the thing everybody depends on keeps working. Order isn't the enemy of goodness, it's most of what goodness looks like from inside an institution. The other narrator is American, thirties, NGO lawyer type, straight out of the Thoreau/whistleblower lineage: for her the moral life is where somebody finally says no from the inside and eats the cost. They've been arguing for three hundred pages and I don't think either is simply wrong, but every time I write the fight it collapses into caricture — rule-worshipping German, narcissist with a conscience — and the book comes out dumber than I am.

So: write me the joint statement they'd actually both sign if they ran an adversarial collaboration in good faith. Numbered section of claims both can accept (including about the cases where each side normally wins), then a table of the disagreements in wording each character would acept for their own view, and — this matters for me — the wording has to be something they could say out loud in a bar, not in a seminar. Then the cruxes: which sub-questions, if settled, would move one of them.

Two things I've been kicking around that I can't tell are real or just props. First: rule-following as deference (you do it because it was handed down) versus as custodianship (you're the one keeping the practice alive, which occasionally means departing from the letter). Does both sides survice that split, or does one of them have to reject it — and is there a matching split on her side, between conscience as sincerity (you're off the hook if you were honest) and conscience as something that still obliges you after you turn out to be wrong? Second, a case: a mid-level supervisor at a regional water utility notices the mandatory reporting form rounds contaminant readings down to the nearest threshold. She files it exactly as the form requires, no panic, the utility's credibility holds, the fix is delayed nine months, definately some kids get sick. Both of them have to be able to say what that shows, and I want each side's prediction about how ordinary people back home will read it.

Also tell me plainly if any part of their fight is only about words.
