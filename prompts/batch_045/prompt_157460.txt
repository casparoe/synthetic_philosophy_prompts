My uncle Ray died last week. He built a trucking company out of one borrowed truck, got up at four every morning for forty years, and was completely open about being proud of that. His younger brother Jerry — same house, same drunk father, worked just as hard — hurt his back, lost a contract, and died broke, and Jerry's two kids will be in the front row. Half the room will be Ray's business people, who will expect to hear that he earned every inch of what he had.

My problem: if I say he earned it, I'm saying in front of Jerry's kids that Jerry somehow didn't, which I dont believe and Ray didn't either. If I say it was mostly luck and breaks, I'm crapping on the one thing he was ever proud of, and his friends will hear it as an insult to him. And praising how hard he worked doesn't get me out of it either, because Jerry worked just as hard, so I'm not even sure what I'd be praising him for.

Ray had a way of putting it that I want to use but can't build a speech around. He used to say he never earned anything, he got paid: the deal says whoever hauls the load gets X, he hauled the load, so X was his, but that's only the deal working, not the universe balancing a scale, and a man who hauled and got nothing was cheated by the deal, not by life. He thought people slid between those two without noticing and built a whole worldview on the slide.

Can you write me a five-minute eulogy that praises the forty years of four-a.m. mornings without saying either that he deserved the company or that it was luck, that doesn't turn into a lecture, and that Jerry's kids and Ray's business partners could both sit through without wincing? Also tell me whether I should name Jerry in it at all, and why.
