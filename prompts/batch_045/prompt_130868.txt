I help run a casual monthly meetup — about 14 people, maybe half of them programmers, 90 minutes, nobody has read anything in advance, and I have no philosophy or ML background myself. I'm doing a session on that idea you see around for keeping powerful AIs honest: instead of checking what the AI is doing yourself, have two of them argue in front of a human judge who only has to pick a winner. I want an activity where the room *feels* why that gets shaky when one side is sneaky, rather than me explaining it.

The bit I'd like it built around is my own idea, so tell me if its wrong: the dishonest side has to make every single step of its reasoning airtight, so there's no one sentence you can point at and say "that's the lie." I have this feeling that if every individual step checks out, then a careful judge who takes their time should always be able to work through the chain and catch the problem. I'd like the game to either confirm that feeling or break it, in the room, with people actually getting fooled before anyone explains anything.

So: rules, roles, a task where the true answer is knowable but expensive to check, and a debrief mapping what happened to the idea. Plus what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and which part of the real worry the game just can't reproduce and I'd have to say out loud myself.
