Two philosophers of mind I know got into an argument after a seminar on Dennett's two-bitser: a robot with only two bits of internal state (four states in all), each covarying with one of four environmental conditions, such that predicting and explaining its behavior by ascribing it beliefs and desires works about as well as it ever does. Dennett deploys it against Searle's claim that derived intentionality is always borrowed from the original intentionality of conscious agents, and that a purely syntactic system has no content of its own.

Their disagreement was about a variant. Suppose we build a self-auditing two-bitser, Sam: alongside its four states it has a monitoring state that fires when conditions make its own first-order reading likely to be wrong (bad light, say), and when that state fires Sam discounts the reading. Sam thus registers, in its own machinery, a distinction between how things look to it and how they are.

1. Maya: So whatever for-the-system was supposed to add is now mechanically present. If Searle still wants to deny Sam original intentionality, he owes an account of what is missing that is not simply the insistence that it is missing.
2. Tomas: The monitoring state is just another state. It is we who specify what it is about, and the discounting is just a causal switch. You have added more as-if structure, not more aboutness. Nothing is at stake for Sam.
3. Maya: At stake is the wrong test, since it smuggles in consciousness. The right question is whether there is a fact of the matter about what Sam's states are about, independent of our interpretive convenience. There is: given Sam's design history and its environment, its states track their conditions and can fail to track them, and Sam does better when they track correctly. That is objective.
4. Tomas: That is a teleological story, not a semantic one. Hearts have functions and can malfunction, and no one thinks a faulty valve represents blood flow wrongly. If failure of function were error, my dishwasher has false beliefs about the dishes. Your objective fact is a fact about design and selection, not about content.
5. Maya: Then run it on yourself. Your states got their functions from selection too. If function underdetermines content for Sam, it underdetermines it for you, and your original intentionality inherits whatever content it has from exactly the mechanism you just declared insufficient. The difference you want is not in where content comes from; it is in who is looking.
6. Tomas: Then we agree more than it looks. I have never denied that content is fixed by third-personal facts. I deny that those facts settle whether a system has a point of view at all. The two-bitser shows the intentional stance is always available; it does not show the stance is ever more than a stance. And your variant helps me: it shows how much machinery you can add without ever generating a subject.

They turned to me to referee and I could not. Please go through the six moves and rate each from clearly mistaken to clearly right, saying briefly why, and flagging where a move is ambiguous or begs the question. Then say what, if anything, both of them are missing: is there a consideration that reframes the dispute rather than merely scoring it — for instance about whether the original/derived distinction is doing any explanatory work anywhere, or about whether a two-bitser is even a fair test case for it?
