So here's what I want: take the octopus case as a case study for functionalism plus multiple realizability. Not a summary of the theory, I've had plenty of that. An actual mapping.

- What the state is even supposed to be here (pain? sentience? something else that the law actually cares about), what the inputs and outputs are supposed to be, and what would have to be true of an octopus for it to realize the same role.

Say what multiple realizability should predict about that, and whether the fact that it took a decades-long fight to settle — the fish pain argument in the early 2000s, with painkiller-sensitive behaviour and nociceptors on one side and the no-neocortex-so-its-just-a-reflex objection on the other — tells you anything about whether the theory is useful or just true by how it's worded.

So the theory has a dial on it that the theory itself doesn't tell you how to set. Is that a genuine problem, and does anyone bite the bullet on either end? Does functionalism have anything to say about partial realization, or is that exactly where it goes quiet?
