My supervisor has me write, for every case we read, whether the non-human thing in it counts as a "historical actor" or just background, and Im trying to reverse-engineer her rule. Her verdicts so far: Tambora going off in 1815 and the 1816 harvest failures across Europe — actor. The Little Ice Age taken as one big package — not an actor, just a backdrop. The potato blight in Ireland — an actor, though she'll insist that's still consistent with the famine being a British policy failure rather than a natural disaster. The malaria that kept the Roman campagna thinly settled for centuries — not an actor, because people had organised their whole year and their whole settlement pattern around it. The Dust Bowl drought — not an actor; the wheat economy is.

My case is the one I'm writing now: a river delta where forty years of slow saltwater creep, made much worse by upstream dams a colonial government built, finally kills the rice crop in a single season and sets off mass out-migration. Is the salt an actor on her usage? I can see three rules that fit her verdicts about equally well — the thing has to be dateable and abrupt; or the thing has to force the outcome no matter what people did; or the thing gets demoted whenever a human arrangement is what actually made it lethal — and they don't all give the same answer here. Which do you think she's running, what would each of them say about my delta, and what single further case would tell them apart best? I care less about the final yes/no than about the rule and about where it breaks.
