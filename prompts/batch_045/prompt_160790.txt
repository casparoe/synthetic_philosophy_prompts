my reading group has been doing a slow read of Aristotle on future contingents — the bit in De Interpretatione about whether it is already true today that there will be a sea battle tomorrow — and we've now spent two sessions on it with three pictures on the whiteboard and no agreement at all. instead of another summary i'd like to close the term with a poem, so: could you write me a sestina? six stanzas of six lines plus an envoi (three lines carrying all six end words, or six lines if you prefer — your call, just tell me what you did). the end words i want are: sea, battle, tomorrow, true, false, open. please take this as seriously as someone who has read a fair amount of philosophical logic and who also actually writes verse — do a bit of planning before you draft, think about which stanza carries what, and don't just gesture at the topic.

the three pictures on our board, which i want the poem to actually hold rather than name-drop: (1) the symmetric one — right now neither there will be a battle tomorrow nor there will not be is true, and yet either there will be or there will not be is true already, so the openness sits below the level of the disjunction; (2) the thin-line one — exactly one of the two really is true now, there is a single line running through the branching futures, and the openness is only our ignorance, an epistemic not a semantic failure; (3) the worry that truth seems to arrive late — once the battle has happened (or definately has not happened), it looks as though it was always going to be so, as if the event reaches backwards and settles the earlier utterance retroactively, which is what makes (1) unstable. one person in the group has real formal logic training and will be in the room, so it can't be hand-wavy, but everyone else is humanities, so no jargon in the poem itself and no stage directions telling us which picture we're in.

the thing i keep coming back to, and the angle i'd like the poem to take, is that what's at stake for a speaker isn't really truth but whether anyone is in a position to say: put the poem in the mouth of a lookout or harbour watch officer at dusk the day before, someone who has to decide whether to send a signal or stay silent — on (1) silence is the only honest option even though something is certainly going to happen one way or the other, while on (2) there is a fact of the matter that this person simply is not in a position to know, so the failure is about credentials rather than about truth. i want the poem to feel like it's about that gap between a future's being settled and anyone's being entitled to say which way it's settled. also let the sestina's rotation do structural work: the same six words coming back in a fixed permutation is itself a little argument about what is closed and what is not — the vocabulary is deterministic while the arrangements are not, or the other way round — and you should decide what the form is claiming and commit to it. give each of the three pictures a home in particular stanzas, and let the envoi break or complete the rotation in a way someone who knows the debate can read either as a verdict or as a refusal to give one.

then one section headed What I was doing with the form, 300 words or so: which lines carry which picture, why those stanzas, what the envoi is doing, and anything in the three-way disagreement the sestina couldn't hold. no footnotes, no bibliography, no citations — i just want the poem and the note.
