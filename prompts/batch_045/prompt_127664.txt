hey — I need a case built to order for a third-year seminar on consciousness. We've just done Block's access/phenomenal distinction and then global workspace theory, and I want to show students that the GWT criterion is really a criterion for access, not for phenomenology. The way I want to show it is a dissociation in the direction that usually gets hand-waved: full access, no phenomenology.

The setup. One scenario, one paragraph, under 200 words. Ordinary human subjects in a clinic, ward, or psychophysics lab — no uploads, no exotic physics, no sci-fi. You get one hypothetical intervention or condition, which you should flag as the made-up bit; it should be a mild extrapolation from something real (a stimulation protocol, a lesion, a drug, a masking paradigm), not a magic box.

The spec it has to meet. Some content is access-conscious by GWT's own lights — globally broadcast, sustained rather than a transient flicker, reportable, available to working memory and to deliberate reasoning and action, with confidence tracking accuracy — and yet, by whatever criterion for phenomenal consciousness you're using, there's nothing it's like for the subject with respect to that content. State your phenomenal criterion explicitly before the scenario, and it can't be defined in terms of report or availability or the case collapses immediately. It also can't just be that the subject says "I didn't see anything" — that's a report, and a GWT person will reply that the report is the evidence of access. The no-phenomenology verdict has to fall out of the mechanism plus your criterion, not get written into the story as "and there was no experience." One more requirement: the access has to be the subject's access — the content available to her, not merely floating around in some subsystem that later causes a report.

Banned moves: blindsight and superblindsight, Sperling-style partial report or iconic memory overflow, zombies, anesthesia / sleep / coma / vegetative patients, split-brain, and anything where the subject is just guessing or confabulating.

Then the check. A table with exactly these columns:
- condition_or_subject: the cell of the design — you'll probably want two conditions differing in exactly one respect
- access_verdict: what the GWT criterion gives here, plus a one-line reason
- phenomenal_verdict: what your criterion gives here, plus a one-line reason
- load_bearing_feature: the single feature of the case doing the work
- if_removed: what happens to each verdict if that feature is deleted

After the table, two short paragraphs. First: how a GWT defender would try to stop the case from counting — I'd gess they either deny this is really global broadcast or just reject the access/phenomenal distinction outright — and whether that reply is doing real work or is question-begging. Second: what I should say to a student who objects that you just defined phenomenal consciousness so that it wouldn't be there.

No citations, paper titles, or reading suggestions anywhere — I can't check them and don't need them. American English please.
