I run a monthly reading group on interpretability — about 18 people, roughly half ML PhD students and half philosophy grad students, 90 minutes — and we keep going in circles on whether it even makes sense to say a network represents anything, or whether that's just a useful way for us to talk. I want one session where they stop trading slogans and actually run into the disagreement themselves.

Here's the gimmick I've been toying with, which you should either build the session around or tell me won't work: instead of a real net, use a hand-built artifact small enough that everyone in the room can inspect every internal state — a cardboard machine with colored pegs and a lookup wheel that maps simple drawn inputs to output labels. The catch is that I can pre-load two different readings of the same pegs: one that fits the training cases and also survives being intervened on with new inputs, and one that fits the training cases just as well but falls apart the moment you try to steer the machine with it.

Design me the runnable version: how to build the artifact, what the two readings are, what each half of the room gets asked to do, a timeline for the 90 minutes, and a debrief that maps the two moments they should hit — the pegs support more than one reading, and yet one reading earns the label and the other doesn't — onto the realism/anti-realism fight, without naming it up front. Also: what will predictably go wrong when I run it, and which feature of the disagreement the activity will flatten out no matter how cleverly I set it up?
