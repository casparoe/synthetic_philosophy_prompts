i'm closing out a decision-theory reading group next week and the last session is on program equilibrium — the setup where instead of picking an action you submit a program that gets handed the other player's source code as input, so "cooperate iff the other program's source is identical to mine" can be an equilibrium in a one-shot PD. I want to end with a villanelle where the two refrains *are* the two players' programs: the repeated lines coming back verbatim are supposed to be the copies recognizing each other, and the last stanza, where both refrains finally land side by side, is mutual cooperation.

But I also want the bad news inside the form, not bolted on: always-defect is still an equilibrium, there are tons of mutually incompatible conditional programs and no equilibrium selection story, so the returning lines should start to feel like a cage or a coordination accident more than like trust. And I'd like the final couplet to bring one refrain back changed by just a word — enough that the programs are no longer identical and the whole thing collapses to mutual defection, which should land hard rather than cleverly.

Constraints: keep it a real poem (not a puzzle with line breaks), and keep it distinct from Hofstadter-style superrationality and from the iterated/folk-theorem story — different argument, and my group will notice if it slides into either.
