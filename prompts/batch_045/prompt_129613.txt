My friend's a microbiologist and we've had the same arguement for maybe six years now about whether some particular thing is alive. Every time it's a different thing: a virus; a seed that sat frozen for something like tens of thousands of years and then sprouted when somebody planted it; a cell line that has been growing in plastic dishes since the 1950s, long after the person it came from died; a mule. And I think I've finally noticed what the fight is actually about, and I want to know whether there's a word for it.

Here's the pattern. Nobody argues about a dog or a rock. Every case we end up fighting over is one where the object and the activity can come apart. Either the thing is sitting there doing nothing at all (the frozen seed, a spore, a dried-up animal that rehydrates later), or the activity is happening somewhere other than in the thing (a virus has to borrow a cell to do anything; the cell line carries on while the animal it was part of is gone). In the easy cases its the same object doing the living the whole time and there is nothing to argue about. In the hard cases the answer to "is living going on here" and the answer to "is this thing alive" come apart, and instead of noticing that we are answering two different questions, we start swapping criteria — no it needs metabolism, no it needs heredity, no it needs a boundary of its own — and the argument goes nowhere.

The other thing I notice is that we never disagree about any fact. We agree completely about what viruses do and don't do. And when we're forced to actually decide, we decide on what the thing would do under different conditions: the seed counts because it would germinate if you planted it, the mule is made to sound less alive because it won't have offspring. So we are classifying a present object by a hypothetical future activity. That feels like a move with a definite structure and I have no name for it.

I've read the Wikipedia page, so I don't need the seven-property list again, and "it's a cluster concept / there's no definition" doesn't satisfy me either — if you think that is the answer then say why it answers what I'm asking and don't leave it there. Concretely: does what I've described have a name, or a cluster of near neighbours I should be separating out? If it's near something established but not the same thing, tell me how it differs and which fits better. If there's genuinely nothing, coin a term and define it, then do the part I actually care about: say which cases should come out as borderline under your account and which shouldn't — should a candle flame be borderline, should a mule be, should the cell line be, should a computer virus be — and what a case would have to look like to break your carving rather than just be a hard call inside it.
