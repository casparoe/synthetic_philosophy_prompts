I work for a legislative review committee in an imaginary jurisdiction, the Republic of Maridal, which has just enacted the following statute and is commissioning short "historical perspective" memos on it. I want Hume's.

**The Settled Preference Act (Maridal), in outline:**

1. *No diagnostic requirement.* Eligibility is not tied to terminal illness, to unbearable suffering, or to any diagnosed medical condition at all. The applicant must simply state that continuing to live has become, for them, a matter of indifference — a thing they have no remaining interest in pursuing — and must be judged by a clinician to have decisional capacity.

2. *Deliberation companions.* The applicant must hold twenty-four structured conversations over eighteen months with a trained lay "deliberation companion," whose job is to keep the applicant's own stated reasons in view and to revisit them as circumstances change. The companion has no veto and no power to declare the request invalid; clinicians are involved only for the capacity assessment.

3. *The flatness clause.* The eighteen-month period is cut to nine months if the applicant's stated desire to die shows no significant fluctuation across the conversations, and extended to thirty months if it fluctuates a great deal. The stated rationale: fluctuation is evidence of unresolved conflict, flatness is evidence of settledness.

4. *Advance arrangements.* An applicant who satisfies the above may arrange now for the means to be provided at a later date, triggered by a specified future event (a diagnosis, a loss of function, and so on), without re-running the process at that time.
