I'm a senior in high school, and there's a pattern I keep watching happen that I don't have a word for. I'd like to know whether philosophy has a name for it, and if not, what it should be called.

Here's the shape of it. Someone in my grade is judged to have done something bad — an old post resurfaces, something said at a party gets around. What follows isn't decided by anyone. No one announces it, no adult is involved, there's no hearing, and there's no point at which it's over. People just stop replying to him, stop sitting with him, stop including him. Each of those steps is tiny, and each one looks like nothing more than a person choosing who to spend time with. But the total result is that someone who had a full social life a month ago now eats lunch alone, no one can point to the act that did it, and — this is the part I have no concept for — no one can lift it either, because there is no one with the standing to say it's finished, and any single person who starts talking to him again just becomes the next target. That happened: one girl did, and within a week she'd been dropped too.

I've tried on the words I know and none fit. It isn't a boycott, because a boycott is something a group decides on and can call off, and nobody here decided anything. Ostracism sounds closer, except that suggests a group expelling someone as a group, whereas here the group never acts at all — a hundred people act separately, and a good number of them would tell you privately that they don't think he deserved any of this and are keeping their distance only because being seen with him is now expensive. A teacher pointed me at the problem of many hands, but as far as I can tell that's about who is responsible when many people contribute to one outcome, and my question is different: I want to know whether the outcome is punishment at all, given that nobody imposed it, nobody seems to intend it as punishment, nobody can end it, and every act composing it is one people would say they have a right to do.

So: is there an established concept in work on punishment or on social sanction for a sanction with no author and no endpoint, made entirely of acts that are individually harmless and individually protected? If the nearest candidates are things like informal social control, cumulative or collective sanction, or expressive condemnation, I want to know which fits and exactly where each stops matching. I especially want to know whether the fact that many participants don't intend to punish — and some don't believe the target did anything wrong — keeps this outside the category that stands in need of justification. If nothing established fits, please name and define one, and mark the boundary cases: what would have to be added for this to be the ordinary, defensible thing, and what would turn it into something else.
