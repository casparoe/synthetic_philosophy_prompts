I work on the modeling team at a county pretrial services agency. Every two weeks we ship a revised version of a risk score that feeds two distinct decisions: a low score recommends release with no conditions, a middle score recommends release with a check-in supervision slot (we have a fixed number of those each month), and a high score triggers a detention hearing. The score is also published to judges, who override it in roughly a quarter of cases. Our outcomes, new arrest and failure to appear, are observed only for people who were actually released, so the detained group has no labels at all, and the override-released group contributes labels from a population the model itself did not select. We do not collect race or ethnicity, which state law forbids, though we do have ZIP code, age at first arrest, and charge severity.

I need a written procedure my team can run in half a day on each revision, to decide which fairness criterion we enforce, at which threshold, and what we say about it in public. My team is three analysts with no formal training in this literature, and whatever we decide goes to an oversight board for sign-off in open session, so the output has to be a justification a non-specialist can follow. Make it concrete: the questions in order, what counts as an adequate answer to each, the point at which the procedure stops and reports that the criterion we were asked for is not satisfiable alongside the others here, and the point at which it must stop and hand the decision to the board because the remaining question is one of values rather than numbers. Where the choice among criteria is genuinely open, commit to one and give your reason rather than listing the options.

Apply it to three situations: we are asked to equalize false-positive rates across groups; we are asked to make the score mean the same thing across groups at all three tiers, so that people given the same score have the same chance of the outcome; and we discover that the only criterion satisfiable given our labels raises detention rates for the group it was meant to protect. Say plainly what the procedure cannot do. Write the whole thing as continuous prose, with no headings, bullets, bold text, or tables.
