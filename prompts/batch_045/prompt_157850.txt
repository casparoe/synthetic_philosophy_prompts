ok so slightly weird request, bear with me.

Three months ago I got put on a new medication (irrelevent what for) and one of the side effects is gaps. I'm awake, talking, driving fine, but chunks just dont get recorded. Concretely: my drive home has two exits off the highway that look identical, ten minutes apart, and maybe half the time I honestly cannot tell whether I've already passed the first one. Not "probably not" — genuinely no information. So the absent-minded driver thing has stopped being a cute puzzle and is now just Tuesday.

I want a short story about it. The setup as I understand it — you do the arithmetic, I'm probably mangling it: first exit is bad (0), second exit is home (4), miss both and you sleep in your car at a rest area (1). Far as I can tell, the best plan you can make before setting off is to carry a coin and peel off with some fixed probability at each exit, which beats both "never exit" and anything a plain non-random rule gets you — but it's unstable, in the sense that standing at an exit with the plan already made, you'd want to reopen it. I want the story to feel that instability rather than explain it.

Here's the part I actually want, though, and it isn't the standard version. My gaps aren't only about which exit. They're also about whether I've decided already. I'll come up on an exit, do the whole deliberation — right, coin says stay on — and then a moment later I'm deliberating again, and I can't tell whether this is a new exit or whether I'm re-running the decision I made twenty seconds ago at this same one. Sometimes the deliberation feels familiar, but of course it does, everything feels familiar the second time.

So put a character in that. Not just "which choice point am I at" but "is this deliberation new." And take seriously what I think is the real nerve here — that "I am deliberating now" seems like it ought to be evidence, self-locating evidence the way "I am at an exit" is, but the only way to use it is a fixed-point thing, what you should do depends on what you'd have done, and in the nested case there are two of those loops feeding each other and I genuinely don't know whether it settles down.

Constraints, sorry, I have opinions:

- No reveal. It is not a tumour, he is not dead, it's not a simulation, it doesn't turn out he's been looping for years. The gaps stay gaps. It's a decision problem, not a plot twist.
- Not horror, not creepy-unreliable-narrator. Dry, a bit sad, a bit funny. Guy doing arithmetic in the dark.
- Don't cure it. No app, no phone call from his daughter, no doctor with good news.
- Don't let him solve it. If anything let him get worse at it: he develops little rituals — moving a coin between the cup holders, saying the exit number out loud, tapping the wheel twice — that he knows are epistemically worthless and does anyway. That's the bit I want landed. What is deliberating for, when its output can't be stored anywhere.

Story, 1200–1800 words. Then a short note after, maybe 300 words, no citations and no paper names (I can't look anything up from here and wouldn't trust myself to anyway), on three things: whether the nested version has a best plan at all, whether randomizing still helps, and whether "I'm deliberating now" does any real evidential work or is just noise you should throw out.

One more: a friend of mine says the moral of the whole puzzle is that you should want to be unable to reconsider — knowledge is bad for you, and the best version of this guy is one who can't reopen the question. I think that's glib and I can't say why. If the story takes a side on it, fine, but let it be a side the story earns, not one the character announces to someone over coffee.
