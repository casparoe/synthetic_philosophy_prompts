I hack together little agent-based sims on weekends and I'm writing a blog post for other devs who've absorbed the slogan version of this stuff — "sure, defecting wins one round, but iterate it long enough and cooperation takes over." I want a counter-case that's actually fair to that slogan, because the whole point of the post is "look, every condition the textbooks name was satisfied and it still collapsed." So: design me a simulation setup where that happens.

The spec:

- Three accounts have to be run on the same setup. (1) One-shot dominance: defecting strictly dominates, so defect. (2) Iterated reciprocity / shadow of the future: cooperate, and crucially I want its precondition genuinely met, not hand-waved — state the threshold condition you're using (I think it's w > (T−R)/(T−P) for a TFT-ish strategy to resist an always-defect invasion, which for 5/3/1/0 gives 0.5, but derive it in a line or two and correct me if I've got it wrong), then plug in your actual numbers and show the inequality holds with some margin. (3) The population-level story — strategies that earn more get copied / spread — which has to come out as "defection wins," and you have to get there from the dynamics, not just assert it.

- Everything concrete enough that I can code it in ~200 lines of Python: payoff matrix (and it should be a normal-looking PD, not something knife-edge like P = 2.99 against R = 3), population size, who plays whom each round, fixed rounds vs. continuation probability, error/noise rate if you use one, what each agent can observe and remember, how strategies update between generations, starting mix, and how long you run it.

- No cheating, and here's what I mean by that: the cooperators must not be playing something dumb. Whatever they do has to be the right move in the noise-free version of the same setup. The result must not be a seed artifact or hinge on one weird strategy in the initial mix — say what you'd log to convince me of that. And no telepathy: agents can't read each other's source code, no perfect predictors, no central authority beyond something a real system could plausibly have (a shared append-only log is fine).

- Then: which single structural feature is doing the work, what happens if I flip just that one variable (I want the sim to go back to cooperation — tell me the sign and why it flips), and what someone who believes account (2) would say to dismiss your case, plus your reply to them.

No bibliographies or "see X (2006)" — I can't evaluate those and I don't care who said it first, I want numbers I can run. Names for strategies in plain English ("copy your partner's last move", "never forgive") are fine.

Give me the actual answer as one JSON object, no prose outside it, with this schema:

- "params": object with keys "T", "R", "P", "S" (numbers), "population" (int), "matching" (string), "continuation_probability" (number), "noise_rate" (number), "strategies_in_initial_mix" (array of short strings), "update_rule" (string), "ticks" (int)
