Every time the scans go up my uncle kills the thread with "history is written by the winners," and his point seams to be that the families' story should win by default, because anything written down was produced by whoever had the power to write. I think that's doing more work than one sentence can bear. Can you lay out the different precise claims that line might be making — about who produces records, about whose version survives, about wether the problem is lies or just silences, about what even counts as "the winners," about whether it's a claim about bias or about what's true or about what we owe the losers — and say for each how plausible it is and whether it actually gets my uncle his conclusion? Flag the readings that quietly assume community memory is less shaped by power than the archive is, since I suspect his argument needs that and I doubt it.
