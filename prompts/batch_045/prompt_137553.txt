Here's the thing I keep going back and forth on, and I want you to argue my side of it. The case that farming was the worst mistake in human history — short work weeks, decent diets, less infectious disease among foragers, then a long decline in stature and health after the Neolithic — is empirically pretty plausible, but I dont think it actually establishes that the transition was a *mistake*. Use this test: judge it by whether it made things worse for the people who made it plus whichever of their descendants would have existed anyway, and set aside everybody whose existence depended on farming happening. Then make two moves. First, argue that the switchers had reasons that survive the affluence comparison — stored grain lets you smooth consumption across bad years in a way foragers cant, and forager affluence rests on a low population density that your farming neighbors wont let you keep. Second, argue that once the extra people are excluded, the confident verdict loses most of what made it sound devastating, and that anyone who wants to count them has to actually size up the tradeoff between a lower average and a vastly larger population instead of just waving at the repugnant conclusion. Skip the "but think of literature and vaccines" reply, and keep it under a page.
