I'm in a reading group on philosophy of machine learning and we got into an argument about Solomonoff induction that one guy in the group basically won, and I cant stop thinking about it because I'm not sure he was actually right.

His story went like this: Solomonoff induction is provably the best possible inductive method, it converges to the truth faster than any other predictor up to a constant factor, and that constant is just an artifact of whichever universal Turing machine you pick as your reference machine for the prior. So, he said, this is Occam's razor finally made rigorous: shorter descriptions get exponentially more prior weight, so the simplest hypothesis consistent with the data ends up with most of the credence, and science's preference for simple theories is vindicated once and for all. And he brushed off the two obvious complaints — sure it's uncomputable, but that's just an engineering problem we'd solve with approximations; and sure the prior depends on the machine, but the invariance result says switching machines only multiplies the prior by a constant, and constants wash out as data comes in.

Two things feel off to me and I want to know whether I'm the confused one. First, I proposed a fix for the machine-dependence worry that he waved away: instead of committing to one reference machine, why not take a mixture over all universal machines, each weighted by how short its own description is? He said this "doesn't change anything" but wouldn't explain why, and I dont really see whether it buys us machine-independence in any real sense or whether it's secretly just picking one machine again in a fancier way. Second, I keep suspecting that "converges to the truth" is doing less work than it sounds like it's doing — does the result say the method ends up believing the true theory, or only that its predictions stop diverging from the true distribution? does it say anything at all if the world isn't computable? and is the exponential penalty on description length actually load-bearing in the convergence proof, or is it mostly there so the prior sums to something finite? Basically: is my friend's picture right, and if not, what exactly does Solomonoff induction establish about Occam's razor?
