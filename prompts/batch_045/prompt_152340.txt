I am drafting the framing chapter of a survey on the epistemology of markets, and I want the specification fixed before I score any views. Fix the purpose of a theory in this area as follows: it is to serve as microfoundations for market design, meaning it must say when a market will have any volume at all, and when its price is something a non-participant may treat as evidence about the world.

Write the desiderata such a theory has to meet: the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it may not collapse, with the motivating case behind each, and mark each requirement as non-negotiable or as a preference of one camp. Candidates I want explicitly adjudicated rather than assumed: explaining nonzero volume without appealing to irrationality; separating informational motives from risk-sharing, liquidity, and noise; respecting that another party's willingness to trade is itself evidence; saying whose beliefs a price aggregates and whether that price is evidence for a bystander; and surviving the fact that no-trade results, once published and commonly known, are themselves announcements that change the situation they describe.

The case I most want handled I have not seen treated. Call a market a blind book when orders are sealed, clearing is at a uniform price, and no participant observes any other order or can condition on another's willingness to trade. An account that explains trade purely through the inferences traders draw from others' willingness to trade predicts that volume collapses to zero in a blind book populated by traders with identical information and only mildly different exposures; suppose instead that volume falls by roughly half and then stabilizes. Build into the list what a theory must do with residual volume of that kind — must it explain it, or may it explain it away — and say whether the list should include protocol-neutrality: that an explanation of why trade occurs not rest on features of the trading protocol that are merely information channels.

Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and name the single requirement that excludes the largest number of the usual families of views (noise traders, heterogenous priors, ambiguity aversion, bounded rationality, hedging). Score those families against the list in a few lines at most.
