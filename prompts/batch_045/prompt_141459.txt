I serve on the Charter Review Committee of the National Tenant Federation, a merger of about ninety local tenant unions formed fourteen months ago under an interim charter; roughly forty thousand members are represented, and the founding convention in late October will vote on a permanent charter. Our committee has to circulate a written opinion three weeks before that convention, with a recommendation on Motion 7, which the sponsors have made a take-it-or-leave-it package: no part of it may be voted on separately. Under the interim charter, an amendment needs a two-thirds vote of the convention and then ratification by a majority of locals within six months. The sponsors' complaint is practical: last spring a technical fix to the dues formula passed the convention easily but died when only forty-one locals voted in time, and they think the ratification stage makes the federation unable to act in an emergency. Motion 7 has three parts. Part A would let any future convention amend the charter by a simple majority, with no ratification by locals. Part B would entrench a Mission Clause declaring housing a human right and committing the federation to non-partisanship and one-member-one-vote, stating that it is perpetually entrenched and may not be amended or repealed, adding that no amendment whose effect would be to weaken or circumvent the Mission Clause shall be adopted, and creating a three-person Charter Panel empowered to disallow any amendment that in its judgment has that effect, whose determinations as to the meaning and application of the Mission Clause shall be final. Part C, moved separately by one large local as an amendment to Part B, would provide that entrenched clauses lapse nine years after adoption unless reaffirmed by a two-thirds vote of a convention.

The committee is split in a way I can't adjudicate. One member argues that Motion 7 is self-undermining: if a simple majority can amend anything, then nothing is unamendable, including the Mission Clause, so Part B does nothing at all; but if Part B really does entrench the Mission Clause, then Part A cannot have validly lowered the threshold either, since the two-thirds rule was the source of the convention's authority to put the motion to a vote in the first place — so either way the motion fails to do what its sponsors say it does. Others point out a different problem: if the Panel has the final word on what the Mission Clause means, and a later convention can by simple majority change the Panel's size, how it is appointed, whether it is funded, or what quorum it needs, then the entrenchment can be hollowed out from the side without anyone ever touching the Mission Clause; a delegate at our hearing also suggested that a convention could simply resolve that a resolution of the convention is deemed to be a ratification by the locals, or redefine who counts as a member or an affiliate, and thereby reach indirectly what it is forbidden to reach directly.

What I need is the opinion itself, three or four pages, addressed to the committee and written so that elected tenant organizers with no legal training can follow it. Please give us a bottom line — recommend that the convention adopt Motion 7 as drafted, vote it down, or vote it down and reintroduce a substitute — and if you recommend a substitute, say in enough detail what the amendment rule should actually say, since we would need wording to move from the floor. Please also say whether the anti-circumvention sentence and the Charter Panel should be kept, dropped, or redesigned, and whether the nine-year lapse in Part C strengthens the entrenchment or quietly gives it up. Finally, I want the strongest version of the sponsors' case stated fairly — that supermajority-plus-ratification lets a minority of small locals freeze a national organisation, that the founding convention is the one moment when broad agreement exists, and that entrenchment is the only protection available if a faction ever takes over — before you explain why it does not carry the day, or tell me plainly if you think it does. Please argue from the structure of the rules rather than by citing authorities; we will not be able to check references.
