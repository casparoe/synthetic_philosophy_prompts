I'm trying to work out what makes belief in a conspiracy theory epistemically respectable, and my verdicts on individual cases don't compose the way I assumed they would. Three mid-level officials of a city transport authority taking bribes from a contractor, supported by a leaked email chain naming amounts, dates and a bank account: reasonable to believe well before it was confirmed. Forty-odd people across a state environmental agency and a chemical firm suppressing a study showing that a widely used pesticide harms children: also reasonable, on the same kind of evidence. Thousands of people across manufacturers, journals and regulators suppressing a cheap generic that cures a common disease: not reasonable — and the difference isn't that the evidence is weaker in kind, it's that at that scale I expect a defector, and I treat the missing leak as evidence against. Two further verdicts complicate this. In the bribery case the officials' denial — which the theory predicted — slightly raised my confidence; but in a theory that has absorbed three successive failed predictions by explaining each failure as staged by the conspirators, that same absorptive move makes me trust it less. And I want to say a theory was unreasonable while it rested entirely on gaps and inconsistencies in the official account, even in the case where it turned out to be true.

My tentative principle: a conspiracy theory earns credence to the degree that the conspiracy it posits is leak-prone — enough insiders with incentives to defect that disclosure would be expected if it were true — and to the degree that it does not itself explain away the absence of disclosure or the failure of its own predictions. Test that against my cases, state precisely what it says and which clause each case motivates, tell me which of my verdicts it cannot keep and why, and give its verdict on two cases I have no settled view about: a theory whose one dramatic leak turns out to have been forged, and an outcome everyone agrees was produced by hundreds of people each pursuing ordinary career incentives with nobody coordinating. Please take a definite position rather than laying out the options — if one of my verdicts has to be overturned, say which one and defend the choice.
