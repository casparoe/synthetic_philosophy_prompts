Hey — I cover science and ideas, and I'm doing the lead item for my fortnightly newsletter on a fight I keep running into in happiness research: the heterogeneity problem. You know the shape of it. Hedonism wants pleasures to be one kind of stuff so you can add them up, trade them off, and rank whole lives, but the relief of a headache stopping, the absorption of finally cracking a hard proof, whatever it is that happens when a joke lands, and the low warmth of an old friendship carrying on dont seem to share any single felt quality — and every fallback (a common neural signature, a desire-satisfaction reading, straight revealed preference) quietly changes the subject. I've also been chewing on something I'm calling the comparability-ladder: maybe we never need a global comparison of two pleasures, only local ones, but local comparisons chain — you can walk from the proof to a doughnut through ten thousand intermediate pleasures, each step defensible on it's own, the two ends absurd when they finally meet. I'd rather be laughed at properly about that than read one more explainer.

So: write me a satire. Form: a leaked draft consultation paper from the Wellbeing Directorate of a national statistics office, along the lines of "Proposal for a Statutory Unit of Pleasure (the hedon)", deadpan bureaucratic voice, numbered sections, around 800 words, dated, with a consultation deadline and an equality-impact paragraph. The document is trying in total seriousness to make pleasure measurable so wellbeing can be aggregated, audited and budgeted, and it should fail — but nobody in it ever says that pleasures have nothing in common, and there are no villains. The joke has to come from the philosophy being right.

Things it has to contain:

- all four pleasures above, with the unit breaking differently on each;
- an abandoned search for a common felt quality, a neural-signature fallback, and a case where someone wants something more and enjoys it less;
- my ladder: a pilot that chains local comparisons into a 10,000-rung "hedonic ladder", every rung individually defensible, the endpoints ridiculous, and the Directorate extremely pleased with it;
- at least one moment where the problem is solved by stipulation — a hedon is defined as whatever the instrument reports — while the document insists it has done no such thing.

No real philosophers, no real companies, no named living people; invented officials only. It should be funny to someone who knows the literature and readable to subscribers who don't.

After the piece, 150 words max: where you took liberties. Flag anything that rests on a contested reading rather than on something both sides mostly grant, and say what a defender of a felt-quality account — or of the ladder idea — would have to show to make you rewrite it. If you think the ladder doesn't survive contact with a philosopher, say that in the note instead of quietly dropping it.

Last line of the whole thing, one sentence, beginning "Verdict:" and naming the single claim the piece actually commits to.
