Trying to work out what I actually think about when you get knowledge from being told something, and my gut verdicts don't obviously add up. Going case by case:

- a stranger at the station tells me the 6.15 goes from platform 4, I believe him, I know it. I have no evidence at all about whether he's reliable.
- my flatmate texts me the time; she sounds completely normal, but she's actually blackout drunk and happened to get it right. I don't want to say I know.
- my colleague says the meeting moved to 3; she got it from someone who was in the room. Knowledge. The same fact passed along a chain of twenty people across the office: I want to say no, but I can't point to the link where it breaks.
- my landlord says the boiler's being fixed Tuesday; he believes it because he dreamt it; it turns out to be true. No knowledge. But a used-car salesman who's lying about the service history points out a dent that really is there, as fake candour, and I do think I know about the dent.
- Dave wildly exaggerates about his own exploits, but tells me there's a fox in the garden: knowledge. Dave also always says "the money's in the post" and never sends it, and when Dave tells me the time I don't think I know it — even though lying about money has nothing to do with clocks.
- a friend tells me our boss groped her: I know, and asking her for evidence would itself be a betrayal. A friend tells me our boss is skimming the till: I don't think I know till there's corroboration, and asking for it is fine. Both are just her say-so.

The principle I keep circling back to is: you get knowledge when the speaker is reliable about that kind of thing and you have no specific reason to doubt them on this occasion. That handles the platform case and the fox, but it also gives me the drunk flatmate, which I don't want, and it says nothing about the chain.

Is there a principle that does better — ideally stated precisely enough that I can see which of my verdicts motivated each clause — and what does it say about cases I haven't listed, like an AI chatbot telling me something true, or a statement someone is visibly being forced to read out? And be straight with me about which of my verdicts it can't save: I'd rather drop one then have a principle with a separate clause for every case.
