Longtime lurker here. Me and three other people from the usual corners of the internet have decided to blow roughly a year of spare evenings on building what we're jokingly calling a pump-debugging kit: a small, public, actually-usable toolkit for taking a thought experiment that everyone deploys as a conversation-stopper and working out how much of its persuasive force is doing real work rather than rhetorical work. Think trolley variants, teleportation and fission setups, experience-machine and zombie-style arguments, torture-vs-dust-specks things, infinite-ethics monsters.

We've sketched four components and I want a defended ordering before we sink the year in:

1. Framing rotation — rerun the same case with the features the theory itself says are irrelevant swapped around (first person vs. third, order the cases are presented in, how much concrete sensory detail there is, the scale, whether it's a lever or a button or a spoken command, hospital vs. spaceship vs. subway). Log which verdicts flip.
2. Carry distance — walk the case away from the original one step at a time (asymmetric versions, iterated versions, versions where the mechanism is explained vs. left mysterious) and see where people stop giving "the same" answer, and whether the decay is smooth or a cliff.
3. Identification swap — replace the person you're invited to imagine being with someone you're much less primed to identify with: a stranger of a different age and background, a far-future person, an AI, yourself twenty years from now. Hypothesis: a lot of pumps are secretly powered by self-insertion, and nobody notices because the self-insertion is the default.
4. The ledger — a public table per pump: which transformations flip it, which don't, and what people claim the point of the pump actually is.

So: which of these is upstream of which, and which would you tell four unaffiliated amateurs with no budget, no IRB, and no ability to run anything bigger than a forum poll to build first? I care about the criteria and the trade-offs — tractability, neglectedness, how much of the rest depends on it — not just your gut ranking. What would you deprioritize as overrated: big-n intuition surveys, trying to settle in advance whether intuitions are evidence at all, formalizing everything as causal graphs? And what concrete result over the next six months would move something up or down your list?

Two more things I want folded in. First, which family of cases should be the training ground, given that "best for developing the method" and "most likely to actually change someone's mind once the method works" probably point different directions. Second, the failure mode I'm most worried about is our kit turning into a license to dismiss any thought experiment you already disliked — "your pump is fragile, therefore it doesn't count." Is the reveal-vs-manufacture distinction salvageable, or is settling that a distraction from just grinding out the tables? No surveys of the literature and definitly no reading list — give me your own ordering and defend it.
