Last night I was disscussing this with my friends after our reading group, and we got stuck on why your reply to the ESP objection feels both clever and like a bit of a dodge. As I recall, you say, and correct me in character if I have it wrong, that if telepathy, clairvoyance, and precognition are real, we can simply put the machine and the human competitor in a room made proof against them, or else allow no communication except the official questions and answers over the teleprinter, so the interrogator's extrasensory powers cannot be used to peek at which is which. My friends and I then invented two variants to sharpen the problem. In variant A, both competitors are in rooms proof against telepathy, clairvoyance, and precognition, and all questioning goes through the teleprinter; but the LLM, being a machine, gets its sampling randomness from a physical noise source, and the human being in the other room is a real live person whose small finger movements are not driven by any noise diode. In variant B, we try to block psychokinesis too by locking the machine's randomizer inside the shielded room and forbidding any wireless link, but then the machine is just executing a stored pseudo-random sequence, which my friend says makes it a fancy lookup table rather than a learning machine.

Here is my opening move: if psychokinesis is one of the four items of extrasensory perception you ask us to accept as an hypothesis, then a room made proof against telepathy is not enough, because psychokinesis does not need to read anything; it can reach into the machine's physical randomizer and bias the sequence of coin flips the machine produces when the interrogator asks for a long run of them. A human asked to imitate coin flips cannot be forced by the interrogator's mind to emit a statistically biased sequence, but the LLM's flips come from a physical noise source that psychokinesis could in principle nudge, so even with your room made proof against telepathy the interrogator identifies the machine by a randomness test. Worse, if you try to escape variant B by making the machine deterministic or by using a precomputed pseudo-random table, you either remove the very contingency that made the machine interesting or you turn it into the old lookup-table worry that you yourself would want to avoid. So I say your ESP discussion definately only answers the reading parts of ESP and not the writing part, and that if psychokinesis is real and can act on a machine's insides, then either the imitation game cannot be made fair or you owe us an account of a room made proof against psychokinesis, which sounds no more possible than a room made proof against telepathy once you think about what proof against mind-over-matter would even mean.
