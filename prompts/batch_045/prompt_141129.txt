I'm a PhD student in distributed data management — my dissertation is on merge semantics for replicated stores, so I spend my days with version vectors, multi-value registers, lattice joins, integrity constraints, and repair-based approaches to querying data that violates those constraints. I've been sitting in on a philosophy-of-logic reading group this term, more or less as a tourist, and I have a suspicion I'd like properly checked rather than waved away. My sense is that dialetheism is either something my field already does routinely under another name or something my field structurally cannot express at all, and I can't tell which. My advisor's view — which I think is wrong but can't refute — is that it's all just SQL NULLs; my own view is that NULL behaves like a gap, not a glut, but I can't turn that into an argument on my own. (This came up at a workshop dinner and I've been chewing on it since.)

So here is the job. Take the dialetheist position apart into its component claims and translate them into my vocabulary one at a time, as faithfully as you can. The claims I have in mind are: the glut/gap contrast itself, i.e. the insistence that some contradictions are true rather than that some questions have no answer, and that this is a metaphysical thesis about truth-bearers rather than an epistemic thesis about our information; the rejection of explosion together with the rejection of disjunctive syllogism that it seems to require, given the standard argument for explosion from addition and disjunctive syllogism; the semantics of the paraconsistent logic usually called LP, in which a sentence may take both truth values and validity is defined as preservation of being *at least* true rather than of truth simpliciter; the motivation from the self-referential paradoxes — the Liar and Russell's, which Priest reads at face value as exhibiting a common inclosure structure rather than as reductios of the notions involved; the need to stop short of trivialism, and the apparently related difficulty of saying, in the dialetheist's own vocabulary, that the contradictions are local and that a given contradiction is *merely* true-and-false rather than a licence for everything; the classical-recapture manoeuvre, whereby classical reasoning is supposed to be recovered inside consistent regions; and the standing objection that the dialetheist's negation is not really negation. For each of these, tell me what maps cleanly onto something in my toolbox — multi-value registers that keep concurrent writes side by side, joins in a lattice, eventual convergence, repairs with the certain-answer and brave-answer distinction over an inconsistent instance, three-valued logic and NULL, negation-as-failure under the closed-world assumption, AGM-style revision with consistency as a regulative ideal — what survives only if I distort it to fit, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all.

I've also been playing with two notions of my own, and I want them either used or diagnosed. Call a replicated store *repair-closed* if every state that violates the integrity constraints can be brought to a constraint-satisfying state by some sequence of admissible updates; my guess is that dialetheism, in my terms, is the claim that some stores are not repair-closed and that this is a fact about the data rather than a defect of the schema. And call a merge *glut-preserving* if, once it has accepted both a fact and its complement, no later admissible update can remove either — as against the *gap-generating* merges I actually know how to build, which answer an inconsistency by withholding, either returning the intersection over all repairs or defaulting to unknown. I suspect every merge in my repertoire is gap-generating, and I want to know whether that is a contingent engineering fact, a consequence of what a query language can express, or a sign that "glut" was never a distinct option to begin with — that the gap/glut distinction collapses as soon as you track provenance and are allowed to ask which replica asserted what. There is one further structural worry I can't shake: my framework has no way of saying, from inside, that the store is in a bad state, without stepping up to a meta-level that is itself assumed consistent, which looks uncomfortably like the dialetheist's own problem with asserting that not everything is true. So please close with the residue report: what my vocabulary carries, what it distorts, what it cannot say at all, and — the part I actually care about — whether that residue is a limitation of replicated-data theory or evidence that the dialetheist's claim was less determinate than it sounds.
