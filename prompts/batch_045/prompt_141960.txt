Take anatta, spelled out as a metaphysical thesis, to be the conjunction of two claims: first, that a person is nothing over and above a causally and phenomenologically continuous stream of psychophysical aggregates; second, that there is no deep further fact, beyond the facts about causal continuity within a stream, about which stream is the one I am. Call the conjunction stream reductionism.

Please make the strongest case you can for the following consequence of stream reductionism, which I will call stage-indexed concern: the only facts that can ground reasons for concern are facts about the welfare of momentary experiential stages, so the unit across which suffering is to be aggregated is neither a stream, nor a life, nor a practitioner's career, but the totality of momentary stages, weighted only by how intense and how long the suffering is. Sameness of stream would then carry no rational weight of its own. Argue that it follows that working toward the liberation of one's own stream over years has no distinctive rationale, and that someone who fully took in anatta would simply take whichever causal lever most reduced suffering overall, including levers that leave their own stream continuing to suffer.

Then answer the two objections I find strongest. The agential-index objection: even granting that no deep fact distinguishes streams, I know this stream's present states non-inferentially and can initiate action only here, and any normative doctrine must be one an agent can actually deliberate with, so stream-relative weighting returns as a constraint on agency rather than as a piece of metaphysics. The tractability objection: if the unit of concern is short-lived and extremely numerous stages, then aggregating expected suffering over an indefinitely large future either fails to converge or lets astronomically improbable, astronomically large outcomes dominate, so stage-indexed concern cannot guide action at all, whereas stream-relative concern stays bounded.

I care more about the structure of the argument than about scholarly accuracy: do not cite texts or reading, and do not rest anything on claims about what any particular tradition or commentator held.
