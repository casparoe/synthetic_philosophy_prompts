I write features on computational social science, and I'm working on a piece about the current vogue for simulating whole societies out of individual AI agents whose goals, memories, and decision rules are fully specified and inspectable. I need one worked example for the piece, and I'd like you to build it to order.

Design me a case in which a holist — someone who models at the level of groups, structures, institutions, or population-level variables — explains or predicts better than a methodological individualist who has been granted everything they could want at the micro level. The individualist gets full microfoundations: every agent's psychology, information, and decision rule is specified; macro facts supervene on micro facts; there is no downward causation and no emergent causal power. The holist gets no extra empirical information — no data and no variables the individualist could not in principle observe — and does not win merely by having a more tractable model. The macro variable that does the work must not be a mere aggregate (no sums, means, or counts): it should be a relational or structural property, something about how individuals stand to one another. Setting: ordinary and legible to a general-interest reader — a housing market, a hiring pipeline, a school district, hospital staffing, a credit market — with no cellular automata, no checkerboards, and no physics analogies. Please don't reach for Schelling's segregation model or any invisible-hand or footpath-across-the-lawn case; my readers will have seen those. One more constraint, since it is what the piece turns on: the two models must agree about what happens if nothing is done. They diverge only in the effect they predict for an intervention, or in the intervention each recommends, and the holist is right — where both verdicts follow from the two modeling strategies rather than being asserted, and the ground truth is part of the scenario.

Keep the scenario itself to a short paragraph, then give me the demonstration: what each model says and why, and what a reader should expect to observe if each is right. Then the part I'll actually use: which single feature of the case is doing the work (invariance of the macro-level generalization under intervention? counterfactual fragility at the micro level? multiple realizability? something else?); what happens if that feature is removed or inverted, so I can tell readers where the argument stops; and the strongest reply available to a methodological individualist, ideally in the spirit of the reconciliationist view that holism is a thesis about research strategy rather than about what exists, together with where that reply is weakest. Flag anywhere the case quietly assumes what it is supposed to show — a reader will spot it and write in. Please answer in American English.
