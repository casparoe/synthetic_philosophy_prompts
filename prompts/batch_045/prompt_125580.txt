I am a finishing PhD student in philosophy, and in three weeks I am giving a job talk at a department that has no one at all working on AI or machine learning. The search is in philosophy of mind. The talk is provisionally titled *Understanding Without a Life: What Minimal-Pair Evidence Does and Does Not Show*, and it defends two claims. The first is methodological: I argue that the debate about whether large language models understand anything has stalled because both sides test the wrong property. Success on a benchmark cannot distinguish a system that is tracking the evidential support actually present in the prompt from one that is tracking surface features that merely correlate with it, so I propose testing what I call *support sensitivity* instead. The instrument is a class of items I have been building, which I call support-inverting pairs: take a task family where a model does well, then construct two prompts matched on everything cheap and statistical — length, topical vocabulary, unigram distribution, and, crucially, near-identical perplexity under the model's own distribution — but in which the evidentially correct completion flips. If accuracy survives only where the surface cue and the real support happen to coincide, and collapses toward chance where they come apart, that is evidence of cue tracking rather than support tracking. On about a dozen task families I get collapse, sometimes nearly to chance. The second claim is the one I actually care about and the one I expect to be hurt on: I run a deliberately rough analogue of the same design on human subjects — undergraduates, time-pressured, working on material outside their training — and I get a partial version of the same collapse. I then argue that our habit of treating the human collapse as a mere performance factor (haste, fatigue, domain mismatch) while treating the model collapse as a revelation about the absence of understanding is not itself supported by anything in the data. I call this the charity asymmetry, and my conclusion is deflationary rather than dismissive: the model's failure is real and diagnostic, but what it diagnoses is a specific structural fact about which cues the training signal made available, not a global verdict that nothing is understood.
