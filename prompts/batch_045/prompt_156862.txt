I am finishing a novel whose central scene is a one-shot prisoner's dilemma between two runs of the same emulation: one man, the same memories and the same code, instantiated in two rooms, each knowing this about the other, neither able to communicate. My narrator wants them to cooperate, on the reasoning that each copy is running the other's deliberation and so each should treat its own choice as settling both choices — the argument I have seen called "superrationality" in the literature a friend pointed me to. My antagonist, a game theorist, is meant to answer her with the argument below. I cannot tell whether I have written him a real refutation or a piece of sophistry, and I would sooner cut a sentence than publish a speech that a careful reader can pull apart. So that nothing turns on vagueness, fix the payoffs: mutual cooperation 3 each, mutual defection 1 each, defecting against a cooperator 5, cooperating against a defector 0.

(1) Against an exact duplicate — same memories, same procedure, same situation — cooperation is rationally required. Whatever I settle on, my duplicate settles on too, so I should choose as though my choice settles both: cooperate and receive 3, or defect and receive 1.

(2) That requirement rests entirely on a probabilistic fact: my act and the duplicate's act are perfectly correlated in the right direction — conditional on my cooperating, it cooperates; conditional on my defecting, it defects. No influence passes between the rooms, so causation is doing no work here.

(3) Correlation is a matter of degree, and what produces it is similarity of decision procedure: the more closely another agent's procedure resembles mine, the more reliably its verdict tracks mine. Numerical identity is only the limiting case.

(4) So the superrational case for cooperation must itself come in degrees. An agent whose reasoning closely resembles mine is owed nearly the cooperation I owe a duplicate; an agent whose reasoning resembles mine somewhat is owed somewhat less; and so on, continuously.

(5) Similarity is also chain-like. Between any two agents you care to name, however unlike, there is a finite sequence of agents each differing from her neighbour only slightly — a sibling, a compatriot, a stranger, a stranger shaped by very different institutions, and at the far end someone whose entire procedure is: defect, always.

(6) Iterating (4) along the chain in (5): each link spends only a small part of the case for cooperation, so the case never reaches zero. Diminished, perhaps, but still in force at the far endpoint. Superrationality therefore requires cooperation with any agent whatever, including one I know will defect whatever I do.

(7) Against a guaranteed defector, cooperating returns 0 and defecting returns 1, however things turn out. No theory of rational choice worth defending can mandate an act that is worse on every possible outcome.

(8) So superrationality is either empty — it obliges cooperation with no one, not even my duplicate — or it is absurd. Either way my narrator has nothing to say to the man in the other room.

My suspicion is that the rot is somewhere in the middle of the chain, but I cannot locate the sentence, and I would rather be told the truth than confirmed in my suspicion. Two constraints, if they help you choose where to cut: the scene requires (1) to survive, because the emotional logic of the book rests on the copies' recognition of each other; and I do not want (7) to go, because I do not want readers concluding that my narrator's view obliges her to be exploited. If (1) and (7) can both stand, I want to know which step is paying for it and what that step costs my antagonist's credibility. If they cannot both stand, tell me plainly which one I have to give up and what the book loses.

Please answer in two parts.

(a) A table with one row for each numbered step, 1 through 8, and exactly these columns: step | verdict (keep, revise, or reject) | flaw (a dash if none) | why_it_reads_as_innocent | price_of_rejecting_it.

(b) A single JSON object, and nothing inside it but these fields: "condemned_step" (the step number), "flaw_kind" (one of: equivocation, quantifier_or_scope_shift, modal_slide, sorites_or_threshold_error, false_premise, invalid_inference), "runner_up_step" (the step number you would cut second), "twin_case_survives" (true or false), "payoff_threshold" (the correlation or agreement level, expressed numerically against the payoffs above, at which cooperation stops being favoured, or null if you think no such number settles it), "narrator_line" (one sentence of dialogue, under thirty words and free of jargon, in which my narrator punctures the argument), "residual_objection" (one sentence: what a sharp reader could still press against the cut you recommend).
