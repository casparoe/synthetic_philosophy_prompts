I got into a long argument with my friend last night about Benjamin Franklin's whole moral-perfection scheme, and now I need someone to settle it. We were stuck waiting for a delayed train (naturally it was raining) and somehow ended up talking about this app he's into called VirtueLoop. It's basically a digital version of Franklin's little book: thirteen virtues, a page for each, and you're supposed to mark black dots for faults. But the app also syncs to your phone and fills in the dots for you automatically. It reads your screentime, your calendar, your bank transactions, even your step count, and at night it decides which virtues you broke. Doomscrolling for two hours? That's a Temperance dot. Impulse buy on Amazon? Frugality dot. Snapping at a coworker over chat? Tranquillity dot, or maybe Moderation. Instead of Franklin's one-virtue-a-week rotation, it draws a random virtue every morning and sends you push notifications to guard that one all day. If you go thirteen days without a dot on the virtue you're tracking, it gives you a Clear Book badge. There's also a pre-commitment feature where it donates twenty bucks to a political cause you hate if your streak breaks.

My friend says Franklin would have loved it. He was an inventor, he liked schemes and gadgets and useful contrivances, he kept all those tables and charts, so obviously he'd upgrade to the quantified-self version: automatic data, pretty graphs, a streak to protect. I totaly disagree. The way I remember it, Franklin's method wasn't really about having perfect data; it was about stopping each evening to examine yourself, using a small number of clearly seperated virtues so you actually knew what you were aiming at, and hammering away at one of them at a time because trying to guard everything at once just meant you got surprised by some other fault. So I think he'd hate the automatic dot-marking, because there's no self-examination left, and he'd especially hate a randomized daily draw, because that throws away the sequential week-by-week habit-building part. I also think an app that gives you a badge for Humility is basically a joke, since the rule for Humility was to model yourself on Jesus and Socrates, not to collect points. But my friend says I'm romanticizing the 18th century and that Franklin would have been first in line for the app.

So if Franklin were alive today and you sat him down with VirtueLoop, or something like it, what would he actually think? Would he use it at all? Would he think the automatic dot-marking is a feature or a cheat? Would he keep his one-virtue-per-week rotation or switch to the app's random daily draw? Would the pre-commitment donation thing strike him as a clever improvement or as a kind of moral laziness? I'm not asking for a citation or a reading list; just tell me what you think his position would be and why, based on what he actually said about the project. And please be straight about how confident you are in your read of him: give me a sense of how sure you are overall, and single out the one point you're least sure about. My friend and I are still texting about it and I want to know who's closer to right, but I also don't want to over-claim that I've got Franklin figured out.
