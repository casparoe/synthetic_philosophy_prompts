My thesis: the "fragment on machines" bit in the Grundrisse keeps getting read as predicting that living labour gets expelled from production until value and profit break down. I think the real limit on automation isn't set by what machines can do but by what I've started calling the repair floor — the irreducible mass of living labour needed per unit of machine-time to keep fixed capital actually functioning as capital: maintenance, calibration, integration, data curation, retraining. Marx was picturing durable hardware; ML-era capital rots fast, so the repair floor can grow even while direct production labour collapses, which is why the "fully automated factory" never quite arrives and why the profit rate doesn't just fall off a cliff.
