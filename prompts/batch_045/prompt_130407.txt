Two friends of mine got into it after dinner the other night and because I was the only person at the table who hasn't read anything on this, I got made the referee. I honestly couldnt follow half of it and I have no idea whos right.

It started with Yusuf saying Malthus was right but early. His version: up to roughly 1800 the Malthusian mechanism is just the correct description of how populations behave — people have more children when real wages go up, the extra labour pushes wages back down, and the positive checks do the rest — and it's still the model people reach for on pre-industrial data. Industrialisation didn't refute the mechanism, he says, it changed the subject. The escape ran on things the theory had no vocabulary for, so the theory didn't fail, it stopped applying.

Marta said that's not a rescue, it's a way of never losing. If a forecast is wrong across the whole window in which anyone could act on it, then "early" and "wrong" are the same word. And she pointed out that Malthus wasn't only forecasting: he drew the policy conclusion too, that relieving the poor mostly just enlarges the population in misery — and that part wasn't early, it was wrong, and it did damage.

Yusuf then split things up: mechanism, forecast, and policy are three separate bets and he'll only defend the first. On the forecast he conceded the timing was off but said Malthus gave rates, not dates, and nothing in the mechanism picks out a century, so getting the century wrong isn't a falsification. Marta came back hard: a ceiling that is always "not yet" is a ceiling with no testable content, and anyway his own numbers put the crunch within decades, not millennia.

Then Yusuf produced the bit I can't stop thinking about. He calls it the excursion test. If something looks like it has broken out of a bound, you have to ask whether the escape is powered by a stock or a flow. A stock — a finite accumulated pile like fossil carbon, or the one-off pulse of fixed nitrogen that came from guano and nitrate deposits and then from Haber-Bosch running on cheap gas — buys you an excursion: a long swing away from the bound that has to come back eventually. What Marta calls a permanent regime change, Yusuf says isn't a transition at all, just a roughly ten-generation holiday funded by a non-renewable windfall, and a deferred forecast is not a refuted one.

Marta said this is exactly the shelter she was complaining about, and then made her strongest point: the preventive check was supposed to be misery and moral restraint, but what actually happened is that getting rich pushed fertility below replacement — which is an anti-Malthusian mechanism, and one that quietly undermines the modern overshoot version too. Yusuf said that's still a population-regulating feedback, that self-limiting growth is Malthusian in structure even when it's anti-Malthusian in detail, and that Malthus probably named the wrong currency anyway: the binding limit looks more like energy and waste sinks than like acres of wheat.

Marta: "everything is limited by something" is not a discovery.
