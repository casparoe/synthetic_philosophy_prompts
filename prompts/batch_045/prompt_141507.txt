I'm helping a supervisor write a survey on the symbol grounding problem. She has been going through our list of systems and assigning each a verdict — grounded, not grounded, occasionally a hedge — and one system is left that I have to draft a paragraph about before she rules on it. So I'd like to predict her verdict and to understand what she is implicitly running.

Her verdicts so far:

- A rule-based symbolic system with a hand-coded ontology, queried in a formal language, no sensors: not grounded.
- A convolutional network trained on photographs with crowdworker-supplied category labels: not grounded — the only link between the label and the things is a person's keystrokes.
- A transformer trained on web text, no perception and no action: not grounded.
- The same kind of model, but trained instead on transcripts of people narrating what they see while they perform a task: still not grounded; testimony about the world doesn't transmit grounding.
- A network trained from scratch on a robot with a camera and a gripper, by reinforcement learning with reward supplied by the environment: grounded.
- A network that receives continuous video and audio and is trained only to predict its own next sensory input, with no task and no ability to act: grounded, though she called it a degenerate case.

The remaining system is a large language model pretrained on text alone, its weights frozen, wired to a real robot through two thin adapter networks: one turns camera frames into a sequence of input embeddings, the other turns the model's output tokens into motor commands. Only the adapters are trained, by reinforcement on real tasks such as clearing a table without breaking anything. After training the composite system discriminates fragile from sturdy objects in what it does, and the discrimination collapses if the camera stream is replaced by a human's verbal description of the same scene. The language model's own weights, and so everything it learned from text, are untouched by any of this.

My questions: is the model's representation of fragile grounded, on her way of thinking? What are the rules that fit her earlier verdicts and come apart on this case, what does each of them predict here, and which is the better guess about her? And what further case would separate them — I was going to ask her about an otherwise identical setup in which the gradients are allowed to flow into the language model itself, but I'm not sure that's the sharpest probe. One further thing I'm unclear about: should the verdict attach to the frozen model, to the composite system, or to the individual symbol, and does the answer to that change what she should say about this case?
