I'm retired — spent my working life in a lab, protein NMR of all things — and for about a year now I've been sitting in on a philosophy/cognitive science reading group at the university near me. The chap who runs it has been going through the frame problem, and I've started noting down his verdicts on which setups actually "have" it, because they strike me as less consistent than he seems to think.

His verdicts so far:

- the old story where a symbolic planner has to work out whether pulling its wagon out of the room also moves the bomb that's sitting on the wagon: yes, textbook case
- a blocks-world planner, six blocks, four operators, closed list of actions: no — he says the domain's too small, there's nothing to choose between
- a chess program: no
- a bat catching a moth: no. An ant finding its way back to the nest: also no
- a driver who is absorbed in the driving and has to notice that the brake lights ahead matter: no, and he got quite sharp about it, said that if this counts then the subject has been changed
- a hand-built rule-based system for diagnosing diesel engines, few hundred rules, curated by its engineers: no, that's a maintainence headache, not the frame problem
- a robot that re-perceives the whole world from scratch each step and carries nothing over from one step to the next: no, nothing to update

Next week's case, which I'm fairly sure he hasn't pre-judged, is this: a household robot trained end-to-end by deep RL in a rich simulated house — no symbolic state, no explicit beliefs, no frame axioms, just pixels plus a spoken instruction in and joint torques out. At test time you tell it to move the vase somewhere the cat can't reach, and the cat was never in its training data. It fails in a way that looks exactly like a relevance failure: it ignores the cat completely, shoves a chair, and doesn't reconsider anything afterwards.

What I want from you: say whether he'll call that a case of the frame problem, and set out the two or three candidate rules his track record is equally compatible with, and what each one predicts here. My own suspicion is that the label has quietly been doing three different jobs — working out what has NOT changed, working out which of the things that might change matter right now, and working out what to give up when something does change — and that the deep RL robot has the second without the first, which may be why I can't tell what he'll say. Tell me which rule you think he's really running, commit to a prediction on the new case with your reasons, and then tell me what further case I should put to him that would finally seperate the rules. Don't hand me a menu without picking from it — I can get that from the reading group itself.
