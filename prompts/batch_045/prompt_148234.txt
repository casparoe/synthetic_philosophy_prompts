I'm in a small reading group on personal identity and last week we did Parfit's teletransporter material. I think I get his reductionist line: what matters in survival is psychological continuity and connectedness, it doesn't matter whether the causal route is the normal one, so strict one-one identity isn't the thing to care about. But one of the grad instructors handed out a page defending what she called the single-stream view: what matters is being the unique continuing psychological stream, so fission isn't survival at all — it's the end of you and the beginning of two other people, even if both have all your memories and your character. She added that when the branches are unequal the closest continuer is you and the other one isn't, which sounded to me like it quietly gives away the whole point, and she said it definately doesn't. I can't tell whether her view is genuinely different from Parfit's or whether it's Parfit's view with an extra clause bolted on that turns out to do no work, and I keep going in circles about it.

Here's the case I made up to force the issue — please run both views through it.
