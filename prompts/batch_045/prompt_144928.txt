I'm a backend engineer, ~10 years, mostly Go and some Rust, and I've spent an annoying chunk of the last two years on access control, audit logs, and GDPR-ish data-deletion tickets. No philosophy training beyond reading Wikipedia when I get curious. I keep trying to read about the nature and value of privacy and bouncing off it, because it all reads like people arguing about what a word means while never saying what the system property is supposed to be. So: translate it for me.

Here's the vocabulary I actually have: ACLs and roles, capabilities vs. ambient authority, encapsulation and private fields, information-flow control / taint tracking / noninterference, provenance and lineage, materialized views and derived caches, side channels, garbage collection and (badly) machine unlearning, differential privacy at a hand-wavy level, multi-tenant isolation, blast radius.

Four accounts to translate. I'll describe them the way I understood them, so correct me if I've mangled one:

1. Privacy as restricted access: you have privacy over some fact or some part of yourself to the extent that others don't have access to it. Feels like it should just be an ACL.
2. Privacy as control: it's not about who actually gets there, it's about who gets to decide. Looks like a capability/consent model with delegation.
3. The flow one: it's neither secrecy nor control but whether a given flow of information is appropriate to the context it happens in. The same fact moving from me to my doctor is fine; from my doctor to an advertiser is not, even where I technically agreed to both. I've seen this called something like contextual integrity, I might be using the label wrong, and I don't need citations.
4. The deflationary one: there is no thing called privacy. What we call privacy violations are other wrongs wearing a costume — a breach of confidence, a harm, a broken promise, an unfairness — and once you've handled those there's nothing left over. Basically a reification bug.

For each one, give me the translation into my vocabulary and be honest about where you had to bend something to make it fit. I want the residue report as much as the translation.

Then the stress tests, which is where I suspect most of this breaks:

- Inference. Access control says who can read a row. But the thing I actually care about is usually a column nobody stored: the you're-probably-pregnant-because-you-bought-these-things case, or a model that infers a diagnosis from clickstream. Noninterference and declassification look like the closest tools I have, but they still seem to be about bits moving, not about what someone is now in a position to work out. Can any of the four accounts say what's wrong with a fully licensed inference, and does my vocabulary even have a primitive for that?
- Other people's rows. My exposure isn't a function of my data alone: a relative uploads their genome and now there are facts about me in the system that I never submitted, that live in someone else's join, and that I can't delete. There's no access event involving my row at all. Write that up in systems terms and then say which accounts can even represent it — my guess is the control one basically can't, because there was no decision I was ever in a position to make.
- Consent under defaults. Everyone clicks accept, nobody reads, the toggle is a formality. If control is the account, is a system where 99.9% of users exercise control by not exercising it a privacy-respecting system? Is that a counterexample to the account or just bad UI, and how do you tell the difference?
- Deletion. We get a deletion request and clear the primary store, the read replica, the search index, the analytics warehouse, the nightly backups we can't rewrite, and then there's the model trained on it. Is the right to be forgotten expressible as anything other than garbage collection plus unlearning, and what does it mean for accounts 1-4 that a fact can be effectively unforgotten by a system that never stored it?

And the part I actually want your opinion on: after the translation, what's left over?

(a) Is there anything the philosophers say that has no counterpart in my vocabulary, and is that because my vocabulary is missing a primitive or because the claim was never well defined?
(b) Is there anything my vocabulary lets me say that the accounts can't — some distinction I use daily that maps to nothing in the debate?
(c) My suspicion going in is that privacy isn't a property of a datum or of a subject at all, but of a whole deployment: an emergent, systemic property, like latency or blast radius, that you can't locate in any single access decision, and that a lot of the argument is a category error of looking for it in the wrong place. Tell me how much of that is right, how much is just me liking a metaphor, and which of the four accounts survives it best.

Please don't give me a reading list or name-drop papers, I can't look anything up anyway. Just work the mapping and say where it breaks.
