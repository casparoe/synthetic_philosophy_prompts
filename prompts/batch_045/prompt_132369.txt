So I'm thinking about spending next year trying to build (or at least defend) a view where the wavefunction isn't a thing in the world but something more like a state of knowledge, and before I sink a year into it I want to know what I should be demanding of such a view. Not a reading list — I can find the papers myself. I want the spec. Purpose: it has to be good enough to be a real alternative to the wavefunction-is-real views, not a relabeling of one of them with some extra bookkeeping bolted on.

Where I'm at: the motivation is the usual one. The state of N particles lives in this enormous 3N-dimensional space, gets handed to you by a preparation procedure, jumps when you find something out, and isn't the kind of thing anyone has ever measured directly — so maybe it's like a probability distribution. But "it's knowledge" is cheap until you say knowledge of what and why that knowledge has the shape it has. So: write me the desiderata.

Things I think belong on the list, but you decide and add the ones I'm missing: what the underlying states of affairs have to be like; why the set of things-you-can-know has exactly the geometry of a Hilbert space (why maximal information is a pure state, why you can rotate any state into any other, why there are exactly this many perfectly distinguishable ones); how interference and entanglement come out right if the state is only bookkeeping — in particular how you get the exponential size of the state space without paying for it in ontology; why nobody can use the underlying state to out-bet the Born rule; what a measurement is and where definite outcomes come from; what happens to Bell-type correlations and to locality; whether it's supposed to be empirically identical to textbook quantum mechanics or allowed to differ somewhere; and whether it has to be a view about measurement outcomes only or also about the world between them.

For each one, the case or consideration behind it, what breaks if you drop it, and whether it's a genuine requirement or just a prejudice of one camp — is locality here a desideratum or a fetish, is "the quantum state has to be derived from the underlying facts rather than posited alongside them" fair or circular, is being able to say what would be different if the view were true non-negotiable.

Two I especially want. First: I keep being told about an argument that runs roughly — prepare two systems independently, assume the underlying state of the pair is just the two underlying states sitting side by side, and you can apparently get to the conclusion that two distinct pure states can never rest on the same underlying state, which would kill the project. Is "distinct pure states must be able to overlap" an actual desideratum, or can you be a wavefunction-is-knowledge person without it? Second, one I made up: an agent who was somehow handed the exact underlying state still couldn't make money against a bookie who only knows the quantum state. I think that belongs on the list, but I can't tell if it's a real constraint, just empirical adequacy in a costume, or in tension with wanting the underlying story to explain anything at all.

Then the parts I care most about: which subset of the requirements can't all be had at once, and which single requirement knocks out the most candidates. A line each at the end on the usual suspects — the pilot-wave one, the collapse ones, many worlds, the "it's just an agent's betting odds" version — is enough; the criteria are where I want the work. Also: if I'm honest I don't know whether my 3N-dimensional motivation is even a good reason to want the view, so tell me if that one definately shouldn't survive onto the list.
