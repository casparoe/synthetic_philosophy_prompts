I am presenting next week at a joint seminar of the machine-learning safety group I work in and the university's value-theory reading group. The group's lead is using the session partly to decide whether my project continues into the next funding cycle, so the discussion matters beyond the seminar itself. My slot is fifty minutes with twenty for questions. I would like a Q&A preparation sheet for that twenty minutes.

The thesis I am defending: an agent can be constrained to avoid side effects without anything remotely like a complete specification of the principal's values, because the right object to constrain is not value at all but the principal's remaining freedom of action, measured structurally. Here is the version I will present.

The agent is given a task metric and a threshold; a plan succeeds if it clears the threshold. Define a reference agent I call the custodian: the least-effort agent that still clears the threshold, where effort is an intervention-cost functional over trajectories (approximate it by control energy, or by the number of primitive interventions required). Let the custodian's trajectory end in world w*. For any candidate plan ending in world w, define the restoration cost of w as follows: for each state s that the principal could still reach from w* within a fixed resource budget B, compute how much more control cost it would take to reach s from w than from w*, and aggregate over those states by a soft maximum. Low impact is then: minimize restoration cost subject to clearing the task threshold. Two design choices I will emphasize: the impact term enters only as a tiebreaker among plans that already clear the threshold, never as something traded off against task success; and both the effort functional and the transition-cost function are supposed to come from the environment's own control structure rather than from anything value-shaped, with B fixed from the principal's resource endowment at deployment time.

I will use two running examples: an agent told to cut a building's energy use, which can either retune the HVAC schedule or permanently seal the windows, both clearing the threshold; and an agent managing a shared irrigation system for several farms, where the question of whose options are being restored cannot be ducked.

The room will be: Ravi, a senior RL researcher from the reward-specification and safe-exploration tradition, who thinks a penalty pulling behavior toward a default policy gets most of the practical benefit at a fraction of the cost, and who will want to know whether the restoration cost is computable in a continuous state space with a learned model; Marta, an econometrician from the policy school who does counterfactual program evaluation, and who will hear restoration cost as an opportunity-cost notion and will ask where the budget and the baseline come from and whether the resulting rule is incentive compatible or dynamically consistent; Jonas, a value theorist who works on incommensurability and distrusts any single currency of comparison, and who will press the claim that a structural measure is value-neutral; and Priya, an empirical safety engineer who has watched penalty-shaped objectives make agents inert or make them game the penalty, and who will ask what this does to a real system on a real budget of computation. A program officer from the funder will be present but will not speak.

What I want from you: eight or so objections, ordered by how much damage they would do in that room rather than by their importance in the literature, each attributed to the person most likely to raise it and phrased the way that person would phrase it rather than in my vocabulary. For each, give a reply I could deliver in about two minutes, and a blunt note on where that reply is soft or question-begging. Also tell me: what I should concede in the first two slides rather than be forced into conceding later; the single reply that has to land if the project is to survive the review; the weakest joint in my own argument that I should prepare to defend rather than hope the room misses; which objection is really a disguised request for me to say what would falsify the project; and the one I should simply concede and move past instead of fighting. Finally, give me the question I am hoping nobody asks, and a holding sentence for it in case it comes anyway. Please skip the objections a general conference audience would raise; I have those covered. I do not need citations or further reading, and I would rather you stay inside the machinery sketched above than bring in outside frameworks.
