Me and a mate got into an argument the other week and I cant let it go. He reads a bit of philosophy and he says that noticing a white shoe is evidence that all ravens are black, because "all ravens are black" is really the same claim as "everything that isn't black isn't a raven" — so any non-black thing that isn't a raven is a point in favour. He even says a white shoe confirms just as much as an actual black raven does. He called it the raven paradox. I half get it, but my gut disagrees in a load of places and I want to know whether my gut adds up to anything.

Here's what I actually feel, case by case:

Black raven in the park: confirms, but barely — I've seen heaps already.

White shoe in my hall: confirms a tiny bit, but way less than a black raven.

Green apple in the fridge: feels like it does even less than the shoe, maybe nothing at all. Both are non-black non-ravens, so I can't say why they feel different, and that bugs me.

Then he went out specifically looking for something that was either a raven or not black, and came back with a white sneaker. That felt like cheating — like it confirms nothing. Same sneaker, but if he'd said "I grabbed the first thing I saw in the garage" I'd count it a bit.

A black raven in a museum display where the curator only puts out black birds: I want to say that confirms nothing, even tho its a black raven.

A bird I couldn't tell was a raven or a crow, but it was definitely black: confirms a bit, less than a clean black raven.

And the one that melts my head: if every non-black non-raven I look at confirms a bit, then after the tens of thousands of them I've seen in my life I should be dead certain about ravens. I'm not. But I also don't want to give up that each one counts a little.

My stab at a rule: an observation only confirms if, before you looked, the result could have come out the other way and killed the hypothesis — which is why a rigged sample confirms nothing and why an expected black raven confirms less. But that rule seems to say that my mate texting "I'm looking at something and its definitely not black" and refusing to say what it is should confirm, and that feels wrong to me. I go back and forth on it constantly.

So: is there one principle that gets my verdicts, including why the shoe and the apple feel different, and whether the local version is different — for "all ravens in my county are black" a white shoe in the county feels like it does real work in a way it doesn't for the universal claim? And what does the principle say about the not-black-text case? Be honest about which of my verdicts I'd have to drop.
