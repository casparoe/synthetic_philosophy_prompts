I am a retired experimental physicist who has taken up philosophy late, and I convene a small study group of other retired scientists. We have argued ourselves to a standstill over Zeno's paradoxes of motion, so I want the document we should have produced instead: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps.

Camp A, the dissolutionists: the Dichotomy and the Achilles are disposed of by the mathematics of the continuum — convergent series, the treatment of a finite interval as a union of infinitely many sub-intervals, motion as occupancy of places at times, and instantaneous velocity as a derivative — so what remains is a psychological illusion about finishing infinitely many sub-tasks.

Camp B, the residualists: the mathematics describes the trajectory but never addresses the modal question of whether an infinite sequence of acts or states can be completed by an agent or a physical process; the supertask worry is the live residue, and the Arrow (a moving body, at each instant, occupying exactly the region it occupies, with nothing in the instant to distinguish motion from rest) raises a separate question about what in the world grounds the instantaneous velocity the dissolutionist helps herself to.

Please produce, in polished prose for a technically literate but non-philosophical readership, and without citations or scholarly attribution — where a view carries a name in the literature, describe it rather than naming it:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims: what both sides accept about the formalism, about what would have to be explained, and about what an adequate treatment of motion would have to deliver.
2. A disagreements table with columns for: the issue; the formulation each side would accept for its own view; the crux that would move at least one side; a concrete case or argument both accept as bearing on that crux; and each side's prediction about it, recorded in advance.
3. A short closing section saying which parts of the dispute are verbal and why, what each side would count as evidence against its own view, and whether there is anything both would agree would actually move the debate.

Two requests of my own. First, I want the residual disagreement stated so that it survives granting the convergence of every relevant series: separate, if they can be separated, the complaint that there are infinitely many sub-tasks from the complaint that there is no last one, and say whether the sting is cardinality, order type, or something else again. Second, at least one crux should turn on a case from my old field: a ball bouncing on a plate with coefficient of restitution below one completes infinitely many impacts in a finite elapsed time, and the equations of motion do not by themselves determine what the ball does after that accumulation time (some authors call such trajectories Zeno). Ask whether this is a genuine instance of Zeno's structure or only a formal analogue, whether the indeterminacy past the accumulation time is the residue of the ancient puzzle or merely a sign that an idealisation has broken down, and record what each camp predicts.

Aim for about 900 to 1,200 words.
