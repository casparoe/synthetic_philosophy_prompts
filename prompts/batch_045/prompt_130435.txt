ok so I just had a genuinely long, slightly drunken argument with a friend about whether generations are real. Like, are Boomers / Millennials / Gen Z actual kinds of people — things that do causal work in the social world and whose boundaries are (roughly) discovered — or are they basically a journalistic artifact: arbitrary cut points dropped onto a smooth birth-year gradient, plus a bunch of age effects and period effects that get misread as something about the cohort, kept alive mostly because the labels are everywhere in the media and people started answering to them. We got nowhere, mostly because we never agreed what would even count.

So: draft the joint statement the two of us could both have signed. Call the two sides something better than we did — maybe the Cohort Realist and the Cut-Point Skeptic. I want:

a numbered section of claims both of them can sign. Include at least: that age, period and cohort effects aren't separately identified from ordinary repeated cross-sectional survey data without some extra constraint (the classic identification problem — say it in your own words); that nobody serious thinks the cut years are metaphysically sharp; that the labels unquestionably do social work even if they're invented; and that the Boomer case is special because there's an actual fertility spike behind it, whereas nothing comparable sits under the later ones.

then a disagreements table. Columns: the crux / the realist's own phrasing of their position / the skeptic's own phrasing of theirs / the one test, case or dataset both would agree actually bears on it / what each side predicts in advance, written down before anyone looks.

The cruxes I think matter, but cut or add:

1. Whether cohort effects come in blocks at all, or are smooth in birth year — i.e. whether you lose real information by lumping 1981–1996 together. Test I came up with: take a long-running national survey with birth year recorded, score the fit of the conventional generation windows, then re-fit after permuting the cut years randomly (or sliding them a few years each way) and see where the real windows land in that distribution. Realist should say in advance which outcome domains it'll beat chance on.
2. Cross-domain coherence: does one partition show up across fertility timing, tech adoption, religious attendance, language use, voting — or does each outcome have its own critical-period exposure window and its own cut years?
3. Persistence: whether a cohort stays distinctive once you compare it to earlier cohorts at the same ages, or whether the profile is mostly an age/period snapshot that dissolves.
4. Exposure timing: Gen Z is supposedly defined by smartphones/social media in adolescence, but that diffusion was staggered by country and by household, so membership depends where you grew up. Is that still a generation or just a graded exposure cohort with a name stuck on?
5. The self-ascription thing: does identifying as a Millennial do any work once you control for age and media exposure, or is it a looping effect where the discourse creates the kind?

Also: flag which bits of our fight were purely verbal — I suspect we were using 'real' to mean two different things, something like does-causal-work-in-a-model vs has-sharp-boundaries-in-nature — and say so explicitly rather than papering over it. Keep it signable by both: the realist should concede something the skeptic actually wants, and vice versa. And please no citations, no reading lists, no 'studies show' — we're doing this from the armchair, that's the whole point.
