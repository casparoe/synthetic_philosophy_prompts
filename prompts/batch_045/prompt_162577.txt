I am in the lower sixth and have been given a single forty-five minute slot to run a session for our school philosophy society: about fourteen of us, aged sixteen to eighteen, in a normal classroom after lessons. We have read a little Foucault on discipline and Bentham's panopticon design, and I want the group to feel how that machinery works rather than hear about it.

Please design one continuous activity for the room — rules, materials, and small stakes in points or tokens, nothing touching real school records or behaviour files — arranged so that within the forty minutes the group does all of the following: invents its own standard for how a member should sit, speak or contribute; is then measured against that standard by an observer whose watching cannot be verified; and produces short written self-reports that get converted into a classification of kinds of people in the room, which is then read back to them. The watching should be intermittent and unverifiable rather than constant, and at a set point it should stop altogether and genuinely stop, while the behaviour carries on. I would also like a brief second phase in which the watching is made symmetrical, so that the watchers can be watched, to see whether the order holds.

What I want visible at the end is that no one can say who was actually in charge, that the categories feel as though they named real sorts of person, and that the group's own vocabulary ended up producing the thing that judged it. No opening explanation of power/knowledge or the panopticon; the terms come in the last ten minutes.

Give me the minute-by-minute timings, the reveal, and a debrief mapping particular moments onto disciplinary power, the arrangement of visibility, and the individual as something produced rather than given. Say honestly what will go wrong on a first run — I expect someone to refuse to play, and I am worried the reveal will land as a trick and leave everyone cynical — and state plainly which part of the idea the game cannot generate by itself and I will have to say out loud. Finally, indicate how confident you are in the design and single out the element you are least sure of.
