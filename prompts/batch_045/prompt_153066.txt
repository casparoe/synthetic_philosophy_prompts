So I let myself get talked onto the jury of a small literary translation prize, which I said yes to before noticing that half the submissions are from languages I don't read at all. We're down to two books and the one I'm drawn to is translated from Korean, of which I have zero. Another juror — a poet who does read it — wrote this long reader's report saying the original is breathtaking, with very specific stuff about the sound of the sentences, the register shifts, the way the narrator's voice frays in the last chapter, and she argues no translation could carry any of it. Two other readers with the language more or less independently agree.

Our chair, who is a translator himself, keeps killing this line of argument with "you have to see it for yourself." He's using it to mean: her report can't do any work for me, so I either learn Korean or I recuse myself from that part of the decision. That feels too quick to me but I can't say why.

Can you lay out the different precise claims that one-liner could be making? Like: is it a claim about knowledge, or about justification, or about what counts as having judged something yourself as opposed to deferring? Is it about testimony generally or about aesthetic properties specifically? Does it matter that I literally cannot access the object, as opposed to just not having got round to it? Does it matter that what she gave me isn't a bare verdict ("it's beautiful") but reasons I can half-check against the English? Does it matter that she's someone I've calibrated against myself on things I can access — we agree on maybe 80% of films and poems in English, so I've got decent evidence she's reliable for me in particular? Does three independent readers converging change anything? And is there a difference between me coming to believe the original is beautiful and me being in a position to answer for that verdict as a juror?

For each reading, say whether it's trivially true, whether it's substantive and defensible, or whether it's just false — and, the bit I actually need, which of them would really get the chair his conclusion that her report should count for nothing in how I vote. Give it to me as a list I can bring to the next meeting, and tell me which single reading I should push him to commit to out loud.
