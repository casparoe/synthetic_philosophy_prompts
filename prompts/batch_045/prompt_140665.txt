The live dispute is this. Dev's camp — call them the structuralists — thinks there's a mind-independent fact of the matter about which computation a physical system implements, fixed by its causal/counterfactual structure at some privileged grain, and that once we have the right theory of what functional-cum-phenomenal profile matters, every system gets a determinate answer, including the weird ones. My camp — call us the attributionists — thinks implementation claims are description-relative, that there's no natural kind waiting to be found at the boundary of sentience, and that in the borderline cases whether there's a subject there at all partly depends on whether a subject can be intelligibly attributed to the thing: on the grip our interpretive practices get, the repertoire we could take up a stance towards, and what we're doing when we count something as a someone. We're not saying anything goes; attribution is constrained. But we think some of these cases are indeterminate in a way that isn't merely epistemic, and Dev thinks that's cowardice dressed up as metaphysics.
