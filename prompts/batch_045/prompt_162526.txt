i'm halfway through an essay on Skolem's paradox for a small phil-maths reading group, and going back through my notes I've realised my position is just a pile of things I picked up separately from different people and never actually checked against each other. Roughly where I've landed:

1. The paradox itself is a non-problem — it's a pun on "uncountable". The model just doesn't contain the witnessing bijection, the sentence it satisfies only quantifiers over its own domain, and once you see that there is nothing left to explain. I'm deflationary to the point of thinking a lot of the "paradox" literature is people being spooked by scope.
2. But I also want to say the theorem has real bite: no first-order theory, however strong, pins down its intended interpretation, and "uncountable" in particular isn't first-order axiomatizable. And I'm attracted to the Putnam-flavoured generalization — that this isn't really special to set theory, it's about what any formalization of anything can fix.
3. I'm a realist about the universe. The quantifiers range over a determinate V, P(ω) is a definite object, CH has a truth value we may simply not know, and I don't buy the multiverse: "there are many models of ZFC" is a remark about formal systems, not about what there is.
4. I don't think the second-order escape works. Quine's complaint that full second-order semantics is just set theory in disguise seems right to me, and even if you grant it, someone in my cohort said second-order ZFC is only quasi-categorical anyway — its models all look like V_κ for κ inaccessible — so the relativity doesn't go away, it just relocates to how tall the universe is.
5. I also want to insist the countable model isn't mistaken about anything. It's not "wrong" that ω₁^M is uncountable; it just has a different domain. Saying the model is in error feels to me like exactly the move the deflationary readers warn against.
6. And my actual positive claim is that the interesting residue isn't the missing-bijection point at all, but the failure of absoluteness between transitive models — that "x is uncountable" flips between a countable transitive model and V even though membership is being read correctly — which looks like a substantive lesson about the set-theoretic concepts themselves, not a quirk of first-order syntax.

Can someone audit this for me before I try to defend it out loud? Which pairs are genuinely pulling against each other and which only look like they are? I honestly can't tell whether I have one problem here or three. Please rank the real tensions by severity instead of listing them all as equally bad, and for each one say what would have to give and what giving it up would cost me — and then whether there's a single revision that cleans up the most. Also, if 4 or 6 is just wrong or half-remembered, say that too; I'd rather find out now than in front of the group.
