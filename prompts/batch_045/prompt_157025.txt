Next week I'm defending a paper at a small interdisciplinary workshop on mechanistic interpretability. My thesis is that the features interpretability work uncovers in trained networks are genuinely representational states, but that the usual philosophical way of vindicating this — a consumer-based or teleological condition borrowed from neuroscience, on which a state represents X only because some downstream mechanism has the job of treating it as X — is both too strong for trained networks and unnecessary. I defend a two-part alternative: an intervention-mediation condition, plus what I call the exploitable-misrepresentation condition, on which a state represents X only if there is some reachable input distribution on which that state fires though X is absent and the network's downstream behaviour then degrades in the way X's absence predicts. Since the second condition can be satisfied at finer and coarser grains, I also argue that representations are granularity-relative: there is no fact of the matter which dictionary in a nested family of feature decompositions is the correct one, and realism should be claimed at each grain rather than at a privileged grain.

The room will be five mechanistic interpretability researchers who work on sparse autoencoders, superposition and steering, and who regard talk of representation as philosophy tax; two systems neuroscientists who study place cells and population codes and who will want a decoder and a lesion; and one senior philosopher of mind who holds that attributing representations is adopting a useful stance rather than discovering something.

Draft my Q&A prep sheet for the forty minutes after the talk: the objections I will actually get from this room, ordered by how much damage they would do, each phrased in the idiom of whichever subgroup raises it, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is softest. Include what I should concede in the first minute, before anyone asks anything; the single reply that has to land if the view is to survive at all; and the question I am hoping nobody asks. My own guess is that the dangerous one is a dilemma — either my two conditions are loose enough that the whole network ends up representing the training distribution, or they secretly depend on an interpreter's interests, in which case I have reinvented instrumentalism — so write that one out in the most detail, with the best answer I have and a frank note on where it fails.
