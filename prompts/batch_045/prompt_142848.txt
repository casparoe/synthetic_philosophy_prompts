we've got this little reading group at work — four of us from the platform team plus one of the designers — and we've been picking our way through Hume's Enquiry over lunch for a few months. Last session was section X, "Of Miracles," and it went better than I expected, mostly because one of our staff engineers actually knows this literature and kept us from sliding into slogans. I'm running the final session and instead of doing a recap I'd like to close with a short poem, maybe 40 lines. (I write Go, I have never written a poem, so don't assume I know the vocabulary.)

The form I want is a sestina. I looked up how it works: six stanzas of six lines, no rhyme needed, where the same six end-words keep coming back in a rotating order — I think the rotation is 6-1-5-2-4-3, i.e. each stanza's end-words are the previous stanza's last, first, fifth, second, fourth, third — and then a three-line envoi that gets all six back in somehow. If I've got the permutation wrong, use the real one. The reason I want a sestina rather than free verse: the thing that got under my skin about Hume's argument is that it isn't really a refutation of the witnesses at all, it's a kind of weight — the same small stock of considerations returns every stanza and there's no slot in the vocabulary for a seventh. I'd like the form to be doing that work rather than the poem just using the phrase "uniform experience" four times.

Content-wise, a few things I'd like in there, though you obviously don't have to hit all of them. One: the argument has two halves that are separate moves — the "a miracle is a violation of a law, and uniform experience is the evidence for the law" half (part I), and the "look at the kind of people who report these things, and how reports spread" half (part II) — and I'd like someone who knows the essay to be able to tell which stanzas are doing which. Two: nobody in our group wanted to say the witnesses are liars, and the poem shouldn't either; the point is that the evidence on the other side just massively outweighs them, and a report can never be more credible than the pipeline that produced it — ten thousand witnesses who all got the story from the same group chat aren't ten thousand independent pieces of evidence, they're one report with a fan-out. Three, and this is the bit I personally got stuck on and would love to see survive into the poem: testimony itself only works because nature is regular, so using witness reports to establish a violation of that regularity feels to me like using the compiler to prove the compiler is broken. Also: make the miracle in the poem something small and physical — a dropped mug reassembling itself on the kitchen floor, that kind of thing — and don't let it turn into an argument about religion one way or the other, that's not what our group is doing and it would make everyone uncomfortable.

Just give me the poem: start with the first line of it, no "here's a sestina" preamble beforehand, and after it at most three or four lines saying which end-word is carrying what and what the envoi is supposed to be doing. If my sestina idea is actually a bad fit for this argument, say so in those closing lines and tell me what form would work better — but write the sestina anyway.
