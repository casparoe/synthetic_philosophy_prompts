Im clerking for the truth commission in the country I'm in now, and I have to draft the recommendation memo on our next amnesty application, so I'd love your read on how our own precedents hang together.

The statute says amnesty is available for politically motivated offences committed during the conflict, in exchange for full disclosure, and we've said almost nothing beyond that about what "full" means or who actually deserves it. Our decisions so far: granted to a young conscript who confessed to shooting one man during a village sweep — account checked out, zero remorse, said he'd do it again; denied to a senior intelligence officer who confessed to a great deal but wouldn't name the minister who signed off on it; denied to a man who confessed fully to a killing in the same years that turned out to be a land feud with no political connection; granted to a mid-level guerrilla commander who confessed to a bombing that killed civilians, named his own superiors (both long dead), and apologised to the families in the public hearing; granted to a police interrogator who confessed to torturing detainees as part of the counterinsurgency, described his methods, named his victims, and expressed remorse.

New applicant: a police station commander who ordered the torture of about a dozen detainees but never laid hands on anyone himself. His account of what he ordered and what he knew is full and verifiable, and he names the provincial chief who set the policy — now a sitting legislator — and his testimony plus the documents he handed over are the first real evidence tying that legislator to any of it. He shows no remorse whatsoever: says the torture stopped bombings, that he'd order it again, and that the people he ordered it on were combatants. The victims' families are demanding prosecution.

What do you think the panel does with him? More useful for my memo: what are the two or three rules that fit all five precedents about equally well, what does each predict here, which precedent is the closest analog, and what is the commission committing itself to whichever way it goes? And what single applicant would actually force those rules apart, if we ever get one?
