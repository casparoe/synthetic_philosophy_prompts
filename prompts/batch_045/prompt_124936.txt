I am writing the specification for the belief-revision component of a deployed tracking system, and I want the specification settled before I look at candidate rules. The component keeps a probability distribution over the cells of a finite grid in which an unobserved asset might be located, and revises that distribution as reports arrive. The reports are almost never statements of fact: they arrive as constraints. The odds are three to one that the asset is north of the river. If it is in the northern sector, then there is a seventy percent chance it is east of the treeline. Something of that shape. So what I need is a rule for updating on conditional and otherwise uncertain evidence: a function that takes a prior and a report and returns a posterior. What I want from you is the statement of what such a rule has to do to count as adequate, not a survey of the rules on offer.

Purpose first, since adequacy is relative to it. This rule will run inside software, unattended, on a stream of reports of varying provenance, over a state space that an engineer may refine next year. So the properties I care about are determinacy, meaning one answer with no ties broken by hand; robustness to the bookkeeping of the stream, meaning duplicated reports, reordered reports, and reports that arrive in a batch rather than one at a time; indifference to arbitrary modelling choices, meaning how finely the grid is drawn and which of two equivalent formulations a report is written in; and freedom from the pathologies that a downstream decision rule could exploit, whether or not anyone is deliberately exploiting them. I do not care whether the rule reproduces anyone's seminar intuition except as defeasible evidence.

The case I will keep returning to is a Judy Benjamin style one, in a four-cell grid formed by crossing north/south with west/east. The prior is 0.30 in the north-west cell, 0.20 in the north-east cell, 0.20 in the south-west cell, and 0.30 in the south-east cell. A report arrives whose content is exhaustively captured by saying that, afterwards, the probability of north should be 0.75. Three demands that sound equally principled give three different answers here. Preserving the conditional distribution within each row, three to two in the northern row and two to three in the southern, pins down a unique posterior. Preserving the west/east margin at one half leaves a one-parameter family, and entropy maximization is one way of selecting from it. Minimizing a divergence from the prior selects yet another posterior, and which one depends on the divergence and on the direction it is taken in. I want to know which of these demands is genuine and which is the prejudice of a camp, and I want the argument to be about the case rather than about the label.

Here are the further cases and the demand each is meant to test, several of them situation-specific to my system. First, a report that pins a single cell, where I take it to be non-negotiable that the rule reduce to ordinary conditioning. Second, the case above, where a report constrains one partition and is silent about an orthogonal one, and where the two partitions are correlated in the prior rather than independent: here the notion of silence becomes hard to cash out, and I want to know whether silence should be understood as preservation of a conditional distribution, preservation of a margin, or something else. Third, the same report arriving over a channel that is known to be accurate eighty percent of the time, which raises the question of whether the update should be a mixture and whether mixing commutes with the update. Fourth, the same report arriving twice, which tests idempotence. Fifth, two reports arriving in one order and then the other, which tests order-independence, and which also tests whether a batched version of the rule exists at all. Sixth, learning with certainty, after the conditional report, that its antecedent is false, and then learning that the report was fabricated altogether: these test whether the rule is dynamically coherent, whether earlier constraint-based updates can be undone by later factual ones, and whether the rule is representable as conditioning on some event in a refinement of the state space. Seventh, splitting one cell of the grid into two subcells without changing anything else and running the rule again, which tests whether the answer depends on how the grid is drawn. Eighth, a report whose antecedent has prior probability zero, of the form that if the bridge had been out the asset would be on the east bank, where conditioning on the report as a proposition is unavailable or trivial, and where something like imaging or a nearest-possible-world shift may be the only option. Ninth, a channel that only reaches assets in the northern half, so that receiving a report, or failing to receive one, is itself evidence, and so that the report is addressed to one asset among many: this tests whether the rule is answerable for selection effects or whether that belongs to a separate component that models the channel.

There is also a distinction I want the specification to make explicitly. A report can be read as a constraint on the posterior credence function, telling me what my probability of north given south, or whatever it is, ought to become; or it can be read as telling me that a conditional proposition is true, which I might then conditionalize on. I want the specification to say which reading the rule is modelling, and to say whether the familiar triviality considerations, of the sort that make it hard for any proposition to have as its probability the corresponding conditional probability, rule the second reading out or merely constrain it.

What I want back is the requirements list, each requirement paired with the case that motivates it and with a statement of why that case has the force it does. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable given the purpose above and which are preferences of a camp: minimality of change under one metric rather than another, entropy maximization, preservation of the unmentioned margin, order-independence, idempotence, invariance under refinement of the state space, representability as conditioning in some enrichment, convexity and uniqueness of the optimization, continuity in the prior and in the report, immunity to diachronic exploitation, approximability by a sequence of Jeffrey revisions, and any I have not thought of. Tell me whether some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, since that is the part I most want: if the list cannot all be had together, the list itself is the result, and I would rather know now than after I have built to it. Tell me which single requirement excludes the largest part of the space of candidate rules. Finally, and briefly, since I will do the scoring myself against my own shortlist, indicate how the standard families fare: conditioning on the most probable report, Jeffrey revision with rigidity, relative-entropy projection in each of its two directions, maximum entropy subject to the constraint, an imaging-style revision, and the treatment of the report as a proposition about a conditional.
