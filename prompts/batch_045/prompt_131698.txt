I host a small podcast about ideas — no academic training on my own part, and an audience of listeners who have heard of relativity but have never opened a philosophy journal. Next episode I am interviewing a philosopher of physics who defends a view I would summarize roughly as follows: whether time genuinely passes is not settled by how things look from the inside but by the large-scale structure of spacetime; passage is real in a universe only if that universe admits a single global slicing into successive nows, a cosmic time; and Gödel's rotating-universe solution to Einstein's field equations is the case that makes this vivid, because it is a physically respectable model in which the light cones tip over far enough out that some timelike paths close back on themselves, so that no such global slicing exists — and yet every inhabitant of that universe has perfectly ordinary experience of one thing following another. She also bites a bullet I find genuinely interesting: on her view the reality of temporal passage is partly an empirical, a posteriori question, settled by what cosmology tells us about the universe's global shape rather than by anything anyone can feel from within it.

I need eight to ten interview questions, in the order I should ask them, each self-contained enough to say aloud, with no equations and no machinery my listeners won't have. Assume I have already given them the summary above; nothing should ask her to restate her position. I want the set to have a single arc — building toward something — rather than touring the topic, and for each question I want one follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give. Please include at least one question on each of the following:

The gap between no shared now and no passage. Special relativity already denies observers a common present, so press her on what the rotating model adds — arguably not just a failure of simultaneity but a failure of any global serial order — and on why that extra feature, rather than the older relativistic point, is what carries her argument. Follow up on the likely answer: if the thesis amounts to saying that passage just is the existence of a global time function, ask whether her argument is substantive or stipulative, and whether a critic who grants every claim about the geometry and denies the identification has been answered.

A patchwork case I have been turning over and have not seen addressed. Take a universe that is Gödelian in one region and expanding with a cosmic time in another, with observers who can cross between them. Does passage hold in one region and not the other, or is the verdict global? If she says global, so that the whole universe lacks passage, follow up by asking what it means for the inhabitants of the expanding region to be wrong about their own successive experience, and whether there was anything they could have done to find out.

Epistemic fragility. Our entitlement to a cosmic time rests on an inferred large-scale model, so ask how sensitive the reality of passage is to that inference, and then what happens if the Gödelian region lies outside our observational horizon: is the reality of time then empirically undecidable? Follow up by asking whether that is a reductio of her view or a consequence she accepts — and if she accepts it, how her view still differs from the flat a priori claim that time is unreal, which she says she does not hold.

What else goes global. If passage is a global structural feature, is the reality of local causal facts hostage to global structure too — is this caused that as fragile as time passes? Follow up on her most likely distinction between causal order and passage.

Something that would make her stop. One question asking what observation or argument would get her to give the view up, and a follow-up that blocks the easy answer: if she says a different cosmology would do it, ask how that answer is available to someone who has insisted that the reality of time is not settled from the inside.

One thing about my own grasp of this: I have been picturing the Gödel point as a village where every neighbour agrees about what time it is but the agreement cannot be extended around the whole village without contradiction. Tell me plainly whether that picture helps or misleads, and if it misleads, replace it and say what it gets wrong.

Finally, commit to something. End with a short paragraph saying which of her commitments you expect to crack first under this sequence of questions and why — a definite bet with reasons, not a menu of possible weak points. And if you think my patchwork case or my village picture rests on a confusion, say so rather than working it in politely.
