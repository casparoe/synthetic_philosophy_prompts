Writing a seminar paper on Darwin on morality and religion and I've built an argument that eats itself, and I can't see where to cut.

(1) Darwin's own worry from the notebooks, roughly: the human mind was developed from the mind of lower animals, so why trust its convictions — would anyone trust the convictions of a monkey's mind?
(2) Generalize it into a test I made up, call it the swap test: take a faculty shaped by selection, hold the faculty fixed, swap out the content it delivers; if the survival-and-reproduction story explains why the creature has it just as well after the swap, then the content isn't tracking anything and the conviction is debunked.
(3) Morality fails the test. Swap "care for kin and reciprocators" for "care for the whole band," or take Darwin's own aside that young women reared under hive-bee conditions would count it a sacred duty to kill their brothers — the fitness story survives the swap untouched. (I think his account of conscience — social instinct plus memory plus comparing past and present motives — already gets you here without the test, but not sure.)
(4) Religion fails the same way: the content of the unseen agents swings wildly across cultures while the cohesion story survives every swap.
(5) But (2) draws no line at morality and religion. Darwin's own theorizing is delivered by faculties selected for surviving, not for being true.
(6) And descriptive belief fails the test too — I can't see why a creature couldn't do perfectly well with a bundle of false but action-guiding representations, so long as they steer it right.
(7) So (2) debunks science and inference, and steps (1) through (6) along with them. Not acceptable.

I'd much rather keep (3) and (4) than keep (5) and (6) — the paper is supposed to be about how Darwinian genealogy deflates morality and religion specifically. So which step do I drop, why did it look fine when I wrote it down, and what does dropping it do to (3) and (4)? Is there a version of the swap test with teeth in the moral and religious cases and none in the scientific one, or is any such version special pleading? My supervisor mutters something about the only honest options being a pre-established harmony between selection and the moral facts or nothing, and I don't know what she means by that.
