Im writing a novel, and theres a scene at a department party where one of my characters — a metaphysician, a bit drunk, showing off — tries to tell a joke and more or less bombs. I need the joke itself, and it has to be one that a room of philosophers would actually laugh at while still reading like something a person would say out loud.

Her view: she's an immanent realist of a stingy sort. Universals are real, but only where they're instantiated — no forms floating around in some separate realm — and she thinks the genuine inventory is short: redness, mass, negative charge. Most of our predicates (being a bicycle, being grue, being the person holding the bad wine) don't pick out anything. The guy she's needling is a nominalist who thinks property talk is just talk about which predicates we find handy.

Write me a joke whose punchline turns on that disagreement specifically: on why there has to be one thing that many things share, or on what a sparse versus an abundant list of properties costs you, or on the regress worry the realist gets landed with. Not a three-philosophers-walk-into-a-bar thing, and not a joke where the word "universal" is just there to signal nerdiness. Thirty seconds spoken, tops.

Then in two or three lines: where does the joke lean on a contested reading of these positions, or fudge them to get the laugh? And if the honest answer is that this material can't carry a joke that's also funny — that the metaphysics and the punchline pull against each other — say that plainly rather than handing me something smooth and empty.
