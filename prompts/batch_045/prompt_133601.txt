I spent last night arguing with a friend about a rescue case and came away holding a position I can't shake. Set it up as starkly as possible: you can save either one stranger or six; no special relationships, no one responsible for the threat, every stake identical, the six strangers to one another. My friend defended the familiar Taurek line that the numbers do not count. I went further and now hold this: what each person is owed in such a case is an equal chance, so the only numbers-sensitive thing you may do is run a fair lottery across all seven lives. That means saving the six outright is not merely optional but wrong, since it leaves the one with no chance at all; it holds however lopsided the numbers become; and it holds even if all six unanimously waive their chances and beg you to save the one.

Tell me what would count against this. I'd like the defeaters as a table, sorted most damaging first, with exactly these columns: Defeater (a short name); What would have to be true (the case, observation or argument); Why it bites (the mechanism, in a sentence); Severity (one of fatal, serious, awkward, no real bite); Conceded? (whether someone holding the equal-chance view should concede it, and in one clause why). Please cover at least: the missing-randomiser objection, that what I may do seems to depend on whether a coin happens to be to hand, which looks morally irrelevant; the collapse worry, that against a billion the equal chance owed to the one shrinks almost to nothing, so the view converges on the aggregative answer it was meant to block; the currency worry, that if chances matter because they matter to each individual, then the size of each individual's stake must matter too, which lets summing back in; the waiver case above; and, if chances are not the kind of thing anyone can be owed at all, what replaces them. Add anything you think bites harder than these. Close with one line on whether the view has been quietly qualified into something that risks nothing, and whether that is a defect in a view of this kind.
