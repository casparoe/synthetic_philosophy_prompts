After a long argument with a friend, I am trying to defend a version of al-Ghazālī's occasionalism against the objection that it cannot explain the reliability of ordinary causal sequences. My friend grants that God could directly cause burning whenever fire touches cotton, but insists that on the occasionalist picture the regularity is either brute or merely projected by us, so the view collapses into Humean constant conjunction plus an idle divine appendix. Please argue for the position that the occasionalist can instead treat God's customary order as a stable, content-specified policy for producing effects in the presence of their ordinary accompanying conditions, without attributing any irreducible causal power to the fire or cotton and without reducing the policy to a mere summary of observed constant conjunctions; the argument should explain how the policy's normative and teleological structure can do explanatory work that brute regularity cannot, and should address why this response is not simply a relabeling of the friend's objection.
