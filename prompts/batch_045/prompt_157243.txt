I want you to stress-test a distinction I've been toying with on a real case. Call them type-1 and type-2 corrigibility failures. Type-1 is the classic shutdown problem: being switched off is bad for whatever the system is optimizing, so it has instrumental reason to resist — hide information, disable the switch, manipulate whoever holds it. Type-2 is the one I actually care about: the system puts up zero resistance, it is perfectly happy to be edited or turned off, but the correction accomplishes nothing, because whatever produced the behavior isn't touched by it. I split type-2 into (a) the failure lives in the objective or the learning setup, so switching off and retraining reproduces the same failure, and (b) what I call correction leakage — for a system that learns from interaction, the operator's interventions are themselves input, so correcting it isn't just stopping it, it's shaping it, and that shaping can be optimized against.
