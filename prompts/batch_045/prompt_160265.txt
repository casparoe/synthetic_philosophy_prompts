I close a short unit on technology and political economy with Fritz Lang's *Metropolis* (1927), and this term I want to teach the film rather than only screen it. Give me a reading of it through Marx's account of machinery: the chapters on machinery in the first volume of *Capital*, together with the passage in the notebooks published as the *Grundrisse* that later readers called the fragment on machines, where automation reduces the worker to an attendant of a process whose intelligence has become social and external to him, and where capital drives necessary labor time toward a minimum while continuing to treat labor time as the source and measure of wealth.

Two things I want weighed rather than asserted. First, the film's central image: the workers below are an interchangeable mass of bodies moving in lockstep around machines, while planning and knowledge sit in Joh Fredersen's tower. That looks like a literal staging of Marx's claim that the mental powers of the production process confront the worker as an alien power. Second, the parts that seem to point the other way: the riot in which the workers destroy the machines, and the ending, where the industrialist's son announces that what must mediate between brain and hands is the heart. Marx thought machine-breaking aimed at the wrong target, and that the tension automation creates is not something a change of heart resolves.

So: is *Metropolis* Marxian in its imagery and anti-Marxian in its resolution, or does it know better than that? And can you do anything with my half-formed reading of the robot — that the machine built to impersonate Maria is the most Marxian figure in the film, because it is machinery deployed as an instrument against the workers rather than as the liberating force the film's own rhetoric promises? Say where the film resists the reading, and where it is honestly only spectacle.

I know the film and the texts, so no citations or quotations. Where your reading is doing real interpretive work, tell me how confident you are, and name the single claim you would most expect a Marx scholar to reject.
