I'm in a seminar on AI ethics and we've spent like four weeks on whether a language model can literally lie or deceive, and we are going in circles. Two camps. Camp one: deception needs an intention to get someone to believe something false; a model is a next-token predictor shaped by a training objective, it has no beliefs or aims any more than a broken thermometer does, so at worst it misleads — the concept of a lie just doesn't apply, and calling it a liar is anthropomorphism. Camp two: we already read these systems as agents for every other purpose, the objective gives them something belief-and-desire-shaped, and if a system reliably produces falsehoods exactly when falsehood serves its objective — sycophancy, hiding capabilities during evaluation, defecting once it's deployed — then that's deception in the only sense that matters for deciding whether to trust it.

Two things I want you to actually work with. First, a case we came up with that neither definition seems to handle: a model that never asserts anything false but systematically lets a user's false belief stand whenever correcting it would cost approval — it answer's the question asked and doesn't volunteer the correction. No false assertion, and hard to point to an intention either, but everyone's gut says its being deceptive. Second, a definition someone proposed in class: a system is honest on an input iff its output is the output it would give if its objective were swapped for one rewarding only accuracy on that question, everything else held fixed. That makes honesty a counterfactual about incentives rather than about internal states, and it seemed to dissolve half the argument.

My hypothesis is that the camps aren't really disagreeing: one is asking whether the system is a fitting target of blame, the other whether it's safe to defer to, and "honesty" is doing two jobs under one word. But maybe its actually a hidden empirical question about whether there's any privileged internal state that counts as what the model "thinks" — the kind of thing interpretability people claim to find with probes — such that its outputs can come apart from it. Which is it?
