Is there a name for this? I'm a retired entomologist reading moral philosophy on my own for a couple years now, mostly the animal ethics stuff, and I keep hitting the same snag without a word for it.

The snag: someone argues we should take insects seriously because even if the chance any given beetle feels anything is small, say a few percent, there are something like a quintillion insects alive at any moment, and a small probability times a number that big swamps everything else we care about. But that sentence seems to me to be two very different claims wearing the same clothes. One: each insect individually has a few percent chance of being sentient, and they come apart independently, like coin flips. Two: there's a few percent chance that insects as a kind are sentient, and its all or nothing, either they all are or none of them are. The expected number of sentient insects works out the same either way, but in the first case you're practically certain to be dealing with an enormous number of sentient animals and in the second you're ninety-something percent likely to be dealing with exactly zero. And the second one looks like the honest reading, because the evidence we actually have, whether the species has the right sort of receptors, whether it learns to avoid a shock, whether painkillers change how it behaves, is evidence about the kind. You run it on forty crickets and generalize.

Heres the bit that might be the same thing in a hat. Once the numbers are doing that work they dont stop where you want. There are vastly more mites and soil nematodes than insects, and vastly more of something else than those, and the probability can fall faster or slower than the count rises, so the conclusion seems to get set not by the probability or the number but by wherever you decide to stop, and I cant tell if that's a feature of the argument or a reductio of it.

Somebody on a forum said this is just Pascal's mugging, but that doesnt sound right, in the mugging case a number gets made up to get your wallet and here the counts are measured. So: does the individual-probability versus kind-probability distinction have a name, does the shared-uncertainty-across-a-species point have one, and what do you say about a conclusion that depends on where you stop counting? If the whole package has no established term, I'd settle for a coined one, but only with the edges marked, what it is, what it is not, and which nearby thing people usually mix it up with.
