Hey — I'm putting together the framing doc for a grad seminar on reduction and emergence, and I want the criteria settled before we look at any positions. So: write me the desiderata that any adequate account of reduction (and of its failure, i.e. emergence) has to meet.

Purpose, so you know what "adequate" means here: the theory should be good enough to say something action-guiding to a working scientist. She's got a macro-level model — a climate model, a population-genetics model, a model of flock behaviour — and a micro-level one, and she wants to know whether it's worth hunting for a derivation of the first from the second, and what it would even mean if there isn't one. So it's not a metaphysical theory of nature's levels for its own sake; it needs consequences for modelling practice.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, and be honest about which ones are genuine constraints and which are just what one camp happens to demand. At minimum I want these on the table:

- Derivation isn't enough: the classic bridge-principle story needs something on top of deducibility even in the easy cases, since the macro vocabulary doesn't appear in the micro one. What exactly does reduction have to add over and above "you can deduce it"?
- Multiple realizability: the macro kind gets realized by wildly heterogeneous micro states. Is that a knockdown, or only a demand for a disjunctive bridge law — and what does each answer cost you?
- The exclusion worry: if the micro story is causally complete, what's left for the macro to do? I want the desideratum here, not the solution.
- Decoupled macro behaviour: cases where the macro regularity shrugs off changes in micro details you'd have expected to matter (critical phenomena and universality, that sort of thing). Should a theory of reduction be required to explain why the micro details drop out, or is that a separate virtue we're smuggling in?

Two things I care about most.

First, I think there's a conflation running through most of this literature and I want the list to force it open: a claim about what exist (are there macro properties over and above the micro ones), a claim about what can be derived (is there a derivation, and how strong is it), and a claim about what's predictable or controllable in practice (do you need the macro model to get the answer at all, even in principle). Those can come apart in both directions. Make each of them a seperate requirement, say which one the "autonomy of the special sciences" arguments actually need, and flag which one you think people really mean when they say "emergent".

Second, there's a failure mode I've started calling phantom reduction: the micro model gets tweaked, in ways nobody thinks of as cheating, until it reproduces the macro behaviour — extra terms, fitted parameters, a convenient choice of boundary conditions — and everyone declares reduction achieved, but nothing has been explained and the tuned micro model predicts nothing outside the regime it was fitted in. I want a requirement that rules that out, and I want you to argue for what it should be: what has to be true of a reduction beyond output-matching? Novel prediction outside the fitted regime? Stability of the derivation under perturbation of the micro assumptions? Bridge principles that are independently checkable? Then say whether your requirement is satisfiable at all, and whether it quietly collapses back into the multiple-realizability requirement or is genuinely independent of it.

Then: is any subset of your own list jointly unsatisfiable? If so, which, and why — that's the part I most want to see. And name the single requirement that does the most work in excluding candidate accounts, plus the one you'd drop first if you had to drop one.

Last, a short scorecard (table is fine, a paragraph each is fine) for three familiar shapes of view: the bridge-law / deduce-the-macro-from-the-micro-plus-bridges picture; the "reduction is a limit or approximation relation between models" picture, renormalization-group and continuum-limit style; and the "emergence is about the causal powers of macro configurations" picture. Just enough to see where each breaks against your list. And please don't pad it with a reading list or citations — I know the literature, I'm after the criteria.
