Looking for a diagnosis more than a verdict here, if that makes sense.

I'm in a Groundwork reading group — four of us, one works on Kant, the rest of us are just interested — and we've totally stalled on the universal law formula. I've started to think the stall is structural rather than anyone being stubborn.

Where we're at. Kant runs the test on the four cases in Groundwork II: the maxim of ending your life from self-love, the false promise, letting your talents rust, refusing to help anyone. Standard reading: they fail in two different ways. The first pair can't even be *conceived* as a universal law, because the practice they depend on dissolves once everyone is in on it — promising collapses into noise, that kind of thing. The second pair are perfectly conceivable as universal but can't be *willed* as universal by a rational agent. Then there's the old Hegelian complaint that with no independent moral content going in, the test can't generate anything out, and there's the rival move that treats all four cases as one phenomenon, practical self-defeat, with the conceivability language doing no real work. Every meeting we rerun those three moves and land in exactly the same place, and reading further just seems to add versions of the same three moves.

My half-formed idea is that a lot of it is hiding in a question nobody asks: what is the "everyone" in "what if everyone did that" actually doing? As far as I can tell people just say *universal* and move on, but there are at least four different counterfactuals in the neighbourhood. (1) Literally every agent adopts the maxim and acts on it on every occasion where it applies. (2) Every agent is disposed to act on it whenever it happens to suit them. (3) Some large but not total share — pick 90% — adopts it. (4) Everyone knows the maxim is *permitted* and adjusts their behaviour accordingly. These come apart badly: a maxim can be harmless under (4) and self-destructive under (1), or fine at 10% uptake and a disaster at 90%, and the threshold where it tips is itself a separate question nobody in my group can see how to settle. Call it the uptake parameter.

So, the actual ask. Is the disagreement about the formula a substantive one about what rationality requires, a verbal one about "can be conceived" versus "can be willed", or — my hypothesis — several people quietly running different counterfactuals with different uptake assumptions under one label, and彼此 not noticing? Sit with the hypothesis long enough to tell me where it breaks. And: would making uptake explicit count as progress, the kind of distinction that dissolves some cases and sharpens the rest? Or is it just pushing the bump in the rug, on the grounds that you'd then have to *argue* for an uptake model, and that argument would secretly be the moral argument the test was supposed to generate for free? If it's the last one, say so plainly.

Please don't walk me back through the four examples, we've got those. And skip citations, I can chase those up myself.
