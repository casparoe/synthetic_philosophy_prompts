I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on indicative conditionals, and I've drafted a short paper that tries to rescue Adams's Thesis from Lewis's triviality results by indexing the content of a conditional to a partition. I've pasted my abridged notes below. I'd like your reaction to the argument itself rather than to the literature around it (I have the bibliography under control), and I'm most interested in whether the central maneuver actually blocks the proofs or merely relabels the problem.

Notes:

1. Adams's Thesis: the credence a conditional deserves is the corresponding conditional credence, Cr(if A, C) = Cr(C|A), for Cr(A) > 0.

2. Lewis's triviality results: suppose a conditional expresses a proposition in an algebra closed under the conditional connective, so that the conditional's probability is a function of the agent's credence function together with the antecedent and consequent. Add Adams's Thesis in full generality and triviality follows: the probability of a conditional collapses onto the probability of its consequent, so Cr(C|A) = Cr(C) for every A of positive probability, and credences take at most a handful of values. The engine, in the version of the argument that assumes conditionalization, is an averaging lemma: for incompatible X and Y, Cr((X∨Y)→C) = Cr(X→C)·Cr(X|X∨Y) + Cr(Y→C)·Cr(Y|X∨Y). The lemma requires the conditional to distribute over disjunction of antecedents, since only then is the proposition on the left the conjunction of the two on the right.

3. My proposal: a conditional's content is indexed to the partition of logical space generated by its own antecedent and consequent; call this the conditional's home partition. Relative to that partition, if A, C expresses the union of the A∧C cells with a selection drawn from the remaining cells in proportion to their measure, the selection being sized so that the union's total measure is exactly Cr(C|A) — concretely, the added selection has measure Cr(C|A)·Cr(¬A). Adams's Thesis is then stated relative to the home partition, and holds by construction.

4. Why I think triviality is blocked: when the averaging lemma rewrites (X∨Y)→C in terms of X→C and Y→C, the embedded conditionals have different home partitions from the original one, since the partition generated by X and C is not the partition generated by X∨Y and C. So the lemma equates propositions carrying different indices and the derivation never gets started. The proofs, I argue, all tacitly assume that a conditional retains its content while its antecedent is re-partitioned, and that is precisely what I deny.

5. Costs I grant: (i) if X∨Y then C no longer entails if X then C, an inference ordinary speakers appear to make; (ii) re-embedding a conditional under a larger antecedent or under another connective shifts its content; (iii) because the selection in 3 is measure-matching, the content of a conditional varies with the speaker's own credences; (iv) proportional selection leaves the content multiply realizable, since many distinct regions satisfy the same description.

6. What I cannot settle: whether 3 is a genuine semantic proposal or only a notational variant of denying that the algebra is closed under the conditional, in which case I have bought nothing that a no-proposition Adams-style view did not already have; and whether the credence-dependence in 5(iii), together with the arbitrariness in 5(iv), turns Adams's Thesis into a stipulation rather than an explanation of why the thesis holds.

Please give me exactly five numbered comments, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. I'd like at least one on whether home-partition indexing really blocks the averaging lemma, one on whether the view collapses into some more familiar position, and one on how damaging cost 5(i) is.
