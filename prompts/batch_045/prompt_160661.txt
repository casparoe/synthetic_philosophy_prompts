In the reading group I've been part of for the past year, the argument about panpsychism and the combination problem has not moved at all. The defenders treat the combination problem as a research programme rather than a refutation, and lean on a parity claim: any view that admits composite objects or macro-properties owes some account of how parts make a whole, so panpsychism is being held to a standard nobody else meets. The critics reply that the parity is superficial — for structural and dynamical properties, determination by the micro-base may be enough, but for subjects the demand is different, since what is needed is not merely that the macro-experiential facts be fixed by the micro-experiential facts but that there be one subject rather than many, and no appeal to brute necessary covariation or to grounding alone answers that.

My hypothesis is that "the combination problem" is not one dispute but at least three, and that one of them may be ill-posed. The heading seems to bundle: (a) whether macro-consciousness is determined or grounded in micro-consciousness; (b) how a plurality of subjects could be one subject rather than merely a collection; and (c) whether there is any determinate base level of subjects to combine in the first place — since a micropsychist who identifies experiences with the intrinsic natures of "the fundamental entities" owes an answer to what those entities are, and if fundamental physics does not in fact supply discrete, separable, countable individuals, the combining may have no fixed relata. I suspect (c) is doing more work than either side acknowledges, and that part of the dispute evaporates once it is made explicit.

Diagnose the impasse on the basis of what each side actually argues: how much of it is verbal, how much is a genuine disagreement in metaphysics about what composition must deliver, how much is a disagreement about what would count as an explanation (a constitutive mechanism, a bridging law, a grounding claim, or a brute necessity), and how much rests on undefended assumptions about granularity and the individuation of the base. Then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a distinction, a result about grounding or composition, a development in physics, or a change of question — or argue that no progress should be expected, and say why.
