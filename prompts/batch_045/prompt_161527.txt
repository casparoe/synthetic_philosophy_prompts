History has no such laws — nothing about human affairs holds without exception — so strictly speaking there are no explanations in history, only 'explanation sketches' in which the law is gestured at but never actually stated. What historians really do, as the narrativists showed, is weave events into a story, and the shape of that story (tragedy, comedy, farce) is imposed by the writer rather than found in the archive. There was an older rival to the covering-law model — the idea that you explain an action by showing it made sense for the agent to do it, given what she was after and what she believed — but that is really just storytelling with a psychology gloss. The upshot for anyone writing history: stop hunting for causes, choose the plot that makes the sequence intelligible, and remember that intelligibility is a virtue of the writing, not a discovery about what happened."
