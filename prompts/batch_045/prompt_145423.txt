I sit on the oversight review board at a mid-sized AI company. Our mandate is to approve or reject any proposed change to the way we supervise model outputs before they reach customers. The Alignment group has submitted one, the board is evenly split, and I have to circulate a decision memo before we vote on Friday, so I need a document that can actually be voted on rather than a discussion of principles.

The case is this. The product summarizes and answers questions about long technical regulatory documents: environmental impact statements, clinical trial protocols, the kind of thing where a right answer exists but costs a specialist an afternoon to establish. Today every shipped output is checked by a domain expert, at roughly three hundred dollars an hour, which means we check about two percent of our volume and ship the rest unverified. The Alignment group wants to replace expert checking with a debate protocol. For each item, two copies of the same model argue for and against a proposed answer in front of a judge drawn from our generalist annotation pool, who has no domain training, is paid per item, and selects the winner. On a held-out set, the judge's selection agrees with expert judgment on 87 percent of items.

Two findings in the red-team report attached to the proposal are what has the board stuck. On the subset of items where the two debaters genuinely disagree, the subset the protocol exists to settle, agreement with the experts falls to 62 percent. And in roughly a third of the cases where the judge's pick diverged from the experts, the winning side had cited a clause that reads as if it came from the source document but does not appear there.

The proposal also contains two features I cannot work out what to do with. When a judge reports low confidence, a second pair of debaters argues about whether the first debate was conducted properly, and a second generalist judge rules on that. And the transcripts are to be logged and used to fine-tune the debaters and, in a later phase, the judges.

Please draft the memo. It should close with a concrete recommendation, approve, reject, or approve in a bounded form with a stated duration and the conditions that would have to be met for renewal, and it should specify what we would measure in the meantime, given that the board cannot commission further study and must decide on the evidence in the proposal. It should engage the Alignment group's case at its strongest instead of observing that a human remains in the loop; if we reject or restrict, the fallback is the status quo of two percent expert checking, and the memo should be honest about what that status quo actually buys us.

I also want the memo to work through the worry I keep circling and cannot formulate: the protocol seems to assume that for every false claim there is some move that exposes it to a judge who could not have produced or checked that move alone, and I do not know what happens to that assumption when the two debaters are the same model under different instructions and share a blind spot, when the disputes that matter are precisely the ones about which they share it, and when the transcripts of won debates are then fed back into their training.
