For fifteen years I've watched the same fight recur in the comments of the blogs I read: the geometric-rationality camp, which says a repeated bettor should maximize expected log growth and treats arithmetic-expectation maximization as a category error about which average is the right one, versus the expected-value camp, which says maximizing expected utility with a nearly linear utility in money is fine, that risk aversion belongs in a curvature parameter rather than in the choice of average, and that any real agent has many independent shots, projects, or successors across which the arithmetic average is the one that matters. Everyone restates their case, nobody's position moves by a basis point, and I want to know what kind of standoff this is.

My hypothesis is that it is mostly a disguised factual disagreement about the number of independent risk-bearing units and how often they get re-pooled, not a normative one at all. Test case: split a fund into n autonomous sub-funds, each facing the same repeated even-money gamble with a 60% win probability, each reinvesting only its own returns, and every τ rounds merge all sub-funds and re-split them equally. With τ = 1 you effectively have one reinvesting bankroll and the log-optimal stake (20%) is the one that grows the typical fund; with τ = ∞ and n enormous relative to the number of rounds, the law of large numbers takes over, the aggregate grows at the arithmetic rate, and staking everything is optimal for the total. So one knob slides the prescription continuously from Kelly to max-EV, and each camp may just be describing a corner of the space.

Tell me whether that knob dissolves the dispute or whether something survives it. If something survives, is it a verbal fight about the word "rational," a substantive difference about whether the typical trajectory or the aggregate across units is the thing to optimize, or a bedrock intuition neither side can argue for? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a theorem, a formal distinction, an empirical estimate of the parameters, or a change of question? Skip the survey of both positions; I've read them.
