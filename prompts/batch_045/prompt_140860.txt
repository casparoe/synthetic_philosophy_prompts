I'm teaching a graduate seminar this term on the metaphysics of dispositions, and our session on finks and masks has bogged down in a way I recognise all too well: after three weeks the students have sorted themselves into two camps who are no longer so much disagreeing as explaining different things, each to its own satisfaction. A colleague from the philosophy of science is sitting in on that session, and rather than hand her a reading list I would like to hand the room a document. So here is what I am asking for: draft the joint statement that a defender of the conditional analysis of dispositional ascriptions and a dispositional realist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. By the conditional analyst I mean someone who holds that what it is for a thing to be disposed to manifest M in circumstances C is for a suitably restricted conditional — if C, then M — to hold of that thing, and who therefore owes an account of the restriction that is not circular. By the dispositional realist I mean someone who holds that the disposition is an intrinsic, causally efficacious property of the thing, in virtue of which the conditional holds when it holds, so that the conditional is evidence for the disposition rather than what the ascription says. I want the claims both sides can sign — about what the fink and mask cases actually establish, about what an adequate account of dispositional ascription has to accomplish, about whether cases drawn from the special sciences (solubility, conductivity, virulence) and cases drawn from ordinary life (fragility, flammability) must be handled by the same apparatus — followed by the residual disagreement stated twice over, once in the analyst's preferred vocabulary and once in the realist's, in wording each would accept for its own side.

For the cruxes, I want each one paired with a case or argument both parties would accept as bearing on it, and — the part that never happens in seminar — each side's prediction about how that case will turn written down in advance, so that the collaboration has something to be wrong about. I have been working up three cases as a shared test-bed, and I would rather you used mine than the stock examples everyone has already seen: (i) a dimmer fink, in which the approach of C lowers the chance of M to some middling value such as 0.3 rather than to zero, so that the disposition is neither simply lost nor simply prevented; (ii) an intrinsic fink that is triggered by the disposition's own beginning to manifest, so that the manifestation would have gone to completion had it not got started; and (iii) a masked mask, in which a condition that would interfere with M is itself interfered with by a further condition, with the result that M occurs after all. For each, say what each theory is committed to about whether the thing has the disposition, whether the relevant conditional is true, and whether the interfering condition should be counted as a mask, a fink, both, or neither — and mark the points at which the two theories' verdicts come apart in a way that could in principle be checked rather than merely asserted. If you conclude that some part of the dispute is merely verbal, say plainly which part and why; my own suspicion is that a great deal rides on whether the chemist's soluble and the vase's fragile are being read as the same kind of ascription at all. Format the whole thing as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. No names, dates or bibliography, please: the argumentative structure is what I am after, and I can supply the references myself.
