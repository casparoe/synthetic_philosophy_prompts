I run a once-a-month philosophy-ish reading group at the public library — nine regulars, mostly retired engineers, plus a retired English teacher and a guy who used to do quality control at a factory. What I want next month (one hour, no slides, just us round a table) is for them to actually feel the strong version: that categorising just is analogising, that a concept isn't a definition you check things against but a cluster of remindings with edges that slip, and that the "essence" is whatever survives when you slide from one situation to another.

So don't explain any of that to me, I've read the book — plan how I get them there. What has to come first and what depends on what, because I kept loosing them last time by starting with the fancy examples. Which single mundane example should carry the hour, what it has to have and what it has to not have (my instinct is that anything shaped like one of those "A is to B as C is to D" puzzles will push them straight back to the literary sense of analogy, but tell me if that's wrong — and ideally I want an example where the same two situations support both a good mapping and a bad one, so they can feel the difference rather than be told it).
