I've got a 45-minute guest slot with a university effective-altruism group -- mostly econ and CS undergrads who've absorbed a fair bit of utilitarianism and just take it for granted that when you say something is wrong you're stating a fact thats either true or false. I need them to feel the Frege-Geach problem, not be told about it. So dont explain the problem to me; plan how I should explain it.

What I want: what has to be in place in the first ten minutes before the problem can even look like a problem (my worry is they'll hear 'expressivism' as relativism and then everything slides), the order the pieces have to come in and what depends on what, and one scenario to carry the whole session. Not the usual 'if stealing is wrong then getting your little brother to steal is wrong' chain, which they'll just nod along to. Tell me what the carrying case has to have and what it has to lack -- I'm guessing it needs an occurrence of the moral sentence where nobody in the room is endorsing it, and it has to be about something harmless enough that no one derails into arguing the first-order ethics.

Then the misconception map. The ones I expect: 'so expressivists have to deny modus ponens', 'the fix is just to give moral sentences truth conditions after all', and someone saying isnt this just about people from different cultures disagreeing. Where in the sequence do I head each off, and is there one I should actually let someone say out loud first and then work with?

One wrinkle I want in but dont know where to put: 'its probably wrong to X'. If saying 'X is wrong' is expressing an attitude rather than describing a state of affairs, its unclear what it even is to be 30% confident that X is wrong -- and that feels to me like a sharper version of the embedding worry than the conditional version, since confidence looks like it has to be in something proposition-shaped. Is that a mid-session pivot, a closing example, or a trap I should stay out of in 45 minutes?

And finally: one question to end on that would show me they've got the problem rather than memorised the slogan, plus what I should flat-out refuse to get into at this level.
