I design build systems and incremental-computation infrastructure, so my working vocabulary is: content-addressed artifacts, dependency edges, invalidation and rebuild, inlining and macro expansion, partial evaluation, irreducible primitives, and the prohibition on cycles. Please restate the contemporary debate about metaphysical grounding and fundamentality in those terms, as faithfully as you can. I care about three sub-disputes in particular: whether in-virtue-of is a relation among facts or is better treated as a connective on sentences; whether grounding can be analyzed in terms of necessity, essence, or counterfactual dependence, or instead resists any such analysis because it is sensitive to more than what holds across possible worlds; and whether fundamentality is primitive or definable as being ungrounded, and absolute or only relative to a level of description.

Then give me the residue report. Which theses translate cleanly, which translate only if you quietly import an assumption the vocabulary does not contain, and which have no counterpart in it at all? Do not rest the translation on one stock example; I want the structural mapping, and I want you to say where it is doing real work rather than decorating.

Two points in particular. In a build graph the interesting objects are the top-level targets, while the leaves are merely given inputs, which seems to invert what the metaphysicians privilege. And content addressing identifies any two nodes whose contents are necessarily equivalent, which, if it carried over, would settle the second sub-dispute by fiat rather than by argument. For each: is it a genuine feature of my framework or a distortion I have smuggled in? Finally, say what the residue as a whole suggests: something my vocabulary cannot yet express, or something the original debate was less clear about than it looks.
