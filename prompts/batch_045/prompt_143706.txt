Me and two other people in a reading group have been arguing about anthropic reasoning for about two years and we have literally not budged on anything, which is starting to make me think were not disagreeing about what I think we're disagreeing about.

The two positions: the self-sampling assumption (SSA) says, roughly, treat yourself as a random draw from the observers who actually exist in whichever hypothesis is true. The self-indication assumption (SIA) says treat the bare fact that you exist as evidence — you should have expected to find yourself existing more under hypotheses that contain more observers, so hypotheses get weighted by how many observers they predict. Everyone knows the flashpoint cases: the presumptuous philosopher, where SIA tells you to be near-certain of the theory that says the universe is a trillion times bigger and to more or less ignore your telescopes, while SSA says your telescopes are all you've got; and the sleeping beauty setup, where SSA gives 1/2 and SIA gives 1/3.

Here's my own attempt to get underneath it — tell me where it goes wrong. Take the cosmological cases, where every observer in a given world has the same evidence, nobody has a special room number or a special memory. In those, as far as I can tell, the two principles agree exactly when the hypotheses predict the same number of observers, and otherwise they come apart by precisely the ratio of the two counts. That makes me think the whole fight reduces to a single parameter: how much your credence should scale with the number of observers. SSA says not at all, SIA says linearly. Fine. But then what fixes the exponent? Why not something in between, or a version that scales with the number of observers like you rather than the number of observers full stop? If nothing fixes it, then this isn't a disagreement about evidence or about physics at all — it's a disagreement about which prior to bring to the party, and there's no court of appeal for that, which would explain two years of nobody moving.

A related thing that makes me suspicious: in an infinite universe both principles look broken to me. The counts aren't finite, so SIA's weighting is infinity against infinity and SSA's uniform draw from an infinite class isn't a draw at all, and whatever answer you get seems to depend on how you take a limit — which looks like the same argument reappearing one level up rather than anything getting settled.

So: verbal dispute, genuine but unarguable clash of priors, a hidden question about whether there's a fact of the matter about which observer you turned out to be, or something I haven't thought of? I want the diagnosis, with reasons tied to what each side is actually committed to, not a summary of the two views. And then the honest part: given your diagnosis, what would progress even look like — a case one side has to swallow, a consistency or dutch book argument, a limit argument — or is the right answer that we should stop expecting this to be settled? 500 words max please, and skip the reading list, I've read the surveys.
