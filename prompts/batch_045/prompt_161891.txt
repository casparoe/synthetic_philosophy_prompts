Me and a friend got into a proper argument about Maxwell's demon last night after a couple of beers and I've been stewing on it since. His position: the demon works fine right up until it has to erase its memory, Landauer's principle says resetting a bit costs kT ln 2, and that is the whole exorcism — information is physical, end of story. My position: fine, but build a demon that never erases anything. Let it write its bits onto a tape that starts out non-uniform (say 90% zeros) and then gets carried off downstream and never looked at again. Now the erasure bookkeeping has nothing to bite on inside the device, and the weight still goes up. He says that's not a different exorcism, it's the same one moved one system over: the blank-ish tape is the low-entropy resource, and preparing it is just erasure done by somebody else. I dunno, maybe he's right, but we also got stuck on a second thing and it's the bit I actually care about: is the budget really kT ln 2 per bit when the tape isn't uniform? A gigabyte of 90%-zeros doesn't feel like a gigabyte of fuel to me, and I think the number has to involve the Shannon entropy of the cells somehow, but we couldn't get it straight and neither of us could definately say what the demon is spending.

So build me the case. I want a demon scenario, engineered to spec, that settles it.

Constraints on the setup: one classical ideal gas, one heat bath at temperature T, one box with a partition or shutter and a weight you can lift. No second reservoir and no temperature difference smuggled in anywhere, no quantum tricks, no cosmology, no black box labelled intelligent being — the demon can be an idealized feedback controller with a finite memory, but it's a physical device made of parts. One paragraph of setup, that's it.

What the scenario has to deliver: net positive work over many cycles, with the gas macroscopically back where it started; no erasure anywhere in the cycle, nothing gets reset, memory cells are written once and then leave; the gate is genuinely controlled by the demon's state, so the old Smoluchowski / Feynman ratchet-and-pawl objection (the flap rattles too, the pawl is warm too) never gets off the ground — say explicitly in the setup why it doesn't; all operations reversible or quasi-static, so there's no cheap dissipation doing hidden work.

Then the part I actually want: derive the accounting, don't announce it. Give me the work per cycle and per tape cell in terms of k, T and the tape's statistics; identify which physical system's entropy goes up, by how much, and show the inequality holds with the numbers in it. Then run the three accounts against the case and say what each one predicts here: the erasure/Landauer account (and why it comes away empty-handed on this design), the fluctuation account (and why it isn't engaged), and the information-reservoir or free-energy account (with the real numbers). Tell me whether the per-cell budget is kT ln 2, or kT times the cells' divergence from uniform, or something else, and where my 90%-zeros intuition is right and where it's wrong.

Finish with: which single feature of your design is doing the work; what happens if I remove it — start with an already-random tape, or recycle the tape through the demon instead of discarding it; and the strongest thing my friend can say to make the whole thing collapse back into the standard Landauer story, i.e. whether this is a genuinely different exorcism or the same one with an extra comma in it. Plus an honest note on what the case can't settle.

What it's for, so you know what counts as cheating: I want to know whether the essential resource in the demon story is erasure, or the low entropy of whatever the demon writes to. If your demon secretly runs off a temperature gradient, or you just state that the second law survives without showing which system pays and how much, the case does nothing for me — the verdicts have to fall out of the physics, not be written into it.
