Here is my situation. Next week I am presenting a works-in-progress paper at a joint session of a political theory department and an economics department's social choice reading group. Forty minutes of talk, thirty minutes of questions, and the questions are what I am worried about.

My thesis: moral compromise between holders of incommensurable value systems can generate genuine gains from trade, and these gains do not presuppose any common measure of value — no cardinalization, no interpersonal comparison, no numeraire, and therefore no covert consequentialism. All they presuppose is that each side can rank small perturbations of a given policy bundle in its own terms, i.e. that each side has local, intrapersonal substitution rates between the dimensions actually in dispute. And because those rates are path-dependent, the order in which a polity makes its compromises partly determines where it ends up.

The argument has three moves. (1) Dimensionality: real disputes between value systems are never single-dimensional. In my running example — a jurisdiction legalizing assisted dying while accommodating conscientious objection — the compromise is a vector with separable dimensions: eligibility criteria, waiting period, psychiatric review, institutional opt-out, guaranteed geographic access, and public funding. Because it is a vector, two sides whose overall rankings of whole packages cannot be placed on a common scale can still log-roll across dimensions. (2) Local substitution rates: each value system can answer "how much more of dimension B would I need to be compensated by, to accept one more unit of movement on dimension A?" for small moves, and comparative advantage does the rest — each side concedes where its own local resistance is cheapest. This is the Ricardian point: trade needs only intrapersonal ratios, not interpersonal comparisons. (3) Non-integrability: conceding changes the conceder. Expressive and identity effects, precedent, shifts in which dimensions are salient, and adaptive changes in conviction all mean the local rates do not integrate into any global exchange rate. That is why there is no numeraire, and it is also why procedure — who sets the agenda, in what order concessions are made — is partly constitutive of the outcome rather than merely instrumental to it. A corollary I defend: because integrity and expressive losses behave like tariffs rather than like transfers, the compromise frontier lies strictly inside the Pareto frontier on the relevant dimensions.
