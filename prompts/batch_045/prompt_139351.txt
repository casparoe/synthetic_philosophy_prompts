I host a tiny internal podcast at my company and next week's guest is a sociologist who defends the view that generations are real kinds and not just handy labels. Her version of it is that a generation is real the way a recession is real: a historically located causal structure you pick out by the process that produced it, not by checking whether members share some list of traits. One reason I actually care right now is that HR just sorted all 60 of us into generational buckets for a two-day workshop, and the sorting visibly failed on real people: my 61-year-old teammate got filed with the boomers and has never once voted the way that bucket predicts, and our 24-year-old intern is far more risk-averse than anyone in it. So I want to press on whether the kind is supposed to be doing explanatory work or just rhetorical work.

Give me eight to ten interview questions that don't let her simply restate the view, with the answer she's most likely to give to each and the follow-up that answer invites. I want at least one on each of these: (1) the age-period-cohort problem, i.e. how anyone could ever tell a genuine cohort effect apart from everyone just being young, or from something that hit every age group at the same time; (2) the boundaries, since if the kind is real the birth-year edges shouldn't drift depending on who is drawing them, and she happens to put people born around 1980 into a neither-here-nor-there category that other writers don't recognize at all; (3) her recession analogy, which I think quietly cuts against her, because a recession gets dated after the fact by a body with criteria, and I dont see what plays that role for a generation; (4) looping, in the sense of whether telling people they are in a cohort with a reputation makes them act like it, and whether she gets to count that as the kind doing work or whether it just shows the kind was made rather than found; (5) export, meaning what happens when a cohort label gets applied to teenagers in countries that share none of the events supposedly constitutive of it. Also give me one closing question about what a workplace like mine should do with these labels if she is right, and one about what would make her give the view up. Please write everything in American English, and keep the phrasing listenable since this is audio, not print.
