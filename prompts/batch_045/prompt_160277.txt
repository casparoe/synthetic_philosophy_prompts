hey, quick context so the ask makes sense. I'm doing scoping work for a small foundation program — roughly $3M over five years, so realistically four to six junior researchers plus a couple of workshops and some commissioned empirical work we don't run ourselves — and the live question is whether "is a bigger population better, and if so should anyone try to make it happen" deserves to be its own research program, or whether it just gets folded into existing longtermist work on existential risk and into ordinary global-priority-setting where population size barely shows up as a variable at all.

What I want from you is an agenda, not a survey. Which open questions in this area should the program actually put money on, in what order, and why? I care much more about your criteria and your dependency map than about the list itself. For each question tell me:

- what other questions its answer is upstream of — I want the dependency structure, not just "this is important";
- how tractable it is with tools we actually have (conceptual work, demography, macro, forecasting), and roughly who'd have to do it;
- how neglected it is, i.e. whether our funding changes anything or whether it'd happen anyway;
- what specific result or evidence over the next five years would move it up or down the list.

Three things I keep comming back to, and I want you to place them in the ordering — and tell me if any of them is a dud:

1. The cohort-lag point. Any fertility intervention now mostly changes who exists after 2080, not the next couple of decades, so basically the whole case has to run on long-run considerations — trajectory, civilization's capacity, the shape of the far future — rather than on near-term welfare. Which makes the program weirdly dependent on views about long-run discounting and on how much weight demographic extrapolation that far out can bear.
2. The "minimum viable population for a technological civilization" question: whether there's a threshold below which recovery from a really big catastrophe isn't possible anymore. If there is, then definately the marginal value of an extra person is huge near that threshold and roughly zero far above it, and "is more better" stops being a question about average value per life and becomes a question about where we sit relative to a cliff.
3. The capture-and-composition worry. Fertility is basically only ever moved by states, and states have historically wanted more population for ethnic-composition and military-capacity reasons, not because of the value of existence. So maybe the honest answer is that any movement big enough to actually move TFR ends up being used for aims this program wouldn't endorse, and the "should anyone try to make it happen" half of the question has a political answer rather than an axiological one.

If the standard material — person-affecting vs impersonal views, the repugnant conclusion, total vs average vs critical-level, the asymmetry — makes your list, that's fine, but say where it sits and whether it's genuinely upstream of anything or mostly downstream of questions people aren't asking. And please add at least one question that isn't on my list and isn't in the usual literature.
