I convene a graduate seminar on thermodynamics and information, and this year I want to retire the way the topic usually ends. Every student arrives already knowing the one-line moral — the demon's information is not free, because resetting its memory costs at least kT ln 2 per bit, and that cost pays for the work — and they leave able to recite it while being unable to do any actual accounting. So I want a problem-set case that the erasure story cannot touch: a device that completes a genuine cycle, in contact with a single heat bath, in which the demon's memory ends the cycle carrying *more* recorded bits than it started with. Nothing is erased; the memory is written to. If the case works, the standard ledger has a zero on the cost side and a positive number on the gain side, and yet the second law is intact, with the compensation sitting somewhere else.

Design me that case in a single paragraph. Constraints: classical throughout, no quantum resources, one heat bath at 300 K, and nothing that could not in principle be built — colloidal particles in optical traps, a bead on a track, a molecular rotor, a tape of bits realized as a sequence of configurable traps. The demon must be physical: it finds out about the system only through a physical coupling, never by stipulation. Its readout must be imperfect, and you choose the error rate yourself and state it; no perfect measurement anywhere in the story. The cycle must deliver strictly positive net work, and everything except the medium being written to must return to its initial state, since otherwise there is no cycle and no appearance of a violation to explain. No second reservoir, no cold bath, no hidden power supply, and no infinite memory: if you use a tape, it is finite, and you say what happens when it runs out.

Then show that the case does what I asked, with numbers a student can check on a calculator:

1. State the work extracted per cycle, in joules and in units of kT ln 2; the number of bits written to the medium per cycle; the Shannon entropy of the record; and the mutual information between the record and the degree of freedom being measured. I will check your mutual information against the binary entropy of whatever error rate you pick.
2. Run three ledgers over the same physical run and give each one a number, in units of kT ln 2: (a) the erasure ledger, cost equal to kT ln 2 per bit erased, which here has nothing to count; (b) the feedback ledger, on which extractable work is bounded by kT times the mutual information acquired, so the gain is paid for on the acquisition side — say where, physically, the compensating dissipation occurs, and what becomes of the difference between the record's entropy and the mutual information, since that difference is the measurement noise and it is not free; (c) the resource ledger, on which the incoming medium is itself a low-entropy consumable and the work is bounded by the medium's entropy increase — say who has to pay to restore the medium, and how much.
3. Identify the one structural feature that is doing the work, and show what happens when it is removed: if the memory is instead reset each cycle, or the medium is recycled inside the device, I expect the case to collapse back into the ordinary one-molecule engine, with all three ledgers giving the same number. Show that collapse explicitly, so I can see the divergence is not an artifact of sloppy bookkeeping.
4. Finally, give me the honest reply from a defender of the erasure ledger: writing to the medium is erasure deferred, the kT ln 2 is paid when fresh blank bits are prepared, and therefore nothing new has been shown. Tell me whether that reply is a real explanation or only a relocation of the system boundary, and what my case does and does not establish about whether erasure is really the locus of the cost or merely one convenient place to put it.

What I do not want: any verdict stipulated rather than derived from the theory, any sneaked-in second bath or power source, any appeal to how the literature words this, and any citation. I know the literature; I want the accounting.
