I'm a PhD student in philosophy, finishing the framing chapter of a thesis on suffering-focused ethics, and I want a short self-diagnostic to hand out at the start of a graduate reading group: a 'which view of the pleasure/pain trade-off do you actually hold?' quiz of ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions. It should sort readers by what they say about cases, not by which label they prefer, so no item may ask directly whether pleasure and pain are commensurable, whether suffering counts for more, or which school they take themselves to belong to.

I care about distinctions that a single weight-on-suffering dial cannot capture, so please build the items around these:

(i) what the threshold is a threshold of — the intensity of a moment, the duration of an episode, a whole life's total, or a person's share of a population's total;
(ii) whether the lexical relation holds only between pain and pleasure or also within the pain dimension, so that enough mild pain can outweigh a single agony;
(iii) whether pain past the threshold lexically dominates any finite quantity of pleasure or is instead merely incomparable with it — I want at least one item that separates a complete, transitive lexical ordering from a partial one on which some pairs go unranked and either option may permissibly be chosen;
(iv) whether the threshold is a feature of outcomes or a constraint on action: I'd like one pair of cases, one about causing a brief agony for the sake of an enormous quantity of mild pleasure and one about failing to prevent one at the same price, though tell me if that pair does not do the work;
(v) whether the threshold is sharp or vague, tested by a sorites-style series in which intensity rises by imperceptible steps while the compensating pleasure grows enormously.

Name the leaves, or mark them unnamed, including at least lexical threshold negative utilitarianism in its per-moment and whole-life variants, a merely weighted or tempered negative-leaning consequentialism, an incomparabilist view, and whatever occupies the cell on which the asymmetry is deontic rather than axiological. Where a combination of answers is unoccupied, say whether it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored, and give each leaf's most awkward commitment in one sentence. Flag any two leaves the quiz cannot separate — in particular whether a very large finite weight on suffering can be told apart from a lexical one by any finite case — and say whether some different item could separate them. No attributions or reading lists; I want the logical space and the positions in it. Please write your answer in British English.
