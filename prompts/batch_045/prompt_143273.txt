hey — I'm on a volunteer committee rewriting the decision procedure for a housing placement org, and I've ended up being the person who has to write the "what counts as a defensible decision around here" page. Concretely: a caseworker gets about 40 minutes with a family, the portal lets them flag at most five units before the family's voucher clock runs out, and we do this a few thousand times a year. So I don't want a theory of rationality in the abstract; I want a spec I could hand to a supervisor who has to audit a written procedure, and to a new caseworker who has to follow it on their second day. Every time I go looking for one I get told that full optimization is off the table for creatures like us and then nothing about what to demand of whatever replaces it.

So: write me the desiderata. Not a reading list — the list of things a theory of bounded rationality or satisficing has to deliver to be adequate *for this purpose* (auditing and teaching a real procedure, not publishing a paper), with the case or consideration behind each, and a verdict on which are non-negotiable and which are just the prejudices of one camp.

Things I think might be on the list, in no order, and I genuinely don't trust any of them:

- the procedure has to be runnable by someone tired, with a stopwatch — it can't contain "solve an intractable problem" as a subroutine
- it has to be teachable: a new caseworker with the manual beats a new caseworker told to use judgment
- it has to be auditable by someone who knows *less* than the caseworker knew at the time (this is the one I care most about; most of what I've read seems to assume an evaluator with the agent's full information)
- it has to be able to say no. There have to be procedures it condemns, and it can't rescue every bad call by positing an unmeasured mental cost afterwards — I've started calling this budget-laundering and I want to know whether that's a real constraint or just my irritation
- it shouldn't grade the interface: if the same units surface in a different order, or the pool is carvable into neighborhoods vs. into price bands, and the theory's verdict flips, it's evaluating a description rather than a decision
- it should say when to spend the budget on reshaping the choice environment instead of on thinking harder (the other blade of Simon's scissors, roughly)
- it should handle the stopping question and the drift question: when do you take the fourth unit and stop looking, and is a caseworker whose standards quietly slide downward after three bad weeks adapting or failing?
- it should survive the fact that we cannot measure cognitive effort in anything like cardinal units, so a theory needing a cost-of-thinking number may just be unimplementable for us — fair demand, or are we whining?
- it should converge to expected-utility maximization as resources go to infinity — I suspect this one is a neoclassical prejudice, especially given the results where knowing less or ignoring information does better

Cases I want each requirement moored to, at minimum: the five-unit cap; the two interfaces that present the same forty apartments differently; the org's distribution versus the family's one shot (whose budget is the theory optimising for, and can it be both); the drift case; and our caseworker who is intransitive across three units but never sees the same triple twice, so the usual arbitrage-style objection doesn't obviously reach her.

Two things I want on top of the list. First, I have a sinking feeling that some subset of these can't all hold together — if so, say which, and what has to give, because I'd rather have four criteria I can defend than nine that are secretly at war. Second, tell me which single criterion knocks out the most candidate theories, i.e. which one is actually doing the excluding.

Last and briefly, no more than a short paragraph: how the three rough approaches I keep running into do against your list — optimization under an explicit computational budget; the fast-and-frugal / fit-to-the-environment heuristics picture; and the version that prices mental effort and tunes the procedure to the distribution of problems. I'll do the real scoring myself.
