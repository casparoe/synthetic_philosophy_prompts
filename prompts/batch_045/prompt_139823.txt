I build and calibrate an operational ensemble prediction system: perturbed initial conditions and stochastic parameterisation schemes, data assimilation, reliability diagrams, Brier and CRPS verification against reforecasts, statistical post-processing of raw ensembles. I have no philosophy training. My group argues endlessly about what a single issued product actually is — say a 30% chance of precipitation in one sector for tomorrow — and I am told this is just the old three-way fight between frequentism, subjectivism, and propensity theories.

Take each position in turn and restate it as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use, saying explicitly what each forces me to say about the issued forecast, about my verification scores, and about the chanciness built into my model. For each, carry over the main objection it has to answer: for frequentism, the reference-class problem (any one forecast belongs to as many verification samples as I care to define — by season, lead time, flow regime, model version — and its measured frequency shifts with the choice); for subjectivism, the worry that unconstrained credence is arbitrary, together with the constraint that credence should track the chances once you know them; for propensity, the objection that propensity-type probabilities resist inversion, so that the direction of conditioning matters in a way that ordinary conditional probabilities do not allow.

Then give me the residue report. What carried over cleanly; what survived only by being distorted (is a probability derived from my ensemble a feature of the atmosphere or a feature of my model, and can my vocabulary even pose that as a question); and what has no counterpart at all — is there anything left in the three-way dispute that cannot be phrased in terms of calibration, sharpness, scoring, and hindcast frequencies?

My hunch is that all three positions collapse, for someone in my seat, into the same practical demand that the system be reliable and sharp, and that the rest is verbal. Tell me whether the residue supports that hunch, or whether the claims my vocabulary cannot express are exactly the ones the philosophers are fighting about — and if so, whether that indicts my vocabulary or the dispute.
