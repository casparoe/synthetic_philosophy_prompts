At work our HR system sorts everyone into a generation straight from their date of birth — you're a Millennial if you were born in this band, Gen Z if that band, no opting out, and the bands are just whatever some think tank picked years ago. But the same system has a free-text "generation you identify with" box, and collegues actually use it: someone born in 1997 insisting they're not Z, and "OK boomer" gets thrown at anyone acting smug whenever they were born. Meanwhile policy people talk about "generational equity" as though a generation can be owed something, and climate and pensions rows treat "future generations" as parties with interests.

What does all of this presuppose about whether generations are real kinds? Is it committed to there being boundaries and shared formative causes out there to be found, or could someone who thinks a generation is just a convenient slice of birth years still make sense of the lot of it? Are the birth-date bit and the self-identification bit even working with the same notion — a demographic cohort versus something more like a cultural or attitudinal kind — or is the practice quietly inconsistent? Where's it underdetermined, in that several different theories of what generations are would all licence it? And what would have to change if we went nominalist: does "generational equity" survive, and can we still say Millennials killed the department store? Answer in American English, please.
