Hi — I'm retired now (used to be experimental solid-state physics) and I've been poking around in philosophy of science for fun, mostly through popular books. I just read a chapter on Kuhn that I half liked and half distrusted, and I want to know which half is which before I repeat any of it at my reading group. The gist it gives is: normal science is puzzle-solving inside a paradigm; then anomolies pile up, the field goes into crisis, and it flips to a new paradigm in something like a gestalt switch, which isn't really a decision made on the evidence. The two paradigms are incommensurable — the word is borrowed from geometry, where the side and the diagonal of a square have no common measure — so there's no neutral language in which the old and the new claims can even be stated, and scientists on opposite sides of a revolution can't really understand each other. Theory choice is therefore conversion rather than argument, and the upshot is that science doesn't get closer to the truth: the new paradigm is just different, and since the world itself changes with the paradigm there's no theory-independent fact of the matter for it to be closer to. The book then says this is why it's anachronistic to claim Lavoisier refuted phlogiston chemistry — the phlogiston people weren't mistaken, they were playing a different game, and oxygen chemistry only won, it wasn't better. Finally it says that later, under fire from critics, Kuhn quietly watered all this down to "local incommensurability," where only a handful of terms change meaning, which basically amounts to conceding the critics' point.

I'm suspicious of the "not closer to the truth" bit, because as a working experimentalist I definitely felt we were latching onto something real, but I don't know enough to say where the chapter goes wrong. Could you go through it and separate what someone who actually works on Kuhn would accept, what's just a fair simplification for a general reader, and what's a real distortion — and tell me whether the phlogiston conclusion survives once the distortions are fixed.
