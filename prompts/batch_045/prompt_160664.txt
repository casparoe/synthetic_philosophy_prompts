Last night me and my friend got into a huge argument about the reference class problem and I'm still annoyed about it. Her position was that the problem basically sinks anthropics wholesale — the doomsday argument, whether you should think you're in a simulation, all of it — because every one of those arguments needs you to say which class you're supposed to be a random sample from, and there's no non-arbitrary answer, so you can just pick whichever class gets you the conclusion you like. I think she's overstating it: it feels like the problem hits some of these much harder than others, and that in the mundane cases — picking a base rate for a one-off project, reasoning about what to expect given that you're the kind of person who'd ask — people muddle through okay enough that "it's all arbitrary" can't be the whole story.

She did float one idea I can't shake, though: instead of choosing a reference class, you average over all the admissible ones weighted by how simple each is to specify, and you only trust a conclusion if it survives that averaging. My gut reaction is that this just moves the arbitrariness into the simplicity weighting, but I genuinely can't tell whether that's fatal or just a different, more manageable kind of mess.

So: which open questions here would you prioritize, in order, and why? I care about the reasoning more than the list — what's upstream of what, whats tractable in three years with philosophy and logic rather than new empirical work, what's neglected relative to its importance, and what evidence or result would move something up or down.
