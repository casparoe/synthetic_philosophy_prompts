Can you write the eulogy I'm giving on Saturday? My aunt edited a small city paper for thirty years and wrote the weekly corrections column under her own byline — she said it was the only part of the paper that was definately true. She was into Arendt's distinction between plain factual truth and the kind of organised lying that doesn't contradict facts one at a time but swaps them out for a tidier story, and she left a note saying: don't tell the room I'm watching over them, but don't turn me into a character either. Her view was that a lie beats the truth because it's built in advance to sound plausible, whereas facts just are what happened and never arrive with a narrative attached — which is a real problem for a eulogy, since a eulogy basically is a tidy story. Half the room are reporters from her old newsroom who'll catch every Arendt refrence; the other half are family and neighbours who knew her as the person with the best tomatoes on the block and who just want comfort. One complication: she walked out of the paper eight months ago after the new owners spiked a correction, and my mother wants that left out of the service — the newsroom half will notice the hole if you do. Five to seven minutes please. Don't resolve the complication by simply leaving it out. Afterward add a short note on how confident you are in the Arendt you're leaning on, and which part of it you're least sure of.
