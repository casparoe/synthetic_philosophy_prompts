I'm an architect; my firm designs correctional facilities, mostly in the American South — intake centers, medium-security housing units, a couple of reentry dorms. Last spring I spent two days at Halden fengsel in Norway on a study tour, and I have been arguing with a colleague about it ever since, because I cannot tell which of the differences between what we build and what they built are moral disagreements and which are institutional habits that each system has mistaken for principle. The popular framing of this as retributivism versus rehabilitation hasn't helped me, because both systems appear to contain both, just wired together differently.

The two practices I want examined, stated as concretely as I can. On the Norwegian side: their correctional system works from a normalization principle, on which the point of a sentence is the restriction of liberty and nothing else — prisoners keep their other civil rights, including the vote; health care, schooling, and library service are delivered by the ordinary public agencies rather than by a separate prison bureaucracy; custody is graded so that people move from closed units to open units to transitional housing as they approach release. But the same system caps ordinary prison sentences at twenty-one years while allowing courts to extend a term in renewable five-year increments when a person is judged to remain a serious risk; Halden itself is a maximum-security prison housing murderers and rapists behind a perimeter wall, with camera coverage, safety glass, and the option of confining a prisoner to his cell for rule violations. On the American side: the practice I actually design around is that nearly everything inside the fence is organized as a currency. Classification level determines housing, movement, and privileges; rule compliance and program participation are converted into credits that shorten the sentence; infractions are converted into lost credits and, at the extreme, into segregation. Discipline, treatment, and sentence length all run through one instrument.

So: reconstruct the implicit theory of punishment each practice presupposes, and keep separate what the practice strictly presupposes from what merely fits it comfortably. I take normalization to presuppose that the deprivations of prison life over and above loss of liberty are themselves punishments requiring justification — is that right, or could a thoroughgoing consequentialist reach the same design on the grounds that harsh conditions raise reoffending, and if so does normalization strictly presuppose anything about desert at all? I take the American credit system to presuppose that rehabilitation is something the prisoner does — a voluntary, desert-apt performance that can be rewarded — even though the official rationale for rehabilitation is that it is something caused in him; is that a genuine contradiction, or can one hold both? And does Norway's risk-based extension mechanism contradict normalization, or can the two be derived from a single view? I also want the residue handled honestly: my own crude map is that a term of imprisonment can be a deprivation owed (sized by the wrong done, fixed at sentencing), a treatment (sized by how long it takes to work, hence not knowable in advance), or a quarantine (sized by how long the danger lasts), that only the first has any principled way of being denominated in days, and that both countries use the first to set the number and then quietly switch to the second or third to decide what actually happens. Tell me whether that diagnosis is real or is just the retributivism/consequentialism split in different words, and whether the sheer fact that a sentence must be expressed in days is itself something neither theory explains. Finally, what would have to change in the buildings — what would I stop designing, what would I have to start designing — if the justification were purely desert-based in a Norwegian facility, purely consequentialist in a Norwegian facility, or purely therapeutic in an American one? I would rather be told that a practice is philosophically underdetermined than be handed a theory that makes it look tidier than it is.
