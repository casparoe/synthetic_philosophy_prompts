Reading my way into the hinge epistemology literature around Wittgenstein's On Certainty and I keep watching the same two camps fail to make contact. One side wants to give our hinge certainties some genuine epistemic standing — not supported by evidence exactly, but warranted or entitled, still the sort of thing that can be assessed. The other side says they aren't beliefs or knowledge at all: non-propositional, animal, not in the game of being justified, so epistemology has nothing to say about them. Twenty-odd years in, every reply is on record and nobody moves. My working hypothesis is that it's mostly verbal: the first camp uses 'belief' functionally, for whatever plays a role in inference, action and interpretation, and the second uses it for something you could in principle suspend or withhold — so hinges are beliefs on one usage and obviously not on the other. But two things don't fit that story: the same proposition seems to be a framework certainty for one community and a live empirical question for another at the very same time (the age of the earth is the obvious case), and children aquire their hinges by being told things, which sounds like it should be decisive for the epistemic camp. So is my diagnosis right, and if not, what is this disagreement actually — a hidden factual question about what our practices really look like, a clash over what counts as data, bedrock intuitions about normativity that neither side can argue for, or two different questions under one label? And given whatever you diagnose, what would count as progress — a test case, a distinction, a different question — or should I just expect the stalemate to be permanent, and why?
