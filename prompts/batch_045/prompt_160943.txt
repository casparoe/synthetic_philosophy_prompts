i run the facilitation team for a city-run citizens' assembly — 40 randomly picked residents, four weekends, and they hand the council a recommendation the council has said in advance it will adopt. Several times a day someone (a member, an expert witness, or one of my own facilitators) objects that whatever just happened was manipulation rather than persuasion, and i have to rule on it in about two minutes, at the table, with everyone watching, then defend the ruling in the public report. My team has no philosophy training and can't stop to think. So: give me a procedure i can actually run — the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it stops with a flat no, and where it has to hand the decision to a person because the question is really about values. Be honest about which steps are judgment calls in a lab coat.

Two things I keep tripping on that the procedure has to handle instead of dodging: influence that isn't in the message at all (the room's cold, the vote is at 6pm after a long day, the option everyone is bored of gets read out last), and influence that is fully disclosed and still works, because knowing about a framing effect doesn't let you cancel it. My instinct is that disclosure shouldn't be a free pass, but i can't say why.

Then run it on three things from last weekend. (a) An expert who genuinely thinks option C is best put it last on her list because she knows people lean toward whatever they heard most recently. (b) We scheduled the final vote right after lunch and a break partly because we knew a tired room defaults to the status quo — and the status quo is the option the evidence supports. We didn't tell the members that part. (c) The members themselves asked us to make the boring option vivid so they'd stop drifting off it, i.e. they asked to be influenced, knowing what they were asking for. I expect (b) to go badly for us and i want to see how badly. And tell me what the procedure can't do — the cases where it hands me a confident-sounding ruling I shouldn't trust.
