"Imagine a creature called the careenium: a big amoeba-like blob living in a swamp, avoiding predators and looking for food. We can describe what goes on inside it in two ways. At the simm level: the blob contains symbol-like structures ('simms'), including 'simmballs' that collide with one another, where these collisions track facts about food and predators, and where a change in the pattern of collisions is followed by a change in the blob's behaviour of the sort you'd expect given what the pattern means. The simm-level description is the one that lets you predict and control the creature's behaviour across wide variation in how it happens to be built; the substrate-level description is in practice useless for that. And here is the student's answer, about 480 words — I've cleaned up the typos and the line breaks so you're not distracted, but the wording is theirs:

"'Do the symbols push the substrate around?' is ambiguous between three questions, and it's worth separating them. First: is there anything real at the simm level at all, or is it just a façon de parler? I say real. The simm-level description supports counterfactuals that the substrate-level description does not: if the careenium's simm about the predator had been different, it would have moved differently, and that holds however the blob happens to be built. On any view where causation tracks counterfactual-supporting or intervention-supporting generalizations, that makes the simm level causally explanatory rather than merely convenient. Second: does the simm level's causation compete with the substrate's? I think yes, and multiple realizability is what shows it. The same pattern of simmball collisions could be implemented in protoplasm, in silicon, or in a room full of people shuffling index cards. So the causal work the pattern does cannot be identical to the causal work done by any particular substrate — the pattern floats free of the stuff it's made of. That means the pattern has causal power of its own, and it exercises that power on the substrate: when the predator-simm changes, the protoplasm gets shoved around by it. People who say the physics already does all the work have the order backwards; the physics does what it does because the simm level has told it what to do. Nobody denies that something at the bottom is doing the shoving, but the question is what is pushing what, and the answer is that the symbols are pushing the substrate around. Third, the exclusion argument. Kim's version runs: the physical domain is causally closed, so any physical event with a cause has a sufficient physical cause; the simm-level property is not identical to that physical cause; so we seem to have two causes for one effect, which is overdetermination. Kim's own conclusion is that the higher level is epiphenomenal — real and supervenient but causally inert — and Davidson's anomalous monism ends up in the same place, since if there are no strict laws linking the mental and the physical then the mental cannot make a causal difference. I want to reject that. The careenium shows that the symbol level is where the action is, so I deny causal closure: if simmballs can move the blob, then the physical domain is not closed, and that's that."
