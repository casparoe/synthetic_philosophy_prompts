A friend and I spent an evening arguing about the special composition question and arrived at the usual dead end. I hold that there are no composite objects at all — there are only simples arranged table-wise, chair-wise, and so on. She holds that any objects whatsoever compose something, so that there is also an object made of my left shoe and the Moon. We did reach one negative agreement: restricted answers (composition just when the result is a life, or when the parts are in contact, or when they are fastened into a cohesive whole) all seem to demand a sharp cutoff somewhere along a continuum of cases, and nothing in the world appears to mark where it falls. So the live contest, as we now both see it, is nihilism against universalism, and I want the document we failed to produce.

Please draft the joint statement we could both sign: a numbered list of claims both of us accept — about how the question is posed, about what a restricted answer would have to deliver and why both of us think it fails, about what each theory owes by way of paraphrase or reconstruction of ordinary and scientific talk of tables, organisms and galaxies, and about which standard moves we agree do not settle anything (bare appeals to intuition about familiar objects, for instance). Then a table of the residual disagreements, each row stated twice: once in the words I would accept for my own view, once in words she would accept for hers, with neither wording a strawman. Identify the cruxes — the sub-questions where an answer would move at least one of us — and say plainly which part of our dispute you take to be merely verbal, if any, and why.

For each crux, give a case or argument both of us would grant bears on it, and record what each of us predicts it will show. Two we kept circling and never resolved: (i) a toy world containing exactly three simples, first clustered, then scattered, where each of us must say what exists at each time, whether the change of arrangement changes what exists, and whether the extra entities the other side admits or denies do any work in a dynamics stated over that world; and (ii) a stipulated cost audit, in which ontological cost is measured by the entities a completed physics would have to quantify over in its laws rather than by the cardinality of everything posited, and ideological cost by the extra clauses needed in the paraphrase schema for ordinary and scientific singular terms — each of us must say, in advance, what we expect that audit to return and what result would move us.

Finally, have each of us state what would count as evidence against our own view, and say whether anything at all is left that both of us would accept as genuinely settling the matter.
