Weird one, but I've been chewing on it all week and I want it argued properly rather than gestured at. Say you could sit Hobbes down, explain the present situation in terms he'd actually recieve, and ask his advice. The situation: maybe a dozen labs and states racing toward more capable AI systems, with a structural feature people call the competitiveness constraint — whoever slows down plausibly loses the lead, maybe permanently — and a second feature called the alignment tax, the time, compute and raw capability you give up in order to make a very powerful system reliably do what its builders actually want. Nobody can verify what the others are doing behind closed doors. Rational parties, gains from defecting, no power able to punish defection: on its face it looks like his setup exactly.

So what would he tell us to do? I don't want the lazy version where he's the scary absolutist and therefore wants a world government to ban GPUs. I want the machinery, and I want you to flag where it runs out.

A few things I especially want: whether he'd even count this as a state of nature, given that his version needs something like rough equality and here a first mover might get a decisive and permanent edge, and the conflict is a race with one prize rather than a war of all against all. What he'd say to a lab that wanted to pay the alignment tax unilaterally — basically the Foole's question, whether he'd call them suckers or say something more interesting. Whether his actual remedy, covenant plus a sovereign with the power to enforce, would push him toward something like a single enforcer over frontier training runs; and then the awkward part, that on his own view sovereigns stand in a state of nature toward each other, so his fix may just relocate the problem one level up and solve nothing. And the bit I think really breaks him: his sovereign can see and punish, whereas we can't reliably audit a training run or inspect a model's internals, so compliance is partly unobservable — does he have any resources for enforcement under that kind of uncertainty, or is this where the theory goes silent?

Last bit, the speculative one: he already described the commonwealth as an artificial person made by covenant. Suppose someone proposes an AI Leviathan, a powerful system used to monitor and enforce compliance between labs. Would that attract him, and what's the first thing he'd worry about? Then tell me what he'd predict we'd actually do, and which side in today's argument he'd think is more self-deceiving.

Keep it under 500 words, prose, no bullets, and lead with the single claim about his position you're most confident in.
