The shape of it: the Molinists, following Molina and then the moderns like Craig and Flint and Freddoso, say that logically prior to any creative decree God knows what every possible creature would freely do in every circumstance in which that creature could be placed, and uses that in deciding which world to actualize. The grounding objection people, Hasker most prominently and the older Adams-style worry behind him, say there is nothing about the creature that could make those counterfactuals true prior to the decree, so middle knowledge is either empty or quietly collapses into something else. The simple foreknowledge crowd, the Ockhamists, say sure, God knows future free acts, but that knowledge is postvolitional, there is no third logical moment and no need for one. And our Thomist just denies that creaturely freedom is the libertarian thing everybody else is helping themselves to. Meanwhile everyone at the table grants that God's foreknowledge is exhaustive and infallible, almost everyone grants robust libertarian freedom, everyone grants God has genuine providential control over history, and every move has a rehearsed countermove. My own hypothesis, which I want you to attack rather than flatter: this isn't really a dispute about God at all. It's a dispute about what a counterfactual is, with similarity-style Stalnaker-Lewis semantics quietly doing work on one side and some more causal or grounded account doing work on the other, plus a second hidden disagreement about what control over which world gets actualized has to amount to. On that reading people have been answering different questions under the heading of middle knowledge: some are asking how God knows, some are asking what makes the counterfactual true, some are asking whether God can be said to choose among feasible worlds.
