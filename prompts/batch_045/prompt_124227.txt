(1) The flying man affirms his own existence while having no sensory or bodily awareness whatsoever. (2) So self-awareness doesn't require any sensory or bodily content: nothing bodily supplies the content of what he affirms. (3) The method behind the argument is a stripping test — if you can coherently remove X and the target state still remains, then X isn't part of what that state essentially is. (4) Run the test again on everything else. The flying man has no memories, since he was just created; no acquired concepts, since he has never perceived anything; no character traits, no history, no distinctive mental furniture; you can take each of these away and self-awareness apparently survives. (5) So what the test leaves you with is a bare, propertyless, self-aware subject — no body, no past, no distinguishing features of any kind. (6) But a subject with no distinguishing features can't be distinguished from another one: create two flying men simultaneously and nothing in the setup, or in what either is aware of, marks them off from each other; and awareness with no content isn't awareness of anything in particular.
