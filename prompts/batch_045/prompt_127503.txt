so I'm a novelist, midlist, four books in, and over the last year or so I've started using an AI assistant for basically every stage of my process: checking whether "gaslighting" is actually a word anyone would have used in 1888, generating thirty versions of a paragraph so I can pick one and move on, playing beta reader at 3am when my actual beta reader is asleep. The prose is definately better. But here's the thing I can't shake: when I reread a page I wrote last month I often can't tell which sentences are mine and which ones I accepted from the model, and if you made me write the same scene right now with no model I think I could still do it, just slower — but I'm less sure I could in five years.

A friend who does philosophy told me to relax and gave me a comparison. She says the AI is like that thought experiment from a famous paper, with two people going to the museum: one just remembers the street the museum is on, the other has something like Alzheimer's and has written it in a notebook she always carries. The point, as she tells it, is that the notebook isn't a tool the woman uses, it's part of her mind. And my setup is supposed to pass the same check: it's right there whenever I need it, I reach for it automatically without deciding to, I generally trust what it tells me without rechecking, and plenty of what's in there — my outline, my voice notes, my character bibles — I put there myself. So nothing is being lost; my mind just has a bigger outside than it used to.

A different writer told me the opposite: an AI is a ghostwriter, not a notebook, and a ghostwriter can be brilliant and still leave you with no idea how your own book got made.

Can you audit this for me? I want the correspondence table first: what plays the notebook, what plays the street address, the memory loss, the handwriting, the reaching into the pocket. Then the places it breaks when the target is a language model and not a notebook. I have some guesses — a notebook only contains what I put in it, I can't mis-remember something into existence in it, and reading my own writing back is near enough reliable — but I want the disanalogies that actually bear on my question, which isn't "am I more productive," it's "am I loosing my grip on how my own sentences work." And then: does my friend's conclusion survive, is the ghostwriter version actually the better analogy, or are both of them missing whatever is specific to something that generates text rather than storing it? If there's a third comparison that fits better, give me that too.
