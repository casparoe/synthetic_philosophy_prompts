The part I want your help with is this, and its the bit I can't find discussed anywhere. Take this scenario seriously as a thought experiment: we build a finite physical device that, for every program under N bits, prints the correct answer to whether it halts, and for anything longer it just runs forever or stays silent. We can't verify its answers in general — the only cases we can check are ones where we already had a proof by other means, and on those it's never once been wrong, over a few million trials. Nothing infinite happens in a single step; it's a finite box obeying ordinary-looking physics. And yet it seems to be telling us things no algorithm can. Except — someone in my seminar said the whole thing is a non-starter, because any function on a finite domain is trivially computable by a lookup table, so the box computes a recursive function and there's nothing to see. I don't think that settles it, but I can't say exactly why. (If it helps, there's also a variant where N creeps upward a little every year and nobody can predict where it stops.)

So, given that setup — and please tell me if you think the setup itself is incoherent or if the lookup-table objection does kill it — how would you rank the questions worth writing on?
