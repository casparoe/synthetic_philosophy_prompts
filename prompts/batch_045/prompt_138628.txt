Some context. I'm a software engineer by trade — I build distributed data systems — and I have no formal philosophy background beyond an undergraduate logic course and a lot of undirected reading, so please define any technical term you use. I'm applying for a nine-month, part-time independent writing fellowship (roughly twelve hours a week, deliverable: a long-form essay of about eight thousand words plus a short methods appendix), and I want to spend it on one question: whether an 'I' can persist, or even be distributed, across brains. The thing that set me off was re-reading Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach and its 2007 follow-up on strange loops with a friend from work; Hofstadter treats a self as a self-referential pattern rather than a substance, and in the personal passages of those books he writes about his wife's death in a way that suggests something of a person really does go on living inside the heads of the people who knew them. My friend, who is a hard-nosed reductionist, said this was obviously just a comforting way of speaking about other people's memories and habits, and we went around in circles. My half-formed idea is that both of us were asking the wrong kind of question. In my day job I'd never ask "is this data still present on the cluster?" as a yes/no question — I'd ask how many replicas there are, at what consistency level, with what tolerated staleness, and whether any of them can still be written to. So I want to ask: for a given choice of resolution (how fine-grained the description of the person-pattern is) and a given error budget (how much distortion we tolerate before we say it isn't the same pattern any more), how many instantiations of a person exist, where, and for how long — and whether the posthumous ones are qualitatively different from the ones that exist while the person is alive, because the live ones are part of a two-way causal channel (I can correct and update your model of me, and your reactions feed back into my own self-model) whereas the posthumous ones are, in version-control terms, a fork with no possibility of a merge: an abandoned branch that keeps drifting under someone else's ongoing experience. I also want the synchronous version of the question, not just the posthumous one: if a large enough chunk of one person's pattern is realized in several living brains at once — a couple after fifty years, a tightly-coupled small team — is that one 'I' spread over several heads or several similar people, and does the unity of consciousness even enter into it?
