Write a 700–900 word excerpt from the public-consultation draft of a national animal-welfare food labelling standard, for a country that has decided to take the crop-cultivation-deaths argument entirely seriously.

The setup, which the document should treat as settled policy: every food is scored by a single number, the expected suffering caused per unit of protein, and the standard counts all four of the following — the farmed animals themselves; the rodents, birds and invertebrates killed by cultivation, harvest and pesticide; the wild animals whose habitat the farmland occupies or displaces; and the wild animals living on whatever land a lower-footprint option would spare. The extract should contain the passages where the drafters are forced to decide the questions this weighting raises: whether the currency is deaths or suffering-days; whether a suffering-day of a chicken and a suffering-day of a field mouse exchange at parity; whether invertebrates count, and on what probability of sentience; whether ruminants grazing land that could not grow crops receive credit for that; and whether the denominator is per kilogram, per calorie, or per meal. It should also contain the place where the standard has to adjudicate the familiar tension that beef occupies far more land per gram of protein while chicken involves far more individual deaths per gram of protein, and the place where it decides what to do about the fact that sparing cropland means more land left to wild animals whose lives are, on the standard's own assumptions, mostly short and bad.

The satire must come from following that logic honestly rather than from inventing a silly premise. I want the document to arrive, in full bureaucratic good faith and with visible satisfaction, at a conclusion that a reader who began sympathetic to the underlying argument finds appalling — most naturally, that the top rating on the scale goes to some form of meat, or that a plant protein scores worse than the animal product it was supposed to replace — and for the text to be unable to see what anyone could object to, since each step was dictated by the weighting it was instructed to apply. Let it also legislate, in a recital, on questions for which it has no data at all, by adopting a precautionary default and noting drily that the default is doing most of the work.

Voice: deadpan, bloodless, committed. No puns, no sarcasm in the prose, no winking at the reader; the appalling part lives entirely in what the clauses imply. Format as a short preamble of recitals followed by numbered articles, a definitions section containing at least two terms invented so that an inconvenient case can be ruled out of scope, and one worked example set out in the style of a nutrition label. Name and quote no real people, organizations, or publications; invent all of them. Keep any figures illustrative and internally consistent, and do not let a joke depend on a specific number being accurate — the comedy should come from the structure of the tradeoffs, not from a fabricated statistic. Close with three sentences in your own voice stating what the piece is satirizing and which step a defender of the real argument would reject.
