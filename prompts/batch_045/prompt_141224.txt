I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums—LessWrong, Overcoming Bias, and the long comment threads on the functional decision theory paper—for well over a decade, and I now teach an advanced undergraduate seminar, cross-listed between philosophy and computer science, on rational choice, decision theory, and agency. I am writing the take-home final and I need one essay question, together with a model answer and a marking scheme, on counterfactual mugging and XOR blackmail. The question must be unanswerable by merely reciting the definitions of causal decision theory, evidential decision theory, and functional decision theory; it should force students to apply them to a single decision function in a variant I will call the Two-Branch Exchanger Case.

In this variant, Omega is a perfect predictor and administers a two-branch case. Omega flips a fair coin. If it lands heads, Omega asks you to pay $1,000; if you pay, Omega gives you $1,000,000, and if you refuse, nothing happens. If it lands tails, Omega does not ask you for money; instead, a perfect-predictor blackmailer sends you an XOR blackmail letter. The letter was sent if and only if exactly one of the following is true: (i) you have a rare disease; (ii) you would pay the blackmailer $1,000 upon receiving the letter. If you do have the disease and you do not pay, the blackmailer reveals that you have the disease; if you pay, they do not. If you do not have the disease, the blackmailer has no secret to reveal, so refusing to pay costs you nothing and paying costs you $1,000. Having the disease revealed would cost you $1,000,000; the prior probability that you have the disease is 50%; and you know all of this. You are told the result of the coin flip: in the heads branch you face Omega's request, and in the tails branch you receive the blackmail letter. You have only one decision procedure: a single fixed mathematical function that maps your total evidence to an action, and the predictors' predictions of what you would have done in the branch that did not occur are perfectly correlated with the output of this same function on that branch's input. You cannot adopt one disconnected policy for the heads branch and a different disconnected policy for the tails branch.

Please write the student-facing exam question itself, self-contained and suitable for a 90-minute essay, then give a model answer of about 700 words, and then give a marking scheme built around five must-hit points, the marks each is worth (total 20), and a one-line note on how candidates typically get each wrong. The question should require students to say what a CDT agent, an EDT agent, and an FDT agent do in each branch of the Two-Branch Exchanger Case, to explain why the intuitively attractive package of paying Omega in the heads branch but refusing to pay the blackmailer in the tails branch is not available to a single decision function, or is available only at catastrophic cost, and to say what the case shows about the difference between evaluating a physical act and evaluating the output of a decision function. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not use any verbatim quotations from published works; paraphrase any letter or request in your own words. Please write the exam question, the model answer, and the marking scheme in American English.
