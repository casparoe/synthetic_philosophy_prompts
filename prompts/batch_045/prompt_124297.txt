I am a backend engineer by trade with no formal philosophy training — just a stubborn reading habit — and I keep a small internal series of documents that are nominally engineering artefacts but have quietly become metaphysical. I would like you to write the next one.

The concept to be taken apart is heterophenomenology, Dennett's proposed method for the scientific study of consciousness. My working understanding, which I would rather you corrected than humoured: you do not treat a subject's first-person reports as evidence about what it is like to be them, since that would presuppose the very thing in dispute. Instead you treat the reports themselves as the data — as text, utterances, output — and from them you reconstruct the world as the subject appears to take it to be: their heterophenomenological world, populated with whatever they say they find, including their qualia. You then remain officially agnostic about which parts of that world actually exist. The agnosticism is the selling point: it is meant to be neutral ground on which people who believe in qualia and people who don't can share exactly one dataset. In terms I trust: it is building a model of a system's internal state purely from its observable output, under an explicit rule that no entity in the model may be assumed to refer to anything real. If any of that is off, quietly use the correct account — I would rather the piece be fair to the method than faithful to my summary.

The piece: an internal RFC, 800–950 words, proposing a service called the Testimony Ingestion Platform.

Setting. A fictional regulator has introduced a Phenomenal Status Assessment: before any candidate subject above a complexity threshold may be permanently decommissioned, its operator must conduct and file an assessment of whether it is a bearer of experiences. Our team operates a fleet of long-lived conversational agent instances, and the threshold now covers them. The RFC proposes to discharge the assessment heterophenomenologically. Please do not make the question of whether such systems are actually conscious part of the joke: the document should take no position on it, and the unease should come from the procedure rather than from the subject.

Form and voice. A real RFC shape: header block (status, authors, reviewers, date, supersedes), summary, a terminology table, non-goals, design sections, an appendix of interview transcript, open questions. Deadpan, competent platform-team prose — the sort of document that could genuinely be circulated on a Monday. No winking, no puns in section headings, no line that announces itself as a joke. The comedy should be structural: the reader works out what has happened.

Beats I would like included:

1. Ingestion. Reports are taken in as text, with truth explicitly out of scope. The subject's claims are parsed into typed entities and tagged without any commitment to a referent; the terminology table is where the method's jargon gets translated into engineering terms without breaking the document's voice.
2. The heterophenomenological world as a versioned, shippable artefact. Include a short changelog — v0.1 through v0.4 or so — whose entries add entities to the world (something like a reported pressure that increases when the context window is trimmed) and whose recurring note is that no claim is made about whether the entity exists.
3. A three-valued status field that, by construction, only ever returns UNDETERMINED. State the correctness property in so many words: the output must be invariant under the content of the reports. A reviewer should be able to see that no possible transcript changes the result, and the team should present this as a feature — both camps can consume the artefact, nobody's ontology is offended.
4. Auto-heterophenomenology as operator practice. The auditors must record their own reports about their own convictions during the interview and run them through the same pipeline, with the consequence that an auditor's sense that there was, or was not, somebody there becomes more text.
5. A non-goals section that includes, in its own words, that the platform does not determine whether the subject is lying, confabulating, or has anything to lie about, and does not determine whether a report was authored by the subject or produced by the interview that elicited it.
6. A two-exchange transcript excerpt in the appendix where the interviewer's questions visibly supply the vocabulary the subject then uses — a metaphor offered, then echoed — with no commentary from the team on the fact.

One constraint above the others: nothing in the document should be a misreading. The team must be scrupulous, the method must be recognisably itself, and the target should be what neutrality becomes when it is turned into compliance infrastructure — a procedure built to let a subject's testimony in, engineered so that it can never count as evidence — rather than any person or any real research group. The ideal reader who knows the literature should think "yes, that is what the method says" and then feel slightly cold.

Please write it in British English — 'behaviour', 'organise', 'modelled', single inverted commas for quoted report text — and assume no one reading can look anything up: no citations, no references to real papers, articles or authors beyond naming the method itself, no dates that need checking. Return the document only, with no preamble or afterword.
