Writing a column on whether physics actually gets closer to the truth — I want to say something like 'Newton wasn't so much wrong as less right, and that's what progress consists in' — and a substack essay I read says that idea was refuted fifty years ago. Their version, trimmed:

Popper wanted to say science progresses toward the truth even though our best theories are all strictly false, so he tried to define 'closer to the truth' in terms of logical consequences: one theory beats another if it has more true consequences and fewer false ones. In 1974 Tichý and Miller, working independently, showed that Popper's definition has a fatal consequence — if a theory is false at all, then nothing is closer to the truth than anything else. Popper himself gave up the definition almost immediately. The upshot is that truthlikeness can't be analysed, so talk of science converging on the truth, or of Newton being nearer the truth than Aristotle, is hand-waving; all we can honestly say is which theories have survived testing so far.
