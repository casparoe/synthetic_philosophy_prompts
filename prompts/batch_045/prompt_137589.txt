Act as a philosopher of mind who specializes in consciousness and who has also followed the long online arguments about these views. Before writing, spend a little while brainstorming the branch points; I would rather have a tree that actually discriminates than a long one, and I would rather you think carefully than reproduce the standard survey structure.

I run a small reading group, and I want a decision tree that takes someone from a single shared starting commitment — that there is a real difference between conscious and unconscious mental states, and that whatever accounts for the difference is a natural, empirically tractable affair — and leads them, by way of cases, to a named position within the higher-order family of theories, or to a deliberate exit from that family altogether.

Constraints:

1. Every branch node is a yes-or-no question about a concrete case, not a definition or a piece of jargon. Among the cases I want used: a creature with no concepts for mental states (a newborn, a non-human animal) in pain; a subject whose first-order visual state is intact but whose introspective-reporting channel has been knocked out; a subject whose introspective channel fires and reports a state to which no matching first-order state answers; and a system whose only relevant output is a first-person pain report, with nothing else in it remotely like pain.
2. One branch must be the temporal question: must the state doing the work be simultaneous with the state it is about, or can a later one make an earlier state have been conscious? I have never seen this used as a branch point in a map of the debate, and I want it placed wherever it genuinely does work.
3. Lay out the logical space first — the questions and which combinations of answers they permit — and only then place the existing views in the cells. For each empty cell, say in one clause whether the combination is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored.
4. Leaves should cover at least: actualist thought-based versions, dispositionalist versions, perception-based or inner-sense versions, global-workspace or global-state versions, same-order views on which a state is conscious in virtue of its own intrinsic structure, and a first-order exit. Where two options cannot be separated by any case you can construct, flag that rather than inventing a question that pretends to separate them.
5. Label leaves with the standard type-names you are confident about; where you could not confidently name a defender, write unnamed rather than guessing. Please do not attach citations or a reading list — I want the structure, not the bibliography.

Then run your own tree on the following respondent, so I can see whether it really discriminates: yes to the conceptless creature, no to the knocked-out channel, yes to the phantom report, no to the bare reporting system, yes to the later-state case. If that pattern lands in an empty cell, say so and name the single additional question that would be needed to place it.

Separate what is settled in the dialectic from what is genuinely contested among people who work on it: do not tell me that any version has been refuted or that any version is the mainstream one, and mark explicitly where you are extrapolating rather than reporting something somebody holds.

End with a single machine-readable line of the form \answer{...} giving the label of the leaf your tree assigns that respondent to, or the single token no-leaf if it cannot place them.
