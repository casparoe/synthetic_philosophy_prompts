Please construct a self-contained argument for the position set out below. I want the case made from first principles: no appeal to who has defended what, no citations, no reading suggestions, no summarizing of the literature for its own sake. Assume a reader who already knows representation theorems, book and money-pump constructions, and the accuracy program, and make every step earn its place by reasoning or by example. Write roughly 900 words of continuous prose, and end by marking explicitly where you think the argument is strongest and where you are conceding rather than claiming victory.

The position: coherence arguments for expected-utility maximization establish at most that an agent who violates certain structural constraints is exposed to a sure loss, and they establish even that only relative to a settlement currency the agent is assumed to want more of, in every state and independently of what else she has. Consequently they do not establish expected-utility maximization. They show something weaker than maximizing; the requirement they yield is conditional on a substantive preference and therefore instrumental rather than constitutive of rationality; and the two main strategies for closing the gap both fail, the accuracy-first strategy because it helps itself to a decision-theoretic norm of exactly the kind it was meant to ground, the interpretationist strategy because it converts a norm into a conceptual claim and thereby loses the normative upshot.

Use the following stipulations. Call an agent package-immune if there is no single take-it-or-leave-it bundle of bets, denominated in the settlement currency, that she would accept and that leaves her worse off in every state. Call her sequence-immune if no finite sequence of individually acceptable trades, offered adaptively by an adversary who knows her dispositions, guarantees such a loss. Call her reverse-exposed if there is some bundle of bets she would decline, or some sequence of trades she would refuse, that would nonetheless have guaranteed her a net gain.

Then do this work:

1. Describe an agent who is both package-immune and sequence-immune, is not an expected-utility maximizer, and admits no expected-utility representation at all — an agent with gappy or merely partial comparability of options, or one following a satisficing rule, for instance. Do not simply stipulate her immunity; show why the usual constructions have no purchase on her.

2. Show precisely how the constructions depend on the currency being state-independent and separable in value, and on the agent being willing to transact at arbitrary stake sizes. Explain why the obvious repair, namely denominating payoffs in the agent's own utility units, is either unavailable, because those units are not something a bookie can hand over, or circular, because it presupposes the representation the argument was supposed to establish.

3. Argue for the asymmetry between avoiding a sure loss and securing a sure gain: no coherence argument delivers the second, and the second is half of what maximizing requires. Say why the presence of reverse-exposure is not, by itself, an incoherence, and what would have to be added for it to become one.

4. Address two objections in their strongest forms. First, the epistemic reconstruction: credences are governed by accuracy considerations, and a scoring-rule argument plus a bridge principle linking accuracy to choice is supposed to recover the decision-theoretic norm without any appeal to the agent's preferences. Explain why this is at best partial — why it delivers constraints on credence rather than on action, and why the bridge principle reintroduces the very norm at issue. Second, the constitutive reading: incoherence is not a form of irrationality but a limit on intelligibility, so that interpretation itself supplies expected-utility structure. Grant what is right here and then explain what is lost: if charity does the work, the claim is a prediction about interpretable creatures, not a requirement, and the *why be coherent?* challenge returns untouched.

5. Finally, consider the limiting case of an agent who knows she faces exactly one decision and will never face another. The diachronic machinery of books, pumps, and long-run scores is idle for her. Say how much of the argument survives for such an agent, and whether what survives is still recognizably a coherence argument.
