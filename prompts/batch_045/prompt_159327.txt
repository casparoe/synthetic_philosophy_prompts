They've had no formal logic, so I can't put *all non-black things are non-ravens* on the board and expect the equivalence to land. I want to do Hempel's raven paradox with them.

Don't explain the paradox to me, I know it. Plan how I explain it to them, thirty minutes, whiteboard, no handout.

What I need from you:

- The order the pieces have to go in, and why: what has to be in place before the paradox can even bite (confirmation vs. proof? evidence coming in degrees? the equivalence move? the intuition that finding a black raven is the *paradigm* piece of evidence?), and which orderings collapse. My instinct is that if I open with the shoe they'll switch of before there's anything to be puzzled about — is that right, and if so what goes first instead?

- The one example that carries the whole thing. - One closing question whose answer tells me they got the real point rather than "its a joke" or "it confirms a tiny bit".

- What I should openly refuse to explain at this level, and say I'm refusing.
