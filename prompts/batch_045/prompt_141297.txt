ive been chewing on Republic VII for a while and i think ive landed on a reading of the cave that i really like, but it turns two bits of the text into pure decoration and i cant tell whether im just wrong about the metaphysics.

Here's the reading. The shadows the prisoners watch are cast by the little figures the carriers hold up behind the wall. Those figures are made in imitation of ordinary objects. Those objects are what they are by participating in the Forms. So you get a chain of ontological dependence: every layer owes its existence and its character to the layer above it. And here's the bit i think does the real work — call it the derivative-truth rule: if A genuinely depends on B, then a belief about A isnt false, its just thinner than a belief about B. When i look at a photo of my dog, nothing in my head is mistaken; im just tracking the dog at one remove. So the prisoners arent hallucinating or getting things wrong, theyre tracking reality at several removes. I take the bed example in Book X to be the same point made explicitly: the carpenter's bed depends on the real one, the painter's depends on the carpenter's, and all three are in the business of being about the same thing.

Where i get stuck is that on this reading the cave is a story about attention — about where you park your mind on a chain that was in place the whole time — and Book VII is built like a story about something much worse than that. Socrates has the prisoner let go and then physically hauled up a rough steep path against his will, his eyes hurt so much he cant look at anything at first, and he has to be eased in via shadows and reflections before he can look at the things themselves. He also makes that joke about people who think education is putting knowledge into a soul the way youd put sight into blind eyes, and then says the turning-around has to happen to the whole soul, not just to some part that takes in information. None of that is necessary if the prisoners were only ever shallow rather than wrong. You would just tell them to look up.

And the return trip looks like the same problem. The freed guy goes back down, cant see in the dark, gets laughed at, and would apparently get killed if he tried to release anyone. On my reading that reads like an afterthought, or like Plato being grumpy about Athenian politics.

So: is my dependence chain just not how Plato's images actually work, or is the drama carrying a load im not giving it credit for? If the prisoners really are wrong rather than shallow, what exactly are they wrong about — theyre not, presumably, failing to refer to anything?
