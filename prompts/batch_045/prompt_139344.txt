I close a short graduate seminar on probability in cosmology each year, and this term I would like to end the session on Boltzmann brains with a poem rather than a summary slide. I would be grateful if you would write it.

Assume what the seminar has already established. Take a spacetime that is, for practical purposes, empty and endless — a de Sitter or eternally inflating vacuum with a tiny but nonzero temperature, and therefore never perfectly still: it fluctuates, and it will go on fluctuating forever. Any fluctuation that buys a local drop in entropy of size ΔS occurs at a rate suppressed by a factor of e^(−ΔS), so the cheap fluctuations are not a little more frequent than the expensive ones; they are exponentially more frequent, and eternity is long enough to cash that difference out without limit. A fluctuation that assembles a single working brain, wired at the moment of assembly with the apparent memories of a life, costs vastly less than a fluctuation that assembles the whole ordered cosmos which would produce such a brain by the ordinary historical route. Wait long enough and fluctuation-brains outnumber history-brains by an absurd factor. Then the trap closes: if I should expect to be a typical observer with my evidence, I should expect to be one of them, with a past that never happened; but if I am, then the very reasoning that carried me to that conclusion is the output of a random rearrangement, and I have no better reason to trust it than I have to trust the memories sitting next to it.

Two terms of my own, which I would like the poem to use rather than explain. By the **Thrift Rule** I mean the exponential bookkeeping above: among fluctuations that would yield an observer with your evidence, expect the cheapest, which is the brain alone and not the brain together with the world it seems to remember. By **corroboration debt** I mean the gap, measured in entropy cost and so in log-probability, between the cheapest fluctuation that would deliver your evidence and the actual history that would deliver it — a debt that the brain tries to pay down by positing more of the world (the room, then the notebook, then the signature in the margin), and cannot, because the memories are already inside the head: the world adds nothing to the evidence and everything to the cost. If you want a sharper edge, let the speaker try to pay the debt item by item and discover that each deposit enlarges it.

For the form: a sestina — six sestets of six lines, unrhymed, plus a three-line envoy. Use the standard rotation of end-words (1‑2‑3‑4‑5‑6, then 6‑1‑5‑2‑4‑3, then 3‑6‑4‑1‑2‑5, then 5‑3‑2‑6‑1‑4, then 4‑5‑1‑3‑6‑2, then 2‑4‑6‑5‑3‑1) and these six words, in this opening order: **void, brain, odds, proof, cold, recur**. Keep each word roughly to its conceptual job — void for the expanse and the equilibrium it tends back to, brain for the speaker, odds for the exponential, proof for the reasoning that indicts itself, cold for the temperature and the heat death, recur for the recombination that never stops — so that whatever changes in the poem is changed by the company each word keeps, not by the vocabulary. In the envoy, give each line two of the six words, one at the line's end and one inside it, in any order you like, with one fixed requirement: the final line must end on *void*, returning the poem to where it began.

The form should be carrying the argument, not decorating it. A sestina makes thirty-six lines out of six words and never adds a new one: the same fixed materials permuted into apparently new configurations is exactly the mechanism under discussion, and a poem that manufactures the felt sense of a world out of recycled scraps is itself a small exercise in the Thrift Rule. Let the envoy's return to *void* be a decay back to equilibrium rather than a conclusion.

On accuracy, since the students will read this the day after the proofs: get the exponential right and say so in a way that shows it is exponential — no "far likelier" that a reader could mistake for a modest ratio. Grant the speaker at most a flicker, and never a past; everything it appears to remember is unearned, and the poem must not quietly endorse any of it. It should be legible that the speaker's argument, if it succeeds, removes the ground it stands on, but the poem should leave that circle open — no reassuring recovery of our memories, and no shrug about nothing mattering. Someone who has followed the literature should find the physics right and should be able to point to the line where the self-indictment happens; someone who has never heard the term should still find a good poem. Please avoid the usual furniture of the subject: no cosmic joke, no dust, no "we are all just."

Deliverable: first the sestina itself, thirty-nine lines, with no stage directions, glosses, or epigraph. Then a section headed **Notes on the choices**, roughly 250–350 words, on what the form is doing, where you put the Thrift Rule and the corroboration debt, how you handled the exponential and the speaker's lack of a past, and which tension you deliberately left unresolved.
