The question I set:

Two players. Player 1 chooses a row (T, M, B), Player 2 chooses a column (L, N, R). Payoffs are written (Player 1, Player 2).

```
          L        N        R
T      (6, 7)   (5, 2)   (0, 4)
M      (5, 0)   (3, 8)   (8, 3)
B      (4, 0)   (4, 1)   (3, 10)
```

(a) Is any pure strategy strictly dominated by another pure strategy? Is any pure strategy strictly dominated by a mixed strategy? If so, exhibit the mixture.
(b) Carry out iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies as far as it goes, allowing domination by mixed strategies at every round. What survives?
(c) What does (b) tell you about the Nash equilibria of the game?
(d) Does the order in which you delete strategies matter? Would anything change if you were also allowed to delete weakly dominated strategies?

Here is what one of the writers handed in:

(a) No pure strategy is strictly dominated by a pure strategy. For Player 1: T loses to B at R (0 < 3), and M loses to B at N (3 < 4), so neither T nor M dominates B; conversely B loses to T at L and N and to M at L and R. For Player 2: L beats R at T (7 > 4) but loses at M (0 < 3) and B (0 < 10); N beats R at M (8 > 3) but loses at T (2 < 4) and B (1 < 10); and L beats N at T while N beats L at M and at B. So nothing is purely dominated anywhere.
But B is strictly dominated once we allow mixed strategies: (3/5)T + (2/5)M pays (5.6, 4.2, 3.2) against L, N, R, against B's (4, 4, 3). In fact any weight p on T from 1/2 to 5/8 inclusive works.

(b) Delete B. Now R is strictly dominated by (1/2)L + (1/2)N: against T that pays 4.5 > 4, against M it pays 4 > 3. R could not be eliminated in the first round, because against B it returns 10, more than either L or N gives there. Delete R. With columns L and N left, T strictly dominates M ((6, 5) against (5, 3)). Delete M. Now L strictly dominates N, since against T it pays 7 > 2. Delete N. Only (T, L) survives, paying (6, 7).

(c) Nash equilibria always survive the elimination of strictly dominated strategies, so every equilibrium is still there at the end. Since a single profile survives, (T, L) is the unique Nash equilibrium of the game — in pure strategies, even though mixed strategies were what let us get there.

(d) The order doesn't matter. Iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies always ends at the same surviving set whatever order you delete in, because deleting a strategy only removes constraints and can never make an already-dominated strategy undominated again. And the same holds if we switch to weakly dominated strategies: the order still doesn't affect the final set, and it can't cost us any equilibria either, since a weakly dominated strategy is never played with positive probability in a Nash equilibrium.
