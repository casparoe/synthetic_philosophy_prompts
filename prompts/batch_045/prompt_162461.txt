I write a column on science and ideas and I've stupidly agreed to run a 45-minute hands-on slot at a literature-and-science festival in the autumn: about 25 adults, mixed ages and backgrounds, no maths assumed, a room with a few tables and chairs and definately no projector. I want to do something on actual infinities rather than just talk at people about Hilberts hotel, so it has to be something they do with their hands and bodies, not a talk with a deck.

The thing I keep circling round, and tell me if its a dud: the hotel stories are always told as if the rearrangement just happens, and I think the whole weirdness lives in that gap. My half-formed idea is to force the moves to be done one guest at a time, under a rule that you may only move someone into an empty room — so the famous "everyone shifts up one, that frees room 1" manoeuvre can't actually be executed by anybody, in a finite hotel or an infinite one, because the very first move has nowhere to go. The story only works if you let everyone move at once, which isn't a process anyone can perform or watch. Then, once they've hit that wall, a second constraint: distance walked counts, so housing infinitely many new arrivals by doubling everyone's room number needs an infinite amount of walking, and no finite budget ever gets you there. I'm hoping it lands as: the hotel's spare capacity is a fact about a finished arrangement, not about anything that can happen — which I take to be roughly the finitist worry, felt in the room before it's named.

Can you turn that into something runnable? I need: how the room is set up and what to bring; rules I can read out in about two minutes with no notation; the rounds with timings and, for each, the instruction I give and the breakdown I'm hoping for (they should hit the wall themselves before anyone names anything — no "bijection", "cardinality", "potential versus actual infinity" until the debrief); how the manager side and the arrivals side are staffed and scored so it stays a game and doesn't become a seminar; and the debrief that maps what actually happened onto the ideas. If there's time I'd also like a five-minute kicker where a different kind of arrival turns up — one that no scheme can house, not because the rooms run out, which I think is the Cantor/diagonal point — but only if it can be done without turning into a proof on a whiteboard. No reading list, I'll do that homework myself.

Also: what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (I'm guessing at least one person says "infinity isn't a real hotel so none of this means anything", and someone else tries to solve it by building an extension), how I handle each, and be straight about which bit of the idea the game can't deliver and I'll have to say out loud myself. British English please, and give me one sentence at the end I could actually steal for the piece.
