I'm in the final year of a philosophy of science degree, and we spent two seminars on the raven paradox last week. The Bayesian treatment we were handed appeared to reduce the puzzle to a point about magnitudes, and I want a number for how much of the puzzle that reduction actually retires. The claim I want the number on concerns a finite version of the case that I worked through afterwards, so let me set it out first.

Consider a sealed depot of 12,000 crates, each holding exactly one object. A manifest I have no reason to doubt states that exactly 300 crates hold ravens and exactly 9,000 hold objects that are not black; it says nothing about how those two groups overlap. Let H be the hypothesis that no crate holds a non-black raven, let k be the number that do, and let m be my prior expectation of k on the supposition that H is false.

Three observations, each of a single crate drawn uniformly at random from the region named:

From the depot as a whole, a green tennis ball: likelihood ratio for H against its denial of 9,000/(9,000 − m).

From the aviary, a wing of 1,200 crates that the manifest certifies to contain all 300 raven crates together with 900 crates holding other birds, and of which exactly 400 crates are non-black, a white dove: likelihood ratio of 400/(400 − m).

A black raven, from either region: likelihood ratio of 300/(300 − m), the same either way.

At m = 150 these come to roughly 1.02, 1.6 and 2.0. If the arithmetic or the modelling is wrong, say so before answering, and tell me what the correct comparison is.

Assuming it is roughly right: my credence is 0.55 that this sensitivity to the sampling frame is the whole of the matter — that once it is made explicit, nothing remains of the puzzle about non-black non-ravens except arithmetic and the empirical question of how raven-poor the frames we actually sample from happen to be. Someone in my supervision group puts the same figure at 0.2, and I cannot locate the divergence. For the claim to be false it should be enough that a competent agent who has fully taken the point on board still has a stable, non-question-begging reason to treat such observations as evidentially irrelevant.

Give me your own figure, decomposed. I would like a probability and one line of justification for each of the following, plus any term you think I have omitted:

Whether the judgement that a generalization is confirmed by objects satisfying both of its predicates has any force of its own, or is wholly explained away by the magnitude story.

Whether the equivalence of the generalization with its contrapositive is doing genuine explanatory work, or whether the same likelihood ratio falls out of a direct count of possible falsifiers, with contraposition incidental.

Whether confirmation is best treated as a two-place relation between evidence and hypothesis or as a three-place one taking the sampling frame as a further argument, and whether that is a substantive revision or only bookkeeping.

How much of the result rides on m, and on my having modelled the prior over k in this way at all.

Whether any of it survives the move from the depot to the hypothesis actually at issue, which concerns all ravens everywhere rather than a closed set of 12,000 crates — whether the finite model is a fair proxy or is smuggling in the assumption that does the work.

Whether the ordering of the three observations is stable across the choice of confirmation measure: ratio, difference, log-odds.

Then show how the terms combine into the headline number; state which single term you are least confident in and what evidence or argument would shift it most; and say where you think the gap between 0.55 and 0.2 most likely lives.
