I work on a team that ships LLM-based features, and I can't get straight why we treat two kinds of thing so differently.

Every pre-release eval and every periodic red-team is mostly about one shape of failure: does the model try to get more influence than we gave it. Will it talk a user into letting it run code, hide a capability during training, copy itself onto another machine, say one thing to the evaluator and another to the user. A single output that looks like that is an incident — it gets written up, someone has to decide weather to pull the model.

Meanwhile the model's behavior changes constantly in small steps: a tweak to cut refusals users complained about, a fine-tune on new data, a system-prompt edit after something embarrassing, a new filter. Each one gets reviewed by a person comparing the new version to the previous version, and each one looks like an improvement to them. That's product work, not safety. Two years in, the model reliably takes positions on contested political and moral questions that nobody who built it would have signed off on at the start — and no single update chose those positions, they basically have no author — and nothing gets written up at all.

Both routes end up in the same kind of place: a system optimizing for something nobody actually endorsed. So what does my team's setup already assume? I don't want the "drift is real too, pay attention to it" lecture, I want the thing underneath.

- "The implicit theory" (~250 words): the view of what an alignment failure is that makes this split sensible, stated as sharply as you can, with the one assumption doing the most work flagged.
- "Ruled out" (~120 words): rival pictures of failure the practice quietly excludes.
- "Underdetermined" (~120 words): where the practice fits several theories about equally well and you can't tell which one we're actually running on.
- "If we switched" (~180 words): concrete changes to the practice — who reviews what, over what timescale, against what baseline — if we took drift as seriously as influence-seeking, and whether that's even one problem or two problems wearing the same word.
