I spent a career as a physical chemist, much of it in a calibration laboratory, and have come to philosophy late — by way of the distinction between fixing the reference of a term and giving its meaning. I would like to test whether I have actually understood that apparatus by building a case with it rather than paraphrasing the stock ones.

Construct for me a single realistic scenario — a standards or calibration laboratory is the natural setting, and I want no Twin Earth or other science fiction — in which one and the same sentence, about one and the same object or quantity, is contingent but knowable a priori at one time and necessary but knowable only a posteriori at a later time, while the referent of every term in it is held fixed across the change. The flip must be generated by the semantics of rigid designation together with a change in how the community fixes the reference of a term — not stipulated — and I take it the case must therefore turn on a rigidified description or an initial baptism rather than on an ordinary definite description used attributively. Keep the scenario to one paragraph.

After it, show the work: state the sentence and its terms, say which are rigid designators and why, give the modal and epistemic verdict at each stage, and isolate the single feature that does the work — plus what happens to the puzzle if that feature is removed (say, if the term is introduced by ostension on a sample, or by an ordinary description). Then tell me what a defender of the description theory would say to defuse the case: my suspicion is that she will claim the sentence changed meaning rather than modal status, and I want to know whether that reply is as cheap as it sounds or whether it costs her something elsewhere. Finally, say what becomes of the argument if the later convention only approximately preserves the original referent, as in the recent redefinition of the kilogram in terms of a fixed constant.

The point of the exercise: Hesperus and Phosphorus, water and H2O, make the necessary a posteriori look like a curiosity of theoretical identification, and I want to know whether it is already sitting inside ordinary measurement practice.
