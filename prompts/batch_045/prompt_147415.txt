I'm in a tiny reading group on food ethics — six of us, meeting in the back room of a pub — and the last couple of sessions went sideways on the crop-cultivation argument. You know the shape of it: harvesting grain kills mice, voles, insects, whatever else is living in the field, cattle are bad converters, so per calorie a beef eater is responsible for more of those deaths than a chicken eater or a plant eater. Most of us took that as settling it and moved on to the next reading. One guy, Ben, keeps insisting the whole argument has been run backwards, and that anyone who actually takes the wild-animal-suffering framing seriously should be eating grass-fed beef, of all things. I told him I'd write up the strongest version of his position before the next meeting, and I'm definately stuck on how to make it more than a gesture, so: please make that argument for me.

Aim it at people who already grant that field-animal deaths and wild-animal suffering matter morally and can be put in the same ledger across species — I dont want to spend words defending that part. Concretely, argue that once you pick the right unit, pasture beats both chicken and plant foods, and that the usual "chickens are more feed-efficient, so chicken over beef" move quietly switches units — feed per calorie instead of animals killed per calorie, or animal-days of suffering per calorie. Ben calls the mechanism here the land-sparing dividend: the land dedicated to your diet is going to hold a population of wild animals either way, and pasture holds fewer of them, and if those lives are on balance bad then the diet that occupies more land per calorie comes out ahead on exactly the thing that was supposed to count against beef. The rough numbers we've been working with, feel free to push back on them but please work with them: a broiler lives around six weeks and yields a couple of kilos of meat, a beef animal lives a year and a half or so and yields a few hundred kilos, grain-finished beef takes something like eight kilos of feed per kilo of gain where chicken takes about two, and pasture needs noticeably more land per kilo of meat than a feedlot does.

Four things I want handled rather than waved at. First: pick a metric and defend it — deaths, suffering-days, or something like animal-years per hectare — since the ranking of the diets flips depending on which one you use, and say plainly what you're counting and why that's the right thing to count. Second, the marginal case: Ben is one person buying one steak, so what actually moves — herd size, land use, anything — and if the honest answer is "barely", say what follows for how he ought to decide anyway. Third, the bit that made us all uneasy: if displacing wild animals from land is the whole mechanism, doesn't the argument generalize to converting wild habitat into pasture pretty much wherever possible, and is that a reductio or something Ben should just bite the bullet on. Fourth, uncertainty — we really don't know whether wild lives are net good or net bad, so tell me what the practical upshot is if you put something like a coin flip on the sign. It's going into a write-up I'm circulating to the group, so the more structured the better.
