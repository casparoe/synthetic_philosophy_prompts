I am trying to write down, for my own benefit, exactly where I stand on the trolley cases and the doctrine of double effect, and I would like an audit of whether the following set hangs together. Here is the set:

(1) Case verdicts. Diverting a runaway trolley onto a siding where it will kill one and save five is permissible. Pushing a heavy stranger off a footbridge into the trolley's path, so that his body stops it, is not. And in a variant I have been calling the mixed-motive switch — the agent throws the switch both in order to save the five and because he wants the man on the siding dead, and would have thrown it anyway for the first reason alone — I also want to say the act is impermissible.

(2) The intended/foreseen distinction is doing genuine work here. It is not a rationalisation bolted onto an intuition; what it tracks is something about what the agent is aiming at in acting.

(3) The distinction between doing and allowing harm, and between killing and letting die, carries no moral weight of its own. Where it seems to, something else is doing the work.

(4) There is no separate means principle. Using someone's death, or their body, as a means is objectionable, when it is, only because and when it involves aiming at their harm; the clause excluding bad effects that are means is redundant.

(5) The test I have been relying on for whether a foreseen harm is intended is counterfactual: if the harm had not been going to occur, would the agent still have had reason to act as she did? If yes, it is merely foreseen; if no, it is intended.

What I want is an audit rather than an exposition. Rank the tensions in this set by severity, and for each pair tell me whether the conflict is real or only apparent, which of my verdicts my principles cannot actually deliver, and what the cheapest repair would be together with what it would cost me. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Please do not spend the answer on citations, reading lists, or scholarly apparatus — I am after the argument, not the literature. Please write in British English.
