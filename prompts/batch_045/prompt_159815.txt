I run a small reading group at work, mostly backend engineers, nobody with a philosophy degree, and we just did a session on Roko's basilisk — the thing where a future superintelligent AI tortures simulated copies of people who knew about it and could have helped build it but didn't. Half the room waved it off as "just Pascal's mugging" and the other half said "you can't be blackmailed by something that already happend", and I could tell neither side had actually located where the argument bites.

So: write me one essay question for a 45-minute in-class write, ~700 words, 20 marks, upper-level-undergrad-ish difficulty, that forces them onto a real crux instead of letting them recite a verdict. My sense is the crux is why the obvious objection — once you've already made your choice the AI gains nothing by torturing anyone, so it has no reason to follow through, so ignore it — isn't the conversation-ender people take it for, and whether the reply "a sufficiently smart agent precommits / your decision and its decision are the same computation, so this is closer to a coordination problem than to blackmail" actually rescues it, and what has to be true about the AI for that reply to go through (its decision theory, its utility function, whether simulated people matter to it at all, how well it can predict you). Please also fold in the self-undermining part: the threat only works on people who have heard the argument, but the AI is supposed to be the good guys' AI, and I want students to say what gives.

Then give me a model answer of 600-700 words and a marking scheme out of 20: about five must-hit moves, marks for each, and a one-liner on how people usually botch each one. Make the A/B line really explicit — a couple of the guys read a lot of LessWrong and can say "acausal trade" and "functional decision theory" without being able to use them, and I want to tell that apart from actually getting it. Dont let the question depend on anyone remembering who posted what when or on citing anything; there's no web access in the room and I couldn't check a citation anyway.
