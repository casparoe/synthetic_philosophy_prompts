I draw typefaces as a hobby, badly but often, and there's a thing I keep running into that I don't have a word for.

Say I've got a lowercase alphabet rougly finished and I hate the *g*. Half the time the fix isn't in the *g* at all: I redraw the *a* and the *e*, and without touching it the *g* suddenly looks right. It runs the other way too — a glyph can match every measurable thing I can list about its neighbours (stem weight, contrast, terminal angle, width) and still look like it wandered in out of another font. And then there's a separate-feeling thing: draw the same *a* shape on its own, on a blank page, and it stops reading as an *a*, even though nothing about the outline changed. I can push an *a* a long way towards weirdness as long as it stays clear of the *o* and the *e*, but I have no idea how much clearance is enough, and it isn't a distance I could measure off the drawing.

So: is there an established notion for whatever makes a letterform both belong to a style and read as the letter it is — anything people have seriously tried to cash out, not just gesture at? A friend said Wittgensteinian family resemblance, but as I understand it that mostly says there's no rule to be had, and that's not my question: it feels like there *is* something the letters of a good font share, and that it lives in the relations between them rather than inside each one. Someone else said *ductus*, but that's about how the pen moves, which isn't it either.

I don't want a label. I want to know whether this is one phenomenon or two (belonging and reading-as feel different to me, but maybe that's my confusion), what exactly it is, what it isn't, and where the edges are — e.g. is a letter clearly by the same designer but from a different project in the style? If nothing established fits, coin a term and define it tightly enough that I could settle a borderline case with it. Please commit: tell me what you think the thing actually is and why, rather than lining up candidate notions and leaving me to pick.
