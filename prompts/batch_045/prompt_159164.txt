i'm TAing a first year seminar on personal identity and next week we do Dennett's Where Am I? — the story where his brain gets taken out and parked in a life support vat in Houston while his body carries on in Berkeley hooked up by radio. I want to put Avatar next to it for a 80 minute session, because the setup is almost a remake: Jake's brain and human body are lying in a link unit back at the base while the avatar body is out in the forest doing everything he does, and getting hurt. But I cant tell whether the film is actually arguing for one of the answers to where am I, or just giving us an emotionally loaded way of not answering it.

Here's the frame I've been messing around with, which I want you to stress-test rather than just apply. As I see it there are four candidate answers:

- anatomical: you are where your brain is
- phenomenal or embodied: you are where the body you see out of and act through is
- interest-relative: you are where your practical stakes are, the place where what happens can happen to you
- second-personal, which is the bit I'm making up: you are where the people who know you are, where you're seen and answerable. The film seems to push this one — Jake's human life back at the base is basically anonymous, while his avatar life gets embedded in a clan, and that see-you greeting does a lot of work.

So: read the film through the puzzle. Which of these does it actually commit to, and which scenes do the work? I especially want you to take on the ending, where Jake is moved for good into the avatar body — is that a transfer or a duplication, and does the film earn the difference or hand-wave it with a miracle? And does my second-personal option collapse into the interest-relative one, or is it a genuine fourth answer? Take a position; I don't want an it depends on which theory you prefer reply.

Sections, roughly:

1. The disagreement — about 200 words. Sharpen the four answers so a first-year can see why each is tempting and exactly where they come apart.
2. The reading — about 700 words. What the film says about where Jake is, scene by scene, and which answer it vindicates.
3. Where the film resists — about 300 words. The places the reading is forced, and the places where the film is being sentimental or metaphysically sloppy instead of making a point.
4. Verdict on the fourth answer — about 250 words. Real rival or re-description? Defend it, and say what would change your mind.
5. What to screen — about 200 words. Two or three scenes, the question to put to the room after each, and what a genuinely good student objection would look like.

No citations or reading list, that part I've got covered, and don't bother quoting the film.
