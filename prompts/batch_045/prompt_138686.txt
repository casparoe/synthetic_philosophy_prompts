The gender-equality paradox — the pattern that countries scoring highest on standard gender-equality indices tend to show the most gender-skewed uptake of fields like engineering and computer science — is often read as evidence that freer societies let stronger innate differences in occupational interest show through. An essay I read recently made the point with a plant analogy: career choice is like a sapling bent by a stunting wind; put it in a greenhouse, where the wind is gone and nothing blocks it, and each plant grows into the shape it was always going to take. The conclusion drawn was that what we are seeing in the high-equality countries is nature expressing itself rather than discrimination, and so there is nothing here for a theory of justice to repair.

Audit that analogy, and fold two things of my own into the audit. First, a distinction: the indices bundle what I will call *unblocking* (legal bars removed, hiring discrimination reduced, parental leave guaranteed, education open) with what I will call *cushioning* (a safety net and a compressed wage structure that let a household get by on one-and-a-half earners instead of two). Second, a comparison: society A has maximal unblocking but a threadbare safety net, so a career break means real poverty; society B has maximal unblocking plus generous childcare, long paid leave, and low wage dispersion, so specialization within a household is comfortably affordable. Suppose the gap is large in B and small in A.

Give me an explicit correspondence table — what plays the role of the wind, the greenhouse, the gardener, the shape, the soil — then separate the disanalogies that matter for the conclusion the analogy is carrying from the ones that do not. Say which of A and B is really the greenhouse, whether "the gap is in B" is evidence for innate preference or for cushioning, and whether the natural-is-not-unjust step survives the audit. If a different analogy does the work better, name it. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble and no restating of my question.
