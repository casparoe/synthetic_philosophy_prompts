I convene a graduate seminar in philosophy of language and mind, and for several years running the unit on rule-following has ended exactly where it began. The skeptical argument is by now familiar to everyone in the room: nothing in my finite history of applying "plus" — no set of examples, no table I have memorized, no dispositional or representational state I am in, no instruction I once received — singles out one function as the one I meant, since a rival hypothesis that agrees with me everywhere I have actually been tested and diverges thereafter (yielding, say, 5 rather than 125 for a sum whose addends exceed any I have encountered) accommodates the evidence equally well; so there is, apparently, no fact of the matter about what I meant, and no fact about which answer would be correct. The two families of reply that structure the secondary literature — the skeptical solution, which concedes the non-existence of the semantic or intentional facts the skeptic doubts and reconstrues meaning ascriptions as claims about what it is licensed for a speaker and her community to assert and to correct, and the straight solutions, which propose meaning-constituting facts, either reductively, in causal, dispositional or information-theoretic terms, or non-reductively, as brute intentional facts — then proceed to accuse each other of evasion: one of having abandoned the very thing in dispute, the other of having illegitimately demanded that a fact be constructible out of materials that are not already semantic or intentional.

My working hypothesis is that this standoff is not primarily a first-order dispute about meaning at all, but a dispute about what a theory of meaning is required to accomplish — whether an account must explain or ground meaning facts in non-semantic, non-intentional terms, or whether it is entitled to help itself to intentional facts as primitive — with the consideration about normativity functioning as the hinge: read one way it is a constraint any adequate theory must satisfy, read another way it is a misleading formulation of the platitude that meaning constrains correct use.

I have two cases I use as pressure tests, and I want to know what each family of reply is committed to saying about them and what that reveals. The first: a whole speech community has, for as long as anyone can tell, applied its addition vocabulary so that it coincides with addition below a certain very large threshold and then returns a fixed value, the divergence originating in a shared artifact everyone relies on for checking. Practice within the community is fully self-consistent, correction reproduces the pattern, no one dissents, and there is no external vantage point from which they are corrected. (The companion case: a single person who invents a rule for herself that no one else has ever used, and who years later finds herself inclined to extend it in a way she would once have rejected.) The second: an artificial system whose entire grasp of a rule is an explicitly stored finite program, verified against every input within the designers' test bound but not beyond — where the skeptic's move can now be re-run one level down, on the interpretation of the symbols constituting the program itself, generating the familiar regress of interpreters.

Diagnose the impasse. Is it verbal, a clash of bedrock intuitions neither side can argue for, a hidden question about grounding and reduction, a disagreement about which cases are allowed to count as data, or an instance of something older and unsolved — the problem of induction, or scepticism about other minds and about the past — wearing a semantic costume? Is my hypothesis right, partly right, or a way of avoiding the substantive question? Give me reasons, not a survey, and no literature: I cannot follow references and do not want a bibliography. Finally, and honestly: on your own diagnosis, what would count as progress here — a distinction, a formal result, a redescription of the question, a decision to stop asking it — and is there any reason to think the debate will ever actually close?
