I've been arguing with my brother about when "better safe than sorry" is actually a reason to do something, and I've noticed I don't apply it the same way twice. Here's where I land, going by gut:

- The town found traces of an industrial solvent in the water. One weak study in rats given way higher doses hinted at kidney damage, nothing either way in humans. I think switching to the far-off reservoir was right even though it cost a fortune.
- Keeping a math tutor app out of my kid's classroom, because nobody's proven it's harmless and it might mess with their attention spans, while the upside is just slightly better test scores: fine with me.
- Refusing the normal childhood vaccines over a possible long-term effect nobody has shown: not fine.

Those last two have, as far as I can tell, the same shape — unverified possible harm to kids from something new — and I go opposite ways on them, and I can't say why.

- My brother won't fly and drives 400 miles instead, because a plane crash feels worse. I think that's silly, but I can't say why without just saying "the numbers" — isn't the whole point of this kind of thinking that you *don't* have numbers?
- Gene-drive mosquitoes that could wipe out malaria where my aunt lives, with unknown effects on everything that eats mosquitoes: I want more study before releasing them, even though I know people are dying of malaria right now. Next to the water one I feel like a hypocrite.
- Keeping my kid home from a sleepover because of a vague bad feeling about the parents and no actual evidence: not fine, that's just me being anxious.

The one I go back and forth on: our village sits under a glacial lake. Engineers say it might burst and it might not. We can spend everything now digging a drainage channel, or wait three years for the survey that would actually tell us how unstable it is. Digging means we never find out, and the next valley with the same problem learns nothing from us. Waiting means we might not be here. I lean toward digging and I feel bad about both halves of that.

Is there one rule that gets all of this, or am I stuck with two that clash? If you've got one, I also want to know what it says about a version of the village where the channel itself has a small chance of setting the collapse off, and about a case you make up yourself.

Please lay it out like this: "The rule" (about 200 words) — stated plainly, no jargon, with the bit of mine that motivates each part of it; "Where I'm wrong" (about 100 words) — which of my calls it flips; "Cases I didn't give you" (about 150 words) — the risky-channel version of the village, my flying brother if the rule has anything to say about him, and one of your own; "The runner-up" (about 100 words) — the other rule you'd keep in play and what swallowing it would cost me.
