Bit of an odd one. I'm on the scientific advisory panel of a research foundation, and our charter has one clause we keep having to apply: we don't fund unconditional prophecies about the course of history. We've now got a track record of rulings and I want to know what we'd actually be committing ourselves to with the proposal in front of us.

What we've done so far:

- funded: a conditional claim that centralising tax collection raises famine risk within a decade, used to compare policies
- funded: a 40-year projection of pension costs under three named policy scenarios
- funded: a universal generalisation about the past with no forecast stuck on it (no state has survived a sustained halving of its revenue without reform or collapse) — minute says generalisations about the past are fine, prophecies aren't
- funded with conditions: a 30-year forecast of malaria burden conditional on current coverage; we made them state what would falsify it within five years
- rejected: dating the next transition in the global order from a theory of civilisational life-cycles
- rejected: reading the spirit of the age off artistic output to say which institutions will decay
- rejected: a widely publicised forecast of the next financial crisis, partly because publishing it could help bring it about

The new one: a consortium wants to revive a law of bureaucratic growth — as communication latency falls, the share of a society's output administered centrally rises, in every case where we have data — and use it to forecast, out to 2110, that central administration keeps growing. Their pitch is that it's conditional, testable on past data, and stated as a generalisation; they've also 'sealed off' the knowledge variable by holding future communication technology fixed, and they've offered to publish only behind a paywall.

So: what should we decide if we want to be consistent with ourselves, and which of our own precedents is the closest analogue? More usefully, tell me the two or three rules that fit our track record equally well, where they come apart on this case, and — if they genuinely underdetermine it — what principle we'd be adopting with each choice and what the next proposal that would settle it would look like. Please also say how confident you are in what you're claiming and single out the bit you're least sure of. British English please.
