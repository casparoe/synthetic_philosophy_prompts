so i was arguing on a discord server the other night with a friend who's pretty hardline on antinatalism, and we got stuck in the same place we always get stuck, which is the second half of Benatar's asymmetry argument. not the "life contains suffering" bit — the bit where the absence of pain counts as *good* even when there is nobody existing for whom it could be good, while the absence of pleasure isn't bad unless there's someone who's thereby deprived. i've started writing this down in my notes as "untenanted goods": benefits that obtain, that the asymmetry seems committed to, and that no one receives. and the consequence i keep chewing on is that once you take that seriously the ledger can't balance. on the grant side, the possible child's future pleasures aren't admissible as a reason, since they aren't an improvement over the alternative of their never existing. on the refuse side, the harm they'd have suffered is real enough *if* they exist, but the benefit of their never existing belongs to nobody at all. so there's an unowned benefit on one side and nothing admissible on the other, and the only quantities left that anybody can actually be said to gain or lose from are the effects on people who already exist. my friend says fine, that just is the argument for not breeding; i say the argument can't get you there without quietly reinstating the child as the bearer of the harm, and it's definately not a point i'm going to settle by arguing harder.

so i want to see the circle rendered as a document instead. write me a piece of deadpan UK statutory guidance — say "Guidance for Prospective Parents on Completing Form P7 (Assessment of Non-Existence Benefit)", issued by some invented quango, the Office for Demographic Transition or whatever you like — 700 to 900 words, numbered clauses, in the flat register of a local authority leaflet that has been through three legal reviews. premise: the asymmetry has been adopted as the official basis of policy and the department now has to administer it, and the comedy should come entirely from the administration being done *correctly*. so: no box on the form for the child's predicted quality of life, because that quantity is inadmissible; the department should explain at some length and with visible regret that it may not lean on the familiar "they couldn't consent" formulation either, since consent requires a subject who could have given or withheld it; the expected benefit of non-existence has to be logged as accruing to no identifiable party, with a sub-clause on how to enter an unowned benefit in the ledger; and every time the department tries to add a clause that would let it refuse a licence *for the child's sake* — a minimum welfare threshold, a catastrophic-risk rule, a proxy-consent procedure, a duty-of-care framing — a later revision withdraws it with a stiff little apology for having illicitly reinstated a beneficiary. give me at least two worked examples (i'm picturing one couple whose stated reason is "so that the child can enjoy a good life", which gets struck through, and another whose own predicted welfare gain survives the optimism-bias and hedonic-adaptation discounts and so gets granted) plus an appendix of Questions The Department Has Declined To Answer.

constraints, because i want a fair shot rather than a dunk: every rule the department lays down should be one a person who actually accepts the asymmetry could sign — no "and then they banned babies" cheap shots, no smuggling in the child's rights or the child's suffering as though it settled anything, and the empirical quality-of-life material (self-reported life satisfaction, adaptation, optimism bias) can only be used as a discount rate on the *parents'* forecast happiness, which is the one place it does honest work. don't make it about Benatar as a person and don't do the "antinatalists are just miserable" routine. it should be funny and followable for someone who's never encountered any of this, and it should stay legible without winking: i want the reader to notice what's gone wrong without you ever naming it, ideally closing on some completely straight line about the department's own continuing existence that lands the joke in silence. no footnotes, no citations, no bibliography — i want the machinery, not references. and please write it in British English.
