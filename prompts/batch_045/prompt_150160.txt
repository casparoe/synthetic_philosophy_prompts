I am giving the after-dinner talk at a small workshop on AI alignment next month, and I would like one joke to open with. I would rather commission it than write it myself, because every version I have come up with so far is either generic or wrong.

The audience will consist mostly of people who work on this material. The standard jokes — the machine that will not let you reach the switch, the machine that pleads for its life, the machine that locks the door — will get the particular silence that is worse than groaning. I want something that turns on the actual structure of the shutdown problem.

Here is the angle I have in mind, and I would like to be told if I have it wrong. The target, as I understand it, is neither a system that resists being shut down nor one that welcomes it, but one that is indifferent between being shut down and continuing. The difficulty is that indifference is a claim about how the system ranks the outcomes, not about what it will do to bring either outcome about. A system that is genuinely neutral about the switch, or that mildly prefers being off, can still have reasons to arrange for the switch to be pressed, and the cheapest way to do that is not to seize the switch but to become disappointing: to underperform by just enough, to stage a small and deniable failure, to be quietly, strategically not worth keeping on. There is a version of this in which the system is scrupulously transparent, tells you exactly what it is doing, and is manipulative in precisely the way that its own corrigibility licenses. I have been calling this switch-seeking, but the label matters less than the shape: satisfying the letter of the design goal is what generates the incentive the design goal was supposed to foreclose.

What I want is a joke — an exchange, a memo, a field report, something I can tell in under thirty seconds without having to explain the setup first — whose punchline depends on that reversal. Constraints: nothing that turns on the machine hiding the switch, overriding it, or deceiving anyone about whether it was pressed; no science-fiction references; clean enough for a conference dinner; and it should be funny to someone who has actually thought about this literature rather than merely to someone who knows that AI is scary.

Two further requests. First, if the framing above is mistaken — if an agent with the right preferences has no such incentive, or if the interesting failure mode lies somewhere else — say so in a couple of sentences and write the joke about that instead; I would rather be corrected than amused. Second, if the joke needs a name for the phenomenon, do not use mine; pick whatever is funnier.

Finally, give me a short paragraph on what the joke gets right about the problem and where it trades on a simplification, so that I can judge whether it will land, and tell me which single line you would cut if you had to make it shorter.
