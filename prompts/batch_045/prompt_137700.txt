I am assembling a verdict table for a handbook chapter on disposition ascriptions, and what I need from you is an extrapolation rather than a summary. One contributor (call her R.) has ruled on the following five cases, each time on the question whether the object in question is fragile at the moment in question.

1. An ordinary soda-lime wine glass sitting in a closed cupboard, never struck in its life: yes.
2. A glass of the same make watched over by a device that, at the instant of any impact, anneals the glass so that it deforms rather than shatters: yes. Here the stimulus removes the very disposition at issue.
3. A glass of the same make that lives permanently encased in foam, so that every impact it ever receives is absorbed before it reaches the glass: yes.
4. A goblet of a rubbery, non-brittle polymer, paired with a device that would render it brittle at the instant of any impact, so that goblets of this kind do in fact shatter when struck: no.
5. A glass of the same make, struck hard, whose incipient cracks are healed as they form by a crack-arresting field generated in the room by the impact itself, so that it never shatters: yes.

Two background commitments are not in dispute: she explicitly rejects the simple counterfactual analysis, and she holds that fragility admits of degrees.

The undecided case is this. A goblet is made of a tough glass-ceramic which, struck in the ordinary way, rings and dents rather than shatters. Through its body the maker has laser-inscribed a lattice of hairline channels carrying stored elastic stress, tuned so that a sharp blow of the sort that would shatter an ordinary goblet releases the stored stress all at once and the goblet flies apart into shards: the sound, the shards, and any test you like run on either are indistinguishable from an ordinary goblet's shattering. Three qualifications. The release occurs only within a narrow band of impact energies: below it the channels never reach threshold, above it they are pulverised before the stress can run. The same total energy delivered as a slow squeeze drains the channels harmlessly. And the stress relaxes over roughly a decade, so a goblet of this kind struck equally hard at twenty years of age merely dents. The goblet now under discussion is three years old, is struck sharply and once with an impact inside the narrow band, and there is nothing else in the picture: no foam, no field, no annealer, no device of any kind.

Does she call this goblet fragile? Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction and no conclusion: (1) your prediction of her verdict on the goblet, with your reason; (2) a first rule that fits all five earlier verdicts, and what it predicts here; (3) a second such rule that fits all five earlier verdicts but comes apart from the first on this case, and what it predicts here; (4) which of the two you take her to be running, and what that commits her to elsewhere in the table; (5) the one further variant of this goblet, described in a sentence, whose verdict would best separate the rules your prediction leaves tied.
