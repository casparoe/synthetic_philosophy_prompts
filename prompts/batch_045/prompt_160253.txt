I'm in a final-year seminar on cultural evolution, and I've built an argument I no longer trust, because it seems to take down the whole Darwinian framework we've been using — and, by its own lights, everything else as well. I'd be grateful for a diagnosis of where it goes wrong.

The argument:

(1) A selectionist explanation of a cultural variant's frequency explains that frequency by the variant's own differential transmission success, and not by what carrying the variant does for the humans who host it. That contrast is just what distinguishes a Darwinian explanation of culture from a rational-agent one.

(2) Grant, as my seminar did after we built the model: in the actual causal structure of cultural transmission, host-neutral transmission biases — conformity to the majority, prestige of the source, emotional salience — do most of the work, while host-aligned biases, such as testing a claim against the world or tracking whether acting on it pays off, do very little. Our simulation had 200 agents, twelve rounds, copying from a neighbour weighted by those three host-neutral biases with a 5% mutation rate and no term for a variant's truth value or payoff; the population's mean truth value drifted to chance within eight rounds and stayed there. I'll stipulate that the simulation is a fair toy model.

(3) So the content of any widespread belief is explained by its fit to those host-neutral biases, and not by its truth or by its usefulness to the people who hold it.

(4) The Darwinian theory of culture is itself a cultural variant. So (3) applies to it: its spread among people like us is explained by its fit to host-neutral biases, not by its truth.

(5) If the truth of a belief plays no role in explaining why it is held, then holding it is not epistemically supported by its truth. The reasons people give are post-hoc dressings on a filtering process that never consulted the truth.

(6) Nothing in (3)–(5) is specific to that one theory, so the reasoning generalises: no one holds any belief for the reasons they think they do, and no belief is supported by its truth.

(7) But plenty of our beliefs plainly are supported — elementary mathematics, for one. And in any case a framework whose acceptance would remove everyone's reasons for accepting anything is not a framework anyone should accept. So the Darwinian theory of culture is one that nobody, including its defenders, should believe: their defence of it is just another variant being filtered by the same host-neutral biases.

My own guess is that the trouble is in the middle of the chain, somewhere between (3) and (5), but I can't say what it is, and I don't want a diagnosis that merely rubber-stamps my guess. I'd most like to keep (1) and (2) — the selectionist framing and the transmission data are the parts of the course I found most convincing — and I'm reluctant to give up (5), because the move from a truth-free causal story to a loss of justification looks to me like the standard debunking template that gets used everywhere else.

So: name the step or premise you would reject, and explain why it looked fine — what made it seem like a restatement of something earlier rather than a new commitment. Then price the alternatives: for each of the other candidates, say what you would have to give up in order to reject it instead, and whether that cost is one the framework can actually bear (in particular, does rejecting (5) cost me debunking arguments generally, or only this use of them?). Tell me too whether the self-referential step, (4) together with (7), is doing any real work or is a red herring: does an argument of this shape undermine itself in a way that matters, or does the fact that it takes down everything else make it easier to dismiss than it looks?

Two further things. Please say what would change your mind about which step is the culprit — what further premise, repair, or consideration would move you to a different diagnosis — and flag where your own reasoning is most likely to fail. And lead with your bottom line in the first sentence: which step you reject, stated up front, followed by the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
