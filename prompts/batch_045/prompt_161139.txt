Hey — I'm putting together a one-page handout for my third-year seminar on suffering-focused ethics, and what I want is a taxonomy, not a reading list. Syllabus is locked, so please don't cite anything or suggest further reading; just give me the structure.

The family of views I want the tree to organize, stated in my own terms so you don't have to guess at labels: call it the T-view — there is some level of suffering such that no amount of pleasure, however vast or long, could outweigh, compensate for, or cancel it. Keep it distinct from the weaker claim that suffering simply gets a much heavier but finite weight, and from the claim that pleasures and pains aren't on a single scale at all.

What I'm after: a tree in which every branch is a question that splits two or more of these views, phrased as a concrete case a student could answer without knowing any of the names. Ten branch questions max, 10–14 leaves, each leaf a named position or "unnamed". Build it from the logical space first — the questions and the answer combinations they permit — then locate the actual positions in that space, and flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really separate rather than forcing them apart. No branch should just ask "is pain worse than pleasure?"

Cases I want used: a two-second pinprick versus ten thousand years of mild contentment; a billion people each with a five-second itch versus one person's minute of joy; a being whose mildest itch is a thousand times worse than our worst agony; someone who knowingly chooses agony to spare others a mild tedium; an hour of agony with the memory then erased versus an hour of tedium that is remembered; creating a barely net-positive life versus preventing an already-occuring severe pain; and a one-in-a-billion chance of vast suffering versus a certainty of modest pleasure. Somewhere in the tree I also want the question of whether the threshold attaches to pain as a sensation or to suffering as something aversive, and whether it holds within a single life, across persons, or both.

Note the empty cells: for each combination of answers nobody occupies, one sentence on whether it's incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Keep the whole handout, tree and notes, under 900 words — terse branch labels, nested bullets or ASCII, no prose paragraphs.

Then a short note at the end, five sentences max, seperating which of your branch questions are genuinely contested among people who work on this from which are just the standard way of carving the space. Don't present a contested framing as the received one, and if you're inventing a distinction to make the tree come out, say it's yours. American English, please.
