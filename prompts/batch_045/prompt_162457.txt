I'm trying to put an actual number on Pascal's mugging instead of gesturing at "it depends on your decision theory." The case: a stranger with no credentials tells you that unless you hand him $5, he will use powers outside known physics to inflict a year of suffering on each of 3↑↑↑3 distinct minds — an astronomically large number. There is no evidence beyond his say-so, and no way you could ever verify either outcome. The proposition I want a credence for: in this case, the correct decision procedure recommends paying. Mine is about 0.1. Give me yours, decomposed.

The terms I think it has to factor into:

(a) That the correct procedure is expected-value-like at all under this much uncertainty about both the probabilities and the scale of the payoffs, rather than something like a stochastic-dominance or leximin-style constraint.

(b) That the prior has no structural floor against the mugger's adversarial naming of the number. I would have thought a proper prior forces the sum over N of p(N)·N to converge, hence p(N)·N → 0. But the mugger names N after the fact, against your prior, so the relevant quantity is the supremum of p(N)·N over the N he could name, and convergence of the sum does not obviously keep that supremum below the $5 threshold. Is there an argument that it does, or is this where the whole question is decided?

(c) The mirror-mugger question: for every hypothesis on which paying prevents N units of suffering, is there a structurally symmetric one on which paying causes N — and if so, does the argument cancel itself out rather than merely get diluted by a factor of two?

(d) Verifiability: does the case change in kind if the mugger first gives a small, checkable demonstration — he reliably produces 10 units of suffering in a box in front of you and says the same mechanism scales up? If it does change in kind, is that evidence that the original case was never really about the arithmetic of huge N?

Give a number and one line of justification for each term, then say how they combine into your headline credence. Finally, tell me which single term that number is most sensitive to — the one where shifting it by 0.2 would move your credence most — and if you think one of my terms is a red herring, say so rather than filling it in.
