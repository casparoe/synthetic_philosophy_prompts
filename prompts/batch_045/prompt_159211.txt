I'll call it the *continuation view* so it's my label, not a name they've heard: what makes a state a pleasure is not how it feels but a practical/normative profile it has — while you're in it, going on is intelligible without any further justification, and stopping is what needs a reason. On this view pleasures aren't unified by a felt quality at all; they're unified by being the states in which continuing is the default. And how *intense* a pleasure feels is then a separate dimension tracking something else (salience, arousal, whatever), which is why felt intensity and pleasantness come apart in people's judgements.
