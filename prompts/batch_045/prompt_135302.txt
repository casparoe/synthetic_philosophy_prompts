I've got no academic background in any of this, so pls forgive me if I mangle the vocabulary. I got interested on my own, from a couple of pop-science write-ups, in the idea (Dennett's, I think) that minds come in a kind of tower, with four kinds of creature stacked on each other. Darwinian creatures: everything they can do was built into them by evolution over generations, they don't really learn. Skinnerian: they can learn inside one lifetime, but only by actually doing things with their own bodies and getting rewarded or punished for it — trial and error the slow, expensive way. Popperian: they can try things out in their heads first, so the bad guesses die instead of the guesser. Gregorian: on top of all that, they get handed ready-made thinking tools — words, numbers, maps, diagrams, categories — by the culture around them, and that lets them have thoughts no single brain could have invented on its own.

The pitch is that it's cumulative: each tier can do everything the tiers below it can, plus something new. Here's the thing I can't stop chewing on, and I want you to argue for it rather than tell me whether it's actually right.

My hunch is that the top tier doesn't fit the pattern at all. A Skinnerian creature pays for every bit of learning with its own skin and its own time. A Popperian creature pays much less — it runs the guesses internally — but it still pays: it's still the thing generating the guesses, and still the thing that suffers when they're wrong. A Gregorian creature, though, mostly doesn't pay for anything. It recieves the results of a search that other creatures, over generations, already ran and already died for. So the fourth tier isn't "Popperian plus tools" — it's a different relationship to who does the guessing and who absorbs the cost of the guessing. Call it laundered foresight if that helps: the foresight is real and the results are real, but the work that produced them happened somewhere else, and somebody else got killed by the bad branches.

So: please argue that the Gregorian tier isn't a further kind of mind in the tower's sense at all, and that once you see what's really going on up there, the tower turns into something else — less a ranking of kinds of minds, more a ranking of who runs the search and who eats its cost. I'd want the argument to include:

1. Why the same style of objection doesn't take out the Popperian tier. If the move from Skinnerian to Popperian is just "same thing, faster", then your argument is too big and it eats itself. Tell me what makes that step a genuinely new kind of mind rather than an efficiency upgrade.
2. The case I keep getting stuck on: something that has the whole inherited toolkit — language, concepts, math, diagrams, the lot — where nobody has shown that it does any hypothesis-testing of its own. Which tier does it belong in on your view, and why is that a better answer than "obviously it's Gregorian"?
3. A consequence for us. If the top tier works the way you say, is the Gregorian creature the individual human being, or is it only the lineage or the culture — with the individual being a fairly modest Skinnerian-with-some-Popperian animal that happens to live inside a Gregorian system? Pick whichever version makes the argument stronger and defend that one.

And give me the strongest thing someone who likes the tower as it's usually presented would throw back at you. No reading lists or citations please, I just want the argument as tight as you can make it.
