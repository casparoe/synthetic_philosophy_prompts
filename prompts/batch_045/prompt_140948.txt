Case 3: I write a 500-claim book. I'm 0.99 confident in each claim, and the errors are independent. Verdict: I believe claim 1, claim 2, ..., claim 500 individually. I do NOT believe the conjunction "all 500 claims are true" (credence about 0.0066). I do believe its negation, "at least one claim is false" (credence about 0.9934). So: I believe every individual claim, but I also believe the book has an error.

Case 4 (the one I keep going back and forth on): The book has 50 chapters of 10 claims each. For any chapter, the claim "chapter n has no errors" has credence 0.99^10 ≈ 0.904. Verdict: I believe chapter 1 has no errors, I believe chapter 2 has no errors, ..., I believe chapter 50 has no errors — all 50 of them. But I do NOT believe the book as a whole has no errors (the conjunction of all 50 chapter claims); instead I believe there's an error somewhere. This feels inconsistent: if I really believe each chapter is fine, why can't I just put them together and believe the whole thing is fine? I feel a lot of pull to say yes — that's what "believing each" seems to commit me to. But my gut also says no, the whole-book claim is under 1% likely. So either I give up the idea that beliefs combine (closure), or I give up believing each chapter, or I give up believing there's an error. I want all three if I can get them, but I suspect I can't.

But the claim "the first 100 claims (ten chapters) contain no error" has credence 400/500 = 0.8. Verdict: I do NOT believe that the first 100 claims are all true, even though I believe each of the ten chapters in that block is error-free.
