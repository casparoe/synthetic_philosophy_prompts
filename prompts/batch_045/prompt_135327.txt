had a huge argument with my friend last night -- she says memetics died in the 90s and i should drop it, i say what died was the version where a meme is a little code copied brain-to-brain and nobody serious ever defended only that. so now i've agreed to give a 20-minute talk to the cultural evolution reading group here and i want to walk in ready for the question period.

My thesis: the unit of cultural inheritance isn't the meme, it's what I'm calling a *scaffold* -- the little rebuilt environment (tools left out in a certain order, an arranged workspace, trained attention, the way a novice's body gets positioned) that makes re-acquiring the trait cheap. Fidelity is a property of the scaffold, not of copying: the copy can be pretty bad as long as the reconstructed niche reliably rebuilds the conditions under which the trait gets reacquired. So selection acts on variants by how cheaply their scaffold rebuilds itself, and high-fidelity transmission is a special case rather than the mechanism. Second half of the argument: this is why lab transmission-chain experiments keep finding degradation -- the design strips the scaffold out and then reports the loss as a fact about culture.

Audience: about half are dual-inheritance / gene-culture coevolution modelers -- they think in transmission parameters, fidelity, biased transmission, population structure, and they will want to see my recursion and my generation time. The other half are anthropologists of apprenticeship and situated learning who think those lab chains are a parody of real transmission and who get allergic to the word replicator. Plus one historian of science who will want to know what makes any of this Darwinian rather than just Spencerian progress-talk with equations.
