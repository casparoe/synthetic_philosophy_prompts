I've been reading about the question people discuss under the heading of moral worth — whether someone who does the right thing does it for the reason that makes it right — and I keep hitting a case type that doesn't match the standard examples. Two instances.

(1) A department chair blocks a hiring decision because she believes the search was procedurally rigged. It was rigged, and that is exactly what makes blocking it the right thing to do. But her belief comes from misreading an email: she thinks the shortlist was cut for an improper reason when it was not, and there is a different, unnoticed defect of the same kind that does taint the process. Had the email read differently, she would have let a rigged decision through.

(2) A tenants' association leader rejects a settlement that would benefit her own building, because she believes accepting would set a precedent that hurts tenants elsewhere. That is true, and it is the best thing to be said for rejecting. But she believes it only because a friend in law school told her so; she cannot reconstruct the argument, and the argument her friend gave was bad. The sound argument is one she has never heard.

In both, the agent acts on the consideration that genuinely makes the action right, and in both the route from that consideration to the action is broken in some way. It is not the familiar case of right action from a self-interested motive, and it is not action that is right by luck; whatever goes wrong here goes wrong inside the agent's own psychology. Nor is it clear to me that the agent's true belief merely needs to be added to the story, since in each case she would have acted the same way had the belief been false.

Does this configuration have a name? More than the label, I want the carving: are (1) and (2) one defect or two, or more than two? How does the case sit with the standard requirement that the agent act because of the right-making feature, and with the views that relax that requirement? Does the breakage remove credit entirely, reduce it, or leave it untouched? And where is the boundary — is there a version where the broken route is harmless, and a neighboring version where the same breakage is clearly worse? If nothing established fits, coin a term, define it, and give me boundary cases. No reading list or citations, please; the distinction itself is what I am after. Please answer in American English.
