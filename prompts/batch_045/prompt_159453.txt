Hey — I'm writing a section of a paper on responsibility gaps for autonomous systems, and I want to argue that the gap doesn't really depend on unpredictability. The standard setup is "no one could have foreseen or controlled what the system did," and I think you can get the same missing-blame result even when the behaviour was completely expected and everybody had the numbers in front of them. So build me a case that does that.

Constraints: one paragraph, realistic and present-day (a hospital, a warehouse, a delivery fleet, an algorithmic scheduler — your pick, but no rogue AI and no sci fi), and:

1. the harmful outcome was foreseen, or at least statistically expected, by the people who rolled the system out, and the system behaved exactly as it normally does;
2. nobody gets off via negligence or wilful ignorance — no skipped checks, no one hid the base rates from anyone else;
3. there's a human in the loop who followed protocol exactly and whose action is a but-for cause of the harm;
4. legal liability lands cleanly on some party, so this isn't a gap in tort or contract, just in blame.

And yet no individual looks blameworthy in the desert-entailing sense.

After the scenario, do the work part. For each plausible blame-candidate — the operator, the developer, the deploying institution, whoever tuned or fine-tuned the model, the regulator — say which of the usual conditions on moral responsibility (causal contribution, foresight, control, fair opportunity to have done otherwise) fails and why, and don't let more than one fail for the same reason. Then run three treatments over the case: absorptive strict/enterprise liability, the "there's a real gap here, so replace blame with compensation and redesign" line, and the moral-crumple-zone move where blame lands on the designated human anyway. Give me a committed verdict on whether the case actually generates a gap or whether one of those three dissolves it, name the single feature of the case that's doing the work, and say what happens if you take that feature out. Finally, tell me how the theorist who comes out worst would attack the scenario — I don't want a case that just stipulates the gap instead of generating it. British English in the answer, and please dont hedge the verdict into mush.
