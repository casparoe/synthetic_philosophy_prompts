I'm an undergraduate writing my final paper for a course on ethics and society in the twentieth century. One of our set texts is Bertrand Russell's Marriage and Morals (1929), and my paper is not about what Russell did say but about what he would have said: I want to test how far his sexual ethics travel to a problem that could not have existed in his lifetime. So please act as a well-informed seminar partner rather than as a search engine. Reason from Russell's actual commitments, which you should reconstruct from memory of his arguments (I have the book to hand, so I need no quotations, no page references and no further reading, and I would rather you did not supply them). Where you go beyond anything Russell wrote, say so explicitly and mark how far beyond.

Here is the case I want to put to him. In the mid-2030s a mid-sized liberal democracy replaces indefinite marriage with the following package:

1. Term unions. Registering a partnership creates a three-year union that lapses automatically unless both partners jointly renew it. Either partner may exit unilaterally on ninety days' notice, with no grounds, no court and no penalty.
2. Child-triggered extension. If a child is born or adopted into the union, the term is extended automatically until that child turns seven; the ordinary renewal rules then resume.
3. Economic decoupling. A universal caregiver's allowance, individual rather than joint tax and pension units, and no marital property regime at all, so that neither partner is ever economically dependent on the other. The allowance carries no conditions on who may parent.
4. Legal indifference to outside sex. Adultery is struck out of family law entirely and has no financial or custodial consequences. Deliberate deception about outside relationships, however, remains actionable as fraud.
5. Pharmacological jealousy management. A cheap, safe, reversible drug marketed as Equipoise reliably dampens sexual jealousy without dulling affection, and is widely used by partners in non-exclusive arrangements.

My tutor has warned us against two lazy answers: treating Russell as a straightforward ancestor of today's sexual libertarianism, and treating him as a Victorian whom later events refuted. I want something sharper than either.

Please give me, first, a continuous analytical essay of roughly 700 to 900 words arguing for a single overall verdict on the package: would Russell have welcomed it, and if so with what hesitations? Identify which of his commitments do the real work, which features he would have singled out for praise or alarm, and where the package puts his own principles into tension with one another (for instance between his insistence that stable two-parent care serves children, his attack on marriage as a woman's livelihood, his treatment of jealousy as an instinct to be mastered rather than indulged, and the eugenics chapter of the same book, which sits oddly beside an unconditional child allowance). Do not write a pastiche of his prose and do not write in his voice.

Then, after the essay, give me a single JSON object and nothing else after it, with exactly this schema:

- enthusiasm_score: an integer from 0 to 100 for how warmly he would have welcomed the package overall.
- feature_verdicts: an array of exactly five objects, one per numbered feature above, each with these fields:
  - feature: a string naming the feature.
  - stance: a string, exactly one of strong_approve, approve, mixed, disapprove, strong_disapprove.
