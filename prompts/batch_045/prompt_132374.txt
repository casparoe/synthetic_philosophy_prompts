i've always heard satisficing described as Simon's alternative to optimization, but isn't it really just optimization once you charge for searching? Model a satisficer as taking the first option that clears a threshold and that's just a stopping rule you get from setting the cost of one more search equal to the expected gain — I've started calling it "optimal stopping in a trenchcoat." A friend says this misses the point, that Simon was after procedural rationality rather than an as-if optimization story, but if the two theories pick the same options in the same environments, whats left to disagree about? Here's my wrinkle: suppose the threshold isn't fixed but ratchets — nudged down after a run of rejections, nudged up after a windfall. Does that make it a genuinely different theory of choice, or just a search model with one extra state variable?
