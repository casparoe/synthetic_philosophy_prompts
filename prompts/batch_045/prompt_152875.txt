im in a social epistemology seminar this term and our last session was on the epistemology of conspiracy theories, and we spent nearly all of it on the self-sealing worry: a conspiracy theory can absorb any evidence you throw at it. you produce the debunking, the debunker turns out to have been paid, you produce the payment records, those were forged, you track down the forger, hes in on it too, and so on without any natural stopping point. our TA gave the bayesian version of it — roughly, a story that spreads its probability more or less evenly over every outcome you could have seen gets no confirmation from the one you actually saw, the likelihood ratio stays near 1, so nothing was ever really supported — and then someone brought up Lakatos's thing about a research programme that keeps bolting on auxilliary hypotheses to survive each new result instead of predicting anything new, i.e. a degenerating one. but the reading also pushed back: institutions genuinely have lied, whistleblowers are sometimes vindicated, and the "no one could keep a secret that big" move is shakier than it sounds. i want to close study group with a poem that holds both of those thoughts at once.

Can you write a villanelle — 19 lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, ABA rhyme, two refrain lines alternating through the tercets and meeting in the last two lines — in which the refrains dont repeat exactly? each time a refrain comes back it should keep its original words but gain a new clause bolted onto it: a fresh auxilliary hypothesis that swallows whatever objection has come up since the last time we heard that line. so the frame of the poem stays perfectly still while the lines themselves get longer, heavier, more hedged, and by the final couplet the accreted explanation visibly weighs more than the claim it was supposed to prop up. the philosophical point should be carried by the structure rather than by a punchline: someone who knows the self-sealing and likelihood-ratio stuff should be able to point at which return is doing which bit of work, and someone who has never heard of any of it should just read a poem about a person who cannot lose an argument.

Please dont make it a sneer at believers — take seriously that the person digging is sometimes the only one whos right, and let that sit next to the accretion without resolving it. no jargon in the lines themselves (no "falsifiable", no "likelihood ratio", no "prior"), no footnotes, no authorial winking at the reader.

Start straight with the poem — no "heres a villanelle" or note on the form in front of it. if you want, two or three lines after it on what you did with the accretions is fine, but the poem comes first.
