Every "agentic" AI product I've used still runs on the assumption that the human is the one acting: I approve the diff before it merges, I confirm before it sends the email, the API key is mine, the terms say the user or deployer eats the consequences, and the thing is sold as a copilot, never as a party to anything. But the same products are built and talked about in ways that look agent-shaped — they form multi-step plans toward ends the user states, revise them when the environment pushes back, use tools, we train them by targeting what they're "trying" to do, and everyone happily says "the model refused" or "it made up a source."

So what does the tool framing actually presuppose? Does it need the claim that agency requires having ends of your own that outlast the task? Or that agency just is answerability — being the kind of thing that can be asked for a reason and can be blamed? Or is "agent" doing moralized, institutional work here, so that calling it a tool is a decision about where the buck stops rather than a claim about what the system can do? Please be strict about which of those the practice strictly requires versus which it merely fits nicely with — I suspect its underdetermined and several theories would license it.

Here's the part I can't get straight. We already have a well-worn institutional category for something that acts on another's reasons, exercises discretion, forms plans, and whose acts bind the principal while not being the responsible party itself: a proxy or attorney-in-fact. Call the AI case "scoped agency" — agent-like within a task, tool-like across tasks, because it has no standing projects of its own. Does that third category dissolve the tool-or-agent question, or does it just push it back one step, into whether its responsiveness to reasons is the real thing or merely looks like it from the outside? And if we did decide these systems were agents in the relevant sense, what would concretely have to change in the product — would the human-in-the-loop confirmation dialog stop being the safe default and start looking like a wrongful way to treat something?
