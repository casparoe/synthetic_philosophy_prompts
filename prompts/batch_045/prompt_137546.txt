ok so our reading group did that dialogue with Einstein's preserved brain — the one where the brain and the philosopher go at each other about whether a book could be conscious — and it went about as well as you'd expect, so I've been told to put my own position in writting so the others can have a go at it. Here it is, six or seven things I'm fairly sure I believe:

1. Substrate independence. What makes something conscious is its causal-functional organisation, not what it's made of. Whatever realises the same organisation as me is conscious in the way I am. No carbon chauvinism, and no "ah but it's only a simulation" move.
2. But it's the occuring organisation that matters, not the merely dispositional one. A book sitting on a shelf, a simulation nobody has ever run, a brain in deep hypothermia: not conscious, even though each has all the right counterfactual structure sitting there waiting.
3. Speed makes no difference in principle. Run the whole thing a million times slower and you get the same experiences, just spread out over longer. There's no minimum clock rate for being conscious.
4. A conscious subject at a time is a process, not an instantaneous state. Nothing has an experience in an instant; experiences take up duration. So a single page-turn of the book isn't a moment of experience all by itself.
5. In the vat case — brain kept alive in a jar, hooked up by radio to a body that walks around and talks — the subject is the whole hooked-up system, not the brain on its own. The brain by itself is cut off and isn't undergoing anything.
6. Whoever's turning the pages of the book isn't a homunculus and isn't the subject either. They're just the causal machinery that keeps the thing going, like the rest of the physics. The book is the subject, if anything is.
7. And I'd really rather not end up saying that whether the book is conscious is vague, or just a matter of which system we feel like pointing at. I want there to be a determinate fact of the matter in each case.

What I want from you is an audit, not a lecture. Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like they are, ranked by how bad the tension actually is rather than all flagged equally, and for each real one say exactly what I'd have to give up and what that costs me elsewhere in the set. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Please don't give me the generic "the book case is just hard" answer — I want to know which of my commitments has to go. And write it in British English.
