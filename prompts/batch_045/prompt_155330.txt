I've somehow let myself get talked into giving the after-dinner speech at Malcolm's retirement do next month. He's the economic historian in my department who has spent basically forty years on the Great Divergence / the Needham question, and the room is going to be half economic historians and sinologists who've read everything he's written and half his family, his grown kids and the vice-chancellor. So it can't be a seminar and it can't be mush.

The problem: my own dissertation chapter argues that "why Britain and not China?" isnt a well-posed question, and I don't know how to write a tribute to a career built on it that isn't either dishonest or a public insult. The version of the argument I'd want to smuggle in goes like this. A contrastive why-question is only well posed relative to a stated contrast class, and this one never states one — the foil could be Song China, Qing China, Gujarat, or the Netherlands, and each of those gives you a different thing to explain. The units don't match either: comparing an island polity of a few million to a continental empire of a couple hundred million isn't a comparison of like with like, and once you make the comparison that actually bites, England against the Yangzi Delta, the "divergence" starts to look like a nineteenth-century accident about coal and colonial resources rather than a deep civilizational difference — at which point it isn't the same question anymore. And it's selected on the outcome: nobody builds a research programme around why Flanders didn't industrialise first, we ask it because Britain won, which makes it less an explanation than a way of pointing at a salient winner and a salient other.

Write me roughly five minutes that honours the work seriously, lands for both halves of the room, and lets me say something true about why the question is fishy without it reading as me calling him an idiot at his own dinner — ideally in a way he'd enjoy being teased by, since he's replying to the toast straight after me. Carry the philosophical point with one concrete example rather than with jargon. Two of his longtime collaborators from Fudan will be at his table, so nothing that lands as "China was the one that failed."
