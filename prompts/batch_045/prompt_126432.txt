I want a case analysis, not a literature survey or a reading list: take one real management episode and run it through one piece of conceptual apparatus, in both directions — what the apparatus says the actors faced and should have done, and what the episode in turn shows about the apparatus.

The apparatus. The background question is whether destroying habitat reduces wild-animal suffering. I find the usual way of putting it unhelpful, because it treats the number of wild lives lived as the variable that matters. I want to work with a decomposition instead. Call the welfare ledger of a region over a period the sum, over every sentient life lived there, of that life's net welfare. Split it into two factors: the number of life-slots the region's energy flow supports, and the average lifetime welfare per slot — where a slot is roughly the share of net primary production required to run one life to its species-typical end. Habitat loss can act on either factor. It can delete slots. Or it can leave the slot count roughly unchanged and simply change who occupies them: in a saturated landscape, the animals that would have lived in the lost patch may be replaced, elsewhere or later, by animals of a different kind using the same energy. Call that second possibility occupancy neutrality, and call the welfare-per-slot of a given occupant its comparative welfare yield.

Two consequences I want you to use. First, under occupancy neutrality the welfare effect of habitat loss is almost entirely a compositional effect: it depends on whether the replacement occupants have a higher or lower comparative welfare yield than the displaced ones, not on how many animals exist. Second, comparative welfare yield tracks life history: the same energy flow can be cashed out as many short lives of a high-fecundity species, most of them ending very early, or as few long lives of a large, slow-breeding species. Adopt the decomposition for the sake of the analysis, but tell me plainly if you think it is misconceived or if one of the two factors does no work.

The case. The Oostvaardersplassen, in the Dutch province of Flevoland — roughly 56 square kilometres on land reclaimed from the sea in 1968, publicly owned, originally zoned for industrial development, then abandoned as unsuitable for cultivation. Greylag geese colonised the wet part; it was designated a Ramsar wetland in 1989. The dry part had been a willow nursery, and managers, worried that dense woodland would destroy the site's value for water birds, introduced Konik ponies, red deer and Heck cattle through the 1980s and 1990s as functional stand-ins for the extinct tarpan and aurochs, to keep the ground open by grazing. The large grazers were left out year-round with no supplementary feeding; the site is fenced, has no corridors to other reserves and no large predators. Many animals starved during a harsh winter in 2005, provoking public outcry and a shift to a culling strategy. In the winter of 2017–18 some 3,300 deer, ponies and cattle died, mostly of starvation; there were demonstrations and members of the public were arrested for bringing in hay. A commission chaired by Van Geel reported in April 2018, and its advice — manage the terrain rather than let it rewild, and reduce the remaining population by relocation or shooting — was adopted that July, itself prompting protests and litigation. The reserve became part of Nieuw Land National Park in 2018. Work from those facts as I have given them; I do not need them checked.

What I want mapped. Who the relevant actors and decision nodes actually were, and what the real option set was at each — creation of the reserve on abandoned industrial land, the introduction of the grazers, the hands-off policy of no feeding and no culling of healthy animals, the unchosen option of corridors to other habitat, the 2005 and 2017–18 die-offs, and the 2018 turn to active culling and population reduction. Then, through the apparatus: which of the two factors each decision acted on, and with what sign. In particular, I want the grazer introduction treated as a compositional shift rather than an addition of lives — large, slow-breeding mammals converting the site's productivity into few long lives, displacing the woodland's r-selected birds, rodents and insects — and I want to know whether the apparatus, if it is sound, should judge that introduction welfare-improving on the site's own terms. I also want the question of exit mode faced directly: starvation behind a fence versus predation versus a bullet is a difference in how lives end, not in how many end, and I want to know whether the ledger can register that at all or whether it needs an extra term that the decomposition does not supply.

Then the reverse direction. Tell me what the episode shows about the thesis that habitat destruction reduces wild-animal suffering. Note that this case is habitat creation by an industrialised state, on land whose counterfactual was agriculture or industry supporting very few wild lives: if wild lives are net negative, the apparatus seems to say that the celebrated reserve is an instance of the very act the thesis condemns. Is that a reductio, a consequence to accept, or a sign that the thesis was never really about habitat quantity at all but about the composition of who gets to occupy the slots? And say where the apparatus falls silent — insect welfare, the empirical sign of wild lives, whether occupancy neutrality holds at this scale, what to do about transition dynamics — rather than forcing it to deliver a verdict it has no materials for.

Finally, commit. Assume for the analysis that wild-animal welfare has moral weight and that no independent value attaches to naturalness or to ecological processes as such; if you think that stipulation is doing the work, say so in a sentence, but answer once, from within it. Give me definite verdicts, with reasons, on three things: whether the pre-2018 hands-off policy was worse, by the ledger, than preventive culling; whether the existence of the reserve has been net welfare-negative relative to its agricultural or industrial counterfactual, with a rough credence attached and the one sub-question that number is most sensitive to; and whether the episode ultimately supports or undermines the thesis. Please write in British English.
