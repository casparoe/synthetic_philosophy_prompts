In a graduate seminar on populism, a visiting speaker used the phrase "the will of the people" twice in ten minutes: first to argue that a constitutional court has no business striking down legislation passed by an elected majority, and then to wave off a question about how one identifies that will when the last general election, the last referendum, and current polling all point in different directions. I would rather hand him a disambiguation than a disagreement, so please treat the phrase as encoding a family of distinct theses and lay them out as a numbered menu.

Distinguish at least: readings on which it is the aggregation of individuals' actual preferences under some procedure (and note that majority preference can cycle, so on such a reading the will may be undefined rather than merely contested); readings on which it is a counterfactual or idealized construct — what the people would endorse under better information or fuller deliberation, or what serves their real interests whether or not they endorse it; readings on which "the people" is not the whole electorate but a morally unified body defined in opposition to elites, so that a numerical majority can fail to be the people and a minority can be it; and readings on which the will is not discovered but constituted by whoever successfully claims to speak for it, so that the claim and the will are one event rather than one reporting the other. For each reading, state the precise thesis, whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false to the point of incoherence, and separately whether it would actually support each of his two moves — the anti-court conclusion and the dismissal of the identification question. Conclude by saying which reading he was most likely helping himself to and whether that reading is one he could defend without circularity.

End with a final line of the form \answer{...} naming the single reading his argument most needs together with your verdict on it — for example, \answer{plebiscitary majoritarianism — substantive, but undefended as stated}.
