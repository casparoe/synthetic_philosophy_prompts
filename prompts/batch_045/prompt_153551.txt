I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I'm preparing a short internal talk for my team about how we argue for designs we cannot directly test. I'd like one joke to open the talk, and I'd like it to be a real joke about the structure of the string theory / multiverse debate rather than a generic nerd gag.

The situation in the talk: my team has spent months on a rewrite of our data ingest layer that we have no way to validate end to end, and we defend it in a design review with three moves that I've come to think are the same three moves made in debates about non-empirical theory confirmation: (1) nobody, after sustained effort, has managed to produce a viable alternative design; (2) rewrites by this team have turned out correct before, so this one probably is too; (3) the new design unexpectedly explains three unrelated bugs it was never built to fix. A junior engineer then asks what observation would count against the design, and the honest answer is: none that we could collect from inside the system.

What I'd like the punchline to turn on is the genuinely self-undermining worry in that debate, which I'll spell out so the joke doesn't depend on prior reading: if a framework is flexible enough to accommodate every possible observation, then the fact that things look fine to us carries no evidential weight at all; and there is a sharper observer-population version of the same worry, where most of the observers your theory allows are momentary, disordered fluctuations rather than long-lived creatures like us, so that being a coherent observer with orderly-looking logs is itself surprising on your own theory. I'd like the joke set in the review meeting, with the punchline landing on one of these reversals: the reasoning that confirms the design also removes the standing of the people concluding that it's confirmed.

Constraints: keep it dry and workplace-appropriate; it should be funny to engineers who know nothing about cosmology and funny, in the same structural way, to someone who knows the debate well. Avoid punchlines that turn only on there being very many universes, on the word string, or on physicists walking into a bar. Please give me the joke itself (setup and punchline, no longer than a short paragraph), then two or three sentences saying which feature of the actual debate the punchline is riding on, and where the software-engineering analogue stops being a fair analogue.
