Write a work of short fiction — roughly 900 to 1,200 words, third person, realistic rather than allegorical — that dramatizes a genuine dilemma in the ethics of pest control.

Setting and situation. Invent a Place: a smallholder farming cooperative in a river delta, with its own name, its own internal politics, and a staple crop that the families involved depend on directly. That crop is under pressure from a leaf-feeding moth. The cooperative must pick one of three responses for the coming season:

1. A systemic insecticide applied as a seed coating. Cheap, familiar, and reliable at protecting yield — but the insects that ingest it die slowly, over many hours or days, and the compound also reaches non-target insects in the soil and in the flowering plants along the field margins.
2. A gene-drive release that will crash the local moth population over three or four seasons. Almost nothing is poisoned or crushed outright; instead, far fewer moths come into existence. Some members argue this is the kindest option on the table. Others argue that preventing a population from existing is either the largest harm on the list or no harm at all, and that this disagreement has to be settled before anything is released, not after.
3. A behavioural programme: dispensers that saturate the fields with the moth's own mating pheromone so that males cannot locate females, together with an experimental spray that blocks the females' olfactory receptors, so that the crop effectively drops out of their perceptible world. Nothing is poisoned.

Requirements for the story.

- A staff agronomist has spent the year building a ledger: expected insect-days of suffering per hectare, for each of the three options. Her ledger shows the third option as overwhelmingly best. It also shows it as best mainly because of where she drew its boundary. The moths that never find the crop lay their eggs on a wild host plant in the surrounding marsh, where most of the caterpillars starve over a fortnight rather than dying in an afternoon — and none of that is on the ledger. Give the strongest available case that this sort of quantified, comparative accounting is the only honest way to approach a decision like this, and the strongest available case that its outputs are artifacts of what happens to be countable, and that the tidiness of a ledger is itself a moral hazard. The agronomist should be intelligent and self-aware enough that she could make either case herself.
- Include the disagreement about the moths. One character holds that insects are reflexive machinery and that no moral weight attaches to them at all. Another concedes that the biology is genuinely uncertain but argues that the numbers — hundreds of millions of animals per season, across this delta alone — mean that even a modest probability of felt pain swamps every other consideration in the decision. A third treats that argument as self-refuting: taken at face value it would condemn agriculture as such and would license campaigns of habitat destruction, which nobody in the room is prepared to endorse, so something must have gone wrong upstream. Make each of these good enough that a careful reader cannot simply dismiss it.
- Add a complication of time horizon: the ledger covers a single season, and it has no way to represent what happens when the moth is suppressed and a different insect expands into the niche it leaves behind.
- Put real human stakes on the other side of the scale. A fifth of the harvest is not an abstraction here; say concretely what it means for two or three named families. Keep everyone reasonable: the conflict should run between people arguing carefully from incompatible starting points, and there should be no villain and no strawman.
- End with a decision rather than a resolution. The cooperative votes, the season starts, and the final page makes clear both what was genuinely gained and what was quietly relocated to somewhere nobody is counting.

Constraints. No essay in dialogue form, no lecturing, no framing narrator who explains the philosophy. The conceptual content should be carried by what the characters notice, choose, and fail to notice, and by the consequences that follow. No citations, no footnotes, no references to real-world authors, organizations, or policy debates. The technical setting may sit slightly ahead of the present, but keep the speculative element narrow and the social world recognizable.
