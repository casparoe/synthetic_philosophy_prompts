hey — i need a parody for a bioethics reading-group newsletter, about 600 words, written as a health insurer's clinical coverage policy in deadpan bureaucratic prose: numbered clauses, then three or four short sample determinations applying the policy. The policy is the plan's official method for deciding whether an intervention counts as therapy or as enhancement.

I want the humour to come from the actual philosophy rather than from generic paperwork jokes, and please don't name-drop real philosophers or add footnotes. Two things in particular: the plan should define therapy as restoration of species-typical normal functioning, and you should follow that definition where it really leads — the ratchet, where population-level functioning drifts down, the normal-function line drifts down with it, and last decade's enhancement is this decade's covered treatment; and the loophole where a member can be deliberately brought below the baseline first (paying out of pocket for a mild induced deficit) so that restoring them counts as therapy. Also give me a clause that sets the reference class by the member's declared life plan, so the same drug is therapy for a member training to be an air-traffic controller and enhancement for one writing a novel, plus a sub-clause on what happens if you change your declared plan mid-treatment.

Where it should land, without anyone ever saying it plainly: that this line is supposed to be doing real work — what medicine is for, what counts as a need, what we owe each other — and that a line drawn at "normal functioning" keeps collapsing into either welfarism or into whatever reference class you happened to pick. And make the positional-goods worry into a reductio: the plan claws back coverage for any intervention whose benefit is competitive, since once everyone has it nobody is better off, and then applies that rule consistently to something the reader thinks of as ordinary treatment (glasses, literacy tutoring, whatever fits).

Start straight in with the policy document itself — no preface, and no winking sign-off at the end.
