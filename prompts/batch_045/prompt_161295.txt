I am a control engineer. For about fifteen years I have designed model-predictive controllers for grid-scale battery storage, and before that I worked on flight control. The vocabulary I am fluent in: plant and controller, the closed loop, sensors and actuators, state estimation, model-order reduction and reduced-order models, unmodeled dynamics and robustness margins, disturbance rejection, reference signals versus cost functions, system identification, adaptive control and self-tuning regulators and the ways those go unstable in the field, algebraic loops and the unit delays we insert to break them, and the fact that the controller is itself physical — it draws power, it has latency, its compute budget is part of the loop budget. I have no philosophy background and no interest in acquiring one, and I would rather you not cite anything: I cannot check it, and I do not care who said it first.

I recently read a summary of a set of problems that go under the name embedded agency, and I want to know whether there is anything in it that my field has not already dealt with. As I understood it, three claims are doing the work:

One: the split between agent and environment is not a feature of the world but a modeling convenience. There is no clean boundary where the agent stops and the world begins; the agent is a physical subsystem of the same world it is trying to model, and what we call its actions are simply events in that world, of the same kind as everything else happening in it.

Two: the agent is much smaller than the world it models. So the standard idealized picture — an agent that holds a full probability distribution over hypotheses about how the world works and updates that distribution by conditioning as evidence arrives — is not merely expensive but unavailable in principle. An embedded agent has to get by with approximate models that it knows are wrong.

Three: self-reference is where it becomes genuinely strange. Because the agent is inside the world it models, any model it has of the world must include a model of itself, and that model must include a model of the model, and so on. I was told this produces something like the following, which I may be garbling: a system that is asked to reason about what it itself will do, and that knows its own reasoning procedure, can get into a position where trusting its own conclusions makes those conclusions unreliable; and there is a toy case where an agent that has a complete model of its own decision procedure cannot settle on the better of two options, because every argument that it will choose the better one can be turned into an argument that it will choose the worse one. I gathered that the conclusion drawn is not "this is hard" but "the standard decision-theoretic framing is the wrong frame."

Here is what I want. Translate each of the three claims into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, one section per claim. Then give me the residue report: for each claim, what translated cleanly, what translated only after you distorted it or quietly added an assumption that is not in the original, and what cannot be said in my vocabulary at all. I want the report in both directions — tell me also what my framework can express that theirs seems unable to, since a one-sided inventory is worthless. If a claim turns out to translate trivially and completely, say so; I would rather have an honest inventory than a dramatic one.

My own suspicion, which you should argue with rather than accommodate, is that this is a non-problem for anyone who has actually built a controller. I routinely ship controllers whose internal plant model contains a reduced-order model of the controller itself — its own actuator lag, its own sample-and-hold, the jitter in its own compute schedule. Nothing bad happens. Nothing regresses. So when you get to claim three, I specifically want you to identify which feature of the self-modeling that control engineers do defuses the problem, and then say whether that feature is available to the embedded agent these people are worried about. If the answer is that it is not available, tell me exactly what makes it unavailable, and do not let the answer be that intelligent agents are somehow different in kind; that is the claim I am asking you to cash out, not to repeat.

Two more constraints. Do not smuggle my framework's assumptions into the translation and then present the result as a faithful restatement — if you have to add something to make a claim sayable in control terms, flag it. And close by telling me whether any of the residue, in your judgment, would change how I should design a controller, or whether it is all bookkeeping that makes no difference at the plant.
