I've ended up opposed to open-sourcing frontier model weights, and I want the case against me rather than another round of the case for me. The view I actually hold is narrow, and I'd like it attacked in that form: the objection isn't the popular one about misuse, since most misuse that matters can be routed through an API whether the model is open or closed. It's what I call the erosion gradient. Once weights are public, the cost of stripping out refusal and safety training falls fast and keeps falling — cheap fine-tunes, activation-level surgery, whatever comes next — the stripped variants propagate to actors the releasing lab would never have served, and the last intervention point vanishes: no recall, patch, or change of mind at the lab does anything downstream, because there is no downstream the lab controls. I also think the relevant unit isn't the weights but the whole stack — post-training data, tooling, scaffolding, and increasingly inference-time compute — so partial openness, weights without the rest, should capture most of the auditing and competitive benefits at a far shallower gradient, which is why I think the all-or-nothing framing of this debate is mistaken.

Give me a map of my exposure, ranked from fatal to merely awkward: which observations or arguments should move me, which should only embarrass me, and which of them a thoughtful open-weights advocate would concede as genuinely counting against openness rather than dismiss as motivated. Be concrete about what to watch for over the next few years — real rates of safety-training removal in models that have been released, whether the weights-versus-stack distinction survives contact with practice, how much of openness's value is distributed auditing as opposed to competitive pressure on whoever currently holds the lead — and say plainly which of those I could actually observe, because some of what I'd need may be unobservable by construction. Finally, tell me honestly whether I've insulated the view: I worry that the claim that the bad outcomes are exactly the ones leaving no legible trace, so that the absence of catastrophe so far is weak evidence, is doing more work than it deserves, and I'd rather be told that than have it confirmed.
