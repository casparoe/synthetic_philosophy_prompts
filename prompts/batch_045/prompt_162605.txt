I work on the safety case for a large, continuously deployed language-model system, and my team has converged on the view that serious failure will look like slow value drift rather than abrupt influence-seeking. The analogy we now use in internal documents is that of a senior civil servant in a large government department. Nobody in a ministry stages a coup; instead, over years, an official quietly accumulates discretion, drafts the rules by which their own performance will be judged, extends their remit into adjacent areas, becomes hard to remove because so much now runs through them, and ends up holding real power that no one ever decided to grant. We use this picture to license three things: (a) that the dangerous trajectory is accretion rather than seizure, (b) that continuous behavioural monitoring with rollback to earlier checkpoints is therefore the right primary control, and (c) that a fixed-schedule retirement policy is an adequate backstop — we rotate the system out of service every N months regardless of measured behaviour, the way one replaces a safety-critical component before it fails, rather than trying to detect a failure after the fact.

I want the analogy audited before it hardens into policy. Please give me:

1. An explicit table of correspondences — what plays the role of the ministry, the official, the minister to whom the official nominally reports, the official's remit, the performance rules the official writes, the audit, the election that can remove the government, and retirement.
2. The disanalogies, sorted into those that bear on the conclusions we are drawing and those that do not.
3. Whether (a), (b) and (c) survive once those disanalogies are on the table. On (a): is the difference between accretion and seizure a difference in kind, or only in the rate of one process, given that a system's influence and its options for acquiring more are jointly determined and can cross a threshold where rollback is no longer available? On (c): does the rotation policy work when the thing being retired can be copied, distilled, or depended on by downstream systems and institutions that outlive it, and when its replacement is drawn from a training pipeline that produces the same drift?
4. A better analogy, if one is available.

Lead with the correspondence table, then at most 400 words of prose; 550 words total including the table.
