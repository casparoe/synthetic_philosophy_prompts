I'm a backend dev, no philosophy training whatsoever, so apologies in advance if I'm describing something that has an obvious textbook name -- though that's kind of the question.

I keep noticing the same shape in really unrelated places and I want to know whether it's been picked out and named before, because right now the only way I have of talking about it is my own clumsy version.

The shape: a system -- animal, program, person, whatever -- has a set of routines it can run. Which routine gets started next is genuinely sensitive to the situation: different situation, different routine, and it can still change course right up until the moment the routine starts. But once a routine has started it runs to completion no matter what. If the world changes halfway through in a way that makes the routine pointless or actively bad, the system keeps going anyway. And it's not that it fails to notice -- it's that nothing it notices can get any grip until the routine finishes. Then it's flexible again, picks the next routine, and goes rigid again. All the flexibility is at the seams; all the rigidity is inside the blocks.

Three places I see it:

- The thing I half remember from a pop-science book I read years ago. A digger wasp drags its paralyzed prey to the burrow, leaves it at the entrance, goes inside to check the burrow, comes back out, drags the prey in. If you move the prey a few inches while she's inside, she comes out, drags it back to the entrance, leaves it, goes in to check again -- and you can apparently keep doing that to her. I genuinely can't vouch for the details, so don't hold me to them; the point is the shape. She can "reconsider" at the entrance and not mid-sequence.

- My own code, which is where I started thinking about this. I've got a job runner where jobs are made of stages. Between stages the job re-reads config, and a scheduler can cancel it, reroute it, reorder the remaining stages. Inside a stage it's completely non-preemptible -- no signal, no cancel, no partial rollback, the stage either finishes or the whole process dies. Increasingly I think of the "intelligence" of that system as living entirely in the gaps between stages, and I have no idea whether that's a deep observation or just a fact about my implementation.

- Me, annoyingly. I can deliberate about whether to start explaining something in an argument, and I can deliberate after each sentence about whether to keep going, but once I've started a sentence I finish it -- even in the cases where I realize halfway through that it's wrong. I finish the sentence and then correct it, every single time. Same with a bunch of other things I won't bore you with.

So:

1. Does this configuration -- flexible at the seams, rigid inside the blocks -- have a name of its own? I have a vague memory that there's a word for the wasp case specifically, but as far as I understand it that word just means "rigid routine," and it doesn't seem to cover the part I find interesting, which is that the very same system is flexible elsewhere.

2. The part I actually care about. I keep loosing an argument with a friend about whether this shape is compatible with doing something freely, or whatever the right phrasing is. She says: sure, the wasp is mechanical, but a creature that can select which routine to run, and re-select at every seam, is not mechanical -- that just is what deliberating is. I say: that only moves the mechanism up one level, because whatever does the selecting at the seam is itself either running a routine or it isn't, and if you add another layer of flexible selection on top of that you get exactly the same question again. Neither of us can say what would even settle it. Is there an established name for this disagreement? Is there an established account of what you'd have to add to "flexible selection over rigid routines" to make it stop being mechanical -- or an established argument that nothing you could add would ever be enough?

3. Boundary cases, because if I get a definition I want to know what it excludes. (a) A system with exactly one seam, right at the start, deterministic after that -- a pocket calculator, basically. (b) A system whose seams arrive at random times rather than when the situation calls for one. (c) A system that can be interrupted by anything at all but has exactly one fixed response to any interruption. (d) A system where the seams are real but the blocks are a millisecond long. Which of these are the same phenomenon as what I described and which aren't, and what's the principled difference?

If there genuinely isn't a name for it, then coin one and define it, and say what falls outside. Please don't just hand me the word for the wasp thing and stop -- a name that only covers the rigidity and ignores the seams is definately not what I'm after, and I'll be able to tell if that's what I got.
