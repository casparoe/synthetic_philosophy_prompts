I should say what I actually do, because I want the translation carried out in my own vocabulary rather than an explanation pitched down at me. I design and maintain data infrastructure: event-sourced services with append-only logs, bitemporal tables where valid time and transaction time are tracked separately, MVCC snapshot isolation, CDC pipelines feeding downstream projections, slowly changing dimensions of the type that mint a new surrogate row on every change, and an entity-resolution layer that has to decide which records in a messy stream belong to the same real-world thing. Last night I had a long, unresolved argument with a friend who is writing a dissertation on persistence. I claimed that the choice between modelling an object as one mutable record whose history is a derived artifact, and modelling it as an append-only sequence of immutable versions from which a current state is projected, is an engineering tradeoff governed by read patterns and audit requirements — a decision about interfaces, not a discovery about what objects are. She said the perdurantist thesis is not a claim about representation at all, and when I pressed her she went to the argument from temporary intrinsics: a thing that is straight and then bent, wholly present at both times, with shape taken as intrinsic. I said my framework has no way to state intrinsicality, so the argument cannot get a grip there; she said that is precisely the point, that my framework is missing the very structure the debate is about. We left it there.

What I want is a systematic translation. Restate endurantism, perdurantism in its worm-theoretic form, and the stage-theoretic variant as well if it deserves separate treatment, in the vocabulary I actually use: primary keys and surrogate keys, validity intervals, transaction-time cursors, system-versioned tables, immutable event logs, materialized views and snapshots, projections and replay, merge and compaction, CRDTs, entity resolution. For each view, say which of its commitments map cleanly onto which of my constructs, and which map only if I bend something — and be explicit when you are bending. Do not quietly import my framework's conventions as if they were the translation: in my world identity is assigned by a key and granularity is a design choice, and I would rather be told when that stipulation is doing philosophical work that the metaphysics is supposed to be doing itself. Use the standard test cases as a check on the translation: a ship whose planks are replaced one at a time, a person whose cerebral hemispheres are separated, coinciding objects, and the question of what is going on when I say that I was once two years old.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I care about. What in the debate has no expression in my vocabulary at all — I suspect mereological parthood is the first casualty, since rows in a table are not parts of anything, and I suspect intrinsicality is the second, along with whatever depends on it. I also want to know where the worries that seem to generate heat among philosophers have no analogue for me: the fear that unrestricted composition of temporal parts yields unwelcome extra objects, the complaint that perdurantism makes persistence too cheap or persons too many, the modal counterpart machinery stage theorists rely on, and the question whether the present is metaphysically privileged, which in my systems is just the transaction-time cursor and nothing else. Then tell me what you think the residue means: is it a defect of my framework, which represents time-richly but mereology-poorly, or is some of it a sign that a chunk of the disagreement was always thinner than it looked, a notation choice with metaphysical adjectives attached? One more thing, because it has been nagging at me since the argument: I once prototyped a store in which we dropped stable identifiers entirely and let a downstream entity-resolution job reconstruct which events belonged to which object, with the boundaries of an object coming out as a clustering result rather than a stipulation. Tell me which position in the debate that design is closest to, what it would cost me, and whether building a system in which the log is the object and the row is a derived projection would have settled anything metaphysical or merely built a four-dimensionalist in silicon. Wherever the answer is contested rather than settled, say so rather than picking a side silently, since I intend to bring this back to my friend.
