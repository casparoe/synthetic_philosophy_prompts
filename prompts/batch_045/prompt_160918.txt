I've been reading Marx on my own for a couple of years (I work in logistics, long story) and I'm trying to write down what I actually believe about automation, specially now with all the AI stuff. Before I inflict it on my reading group, can someone tell me if this set hangs together:

1. Profit is unpaid labour. Workers produce more value than they get back in wages and that gap is the profit. I dont mean anyone is paid below market or is starving, the gap itself is the point.
2. Real wages can genuinely go up and workers lives can get materially better under capitalism without it stoping to be exploitation. People keep telling me this contradicts 1. I think it doesnt, but I've never been able to explain why very well.
3. Machines as such are not the enemy, its who owns them and what they get used for. Labour-saving tech ought to mean less necessary work, and I think wanting that is a good thing, not some reactionary dream of going back.
4. But I also think workers are usually right to fight labour-saving tech in their own workplace, and I have a lot of sympathy for people who smashed machinery historically. Under capitalism the machine really is a weapon pointed at them, and it'll be fine in fifty years is not much of an answer to someone losing their job next month.
5. Capitalism will basically choke on automation. The less living labour there is in production, the less unpaid labour there is to take profit out of, so the system runs out of fuel on its own. This is why I'm less worried about mass unemployment than about the whole thing decaying into something weird.
6. AI is different in kind and not just degree, because it automates the planning and knowing side rather than just the doing side — that bit of Marx where even the worst architect is better than the best bee because he raises the thing in his imagination before he builds it. Everything he says about machinery seems to assume the machine is dumb and the human is the one who knows whats supposed to happen, so I'm not sure any of it transfers. Though maybe the reply is that there's way more hidden human labour behind AI than the demos suggest (labelling, clean-up, people fixing its output), in which case it isnt different in kind at all and I've been suckered by marketing. I cant tell which.

Which of these actually clash with each other and which only look like they do? Go through the ones that genuinely clash in order of how bad the clash is, and for each say the cheapest thing I could give up and what it would cost me. I'm not attached to any of them, so tell me straight which one has to go.
