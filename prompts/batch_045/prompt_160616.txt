I'm trying to work out what I actually think corruption is, because my gut verdicts on cases don't line up with each other. Here they are:

- a ministry procurement officer awards a contract to the firm that paid for his daughter's wedding: corrupt, obviously
- the same guy takes a box of chocolates at New Year from a supplier with a bid pending: not corrupt, though he should've refused
- a village chief gets a goat from a family after settling their land dispute in their favour, where refusing would be a genuine insult: not corrupt, and I don't want an anti-corruption agency anywhere near it
- a mayor steers a no-bid contract to a company whose CEO gave 50k to her campaign, even though she'd honestly have picked that company anyway: corrupt
- a legislator votes for a bill that a union spent 2 million backing her campaign over, and she actually agrees with the bill: not corrupt
- a doctor takes a disclosed speaking fee from a drug company: not corrupt, but something feels off
- a banking regulator takes a job at a bank she used to supervise, 18 months after leaving: not corrupt, but worse than the chocolates and I can't say why
- an aid worker pays a customs official to release a shipment of vaccines: not corrupt

The test I keep falling back on is that a transfer is corrupt when it leaves the office-holder holding a debt that can only be discharged by using the powers of the office — call it the office-debt test. But it seems to convict the regulator, since the only way she can "earn" that future job is by going easy now, and I don't want to convict her; and it has nothing to say about why the mayor and the legislator feel different to me, since both look like money in, official act out.

Is there one principle that gets most of this, or two principles covering different halves? Tell me which of my verdicts it forces me to drop, what it says about the case I'm stuck on — the guy at the water utility who move my leak up the queue because I slipped him twenty bucks — and whether the principle you land on is really universal or just "office as a public trust, Western edition" with the serial numbers filed off. Please don't point me at any literature; I can't look anything up.
