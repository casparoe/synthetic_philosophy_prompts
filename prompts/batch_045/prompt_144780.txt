My uncle Ray died last week at 78 and I'm doing the eulogy on Saturday. He was, by any honest description, a pleasure-seeker, but never in one register: he restored wooden boats, ate extremely hot curry, did the crossword in ink, sang bass in a church choir though he wasnt religious, swam in the sea every month of the year, and gave whole afternoons to helping neighbours fix fences for nothing. The family keep saying somebody should find the thread, and since I'm the one with the philosophy degree it's landed on me.

The trouble is I've been reading about hedonism lately — the old objection that pleasures don't share any one felt quality, that the taste of a mango and the satisfaction of finally cracking a hard problem and the shock of cold water have nothing sensory in common — and I'm half convinced the golden-glow story is just false, even though it's what everyone expects a eulogy to say. Also he used to claim the swim was only good because of the whisky after it, which makes me think his pleasures weren't self-contained feelings at all. But a list of hobbies isn't a eulogy either, and "he got what he wanted" makes him sound like a toddler.

So write it: some unifying account of what he was after that doesn't pretend there's a single sensation running underneath all of it, and doesn't collapse into "he had a lot of appetites and he fed them." It has to land as love for a room of his sailing club, his choir and his grandchildren rather than as a seminar, and he shouldn't have called bullshit on it. No theory words in his mouth. 350 words max.
