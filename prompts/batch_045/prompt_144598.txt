I'm a philosophy PhD student working on whether the Great Silence is even the sort of thing that could count as evidence, and I'd like to see one particular dissolution of it dramatized rather than argued. Please write a short story — a thousand to fifteen hundred words, literary but controlled — in which someone working on SETI comes to experience the Fermi question as ill-posed rather than merely unanswered.

The mechanism I want the story built around: the paradox treats a civilization as a countable, persisting unit, so that the galaxy contains some number N of them, each with an observable signature, and our failure to find any licenses an inference to N being very small or to the silence being dangerous news about our future. But intelligence does not obviously arrive in such units. Whatever bears minds can merge, split, change spatial scale, go dormant, or reorganize internally, and none of those events need be anyone's death. The count is therefore not conserved over the timescales the argument runs on: a galaxy can move from containing many agents to containing one very large and very slow one, or the reverse, with nothing destroyed along the way. My suggestion is that the silence is what that instability looks like from the inside — not evidence of absence, but the observational profile of a process whose unit of description is not the one we are counting in.

Constraints. Do not resolve anything by extinction, by a filter ahead of or behind us, by concealment or a zoo, by interstellar travel being impossible or unattractive, or by aliens being too alien for us to recognize their signals. Whatever is out there is extant and present in the story's galaxy; the difficulty lies entirely on the side of the question. Keep the science honest — slower than light, no new physics doing the philosophical work — and have the story face rather than dodge the thermodynamic objection that a galaxy-spanning process should still show up as waste heat and appears not to. I'd like the arc to end with the character asking a better question instead of learning a fact, and with the dissolution landing as a kind of loss and not only a relief.
