Ok so context: I build LLM agents for a living, and for the last couple of months I've been going through the logs of where ours goes wrong. Almost every failure has the same shape: it can generate a plan fine, but it can't kill a bad plan before it spends twenty minutes executing it, and nothing carries over to the next run. A friend said this is basically Dennett's ladder of kinds of minds — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian creatures — and I get the gist, but I want to use it as a measuring stick rather than a story.

So: treat those four grades as upgrades I could in principle bolt onto my agent, and give me fractions (summing to 1) for how much of the gap between it and a competent junior engineer each one would close, with one sentence of justification each.

Two wrinkles. First, I want a grade that isn't in Dennett's list: call it a Curatorial creature — one that accumulates and reuses designs produced by others (past runs, other instances, other agents) but has no way of testing them, so it hoards failures just as happily as successes, which is roughly what our memory feature does today. Where does that go on the ladder, and does it belong on the ladder at all, or is it a side-branch that shows the ladder is the wrong shape? Second, tell me which of Dennett's four is least like a step up and most like a separate axis entirely, and whether spotting that should change your fractions.
