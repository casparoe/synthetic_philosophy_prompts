The situation: I am giving a twenty-minute talk next month to a mixed workshop of animal ethicists and people who work on AI welfare. I constructed the argument below about a month ago, and I cannot accept its conclusion, but every individual step still looks sound to me. I do not want a map of the literature, a bibliography, or a survey of positions; I want this specific argument diagnosed. Call it the Dimmer Switch Argument.

(1) Anti-chauvinism. Whether a system has moral status, and how much, is fixed by that system's own empirically detectable properties its causal organization, its discriminative and learning capacities, the way its states are integrated and how it regulates itself not by its species, its substrate, its origin, or the strength of my sympathy for it. Anything else is prejudice with a technical vocabulary.

(2) Continuity. Every candidate status-relevant capacity anyone has proposed learning from aversive input, integrating information across sensory modalities, flexible goal-directed re-planning, having a valenced state that the system's own behavior is organized around, some form of self-monitoring is a matter of degree. Across phylogeny these shade into one another, and so do they across ontogeny: compare a late-term fetus, a preterm infant, a newborn, a toddler, a fish, an insect, a nematode.

(3) No magic. There is no further, non-empirical fact no soul, no irreducible phenomenal residue hovering behind the functional and behavioral facts that could supply the bright line that (2) fails to provide. If there is a line, it is a line in nature, not in the sky.

(4) Monotonicity. If x has some degree of moral status, then any system that possesses all of x's status-relevant capacities and additional ones has at least as much moral status as x.

(5) The graduated nociceptor. Consider a system with four features: (a) a discriminative sensitivity to a class of conditions that damage or threaten it; (b) an internal register whose value rises with exposure to those conditions and decays only slowly; (c) a global reallocation of the system's priorities as a function of that register's value, such that the register is not merely a signal to a single downstream actuator but a variable the whole system reorganizes itself around; and (d) no representation of itself as the thing that is in that condition. Call this a graduated nociceptor. It is, by construction, a strict subset of what an insect plausibly has, and it is something a suitably engineered thermostatic control system could in principle realize. If a graduated nociceptor has zero moral status, I want to be told what further property is doing the work, and why that further property is not precisely the spooky item (3) rules out.

(6) Therefore no threshold can be located at which moral status is exactly zero. Drawing a line higher up would be arbitrary in a way (1) and (2) forbid; drawing it at the bottom would require the brute discontinuity that (3) forbids.

(7) Therefore moral status is a positive scalar quantity with no zero floor: every valence-sensitive, self-regulating system is on the dimmer, thermostats and their engineered cousins included, simply at very low settings.

(8) The consequence I cannot swallow. For every positive epsilon, there is a possible system such that destroying or tormenting it for a moment's idle amusement is wrong, but wrong by less than epsilon. And if moral statuses are additive across systems, then a sufficiently large collection of such systems, each tormented for fun, generates an aggregate wrongness that outweighs any single familiar harm to any single familiar animal. So the view that was supposed to widen the moral circle either trivializes wrongness there is always a degree of wrongness small enough to be worth ignoring or generates a Repugnant Conclusion at the bottom of the scale.

(9) I reject (8). So something upstream has to go.

What I most want to keep is (1) and (3): I would rather surrender (5) or (7) than retreat to a criterion that points at species membership or at something non-natural. My supervisor says the argument is just a sorites, that I should say vagueness and move on. That does not satisfy me, because I want to know what the vagueness or the threshold is *in*: the base properties, the relation that confers status, or the strength of the reasons that status generates.
