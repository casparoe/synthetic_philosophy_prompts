I've been going round in circles on this for months and I want someone to tell me what I actually believe. It started with an old dialogue I read years ago in some anthology — a guy talks to Einstein's brain, and then someone points out you could in principle replace the brain with a gigantic book, every question you could ask and the answer Einstein would give, and asks whether the book is the thing doing the answering. My gut says no, obviously not. But then I started making up other cases and my gut definately doesn't line up, and I'd like to know whether there's one rule I'm groping for or whether I've got two different questions tangled together.

Here's where I land:

1. Einstein's brain alive in a jar, being fed signals and replying somehow: yes, feels like something's home in there.
2. The giant book sitting on a shelf, nobody reading it: no, obviously.
3. The book with a person looking up the answers and writing them down: no — the book contributes nothing, and I don't think the person understands anything either, they're just copying. Still, the whole setup produces Einstein's answers, and it doesn't feel like anything is added.
4. Same information on a hard drive, program doing the lookup: no, same reason.
5. A simulation of the brain's actual neurons running the firing dynamics on a big computer: yes. Fairly confident.
6. The very same simulation done with pencil and paper by millions of people, one update per day, finishing in about nine thousand years: I still want to say yes, but it feels much shakier than 5 and I can't say why.
7. Every intermediate state of that simulation written on index cards and stored in a warehouse, in order: no, definitively.
8. Neurons replaced one at a time with little chips that do exactly what the neuron did: yes, he's still there. Sure about this one.
9. The whole replacement done in one operation while he's under — brain out, functionally identical chip in, every behaviour preserved: I feel like he died. I know that's the same end state as 8 and I hate that I feel this.
10. A machine that produces Einstein's answers by a completely different route — no neurons, no lookup, some other physical process that's just as complicated and just as causally tangled, but organised nothing like a brain: this is the one I go back and forth on. Sometimes yes, sometimes no.

My own guess at a principle was something like: it has to actually run the causal dynamics at the right grain of detail, not just match the input-output pattern or store the states. But that falls apart on 9, where the dynamics are running fine, and it doesn't explain why 6 counts and 7 doesn't, since nothing is running in 6 in any normal sense of running.

So: can you give me two or three candidate principles, stated sharply, with each clause tied to the case that forces it, and say what each one predicts about cases I haven't listed — say, two copies of the same emulation started at once, or a brain emulation that's switched off for five hundred years and then resumed. And please tell me plainly which of my verdicts no decent principle can keep. I'd much rather drop one of them than end up with a rule that has a special exemption bolted on to save case 9. Also, seperate what people who actually work on this agree about from what's still fought over — I keep reading very confident-sounding stuff and I can't tell which parts are just somebody's favourite theory. And don't give me a reading list, I can't get hold of any of it anyway.
