You are a philosopher of physics who works on the metaphysics of time. Think the question through before you write: list the candidate presuppositions one by one, test each against the practice, and drop the ones the practice does not really need. Then answer in under 500 words, with no citations or bibliography.

I am preparing a seminar session on presentism and eternalism and want to open from a practice rather than from the usual dialectic. So: working cosmologists state a single age for the universe — about 13.8 billion years, with error bars — date every event as so many billions of years before *now*, and run one time axis across a map of the whole cosmos, including regions we never interact with. The same physicists teach that simultaneity is frame-relative and that "now" carries no relativistic cash value. What view of time does that practice presuppose, if any?

Three things I want separated. First, what it strictly requires: does it need a physically privileged slicing of spacetime into hypersurfaces of cosmic time, and is that the same commitment as eternalism, or does presentism have equal use for such a slicing — the cosmic present being simply the one leaf there is? Second, what it merely fits well with: is the practice in fact compatible with presentism, the growing block, and eternalism alike, so that the familiar claim that relativity settles the debate is overblown in both directions? And what, if anything, does it genuinely rule out — for instance a view on which there is no objective fact about how much time has elapsed since the Big Bang, or no objective temporal distance between a distant event and us? A colleague tells me that in the standard model the elapsed proper time since the Big Bang is greatest for observers who have remained comoving, which would make the chosen slicing look principled rather than merely convenient; if that is right, tell me how much work it does, and if not, say what the practice is left resting on.

Finally: what would cosmology have to give up in a spacetime with no global time function — a rotating universe admitting closed timelike curves, say? Would "the age of the universe" mean anything there, or would the practice survive only as a local one? Close with a single bottom-line sentence on whether this practice is evidence for eternalism, for presentism, or about neither.
