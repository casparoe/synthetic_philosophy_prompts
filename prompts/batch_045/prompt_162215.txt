I teach a graduate seminar on strategic rationality, and the final session of the term is on nuclear deterrence: the paradox that a threat is most credible when carrying it out would be plainly irrational, and the family of maneuvers — brinkmanship, delegation, commitment devices — by which a state tries to make its own future intentions irrelevant to its adversary's decision. I want to close with a poem rather than a summary, and I have one specific piece of machinery I want the poem to be about.

The scenario is one I constructed myself and have not seen discussed anywhere. A state that fears a disarming first strike installs a device. It is not a doomsday machine: it does not fire automatically and unconditionally. Its specification is published in full beforehand and cannot be revised afterwards. If an unambiguous first strike is detected, the device authorizes retaliation with probability q, where q was set once, publicly, at a level chosen so that any first strike has negative expected value for the attacker. The design is public knowledge. It is public knowledge who chose q. It is public knowledge that q was chosen to make the arithmetic come out that way.

What I cannot settle is whether this reproduces what the classic maneuver is supposed to buy. On the classic picture, the point of arranging matters so that events you do not control might trigger the retaliation is that your hands are off the wheel: what faces the adversary is not a decision you will later make but a risk you can no longer cancel. In my scenario the risk was manufactured deliberately, out of arithmetic, and the adversary knows it. Two readings pull at me. On the first, the device achieves nothing. Conditional on the draw coming out in favor of retaliation, there is still a human decision left to make, and if retaliation would be pointless once your cities are gone, the adversary should expect that decision to go the way such decisions always go; so the device is only an odd idiom for stating a threat, and the credibility problem survives untouched. On the second, once the device is genuinely beyond recall, the loss of control is real even though it was manufactured, and the state has purchased credibility at the price of a standing chance of doing something it will later regard as pointless: a doomsday machine with a die inside it. I keep oscillating between these, and I want the poem to stop oscillating.

Formally: a sestina. Thirty-nine lines — six stanzas of six, then a three-line envoi — unrhymed, in steady iambic pentameter, with the six end-words rotating by the standard rule (a stanza ending in the order A B C D E F is followed by one ending F A E B D C, and so on through the cycle), and with all six words present in the final three lines, three of them as line endings and three inside the lines. The rotation must be doing the philosophical work: the same six words returning in a new order each time is the crisis recurring one rung higher, the vocabulary identical and therefore the failure to tell this crisis from the last one made structural. Choose the six words yourself, but no abstractions of the deterrence vocabulary and no jargon; give me six plain words a first-time reader can hold, each of which can mean one thing early in the poem and something else by the envoi. Put the turn in the fifth or sixth stanza — the moment it becomes clear which of my two readings is right — and let the envoi state what the device has in fact purchased.

The voice is the custodian of the device: the person who chose q, speaking on the night it goes live, addressing the adversary they will never meet. Not a villain and not a prophet — someone who did arithmetic and now has to live with having done it.

I do not want a poem that hedges, and I do not want one that stages both readings and leaves the reader to choose. Commit to one, let the sestina's machinery carry that commitment, and then add a note of no more than a paragraph defending it: state what the poem assumes about the adversary's rationality, about whether the device is truly beyond recall, and about what the adversary believes the custodian will do when the draw comes out the wrong way; and say plainly where the poem knowingly does violence to the theory in order to work as a poem. A committed poem that is slightly wrong will be more use to me than a balanced one that nobody remembers.
