hey — I sit on the deployment review committee at the lab I work at, and for the next round they've asked each of us to write down our own position first so the others can take swings at it. I'd definately rather find the cracks myself than have someone else find them in the meeting. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Safety measures should be built to work even if the model is actively trying to get around them. Assume misalignment, go for control rather than trust, don't put anything in the safety case that requires the model to be well-intentioned.
2. I don't actually think the scary failure mode is a treacherous turn. What I expect is much duller: a system that's subtly wrong in ways that are expensive to check, plus reviewers who sign off because they're busy and the output looks fine. No villain anywhere, just slow drift.
3. I'm comfortable shipping a system I'd put at maybe 60–70% not-misaligned, as long as the control measures are in place and the blast radius of any single deployment is bounded. The case in front of us right now is an internal research agent that writes and runs code, a human approves any tool call above a risk tier, and a weaker model watches the transcript.
4. What I want out of alignment work isn't that the model ends up with good values. It's that the model stays corrigible — does what the operator asks, accepts correction, doesn't resist being shut down or modified.
5. I don't buy the alignment tax. As far as I can tell, making a system safe is mostly just good engineering and doesn't cost much capability.
6. I think we should be willing to delay or cancel a deployment, even at real commercial cost, whenever we can't verify the system is safe — and I'd be fine with a regulator forcing that on everyone.

So: which of these actually fight with each other, which only look like they do, and for each real conflict what's the cheapest thing to give up and what does giving it up cost me? Please rank them by how bad they are instead of handing me six equal-sized problems. And if any pair looks inconsistent only because I've been sloppy about a distinction, tell me which distinction does the work. I don't know where the problem is — if I did I'd have fixed it before writing this down.
