Writing a dissertation chapter on fiscal crises and state formation, and I keep arguing with a friend in the philosophy department about what actually counts as an explanation in history. Here are my verdicts, going purely on gut, and I want the principle behind them.

1. "States that debase their currency by more than 30% within a decade get inflation within two years," applied to third-century Rome: explains, but thinly — like an answer to a why-question I wasn't asking.
2. A 900-page narrative that strings together everything documented about 1792–94 in order: not an explanation of the Terror. A very long chronicle.
3. The July Crisis — the assassination, the misread telegram, the blank cheque, the mobilisation timetables — stitched into one story with the ministers' reasons inside it: explains, and I feel no need for any law underneath.
4. "Fiscal crisis plus elite fragmentation produces revolution," fitted after the fact to 1789, 1917 and 1979: explains nothing, even though the fit is perfect.
5. A cliodynamics-style model that recovers the ~50-year instability cycles in three premodern states from a few coupled equations, with no archives and no named individuals: explains, weakly — and I can't say why that feels different from (4).
6. "The bridge collapsed because the load exceeded the rivets' tensile strength": explains completely, and I want no story at all.

So the pairs I can't reconcile are (4)/(5) and (1)/(6): structurally similar, opposite verdicts. My tentative principle: an account explains an outcome iff it puts you in a position to answer some counterfactual about that outcome that you couldn't answer before. Test it against my six and repair it if it breaks, then tell me what it says about two cases I can't settle — explaining why something didn't happen (the Song economy never industrialising), and explaining an outcome whose causes look like pure absences (a garrison that doesn't mutiny because nobody organised it). And be straight about which of my verdicts a decent principle would make me give up.
