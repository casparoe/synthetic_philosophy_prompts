I recently joined the review panel for a new interdisciplinary seed fund at my college (Mind, Agency, and Value), and I have been asked to draft the one-page guidance reviewers will use to score proposals. Every meeting so far has ended in the same exchange: someone calls a proposed mechanism a skyhook, someone else calls a proposed explanation greedily reductionist, and the discussion stops there. I would rather mock the pattern than keep relitigating it.

Write a satirical internal document: a mock reviewer rubric for the fund, titled along the lines of "Crane/Skyhook Review Protocol (Form CSRP-4)." 600–700 words, American English, formatted as numbered scoring criteria — each with a point range, a definition, and a one- or two-sentence sample reviewer comment — followed by a short "Appeals" section.

Constraints:

1. Voice: deadpan committee prose, the flat confidence of an institutional handbook that is certain about a distinction it cannot state precisely. Stay in register; do not let the narrator wink.
2. Legibility: a reader who has never met Dennett's crane/skyhook vocabulary should be able to pick up what a crane and a skyhook are from the first two criteria alone, without being lectured.
3. The humor has to come from real features of the view rather than invented ones. I want these four in particular:
   - The grounding regress: a crane only lifts with machinery that was itself lowered into place, so any chain of grounding either continues indefinitely or stops at something that looks, from below, exactly like the skyhook it was supposed to replace. Let the panel discover this and then deal with it bureaucratically.
   - No fixed cutoff: the same mechanism reads as a well-grounded crane inside an established field and as a miracle in an interdisciplinary proposal, because how much accumulated design work is required is never specified.
   - Unfalsifiability: "greedy reductionism" is supposed to mark explanations that explain away rather than explain, but whether a lower-level account explains away the higher-level phenomenon seems to depend on whether the reviewer already wanted that phenomenon to be real — so the charge functions as a verdict, and it gets used in both directions.
   - The mirror error: holists invoke "skyhook" to refuse any mechanistic story at all, then reintroduce their own lift dressed up as emergence, as sui generis normativity, or as a brute fact at the higher level.
4. Carry the reductio through: applied honestly, the protocol should end up either defunding everything or licensing everything, and I want the reader to have felt which way it goes before the Appeals section quietly concedes it.
5. The target is the practice — these words functioning as conversation-stoppers in interdisciplinary argument — not any person, and not interdisciplinarity itself.

Begin directly with the rubric: its title, then the criteria. No preamble and no closing commentary.
