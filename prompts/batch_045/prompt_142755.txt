I got pulled onto the three-person committee rewriting the comment rules for our local paper's site (long story — somebody resigned over a letter to the editor) and its just hit me that we don't have a theory, we have a pile of gut calls and one extremely long email thread. So before we write anything down: what would an account of slurs have to get right to be the sort of thing three editors who vote differently could consistently apply, and defend to readers in a note?

Give me the requirements list with the case behind each. The cases I keep circling: the same word in a quotation from a bigot we're reporting on versus in a reclaimed use by someone in the target group; an out-group writer who uses it warmly, means nothing by it, and who I still want to bounce; a slur borrowed into a language where none of the history travelled with it; an insult aimed at a group that has most of the power in this town (rich people, landlords) that's rude but that nobody would put in the same box; an old ethnic slur that's basically gone inert; and the case where two members of the target group disagree with each other about whether the reclaimed use is okay.

Then the distinctions it can't collapse — offensive versus derogatory versus merely taboo; what's said versus what's expressed versus what's implicated; the word-type versus the token; the speaker's attitudes versus the word's content — and mark which of the requirements are non-negotiable and which are hobby horses of the semantic-content camp or the pragmatic one.

I suspect two requirements can't both hold: that the account give the same verdict no matter who's in the room, and that it explain why offense tracks who's speaking and who's listening. Am I giving up too early, or is that itself the result?

Last thing, and the one I care most about: is it a genuine requirement that a theory explain the "the X-slur" euphemism — that naming a slur without using it half-works, works better in some mouths than others, and has no real analogue for ordinary insults? If it's genuine, say whether it's the requirement that kills off most of the field; if it's just my weird fixation, say what to swap it for. Skip the full scorecard of who wins.
