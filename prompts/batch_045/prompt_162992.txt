I keep saying things like "science is getting closer to the truth" and then realising I have no idea what I mean by closer, so I'm trying to work backwards from my own gut verdicts on pairs of theories to whatever principle is behind them. Here they are, most of them pretty confident:

1. Newton's mechanics vs Ptolemy's astronomy: Newton, by a mile. (Both abandoned, both in some strict sense false.)
2. Kepler vs Copernicus: Kepler, but only a bit.
3. Ptolemy vs Copernicus: Copernicus, but only a bit — and not because it predicted better, because it basically didnt. So something about being right about what's in the middle counts separately from predictive accuracy.
4. Wegener's continental drift (continents move, but they plow through the ocean floor, which is impossible) vs the land-bridge story (matching fossils on distant continents explained by sunken isthmuses, which is perfectly possible physics): I want to say Wegener is closer, even though his mechanism was worse than theirs.
5. Fresnel's partly-dragged ether vs a fully-dragged ether: Fresnel. The reason I reach for is that his dragging coefficient is exactly what reappears inside special relativity's velocity addition — not that he had more true consequences, since apart from the Fizeau experiment the two theories did about equally well.
6. Kelvin's cooling calculation for the age of the Earth (tens of millions of years, wrong because he didn't know about radioactive heating) vs a bishop's six thousand: Kelvin, hugely. Both false, but one was wrong in a way that was itself a discovery.
7. The one I'm shakiest on: 'the Earth is 4.5 billion years old' vs 'the Earth is somewhere between one million and ten billion years old'. The first is false, the second is true, and I still want to say the first is closer to the truth.
8. But: germ theory vs Newton's mechanics — I don't think there's any fact of the matter about which is closer. Within a domain I'm happy to rank (germ theory beats miasma easily); across domains I go quiet.

The rough rule I've been using is: T1 is closer to the truth than T2 if you'd have to change less of T1 to get to the truth. That seems to handle 1 thorugh 6, and 7 too (the precise false one only needs a small nudge, the vague true one needs a lot of filling in). But I think it quietly ruins 8, since on that rule everything gets ranked by how much of it survives into whatever we now accept — and germ theory survives more of itself than Newton does. Also 'less' is doing all the work and I cant say what it means.

And here's the case I go back and forth on, from a made up planet so nobody's intuitions are already trained: two theories of the tides. Theory A says tides are caused by the planet's spin dragging the oceans around — wrong cause — but its formula for the timing and height of high tide is exactly the one the true theory later derives, and it's right to the minute. Theory B says tides are caused by the pull of the planet's large moon — right cause — but it's off by a factor of ten on amplitude and can't handle the spring/neap cycle at all. Some days I say A is closer, some days B.

So: can you state a precise principle — or two or three candidate principles, with each clause tied to the case that motivates it — that gets as many of 1 to 8 as possible? Tell me honestly which verdicts it can't accommodate and which of my verdicts you'd want me to drop. Test my 'change less' rule against the cases and either repair it or scrap it, and say what it costs. Then give me the verdict on the tides case, and on two cases I haven't listed: (a) a theory that's completely true but only about a tiny bit of the world — a perfect and exhaustive theory of the tides on one single beach — vs Newtonian mechanics; (b) a complete, error-free theory of caloric fluid, i.e. right about everything concerning something that doesn't exist, vs a vague but roughly correct theory of something that does.

One last thing: a friend told me there's a proof that this whole project can't work — that once a theory is false, comparisons of closeness to truth collapse or can only be made when one theory entails the other. If that's roughly right, say so in plain terms, and tell me which of my verdicts it forces me to give up.
