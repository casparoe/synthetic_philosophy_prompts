It's me in the philosophy department plus a cognitive neuroscience group who are sympathetic but keep asking what the conceptual payoff is meant to be.

Where I'm starting from. The debate about whether phenomenal consciousness outruns cognitive access keeps getting run as a yes/no question with essentially one kind of evidence: show people more than they can report, ask them to report, then argue about what the failure to report means. But report is the end of a long chain — selective attention, encoding into working memory, some metacognitive read-out, a willingness to say you saw something when you're not sure, language, task set — and a breakdown anywhere along that chain looks, from the outside, exactly like "it wasn't conscious". So I want to stop treating access as one thing with a threshold on it, and instead treat it as a profile across downstream consumer systems: for a given content, which systems can use it, and for what.

The bit that's mine, and that I'm least sure about: I want to add a marker I've been calling 'revision-sensitivity'. The question isn't can the subject report the content, or does the content influence behaviour, but could the subject have used it to catch and correct their own mistake — is the content available to whatever it is that notices you've gone wrong and says no, that wasn't it. My hunch is that this tracks phenomenal consciousness better than either report or mere priming does, because it needs the content to be there *for* the subject in a way that blind influence on a response doesn't.

Where global workspace theory comes in. As I understand it, the workspace story basically identifies a conscious content with one that's been globally broadcast to a whole set of consumer systems, and the evidence people cite for it — ignition, the late electrophysiological signature, the all-or-nothing character of whether a stimulus goes global — is evidence about broadcast. But if access is really a profile rather than a threshold, then the interesting cases are the partial-broadcast ones: priming survives, maybe some low-level response tendencies survive, but nothing is available for metacognitive or self-corrective use. And I think those cases are more trouble for the workspace picture than the overflow literature has let on, because it either has to say "that just wasn't broadcast enough, so not conscious" — in which case the threshold is doing all the work and needs to be stated without circularity — or it has to go graded, in which case it looses the clean identification and owes us an account of what a degree of consciousness even is.
