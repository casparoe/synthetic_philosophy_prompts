I'm a philosophy student writing up my notes from tutorial, and I want to anticipate a case my tutor is going to spring on us. Over the past few weeks he has given firm verdicts on the following, stipulating in each that there is no other way to save the five:

1. Bystander throws a switch, diverting a runaway trolley from five onto a track with one: permissible.
2. Bystander on a footbridge pushes a heavy stranger into the trolley's path to stop it: impermissible.
3. Surgeon kills a healthy patient to harvest organs for five: impermissible.
4. Bystander diverts the trolley onto a spur that loops back to the main track, where the one worker's body is what will stop the trolley before it reaches the five: impermissible.
5. As in (1), except that the worker on the side track is already doomed — a collapsed gantry has trapped him and will crush him in under a minute whatever anyone does: permissible, and he volunteered that this struck him as even easier than (1).
6. As in (1), except that the agent is the trolley's driver rather than a bystander: permissible; he says the role makes no difference.
7. As in (1), but one against one rather than five against one: impermissible.
8. As in (1), except that the agent innocently caused the runaway himself: still permissible.

The new case is exactly as in (4) — the looping spur, the worker's body the only thing that will stop the trolley — except that the worker is already doomed in the way described in (5): trapped by the gantry, dead within the minute whatever anyone does, immovable, and diverting the trolley costs him only seconds of life.

He has never said what principle he is running, and I'd like you to reason from the pattern of verdicts rather than from what the literature standardly says. Please do the following: set out the two or three rules that fit his entire track record — I can see that the doctrine of double effect with a no-means condition fits, but I want the serious alternatives, including any comparative rule on which what matters is whether the person harmed is left worse off than they would otherwise have been, and any rule turning on whether a person's body is recruited into your plan without their consent; say what each rule predicts about the new case; give your best guess at his verdict with the reasons for preferring it; tell me which of the earlier verdicts is doing the most work in narrowing the field; and name one further case, not among those above, that would separate the surviving rules most cleanly. No citations.
