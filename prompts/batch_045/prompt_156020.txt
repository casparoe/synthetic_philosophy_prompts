My thesis, so the objections have something to bite on. I'm trying to split the difference by splitting the desert claim itself: desert is determinate in direction and indeterminate in magnitude.

(1) There's a workable compatibilist desert-base. An agent is an apt target of blame iff she acted badly, the relevant moral reasons were epistemically available to her, her reasons-tracking machinery was working well enough that she would have responded to those reasons had she taken them to be decisive, and the action actually came through that machinery rather than bypassing it (no coercion, no seizure, no direct manipulation of the states that issue in action). Call that the gate.

(2) Nothing in the gate fixes how much hard treatment she deserves. There is no desert magnitude. Harm-mirroring needs a unit of suffering and a baseline of welfare; quality-of-will accounts need a way to make attitudes cardinal; ordinal proportionality only ever gives you "worse than."

(3) So once the gate is passed, the amount of hard treatment is a legislative question — deterrence, incapacitation, rehabilitation, cost — but constrained as to kind: the response has to be addressive, the sort of thing that can communicate censure to her as a reasons-responsive agent. That's my argument for why a tax, a fee, or a public-health quarantine order isn't punishment.
