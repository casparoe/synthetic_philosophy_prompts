Me and my mate got into a pub argument last night about insects that went on way to long. He's the sort of vegetarian who won't eat anything that might suffer, and his new thing is this: if there's even a small chance — say one or two per cent — that a beetle or an ant has some kind of inner life, then because there are completely absurd numbers of them (he was quoting something like twenty quadrillion ants, I've genuinely no idea whether that's right or he made it up on the spot), the expected suffering wrapped up in ordinary stuff like mowing a lawn, driving, or planting a field must dwarf everything going on in factory farms, and we should all be reorienting our lives around it. I pushed back with the obvious move, that at some point a tiny probability times a colossal headcount just looks like a mugging, but honestly neither of us had a position, we were just running the same two manoeuvres at each other.

So what I want isn't the answer, it's the spec. Before I go and read any of this literature I want to know what I should be demanding of a theory: a theory of which creatures to count, how confident to be about each, and how much whatever they've got counts for — something that could actually be fed into a decision rather than admired. Concrete case I want it tested on: another friend of ours runs a small cider orchard and is thinking about dropping an insecticide spray and eating a big fall in yield; whatever the theory is, it has to be able to say something usable about that, not just shrug. Give me the desiderata, what any adequate theory has to deliver, with the case or consideration behind each one, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are just what one camp wants.

Things I want covered, but don't only tick my list — tell me if any of mine are bogus. (1) It has to keep seperate three things I think we conflated all night: whether an insect counts at all, how many of them there are, and whether their lives are on balance good or bad for them — my mate slid straight from "they might matter" to "we're causing a catastrophe", and I don't see how that follows unless you also get a sign on the third. (2) It has to say what would count as evidence, given insects aren't just small mammals: no cortex-ish structure, tiny brains, but they learn, and some seem to keep behaving differently for a while after an injury — a rule that just demands vertebrate-looking hardware seems like cheating, and so does one that accepts anything that looks vaguely like pain behaviour. (3) It has to respect genuine uncertainty without collapsing it, so neither "unknown means zero" nor "unknown means fifty-fifty". (4) It has to be stateable before you know the numbers — it can't be a rule whose whole function is to spit out my mate's answer or mine. (5) It has to either bite the bullet on the fanaticism worry or explain why it doesn't bite here. If, as I suspect, some subset of the desiderata can't be satisfied together, say exactly which ones and why, because that's the bit I most want. Two or three lines at the end on how the rough families of views do — behaviour-based tests, hardware-based tests, the we-basically-can't-tell view — is plenty; no reading lists, I'll find those myself. Hard limit: 700 words, table's fine if that helps you stay inside it.
