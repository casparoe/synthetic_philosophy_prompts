Hey — I write novels (literary-ish crime fiction, three books out, halfway through a fourth) and my philosphy is basically one intro course I took fifteen years ago plus whatever I absorb in arguments with my writing group, so please keep the jargon light on your side and put the weight on mine.

Where I'm stuck: there's a character, Inspector Halloran, who's been through a lot. Minor figure in book one, now played by two different people (the audiobook reader and the actor in the TV adaptation), killed off in book three, brought back as a narrator in book four. And at a panel last month someone asked whether the version of him that shows up in the tie-in game — where he's basically a procedurally generated dialogue system that never says the same line twice — is really the same character. Everyone had an opinion and none of them were about the same thing. The way I handle this in my own outlines is to separate a character's slot (what he does in the plot: he's the obstructive authority figure, he withholds the file, he's the one the protagonist has to go around) from his texture (the prose voice, the smells he notices, the way his interiority reads on the page in deep POV). Recast the actor, or hand him over to a machine, and the slot can stay perfectly intact while the texture is completely different. That sounds to me like something I half-remember from that course — the idea that mental states are functional states, definable by their job rather than by what they're made of, and so capable of being built out of totally different hardware, which is supposedly why you can't just identify pain with one particular brain state and why the creature-with-different-hardware example everyone repeats is meant to be devastating. So here's the ask: restate that whole position — functionalism, the multiple-realizability argument against identifying mental states with physical states, and whatever the strongest thing people say against it is — as carefully and unflatteringly as you can in my vocabulary: slots and textures, casting and recasting, adaptation between media, character bibles, arcs, reader uptake, authorial say-so, deep POV and interiority, retcons.

Then give me the residue report: what comes over cleanly, what only comes over if you bend it (and tell me how you bent it), and what in the philosophy has no counterpart at all in how I think about characters. Three things I'm especially curious about, though don't let them crowd out anything bigger I'm missing: (a) in my world nothing pushes back on a character the way the physical world pushes back on a person, so inputs and outputs are kind of negotiated between me and the reader — does that gut the causal part of a functional definition, or can it be rebuilt out of what the reader will accept? (b) whether the same character survives a recast is, in my trade, at bottom a question about convention and uptake, and I have a suspicion that translating multiple realizability into that quietly turns the philosophy dispute into a dispute about what were willing to count, which would mean the translation is doing less work than it looks like it's doing; (c) the texture half of my distinction — the felt, sensory, what-it's-like stuff I write from inside a character's head — seems to me to be exactly what a slot-based definition leaves out, and I can't tell whether that's because my craft vocabulary cheats by simply letting me stipulate interiority whenever it's convenient, or because it's a genuine hole in the position. Pick a side on each and argue for it, dont just tell me both are defensible.
