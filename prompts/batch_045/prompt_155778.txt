I work on the metaphysics of mind and have been using Dennett's tower of four kinds of minds — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian — as a framework for thinking about what kinds of behavioural flexibility a system can have. A colleague has an argument that the tower is really only one storey tall, and I cannot locate the mistake. Here it is, as I have reconstructed it.

(1) To ask what kind of mind a creature has is to ask what process actually does the work of producing its adaptive behaviour — the machinery that causally generates the flexibility you observe. The slogan is that kinds of minds are kinds of causal processes.

(2) A Popperian creature's prescreening of candidate actions is itself carried out by some mechanism. That mechanism cannot be another Popperian creature, on pain of regress; and Dennett himself insists that the homunculi populating the inner environment are dumb — at best Skinnerian devices, and ideally hard-wired ones.

(3) So the machinery that does the work in a Popperian creature is Skinnerian at best: differential retention of candidates that have actually been produced inside the head, scored by an internal signal.

(4) The same holds a fortiori for a Gregorian creature. Language and other mind-tools enlarge the pool of candidates and make them easier to score, but the scoring is still done by (3)'s machinery; and the tools themselves were shaped by selection over tokens that actually occurred — in public, or in other heads.

(5) Hence every creature above the Skinnerian level is, at the level of the machinery that does the work, Skinnerian. And Skinnerian plasticity is itself a product of Darwinian selection: it differs from hard-wired design only in how much information is written in during an individual life and in how fast the loop runs.

(6) So the tower collapses into a single kind, Darwinian, with the four kinds being Darwinian mechanisms running at four timescales.

(7) The argument generalises: nothing that is implemented by lower-level machinery is thereby real. So a chess program does not really represent anything, no system has beliefs and desires, and human deliberation is operant conditioning. That is absurd — but my colleague says the absurdity is mine, since I am the one who wants the higher levels to be real while denying that they do any work.

I want, if I can, to keep three things: that a creature able to reject candidates it has never executed is genuinely different from one that must blunder in the world; that the tower is cumulative, each kind a platform the next is built on; and that none of this requires a smart homunculus. I am least attached to the Gregorian level's being a distinct kind.

If your diagnosis is an equivocation about levels — implementation versus the space of designs a creature can search — I also want it to tell me what to say about two cases he presses. First, a creature with no innate inner environment that acquires one wholesale from culture, by internalising the speech others address to it: Gregorian without ever having been Popperian, which the cumulative reading seems to forbid. Second, a system that generates candidate plans in natural language, filters them by predicting the text that would follow each, and executes the survivor: never shaped by the world, yet screening candidates before acting.

Which step do you reject, and why did it look compulsory? Price the alternatives: what do I lose by giving up (1), as against (3), (4), or (5)? And whatever the tower survives as — a classification of mechanisms, of what a creature's behaviour can be sensitive to, of the information available to it before it acts, or of something else — say what, on your reading, it is no longer able to tell us.
