me and a friend spent like two hours last night arguing about Hofstadter's knob-twiddling picture of creativity and we didnt get anywhere. He says every creative act is just moving around inside some pre-given space of variations, twisting a bunch of knobs (pitch, rhythm, tempo, level of detail, whatever), and that the genius is in which settings you pick. I said that's circular: half the time you don't have the space until somebody's already made the thing, so "picking a setting" is doing no work.

So let's test it on something dead simple. Take the tune of Twinkle Twinkle Little Star as your theme. Here are seven things someone might do to it:

1. play it up a major third
2. keep every pitch and duration but put it in 5/4 so the barlines cut across the phrases
3. reharmonize it over ii-V-I turnarounds, landing the melody notes on 9ths and 13ths
4. ornament every note with trills and turns so the skeleton stays audible under a dense surface
5. invert it, reflecting every interval about the starting note
6. swap the parameters: let the tune's durations become the pitches of a new line, and let its pitch-contour become the rhythm
7. don't vary the tune at all — vary the rule. Twinkle's procedure is roughly "phrase, phrase-again-with-a-tail, then something contrasting, then home"; write a piece that runs that procedure on a tune you invent, and then runs it again on its own output

Rate each one 0-10 for how much genuine creativity it shows, and sort them into three tiers: (a) knob-setting inside an inherited space, (b) knob-discovery — finding a knob that wasn't in the space before, (c) space-redrawing — where the axes themselves move, so what used to be a combination of settings is now one setting, or one setting is now several.

Two more things, because these were the bits we actually got stuck on. First: for each item give me a fraction, like 0.7, for how much of the creative credit belongs to the space you inherited versus the particular coordinates someone chose. Second: my friend's fallback was that some knobs are "deep" — turning them changes which other knobs exist or what range they have. Say which of the seven touch a deep knob, and whether depth as such adds any creativity or just conceals a pile of ordinary knob-setting inside one move.
