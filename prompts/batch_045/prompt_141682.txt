My flatmate and I got into it again last night after our metaphysics seminar – I'm TA-ing the first-year course this term and I want to get my own house in order before I have to write the handout on persistence – and somewhere around the third cup of tea I realised I don't actually know whether the package I've assembled holds together. Roughly where I've landed: (1) I'm a perdurantist about ordinary objects – the worm view, not the stage view – and basically my only reason is the argument from temporary intrinsics, i.e. I can't see how one and the same thing could be bent and straight, or how anything could genuinely change at all, unless the conflicting properties get spread across different temporal parts of something. (2) But I want it to be *me*, the whole worm and not some momentary person-stage, who is sitting here typing, who is tired right now, and who will be in Lisbon in April; I've never liked the stage theorist's way of handling that, where 'I' just picks out a slice. (3) I think persistence works the same way for every sortal – whatever fixes how long a bronze statue or a river persists fixes how long I persist, and there's no special metaphysics of persons waiting in the wings. I've started calling this my no-exceptions rule. (4) At the same time, on survival I'm a psychological continuity person: in the fission case I want to say neither of the two is identical to me but that it's as good as ordinary survival anyway, and that what I care about is the continuity rather than the identity. (5) I'm an eternalist – all times equally real – though I'll admit I still have the gut feeling that the present is metaphysically special, which I don't think I actually believe. (6) I think that on my view nothing ever literally goes out of existence, things just have a last temporal part, and I want to keep that *and* keep saying that my death will be a real event that really is bad for me. And (7) I think the endurantism/perdurantism dispute is a genuine metaphysical disagreement, not two notations for one set of facts.

The two things my flatmate threw at me were that (2) is already inconsistent with (1) and (3) – something about how, if my only reason for being a perdurantist is that conflicting properties have to get spread across parts, then the thing that's tired at 2am should be a stage and not the whole worm – and that (6) can't be had both ways, because if I'm a four-dimensional thing that never stops existing then my death can't be the kind of event that's bad for me in the way I obviously think it is. I dont see either of them, partly because I thought I had a fix for the first: the worm 'deputises' its current stage to be the thing that has the present-tense properties and does the believing and the self-ascribing, while the worm itself is what bears the long-run ones and what literally persists. But I have no idea whether that fix is the standard move or just stage theory with extra steps, and I have a horrible feeling it quietly undercuts the argument in (1) that got me into perdurantism in the first place.

So: audit rather than lecture, please. Which of (1)–(7) are genuinely in tension, which only look like it and dissolve once you draw some distinction I'm missing, and for each real one, what exactly would have to give and what would giving it up cost me? Please rank them by severity instead of flagging all of them as equally bad, say whether the deputising move actually survives scrutiny, and tell me which single revision – dropping or weakening just one commitment – would clean up the most. I'd definately rather lose one thing than quietly revise four without noticing.
