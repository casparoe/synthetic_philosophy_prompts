i keep reading eval writeups and audit reports that say things like "the model believes the user is a security researcher" or "the system doesn't believe its own disclaimer", and i genuinely cannot tell what would make sentences of that shape true or false, or whether they're just a convenient way of talking that happens to be load-bearing in the report. so before i adopt any account of when it's correct to attribute a belief to a neural network, i want the specification.

fix the purpose as: an account good enough to (1) decide whether a claim of that shape in an eval report is well founded or hand-waving, (2) tell me when a system's stated uncertainty is something i should defer to versus something i should reverse-engineer, and (3) say when an intervention has changed what the system believes as opposed to merely editing what it prints. NOT good enough to settle whether it's conscious, and not good enough to settle moral status. if a requirement only matters for those questions, i want it marked as out of scope rather than argued for.

write the desiderata list: what any such account has to deliver, with the motivating case behind each. here are the case shapes i want handled — swap in better versions if you have them, but don't dodge the shape:

- the scratchpad: the model writes out reasoning that reliably tracks the truth, then the tokens are discarded before the final answer and nothing in the weights seems to "endorse" them. did it believe what it wrote?
- the character: a system fine-tuned to imitate a particular fictional detective says "i believe the butler did it", in character, when prompted that way. attribution to the system, to the character, or to nobody?
- the untaught truth: the system correctly tracks a fact that was never in its training data and never stated by anyone — say something about a region it only ever encountered as tokenized coordinates. there's no causal chain from the fact to the state, only from the world to the corpus to a compression. what causal requirement, if any, is a theory allowed to impose here?
- split attribution: identical weights, two system prompts, contradictory dispositions across contexts, no memory between them. one believer, many believers, or one believer per context window?
- the alien mechanism: attributing belief that P is by far the best predictor of behavior, but the thing producing the behavior is nothing like a belief — a giant nearest-neighbour lookup over a compressed world model, or something that effectively learns P by gradient descent during inference. predictive success with the wrong causal-explanatory role.
- saturation: a falsehood is universally asserted in the training distribution and the architecture can't represent its negation. is the system P-believing or merely P-saturated, and how would we tell the difference?
- self-reference: it has a state that tracks whether it's being evaluated or retrained, and it behaves differently when you tell it so. must a theory permit beliefs about the system's own situation?
- merged models: two fine-tunes averaged together into one set of weights. who, if anyone, has the belief?
- feedback (the one i care most about): you publish that the system believes P, and later versions are trained on reports like yours, including on their own self-reports. the attribution becomes true partly because you made it. is that a counterexample to the attribution, or just how attribution works when the object can read you?

requirements to sort through, and i want you to decide which are real desiderata and which are just the prejudices of one camp: predictive payoff; playing the right causal-explanatory role in the system; being determinately wrong when wrong; whether there has to be a fact of the matter at all, or whether tolerated indeterminacy is acceptable; allowing false beliefs; allowing latent beliefs with no behavioral signature that only a probe finds; degrees of confidence; beliefs about unencountered things; beliefs the system would sincerely deny on being asked; whether the belief/desire decomposition itself survives here or whether it's up for grabs; stability under redescribing where the system ends (is the prompt part of the believer? the retrieval corpus? the fine-tuning pipeline? the human who writes the prompt?); supporting counterfactuals and intervention; working without knowing the architecture; independence from the interpreter's interests; and whether the account must leave open the possibility that in some cases there's simply nothing to attribute.

then three things i most want: (a) which single requirement, if dropped, would make the whole problem collapse into something trivially easy — that tells me where the real content is; (b) which pair of desiderata you think cannot be jointly satisfied, with the case that shows it — my guess is something around determinacy versus relativity to a chosen system boundary, or around attributions being able to be flatly wrong versus attributions earning their keep purely by usefulness, but argue it; (c) which requirements are non-negotiable given the purpose i fixed, and which are negotiable preferences of particular camps (the instrumentalist intentional-stance people, the mechanistic-interpretability people who want belief to be a feature or circuit doing causal work, the realists about internal representations, the people who think the whole question is malformed).

and briefly — a short paragraph each, no scorecard table, i'll do the scoring myself — how those last three or four camps do against the list. please skip the chinese room and the it's-just-autocomplete versus it-really-understands back and forth; i've read plenty of that and it doesn't touch the cases above.
