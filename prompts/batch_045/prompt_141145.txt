I'm taking a formal social choice course this term, and we did the doctrinal paradox — majority voting on the premises and on the conclusion come apart, so the court's output is inconsistent. I've since noticed something in real committee work that has the same smell but isn't quite that, and I can't find a name for it.

Two cases. On my college's student-union finance committee (nine members, unchanged all year) we carried in Michaelmas, 5–4, a motion to fund the debating society's travel. In Hilary we carried, 5–4, a motion to fund the jazz band's travel, and on the same agenda we defeated, 5–4, a motion to fund both. Nobody's mind changed between the meetings: the four members who voted for both grants also voted for the joint motion, and everyone who voted against the joint motion voted against at least one of the two grants. Each meeting's outcome is coherent on its own — the Hilary minutes assert only that one grant is made and that not both are — and every individual member's record is coherent. The contradiction lives entirely in the year's record taken together. The same shape turned up in my department's curriculum committee, except that the earlier decision was made by last year's membership and the later, conflicting one by a partly new membership with two replacements, again with no individual reversing themselves.

The version we covered has everything on the table at one moment, and the diagnosis there is about premise-based versus conclusion-based procedure on a fixed agenda. Here every snapshot is fine, and it is the growth of the agenda — the arrival of a second decision that makes a connection visible for the first time — that does the work. So: does this have a name of its own, or is it only the familiar paradox with a time index? I don't want a label dropped on me; I want the carving. Specifically: how does it differ from (a) the ordinary discursive dilemma, (b) a group simply changing its mind, (c) the path-dependence and order-dependence results where the agenda is fixed but the sequence of votes varies, and (d) my second case, where the membership turned over — is that the same phenomenon or a different one? And does the diagnosis change if the earlier decision was formally reopened and the rescission motion failed too?
