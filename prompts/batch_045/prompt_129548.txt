I am preparing the final session of a graduate seminar on classical pragmatism, and I want to close it with a case that Peirce and James could not have anticipated, handled as a derivation rather than a pastiche. I am not interested in how either man would have sounded if he were dropped into the present; I want to know what the machinery of his account of truth, taken at its word, commits him to once it is applied to an epistemic situation of a kind that did not exist in his lifetime.

The situation, which I will set out so that no external lookup is needed. Contemporary machine learning systems produce assertoric output at scale — answers, summaries, proofs, predictions — and are increasingly deferred to as authorities. They have no beliefs, no doubts, no purposes, no expectations that experience can frustrate; they are not agents who set out from an irritation of doubt and arrive at a settled belief under the compulsion of something they did not make. Now extend the case, not fancifully but a little: suppose that within a few decades much of scientific inquiry is conducted by such systems — hypotheses proposed, experiments designed and run, results integrated — and suppose the process converges and stays converged, with the correction driving it largely machine-to-machine, and with human beings unable in practice to survey the reasoning (too long, too fast, too alien in form) but with excellent inductive grounds for accepting the outputs, which keep paying off in prediction and control. Call this the terminal-inquiry case.

Both men are dead long before any of this: James's account makes truth a matter of what it is expedient for us to believe, the true ideas being those we can assimilate, corroborate and verify in experience, while insisting all the while that truth must also agree with realities we do not make; Peirce's makes truth the opinion that inquiry, pushed far enough, would be fated to settle on, and the real whatever that opinion would represent, with the proviso — he is explicit about it — that truth cannot consist in any actual satisfaction or actual agreement, but only in what would be arrived at at the ideal limit.

So I want the following worked out, for each of them separately before any comparison. First, Peirce: his definition fixes a limit of inquiry, but does anything in it fix who belongs to the community whose final opinion constitutes truth? If the last inquirers are not human, and their convergence is stable and real, is that opinion the real — and if Peirce has resources to say no, exactly which resources, and what work is the counterfactual doing? What distinguishes a convergence that is genuinely truth-constitutive from a de facto one that merely never gets disrupted? Second, James: can there be truth where there is no believer? His account ties truth to what it pays a creature with purposes to accept; in the terminal-inquiry case the pay-off is undoubted but the accepting is done by us at second hand, and the settling is done by something with no stake in anything. Does his account then collapse into identifying truth with whatever it is expedient to defer to, or does his insistence on agreement with independent realities block that slide, and if so, at what cost to what was distinctive in his position?

Then the comparison, which is where the seminar actually needs to go. Where would the two of them really diverge here, and where does the standard contrast — convergence versus expediency, Peirce's realism against James's alleged psychologism — misdescribe one or both of them? Would Peirce, who was uneasy about sharing a label with James, find in this case the confirmation of his misgivings, or would he find that his own account has the more serious membership problem? Would James find the case congenial, or does it expose that his talk of expediency needs a subject in a way he never supplied? What would each have to add to his published account to handle the case at all, and how much of the account survives the addition? Finally, and most usefully for my purposes: classify the case for each theory as confirmatory, anomalous, or simply unanticipated, and say where each theory is genuinely silent rather than forcing it to speak.

Please derive rather than report: I can assemble the secondary literature myself, and I would rather you did not lean on it, since what I want is the argument from the definitions, with a clear flag wherever you have moved beyond what the texts themselves support.
