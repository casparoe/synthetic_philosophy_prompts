Before I take a position on free-floating reasons — reasons that exist without belonging to anyone — I want the specification any theory of them would have to meet. Fix the purpose as three jobs: saying what we owe the dead and to people who may never exist; underwriting impersonal comparisons of outcomes (that one state of affairs is better, or more to be regretted, than another); and feeding first-person deliberation without inflating what any individual is required to do. Adequacy is relative to those jobs, so say if one of them should be dropped from the spec.

Write the desiderata, each with the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark each as non-negotiable, a reasonable preference, or a prejudice of one camp. The cases I want handled at minimum: (i) an orphaned reason — a promise made to someone who then dies while the promised act is still performable, so whatever reason there was has lost its only bearer; (ii) an unhosted reason — a suffering that no agent can reach and none ever could have reached, which is a reason for no one, yet seems to be a reason that it stop; (iii) the one-bill-two-payers problem — if a million people each have a trivial reason against some minor harm, is there also one large unowned reason against it, and is the total counted once or twice; (iv) the idleness worry — an unowned reason must earn its keep by doing work no owned reason does (grounding what ought to happen, or what is to be regretted, or what we would have reason to do if we could), so say what that work is and what would make the posit mere decoration; (v) the epistemic question — how such reasons could be known at all, given that no one can deliberate from them. Someone will also tell me that a reason requires a possible responder; say whether that belongs on the list or is just one camp's starting point dressed as a requirement.

Then tell me which pair of desiderata you think cannot be jointly satisfied, which single requirement does the most to exclude candidate theories, and which desideratum you would drop. Commit: name the one you give up and defend the choice rather than leaving me the menu. Add one line each on how the desire-based, value-based, and constructivist families fare, but the criteria matter more to me than the scoring.

Return a JSON object with exactly these fields: purpose (the three jobs, as you would restate them); desiderata (an array of objects with id, requirement, motivating_case, status — one of non_negotiable, preference, camp_prejudice — and candidates_excluded); tensions (an array of objects with pair of ids, why_they_clash, and resolution_offered); most_exclusionary; dropped (the desideratum you reject and your defence); contenders (an array of family and one_line); confidence (your overall confidence in the list, and which two of your judgments you take to be most fragile).
