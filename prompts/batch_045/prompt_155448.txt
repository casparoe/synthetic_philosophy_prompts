Hey — I've been reading about Hacking's looping effects and Im stuck on something I cant quite get a gut-level grip on. The basic story I get: you classify people under a psychiatric kind, they come to see themselves / act differently in light of that, the kind drifts, lather rinse repeat.

Heres the bit im chewing on. Ive started calling a variant of this *anticipatory looping*: people who dont (yet) fall under a category shift their behavior because they expect that being classified would change them — someone reads about a diagnosis and starts pre-emptively steering toward (or away from) the person they think that diagnosis would make them. Its not the classified reacting to being classified; its the not-yet-classified reacting to a prediction about classification.

Two things I want straight. First, is this actually a different animal from ordinary looping, or is it just ordinary looping with a longer causal chain — and if its the latter, does it collapse into something boring like self-fulfilling prophecy / demand characteristics? Second, does it change the philosophical upshot? My vague sense is that it cuts against the argument people run from looping to "psychiatric kinds aren't like natural kinds, because the instances talk back": in anticipatory looping the people doing the reacting aren't instances yet, so the usual story about what destabilises the kind seems to not apply. But I also half think that makes things worse for the kind, not better, and I can't tell which.

Please don't just restate the standard looping story at me — I want to know where my distinction is confused, what the real work is being done by, and whether it matters. Also tell me what would change your mind here, and where your reasoning is most likely to go wrong.
