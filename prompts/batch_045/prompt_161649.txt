So I've more or less talked myself into the view that cumulative culture is genuinely Darwinian and not just metaphorically so: know-how piles up across generations because population-level selection among heritable variants does real explanatory work, and not because each individual learner independently reconstructs the same good solution. I don't care whether anyone likes the word meme, I care whether the machinery earns its keep.

Here's a test I've been kicking around that might just be a bad test. Call it the Weismann test: a system counts as Darwinian, I want to say, only if there's some barrier-like separation between the variants that get transmitted and the modifications an individual acquires during its lifetime, so that what gets inherited isn't simply the improvements the last generation worked out. Culture obviously leaks here — someone figures out a better way and passes the improvement straight on — but I think lots of transmission does have barrier structure (recipes copied word for word, tunes fixed by recording, blueprints). So my view is patchy on purpose: Darwinian where the barrier holds, guided variation everywhere else.

What would count against it? I want defeaters ranked fatal to merely awkward, and I want you to separate what a defender of the view would itself concede counts against it from what it would wave away. Two worries of my own: one, that I've defined selection so loosely that "the variants that spread are the variants that spread" ends up doing all the work and the view can't lose; two, that some purely non-selective model — copy the most common variant, copy the most prestigious person, neutral drift plus population size — predicts the actual frequency distributions better on the cases everyone treats as the showpieces of cumulative culture. I'd personally be moved most by the second, especially if it also explained why the same solution keeps turning up independently in disconnected groups. Also: if the Weismann test is a bad way to draw the line, say so, and say what should replace it.
