My reading group has been going round in circles on van Fraassen's Reflection principle for about three months and literally nobody has moved an inch. One half thinks the Dutch-strategy argument settles it — violate the principle and a bookie can guarantee you lose money, so what's left to discuss. The other half thinks the counterexamples settle it: agents who know they'll forget things, agents who know they'll be drugged or brainwashed into some false belief, Sleeping-Beauty-style cases where you're certain now that your later credence in A will be 1/3 while your current one is 1/2. Everyone knows all the same cases and all the same moves, and they just assert past each other.

My hypothesis is that it isn't one disagreement. Part of it looks verbal — 'Reflection' doing duty both for a near-trivial fact about conditional probabilities over the partition of your own future credences and for a substantive norm about deferring to your later self — and part looks like a hidden formal question: whether self-locating propositions can be objects of conditional credence at all, which would make some of the standard counterexamples badly formed rather than counter. But maybe the real thing underneath is a clash about what counts as evidence here, pragmatic Dutch-book arguments versus intuitions about cases.

What I want you to engage with rather than rehearse is two repairs we came up with ourselves. (1) De-biased Reflection: if I now know the exact distortion my later self will apply — say a drug I know will make me double the weight of whatever evidence I recieve — then Reflect not on 'my later credence in A is x' but on 'the evidence that would produce x in a distorted me', treating the later credence as a noisy signal to be inverted. (2) Plan Reflection: stop constraining two credence functions at different times and constrain instead the whole policy mapping total evidence to credences, so the requirement is that your current policy not be one you'd want to revise. Does either dissolve the stalemate or just relocate it?

So: what kind of impasse is this, really — verbal, different questions under one name, bedrock disagreement about what a rationality constraint is for, or just hard? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a formalism, a distinction, a new case, a change of question? Please seperate what's actually settled among people who work on this from what's live controversy, don't smooth a contested claim into the consensus view, and flag where you're extrapolating rather than reporting. I don't need citations.
