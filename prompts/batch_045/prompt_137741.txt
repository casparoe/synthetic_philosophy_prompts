Where she is, so you don't have to guess. Harsanyi's aggregation theorem: fixed set of people, each person's preferences over lotteries satisfy the vNM axioms, the social ordering over the same lotteries also satisfies the vNM axioms, and if every individual is indifferent between two prospects then society is too — from which you get that society ranks prospects by Σ wᵢ·E[uᵢ], a weighted sum of individual expected utilities. Two usual complaints: (a) the social observer's independence axiom already bakes in unconcern with fairness — a linear functional can't prefer a coin flip over handing an indivisible good to one of two equally deserving people, which is basically Diamond's objection; (b) individual vNM utilities are only fixed up to person-specific positive affine transformations, so the weights don't buy you interpersonal comparability unless you smuggle in a normalization. (As I understand it Harsanyi himself leaned on a separate veil-of-ignorance / equiprobability move to get equal weights, and I want to press her on whether the bare theorem is doing any work.)

Her reply. She splits the uncertainty in a social prospect into uncertainty about *states of nature* (will the dam hold) and uncertainty about *identity* (who ends up with which life). Her axiom: the social ordering must satisfy vNM independence with respect to state-uncertainty, but is allowed to violate it for identity-uncertainty — that's the only place, she says, where a fairness concern is legitimate rather than mere risk-preference on the observer's part. Her claimed "generalized Harsanyi representation" is V(p) = Σ wᵢ·E[uᵢ] − λ·MAD, where MAD is the mean absolute deviation of the individuals' expected utilities from their weighted mean, and λ ≥ 0. λ = 0 recovers Harsanyi's weighted sum; λ > 0 reproduces the coin-flip preference, since the gamble has zero dispersion and "give it to A" has some. To make the cross-person comparison in the MAD term legitimate she proposes "anchor normalization": rescale each person's utility function so that everyone's utility gap between two fixed anchor outcomes (she uses a life of constant severe pain and a life of mild contentment) equals 1, and she claims λ is then uniquely identified.

What I want from the questions:

My worry, which I don't want to have to argue myself: in the veil-of-ignorance model who-you-are just *is* another state, so "independence for states but not for identities" looks like a preference over the *source* of uncertainty rather than over prospects. Give me the question that makes her do the work, plus a follow-up keyed to what I assume she'll say (that identity is special because fairness is about persons, not states) and then a second follow-up asking what makes a partition of states identity-constituting.
