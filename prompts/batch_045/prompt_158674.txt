I write Monte Carlo particle-transport code for a living, so I spend my days counting events: billions of collisions per simulated second, each one a few floating-point numbers. I have been reading about panpsychism on the side, and I want to pin down one number rather than collect more readings.

The claim I want a credence on: conditional on panpsychism being true in its intrinsic-nature form — every fundamental physical entity has a phenomenal character, not merely relational-dispositional properties — the bulk of the suffering in the observable universe is not realized by organisms but by everything else: microphysical interaction events, radiative processes, accretion disks, and the rest, simply because their event counts exceed the number of organism-moments by tens of orders of magnitude.

By resolution I mean this. Suppose we had, first, a settled fundamental physics; second, a mapping from physical states to phenomenal states with a privileged zero point and a scale; and third, an agreed rule for aggregating valenced states into a total — summing all of them, versus averaging, versus a threshold below which a state carries no moral weight, versus giving lexical priority to the worst single episode. Given those three we could compute, to within an order of magnitude, where the mass of negative valence sits. Give me your credence that it comes out on everything else, under the summing reading, with the threshold reading and the lexical reading counted as ways the claim fails.

Decompose it. At minimum:

1. whether valence is determinate and additive at the fundamental scale at all, rather than simply undefined for a particle interaction — I care about the difference between undefined and merely tiny, the way a denormal float is small but is not zero;
2. given that it is, whether per-event valence falls off slowly enough with event size or complexity that the cardinality advantage survives the scaling, so that intensity, structure, or integration does not outweigh pure count;
3. whether valence has a privileged zero and units in the first place, so that summing it across heterogeneous events is even well-defined, and whether the global net comes out near zero by something like a conservation law, which would make gross magnitude rather than net the operative quantity;
4. whether suffering requires a subject, a welfare, or a unified experiencing entity, so that a cloud of micro-valences belonging to no one does not add up to anything ethically relevant;
5. whether the aggregative rule is the totalist one rather than averaging, threshold-gated, or peak-sensitive.

Give a number and one line of justification for each term, show how they combine into your headline figure, and tell me which single term moves the final number most if you swing it.

Add any term you think I have missed. One I cannot shake: if what is being summed is a quantity defined over events, the total looks dependent on how spacetime is sliced, and I do not know whether that makes the question ill-posed or merely demands the right formalism.
