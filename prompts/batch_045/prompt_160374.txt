I am a retired geochemist, lately reading philosophy of science in a rather unsystematic way, and I would like to know whether a pattern I keep recalling from my own former field has a name.

Two instances. For some thirty years a certain family of isotope ratios refused to fit the accepted model; they were written off as contamination, and one learned not to report them. When a new model appeared, not constructed with those ratios in mind, the same numbers were re-reduced with better corrections and put forward among the strongest reasons to accept it. Second: a morphology described and illustrated repeatedly in the older literature was treated as noise until a theory arrived that made it the expected signature, after which it was cited as striking confirmation.

What interests me is that in neither case was the evidence simply known in advance and later accommodated. Before the theory it was recorded but discounted, or recorded in a vocabulary in which it was not yet the sort of thing one could predict; afterwards it was re-measured and re-described, promoted from artifact to datum, and it was the reconstituted item that confirmed. I gather that philosophers are suspicious of confirmation by evidence already in hand, on the ground that such evidence cannot raise a theory's probability. But that worry seems to picture the evidence as fully formed and already firmly believed, merely waiting to be explained. In my cases the change is partly in the evidence itself.

Does this configuration have a name of its own? If it resembles an established concept, I should want to know exactly where the resemblance stops: how these cases differ from it, not only what they share with it. A bare label, or a reassurance that this is only the familiar difficulty about old evidence read at a slightly wider angle, would not tell me what I am after. If nothing established fits, please coin a term, define it, and set out the boundary cases that would decide whether a given instance belongs to it.
