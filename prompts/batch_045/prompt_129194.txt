I'm writing a short internal piece for the engineers I work with about how to talk about what is inside the models we train, and I keep needing a distinction I can't find a name for. I'd be grateful for help carving it.

The distinction, in my own words. When people want to show that a network represents some feature X, they tend to offer one of two kinds of evidence, and in my own experiments these come apart constantly.

The first is read evidence. From the internal state alone I can predict, well above chance, whether the system's output will treat its input as an X. I trained a small classifier on activations from a model whose labels I already knew, got near-perfect prediction from a single direction, and was briefly convinced the model had learned the concept.

The second is write evidence. I push the internal state along some direction and the system's behavior changes in the way I would expect if it had come to treat the input as an X. Often, though, the same push changes a pile of other things too — outputs with nothing to do with X, sometimes more than it changes the X behavior.

Some states are good for predicting and useless for steering. Some are the reverse. A few do both, and those are the only ones that actually convince me. And I keep watching the argument slide: the two kinds of evidence get cited interchangeably, and when one is challenged the other is quietly substituted in its place — the steering doesn't work, well, but look, it decodes — so that the claim that the network represents X never rests on a fixed criterion for very long.

Three questions.

1. Is there an established name for the two kinds of evidence, or for the gap between them? I have been privately calling the read-good states markers and the write-good states handles, and using leakage for the collateral changes a write produces; I want to know whether there is vocabulary I should be using instead, and whether the three-way split I am drawing has a precedent somewhere I haven't looked.

2. Does the gap tell us anything about whether these systems really have representations? I can't tell which way it cuts. A handle that changes behavior looks like the strongest possible evidence of a causal role; but a handle that changes everything may be nothing but a volume knob, and a marker that predicts perfectly may be the system's own forecast of what it is about to say rather than a record of anything it noticed.

3. If nothing established fits, I would like a coinage with a definition and, more importantly, with boundary cases: what a state would have to be like to count as a representation on your account, what would clearly fail to count, and how the awkward middle cases should be settled — marker but not handle, handle with heavy leakage, both but only after further training on a new objective, and the case where read and write evidence point at different features entirely.

I'm not after a literature survey — I couldn't chase references down anyway — and I don't want a name if a name is all there is. What I want to know is whether the distinction is doing real work, or whether I have mistaken a difference in my measurement tools for a difference in the system. Please structure your reply in three short parts corresponding to my questions, and keep the whole thing under 600 words.
