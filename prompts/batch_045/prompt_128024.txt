A senior colleague of mine, who works on pragmatics, has given me a run of verdicts on utterance cases over the years, and I am trying to work out what rule she is actually applying.

Her verdicts so far:

- 'Some of the students passed' — implicates that not all passed; the implicature is cancellable ('in fact all of them did') and forms no part of what is said.
- 'Can you tell me the time?' — what is said is a question about the hearer's ability; the request is a particularised conversational implicature derived from the maxims, not a further sense of the sentence and not a distinct illocutionary force.
- 'What a delightful afternoon', said during a downpour — the speaker means that the afternoon is wretched, by flouting the maxim of truthfulness; and she means it even if the hearer fails to notice that anything is up, so uptake cannot be a requirement on speaker meaning.
- A reference that praises a candidate's handwriting and punctuality and says nothing whatever about their philosophy — implicates that the candidate is no good at philosophy; the implicature would vanish if the same thing were said plainly, so she is untroubled by detachability in that kind of case.
- 'Would you mind closing the door?' — she concedes that the request reading is now so routine that nobody reasons from a cooperative principle, but holds that this does not damage Grice's calculability claim, since the derivation need not be conscious.

The new case. A cabinet minister knows that one particular journalist treats everything she says as meaning its opposite. She says to him, in a corridor, 'I have complete confidence in the Health Secretary', intending him to conclude that she has doubts — but intending him to believe he has worked that out for himself by seeing through her, rather than intending him to recognise that she is conveying it. She says the same sentence in the same breath to an intern beside him who has no such policy, and the journalist does in fact draw the conclusion she intended, in the way she intended.

What would she say is implicated here, and to whom? Give her verdict on the journalist and on the intern, set out the rule about implicature and speaker meaning she seems to be running, give at least one alternative rule that fits her five earlier verdicts just as well but comes apart on this one, and name the further case that would best separate the two. State how confident you are in each part of what you claim, and single out the claim you are least sure of.
