I spent forty years in experimental plasma physics and have only in retirement started reading philosophy of science with a small group, so I would be grateful for an answer in American English that avoids the shorthand of the field.

We are working through a book manuscript on laws of nature in which the author rules on a sequence of invented cosmoses. Her verdicts so far: a large cosmos containing many gold spheres, every one of them under a mile across, contains no law about gold spheres, only an accident of arrangement; a cosmos in which no signal ever propagates faster than a fixed speed, though nothing ever approaches that speed, does have that bound as a law even though the law has only negative instances; a cosmos consisting of exactly one particle moving uniformly forever is not law-governed at all; and a cosmos containing a single supercritical lump of fissile material that sits indefinitely without fissioning is not a genuine possibility, though it is unclear to me whether she thinks the mosaic of events rules it out or something over and above the mosaic does.

The case I have to present next week is one I invented, because it seemed to me to isolate the real disagreement. A cosmos whose entire history is a few dozen events involving two kinds of particle and one force. Two candidate dynamical rules, R and S, each generate that whole history from the same initial state, are equally compact by any measure I would have trusted in the laboratory, and are stated in equally natural vocabulary; they agree on every event that occurs and first diverge only at a step the cosmos never reaches, since it ends long before. There are no inhabitants, no records, and nothing outside it. In my old work we would have said the data underdetermine the model and gone to run another experiment; here no further experiment is possible.

Please give me: the two or three rules about what makes something a law that fit her four verdicts about equally well; what each predicts about my cosmos, both as to whether it has a law at all and as to whether that law is R, S, neither, or the part R and S share; your best guess at her verdict with the reason for preferring it; and the single further cosmos you would put to her to settle which rule she is really running. If her four verdicts genuinely underdetermine the case, say what she would be committing herself to with each answer. Please do not give me references or a reading list; I want the argument, not the bibliography.
