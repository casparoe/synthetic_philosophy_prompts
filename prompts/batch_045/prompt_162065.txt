I'm in the final year of my philosophy degree, taking a course on freedom and moral responsibility, and we've just finished the week on Frankfurt-style counterexamples to the principle of alternative possibilities. I found the seminar papers heavy going, so I also read a chapter in an introductory book on free will aimed at general readers, which summed the debate up like this:

Frankfurt's target is the principle of alternative possibilities, PAP: you are morally responsible for what you have done only if you could have done otherwise. In his example, a scientist called Black secretly installs a device in Jones's brain that monitors his neural activity and will force him to vote for Smith if it detects any sign that he is about to decide differently. In fact Jones votes for Smith on his own, the device never fires, and yet we still hold him responsible. So responsibility does not require alternative possibilities. What it requires is a certain structure in the agent's will: Jones acts on a desire that he wants to be the one that moves him, a second-order volition, which is the core of Frankfurt's positive account of freedom. That is why the example matters: it establishes that moral responsibility is compatible with determinism. The objection that Jones retains a mere flicker of freedom, because he could still have tried to vote differently or formed a different second-order volition, was answered long ago, and defenders of PAP have largely abandoned the principle.

Something here makes me uneasy — the way the example is supposed to bear on determinism, and the bit about second-order volitions — but I can't say where the chapter goes wrong or whether the mistakes matter. Our tutor also floated a variant I haven't seen in the reading: Black's device is not set to wait for any advance sign of how Jones will decide, but is wired so that the very neural events which would constitute Jones's deciding otherwise are themselves what trigger the override, making the intervention simultaneous with the decision rather than prior to it. She suggested that this gets round the objection that such cases need determinism, or an infallible predictor, to work at all.
