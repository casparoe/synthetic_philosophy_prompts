Hey — pretend you're a decision theorist who works on intertemporal choice and has also spent some time around labour economics. Do a bit of brainstorming before you commit to anything, and think it through carefully.

I want a thought experiment built to order. Its use: in a seminar I'm pushing back on the usual line that a positive pure rate of time preference is just an irrational bias — that a good in ten years is no less a good, so discounting it is only a failure of imagination or nerve. So I need a case where the impatient person comes out ahead.

Spec: two people identical in every respect — same period utility over money, same skills, same health, same beliefs, same outside options; both consistent exponential discounters; the only difference is the pure rate (one zero, the other something like 8% a year). The one with the positive pure rate has to end up strictly better off over the stretch of life in question, and better off by the standard the zero-discounter herself accepts: the undiscounted sum of period well-being, not by the impatient person's own lights. One paragraph, ordinary setting (a workplace, a tenancy, a clinic queue — no labs, no trolleys). No risk of death, no uncertainty, no changing tastes, no hyperbolic discounting, no commitment devices and no "she adopts a rule": the edge has to come from the rate itself, not from something bolted on top of it.

One hint, because the obvious version can't work: if utility is separable across periods and the options on the table don't depend on the person's own rate, then the zero-discounter is by definition maximizing the very quantity you're comparing with, so she can't lose. Your case therefore has to make the options themselves respond to her patience — say, a counterparty no better than a shrewd human who reads, correctly, how much she'll put up with. That's an allowed lever, but then say why the patient person can't simply imitate the impatient one.

Deliver: the scenario; both plans with actual numbers (the rate, the delay, the amounts) and the two undiscounted totals; which single feature is doing the work; what happens if you delete that feature; and the strongest objection a defender of zero pure time preference would make — the one that says your case cheats. If you don't think the spec can be met honestly, say that up front, then give me the closest honest case and what it does show.
