hey — I'm drafting a novel set in a city where the municipal government runs an entire directorate for citizens who can't make up their minds, and I need an in-world document I can paste more or less straight into chapter four. The Bureau of Determinate Choice (everyone calls it "the Bureau") holds that indifference is never a fact about the world, only a failure of self-knowledge: for any two options put before a citizen, a preference exists and the Bureau's job is to extract it. I want the document to be their public guidance leaflet — something like "So You Are Indifferent: Guidance for Applicants" — deadpan bureaucratic voice, numbered Q&As, around 700 words, with one short worked case appended at the end (I was thinking a woman choosing between two flats identical in rent, light and commute, who insists she genuinely doesn't mind).

The comedy has to come from getting the actual philosophy right rather than from zany bureaucracy, because the joke is meant to be on a position: the thought that a rational will has to act on a reason, so that no preference means no permissible action, and the ass in Buridan's example starves. Please work in, without ever naming or footnoting them: the move that any tiny cost reveals a preference — the Bureau's "Penny Rule," where an applicant who would pay a cent for A over B is declared to have preferred A all along, so that preference gets manufactured out of nothing; the difference between options that are exactly equally good and options that are merely on a par and not precisely comparable, which the Bureau collapses; the fact that an applicant's stated preference reverses depending on which option she saw second, which the Bureau treats as fraud rather than as evidence about her; the coin flip, and whether authorising one counts as acting for a reason or as giving up; and the option of simply not choosing, which the Bureau rules is itself a choice requiring its own preference statement. Also give the Bureau a motto, and let the leaflet mention, in passing and with no self-awareness, that its own staff canteen has served no lunch since the founding because the kitchen cannot establish a preference between two identical specials.

Keep the irony sustained: nobody in the document should notice anything is wrong. After the leaflet, add me maybe 150 words on where it takes sides in live arguments — whether the penny argument really establishes a preference, whether all indifference is really the same kind of thing, whether randomising is a rational tie-break or a confession that reasons have run out — and say which single objection from a philosopher who disagrees would most make you rewrite it, and why.
