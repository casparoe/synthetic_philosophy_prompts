I work as a software engineer — compilers and runtime systems, no formal training in philosophy or statistical mechanics beyond what I've picked up on my own — and I've developed a hobbyist interest in reversible and adiabatic computing, which is how I ended up in an argument about Maxwell's demon at a pub quiz a fortnight ago with two friends, one of whom does condensed-matter physics. I came away holding what I take to be the mainstream modern line, and I'd like it stress-tested. The view I hold is this: the demon is not defeated by the cost of finding things out, since measurement and the copying of a record can in principle be carried out reversibly and without a dissipation floor, but it is defeated by the cost of clearing its memory afterwards. Erasing a bit whose value you don't know dissipates at least kT ln 2, about 0.018 eV or 3×10⁻²¹ J at room temperature; a logically reversible computation has no irreducible thermodynamic floor at all; therefore every unavoidable cost in computing is a cost of throwing information away rather than of moving or acquiring it. I also take the modern feedback-control formulation — where the work you can extract from a system using a measurement is bounded by roughly kT times the mutual information between measurement and system — to be the same claim in better notation, because the correlation you exploit has to be re-established, and the record cleared, before the next cycle.

What I want is an honest map of what would count against this, and I'd like you to engage with the specific lines of attack I've half-worked out rather than only the standard ones. First: kT ln 2 is surely the cost for a symmetric one-bit memory, but a memory whose two logical states have wildly different numbers of microstates ought to have a different bound, and I have the impression that erasing into a state with far more microstates can be made arbitrarily cheap — possibly free — which looks like it would let a demon with the right memory run a closed cycle for nothing. Second, I vaguely recall a result arguing that erasure need carry no energy cost at all because the required entropy increase can be paid for in a different conserved quantity, angular momentum being the example, and I can't tell whether that refutes my view or only a sloppy statement of it. Third, and this is the one that bothers me most: erasing a state you already know costs nothing — you can just rotate it — and the demon does know what's in its memory immediately after measuring. So why can't it rotate the bit back to its standard state for free, and if the answer is that the information is merely shoved into the record or into the control machinery, where exactly does the bill finally land once you insist that the demon start each cycle in a standard state and reuse the same finite hardware? I'd also like your assessment of three further loose ends: claims from non-equilibrium statistical mechanics that logical and thermodynamic reversibility have no a priori link, so that a logically irreversible step can in principle be thermodynamically reversible; the fact that the bound is approached only quasi-statically and on average, whereas fluctuation theorems permit individual trajectories to come in under it, which might matter for a demon in a small system running for a finite time; and the possibility of running the demon off a stock of pre-existing correlation or shared randomness as a kind of fuel, rather than off a fresh measurement each cycle.

Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be explicit about which ones a committed defender of the Landauer line would itself concede as counting against the view, as opposed to what only its critics allege. I'm also worried about a structural feature of the position: in practice every proposed demon that seems to work gets diagnosed by finding some entropy it exported somewhere — into a larger memory, into the production of blank tape, into the reset of the controller — and that diagnostic looks like it could be applied to any conceivable counterexample, which would make the claim unfalsifiable in the way that conspiracy theories are. Tell me honestly whether it has been qualified into that shape, and whether that is or isn't a defect for a claim of this kind. What would move me most, I should say, is a fully specified closed cycle — the memory, the bath, the protocol, and the accounting, with no hand-waving about where the entropy went — that yields net positive work; abstract reassurances that the second law guarantees the accounting will balance will not.
