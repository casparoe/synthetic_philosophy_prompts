I got into this after my dad died last year. Someone at the funeral said something like well, he'd earned it, meaning it was payback for something in a previous life, and I hated that. But it got me thinking, and I've ended up building an argument that I'm now fairly sure is broken somewhere, because if it isn't, it breaks something else I don't want to give up.

The version of karma I'm working with, the way I've got it in my head:

(1) The good and bad that come to you are payback for the good and bad you've done, and it's proportional. Nothing comes to you that you haven't got coming.

(2) Here's the bit I added myself, call it the ratchet. The payback doesn't just land on you and stop. Getting handed what you've got coming changes you: getting away with something makes the next one easier, getting knocked about makes you flinch or lash out. So one round of payback feeds into what you do next, which earns the next round, and so on.

(3) Now run it backwards. Take any act anyone has ever done. The shape of it was partly set by the payback they'd already taken, which was set by what they'd done before that, which was partly set by the payback before that, and there's no first one to stop at.

(4) You only deserve anything for an act if the act is really yours — not something that was, at bottom, done to you and then came back out of you.

(5) By (2) and (3), no act is ever really yours in that sense. Everything anyone does is downstream of payback for something earlier.

(6) So nobody has ever deserved anything.

(7) So if karma is true, desert is impossible. It's meant to be a theory of desert and it switches desert off. It eats itself.

(8) And it doesn't stop there. Put 'your genes, your upbringing, everything that ever happened to you' where 'payback' is in (2) and (3), and the same argument says nobody deserves to be punished either. That I don't want. My brother's ex put him in hospital and I've got no problem at all saying she deserved what the court gave her.

So something in (1) to (6) has to be wrong and I can't see which. My mate says the fix is to stop treating karma as desert and treat it as a debt instead — there's a ledger, and debts can be written off or paid by somebody else, whereas desert can't. I can't tell if that's a real fix or just changing the subject.

Which step would you throw out, why does it look fine when it's written down like that, and what am I signing up for with each of the ways out? I'd rather keep (4) than (1) if I had to pick, but I also want to keep saying some people deserve punishing, so if those two pull apart, tell me which one is actually doing the work.
