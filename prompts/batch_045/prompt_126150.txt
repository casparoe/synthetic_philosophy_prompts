I'm on the program committee of a small EA-adjacent foundation, and we recieved two grant proposals that both take it as a working assumption that we're probably in a simulation, and that then go in completely opposite directions:

Continuation — money for making our civilisation cheap to keep running and legible/valuable to whoever is running us: durable compressed records of what happens here, steering toward outcomes a simulator would plausibly want to see, and exploratory work on whether a run could be ported or handed off to a successor run rather than just switched off at the end.

Indifference — money for bog-standard suffering reduction, on the argument that the simulation hypothesis is decision-theoretically inert. Their case has two prongs: if this is a replicated run, i.e. a bunch of identical copies of our history executing in parallel as one batch, then whatever we do gets multiplied by the copy count on every horn of the trilemma, so it can't discriminate between interventions; and if it's a single run that's winding down because whatever question it was spun up to answer has basically been answered, then anything with a long time horizon is worth roughly zero and only the near-term stuff is left. Either way, they say, you land back on ordinary near-term altruism.
