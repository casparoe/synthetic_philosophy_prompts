The argument my colleague gave, as I reconstructed it:

(1) At any given time, exactly one of the pair {the job crashes at 02:00, the job does not crash at 02:00} is true. No gaps, no both.

(2) A statement about what happens at 02:00 does not change its truth value as 02:00 approaches. If "the job crashes at 02:00" is true at 01:59, it was true at 17:00, and it was true last Tuesday.

(3) Whatever was the case at 17:00 is now settled. Nothing I do at 23:00 can make it not have been the case at 17:00.

(4) So at 17:00 it was already settled whether the job crashes at 02:00 — settled in the sense of (3): already the case, and now beyond anyone's reach.

(5) If the crash was already settled at 17:00, then whether it happens is not something my later behaviour — including merging the patch — can make any difference to.

(6) So my patch made no difference to whether the job crashed. If the crash was settled, it happens whatever I do; if the non-crash was settled, it fails to happen whatever I do.

(7) But that is false, and I know it is false: the patch is precisely why it didn't crash, and it's why my job exists.

(8) So one of (1) through (5) has to go.
