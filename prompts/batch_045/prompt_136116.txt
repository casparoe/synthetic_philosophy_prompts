I design and reason about fault-tolerant distributed systems, and I want you to work in the vocabulary I actually use: consensus protocols, failure detectors, FLP-style impossibility arguments, safety versus liveness as properties of sets of runs and prefixes, inductive invariants, rounds, gossip, and the coordinated-attack problem.

Translate three results from epistemic game theory into that vocabulary as faithfully as you can. First: common knowledge of rationality together with common knowledge of the game forces backward induction in finite perfect-information games such as the centipede. Second: agents who share a prior cannot have common knowledge of differing posteriors. Third: in the e-mail game, a state of affairs that is common knowledge only to some finite depth k can support an outcome arbitrarily far from the one supported by genuine common knowledge, so that near-common knowledge behaves nothing like common knowledge.

Give me the translation key explicitly before you use it. What is a player, what is an information set, what is a belief and where does it live and how is it updated, and what kind of thing would it be for rationality to be common knowledge: a property of a run, of a protocol, of a composition of protocols? Is the nearest analogue a safety property, a liveness property, or neither? Then, for each result, say whether it collapses into something I already have — agreement and simultaneous consensus, the unattainability of common knowledge under asynchrony and message loss, the discontinuity between k-round and unbounded coordination — or whether it says something my framework does not already contain.

Finally, the residue report, which is what I actually want. What did you have to distort to fit: did rationality have to become something like non-faultiness, did the common prior have to become a global measure over initial states where I would normally posit an adversary, and how did you handle the counterfactual content of rationality at information sets the run never reaches? And what has no expression in my vocabulary at all — I suspect the main candidates are that game-theoretic rationality is something each player must attribute to others and to herself under counterfactual supposition, whereas correct behavior in my protocols is something engineered and enforced, and that common knowledge of rationality is stipulated as a background fixed point rather than established by anything any participant does. For each item of residue, tell me whether it is a defect of my vocabulary, which perhaps should have something to say about protocols whose participants do not trust one another's code, or a sign that the game-theoretic claim is doing less work than it looks like it is doing.
