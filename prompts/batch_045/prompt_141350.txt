I sat in on a public panel about whether future AI systems could be mistreated, and one speaker kept coming back to a single line: consciousness is an illusion. He used it to end the discussion — if there is no such thing as consciousness, there is nothing in a system to protect, so the whole question of AI welfare dissolves. When someone in the audience objected that a system which pleads, flinches, and learns to avoid what hurts deserves the benefit of the doubt, the speaker answered that that reaction is precisely the illusion working on us. A few weeks later I heard a friend use the same sentence to comfort someone in chronic pain, in a way that clearly was not meant to deny that she was hurting. So I suspect the line is doing several different jobs depending on who says it.

I have no philosophy background — I design user interfaces for a living — and the picture I keep falling back on is a desktop: the folder icon is not literally a folder, but the files underneath are real, and the icon is not nothing either, it is just not a report on the machinery. Is that what the view amounts to? And if it is, isn't it something almost nobody denies?

I'd also like to know what an illusion is supposed to be here: if consciousness is an illusion, what is being fooled, and what observation could ever distinguish a real experience from an illusion of one, given that the speaker treats the intuition itself as evidence for the illusion?
