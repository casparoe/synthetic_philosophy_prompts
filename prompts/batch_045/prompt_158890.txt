I'm a philosophy PhD student who has been asked to lead a ninety-minute seminar session on Skolem's paradox for a graduate reading group: students who know Cantor's diagonal argument, have heard the Löwenheim–Skolem theorem stated, but have never considered a model of set theory as an object in its own right.

I don't need the explanation; I need the plan for it. Give me the order in which the components have to be introduced and, for each, which later move it exists to make possible — specifically, whether the group has to be given the downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem before the claim that ZFC has a countable model can do any work for them, or whether countability should be handed over first as a brute fact and the theorem only named once the puzzle has bitten.

The carrier I have in mind is a countable transitive model whose ω is the genuine ω and whose reals are countable from the outside, chosen so that the group cannot mislocate the puzzle in non-standard natural numbers; tell me whether that is the right carrier, what it costs me, and whether any stage of the session would be better served by a non-transitive or ill-founded model instead.

Then the misconception map, located in the sequence rather than merely listed: say where each has to be defused — that the model is simply wrong about its own reals, that Cantor's theorem has been refuted, that this is a defect of first-order logic which second-order logic repairs, and the one I expect to be hardest, that the enumeration of the model's reals exists somewhere and the model merely fails to contain it.

The session is meant to set up next week's discussion of the relativist conclusion about set-theoretic notions drawn from the paradox, so tell me what the group must be able to state in their own words for that discussion to be possible, without my having loaded the dice toward the relativist or the realist; give me one question for the last ten minutes that would show the internal/external distinction has landed rather than been memorized, and one thing I should openly refuse to explain at this level, with the reason.
