hey — i'm wrapping up a six week intro unit on sets, infinity and the paradoxes, and ive taken to closing the last session by reading something aloud instead of throwing up a summary slide. can you write me a villanelle for that?

voice: the Russell set — the thing that (putatively) has as members exactly the sets that aren't members of themselves. first person, and it should sound like it's being completely straight with us, not spooky and not triumphant.

the two refrains should be the two halves of the biconditional, one each. my rough drafts are "I hold the sets that never hold themselves" and "So I am one of them — and so I'm not", but rewrite them if you can get them shorter or more singable; i want someone to be able to repeat them on the walk home.

what i care about most is that the form does the work. the refrains keep coming back no matter how many stanzas go by, which is exactly what the paradox does — every line of thought that tries to settle it reinstates it. and when the two finally land next to each other in the quatrain, that adjacency should be the moment you see they were never two separate claims but one equivalence, so what breaks isn't the reasoning (it's a perfectly valid reductio) but the assumption that a description like that one picks out a set in the first place.

for the middle: give each of the five tercets a different way out and let the refrain close it down. i have in mind (a) just deny that it exists, (b) call it too big to be a set, a proper class, (c) sort everything into types or levels so the question can't even be asked, (d) blame the predicate instead — say its extension outruns any boundary you try to draw. for the fifth, maybe let it be both and give up non-contradiction, or only allow comprehension to sieve subsets out of a set you already have, or say there's no determinate totality of all the sets — pick whichever one you can actually make land in a tercet. someone who knows this stuff should be able to point at a stanza and say which escape route it's trying and why it doesn't untangle the pull.

accuracy stuff so my TA doesn't wince: don't have it blame bivalence or the law of excluded middle, that's not where the trouble is, and don't let it sound like we discovered an actual contradiction in mathematics — what's on the books is the theorem that there's no such set. also please dont turn it into a horror poem about a monster set, and no invented technical words; say it in the set's own plain language.

should read aloud in under a minute. thanks!
