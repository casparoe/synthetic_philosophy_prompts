I'm teaching a 90-minute seminar on Foucault on discipline to about 24 undergrads in a social theory course, and I want an activity rather than a lecture. The thing I want them to feel rather than hear about is the gap between power that forbids and punishes from above and power that just arranges space, timing and visibility so people end up correcting themselves — and especially that it keeps working even when nobody is actually watching. So nobody should hear the words discipline or panopticon until the debrief.

The hook Ive been toying with: a repetitive micro-task for the first half hour, where the norm they get scored against is derived from their own behaviour in the first ten minutes instead of announced up front, and where each of them produces a short written record about themselves that gets used later in the session to make a judgement about them. The point of that last bit is that they should come away feeling the assessed individual is an effect of the setup, not a person the setup merely happened to act on.

Then I want a second half that switches register: same task, but now run the way he talks about apparatuses of security — tuning rates across the whole group, leaving a tolerable amount of deviance alone, instead of correcting each person toward a norm — and a debrief that makes them say what actually changed.

Give me rules, materials, a timeline and the debrief. Also: what will predictably go wrong the first time (I can't exactly stop being their teacher or walk out of the room), and which part of the idea the exercise just can't stage, so I have to say it out loud afterwards.
