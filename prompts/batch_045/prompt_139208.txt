Hey, I'm a backend engineer with zero philosophy background and I fell into a rabbit hole this weekend because of something that happened at work, and now I want a story out of it rather than an explanation. Quick context: there's a guy on my team, call him Marek, who for about two years has quietly been the person who jumps on the pager at 3am when he isn't on call, fixes the flaky integration tests nobody owns, and answers random questions in the team channel on his days off. When he started, people made a big deal out of it, hero stuff, going above and beyond, the whole thing. Now nobody says anything at all, and last month he was on vacation, a Saturday deploy broke, he didn't respond, and two people in retro more or less said he'd let the team down. He hadn't broken any rule, he just didn't do the extra bit. So he went from being praised for doing it to being blamed for not doing it, and nobody ever changed the job description, nobody ever asked him to promise anything, and he never said he'd always be there. A friend told me there's a term for acts like this, where doing them is praiseworthy but not doing them isn't blameworthy, supererogation or something, but what actually interests me is the drift. My half-baked theory, which I'm calling an expectation ratchet, is that the act itself never changes but the people around it slowly convert it into an obligation just by relying on it and then forgetting they ever relied on it, and that this is kind of a scam, because it means you can lose your options without ever agreeing to give them up. But I can also see the other side: that if everyone can see you will do the thing, and they plan around it, then not doing it does harm, and maybe reliability that others build their lives on just is a duty no matter how it started.

So could you write me a short story, somewhere around 900 to 1200 words, that lives inside that tension instead of solving it. Please don't set it in a software company, I get enough of that, and please don't make it a story where the answer turns out to be that everyone should just respect boundaries and the crowd was wrong, that version feels too easy. Set it somewhere concrete and a bit physical: my suggestion would be a small valley town whose power comes from an old run-of-river hydro plant, where the intake screens clog with debris every spring melt and need to be cleared by hand, and where one person on the rotating crew has for years hiked up to clear them on her own time, in bad weather, because someone has to and it was never written down as anyone's job. Have the town come to depend on that without ever quite deciding to, have the praise slowly stop, and then have a season where she doesn't go up, for a reason that is neither heroic nor contemptible, and let whatever happens happen. I'd like the story to make me feel both that she definitely wasn't obligated and that the town's resentment isn't just stupidity, and I'd especially like it if the story brushes against the question of whether quietly doing something for years counts as a kind of unspoken promise, and whether the praise people gave her at the start was itself a payment that turned the whole thing into a transaction all along.

Format-wise, keep it plain: no title, no headings, no bold or italics, no bullet points, no tables, no scene-break markers or act labels, just continuous prose in paragraphs the way you'd write an email to a friend who asked you for a story. Start straight in with the first line of the story rather than with any preamble about what you're about to do, and when the story is over, give me two or three sentences in the same plain prose about which part of the knot you think is the genuinely uncomfortable one and why, and then stop.
