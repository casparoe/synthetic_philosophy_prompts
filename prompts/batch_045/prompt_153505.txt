I'm doing a piece on intellectual property and I keep hitting the same weird asymetry, and I want to know what it actually assumes. As IP lawyers keep telling me (correct me if I've got it wrong): in copyright, independent creation is a full defence — if I write a song in a cabin with no radio and it happens to come out note-for-note like yours, I haven't infringed. In patent, independent invention is no defence at all — if I build the thing in my garage and someone else already holds the patent, I'm an infringer even though I never heard of them. Same broad label, opposite answer about whether you had to take anything from anybody.

What does that split presuppose about what's being owned? Is patent law quietly treating an invention as something already out there to be found, so two people can't each find it first, while copyright treats a work as something authored, so it's the making and not the pattern that matters? Or is the asymmetry just administrative — about what's cheap to register and police — and compatible with several theories at once?

And one more thing a source pitched me, which I can't tell is deep or silly: a "simultaneous invention window," where if two parties independently hit on the same thing within a year of each other, neither gets an injunction and both get compulsory licenses. If that were adopted, what would a patent have become — what would it be for?
