I'm helping out (volunteer, mostly) with a local truth-and-reconciliation-ish project, and I keep hitting the same weird pattern and I don't have a word for it. My internet is basically nonexistent out here, so please don't send me off to books or articles — I want the concept, not pointers.

Here's the pattern. A community's memory of something and the documentary record disagree, and the memory loses on every checkable detail — the date, the number, who did it — but the details aren't random. They seem to be carrying something the files don't. Some made-up-but-typical examples:

A village says soldiers came in 1963 and took forty men away. The district police diary for 1963 shows a patrol passing through and no arrests. But there's a 1961 court record convicting eleven men of unlawful assembly, and the 1964 tax roll is missing thirty-eight names. So the memory is wrong about the year, wrong about the count, wrong about the mechanism — and yet it's the only thing in the archive or outside it that says a whole generation of men stopped being there. It stitched three bureaucratic incidents into one story and kept the part that matters.

Or: a family insists a grandfather was shot in the town square. The prison register says he died of disease in custody. But the register was weeded in the 70s and nobody outside the family knows he was ever a prisoner; and "the square" keeps the thing in public, where the town has to walk past it.

And the part that really got me: after we publish a correction, the memory doesn't get replaced by the right story, it gets replaced by nothing. Local officials start saying "nothing much happened here," which is also false — and is downstream of our correction.

So the thing I want a name for is this: a memory that's false about the event but true about something else, where the inaccuracy itself is doing the work — sometimes pointing at a relationship (who kept the files, who got to write things down) rather than at an event at all. Not the big "memory versus history" contrast, I mean the narrower thing where the error is load-bearing.

Does that have an established name? If it does, tell me what the nearest two or three concepts are and why this isn't just one of them — I specifically want to know how it's different from ordinary myth-making, from straight denialism, and from a story that's just degraded over retellings, and whether there's any test that separates them in practice. If it doesn't have a name, coin one, define it tightly, and give me the boundary cases where you'd say "no, that's the other thing."

Also: how confident are you in the distinctions you're drawing, and which part of what you just told me are you least sure of?
