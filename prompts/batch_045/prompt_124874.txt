I'm doing one of those live science-and-ideas nights next month — ten-minute slot, me and a cellist, in the back room of a pub, and I want to close my segment with something that isn't the usual information-is-physical kicker where everyone nods and nothing lands. My bit is on Maxwell's demon, and specifically on the part of the story that I think never survives the popular telling: the demon doesn't get caught at the moment it measures. Measurement can in principle be done reversibly, for free. It gets caught later, when it has to clear its memory to make room for the next measurement, at Landauer's kT ln 2 per bit, dumped as heat into the very bath it was trying to mine. And the thing I keep failing to get across to readers is that this makes information a kind of fuel rather than a kind of sin: the demon can absolutely run its engine, extract real work, and stay ahead for as long as somebody keeps handing it blank tape. The ledger only balances when the tape gets wiped. My editor says that's the actual story and I should do something with it, so here I am asking for a poem.

What I want is a sestina — six stanzas of six, then the three-line envoi. The six end-words that come cycling back should all be working nouns or verbs from the setup; my list is door, bit, hot, erase, memory, cost, but swap in better ones if they scan, as long as there are no proper names and no word like entropy being used as an incantation. The form has to be doing the physics: the same six words arriving forever in new orders, impossible to get rid of, is exactly what it is like to own a memory that fills up, and I want the reader to feel the tedium of that cycle by the fourth stanza. The envoi is where the account closes — one bit wiped for every bit remembered, the heat showing up back in the bath, the demon no further on than it started. Please don't let it turn into a lecture about how thermodynamics always wins in the end: the demon wins locally, for a stretch, and the poem should let it enjoy that.

Voice-wise I think it should be the demon, or whoever tends the demon — first person, plain-ish diction, readable aloud over the noise of a pub, iambic-ish pentameter if you can manage it but I won't be counting syllables on you. And the bit I actually care about: there are two people in that room who build microscopic information engines for a living, and I would rather die than stand up there and say something false, so after the poem give me a few lines of note on which line each piece of physics is hiding in and where, if anywhere, you fudged it — especially the kT ln 2 and the claim about measurement being free. If one of my six words can't be made to land without lying, tell me which one and what to replace it with. Also I have no idea whether a sestina can even hold an argument about irreversibility, so if you think another repeating form is better say so and say why, but I do want something that comes back around.
