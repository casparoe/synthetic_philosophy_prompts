The distinction I want it to target: there are really two claims inside the safety story. (a) Competence: because hard problems get decomposed into sub-questions that a human, or a slightly-amplified human, can answer, you can get performance beyond what any one person could produce in one go. Then a second wrinkle so they cant stop at the observation that distillation is lossy: suppose we distill the deliberation traces rather than just the final answers, so the model reproduces the reasoning before it commits to a verdict. Does that close the gap? I want them to see both why it looks like it does and why it might not.

Write the question, a model answer of about 800 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 built around five must-hit points, each with a one-line note on how students typically get that point wrong. Closed book, 90 minutes.
