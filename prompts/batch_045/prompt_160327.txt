Hey — I run a very low-stakes philosophy meme account and I need one more joke, this time on Kavka's toxin puzzle. Quick recap of the setup in case you're rusty: an eccentic billionaire offers you a million dollars if, by midnight tonight, you form the intention to drink a vial of toxin tomorrow. You never actually have to drink it; drinking it would just make you sick for a day. He reads intentions directly, so no lying or tricking him.

Here's the specific thing I want the punchline to hinge on, and it's not the usual "haha, can't make up his mind" stuff: the reason you'd have for *forming* the intention can't be a reason for *carrying it out*. The money is paid for the intending, not the drinking, so at the moment you'd be picking up the vial the million is already yours and there's nothing at all on the scale — not even a desire that's merely outweighed, the way it is in those "jumping out of the burning building / dentist appointment" cases where you do something you don't want to do. The worry is about an intention that can't survive long enough to be a reason for the action, not about weakness of will.

And please build the joke around this wrinkle, which I don't think anyone has run: the billionaire, annoyed that nobody ever collects, changes the terms. Now he pays for forming, by midnight, the intention to *form the intention* to drink the toxin tomorrow. Same money, one level up. So the laugh should come from what happens when you try to climb that ladder: does the extra floor help you (can you intend to intend without believing you'll ever intend? does the recursion bottom out somewhere? do you collect by going up a level every night forever?), or does it just reproduce the original failure one storey higher, or does it hand you some new loophole?
