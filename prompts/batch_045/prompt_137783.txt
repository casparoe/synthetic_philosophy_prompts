Hey — I'm helping run a one-off adversarial collaboration session for a consciousness reading group, and I need the document the two sides are supposed to walk out with. Half the room is IIT-curious (consciousness = integrated information, the Φ measure, the exclusion postulate, cortex-in / cerebellum-out, and they've made peace with the idea that a big grid of logic gates might out-conscious you) and the other half thinks IIT is a reformulation of facts we already knew, dressed up in a quantity nobody can actually compute for a real brain, with panpsychism smuggled in the back. Assume both sides are good-faith and know the literature; nobody gets to be a strawman.

Draft the joint statement. Two parts, in this order.

First, a numbered list of agreed claims — the stuff both sides can sign without wincing. It has to be genuinely substantive, not 'consciousness is interesting' filler, and it shouldn't be phrased in IIT's native vocabulary in a way that loads the dice. I'd want things like: that neuron count alone obviously doesn't settle which structures matter and any theory has to earn the cortex-versus-cerebellum contrast; that the split-brain-ish cases are where theories get separated rather than confirmed; that appearing to assign experience to a thermostat-shaped system is at minimum a cost, even if one side thinks it's a fatal one; that a theory's quantity being uncomputable in practice is a real problem for its scientific standing, even if the theory is true. Give me 7–9 of those, and mark which ones are real agreement and which are agreements to stop fighting about a word.

Second, a disagreements table. Columns: the crux; the IIT side's own formulation of its position on it, in wording an IIT person would accept; the critic's own formulation, same rule; the single case or argument both sides agree would actually bear on it; and each side's prediction about how that case comes out, written down in advance.

The cruxes I'd like covered: whether part of this is verbal at all (is one side talking about experience and the other about what's globally available for report — and if so, say plainly which parts of the fight dissolve and which don't); the throttled-bridge case, where a subject's two cortical hemispheres are left connected only through a channel so slow and narrow (a few bits a second, long latency) that neither side can wriggle out of it — does the subject become two subjects, one degraded subject, or something graded; the duplicate-graft case, where a slab of cortex is matched by a causally identical slab running in perfect lockstep, contributing nothing new downstream — does experience double, and what would either answer tell us; the grid-of-gates / panpsychism worry, as bullet-bitten versus reductio; and whether the zap-and-measure-the-EEG-response complexity proxy that IIT folks lean on is a fair test that discriminates between the two pictures or just something both pictures predict anyway.

Also: for each crux, say which side it would actually move if it went the wrong way for them — some of these only cut one direction, and I want to know which. And end with a short section on what each side should concede about the disagreement that's left over. Please skip the history of the debate, the citations, and any 'further reading' — I only want the claims, the formulations, the cases and the predictions. Two pages-ish, definately no more than that.
