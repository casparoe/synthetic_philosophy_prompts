ok so my section TA handed us this argument on the cave + the forms and I have been going in circles on it all week:

(1) You can only have thoughts about X if you have concepts that reach X, and your concepts reach only what you have actually met with.
(2) The prisoners have met with only shadows.
(3) So they cannot think about the objects outside the cave -- not truly, not falsely, they can't think about them at all.
(4) If you can't think about X, you can't be wrong about X either.
(5) But the whole point of the story is that the prisoners are wrong: the ascent is supposed to correct a mistake, not to switch on a mind that had nothing in it.
(6) So the allegory is broken -- either it never described error in the first place, or (1) is false.
(7) And if (1) is false it collapses one level up to: we have only ever met sensible particulars, so we can't think about the forms, so no recollection, no wanting what you lack, no form of the good as the thing every soul is aiming at.

Then she added a twist that I think makes it worse. Suppose the puppet-masters sometimes cast shadows of things that don't exist at all -- pure made-up shapes with no original. Now the prisoners can't even think "some of what I'm seeing is fake", because being fake would have to be a concept that reaches the real thing it falls short of.

I would rather drop (1) than loose the ascent, but dropping (1) seems to take away Plato's own explanation of why the prisoners are stuck and why someone has to drag them out. Also: isn't this just Meno's paradox with extra steps? My friend says no and I can't say why he's right.

Which step is actually broken, what does fixing it cost, and does the fake-shadows twist change anything?
