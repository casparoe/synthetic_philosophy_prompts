Here's roughly where I've landed.

(1) I'm fairly fanatical about the far future. I accept that the expected value of modestly reducing existential risk swamps anything I could do in the near term, because of the sheer number of lives that could exist downstream. Small probabilities of astronomical payoffs dominate the calculation. I know this is uncomfortable, I know the standard objections, and I've made my piece with it.

(2) I give maybe a quarter to a third to us being in a simulation of the ancestor-simulation sort — the Bostrom-ish trilemma where either civilizations like ours die out, or they don't bother running simulations of their ancestors, or we're almost certainly in one. Not more than a third. But not negligible either.

(3) I don't think (2) dents (1) at all, because if we are in a simulation, the people in the future of that simulation are still people having real experiences. Value doesn't care what the hardware is made of. So "it's only a simulation, nothing matters" seems to me like a straightforward fallacy, and I'm fairly confident about that one.

(4) But I do think a simulation is probably resource-limited in a way that base reality isn't. Whoever's running it has finite computing budget and, plausibly, finite patience and finite interest. They'd run the interesting bit and then stop, or run it at low resolution, or only simulate one region or one era. So conditional on (2), I'd expect our future to be short — centuries or millennia, not the billions of years that base-reality physics appears to permit. My working guess is that (2) mostly converts an astronomically large future into a merely large one.

(5) I think the right way to estimate how long our future is, is the ordinary empirical one: look at the track record — how long we've already lasted, what physics allows, whether we see any evidence of civilizations getting terminated — and I don't discount for "simulation shutdown" beyond what that evidence supports. Though here's a worry I can't shake: if we're in a simulation, the past I'd be extrapolating from is part of the simulation's content. It may have been written rather than generated. In which case extrapolating from it to the future is a bit like inferring the length of a novel from how good the first three chapters are.

(6) I reject the package that says, if we're in a simulation, the rational thing is to perform for the simulators — be interesting, avoid the boring ending, try not to get switched off. That strikes me as Pascal's wagger with extra steps, and I'd be surprised if it's in real tension with anything else I hold, but I'm including it because someone in the group insists it follows from (2).

Now: (1) and (4) feel like they collide and I can't see my way out of it, and I've been told (4) and (5) are actually fine together, for a reason I can't evaluate: even if most simulations are short, most observer-moments are in long ones — if the sims are numerous and varied, a randomly sampled conscious moment is more likely to land in a big, long, populous simulation than a stubby one, so conditional on finding myself here I should expect a long future after all. I don't know whether to believe that. It smells like the sort of move that would be extremely convenient if it were true, which makes me suspicious of it.
