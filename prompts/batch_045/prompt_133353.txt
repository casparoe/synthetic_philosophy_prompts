Some colleagues and I sit on a committee that has to rule on whether novel engineered systems must be treated as alive for the purposes of a containment and research-ethics regime. So the account we adopt has to be one that can return a yes-or-no verdict about a particular thing at a particular time. Before we start reviewing candidate definitions, I want the specification.

Write the desiderata that any adequate account of what it is to be alive would have to meet, each with the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark which you take to be non-negotiable and which are only preferences of a particular camp or artifacts of our regulatory purpose. The cases I want the list tested against: fire; a computer worm; a prion; a dormant bacterial spore and a desiccated tardigrade; a mule; a skin cell in culture; a siphonophore or a fungal mycelium, where it is unclear what the individual is; a virus that replicates only inside a host cell; and two hypotheticals of my own — a far-from-equilibrium chemical network that metabolizes, persists, and evolves, but has no individuated units at all, only a diffuse patch that grows and splits (call it a tessellate); and a chimera of a cell and a machine in which most of the free-energy transduction is done by a fabricated silicon component.

I also want the distinctions an account must respect rather than collapse: alive versus organism versus part of a living thing versus product of life versus once-alive versus dormant versus never-alive; and whether being alive admits of degree.

Then three things. Which two desiderata do you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — I expect the general-enough-for-chemistry-we-have-never-seen requirement and the sharp-cutoff requirement are the ones that collide, and I want to know whether that collision is a defect in the definitional project or a sign that our committee has been given a purpose no definition can serve. Which single desideratum does the most work in excluding otherwise-popular definitions. And whether the requirement that a definition not presuppose what origin-of-life research is still trying to find out is a genuine constraint or an academic prejudice that a regulator should ignore. Keep it to the criteria and their cases; I can run the candidate definitions against the list myself.
