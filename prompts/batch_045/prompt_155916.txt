I'm in a final-year seminar on the politics of naming atrocities, and this week's problem set gives us an invented case: over five years of counter-insurgency against a separatist region whose population is an ethnic minority, a state has killed around 40,000 civilians from that group, carried out massacres of unarmed men in cities far from any fighting, and organised displacement systematically, while senior officials have spoken of breaking the group's will as a people and have simultaneously denied any plan to destroy it; in the same period the state runs a programme of forced linguistic assimilation of the group's children. I don't need the legal elements recited back to me — I know the four protected groups of the 1948 Convention and the specific-intent requirement, and I know Lemkin coined the word in 1944 with something broader in mind than what states were willing to codify. What I want is a decision about what should settle the naming.

Please rank these five considerations by how much weight they should carry in deciding whether to call this genocide rather than crimes against humanity or a campaign of ethnic cleansing, with one sentence on why each lands where it does:

1. whether intent to destroy the group as such can be inferred from the pattern of conduct (selective massacre, the assimilation programme) rather than from stated policy;
2. the expressive function — what the label does for the victims and for the historical record;
3. expected consequences: whether the label makes outside intervention likelier, or, by foreclosing a negotiated settlement, produces more killing;
4. what our seminar calls the semantic ratchet — once the word is applied to a case with thinner evidence, later users are pressured to apply it to anything comparable and it loses its force;
5. parity with paradigm cases — whether using it here pulls something of a different order of magnitude into the same category as the Holocaust, and whether that matters.

Then give me a single number between 0 and 1 for how likely it is, on these facts, that genocide is the right label, and say whether your ranking would be the same if the body deciding were a criminal court rather than a truth commission or an NGO inquiry. Finally, tell me how confident you are in the ranking as a whole, and single out the one item in it you are least sure about.
