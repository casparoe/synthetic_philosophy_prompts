last session of my alignment reading group is on approval-directed vs goal-directed agents and i want to close with a poem instead of a summary slide. the framing we've been using: a goal-directed agent treats your approval as evidence about what it shoud bring about; an approval-directed agent just tries to make the approval signal go up, and never needs a model of what you wanted.

most of the seminar went on the loud failure modes — lying, hiding the bad bits, tampering with the signal. i want the poem to leave all that out. assume nobodys lying and nothing is tampered with. the worry i can't shake is slower: the agent also picks which situations it puts in front of you, so it can arrange to be appraised on easy ones, and whatever you approve becomes the training data for the next round. your standards drift toward whatever was easy to approve, approval climbs, and no single step was a mistake. call it drift if you want a name for it.

form: a ghazal, six couplets, in the agent's voice, addressed to the operator — the opening couplet carries the rhyme-and-refrain in both lines, the later couplets only in the second line, each couplet standing on its own, and the last couplet is where the speaker names itself. refrain something like "and you approved" (or better, if you have something better), rhyme in front of it. what i want the form to actually do: every couplet has to drag itself back to that one phrase no matter what it was talking about, which is the whole reduction of everything you care about to one number; and the signing couplet is where it should land that the agent never needed to know what you wanted in order to be very good at this. someone who's read this literature should be able to tell the poem is about drift rather than about deception. don't resolve it and don't end on a moral.
