my artificial life reading group did a semester on Hofstadter's typogenetics, and one guy in it (Dan) keeps a running list of submitted strands that he marks as genuine self-reps or not. He's consistant enough that people now bet on his calls. The record:

- the long strand from the book whose enzymes, run on it, turn out two copies of itself by way of a copy step: yes
- a strand that duplicates itself in one block-copy move, without ever reading out the individual bases: no, "thats a photocopier, not a self-rep"
- a strand that only reproduces when some other strand in the pool is around to supply one of the enzymes: no, "thats being replicated"
- a two-strand cycle where A's enzymes build B and B's enzymes build A: no, "the pair is, neither one is"
- a strand whose output is one base off from itself, where the off base is the third base of a codon and doesn't change the enzyme that codon codes for: yes, "same creature"
- a strand that makes one exact copy of itself plus a pile of junk fragments: yes

New submission, and this is the one we're arguing about: a strand whose enzymes, applied to it, print out a strand with a different base sequence — about a third of the bases differ, all silent third-position swaps — that translates into exactly the same enzyme sequence. (The code is many-to-one, so different strands can translate alike.) It never makes an exact copy of itself; it makes synonym after synonym, and each synonym does the same thing again.

Is Dan going to accept it? I care less about the yes/no than about the rule he's actually running — identity of bases, identity of translation, "the copying has to do real work", something else — which of the other rules fitting his record equally well say the opposite here, and what further strand would pry them apart. I was thinking something whose output translates to a *different* enzyme sequence that happens to do the same job, but you tell me. If his precedents genuinely underdetermine it, say what he'd be committing to either way. For context, Dan's whole reason for caring is the Godel analogy: a self-rep is supposed to be like a sentence that manages to be about itself, and I suspect that's the hinge.
