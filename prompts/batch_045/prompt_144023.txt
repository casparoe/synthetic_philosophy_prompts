i'm in an indian epistemology course right now and we're doing the nyaya stuff on pramanas, and i realised i've been lowkey sorting cases by a rule i can't actually state. help me state it, or tell me my verdicts are a mess.

the set-up we were given: a pramana is a source of a *new* true cognition; the nyaya list is four — pratyaksha (perception), anumana (inference, which runs through an invariable concomitance like smoke-with-fire), upamana (comparison: coming to know what a word denotes from its similarity to something you remember), and shabda (testimony: coming to know something from understanding what a trustworthy speaker said). memory and tarka — the "if there were no fire there'd be no smoke" kind of backing-reasoning — are supposed not to be pramanas.

here are my gut verdicts, and i've included the ones i'm shaky on because i think those are where the rule shows:

1. smoke on the hill, so fire on the hill: anumana, no drama.
2. my roommate texts "the 6:15 is cancelled" and i come to know it: shabda. i know i *saw* pixels, but that's not where the knowledge comes from.
3. i see a guy's face across the street and after half a second realise it's my old roommate from six years ago: i want to say pratyaksha, and that the memory doing the recognising isn't a pramana. but it feels like the memory is load-bearing in a way that "just a causal condition" doesn't capture. verdict: perception, but grudgingly.
4. a printed sign reading BRIDGE OUT on a road i'm driving down: shabda, not perception — even though i read it with my eyes and there's no speaker in the room.
5. i glance at the counter and come to know my keys aren't there: i really don't want to call this inference (what's the pervasion?), and "perception of an absence" sounds like a dodge, but i also don't want to add a fifth pramana just to save this one case.
6. i read 38.2 off a thermometer and come to know i have a fever: my verdict is pratyaksha. but if i then found out the thermometer was calibrated by some guy who eyeballed it, my verdict slides to shabda — and if i find out the guy is unreliable, it slides to *no pramana at all*. that sliding bothers me; is it a sign i'm using the wrong test?
7. a field guide says "a tapir looks like a pig with a short prehensile snout"; i see one at the zoo and come to know that's a tapir. my verdict: upamana, and it's genuinely doing its own work — not just "shabda from the guide plus perception of the animal." but i can't say what's left over once you subtract those two.
8a. i infer fire from smoke in the kitchen; then my roommate texts "there's a fire." my verdict: the text adds no new pramana. it's reassurance, not a second source.
8b. i infer fire from smoke; then i walk in and *see* the flames. my verdict: that's a new pramana and genuinely new knowledge, not just more confidence.
— these two are the pair i can't reconcile. structurally they're the same: a second, independent route delivers a proposition i already had by inference. why does one feel like knowledge-twice and the other like nothing added?
9. i'm not sure the grey haze is smoke; i run the tarka ("if there were no fire there'd be no smoke"), and *then* i'm willing to infer. verdict: genuinely helpful, still not a pramana.
10. remembering where i parked: knowledge, not a pramana. fine with this.
11. i overhear half a sentence in a language i barely speak and fill in the rest from context, and come to know the speaker is angry: no idea. feels like a blend, and i don't know which pramana owns it.

my own guess at the principle: a pramana is whatever *fixes the content* — the thing that answers "how do you know?", the thing that would have to go wrong for this cognition to be false. so the pixels in case 2 don't fix the content, the speaker does; and in 8a the content was already fixed, so the text fixes nothing.
