I am writing a short piece on a claim I would like to test rather than assert: that measures which make a party less exposed to its partner's defection — hedging, hardening, pre-building fallback capacity, keeping an outside option warm — can destroy the very cooperation they were meant to be insurance against. I need a worked case that isolates this effect, and I would like you to construct it to the following specifications.

The object: a two-player repeated prisoner's dilemma, with an explicit payoff matrix, in which adding a purely defensive option to the stage game makes full cooperation unsustainable.

Definitions and constraints:

1. The stage game has three actions. Cooperate and Defect form an ordinary prisoner's dilemma: with the usual labels, T > R > P > S and 2R > T + S. The third action I will call withdrawal. Withdrawal pays the withdrawer a fixed amount E regardless of what the other player does — E is a floor, not a gamble — and it leaves the other player with exactly what they would have received had the withdrawer defected: S if they cooperated, P if they defected. Withdrawal is therefore protective rather than punitive: against a cooperator it earns less than defection would, and it never leaves the other player worse off than defection would.

2. E must lie strictly between P and R. Below R, so that no one is ever tempted to withdraw on the cooperative path: the case must not work by handing either player a more profitable form of betrayal. Above P, so that withdrawal is genuinely attractive as a refuge. The effect I am after should come from withdrawal capping how bad a punishment can be, not from withdrawal being a better way to cheat.

3. Setting: ordinary and recognisable — a standing commercial, professional or institutional relationship between two parties who will plausibly meet again. No science fiction, no copies or clones, no perfect prediction, no binding contracts or external enforcer, and no noise: assume each round's actions are perfectly observed at the end of the round. Withdrawal must be repeatable and inexhaustible.

4. The game is repeated with a fixed continuation probability, which you should state, saying whether it is a discount factor or a per-round survival probability.

What I want you to deliver:

(a) The scenario in one short paragraph, with the three actions named in the story's own terms; then the three-by-three matrix with explicit numbers, and a line checking the prisoner's-dilemma inequalities on the cooperate/defect block and the placement of E.

(b) A single continuation probability at which cooperation is sustainable *without* withdrawal available: name the strategy profile and verify the incentive constraint arithmetically.

(c) A proof that at that same continuation probability, with withdrawal available, no subgame-perfect equilibrium sustains cooperation in every round — and I want the strong version: not merely that grim trigger or tit-for-tat fails, but that no punishment whatever can do it, because withdrawal guarantees its user E per round and so puts a floor under what any punishment can inflict. If the strong version requires a caveat — restriction to pure strategies, or discounting rather than the limit of means — state it honestly rather than quietly assuming it.

(d) The general condition under which cooperation can be sustained, in terms of T, R, P and E, checked so that it reduces to the familiar threshold when E = P. Then say what happens if withdrawal is usable only once, or costs something each time it is used, or is observable and can be met with retaliation: does deterrence return, and at what point?

(e) The asymmetric variant: only one side can withdraw. Which side's cooperation fails, and is the side that can withdraw better or worse off than it would have been under cooperation? Answer using your numbers.

(f) The single feature of the construction doing the work, in one sentence; then the two strongest objections from someone who wants to maintain that defensive hardening cannot undermine cooperation, with your reply to each.

(g) Two or three sentences on what this implies for the familiar evolutionary claim that repeated interaction promotes cooperation: in a well-mixed population playing this game, what replaces mutual defection as the fallback outcome, and does the standard shadow-of-the-future story need qualifying?

Please generate the verdicts from the arithmetic rather than stipulating them: the numbers have to be seen to work. Keep the whole answer under 900 words, the scenario itself to one paragraph.
