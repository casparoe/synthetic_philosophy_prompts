Im a journalist and I'm closing out a magazine piece on how the phrase 'Western values' actually works in an argument — specifically the trick where the phrase never really gets defined and takes its content instead from whatever it's being contrasted with that week (a communist, an islamist, a campus activist), so the list shifts while the phrase itself stays put. I want to end with a short poem: five quatrains, loose meter, the same refrain line closing each of the first four, the refrain being the bare phrase used as if it explained itself. Each of those four quatrains should name a different opponent and a different list of values, and at least one item per list should be something the phrase's own defenders would have denied a hundred years ago. The turn comes in the last quatrain, where the reader should realize the content was being set by the antonym all along rather than by any definition. It shouldn't preach in either direction — it should land for a reader who thinks the category names something real and for one who thinks it's empty. A political theorist should recognize the mechanism at work, not just see democracy and free speech lined up in verse.
