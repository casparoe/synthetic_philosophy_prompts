I'm writing the chapter of my dissertation in which I have to state my own positive view on moral responsibility, and I want the package audited for coherence before I commit to it in print. Roughly where I've landed:

(1) Strawson's core negative point is right: the practice of holding people responsible needs no external metaphysical vindication. The reactive attitudes partly constitute the personal relationships that give human life much of its shape, and no thesis about determinism, or about our not being the ultimate authors of our own characters, could give us reason to abandon that stance wholesale.

(2) Exemption is nonetheless real, and is not merely an excuse: for young children, for people with severe cognitive impairments, and for at least some psychopaths, the objective stance is the correct one.

(3) Resentment and indignation are fitting only when the wrongdoer's conduct expressed a deficient quality of will toward the person wronged, and their fittingness is fixed by what that conduct expressed rather than by whether holding the person responsible would do anyone any good. I would add that genuine repentance makes resentment unfitting, and not merely bad policy.

(4) Cases of pure thoughtlessness or carelessness — no ill will, no disregard the agent would avow, merely a failure to have the other person in mind at all — are fully blameworthy all the same.

(5) Causal history can matter, though: an agent whose agency was covertly engineered so that at the moment of action she is reasons-responsive and acts from exactly the kind of ill will described in (3) is not, or not fully, blameworthy, and I am inclined to say the same of someone whose deepest commitments were shaped by abuse she never chose.

(6) We owe it to people to hold them responsible. To excuse someone by citing his upbringing or his social circumstances is frequently not generosity but a failure to take him seriously as an agent, and to decline to resent a wrong done to me can itself be a form of condescension.

I don't know where the strain in this set is, and I have no preferred revision. Tell me which pairs genuinely conflict and which only appear to, and rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than flagging them all as equal. For each one: what exactly has to be given up, and what giving it up would cost me elsewhere in the set — in particular whether the history-sensitivity in (5) can be given a principled stopping point short of the excuses I want to rule out in (6). Then say which single revision would clean up the most.
