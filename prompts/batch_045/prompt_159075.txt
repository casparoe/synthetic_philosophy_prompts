ok so bear with me, this is a bit of a weird one but I'm genuinely stuck on it and it's actually kind of time-sensitive.

I work at a startup that makes what we call adaptive drama: short interactive stories you watch in a VR headset, and the headset reads your heart rate and facial expression off its own sensors and retunes the story in real time — pacing, music, how bad the betrayal feels, whether the ending forgives or punishes — so it always lands on you personally. All the performers are synthetic, there are no actors at all.

My city (about 40k people, extremely proud of its summer festival: open-air dances in the square, volunteer-run everything, basically everyone shows up) is deciding whether to spend part of the festival budget on this. The plan would be: everyone gets a headset, everyone goes through the same story at the same time, in the same square, and then there's a moderated group discussion afterward. Our pitch has been that this isn't people sitting alone watching Netflix — it's shared, participatory, civic.

Someone in the local Facebook group, of all people, dragged Rousseau into the thread. Apparently he wrote something long arguing that his own city shouldn't build a theatre. I read a summary and I think I got the gist: theatre lets you feel like a good person without actually doing anything, it turns citizens into passive spectators, he had stuff to say about actors as a degraded class, and he thought a small republic should have real public festivals where people are participants instead of an audience.

Here's what I can't figure out: which of those complaints would still bite on this thing? We've kind of engineered around all of them on paper — nobody's passive, it happens in public, everyone's doing it at the same moment, there are no actors, and the whole point is supposed to be moving people toward actually doing something. But I have this nagging feeling he'd still hate it, and for a reason I'm not seeing. Specially since the emotions are literally being manufactured by an algorithm tuned to your own body, and since everyone's in the square together but sealed off in their own headset.

So: what would Rousseau actually say about this specific proposal? Would the "now you're a participant" part win him over at all, or does he have a deeper objection that our pitch walks straight into? And is there anything in his argument that would weirdly support it? The council votes in two weeks and I'd rather be able to say honestly where the thing is and isn't vulnerable than just shill for my own product.
