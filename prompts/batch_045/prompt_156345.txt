I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training at all, and I got dragged into a mailing list argument about whether a complicated enough program could have mental states. The functionalist-sounding position felt obviously right to me at first: mental states are like interfaces, not implementations; what matters is the role a state plays, not what it's made of. Then I tried to write down what that actually commits me to and I ended up somewhere I definitely don't want to be. I want to know which step to drop and what dropping it costs me.

(1) Role sufficiency: if a system has states standing in the causal relations that define mental state M — the inputs that typically bring it about, the other states it brings about, the behavior it produces — then that system is in M. What it's built out of is irrelevant.

(2) Multiple realizability is unbounded: there's no privileged stuff and no privileged list of acceptable realizers. Neurons, silicon, whatever, as long as the role gets filled.

(3) Realization is a mapping: a physical system realizes an abstract spec (a transition table over inputs, internal states and outputs) just in case there is a mapping from its physical states onto the abstract ones that preserves the transitions. This is roughly how I'd explain what it means for a chip to be running my code, so it feels like the natural way to cash out (1) and (2).

(4) If (3) is right, speed, size and part count aren't part of the job unless the spec says so. A transition that takes a nanosecond and one that takes a century are equally good realizations, provided the environment is slowed to match and inputs get queued up somewhere. I work on distributed systems, and I also can't see why the spec cares whether the pieces sit in one box or are spread across a thousand datacenters, or across a country.

(5) Any spec for a finite life at finite resolution is finite: finite inputs, finite internal states, finite outputs. So it's a finite state machine.

(6) Every finite state machine can be realized as a lookup table: state plus input mapped to next state plus output. No inference, no arithmetic, just stored answers.

(7) So by (1) through (6) there is a lookup table that is in every mental state I'm ever in. And a lookup table is, physically, pretty much a book plus something to turn the pages.

(8) A book has no mental life. Nothing is going on in there.

(9) So at least one of (1) through (8) is false.

Two things that make me more suspicious rather than less. First, somebody has to fill the table in, and in the version I built the answers were computed in advance by whoever wrote the book. If the role is the whole story that shouldn't matter, but my instinct that (8) is true seems to lean on it. Second, the argument seems to prove too much: under (3) the mapping is chosen by whoever is doing the describing, so a rock with enough distinguishable states realizes my spec under some mapping, and every spec under some other mapping. A friend says there are known versions of both the lookup-table thing and the whole-country-as-one-brain thing, so I don't think I invented this, but I don't want a name and a shrug.

There's also a version of this that bugs me in my own vocabulary: if the role is all there is, a compiler is allowed to do dead-code elimination on my inner life. Any state whose role is exhausted by its downstream effects can be swapped for whatever produces those effects, including something with no structure at all. So role sufficiency can't tell me apart from a system with the same role and nothing going on inside. Is that the same problem as the book, or a separate one?

I'd like to keep (1) and (2) if I possibly can, since that's the entire reason the view appealed to me — it's literally how I think about software and hardware. I'd rather not give up (8). My guess is the trouble is somewhere around (3) or (4), or that realizes shifts meaning between (3) and (6), but I can't pin it down and I'm not confident. Which step do you reject, why did it look fine, and what do I have to pay for rejecting it? If more than one step could go, price the alternatives against each other.
