My grad seminar has ground to a halt on the punishment half of the free will dispute, three weeks running, and the pattern is always the same. The skeptic side (Caruso's sort of position) says no one is ever responsible in the desert-involving sense, so we should deal with crime the way we deal with carriers of a dangerous disease: isolate, treat, deter, protect — but never hard treatment because someone has it coming. The compatibilist side (Dennett's sort) says that objection only lands against a metaphysically inflated desert that requires a self-causing agent, and that the ordinary, reasons-responsiveness notion survives untouched and is exactly what our practice of holding people responsible runs on. Then everyone repeats themselves.

I have been pushing a distinction on them, and I want to know whether it is the real structure or just tidy. Desert seems to do three seperable jobs in a penal code: it can be a licence (may we impose suffering on this person at all), a metric (how much), or the intended message (the suffering is meant to say you had it coming, and that is supposed to matter). The skeptic can concede a great deal on the second — proportionality gets re-derived from deterrence and incapacitation — and still fight about the first and third. The compatibilist can concede that the message is not metaphysically warranted and still insist it is socially load-bearing.

My hypothesis is that once you split it that way, the residual fight is either verbal or else a plain normative disagreement about whether a state may say something it cannot metaphysically justify on the grounds that saying it works. But maybe the real work is being done somewhere else: a buried empirical question about what desert-talk actually does to compliance and reoffending, a clash over whether the reactive attitudes are evidence or merely material to be handled, or bedrock intuitions neither side can argue for.

Give me the diagnosis, not a verdict, using what each side actually argues. Tell me what each side would have to give up for the thing to end, whether the question is ill-posed or merely hard, and where your own diagnosis is most likely to fail. Hard limit: 400 words, prose, no survey of the literature.
