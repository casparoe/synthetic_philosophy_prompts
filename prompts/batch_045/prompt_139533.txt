I help moderate an online legislative game in the Nomic style: players propose rule changes, and among the opening rules are ones specifying how rules get changed — voting thresholds, notice periods, and what has to happen for a change to count as passed. We are about sixty rounds in, and a dispute has landed on me that I have to rule on, and our own vocabulary has proved useless for settling it. The situation, as concretely as I can put it: early on we passed a rule — call it E — saying that any rule in force for at least thirty days may only be amended by a three-quarter majority, and that E itself may never be amended or repealed. Later, by a simple majority, someone passed a rule — call it R — purporting to lower every amendment threshold to a simple majority "with effect from the beginning of the game". R was voted on under the simple-majority procedure that existed before E, and a subsequent poll of players split almost exactly down the middle on whether R is now in force. There is also a rule, S, saying that no change takes effect if it contradicts a rule already in force; and a rule instructing me, as moderator, to adjudicate using "the rules as they stood immediately before the disputed change". Finally, one of our older rules has been ignored by everybody for months without ever being repealed.

What I would like is a translation, not an explanation. I work as a backend and infrastructure engineer, and the vocabulary I actually think in is: schema migrations and the frameworks that validate them, access-control lists and permission tables, who holds the right to modify the table of rights, self-signed certificates and roots of trust, monotonic versus non-monotonic rule sets, quorums and consensus, invariants, fixpoints, rollback, idempotency, and the distinction between what a system is configured to enforce and what it does enforce. I have no legal training and only a smattering of philosophy. So please restate, as faithfully as you can, the standard moves that legal philosophers make about self-amending rule systems — that a system needs some ultimate criterion of rule-membership which cannot derive its authority from the system it governs; that the power to change rules can itself be limited, and that limits on that power can be limited in turn, or entrenched, or declared unlimited; that a change to the rules about change may or may not apply to itself, and that both answers can look coherent; the contrast between a rule's validity and its being generally obeyed, including what is supposed to happen when the highest rule of a system stops being accepted; and the claim that following a rule is a matter of treating it as a reason for acting rather than predicting that someone will enforce it — and restate each of them in my vocabulary, one at a time.

Then the part I actually care about, the residue report. For each item, tell me what carried across cleanly, what only carried across after you quietly changed its meaning — and say explicitly when you have done that, because I specifically do not want validity secretly redefined as "whatever the enforcer will do" and then presented to me as a translation — and what has no counterpart at all in anything I could model as a permission system or a migration framework. Having laid that out, give me your view on whether the residue is a defect of my vocabulary or a sign that the original claim was doing less work than it appears to. Then bring it back to E, R, S and my dead letter rule: on the translation as you have given it, is our dispute a substantive one with a right answer, or an artefact of an under-specified system, and what is the smallest addition to our rule set that would make it decidable? Please do not give me reading lists, citations or named authors — I have no library access here and they would be no use to me — and alongside your answer tell me how confident you are in each piece of the translation, flagging where you are reconstructing or improvising rather than paraphrasing something settled, and single out the one claim in your reply that you are least sure of.
