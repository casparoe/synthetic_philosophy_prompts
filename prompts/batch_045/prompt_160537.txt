I had a stupidly long argument last night with my friend Sam about moral trade and I want it settled properly. So you know what we were actually fighting about: the idea is that two people who disagree about morality can still gain by trading, roughly the way two countries with different productivities do. I'm a welfarist consequentialist, Sam has strong agent-relative constraints — he thinks he isn't permitted to lie or break a promise whatever the payoffs. I can bring about cheaply the thing he thinks matters (say, preserving some dying language), and he can bring about cheaply the thing I think matters (insect-welfare research, whatever), so each of us does the other's thing and both of us come out ahead by our own lights. Straight comparative advantage. Sam's line was that this isn't trade at all, for two reasons: the thing he does for me is, by his own lights, not merely costly to him but forbidden, and there's no common unit in which to measure how much each of us gains, so "gains from trade" has no content. I said I already have words for both of those and he said I was bulldozing.

I work in market design — mechanism design and implementation, incomplete contracts, repeated games, some social choice. That's the vocabulary I think in, so use it and don't explain it back to me. Please restate moral trade, and the main objections to it, as carefully as you can in that vocabulary, and then give me the residue report. Concretely:

- The basic setup: agents whose type is a moral theory held as private information, allocations that are whole states of the world which both of us jointly consume rather than seperate bundles, each theory ranking the entire state. Where does the pure-exchange-economy analogy break, and what's the right object instead — public good provision, team production, common agency, something else?

- The gains-from-trade claim: comparative advantage in producing value, no numeraire, no price system, incomplete markets. Is the missing common currency a real problem or just barter with extra steps?

- Enforcement: no courts, no verifiability of the moral quality of what you actually did, no unwinding the act, successors who don't share your theory. Give me what fits — self-enforcing agreements, reputation, escrow, repeated-game logic — and say which of them actually has teeth here.

- Incentives over types: both sides have private info about their own theory and both gain by misreporting it. There's a classic impossibility result about ex post efficient bilateral trade under two-sided private values — Myerson–Satterthwaite, if I'm remembering the name right, but I don't care about the reference, just the shape of it. Does it bite here, and does anything about moral trade (publicly declared theories, a mediator with commitment power, correlated evidence) get around it?

- The one I actually care about, which is where Sam thinks I'm bulldozing. Call a trade 'clean' for someone if their side of it is merely low-priority by their own lights, and 'dirty' if it's positively forbidden by their own lights even though the package as a whole is best by their own lights. Sam says dirty trades are where the whole thing stops looking like trade. Try to translate 'forbidden for me to do, though I still judge the package worth doing' into my vocabulary — menu-dependent preferences, psychological game theory, guilt terms in the utility function, whatever — and then tell me honestly whether that translation keeps the normative claim or converts it into a taste. And: can a dirty trade always be restructured into a clean one at some cost, and if so does Sam's objection just collapse into a price?

- One more case: trading with your own future self across moral uncertainty — I commit now, while I mostly believe theory A, to hand resources to whichever future me believes theory B. I said that's just a multiple-selves commitment problem; Sam said it's incoherent, or at least different. Handle it, and say which of us was closer.

Then the residue report: what carried over without loss, what carried only with distortion, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. And give me a verdict on Sam. Is what's left over a genuine blind spot in the framework I use, or is he dressing up something I can already model in moral language? If it's a blind spot, say what I'd have to add to my framework to even see it.
