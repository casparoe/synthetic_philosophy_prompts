Hey — I'm a political philosophy PhD student (4th year, dissertation chapter on algorithmic fairness, mostly pretrial/recividism risk scores and hiring screens) and our work-in-progress group has asked me to do a short "here's where I stand" session before I defend the chapter proposal. I started writing my position down and two people who've read it say it's messier than I seem to think, but they won't say where. So: audit please.

Roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm convinced by the standard trilemma: you can't have a score that means the same thing across groups, equal false positive rates, and equal false negative rates all at once, unless the groups share a base rate or the predictor is basically perfect. And I read it as telling us something structural, not a temporary engineering annoyance — there's no single "fair" target out there to be discovered, so any real deployment has to pick which failure mode it's going to live with.

(2) Of the three, calibration is the one I treat as non-negotiable. A score of 7 should carry the same evidential meaning whoever it's attached to, otherwise the whole exercise of handing a decision-maker a number is a kind of fraud.

(3) I think the COMPAS-type finding — big gaps in false positive and false negative rates across race — is a genuine injustice and not just a statistical artefact, and I think correcting those gaps should be a priority.

(4) I don't think the protected attribute should ever be an input to the model that actually makes the decision.

(5) I think individual fairness — similar people should be treated similarly — is the deep principle here, and the group-level metrics are best understood as operationalizations or stand-ins for it.

(6) I'm sympathetic to demographic-parity-flavoured asks in some domains, e.g. I don't think it's crazy to want hiring or admissions pipelines to produce roughly equal selection rates across groups.

(7) I won't accept levelling down: I don't think we should deliberately make the system worse for members of the already-better-served group just to shrink a disparity.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, ordered by severity, and for each one what would have to give and what the cheapest fix costs me. Also tell me which pairs only *look* like they clash but actually dissolve once you're careful — I'd rather you discriminate than flag all of them, and I genuinely don't know which is which. Don't tell me which one I should drop; I want the tensions and my options.

One more thing: be explicit about what's actually settled among people who work on this versus what's contested. I don't want to stand up and present somebody's contested take as the received view, so flag the places where the literature is genuinely split (including about how to read the impossibility results themselves) and don't smooth that over.
