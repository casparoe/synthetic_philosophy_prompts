I sit on a committee that has to write guidance for two quite different jobs, and I want the specification before I look at any candidate view. Job one: at the bedside — telling the family of a patient who is behaviourally unresponsive whether there is residual experience at all, and roughly how much, on the basis of data we can actually get (clinical exam, EEG, perhaps a magnetic pulse and the EEG response to it, in a patient whose individual connectivity we do not know). Job two: in a statute — deciding which animal taxa fall above a line that triggers welfare protections, which requires the quantity to be comparable across architectures that share no obvious common currency. If the two jobs pull apart, optimise for job one and say where the sacrifice falls.

What I am asking for is a spec for a *measure* — a quantity with a principled zero point — not a yes/no test of responsiveness and not a metaphysical account of what consciousness is. But tell me first whether that separation is coherent: can a measure be adequate for my purposes while remaining silent about which physical systems have any consciousness and why, or does any usable measure smuggle in enough of a theory that I should have been asking for the theory?

Below is the pile of requirements I have accumulated. Some of them I suspect are genuine desiderata, some I suspect are prejudices of one camp dressed as requirements, and some probably collapse into each other. Sort them, revise them, throw out the ones that are not real, and add the ones I have missed — with the case or consideration behind each, and marked as non-negotiable or as a preference of a camp.

1. *Clinical estimability*: the quantity must be estimable at the bedside, non-invasively, without knowing that patient's full causal architecture.
2. *Divergence from behaviour*: it must come apart from behavioural responsiveness wherever first-person reports say experience persists without it — dream-enactment during sleep, dissociative anaesthesia with eyes open and no response, unresponsive patients who can willfully modulate their brain activity on instruction, awareness under anaesthesia with subsequent recall.
3. *Principled zero*: the difference between none and a little must not be set by a threshold fitted to the very behavioural measures the quantity is meant to replace.
4. *Description-invariance*: the value must not swing with the spatial or temporal grain of the description — cells or columns or regions, milliseconds or hundreds of milliseconds — and if a grain must be chosen, the choice must fall out of the theory rather than from convenience.
5. *Composition*: it must take a stance on (i) whether two identical, non-interacting brains contain twice as much as one, (ii) whether causally joining them changes the total, (iii) whether one system can contain nested subsystems that are themselves conscious, and (iv) what happens when two overlapping candidate systems come within a few percent of each other — a patient's consciousness must not flicker or split because measurement noise decided which of two near-ties is maximal.
6. *Structure over size*: it must not rank the cerebellum's contribution as large merely because of its neuron count — on the usual estimates it contains several times as many neurons as the cerebral cortex — or because of its metabolic activity, and it must either explain why a structure that large contributes so little to waking experience while much smaller cortical and brainstem lesions can abolish it, or explain why that appearance is misleading.
7. *Splitting and subtraction*: it must say what callosotomy and childhood hemispherectomy do to the amount, and the answer must not simply be read off the number of hemispheres remaining.
8. *Shape of degradation*: it must discriminate dreamless sleep, dreaming sleep, sedation, dissociation and seizure states, and it must respect the distinction between *less* consciousness and consciousness *of a different kind* — or argue that the distinction is spurious.
9. *Functional preservation*: for the familiar fading- and dancing-qualia cases, and for my variant — tissue progressively rewired so that overall input-output behaviour is preserved while internal integration is degraded — it must say whether the quantity declines, and you must tell me whether "must not decline when function is preserved" is a desideratum or a functionalist prejudice.
10. *Cross-architecture comparability*: for job two, whether numbers from a fish, a pig and a human are on one scale at all.
11. *Non-inflation*: it must not assign large amounts to simple systems — a large grid of logic gates, say — nor let consciousness switch on abruptly at some complexity threshold for no principled reason. Is "no panpsychism" a requirement or a taste?
12. *Computability in principle*: quantities defined as a maximum over all partitions or all subsystem compositions are superexponentially expensive and in practice get replaced by proxies; say whether literal computability is required, or whether a licensed proxy can inherit the standing of the quantity it approximates.
13. *Version-robustness*: if the formalism is revised, the verdict on a given patient should not flip. (The major research programmes have all been revised more than once; I take that as a fact about the field, not a criticism, but it affects how much weight I can put on any single number.)

Two things I want beyond the list. First: which subset of the desiderata, once cleaned up, is jointly unsatisfiable? That is the part I most care about, because if the spec itself is impossible then my committee needs to know which requirement to sacrifice rather than which candidate to pick. Second: which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidates?

Then, briefly — a table, a line or two each, no more: how integrated-information-style measures, global-broadcasting accounts, higher-order or attention-schema accounts, and behaviour-calibrated complexity indices derived from the EEG response to stimulation each do against the final list. The scorecard is not the deliverable; the criteria are.
