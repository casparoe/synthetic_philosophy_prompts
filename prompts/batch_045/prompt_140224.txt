first case: Alice has to choose between three things at one single moment, say three used bikes A, B and C. She strictly prefers A to B, B to C, and C to A. Nothing is changing over time, her preferences just loop. Some guy offers to swap whatever bike she's holding for the next one in the loop, for $1 each time.

second case: Alice's preferences are totally fine at any given moment, they just change as time passes. Sunday-morning-Alice wants to study all day; Sunday-afternoon-Alice wants to play video games instead. (Or the money version: on Monday she'd rather have $10 on Friday than $11 on Saturday, but once Friday rolls around she'd rather wait for the $11.) Her friend charges her $1 every time she changes her mind about the plan.

what I want to know:

- Are these really the same argument, or two different arguments wearing the same name? And what is each one even supposed to show — that the preferences are irrational, or just that they're expensive if someone shows up who's willing to exploit them? Bc in the first case, if Alice can see the whole loop coming, she can just say no to the second swap and never lose a cent. Does that mean the argument has no bite at all against someone who thinks ahead? Or is she still in trouble somehow, like she ends up with A even though she'd rather have C and there's no sequence of moves that gets her there?
- The two fixes people talk about, "sophisticated" choice (anticipate what your future self will want and pick the best plan you'll actually follow) and "resolute" choice (make the plan that looks best from the earlier point of view and then stick to it even when you'd rather bail) — do both of those make sense in both cases? It feels to me like being "sophisticated" does basically nothing in the first case and "resolute" is weird there too, but I cant really say why, so I want that spelled out.
- What if her loop is only barely a loop, like she'd pay at most a penny to move from B to A, a penny from C to B, a penny from A to C? Then no $1 pump works. Does the argument need her to be willing to pay some decent amount for each swap, and if the only pump that works costs her a penny per lap, is going round forever actually a problem or not really?
