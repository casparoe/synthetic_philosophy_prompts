I help run a grant-making DAO. We vote one-person-one-vote on binary calls like "do we fund proposal A or proposal B", "is this team's claimed benchmark real". Someone in the group chat who's been reading about Condorcet's jury theorem has started arguing that we should maximise turnout, never weight by track record, and basically stop bothering with expert review. I wrote his argument out as cleanly as I could and now I can't find the hole, even though the conclusion is nuts.

(1) Condorcet's jury theorem: if n voters each independently have probability p > 1/2 of picking the correct one of two options, the chance that a simple majority is correct rises with n and goes to 1.
(2) Nothing in the theorem sets a floor on how far above 1/2 p has to be. p = 0.5001 is fine, it just needs more people than p = 0.9 does.
(3) So crowd size is a full substitute for individual competence: for any target reliability short of certainty, and any panel of experts however good, there is some number of 50.01%-accurate voters that beats it.
(4) Adding a voter who is even slightly better than a coin flip can't lower the group's accuracy — at worst it leaves it the same, if ties get broken randomly. So excluding anyone can only hurt.
(5) We can't measure anyone's p, so the safe move is to include everyone and weight everyone equally: excluding someone risks throwing away a positive edge, and (4) says including them can only help. (Someone in the chat also said the optimal weights are proportional to the log-odds of each voter's competence, which would mean equal weights are only right if everyone is equally good. My friend says that's a different theorem and irrelevant, since we can't estimate p anyway.)
(6) So for any question at all, technical or not, a big enough poll is an arbitrarily reliable truth machine: we never need experts, review, or weighting, we just need bodies. Same goes for juries and medical panels.
(7) That can't be right. It says expertise is worthless in collective decisions, and if it were, our whole review process is theatre.

Three things I noticed but can't place. Everyone in the DAO reads the same chat, so their errors are probably correlated, and I don't know how much that matters given the theorem is supposed to be robust-ish. The questions we route to a vote are the hard ones nobody on the core team could settle, which feels like it should matter too. And somebody mentioned a "swing voter's curse", where a voter who knows they're below average should just abstain — that sounds like it could either wreck the argument or be totally irrelevant.

I definately want to keep (1), it's a theorem. I'd also like to keep the bit of (3) that says a big crowd of mediocre forecasters can beat a small panel of good ones, because I've watched that happen. I can't keep (6). Which step do I give up, why did it look fine, and what does giving it up cost me? Please don't just name-drop literature at me or say "it depends on the independence assumption" — if a worked counterexample with actual numbers is cheaper than a proof, give me that, and price the other worries I listed above while you're at it.
