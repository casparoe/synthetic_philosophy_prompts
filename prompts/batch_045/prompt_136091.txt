I am a retired quantitative geneticist who has come to philosophy rather late, and one thing in what I have been reading about distributive justice looks to me like an error of exactly the kind my own field learned to avoid a long time ago.

As I understand it, the family of views that turns on the distinction between brute luck and option luck asks us to decompose what a person ends up with into a part attributable to her circumstances and a part attributable to her choices, compensating the first and letting the second stand. Please compare this with a principle I keep wanting to put in its place, which I will call the attribution-residual view: compensate inequality to the degree that it can be attributed to circumstance; and where an inequality cannot be attributed to anything — because the relevant counterfactuals are unobservable, or because circumstance and choice interact so that no additive decomposition into their separate contributions is valid — treat it as unexplained variance. In my field the residual term was a fact about the limits of our model, not a property of the individual organism, and we would never have charged it to the animal's account; the residual view extends that discipline to justice, and says that unattributed inequality should be shared rather than borne by the person in whom it happens to appear.

Compare the two principles on three cases: a person who knowingly declines cheap insurance and is then badly injured; a person who makes a reasonable choice whose payoff depends on how many other people independently make the same choice; and a person whose decisive preference was itself shaped by the very circumstances that make the resulting outcome bad.

Then two further questions. Is the attribution-residual view a genuine rival to luck egalitarianism, or only a methodological correction to it — an error theory about whether the choice/circumstance distinction can be applied to real lives at all? And does it actually escape the familiar worry that these views abandon people to the full consequences of their own option luck, or does it reproduce that harshness in a less visible place?
