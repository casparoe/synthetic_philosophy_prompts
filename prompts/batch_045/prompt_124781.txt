hey — I'm working on karma as a *theory of desert*, not the bumper-sticker version: the serious claim that a person's suffering can be owed to them in virtue of what they've done, and that the debt can carry across lives. Before I try to build a version of it I want the spec. No citations or reading lists please, and don't lean on precise scriptural wording either — I'd rather have the criteria in my own terms so I can see what I'm actually committed to. Just: what does a theory of karmic desert have to get right to be worth defending?

Purpose, so the list isn't free-floating: I want a version that does the job retributivism does in normative ethics — explaining why it's non-accidentally *fitting* that the wrongdoer is the one who suffers, and not merely that suffering tends to follow bad acts — but with no punisher, no sentence, no judgement day. If a candidate quietly collapses into consequentialism about character formation wearing spooky vocabulary, I want a requirement on the list that catches it.

Cases I keep circling:

- the beginningless ledger: if the series of lives has no first term, everyone has an infinite backlog of unpaid desert, so "she must have deserved it" never comes out false. A theory under which no suffering could ever be undeserved looks like it has explained nothing. What's the positive requirement here — falsifiability, a cap, some statute of limitations — and is it even compatible with saying there's no first moment?
- asymmetric memory: I remember what I did last year, not what I did in a life I can't recall. Retributivism in its communicative moods wants the suffering to be something the agent can recognise as answering her act. If karmic desert mostly discharges with no such recognition, what's lost, and is that loss fatal or is it the point?
- the pre-contact ancestor: someone who lived and died where nobody had ever heard of karma, reasoning in a wholly different normative vocabulary. Does she accrue it? This smells like the "no law without promulgation" worry but I can't tell whether I'm demanding something real.
- intention vs. outcome: I half-remember a line somewhere in the Pali canon identifying karma with intention — don't quote it at me, and I don't want to build on the exact wording. Structurally: if intention is what generates desert, what happens to the person whose careful cruelty accidentally saves a life, or the one who wrecks hundreds of lives through pure negligence? Does a desert theory need intention-sensitivity, and what does that cost it?
- the currency problem: lying vs. breaking someone's arm. Desert theories of punishment always get stuck on proportionality. Does karmic desert need a common unit, or can it get by with a partial ordering, and what would make the comparisions principled instead of hand-waved?
- laundering: do good acts cancel bad ones on one balance sheet, or does each act generate its own owed suffering that can't be offset? And is there a version where merit can be transferred or redirected to someone else — I know some traditions do something like this — and if so what does that do to the desert model? It feels like it should be a hard conflict and I can't say why.
- the branch case: supposing a single stream of consciousness could split into two continuations, does the owed suffering duplicate, halve, or attach to neither? I mainly want to know whether desert needs strict numerical identity or something looser.
- the reformed agent: someone who genuinely transforms. If the old debt still has to be discharged, karma is pure retribution; if it dissolves, it looks like it was only ever causal habit. Is this the fork the whole spec turns on?

Give me the requirements list with the consideration behind each, mark which are non-negotiable and which are just my tastes, and flag the two or three I probably can't have together — that's the part I care most about. Then score three readings against the list, briefly: karma as cross-life desert, karma as nothing but causal effects on character, and karma with transferable merit. And tell me which single requirement does the most work knocking candidates out.
