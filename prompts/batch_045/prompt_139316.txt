The topic is whether generational cohorts are real kinds, and I don't just want the standard realist-versus-skeptic standoff restated. My half-formed idea, warts and all: I think both sides are fighting about the wrong thing. The realists point to survey data showing stable differences between birth cohorts in attitudes and behavior; the skeptics say the boundaries are drawn by journalists and marketers, that any apparent cohort effect is confounded with age and with historical period (the usual age-period-cohort problem), and that a few years either way moves people between categories with no difference in the underlying person. Both camps, as far as I can tell, assume that a generation is either a real kind with reasonably sharp causal edges, or a merely conventional slice of a birth-year continuum. I want a third option: something like standpoint-relative or historically indexed kinds — real relative to a location in a historical process, but not the sort of thing that could show up in a finished science of society. I don't have a good name for it and I'm not sure it survives contact with the obvious objection, which is that it's just a polite way of saying they're not real.

The other angle I want built in, and this is the part I think is actually new: the label does two different jobs at once. Officially a generation is an onset cohort, defined by birth years. But the work people make the label do is about exposure — roughly, were you an adolescent when always-on internet and smartphones became normal where you lived. That makes the real criterion a threshold on a technology diffusion curve, which has three consequences I want the proposal to take seriously: the boundary is graded rather than sharp; it's place-relative, so two people born the same year can belong to different generations depending on which country they grew up in; and it can shift retroactively as diffusion estimates get revised. So one word is carrying two definitions that disagree with each other, and I suspect a large share of the are-they-real fight is just that disagreement showing up as a metaphysical one. I'd like the proposal to make that hypothesis precise enough that it could turn out to be false.
