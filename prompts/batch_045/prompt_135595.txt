I want a specification, not a survey, and I want it before I look at any candidate theory again. The target question is what it is for a particular object to have a disposition — to be fragile, soluble, conductive, brittle at a given temperature — and my purpose for the theory is fixed: it has to be good enough to underwrite dispositional explanations and predictions in the special sciences, of the form the girder failed because the steel was brittle at that temperature. That purpose carries two constraints I am sure about. The theory must not let disposition ascriptions become empty, on the model of the old joke about the sleeping powder's dormitive power: if the ascription does no work beyond restating the behaviour, it earns nothing. And it must not make ascriptions in-principle uncheckable, since scientists do in fact get evidence for and against them.

Write me the desiderata, then. For each: the case or consideration that motivates it, and why it is a requirement rather than a taste. Be complete about the distinctions a theory may not collapse: possession versus manifestation; a disposition versus its causal basis; a mask versus the absence of a disposition; an enabling condition of a stimulus versus an interferer with a manifestation; a single-track versus a multi-track disposition; a genuine disposition versus an accidental uniformity of behaviour that no one would call dispositional. Include the standard hard cases — the fink, the mask, antidote cases, the reverse-cycle or mimic case, multi-track fragility, degreed and probabilistic dispositions, dispositions that are never manifested, dispositions whose manifestation is a process rather than an outcome, and dispositions to acquire or lose other dispositions — but do not stop with them, because it is the non-standard ones I think will do the discriminating. Add at least these, which I have not seen handled in the literature:

- The self-healing slab. A composite whose microstructure makes cracks propagate readily under load, but which also contains an intrinsic repair mechanism that seals any incipient crack faster than it propagates. Under every possible load test the slab behaves like something that is not brittle. Here the interferer is internal to the object rather than a wrapping or an external device. Does the theory have the resources to say whether this is a mask, a modification of the basis, or the absence of a disposition, and on what grounds?
- The late fink. A monitoring device attached to a glass permits a crack to initiate and then reverses it, restoring the molecules to their prior configuration, before the crack becomes detectable. The manifestation begins and is undone. Does the glass break? Does it manifest its fragility? What this probes is whether the theory needs a notion of partial, interrupted, or degraded manifestation, and whether manifestations are events with temporal parts that can be cut short.
- The chancy fink. A device that, on a trial where the vase is struck, prevents breaking in nine cases out of ten. Is this a fink operating on a probabilistic disposition, a disposition of reduced degree, or a disposition that has partly gone away? I want to know whether the theory can grade possession, or whether it must draw a sharp line and where that line falls.

Two further requirements to consider rather than assume: a theory should not generate infinitely many gerrymandered dispositions, one for every stimulus-and-manifestation pair we can describe, including the disposition to shatter if struck while a demon sneezes nearby; and it should say something about what grounds a disposition in its basis without either collapsing the two or generating a regress when the basis is itself characterised dispositionally.

Then the part I most want. Mark for each requirement whether it is non-negotiable given my stated purpose or merely the preference of a camp — reduction to categorical bases, compatibility with a Humean treatment of laws, preservation of ordinary ascriptive practice, and the fundamentality of powers all look to me like candidates for the second category. Flag any pair or subset of the requirements you think cannot be jointly satisfied, with the argument for the incompatibility; if the list itself turns out to be the interesting result, say so. Tell me which single requirement excludes the most candidate theories, and which requirement on my own list you would delete as a prejudice. A table is fine for the list, but keep the prose on the reasoning behind each item and keep any scorecard to a few lines at the end — the simple conditional analysis, conditional analyses with an interferer or exclusion clause, basis-identity accounts, and powers or potentiality accounts against four or five of the requirements is all I need there, since the criteria are the deliverable.
