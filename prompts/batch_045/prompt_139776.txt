I am putting together a take-home diagnostic for a graduate seminar on the metaphysics of modality. It will be handed out and completed before the week in which we discuss the concrete-plurality view of other worlds and the standard replies to it, and its purpose is to sort students by what they are willing to say about cases rather than by which label they have picked up in the corridor. So: no item may contain the name of a position, the name of an ism, or the name of a philosopher, and no item may be phrased in the formalism of quantified modal logic. Ordinary language, but unambiguous ordinary language.

Please build the instrument as a tree, not a list. Each internal node is one question; each leaf is a named position; and I want the tree built from the logical space rather than from a roster of names. That is: give me the questions first, note which combinations of answers they permit, then locate the actual positions in that space, and show me the regions nobody occupies as well as the ones that are crowded.

Specifications.

1. Size and audience. Seven to nine branch questions, eight to twelve leaves, pitched at students who have done modal logic but no metaphysics of modality. Every node must be phrased so that someone can answer it without already knowing which position it leads to.

2. Nodes I want on the tree, in whatever order you judge best. A realism fork (are there modal truths that hold independently of what anyone can know, verify, or stipulate?). A grounding fork (are such truths made true by something non-modal, or is modality primitive?). If the first horn: by concrete entities, by abstract representatives, by recombination of actual constituents, or by the natures or causal powers of actual things? An alien-constituent fork (could there be a fundamental property, or a fundamental kind of individual, that nothing actual instantiates or could have instantiated?). A cardinality fork (could there be more ways for things to be than there are sets of sentences, or than there are recombinations of what actually exists?). A maximality fork (must a possibility settle every question, or can there be partial ways for things to be — and if partial ones are allowed, what happens to the analysis of possibility as truth at some world?). A de re fork about individuals, put as a case. A quantification fork (does anything we quantify over fail to exist in the ordinary, actual sense?). An actuality fork (is actualness a property that some things have, or is it a term whose reference shifts with the speaker?). And one fork about the epistemology of modality: on the view in question, how does anyone ever come to know a modal claim? If you think the epistemological question collapses into one of the others, say which one and why rather than dropping it.

3. Cases. At least four nodes must be concrete cases rather than abstract claims. Use these unless you have structurally better substitutes: a wooden table that might have been made of entirely different material; a human being qualitatively indiscernible from you but numerically distinct from you; a fundamental property that nothing actual could possibly instantiate; and the possibility that there might have been nothing concrete at all.

4. Leaves that must appear, each with the name it normally goes by and one sentence on its most awkward commitment: concrete modal realism; abstract or ersatz realism (tell me whether you split this into a linguistic and a propositional variety or keep it as one leaf, and why); the combinatorial reduction; grounding of modality in the essences or causal powers of natural properties; brute or primitive modal facts with no further ground; fictionalism about possible worlds; and a deflationary or expressivist rejection of modal facts altogether.

5. Two regions I want diagnosed explicitly, because I think they are the interesting ones. First: a concrete plurality of worlds without plenitude — many concrete worlds, but not every recombination of their constituents is itself a world. Say what goes wrong with it. My own suspicion is that modality quietly re-enters through the back door, since something must then determine which recombinations occur, and that the view therefore loses the very advantage that motivated accepting the plurality. Tell me if that is right and where exactly the re-entry happens. Second: the variant that constructs its worlds out of abstract materials but helps itself to modal notions in the construction. Is that region genuinely occupied, or is it only ever set up as a polemical target? For each empty or contested region, tell me whether it is empty because the view is incoherent, because it is unattractive once the costs are counted, or merely because nobody has bothered to develop it.

6. Indistinguishability flags. State plainly which pairs of leaves your questions cannot separate, and say what kind of further question would be needed to separate them — a metaphysical one, a semantic one, or merely a methodological one about theoretical virtue. I expect trouble in at least three places: concrete realism versus fictionalism at the level of first-order verdicts about what is possible; grounding in essences versus brute primitivism; and fictionalism versus deflationism, if the deflationary view still relies on modal idiom when it explains itself.

7. Deliverable. A numbered tree, with the question at each node, arrows labelled by the answer, and each leaf carrying its name plus its most awkward commitment. Then a scoring table that lets the thing be used as a quiz: for each leaf, the pattern of answers that lands there. Then the list of unoccupied combinations with your diagnoses. Then one closing paragraph saying which single question does the most discriminating work, and which answers are wild — that is, which answer patterns lead to no occupied leaf at all — with a note on what a wild pattern suggests about the student who produced it.

One caution. I want the tree to generate the verdicts rather than stipulate them. If one of my specifications above pushes some view into a place its defenders would disown, flag that at the leaf rather than quietly relocating the view or quietly relaxing the question.
