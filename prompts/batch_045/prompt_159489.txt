Hey, I need a parody piece for something I'm putting together. Write me a FAQ page — numbered Q&A entries, around 600 words — for a startup selling "Grounding-as-a-Service" to companies whose LLMs keep making things up. The bit is that the FAQ uses "grounding" purely in the vendor sense: answers anchored to retrieved documents, citations attached, everything traceable — and never once notices that the symbol grounding problem is about something else entirely, i.e. whether a system's symbols hook up to anything non-symbolic at all. Every answer should slide from one sense to the other with complete confidence.

Things I want in there: a pricing tier that bills per grounded concept; a testimonial from a model saying it finally knows what red is because we appended the hex code and a dictonary definition to its context window; a customer asking whether a system trained only on shipping news can learn what a boat is, answered with "yes — we've added considerably more shipping news"; an enterprise tier where the model gets a camera and a robot arm but only for nouns, and only during business hours; an SLA guaranteeing that 99.9% of tokens are grounded in verifiable sources; and one line of fine print at the end that quietly gives the whole thing away.

Keep it deadpan corporate — no winking, no "as if," nobody breaks character. A reader who actually works on grounding should be able to tell that whoever wrote it gets the regress (definitions leading to more definitions, documents to more documents) and the thought that anchoring a symbol has to make a difference to what the system can do, not just to what text it has seen.
