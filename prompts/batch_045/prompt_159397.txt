I would like a map of the exposure of a position I currently hold, and an honest account of what should move me off it.

The position: trained neural networks are genuinely representational systems. Not merely that our descriptions of their internals are useful, but that there is a fact of the matter about which features a given network has latched onto, that these features do explanatory and causal work inside the network, that they are the same features our interpretability tools sometimes recover, and that this is why interventions built on them do more than fit the data we have already seen. Call this representational realism. Its rival I take to be some form of instrumentalism: interpretation is a projection, the units are artefacts of our probes and of the dictionaries we impose, and the only thing that constrains an interpretation is behaviour.

Please treat that single position as the object, and tell me what would count against it. Specifically:

1. A ranked map of defeaters, from fatal to merely awkward. For each one, state what exactly would have to be true, how plausible it is, and whether it is a defeater a realist should concede because it follows from what realism itself claims, or one that only a critic would raise. Mark which items have arguably already happened in some form, described at a level of generality that requires no lookup: failures of features to transfer, probes that report features the network never uses, steering directions that work in distribution and collapse out of it, interventions that change behaviour without changing anything a realist would call the representation.

2. Where the position sticks its neck out. What does realism predict that instrumentalism does not also predict? I have in mind three families of case, and I want you to say which of them genuinely discriminates and which is compatible with both views:

   (a) Portability. If a direction or feature discovered in one model is genuinely representational, does realism predict that a nontrivial part of it should reappear in an independently trained model — same architecture, different seed and data order — and that interventions built on it should transfer along with it? Or can the realist absorb a total failure of transfer?

   (b) Grain. Feature dictionaries seem to split one feature into ever finer conjunctive features as they are widened, apparently without a natural stopping point. Does realism predict a stopping point, and what would the absence of one show?

   (c) The ontologically alien case. Train one model on English and another on a language possessing a single term whose extension is, from our point of view, the union of two unrelated categories — say one word covering exactly the dangerous things and the wet things. The second model turns out to have one clean direction where the first has two, and each of our two probes lights up roughly half of it. Is that a discovery about representation, or a demonstration that representation is relative to our vocabulary? What would the realist have predicted in advance, and is there any answer here that could not be retrofitted afterwards?

3. The insulation question. I worry that realism has a standing escape route: every failure gets redescribed as a failure of method rather than of the represented feature — the feature was there, our basis was wrong, our dictionary too narrow, our intervention too coarse. Tell me how exposed the position actually is, distinguishing cases where that move is legitimate, because methodological failure really is the best explanation, from cases where it has become protective. And if the position turns out to be well insulated, say whether that is a defect for a claim of this kind: whether any empirical difference remains between realism and instrumentalism, whether realism collapses into the milder claim that there are real patterns which the instrumentalist can happily accept, and whether what survives is only a difference in what we are willing to say about successful prediction.

4. Finally, my own weighting of the evidence, which I would like you to interrogate. I find intervention evidence more compelling than geometric evidence, and I find a representation's failure to survive a change of training seed more troubling than its failure to survive a change of architecture. Are those the right weights, or am I simply being led by whatever happens to be easy to measure?
