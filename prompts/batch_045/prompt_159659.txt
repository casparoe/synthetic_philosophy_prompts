I keep noticing an asymmetry in how frontier AI labs actually spend their safety effort, and I want to know what it commits them to. The expensive stuff — pre-deployment dangerous-capability evals, red-teaming for scheming and sandbagging, bio and self-exfiltration test suites, capability thresholds written into published scaling policies that gate whether a model ships at all — is all aimed at a model doing something loud: crossing a line, deceiving its operators, reaching for influence. The stuff that would catch the thing being optimized slowly coming apart from what anyone would actually endorse seems thin by comparison. Two examples: when the graders in the RLHF/RLAIF loop are themselves models from a generation or two back, the target is defined by an artifact that is itself moving, and as far as I can tell nobody maintains a held-out set of freshly collected human labels as a fixed anchor to measure the movement against. And the fine-tunes, distills, and quantized versions that serve most of the actual traffic get a fraction of the evaluation the base model got, while post-deployment monitoring is mostly watching for user misuse rather than for the model's own dispositions shifting.

My read is that the pattern presupposes a specific picture of what failure looks like: discrete, agentic, and visible in the outputs, plus the assumption that our measurement proxies stay glued to the thing we care about even as the system moves around under them. But I dont trust that read. There's an alternative explanation: labs spend there because that is where spend is legible and measurable, and drift is nearly unmeasurable with current tools, so a lab that genuinely believed drift was most of the risk would still buy exactly this portfolio.

So: what does the practice strictly presuppose about the shape of failure, versus what it merely fits well with? Is the influence-seeking picture load-bearing, or could someone who took slow value drift as the primary failure mode endorse the same spending for independent reasons? Where exactly is the practice underdetermined — which rival pictures of failure would license the same behavior? And what would a lab that really believed in drift as the main failure mode have built that doesn't exist: what metric, what institution, what line on a system card?

Mark which claims you're confident in and which you're reconstructing plausibly, and if the question is confused as posed — if there's no real asymmetry here, or influence-seeking versus drift isn't the right axis to be carving at — say that plainly instead of answering politely. Under 600 words.
