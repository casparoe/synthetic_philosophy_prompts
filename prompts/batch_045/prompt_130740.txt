Me and my roomate got into a 2am argument about whether trained neural nets really have internal representations or whether interpretability people are just telling flattering stories about matrices — he says every 'the network represents X' claim is us projecting, I said no, some internal states carry content and others dont and that distinction is doing real work. I want something sharper than either of us managed before we pick it up again this weekend.

So: write me the desiderata for an account of representation in neural networks. What would have to be true of a network's state for it to count as representing X, good enough that the account can tell discovery from redescription — i.e. say when an interpretability result found a real representational structure versus when it found a useful summary we could have written down anyway. For each requirement give the case or consideration behind it, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are really just what one camp already wants to be true.

Cases it has to handle (I made these up, dont go looking for them, theyre just where we got stuck): two nets, same architecture and data, different seeds — one has a cleanly probeable direction for 'is this text in French', the other has no such direction, and theyre behaviourally indistinguishable on held-out inputs, so does only one represent Frenchness and is representation allowed to be seed-relative? A direction that predicts a feature perfectly but where steering along it and lesioning it both change nothing downstream — represented-but-unused, or not represented? A vector taken from net A and injected into net B, which then behaves as if it took the feature to be present — does B now represent the feature or is it just being poked? And the co-occurrence case: horns and stop signs always appear together in training, one direction fires on both, and given superposition a single direction routinely does this for many features at once — so what is the content, if anything.

Distinctions I dont want collapsed: representing X versus X being causally efficacious in behaviour versus X merely being recoverable by a probe; the net representing X versus our model of the net representing X; token activations versus dispositional features. Consistency constraints from how nets actually are: probes can be trained to pull almost anything out of a high-dimensional activation space, features appear and split and merge over training, nets can contain features nothing selected for, and the same function has wildly many internal implementations.

Then the part I care most about: which pair of desiderata cant be jointly satisfied — Im suspicious that 'content is fixed by causal role in the network's own behaviour' and 'a net can represent what it never uses, or represent falsely' are in genuine tension, and if they are, say which to drop and why. And name the single requirement that knocks out the most candidate accounts.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — what the right standard actually is — then defend it. No preamble, no restating my question.
