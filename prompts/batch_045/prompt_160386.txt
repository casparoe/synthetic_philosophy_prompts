I am finishing the methods chapter of my dissertation in political science, on what can legitimately be inferred from a single well-documented historical episode, and I have reached the point where a survey of positions is no longer useful to me. What I need is a specification.

Fix the object and the purpose, because I take adequacy to be relative to a purpose. The object is a retrospective case explanation: an account, produced after the fact by someone who already knows how the episode ended, of why outcome O occurred in case C. The purpose is prospective. I want such explanations to be usable as one input to reasoning about a live analogue L that has not yet resolved — say, whether a governing coalition under sustained fiscal strain will hold together over the next two years. So the question is not what makes a historical account illuminating or well written; it is what a theory of retrospective case explanation would have to deliver for its outputs to be licensed as evidence about an unresolved case, given that the producer of the account cannot un-know the ending, and given the documented tendencies of hindsight reconstruction and of narrative shaping that the methodology literature on this topic concerns itself with.

Below is my own draft list of candidate requirements, which I expect to be partly confused. Each has a gloss; I want you to repair, extend, and triage them rather than to ratify them.

1. Outcome-blind evidential weighting, tested by an erasure procedure: delete from the write-up every sentence that states or presupposes O, or its timing, and ask whether the surviving evidence still supports the stated causal ranking.
2. Live-alternative bookkeeping: at each juncture the account must name what else was still possible at the time, and must sort junctures into the unforeseeable, the foreseeable but unheeded, and the foreseen and nearly averted.
3. Base-rate anchoring: confidence must be calibrated to how often comparable initial conditions produced O, with the reference class fixed as it was at the time, not reconstructed from the class of cases in which O in fact occurred.
4. Conjunctive bookkeeping: where the account needs several steps all to break the same way, the compounding must be displayed rather than disguised by narrative flow as a single tendency.
5. A negative-control or swap test: run the same explanatory procedure on contemporaneous cases where O did not occur; if it returns equally tight accounts, it has no discriminating power, and the theory must say what follows — refusal to explain, downgraded confidence, something else.
6. Documentary-shadow correction: the surviving archive is itself partly an effect of O, since records are kept, memoirs written, and inquiries convened because the episode turned out to matter, so the evidence base is outcome-selected and not merely neutrally incomplete.
7. Grain stability: the causal claim should survive redescription at coarser and finer grain, from individual deliberation to structural pressure, and if it holds at only one grain that must be declared.
8. A reverse-narration test: if the account reads as naturally backwards from O as forwards from the initial conditions, treat that as evidence of rationalization rather than of causal asymmetry.
9. Symmetric treatment of non-events: the theory must be able to explain why O did not occur in the years before it did, and to handle absences as explananda, not only presences.
10. A stated failure condition: the account must say in advance what findings would have led its author to withdraw or downgrade it, on pain of being able to accommodate any archive whatsoever.
11. A transport license: the deliverable must identify which features of C are load-bearing for transfer to L, and must say how far and under what changes the license extends, since my purpose is exactly transfer.
12. An anti-deflation constraint: the output must remain a causally unified account of a particular episode — an explanation — and may not discharge its obligations by returning an unordered weighted list of factors.

What I want from you:

First, restate the desiderata properly, each with the case or consideration that motivates it, in the form of requirements on a theory rather than instructions to a historian. Say explicitly which of my twelve are genuine desiderata for the purpose I have fixed, which are prejudices of one methodological camp dressed up as requirements, which are really the same requirement counted twice, and what is missing. I am particularly unsure whether the anti-deflation constraint is a desideratum or a professional preference, and whether the reverse-narration test earns its place or merely restates the erasure procedure.

Second, mark each requirement as non-negotiable for the stated purpose or as negotiable, and say what would have to change about the purpose for the negotiable ones to become binding.

Third, and this is the part I care most about: identify which subsets of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied. I suspect a real tradeoff between narrative unification and calibrated conjunctive contingency, and another between correcting for the documentary shadow and keeping evidential weights independent of O, since diagnosing the selection seems to require the very knowledge one is trying to neutralise. If the full list is unsatisfiable, say so and treat that as the result, and then tell me the strongest weaker purpose the truncated list will support — that is, what I should retreat to, and what I should stop claiming in the chapter.

Fourth, name the single requirement that does the most work in excluding otherwise attractive theories, and justify the choice.

Finally, a compressed scorecard, no more than about 150 words, rating four families of approach against the list: covering-law and statistical-relevance accounts, mechanism or process-tracing accounts, counterfactual difference-making accounts, and narrativist accounts that treat the shape of the story itself as the explanation. Keep the argumentative weight on the criteria; the scorecard is for orientation only.

Anchor each requirement on either my coalition-under-fiscal-strain case or on a real episode you are confident any reader of the chapter will recognise, and say which you have used. Please do not supply references or reading lists; I have that part covered, and I want the specification itself.
