I'm in a reading group that straddles philosophy and cognitive science, and last week we got stuck on a question that I now think we were asking badly. We were arguing about whether a trained artificial system — a chess engine, a language model fine-tuned for a narrow task, a robot policy trained in simulation and transferred to hardware — can be said to know how to do something, in the same sense (if there is one sense) in which a person knows how to ride a bicycle or how to defuse a conversation. Half the group wanted to say the question is empty until we know what an account of knowledge-how is supposed to do, so I want the specification before we go further: write the desiderata that any adequate theory of knowing how must meet, where 'adequate' means adequate for this purpose — good enough to return a verdict on a system whose behavior is observable, whose internal states are in principle inspectable, and whose phenomenology is unknown.

The list should be built from cases and distinctions rather than from the names of views. Please cover at least these, each with the consideration that makes it a requirement: the difference between knowing how to do a thing and knowing how one does it (a successful agent who cannot say or articulate what they are doing, versus one who can), the difference between actual success and counterfactual robustness across novel circumstances, the fact that knowledge-how appears to come in degrees and to be lost gradually, the possibility of retaining knowledge-how while losing the ability (someone who can no longer play but still knows how the piece goes), the productivity of knowledge-how for actions never performed (knowing how to get out of a burning building one has never been in), and cases where a crew or team knows how to do something no single member knows how to do. For each requirement mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one camp, and flag any cluster you think cannot be jointly satisfied — my own suspicion is that the productivity requirement and any requirement tying knowledge-how constitutively to present ability are in tension, and separately that demanding agent-access to the method conflicts with competence-without-access, but I want your own verdict on where the real incompatibilities lie. Then, briefly, score a view that treats knowledge-how as a species of knowledge-that, a view that ties it to ability or dispositions, and any hybrid you think worth scoring, against the list — I'll fill in citations and reading myself, so please don't spend space on bibliography or on naming who holds what.

Be committed rather than even-handed: I want you to take a definite position on which two or three requirements are truly non-negotiable, which single requirement excludes the most candidates, and where a cluster is unsatisfiable, which member of it you would actually drop and why — with your reasons stated, not with the tradeoff handed back to me to weigh.
