I really dont want to turn in the kind of list where half of it is filler like what first got you interested in the guilt question, so help me write questions that bite.

Setup: the guest is Karl Jaspers, defending the four-way scheme from his short book on the German guilt question published right after the war — criminal guilt (individuals, before a court, for identifiable acts), political guilt (everyone belonging to a political order is liable for what that order does and can be made to pay for it), moral guilt (individuals before their own conscience, including for what they went along with under pressure), and metaphysical guilt (not about what you did at all but about human solidarity — surviving and living comfortably while others were destroyed, answerable to God rather than to any institution, and not expiable by anything you could do).

What I need for each question: the question itself, then in brackets the one- or two-sentence answer he's most likely to actually give (no strawmanning — I want the real move from the book), then one follow-up aimed at exactly that answer, ideally one that makes the answer cost him something.

Ground rules: nothing about his life or about how the book was received, no reading-recommendation or citation questions, and assume he won't get himself off the hook by saying it's all explained in chapter three.

At least one question each on:

- how political guilt is supposed to not be the collective blame he's officially avoiding, given that his move is to say it's liability for consequences rather than blameworthiness — press him on why he keeps the word guilt for something that apparently isn't blame, and on whether the liability outlives the political order that ran it up;
- whether metaphysical guilt is genuinely a fourth thing or just the religious shadow of moral guilt, and what's left of it if you take the God part out and put an audience of other humans in its place — my hunch is he'll say the solidarity is what carries it, so build the follow-up for that answer and see if the category then collapses back into one of the other three;
- the coercion line in moral guilt: he wants going along under threat to still leave moral guilt, but also says there's a point where you can't demand martyrdom — make him say where that line sits and whether it's a principled limit or just pity for human weakness, because if it's the latter then moral guilt is a matter of degree and not the clean binary it looks like;

- whether the scheme can settle anything concrete: make him derive from the four types who owes what to whom — reparations, official apologies, truth commissions, memorials — because if only political guilt grounds anything enforceable, then who gets paid is settled by something that has nothing to do with desert, and I want him to either own that or explain why it isn't as bad as it sounds.
