I'm in a reading group going through some metaphysics, and I keep tripping over the same shape of thing and I have no word for it. Setup: there's this relation where one fact holds because of, or in virtue of, another — the set exists because its members do, the thing's being fragile holds because of how its molecules are arranged, whatever. Call the stuff at the bottom of the because-chains the base. My problem isn't the chains. It's something hanging off the side of them.

Whenever somebody gives one of these stories there's usually, tucked in somewhere, a clause that says in effect "and that's all" — nothing more at the bottom, or nothing else of this kind. Three places I keep seeing it:

1. Natural numbers. You say: 0 is a number, the successor of a number is a number, and *nothing else is*. That last clause isn't a fact about any number, or about successor either — 0 and successor would both be perfectly fine with extra junk numbers floating around — but it isn't doing nothing: it's what makes proof by induction work, and it rules out a whole bunch of otherwise consistent worlds. It seems to be about the collection as a whole in a way that no fact about any member is.

2. The inventory of reality. Suppose somebody has finished the whole story: here are the fundamental entities, everything else holds in virtue of them. Compare (a) these entities are fundamental, with (b) only these are. (b) can't be built out of any of them, since each of them gets along fine with there being more; but it also doesn't feel like a fundamental item itself, because it seems to be *about* the base rather than part of it — to depend on all of it at once and on no particular bit of it.

3. Laws from the mosaic. The kind of story where the laws hold in virtue of the total collection of particular facts. What comes out depends on the whole collection, including on the collection's being complete: add some stray unrepeatable event in an unobserved region and the "best system" could come out different. So the dependent layer seems to depend on the base *plus* a that's-all condition, not on the base alone.

My crude test for the thing I'm after: a that's-all fact is one you can't break by tweaking anything in the base, only by *adding* to it. But that test worries me, because it also seems to fit boring negative existentials like "there are no unicorns," and if my exciting observation is just negative existentials in a trenchcoat I want to be told.

Somebody in the group said "brute fact," and that seems wrong to me — bruteness is supposed to be a property of the bottom level, the stuff that isn't there because of anything else, whereas these that's-all things look like they *do* depend on the base, just weirdly, collectively. Someone else said "second-order fact," and that seems not quiet it either: being about a collection isn't the same as being about facts, or so it feels to me.
