hey — im in a philosophy of social science seminar and we've spent the last two and a half weeks on Popper's Poverty of Historicism, and the final piece for the class can be anything we want (our prof is pretty relaxed about format), so I'd like to do something funnier than another essay. Can you write me a piece of satire?

Form: an internal document from a fictional state-funded Institute for Nomothetic History — the outfit whose job is to discover the laws of historical development and issue unconditional prophecies about the future of society. Specifically, their "Instructions to Applicants" for the quinquennial Law Submission Round: numbered clauses in flat, deadpan bureaucratic prose, around 700 words, followed by a short appendix with one badly completed application form and reviewer comments in the margins.

The comedy has to come entirely from the paperwork colliding with the actual philosophical arguments against historicism, without anyone inside the document ever noticing that anything has gone wrong. Things I want it to land:

- Applicants keep submitting trends; the Institute only certifies laws, i.e. unconditional predictions. But every field on the form forces them to state initial conditions and background assumptions, so there's a "Schedule A (Conditions of Validity)" to attach — and a law with a Schedule A is, formally, only a conditional technological forecast, which is exactly what the charter does not fund. Please give the Institute a bureaucratic way of papering over this (it keeps describing conditional forecasts as laws "for funding purposes").
- Somewhere in the middle, a field like "Will publication of this law alter the event it predicts?" with a Class-O filing category whose remedy is publication in the Retrospective Supplement — i.e. after the thing has already happened.
- A clause forbidding any law from predicting the content of future scientific knowledge, plus whatever loophole applicants have found for that.
- The whole-society problem: a character-limited field asking applicants to describe the complete present state of society, and/or applicants chopping one unique sequence into "epochs" to get the three instances the Institute requires. (Epochs supplied by the applicant.)
- A Schedule H for human remodelling — laws certified as inevitable have to come with a plan to make people fit for the coming society, which quietly concedes the thing wasn't inevitable.
- One marginal comment from an annoyed Biology Liaison Office noting that the same expectation-driven business seems to happen in molecular biology too, so maybe it isn't specific to history after all; the Institute should handle this by referring it to an inter-departmental memorandum that never gets issued.

Constraints: nobody in the document ever says "Popper" or "historicism", nobody announces a moral, and no one should ever claim trends don't exist — the complaint is only that they aren't laws. Everyone at the Institute should read as sincere and competent, just trapped by their own forms. I'd like it to build to a straight-faced reductio: the Institute's most-certified, most-celebrated achievement being a law that can only ever be published after the fact and is therefore not a prediction at all. Aim for a reader who knows the book laughing rather than wincing.
