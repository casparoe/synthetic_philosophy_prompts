My brother-in-law and I started doing a dumb little side thing about four months ago: we put small money on prediction markets and the occasional sports line, maybe 20 to 40 bets a month, evenings and weekends. It was funny money at first, but I got promoted in the spring, we suddenly have real surplus cash, and we've now got about $42k set aside with another $3k a month going in from salary. My wife has reasonably started asking what the actual plan is, and "we're pretty lucky so far" is not a plan (also I know that's the worst possible signal).

I've read the basic theory: bet to maximize expected log growth rather than expected dollars, and in practice bet some fraction of the theoretically optimal stake because your probability estimates are noisy and you'll get ruined by your own overconfidence. What I don't have is anything my brother and I can actually run. So I need a procedure, not vibes.

Constraints: it has to be runnable on a phone in about ten minutes per bet, my brother has to be able to run it alone when I'm not around, and every run has to end in either a dollar amount or an explicit no.

Here are the things that keep tripping us up, which the procedure has to handle rather than assume away:

- Our bets usually aren't independent. One news event can settle four of them the same direction. My brother's rule is "if a single news event could resolve them together, they're one bet" — but we have no idea how to size a cluster like that, or how much correlation to assume when we genuinely can't measure it.
- Where I live, winnings are taxed as ordinary income at about 30% at year end and losses only carry forward against future winnings. So a gain in December and a loss in January are not symmetric for us. Does that mean sizing should depend on the calendar, and is there a case for deliberately realizing losses before the year boundary?
- Every bet has overhead: fees and spread, plus maybe fifteen minutes of our time. I suspect a lot of our small positive-edge bets are negative once you count that. What's the minimum edge worth bothering with?
- We have a floor, not an infinite horizon. Under $25k and we shut the whole thing down, and my brother walks. We may also need to pull money out for a house deposit in roughly two years, which means selling open positions early at a haircut.
- We disagree with each other fairly often. When our probability estimates differ by more than five points, whose number goes into the formula, and how should we combine them?
- We've been writing our estimates down for four months now. I want an automatic shrink factor: something that looks at how well-calibrated we've actually been and scales stakes down when we turn out to be overconfident, instead of us arguing about it.

Please write the procedure: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each one (including what to do when you can't get a real number, which will be most of the time — these are one-off markets with no clean base rates), where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a human because the question is really about values rather than facts.

Then run it on three cases: (1) a near-certain thing at 91% when we think it's more like 94%; (2) a cluster of three separate bets that all turn on the same election outcome, priced a bit differently on three different venues; (3) our current favorite, a longshot we're both convinced is mispriced — market says 4%, we think 8%, pays about 25 to 1 — where I suspect the honest answer is a number we're both going to hate.

Also tell me plainly what this procedure can't do. And please write the whole thing in American English.
