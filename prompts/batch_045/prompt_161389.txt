I am putting together a handout for a graduate seminar on experimental philosophy, and I would like a diagnostic decision tree for the side-effect effect: beginning from the bare asymmetry (in the familiar chairman-style vignette, where a company officer adopts a profit-maximising program while saying he does not care that it will harm — or, in the other version, help — the environment, people are markedly more willing to say he brought about the harm intentionally than the help), a student should be able to answer a handful of questions and arrive at a named account of why the asymmetry is there.

Three constraints matter for my purpose. First, the tree must sort students by what they predict about cases, not by which label they find attractive: no node may ask whether the concept of intentional action is itself normative, or whether the effect is pragmatic, and each node must be phrased as a concrete vignette or manipulation that a student can respond to without knowing which account it discriminates. Second, I want the tree built from the logical space rather than from a roster of views: lay out the probes, note which combinations of answers they permit, then locate the actual accounts in those cells, and mark any unoccupied combination with a clause on why nobody lives there (incoherent, unattractive, or merely untried). Third, I am assembling this without access to my university's subscriptions, so build it from the cases rather than from the literature: no citations and no author names unless a name is the only way to identify a leaf, and no assertions about what any particular study found — if a node depends on an empirical result, say so and give me both branches.

The probes I want used as branch nodes, in whatever order makes the tree cleanest: replacing the yes/no question with a forced choice, a graded scale, or a three-option format that allows a distinct side-effect answer, and counterbalancing order; swapping 'intentionally' for 'in order to', 'for the sake of', 'deliberately', or 'did he foresee'; a side effect that breaches etiquette, a local convention, or prudence rather than morality; a case where the agent's community treats the side effect as required or forbidden and the agent shares that view while the reader does not; a case where the agent aims at the side effect but the program does not in fact produce it, or where he has no control over whether it occurs, or where the system producing it is a piece of software with no mental life; and a case where the agent is coerced or otherwise exempt from blame but the outcome is equally bad.

The leaves I want reachable are: the effect is an artifact of question format or demand and tells us nothing about the concept; moral considerations are partly constitutive of intentional action; the question is heard as asking for a reason or as an invitation to criticise, and the asymmetry is conversational; intentionality judgments inherit an independently formed blame or praise judgment; the asymmetry reduces to what the agent knew, foresaw, or could control, with normativity doing no work; and the concept tracks whether the outcome is the sort of thing the agent was disposed to promote or prevent. I suspect the third and fourth of these may be hard to separate; flag any such pair rather than inventing a distinction, and tell me what further probe would separate them if one exists. Give each leaf a name (or 'unnamed' if nobody defends it) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

Deliverable: the tree as a nested list with each branch labelled by the probe that splits it, then a compact table with one row per leaf (name, the path that reaches it, the awkward commitment), then two or three sentences on the empty cells and on any leaves the tree cannot distinguish. The whole answer should be under 700 words.
