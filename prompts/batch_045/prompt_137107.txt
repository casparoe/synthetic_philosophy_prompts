I keep coming back to the Wells Fargo cross-selling scandal as a case of reward tampering, and every time I try to set the mapping up it slips. The facts as I've got them: 2016, roughly a million and a half checking and savings accounts plus half a million credit cards opened without customers' consent, fines in the low hundreds of millions, CEO out. Branch staff also set account PINs to 0000 and put their own contact details on the paperwork so customers wouldn't get notified, the sales quotas were unreachable on the bank's own arithmetic, and blame was initially put on rogue branch employees and later on top-down pressure. Don't hold me to any of the numbers — I don't want the analysis resting on exact figures.

Three things I can't square. One: if the branch employee is the agent and products-per-household is the reward signal, then opening a fake account isn't misreporting what already exists, it manufactures new things for the metric to count, and on top of that it cuts the feedback path from the customer. That doesnt look to me like plain proxy gaming and it doesn't look like classic wireheading either, so is it tampering with the channel, a third thing the taxonomy has no slot for, or am I overcomplicating it? I've started calling it counterfeiting and I want to know whether that collapses into an existing category or earns a place of its own, with boundary cases. Two: the fake accounts generated real fee revenue, so the proxy and the actual goal tracked each other for quite a while and the divergence only surfaced downstream, through complaints and regulators. Three: the framework wants one optimiser with one objective, and this was a multi-level principal-agent mess where middle management had reasons not to look.

So: pin down the mapping you think is right and say what plays the agent and what the corrupted channel is; tell me whether counterfeiting is a genuine category and where its edges are; say whether anyone would have predicted this in advance from the shape of the incentive or whether we're only diagnosing it with hindsight; be explicit about where the theory goes silent; and say whether the case supports the convergent-risk story about countable proxies or instead shows that the interesting variable is organisational and the objective-shape stuff is a sideshow. No literature review or reading list — I can't look anything up, and I've read the usual stuff anyway.
