The three-stage thing I have in mind (fix it if it's wrong):

The lamp is a leaky indicator of the sector (say 0.9 if Hi, 0.2 if Lo). So the lamp is a genuine common cause: the outcome isn't settled when it lights, and seeing it tells you nothing about the machine's design or the printed number, the unconditional chance stays 0.80 either way. Participants get none of this. What they get is a printed table of the last 300 runs: how often the lamp lit, and how often red came up on lamp runs vs no-lamp runs. Then they commit a number.

Pick the actual numbers yourself, but they have to satisfy: the marginal chance is genuinely 0.80 whatever the lamp does; the conditional chance given lamp-on differs from 0.80 by at least ten points (I'd like it vivid — think 0.9 vs 0.55, not 0.82 vs 0.78); and the frequency table is consistent with the mechanism without giving it away.
