I'm an undergraduate in a seminar on computation and cognition, and next week I have to hand in a two-page proposal for the final paper (about 5,000 words, due at the end of term). The scope has to be honest for that: not a dissertation.

The topic is Hofstadter's MU-puzzle and the idea of jumping out of the system, but I don't want to write another paper that rehearses the mod-3 argument and concludes that insight just is stepping outside the system. Here is the half-formed idea.

Take the MIU system: axiom MI, and the four familiar rules — append a U to any string ending in I; double everything after the M; replace three consecutive I's with a U; delete any UU. Now define a family: for each k ≥ 2, MIU(k) is the same system with the third rule replacing k consecutive I's by a U. Working through small cases, MU looks derivable in MIU(k) exactly when k is a power of two: k = 2 and k = 4 have short derivations, while k = 3, 5, 6 and 10 all seem blocked, and in each blocked case by the same move — count the I's modulo k and note that the reachable residues are the powers of two, which hit zero just in case k is itself a power of two. I want to use this family as a test case for a sharper question.

I suspect that at least three different moves get called jumping out of the system, and that the paper's contribution would be to separate them: (1) abandoning the search for a derivation in favour of a proof about all derivable strings — a move forced by the logical form of the question, since no finite amount of search can ever establish non-derivability; (2) moving from the object system to a stronger theory, here elementary arithmetic about residues and powers of two, in which that proof can be stated; (3) moving up once more, to a single argument covering every k at once, which has to yield a construction in the yes cases and an impossibility proof in the no cases. Is this a real hierarchy, or does it collapse — is each jump only search in a larger space (over candidate invariants, over candidate moduli), and therefore no more of an achievement than search one level down?

Please turn this into a proposal: state the question so that it has a determinate answer; explain why the standard framing of the puzzle leaves it open; give the approach and the order in which I would actually work through these systems, and why that rather than the obvious alternatives (just generalising the mod-3 proof, or just writing a program that searches for invariants); set out the possible conclusions and what would count as the project failing, including the live possibilities that the power-of-two conjecture is false or that it is true but settles nothing about jumping out; and add a candid note on what would make this too big for 5,000 words. No citations or bibliography, please — I will supply those, and our reading list is idiosyncratic anyway.
