I teach a graduate seminar on rational choice and am preparing the session on imperfect recall. I want the central case restated in a vocabulary several of my students (and I, at one remove) find native, and I want to know what the translation loses. Assume I have a working but second-hand command of that vocabulary — Markov decision processes and their partially observed variants, policies and policy parameters, value functions, Bellman equations and their fixed points, on-policy state-visitation distributions, policy-gradient objectives — but do not lean on my knowing any result I would have to look up; state the formalism explicitly.

The case: a driver is driving home along a road with two indistinguishable intersections; he cannot tell them apart and cannot recall whether he has already passed one. Exiting at the first lands him in a bad part of town (payoff 0), exiting at the second brings him home (payoff 4), and continuing past both leaves him at a motel for the night (payoff 1). Before setting out, all he can fix is a single probability of exiting at whatever intersection he finds himself at. The ex-ante optimum is a mixture; work it out. The puzzle is that when he finds himself at an intersection he has a reason to act otherwise, and the reason does not disappear when he corrects his belief about which intersection this is — because the probability that a given intersection is the first is not an exogenous prior but is induced by the very policy whose value he is evaluating, and he cannot commit his later self to a different policy.

What I want from you:

1. A translator's version of the whole argument in the vocabulary above. In particular: the planning problem as maximizing return over a policy parameter; the action-time problem as evaluating an action at an information set under a belief; the self-locating belief (which of two indistinguishable states am I in) as the on-policy posterior over states at a given observation; and the two families of answer — resolutely stick to the ex-ante plan, versus require the action to be optimal given the beliefs that the policy itself induces — as a choice between a committed hierarchical policy and a self-consistency fixed point of a policy-improvement operator.

2. The residue report: what does not survive. My suspicion is that the crux is a term in the ex-ante objective for the way the policy reshapes the distribution over its own information sets — the derivative-of-the-visitation-distribution term that the policy-gradient theorem lets us discard when recall is perfect, and that we cannot discard here because the information sets overlap. Tell me whether that is the right place to look, and whether the resulting objective is best described as a non-causal functional of the policy (one in which the policy both selects actions and fixes the probability of being in the situation where those actions are taken), and whether your formalism excludes objectives of that shape by construction or merely has no convenient name for them. Then tell me whether anything in the vocabulary can express the difference between the driver asking what he should do now and asking what he should have planned, or whether that distinction is invisible from inside the formalism. Where the translation forced you to fudge something, say where and why.

3. A test of an idea of my own. I have been toying with presenting the second answer as an equilibrium among the driver's successive time-slices, each choosing an action while taking the others as given, with the beliefs over location computed from the same policy — a game-theoretic fixed point rather than a decision-theoretic one. Tell me whether that reframing buys anything the self-consistency fixed point does not already deliver, or whether it only relabels it while importing assumptions (common prior, common knowledge of rationality, agreement to coordinate) that the single-agent problem does not require.

Conclude by saying which part of the dispute you take to be a substantive normative disagreement about what rationality demands, and which part is a choice between two well-defined optimality criteria, and what turns on the difference. I have the sources; do not give me references.
