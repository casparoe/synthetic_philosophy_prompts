ok so im student council president (senior) and i need you to write me a speech. context: theres this gate at the back of our school by the tennis courts and its chained shut during lunch (11:50 to 12:40) and everyone hates it bc its the shortcut to the taqueria and with it closed the whole front hallway turns into a stampede. weve been complaining for like two years and the principal finally caved and said the student body gets a nonbinding vote at mondays assembly, and i get three minutes of opening remarks before the vote. i personally think we should NOT open it yet and i have to say that out loud without getting booed.

heres my problem. my civics teacher wrote this thing on the board last semester, some british guy, Chesterton i think, about a fence or a gate across a road, and the point was basically: if you cant say what its for, you dont get to take it down yet. i want whatever is actually good in that, but i cant use it straight, for three reasons. one, its not some ancient custom — the chain went up around 2017 when i was in middle school, put there by a vice principal who has since retired, so i cant do the wisdom-of-our-ancestors thing even if i wanted to. two, i genuinely do not know why its there, and im not going to invent a safety reason or a story about a kid getting hit by a car, since making up a justification is exactly what im accusing the other side of skipping. three, i cant do respect-your-elders / the-adults-know-best, the room will turn on me and also i dont believe it.

two more things i need dealt with and not dodged. my friend dev says the only version of the argument that actually has teeth is: if we open it and there was a reason, the harm could be big and unfixable, whereas if we keep it shut and the reason was dumb, we all just walk an extra four minutes for two more weeks. but he also says that argument proves you should never change anything ever, and i dont have an answer for him. and second: waiting isnt free or neutral. seniors graduate in may, we already waited two years, and "wait and find out" is itself a decision that keeps the hallway a nightmare.

so: about three minutes, out loud, to roughly 500 teenagers plus the teachers plus mr. ruiz the head custodian, whos been here thirty years and honestly probably knows why the chain is there, but nobody has ever asked him and i dont want to drag him into it or make it sound like im blaming him. no philosophy vocabulary, no saying "Chestertons fence" or "epistemology", nothing that sounds like im reading off a worksheet — it should sound like me, and a little funny is fine. it needs to end with something specific and doable (i was thinking: two weeks, someone actually goes and finds out, and if nobody can produce a reason by then the chain comes off and thats it — but if thats a bad ending, fix it), and it has to make clear to both sides that if it turns out there is no reason, i will be the one holding the bolt cutters. thanks
