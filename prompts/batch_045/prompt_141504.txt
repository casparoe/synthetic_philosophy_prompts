Hi — I'm on the methods committee at a small forecasting outfit. About forty forecasters each put a weekly probability on the same questions; we pool them by averaging in log-odds space and then pushing the result a bit further from 50%, which has scored well for us.

Two staffers want to change the workflow: before submitting, everyone sees the current pooled number and the spread, and gets two rounds of revision. Their argument is that people who are reasoning well and who've seen each other's estimates can't just keep disagreeing — someone told them that's an actual theorem — so after a few rounds the panel should converge, the pooled number should be better, and anyone who still refuses to move is being irrational and can be dropped from the panel.

I'm not sure, and I have two worries I cant quite fit together. One: we recruit from deliberately different backgrounds precisely so they start out disagreeing, and that disagreement isn't sloppiness. Two: they'd see only the numbers, not each other's reasoning, and then we'd be averaging numbers that aren't independent any more — I genuinely can't tell whether that should make our push-away-from-50% step more aggressive or less. (Also a couple of the questions have fuzzy resolution rules, which might matter.)

We vote Thursday. Could you draft the one-page memo I'll send round? It needs a bottom line — adopt, reject, or a limited pilot with a review date — and reasons the two staffers could actually answer back to, taking their argument at its strongest instead of just dismissing it. And at the end, say what would change your recommendation, and where your own reasoning seems most likely to be wrong.
