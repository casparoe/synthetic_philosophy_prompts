I'm writing my term paper for a seminar on the philosophy of art, on whether images produced by generative models can be instances of creativity, and I want to use a passage from Poe's essay "The Philosophy of Composition" as my historical foil — the essay in which he claims to reconstruct, step by step, how "The Raven" was made. The passage is this:

"Most writers – poets in especial – prefer having it understood that they compose by a species of fine frenzy – an ecstatic intuition – and would positively shudder at letting the public take a peep behind the scenes, at the elaborate and vacillating crudities of thought – at the true purposes seized only at the last moment – ... – at the cautious selections and rejections – at the painful erasures and interpolations – in a word, at the wheels and pinions – the tackle for scene-shifting – ... – the cock's feathers, the red paint and the black patches, which, in ninety-nine cases out of a hundred, constitute the properties of the literary histrio. I am aware, on the other hand, that the case is by no means common, in which an author is at all in condition to retrace the steps by which his conclusions have been attained. In general, suggestions, having arisen pell-mell are pursued and forgotten in a similar manner. For my own part, I have neither sympathy with the repugnance alluded to, nor, at any time, the least difficulty in recalling to mind the progressive steps of any of my compositions ... I select 'The Raven' as most generally known. It is my design to render it manifest that no one point in its composition is referable either to accident or intuition – that the work proceeded step by step, to its completion, with the precision and rigid consequence of a mathematical problem."

Here is what I can't settle. Earlier in the essay he says he begins by asking himself "Of the innumerable effects, or impressions, of which the heart, the intellect, or (more generally) the soul is susceptible, what one shall I, on the present occasion, select?" — so the choice of the intended effect seems to sit outside the derivation, and everything Poe calls "the precision and rigid consequence of a mathematical problem" is the deriving of the parts from an effect already chosen. My roommate, who works in machine learning, seizes on this: she says the modern pipeline is exactly Poe's composite — a human picks the effect, a mechanical procedure derives candidates, and the human does the "cautious selections and rejections" (which is, she points out, what rejection sampling literally is). On that reading, creativity was never housed in the single author at all, and the usual objection that a model lacks inner experience or "fine frenzy" loses its force, because Poe's own witness is that the frenzy was always a publicity story. My worry is that this exploits an ambiguity: saying a process is reconstructible step by step is not obviously the same as saying it is mechanical or specifiable by a rule, and Poe may mean only that every decision has a reason he can now give. A second worry pulls the other way: he concedes that authors generally cannot retrace their steps, and that "suggestions, having arisen pell-mell are pursued and forgotten in a similar manner" — which might make his own case an exotic exception, useless as a model of creativity in general.
