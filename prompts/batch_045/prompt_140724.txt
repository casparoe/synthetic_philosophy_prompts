I write about science and ideas for a living, and I'm trying to pitch a piece on how much of a given historical outcome was baked in from the start — my test case is the outbreak of the First World War in the summer of 1914. Every time I raise it in the newsroom my senior editor kills it with the same line: "there are no what-ifs in history." I'm pretty sure that sentence is doing more work then it deserves, but I can't say exactly what.

Here's the kind of work I'm writing about, so you know what the line is being used against. A small group of modelers I've been talking to rerun the July Crisis thousands of times in an agent-based model, each run nudging one small thing — a telegram arriving late, a minister away on holiday, a mobilization timetable a day longer — and they count how often the shooting starts anyway. They call that share the outcome's "fragility score," and they're upfront that the model is theirs, so what comes out the other end is partly their own assumptions. But it's not nothing: it's a way of asking whether 1914 was a hair-trigger situation or an accident of a few people and a few train schedules.

So: lay out the different precise claims "there are no what-ifs in history" could be making, from the dead obvious to the genuinely radical. For each one, tag it as trivial, substantive and defensible, or actually false, and say what it would imply — would it really rule out talk about fragility scores and near misses ("if the archduke's driver hadn't ended up on that street corner, or whatever really happened there, the war probably still comes; if Wilhelm II had died in 1910 it probably doesn't"), or does it only rule out something weaker that nobody was claiming?

Please write in American English, and open with one sentence giving me the bottom line: which reading my editor is almost certainly reaching for, and whether it survives. Then give me the menu as a compact table, one row per reading, with columns for the claim, the tag, and what it implies for the fragility-score stuff. Keep the whole thing under 300 words, no citations or links.
