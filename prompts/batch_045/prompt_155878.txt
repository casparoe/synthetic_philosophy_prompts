ok so I keep coming back to Plato on getting drunk — I mean the Laws books 1 and 2 bit where the Athenian stranger defends the symposium as an educational institution and not as a party — and it reads like something an applied-rationality org would definately roll out if their funding came from a Cretan colony. The argument is genuinely weird in a way I love: you can't acquire self-control by avoiding the state in which you'd lose it, so you induce the state and practice not yielding in it (explicitly modeled on how you train courage by exposure to what's frightening, not by hiding from it), the whole thing has to be run by someone who stays stone sober, impairment doubles as a cheap quick low-stakes diagnostic of what someone is really like once their manners are off, the old get dosed as a medicine for the hardness of age (souls like iron in a fire, that image), and the criterion for whether any of this is working is whether you've come to take pleasure in the things the decent sort of person takes pleasure in. That last bit is where it eats its own tail and I want that on the page.

So: write me a parody of an internal program-rollout memo, ~700 words, from an invented applied-rationality institute announcing a mandatory quarterly disinhibition-conditioning offsite for all staff. Structure: memo header (To / From / Date / Re), 4-5 deadpan body paragraphs, then an FAQ with three or four Q&As, then a short liability-and-consent waiver paragraph. The ops person writing it is fully bought in. No winking, no nudging the reader, no italicized asides — I should be able to tell what's being ironized purely from how earnestly it's defended.

The humor has to come from getting the actual argument right, not from generic office-party drunkenness:

- the exposure-training logic rendered in modern self-improvement jargon (triggering the failure mode on purpose, calibration under load, debugging in the danger zone), while keeping Plato's real point intact: the state being induced is exactly the state the virtue is supposed to protect you from
- the supervisor exemption: the facilitator stays sober, is never dosed, is never graded, and holds the clipboard — and nobody in the memo registers this as odd
- impairment-as-assessment: the offsite doubles as a performance review, since it strips off politeness and reveals true priorities, and it's pitched as safer and cheaper than letting people be tested by real stakes
- the senior-staff dose: the older cohort gets more, as a remedy for rigidity, and is also the cohort whose taste is the standard of correctness (they're the ones too embarrassed to sing unless dosed)
- the circle: right output = enjoying what the well-calibrated enjoy; well-calibrated = the people who already enjoy it

At least one FAQ has to come from a genuinely skeptical staffer who raises the regress (who checks the checkers — or: if it works nobody needs the wine, and if it doesn't the wine makes them worse), answered with a straight-faced non-answer that just restates the criterion or defers to the senior cohort's judgment. Don't let the memo concede anything.

Keep it readable for someone who's never opened the Laws — inline glosses for anything Greek, no footnotes, no citations. Target the shape of the argument, not Plato as a person.
