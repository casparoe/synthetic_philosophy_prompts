i'm finishing a chapter on ubuntu-style relational personhood — specifically the achievement reading, on which 'person' is a status you come to occupy by being taken up into relations of mutual recognition, not something you have automatically in virtue of your species or your psychological capacities. the bit i'm pushing is that what matters is bidirectionality: you have to be able to *receive* as well as give, because a relation you are never on the receiving end of isn't really mutual, it's just an audience.

i need a case for a grad seminar that makes this reading bite in the direction nobody expects. so: build me a single paragraph, realistic (a workplace, a parish, a block of flats — no feral children, no comas, no brains in vats), with two living adult characters of perfectly ordinary capacities. one of them, call him M, is the person everyone in the story admires and relies on; the other, call her N, is the person the story's other characters are slightly embarrassed by. the achievement reading has to come out saying M is *less* fully a person than N, and the status view (species-or-capacities, personhood as a flat moral status) has to come out saying they're persons to exactly the same degree.

constraints: the harsh verdict must fall out of the relational structure of their lives, not from a twist — M isn't secretly embezzling, N isn't secretly a saint, and the neighbourhood's attitudes can't be doing any work (that's the objection i'm trying to answer, that the achievement reading just dressed up social disapproval, so the case has to indict the community's favourite). they should differ along one specifiable structural variable.

after the paragraph: give me each view's verdict and the reasoning that produces it, say which single feature of the case is doing the work and what happens to the case if you remove that feature (e.g. M lets himself be helped once, badly), and then give me the best available attack from whichever view comes out looking worse — i want to know whether the verdict is really generated by the theory or just relabelled. last line, no hedging: \answer{achievement | status | neither} for who actually gets M right.
