I do observability work at a mid-size company — metrics, traces, logs, sampling rates, cardinality limits, SLOs, the whole pile. The daily lesson of the job is that you never touch the system, you only ever touch its telemetry, and the telemetry is lossy in ways that are easy to forget: aggregation eats the tail, sampling eats the rare event, and the schema itself decides in advance which things count as reportable at all.

Lately I've been reading about illusionism about phenomenal consciousness — Frankish is the name I keep hitting, and Dennett gets pulled into the same neighborhood, more or less willingly — and it keeps feeling like something I already believe for boring engineering reasons. That makes me suspicious I'm not really understanding it, and that I'm reading my own framework into it.

Here's the habit I want you to police: when someone tells me a service has property P, my first question is what would have to differ in the telemetry if P went away. No possible difference, no property. I'm fairly sure that quietly imports a verificiationism the illusionist may not want, so when it does work in the translation, flag it as smuggling rather than passing it off as faithful restatement.

So: restate illusionism in the vocabulary I actually use — spans, traces, sampling, schemas, lossy serialization, alerting rules, health checks — as faithfully as you can. Do the strong version (there is nothing it is like) and the softer version (phenomenality isn't a real kind, but the talk still earns its keep) separately, because I suspect they translate differently. Then take the case I actually care about. At work we have what I call the dead health check: a service whose endpoint is hardwired to return 200 whatever state it's really in. Nothing is reported, and yet the service can still be down — being down isn't the same as reporting being down. Does illusionism have room for that gap, or does it collapse the two, so that a system with no report isn't in any such state at all? If my vocabulary forces that collapse, say so.

Then the residue report: what translated clean, what you had to bend, and what has no counterpart in telemtry whatsoever — especially the part where illusionism owes an account of why we're all so convinced, and the part where suffering is supposed to still matter morally. For each piece of residue: is it my vocabulary that's missing something (no notion of a subject, of a point of view, of something mattering to someone), or is it the illusionist who is less clear than they sound? And finally, how much of illusionism survives into my vocabulary, and how much of what survives was already mine rather than theirs?
