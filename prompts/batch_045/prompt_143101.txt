Hi — hoping someone here has read more of this literature than I have. I keep running into the same shape of thing in writing about money in politics, and I want a word for it if there is one. But more than the word: I want to know whether what I'm looking at is one thing or three things stuck together, because I keep sliding between descriptions of it and I can't tell if I'm describing a real pattern or just getting annoyed at the same article over and over.

Here's the pattern as I see it. Everyone agrees there's too much money in politics; a reform gets passed that closes off the channel the money is most visibly flowing through — hard money caps, party soft-money accounts shut down, a disclosure threshold tightened, a cooling-off period before a former official can lobby their old office. A couple of cycles later the money is somewhere else: an outside group that technically doesn't coordinate, a trade association that doesn't have to name its funders, a "strategic advisory" firm staffed by the same people who used to file lobbying registrations. Fine — money relocates, everyone says money is like water and finds an outlet.

The bit I don't have a word for comes after. The reform gets scored by whether the original channel dried up. Registrations fall and reformers announce that disclosure worked; registrations climb back and opponents announce the reform was theater. The number is real and it really moved. Nobody is measuring the thing the reform was supposed to change — how much sway money actually buys, whose preferences the system tracks — because that's expensive and contested to measure, and both sides in the argument do better arguing about the cheap number. And as far as I can tell nobody is lying: the channel-level figure is accurate, it's just a figure for a different quantity than the one everyone is arguing about, and everyone knows that, and nobody has an incentive to be the person who says it out loud.

Things I've been pointed at that don't seem to be it: regulatory arbitrage (usually about deliberately exploiting a gap between two rules, and here a lot of the relocation is just people doing their jobs under a new rule rather than hunting for a loophole), Goodhart's law (the measure isn't gamed — it's knowingly used as a stand-in, which isn't the same failure), the waterbed or balloon effect (right about the relocation, silent about the scoring), and the streetlight effect (too general, it's about where you look, not about why you keep looking there after you've been told). Maybe I'm dismissing one of those too fast, in which case say so and say why.

My hunch, which I can't tell is deep or obvious: the two halves are one thing. You can only write a rule against a channel, because channels are the only part of this you can define in a statute — so every reform is scored against the channel it closed, and being scorable against that channel is what makes it look like it worked even when nothing much changed. The relocation isn't an unfortunate side effect of the reform; it's what you should predict from the fact that the reform had to be written against something nameable.

So: does this have a name of its own? If it does, what's in it and what isn't? If it doesn't, give me one and define it tightly enough that it doesn't collapse into the four near-misses above, and tell me what would count as a genuine non-instance — a reform that actually changed how much money buys rather than where it flows — under your definition. Two cases I want handled separately, because I think they come apart: (a) a court decision reopens the channel rather than the industry finding a new one, and (b) a public financing scheme with small-donor matching, where the total goes up but the causal link between any one donor and any one outcome gets weaker. Is (b) the same phenomenon with a happy ending, or a different animal entirely? And be explicit about whether your concept needs anyone to intend the relocation, or whether it's purely structural.

Don't send me a reading list or a pile of citations, I've got library access and I'll find those myself. I want the concept and its edges.
