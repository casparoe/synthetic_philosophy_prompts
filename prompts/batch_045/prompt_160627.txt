I co-direct a digital history project that is annotating a corpus of roughly 900 passages — drawn from monographs, commission reports, newspaper coverage, and survivor testimony — in which a causal claim is made about one well-documented disaster: the breaching of a colonial-era dam in 1902 that flooded a downstream district and killed several hundred people. Our coders annotate claims, not the world: the first field records the causal structure a passage commits itself to, the second records which of the causes it names should be narrated as *the* cause. We want the manual's taxonomy built from the logical space rather than from a list of famous labels, so that two coders who disagree can be shown exactly where their answers diverge.

Please write the decision tree for that manual, with the following constraints.

**Anchor cases.** Every node question must be anchored in one of six calibration cases from the corpus, and must be answerable by someone who has read the case file but has no philosophy training:

- A. Two demolition charges, planted by unconnected saboteurs, each independently sufficient to open the breach; both detonated within the same second. The report says the breach was caused by the north charge and by the south charge.
- B. Same two charges, but the north one fired at 04:12 and did the work; the south one was timed to fire at 04:20 if and only if the north one failed, and never fired. A monograph still calls the south charge a cause, because it guaranteed the outcome.
- C. The breach required three things present that morning — a weakened masonry course, an unusually high reservoir level, and a intake gate left open overnight so that debris struck the dam face. None was sufficient alone; each was necessary given the others. The testimony blames the gate alone.
- D. The night watchman was asleep and so did not shut the gate; had he been awake he would have shut it. A survivor blames the watchman's sleep.
- E. A petition delivered two months earlier, which the district engineer read and which changed no decision: he had already refused the repairs. Nearly every narrative in the corpus names the petition as a cause. Under any difference-making test it made none.
- F. The two charges, but described at finer grain: one opened the breach on the north face at 04:12:04; the other widened it twelve minutes later. Under coarse descriptions the case looks like A; under fine ones it does not.

**Nodes.** Eight or nine branch nodes, numbered, each phrased as a question a coder can answer from a case file without already knowing which position it leads to. They must jointly cover at least these fault lines: whether a cause has to be a difference-maker under hypothetical intervention; whether overdetermination should be dissolved by redescribing the outcome more finely in time, place, or manner (include a grain node, and make it ask whether the finer description is genuinely available or merely gerrymandered to fit); whether a cause may be an insufficient but non-redundant part of an unnecessary but sufficient condition; whether omissions and absences can be causes; whether a cause can be selected without a contrast class; and whether a passage that ranks its causes — calling one the real, decisive, or underlying cause — is thereby committing to something the listed causes alone do not settle.

**Leaves.** Seven named positions, each with a one-sentence statement of its most awkward commitment and a note of which calibration case presses it hardest. The set must include: a strict interventionist difference-making view; the insufficient-but-non-redundant-part view; a view on which apparent symmetric overdetermination dissolves under finer description of the outcome; a contrastivist or interest-relative view about causal selection; a production or process view that retains causes where counterfactual tests fail; a view that treats omissions such as the watchman's sleep as causally inert; and a narrativist or anti-realist view about causal selection in history.

**Output fields.** The tree must be able to return different verdicts for the same passage on the two fields, and must state when a split verdict signals a merely verbal dispute (same counterfactual commitments, different event description or contrast class) rather than a substantive one. It must also allow a coder to record that a named item is causally inert but frame-setting: it fixed the terms, options, or contrast class within which the operative causes worked, though it changed nothing downstream. Say how that code is earned and how it is to be kept from becoming a wastebasket.

**Gaps and collisions.** Identify at least three cells of the logical space that no named view occupies, and diagnose each as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate, and give the further question that would separate them if we could ask it.

**Format.** A short preamble for coders of no more than 200 words; then the numbered nodes with branch conditions; then the leaves; then a short note on how to resolve inter-coder disagreement. The manual is used offline in an archive with no network access and by coders with no philosophical training, so identify every position by its content and never by an author, title, or citation — no references, bibliography, or further reading of any kind.
