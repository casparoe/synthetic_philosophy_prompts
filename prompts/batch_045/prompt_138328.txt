Analyze the Jesus Seminar as a case study in the criteria of authenticity used in the quest for the historical Jesus, and then say where the criteria break down. The episode: the Jesus Seminar was founded in 1985 by Robert Funk under the auspices of the Westar Institute; it had roughly 150 members, including both trained scholars and lay participants; members voted by casting colored beads to register graded judgments about whether individual sayings and deeds attributed to Jesus were authentic, and the project published its results in color-coded form, most prominently in The Five Gospels (1993), with later volumes on the deeds of Jesus and a composite gospel. I am not asking for a summary of the conclusions of the Seminar or for a bibliography of the quest; I know the general outline. What I want is a disciplined mapping in both directions.

Treat the criteria-of-authenticity approach as the theory: the use of tests such as multiple attestation by independent sources, the criterion of embarrassment, dissimilarity from both first-century Judaism and the early church, coherence with material already judged authentic, and Palestinian or Aramaic linguistic and environmental features. Say what the theory implies the participants were doing when they voted: who or what the relevant actors and options are (is the unit of analysis the individual tradition, the voting Fellow, or the aggregate?), what counts as evidence for or against authenticity on the theory, and what the Fellows should have concluded about specific kinds of material if they were applying the criteria strictly — for example, apocalyptic sayings about the imminent coming of the Son of Man, parables and sapiential aphorisms, passion and resurrection predictions, and sayings preserved only in the Gospel of Thomas or only in the Gospel of John.

Then identify which features of the episode the theory explains: the low overall authentication rate of the Seminar, the non-apocalyptic portrait of Jesus as an itinerant sage, the willingness to treat the Gospel of Thomas as a possible source of authentic material, the reliance on the criterion of embarrassment, and the suspicion of the Gospel of John. Next, identify what the theory misses or misdescribes: the bead-voting and averaging procedure, the decision to place the burden of proof on advocates of historicity, and the working premise that Jesus was not an apocalyptic prophet. Are these required by the criteria, compatible with them, or independent additions that do the real explanatory work?

Finally, ask what the episode shows about the criteria themselves. Is the Jesus Seminar a confirmation of the criteria, an anomaly they cannot handle, or a case they never anticipated — namely, the application of pericope-level tests through a collective, graded voting procedure in order to settle the global question of who Jesus was? Be explicit about where the theory is silent rather than forcing it to speak, and give a verdict on whether the criteria could ever, even in principle, settle the identity of Jesus rather than merely rank discrete traditions.
