Construct a thought experiment for a paper I'm writing on whether the responsibility gap for autonomous systems is a genuinely novel moral problem or a familiar problem in new clothing. I need a case in which every familiar explanation of the gap is unavailable, so that whatever residue is left over — if any is — is the real thing.

The scenario: one paragraph, set in a mundane institution that could plausibly procure such a system within the next year — a hospital, an ambulance service, a water utility, a warehouse, a school district. No autonomous weapons, no science fiction, and no system with beliefs, intentions, or agency: the machine is a controller or a predictor and is not a candidate for blame. The harm must be a death or serious injury to an identifiable person, not a line in a cost-benefit table.

The gap your case generates must not be traceable, even in part, to any of the three usual suspects: the system's opacity or unpredictability, the diffusion of decisions across many hands, or the truism that a machine cannot be blamed. Each must be absent from the case, or present in a form that cannot be what is doing the work. In particular, the gap must survive the fact that the outcome was foreseen in aggregate before deployment and accepted as the price of a system that is, on the whole, the best available option.

Then run the check, response by response:

- Blaming the human in the loop must be blocked by the design of the case rather than by stipulation: overriding the system has to be something no reasonable person in that role would have done.
- Assigning liability to the manufacturer or the operator must succeed cleanly — compensation is paid, nobody litigates — and must nonetheless leave the moral question open.
- The abstentionist response, that one should not field systems whose harms cannot be traced to a responsible agent, must be unavailable because the alternative is worse.
- The debunking response, that this is only an ordinary accident without a culprit, must fail.

Close by naming the structural feature of the case that is doing the work, saying which of those four responses becomes available again if that feature is removed, and giving the strongest version of the objection that there is no gap here at all, together with your verdict on whether it lands.
