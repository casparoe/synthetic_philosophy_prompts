hey — you're a professional philosopher who works on anthropic reasoning and self-locating belief, so please treat this like a real puzzle: brainstorm two or three candidate diagnoses before you commit to one, and tell me straight if you think the whole thing is built on a mistake I can't see.

Some background on how I got here. A few weeks ago a friend started argueing that SIA should make us much less worried about existential risk: conditional on finding yourself existing at all, you're supposed to favour worlds with vastly more observers, and a world where humanity ends early has vastly fewer, so "I exist" is evidence against doom. I said that sounded like the presumptuous philosopher in a Halloween costume. He came back with an argument that anthropic reasoning does nothing whatsoever — which would kill his own move too, and he's fine with that. I've been turning it over and I can't find the exit. Here it is as he gave it:

(1) Anthropic reasoning is just Bayes' theorem applied to the datum E = "I find myself existing, here, now, as the observer I am." SSA and SIA are rival ways of filling in the numbers, not rival kinds of reasoning.
(2) Posterior odds = prior odds × likelihood ratio, so all the action is in P(E|H)/P(E|H').
(3) So compute the likelihoods. Let "I" pick out one particular person with one particular history. Take any two hypotheses still in play — say "the universe contains 10^24 observers" and "it contains 10^80." Under each, either I exist or I don't. If I don't exist in that world, then it's refuted outright by my evidence and no anthropic adjustment is needed. So every hypothesis that survives at all has P(E|H) = 1.
(4) Hence the likelihood ratio is 1:1 no matter how much the two hypotheses differ in how many observers they contain. SIA's trillion-fold boost for the big world is an arithmetic error, and so is the doomsday argument's push toward early doom. The whole literature is noise.
(5) And it generalises: the same holds for any purely self-locating datum. Sleeping Beauty's evidence is "I am awake now," and she is certain to be awake at some point under heads and under tails alike, so both likelihoods are 1, so neither thirds nor halfers get to update on anything self-locating.
(6) So: anthropic reasoning does nothing, self-location does nothing, end of story.

Here's why I don't believe it even though part of me wants to. There are three things I really don't want to give up. Opening the curtains of my hotel room and seeing the Eiffel Tower obviously does update me on which city I'm in, and that looks like a self-locating update. Anthropic selection effects are the standard reply to fine-tuning — of course the constants look life-permitting, who else would be around to look — and that seems legitimate to me. And I'd still like to keep the claim that the presumptuous philosopher's trillion-to-one leap, made from an armchair with no new data, is bogus. If the argument takes all three of those down with it, I'm not buying it, but I can't say which step is the fake one.

He says that's just the usual weirdness of these cases and the argument survives; I say something about the word "I" in (3) is doing work it has no right to do, but I can't say what, or what it would cost me to say it properly.
