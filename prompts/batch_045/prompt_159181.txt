I am writing a thesis chapter on the metaphysics of synchronic consciousness, and before I return to the literature I want a specification rather than another survey. What I need is a statement of what an account of phenomenal unity has to be able to do in order to be adequate for one particular job. The job is adjudicative: I want a theory that could be handed to someone with a callosotomy patient, a hemispherectomy patient, or a surgically split animal preparation in front of them, together with the behavioural and neural data, and that would tell them what the data settle about (i) how many conscious subjects are present, (ii) how unified each subject's field is at a time, and (iii) what would move the verdict either way. It should also say what turns on the verdict: why anyone other than a metaphysician should care whether the answer is one or two.

So: give me the desiderata. For each, the case or consideration that motivates it, and whether you count it as non-negotiable or as a preference internal to one tradition. I especially want you to mark the desiderata that are doing covert work for a favoured theory rather than constraining all of them. Then flag any subset you think is jointly unsatisfiable — that is the part I care about most — and name the single requirement that excludes the largest share of the views currently in play.

The candidates I keep returning to, in no particular order, with my own gloss; reject any that are really camp commitments in disguise:

It must get the ordinary case right: a waking subject whose visual, auditory, tactile, proprioceptive, affective, and cognitive states at a time are experienced together — without inflating that into something metaphysically spectacular or deflating it into nothing.

It must handle the surgical cases: patients who produce conflicting manual responses to a single chimeric stimulus, who show intermanual conflict and alien-hand phenomena, and who verbally avow one thing while a hand does another. And it must handle the cases pulling the other way: hemispherectomy and callosal agenesis patients who are, on ordinary testing and ordinary self-report, strikingly normal.

It must have a principled way of excluding pseudo-splits: blindsight, unilateral neglect and extinction, inattentional blindness, hypnotic and deeply absorbed states, sleepwalking, automatic writing. If its criterion for a fracture is merely that there are two informationally insulated processing streams, it will over-generate, and I want to know what stops it.

It must keep apart phenomenal unity, access- or report-based unity, diachronic unity of a subject or self, unity of agency or motor control, and personhood in the moral or legal sense. My guess is that a large share of the apparent disagreement in this area is a collapse of two of these.

It must say whether unity admits of degree, whether the relation it appeals to is symmetric and transitive, and whether unified means one subject, one field, or one state. My suspicion is that unity is an all-or-nothing equivalence relation and unity admits of intermediate cases, which is what the surgical data look like, cannot both be required; tell me whether that tension is genuine and which horn to take.

There is also a methodological requirement I think is under-discussed and want on the list, which I will call the report bottleneck. In the split-brain cases the first-personal evidence is delivered almost entirely through one hemisphere's language production, and in the rest of us introspective report is itself the output of a single narrow channel. So the theory must say what evidence could in principle distinguish one subject with two partially disconnected experiential streams, two subjects in one body, and one subject with one stream plus confabulation in the reporting channel; and it must not secure its verdict by defining unity as whatever reaches that channel, since that begs the question. Relatedly, it must take a stand on the mute-stream case, where a hemisphere is a serious candidate for having experiences but has no route to behaviour or report at all. I can construct that case; I cannot see which theories can even formulate a verdict about it, or whether being unable to is itself a strike.

Two more I want adjudicated rather than assumed: whether the theory must show that unity does explanatory work — that unified contents are what make deliberate action, self-knowledge, or rational inference possible — or whether it may conclude that unity is idle with respect to all of that, and whether that conclusion is a desideratum violated or a discovery; and whether the theory may answer at the subpersonal level, in terms of a neural or information-theoretic signature, and if so whether it must commit to unity's having one mechanism across cases or many.

No bibliographies or reading lists. A compact table scoring the familiar families — subsumption accounts, primitive co-consciousness accounts, integrated-information accounts, global-workspace accounts, quietist or dismissivist accounts — is welcome but secondary; I want the criteria argued, with the cases doing the arguing. Where a requirement is motivated by a case I have described loosely, say so and sharpen the case as you go.
