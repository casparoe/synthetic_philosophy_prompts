I work as a database engineer, and my working vocabulary is that of a system with multi-version concurrency control: append-only write-ahead logs, snapshot isolation, commit order, checkpointing, log truncation and compaction, the partial causal order given by happens-before, and reads that resolve against a chosen snapshot rather than against the state of the world. I can use claims like no transaction ever observes an uncommitted write, or this read resolved against snapshot 41 and not snapshot 40, with full precision, but I have no philosophy training.

Please restate the dispute between presentism and eternalism in exactly that vocabulary. I want, translated one by one, the claim that only presently existing things exist, the claim that past and future things exist tenselessly, the growing-block variant in which the past accumulates and the future does not exist, and whatever position is nearest to a system that has a total commit order but no global clock and therefore no privileged now. For each, say what exists has to be rendered as in my terms: stored, committed, queryable, visible at a snapshot. Where more than one rendering is possible, say which one the position needs and flag places where the choice of rendering is doing argumentative work.

Then I want the residue report. Two places worry me. First, a store like mine can answer historical queries only because it retains log records, so the presentist's difficulty about what makes past-tensed claims true looks to me like the difficulty of a database that has truncated its log and is then handed a historical query: is that a fair rendering, or does it smuggle in the eternalist assumption that retained data is retained reality? Second, checkpointing and compaction mean my system keeps the remote past only in compressed form, at degraded granularity, and I have been toying with the idea that this is itself an ontological option rather than an engineering compromise: the remote past exists, but thinly, in proportion to how much of it the store still distinguishes. Tell me whether that is a coherent third position, a version of the growing-block view, or an implementation detail in a metaphysical hat.

Finally, report what has no counterpart on either side. I have no word for the difference between a snapshot's being the current one and time's seeming to pass, and no word for fundamentality. Tell me whether those absences are the interesting residue, and whether each is a blind spot of my vocabulary or a sign that the disagreement being translated is thinner than it looks.
