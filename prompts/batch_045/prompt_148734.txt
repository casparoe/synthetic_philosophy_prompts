Hey — I run a fortnightly study group for a mix of first-year philosophy kids and CS kids, and I'm trying to build one decision tree (or quiz, whatever works better) that takes someone from "I guess everything that happens follows from what came before plus the laws" to a named position on determinism and predictability. I keep botching it because I mash three questions together that feel identical but really aren't, and my flatmate who does ML keeps saying "deterministic systems can be unpredictable, so what's the fuss" — which I'm pretty sure only lands them in one cell of a much bigger space.

So here's what I want the tree to sort on. First, whether the state of the world plus the laws fix all later states — and if not, whether the slack is law-governed chances (stable, measurable probabilities) or something more bare, like genuinely singular events that nothing even statistically constrains. Second, predictability, but split by *who is doing the predicting*: an outsider with unlimited precision and no size limit (the demon-ish one), an embedded predictor that is itself part of the system and has to forecast its own future including the effects of making the forecast, and a third kind of agent who only ever needs coarse-grained, statistical, or "it'll come back near here eventually" forecasts. Third, how mistakes in the initial data behave: do they stay small, grow gently, or blow up so that any finite specification of the starting state eventually stops helping. And fourth — this is the one I think people forget — whether the correct evolution is even the sort of thing a finite description could compute, i.e. a world that is fully deterministic but where no step-by-step shortcut exists and you'd have to just let it run to find out.

Concretely: 8-10 branch nodes, each written as a question someone could answer without already knowing which view it leads to, in plain language, and with any technical term (Lyapunov-ish stuff, measure talk, computability) glossed in half a clause. At least three of the nodes should be anchored to a concrete case rather than a formula — weather forecasting, a double pendulum, a pseudo-random number generator, a radioactive decay, someone trying to predict what they'll type next, that kind of thing. Give me 6-8 leaves, each with the name of the position (or "unnamed" if nobody holds it) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Please build it from the logical space rather than from a list of isms: lay out the questions, note which combinations of answers are actually unoccupied, and say for each whether that's because the combo is incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely can't pull apart — I suspect compatibilist and incompatibilist flavors of determinism will collapse together here even though their holders fight constantly, and I'd rather you say that explicitly than fake a node that separates them.
