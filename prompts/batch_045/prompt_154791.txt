I work in causal inference — directed acyclic graphs, do-calculus, confounding and common causes, mediators and colliders, instrumental variables, surrogate endpoints, principal stratification. I use this apparatus daily, so please don't explain it to me, and don't give me a bibliography or named authors and papers; I will add references myself, and I want the argument rather than the literature.

What I want translated into that vocabulary: epiphenomenalism about phenomenal consciousness, the view that experiences are produced by physical or neural states but themselves cause nothing, together with what gets called the paradox of phenomenal judgment. Roughly: if experiences cause nothing, then my judgement that I am in pain is not caused by my being in pain, and it becomes hard to see how that judgement is about pain, or justified by it, or why the epiphenomenalist should think her own introspective reports give her any reason to accept a theory that makes them idle.

Two things I would like handled. First, I suspect that causation is doing several different jobs in that paradox, and that the versions come apart once you force them into my terms. Separate out at least the following three readings and translate each: (a) the evidential reading, on which the absence of a directed path from experience to judgement means the judgement has the wrong causal history to count as knowledge or as justified belief; (b) the self-undermining reading, on which the theory predicts that the very belief supporting it is not explained by the phenomenon it concerns, so that the reliability of that belief is left unexplained; (c) the semantic reading, on which reference and content are supposed to be fixed by causal contact, and epiphenomenalism removes the contact. Say which reading, if any, the translation makes look strongest.

Second, here is my half-formed thought, which I want tested rather than flattered. In my terms the dependence of report on experience looks purely associational: the neural state is a common cause of both, so the report is informative about the experience in the way a confounded marker can be informative, while intervening on the phenomenal node changes nothing downstream. My guess is that the philosophical worry is not about association at all but about what happens under intervention on the phenomenal node itself — that first-personal authority appears to require the report to track the state under interventions that set the state, not merely under interventions on its ordinary causes, and perhaps even to require tracking when those ordinary causes are held fixed. Does that distinction exist in causal-inference terms, or am I inventing something? If it exists, is it the distinction the paradox trades on, or a different one? And can you state, in my vocabulary, what would have to be true of the graph for the report to have whatever it is that the paradox says it lacks?

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the epiphenomenalist claim and of the paradox translate cleanly into graphical and counterfactual terms, which survive only with distortion and what the distortion is, and which have no expression in that vocabulary at all — I expect normativity, aboutness, and the first-personal character of the judgement to be candidates. For the residue, tell me whether it points to a genuine gap in my framework or to an unclarity in the position I asked you to translate, and if the latter, say what would have to be made precise to remove it.

About 1,200 to 1,500 words, in British English. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — the single claim you would defend about how much of the paradox survives the translation — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and without restating my question back to me.
