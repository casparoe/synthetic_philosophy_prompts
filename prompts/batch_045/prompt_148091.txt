Writing a piece on moral foundations theory — the five- or six-foundation setup, care and fairness on one side versus loyalty, authority, sanctity and liberty on the other, and the finding that self-described liberals score high on the first two and much flatter on the rest. Every person I interview reaches for the same analogy: morality is like taste. Foundations are receptors on the tongue, few of them, innate, the same ones in everybody; culture is the cuisine that activates them; political disagreement is just different palates. One source wheels it out to conclude that there is no fact of the matter about who is right — it is sweet versus sour, so the psychology of left and right tells us nothing about who is right. Another uses the exact same picture to conclude the opposite: that the left is missing receptors and should start cooking binding-foundation dishes if it wants to win elections.

Can you audit it the way you would audit any analogy that is doing argumentative work? Give me the correspondence table — what plays the role of the receptors, the tongue, the dish, the cuisine, the supertaster — and then the places the mapping gives out.

Two things I want flagged specifically. First: the picture of taste being imported looks like the old tongue map, sweet at the tip and bitter at the back, which as far as I can tell is not how taste works — receptors are distributed across the tongue, most of what we call flavour is actually retronasal smell, and the number of basic tastes is unsettled. Does it matter that the source domain as popularly imagined is itself wrong, and does the analogy get stronger or weaker if you swap in the real biology? Second: sensitivity to bitterness varies genetically, and if that is what carries the analogy then it quietly imports a genetic story about why left and right differ, and with it a cant-help-it excusing move. Does it have to?

Then the payoff: once the disanalogies are on the table, can this analogy support either conclusion, or is it carrying them the whole way? And is there a better analogy — something with learning, training or expertise built into it rather than fixed receptors, music or language maybe — that does real work on the question I actually care about, whether the psych of left and right tells us anything about who is right?
