Please act as a professional philosopher specializing in philosophy of language and meta-semantics, with a thorough command of Quine's indeterminacy of translation, the inscrutability of reference, and the post-Quinean replies and objections. I would like you to think carefully and work through this in stages: begin by brainstorming two or three candidate diagnoses of the argument below and sketching what each would cost, then commit to the one you think is strongest and argue for it. Please write your answer in American English.

I am a PhD student in philosophy, working on a seminar paper in which I want to push back on Quine's indeterminacy thesis, and I have built an argument that I now find suspicious because its conclusion is one I cannot accept — it seems to turn a thesis about translation between languages into global meaning skepticism. I presented a version of it at our department's works-in-progress group on Tuesday and got three incompatible replies, so I am hoping for a cleaner diagnosis. The argument runs as follows:

(1) Indeterminacy of translation: the total evidence available in principle to a radical translator — the natives' dispositions to assent to and dissent from sentences under sensory stimulation, together with everything behavioral that could in principle be added to that — underdetermines the correct translation manual. Two manuals can each be fully adequate to that total evidence while assigning incompatible truth conditions to some native sentence.

(2) Inscrutability of reference, via the proxy-function argument: the same evidence cannot settle whether the native term in the gavagai example refers to rabbits, to undetached rabbit parts, to rabbit-stages, or to some other proxy-substituted ontology. Any permutation of the ontology that preserves the truth conditions assigned to whole sentences yields an equally adequate manual.

(3) The linguist's epistemic position with respect to her own idiolect is not relevantly better than her position with respect to the natives'. Her evidence about what she herself means is, at bottom, of the same kinds: her own dispositions to assent, her own overt behavior, her own history of utterance. Introspection gives her no extra evidential purchase on reference, since reference was supposed to be inscrutable on the basis of precisely such evidence.

(4) Now consider what I call the mirror case. Take the homophonic manual for the linguist's own language — the manual that maps her words onto the same words — and take its proxy-permuted rival, which maps "rabbit" onto rabbit-stages and permutes the rest of the ontology in the way the proxy-function argument permits. Because such a permutation preserves truth conditions at the level of whole sentences by construction, this rival manual fits every bit of evidence the linguist has, including her own dispositional evidence. So by (1) and (2) applied to her own case, it is indeterminate whether she means rabbits or rabbit-stages by her own word.

(5) If it is indeterminate what she means, then it is indeterminate what the sentences of her own seminar paper mean — including the very sentence in which she states the indeterminacy thesis. The thesis, if true, is one whose own content is indeterminate, and it seems to deprive itself of a determinate claim.

(6) Nothing in the argument turns on her in particular. So for any speaker and any term, there is no fact of the matter about what is meant, and hence no fact of the matter about what anyone believes. That is not a thesis about translation between languages; it is global meaning skepticism.

(7) Global meaning skepticism is false — or at any rate, if it were true, there would be no fact of the matter about whether it is true, and my paper would be over before it began. So at least one of (1) through (6) has to go.

Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost me? For what it is worth, I would most like to keep (1) and (2), since those strike me as the core Quinean claims I am trying to engage with rather than dodge, and I would prefer to cut the bridge from the radical-translator case to the speaker's own case in step (3) or (4) if that can be done on principle rather than by fiat. Someone at the meeting muttered that the indeterminacy at issue is only ever indeterminacy relative to a background language or a fixed background manual, and never indeterminacy tout court, but she did not spell it out, and I do not see that it helps: the proxy permutation in my mirror case can be run relative to any background I care to pick, so the relativity seems to travel with me rather than stop the argument. I also want to know whether there is something illegitimate in running (1) and (2) together in the first place — whether the term-level inscrutability argument silently presupposes determinacy at the level of whole manuals, so that the two cannot be composed the way my argument composes them. If so, I want the exact place where the scope or order shift happens, named precisely rather than gestured at. Finally, please price the alternatives explicitly: what do Quine's own resources (including any appeal to the privileged status of the homophonic or identity manual, or to decision rather than evidence), Davidson-style charity, Lewis-style eligibility or naturalness, and the causal or informational replies each have to give up in order to block the argument, and which of them blocks it at the fewest steps?
