Last week we did the scheme where you dont train one model to answer hard questions directly: you answer a hard question by recursively splitting it into sub-questions, each answered by another copy of the same model, with a human at the leaves, and then you train a single flat model to produce the final answer with the tree of sub-questions thrown away. Then you iterate: run the same procedure on top of the model you just distilled, distill again, and so on. The fight in the room was about weather that pipeline preserves the two things youd want it to preserve — that the whole assembly is trustworthy in a way no individual step is, and that it ends up more capable than what you started with. One guy kept saying it's basically just inlining and I think that's too glib but I couldnt say why at the time.

I want the whole argument in my vocabulary.
