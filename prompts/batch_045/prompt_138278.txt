I'm a mathematics PhD student who sits in on a fortnightly reading group in philosophy of science, and at the last meeting the group got bogged down in Hempel's raven paradox and never got out again. Two of the regulars, Tobias and Nadia, had most of the argument, and I've tried to reconstruct it faithfully. (1) Tobias opened by saying that Hempel's own response to the paradox is essentially correct and that the residual shock is merely psychological: because "all ravens are black" and "all non-black things are non-ravens" are logically equivalent, and logically equivalent hypotheses must be confirmed by exactly the same evidence, my noticing a white shoe really does confirm that all ravens are black — it simply does so by an absurdly small amount, since there are vastly more non-black things in the world than there are ravens, so that each one carries only a minuscule share of the evidential work that a single black raven performs at one stroke. (2) Nadia replied that the "it's just tiny" answer cannot be the whole story, because the size of the effect is not fixed by the relative headcounts alone — it depends on how the object was selected. If I deliberately look down at my own shoe, she said, I already know that it is not a raven, and "all ravens are black" says nothing whatsoever about the colours of non-ravens, so the observation cannot raise the probability of the hypothesis by any amount at all; and since the equivalence is supposed to hold, the same must go for "all non-black things are non-ravens", which means one of Tobias's two principles has to be surrendered. (3) Tobias answered that the shoe case is degenerate, and degenerate only because Nadia has idealised it: if I were genuinely certain that the thing before me was a shoe, then yes, the confirmation would be zero, but identification is fallible — it might be a stage prop, a trick of the light — and the moment there is any chance at all that the object is a non-black raven, positive confirmation returns. He proposed replacing the example with a pale bird glimpsed briefly in bad light which, on closer inspection, turns out not to be a raven: there, he claimed, the confirmation is real and substantially larger than the shoe's, precisely because this object had a serious chance of turning out to be a non-black raven. (4) Nadia came back with the observation that if that is right, then whether an observation is evidentially relevant is settled by my background probabilities and by the procedure that produced the observation, and not by logical form or by whether the observation is an instance of the generalisation at all; and she pressed Tobias to say which of the two conditions generating the paradox he was actually abandoning — the principle that a generalisation of the form "all F are G" is supported by an observed F that is G and undermined by an F that is not, or the principle that whatever confirms a hypothesis confirms every logically equivalent hypothesis — since on his own account, she said, the second of these has been made idle. (5) Tobias said that neither needs to be abandoned, because on any probabilistic account the equivalence principle is a theorem: logically equivalent hypotheses have the same probability conditional on any evidence whatever, so they simply cannot be confirmed differently, and what must go is instead the thought that being an instance of a generalisation supports it, since that is neither necessary nor sufficient for support — which, he added, is just the familiar lesson from projectibility worries of the "grue" variety, that which predicates we project is not settled by logic.

I told them I would write up a verdict and then found that I could not produce one I was willing to stand behind. Please adjudicate the exchange move by move: for each of (1) through (5), say where it falls on a scale from clearly mistaken to clearly right and justify the rating briefly — where a move is right, say whether it is right for the reason actually given, and where it is wrong, locate precisely the step that fails. Then say who is more right overall, and, most usefully for me, say what both of them are missing: which distinction, possibility, or consequence of the paradox never makes it onto the table in the exchange as I have reported it.
