My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about whether dialetheism is a serious option or a philosophical novelty, and we got nowhere because we were measuring against different standards: every cost I raised she answered by saying it was only a cost for someone who wanted classical behaviour back in ordinary contexts, and every virtue she raised I answered by saying it had been bought by weakening the conditional until it could no longer do any work. So before we go another round I want the standard rather than the verdicts.

The job, to be explicit: I want to know what a logic would have to deliver to count as adequate as the underlying logic of a theory of truth for a language that contains its own truth predicate, keeps the unrestricted T-schema for every sentence of that language, and treats the paradoxical sentences as truth-value gluts. That is the only job in view. I do not need the same logic to handle inconsistent databases, inconsistent mathematics, or the history of the infinitesimal calculus, though I have included one such case below because it tests something I care about.

Here is the unsorted pile of demands the two of us produced, each with the case that prompted it. I suspect two or three are camp prejudice rather than genuine desiderata, and I suspect some subset of the rest cannot be had together.

1. Explosion must fail: A together with not-A must not entail an arbitrary B. Case: the liar sentence, which by the theory's own lights is both true and false.
2. The conditional must detach: modus ponens, and ideally conditional proof with discharge of suppositions. Case: any argument that proceeds by supposition; her line was that a conditional which cannot detach is not a conditional but a piece of notation.
3. Curry must not trivialize the theory: with biconditionals linking the truth predicate to every sentence, including conditional sentences, and a conditional that detaches, a Curry sentence built on an arbitrary claim collapses the whole theory, so one of the T-biconditionals, the conditional, or some structural rule such as contraction has to be surrendered.
4. Classical recapture: in contexts where no contradiction is in play, the classical consequences must return. Case: ordinary arithmetic; if the theory forces mathematics to be rebuilt from the ground up, or leaves the reliability of classical practice mysterious, that is a real cost.
5. Recapture must be checkable locally. If the classical consequences return only under an antecedent saying that the relevant premises or context are consistent, the theory owes an account of how a reasoner of bounded resources determines which region she is in. My case is the working scientist who is not going to audit her entire corpus for hidden contradiction before each inference; if the honest answer is that she cannot tell, then recapture is a fair-weather guarantee and I want it marked as one.
6. Gluts must be statable in the object language: there must be a device for saying of a sentence that it is both true and false rather than merely neither, since otherwise the theory cannot so much as formulate its own claim about which sentences are gluts; and the device must not generate a fresh revenge paradox of exactly the kind the theory was built to accommodate.
7. Contradiction must be confinable: a glut in one corner of the language must not propagate through the truth predicate, the conditional, or normative and attitude operators into unrelated corners. Case: a legal code containing two conflicting statutes, which still has to guide action without entailing that everything is permitted and without letting one statute silently defeat the other.
8. The theory must say which contradictions are true: a criterion, or at least a principled constraint, so that some sentences are both true and false does not become a licence to accept any contradiction anyone proposes.
9. It must be usable in principle: a sound and complete proof theory, a semantics the theory can in some sense state for its own language, and some account of how finite agents would apply it.

Turn that pile, together with anything I have missed, into exactly five requirements for adequacy at the job described above, ordered so that the first does the most work in excluding candidates. For each, state the requirement, name the case that earns it its place, and mark it non-negotiable or a preference of one camp; where a camp preference has crept in disguised as a requirement, say whose intuition it really is. If two of your five cannot be jointly satisfied, indicate which you would drop, inside the entry for the one you would keep. Do not score the familiar non-classical logics against the list; I will do that myself. No introduction and no conclusion: exactly five numbered entries, one or two sentences each, with the case and the tag contained inside the entry.
