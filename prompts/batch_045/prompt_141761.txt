I left school at eighteen and have spent the last seventeen years in television, mostly as an online editor with a sideline in video engineering on live music and sport. Everything I know about cameras, latency and tape I learned on the job, so my vocabulary here is a working one: the live feed versus the recorded timeline, frame buffers and delay, what went out on air versus what sits on the edit decision list, dropped frames and hung frames, backdated timecode, a proxy render versus a full-resolution render, re-conforming after the fact, the confidence monitor in the gallery, and the difference between a non-destructive edit and one that is baked in. I think in those terms more comfortably than in any others.

Over the past year or so I have been reading about consciousness on my own, and I have got stuck on an argument I keep running into about filling in. As I understand the set-up: a dot is flashed briefly at one position on a screen, goes off, and a fraction of a second later a differently coloured dot is flashed at a position some distance away. What people report is not two successive flashes but a single dot travelling between the two positions and changing colour partway along its path. The difficulty is supposed to be that the colour it appears to change into is the colour of the second flash, which had not yet occurred at the moment the change seems to happen, so the visual system appears to have anticipated something it could not then have known. The standard move is to say the change must have been filled in after the second flash. And then the argument splits into two readings I have seen labelled Orwellian and Stalinesque: on the first, there really was an experience of a moving dot at the time, and the record of it was afterwards quietly rewritten to include the change; on the second, no earlier version ever existed and the whole experience was assembled late, after the information was in. I do not care who coined those labels or in which book, and I am not asking for a reading list or citations: I have no library access and no way to check them, so please keep everything at the level of positions and arguments.

What I would like is a translation, done as honestly as you can, of that whole dispute into the vocabulary I listed above. Restate each of the two readings, and restate the argument from the two flashes that is supposed to force a choice between them, in terms of signals, frames, buffers, renders, timecode, transmission and reconstruction — with no philosophical term left in that could have been cashed out in one of mine. Then give me the residue report, and the residue is the part I am actually interested in.

Four things I particularly want the report to settle. First: in every case I deal with there is a stored or transmitted artefact and a reader standing outside the process that produced it. In this case the viewer is supposed to be inside the machine. Does your translation quietly smuggle in an engineer watching a confidence monitor, and if so, what is left of the dispute once you take him away? Second: in my world, two pipelines that emit bit-identical output are not two different processes in any sense I would pay for; I would choose between them on cost, robustness and maintainability. Is there anything in this dispute that plays the role of cost or maintainability — some criterion that could separate the two readings even though no signal I could put a probe on distinguishes them — or is the choice between them, in my terms, a choice between two implementations with identical output? And if it is, say plainly whether you think that shows the question is empty or shows that it is a question of a kind engineering never has to answer. Third: where does ordinary latency fit? With an as-live feed, the audience at home has its experience of an event several seconds after the event, of a moment that is already past, and nothing has been falsified or rewritten; it is just delay. My own half-formed thought is that a lot of what gets described as filling in might be nothing but delay plus the banal fact that a system can emit a description of an interval only once it has the interval, and that this is a third possibility the two-way menu leaves out rather than a version of either. If you think it collapses into one of the two, say which and show me why. Fourth: what would a measurement that settles it look like in my vocabulary — something like a probe on the bus versus a probe on the rendered output — and if no such probe is available even in principle, tell me whether that is a sign the question was badly posed or a sign that it is a real question that my framework has no slot for.

Finally, and this may be the most useful thing you can do for me: tell me which habits of thought my vocabulary will carry into this topic and do damage with. I notice I keep assuming that there is always a stored frame, always a timecode, always a clean difference between what happened and what was recorded, and always someone who could roll back to an earlier version. Point out where each of those assumptions is doing hidden work in the translation, and where it is distorting the thing being translated rather than illuminating it. I would rather have one worked translation with its failures marked than a tidy summary of what people say.
