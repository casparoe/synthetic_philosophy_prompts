Hi — I run a fortnightly reading group for philosophy grads, and next session we're doing the question of whether the Gettier cases actually share one structure or whether 'Gettier' is just a family-resemblance label for a pile of differently-behaving examples. I want a paired case to put in front of them.

What I'm after is two subjects — call them Ash and Blake, or whatever — who end up with the same true belief about the same thing, on the strength of exactly the same evidence, in the same ordinary setting (a hospital ward, a newsroom, an airport ops room, a school, whatever you like). Everything either of them could notice, check, or recall is identical: same proposition, same time, same training, same confidence, same everything accessible. And the intuition should be that Ash knows and Blake doesn't.

The catch is where the difference between them is allowed to sit. It has to be in the aetiology — how the information actually reached them, what happened upstream, behind their backs — and not in anything either of them has access to. So here's what counts as cheating:

- Blake must not have a false belief anywhere in the chain. The no-false-lemma fix can't be what does the separating.
- Blake's method must not be unreliable in general. A one-off upstream fluke is what I want, not 'Blake gets things wrong a lot'.
- No hostile environment: no fake barns, no disguised mules, nothing rigged about the setting they're both standing in.
- Blake should be safe in the standard sense — in the nearby worlds where she forms the belief the same way, it's true. If the only way you can get the intuition going is by making her unsafe, then the case has failed at the job I want it to do, and I'd rather you said so than fudged it.
- And don't just announce the verdicts. 'Intuitively Blake doesn't know' written into the scenario is useless to me; the reaction has to be produced by the story itself.

Keep it realistic, one paragraph for the scenario, no demons, no perfect predictors, no lotteries, no sci-fi. A testimonial chain is the obvious place to put the difference, but you're not obliged to use one.

Then the diagnostics, which can be as long as you like. For each subject separately: the belief, the method *as you are individuating it* (flag it if the verdicts move when you describe the method more finely or more coarsely), the nearby worlds that matter for safety, and the nearest not-p world for sensitivity. Then tell me what each of these says about Ash and about Blake, and why: JTB plus no false lemma; Goldman-style process reliabilism; Sosa/Williamson-style safety; Nozick-style sensitivity/tracking; and the credit-or-aptness condition (Sosa's AAA framework, Greco's ability condition). I want to know which of those five can't tell the two apart at all, which ones do the seperating, and why they can see something the others can't.

Finish with: what single feature of the case is doing the work; what happens to the pair if you delete that feature — does it collapse into two people who both plainly know, or into something else; and how someone who thinks safety is the whole anti-luck story would push back on Blake's case. And if you think the pair honestly can't be built — that the intuition about Blake evaporates once you hold safety and reliability fixed — tell me that instead and explain why. I'd much rather have that than a case that cheats.
