Me and a friend have been going round on this for a couple weeks and I want a number to argue with.

The question: is the left/right distinction real? I don't mean 'do people use the words' or 'are there two big coalitions' — I mean something more like this. If you wiped out every current party, brand, media ecosystem and elite cue in a wealthy Western democracy and let politics reassemble from scratch, would the positions re-bundle into roughly the same two piles? Equivalently: when a genuinely new issue shows up — commercial human gene editing, whether an AI system is allowed to make hiring decisions, what to do about lab-grown meat, whatever — does the axis predict who lands where better than a coin flip, or does each side just take whatever position its coalition happens to settle on and then retro-fit a principle afterwards?

My credence is about 0.55. Hers is about 0.85. Give me yours as a single number on that proposition (and say, briefly, what you would count as it being settled), and then decompose it. Each sub-question with a rough number and one line of why, then how they combine. The ones I want in there at minimum:

- that there is some underlying disposition doing the work — something like a differential sensitivity to hierarchy versus equality, or openness to change versus protection of what already exists — as against the bundles being downstream of elite coordination and historical accident;
- that whatever the disposition turns out to be, it is the same one, or the same small set, across different countries and a couple of centuries, rather than a local accident of which groups happened to ally with which;
- that it would actually predict the novel-issue cases above, rather than only rationalize them after the fact;
- whether real here should be cashed out predictively at all, or whether causal/explanatory unity is the thing that matters and prediction is the wrong test.

Two constructions I came up with that are doing a lot of work in my own number — feel free to tell me theyre bad tests. First, the scramble test: keep every issue position, randomly reassign each one to one of two platforms, show people both the real pairing and scrambled pairings with no labels, and see whether they systematically pick the real one as the coherent one. Second, the full swap: overnight both sides adopt the other side's entire platform but keep their voters, donors, symbols and cultural identity — how much of the left/right distinction survives a year later? What each test would actually show if it came out one way or the other is half of what I'm after.

Then: which term in your decomposition is your final number most sensitive to, which one are you least confident in, and — given me at 0.55 and her at 0.85 — which sub-question is our disagreement most likely living in?

Spend no more than a sentence or two on it depends what you mean by real. Name the reading you think is load bearing, say why, and move on.
