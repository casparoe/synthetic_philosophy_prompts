Where I've got to. The tempting thesis is that the probability of an indicative conditional just is the corresponding conditional probability: for a credence function P, the probability of if A then C equals P(C given A). Lewis's result is supposed to show this can't hold in full generality, because it collapses into everything being probabilistically independent of everything else — which would mean nothing could ever be evidence for anything. Concretely: fair die, A = the roll came up even, C = it came up six. P(C given A) is 1/3, so the thesis says the conditional gets probability 1/3. But split its probability by whether C holds: conditional on six, the conditional if even then six should be certain; conditional on not-six, it should be impossible. That gives 1 × 1/6 + 0 × 5/6 = 1/6. Contradiction, and the general version apparently gives P(C given A) = P(C) full stop.
