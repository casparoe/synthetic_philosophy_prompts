Hey, I'm a junior and me and my friends got into this argument at lunch about why basically every society we know of has had some kind of religion, and it's been stuck in my head all week. One friend says religion is group glue: believing that a powerful being watches how you treat other people makes strangers cooperate, so groups with that belief out-competed groups without it. Another friend says that's backwards, that religion is a byproduct of mental machinery built for other jobs — spotting agents behind every rustle in the grass, assuming minds survive bodies, that kind of thing — and the beliefs then get picked over by culture afterward. My psych teacher threw in a third one, that religious ideas spread the way catchy songs do, not because they're useful and not because our brains are built for them, but because some versions of an idea are just stickier in memory and get passed on more. Someone online argued a fourth: it's really the costly rituals doing the work, that doing something painful or expensive together is what bonds a group, and the beliefs are a story we tell afterwards to explain the ritual. And I want my own fifth one in the running even though nobody takes it seriously: religion sticks mainly because it's one of the few things people have that makes the bad parts of life — death, bad luck, being alone — feel like part of a story rather than just random noise, and the cooperation and memory stuff is downstream of that.

I've been trying to score them instead of just arguing louder, so I made up two tests. Test one, the swap: imagine you could rewrite a religion's content while leaving human brains and human societies exactly as they are, so its god doesn't care at all how you treat other people, its rituals cost almost nothing, and its afterlife has no punishment in it — on each of the five accounts, would that religion still spread and stick? Test two, the fade: in rich, safe, well-run countries where people are healthier and less scared than at basically any other point in history, some of these accounts seem to predict religion should mostly dry up, and yet a lot of people there apparently drop the church part but keep believing in something vaguer. I don't actually know how big that pattern is, I'm just going off what people say, so feel free to tell me if I've got it wrong.

So: rank my five explanations from strongest to weakest as accounts of why religion shows up in nearly every society, and give each a score out of 10. Judge them mostly on whether they handle the boring everyday facts — why people pray right before something scary, why bad events so often get explained as somebody's fault, why kids pick this stuff up so easily — not just the big dramatic ones, and use the swap test and the fade test as tiebreakers when two of them feel equally good, saying out loud when one of the tests is what moved something up or down. Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, best to worst, each one the explanation, its score out of 10, and a sentence or two on why it lands where it does, with no intro and no conclusion.
