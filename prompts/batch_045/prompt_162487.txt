I work on the numbers question, and I want a diagnosis of why the debate has stalled — not a verdict on which side is right. The dialectic has become ritualised: Taurek's argument that where I cannot save everyone, saving the one rather than the five wrongs or harms no one, so a lottery giving each person an equal chance is not less defensible than saving the greater number; the aggregators' replies that the five collectively have a stronger claim than the one, that each of the five has a claim the one cannot answer, and that the equal-chance proposal is itself aggregative because it maximises expected lives saved; and the residual complications about whether Taurek's conclusion is only permissive, and about what a rescuer who saves the one should feel about the five she leaves.

My hypothesis is that this is several disagreements sharing one title, and that the impasse is partly about which question is being asked. Specifically, I suspect three separable issues are doing the work: whether the case is fundamentally allocative — a scarce good to be distributed among claimants, governed by something like a tie-break among equal claims — or fundamentally axiological, a question about which outcome is impersonally better; whether aggregation is being rejected in the axiology or only in the decision procedure, since one might deny that benefits aggregate across persons while still using an aggregative rule under uncertainty; and whether the agent occupies a role-relative standpoint, deliberating as a rescuer with a duty to these people, or an impersonal standpoint from which she simply produces the best outcome.

A test I have tried: substitute for the six people six episodes of suffering within a single life, and see whether a theorist's verdict flips. Some of the arguments I have seen clearly turn on the separateness of persons and go silent intrapersonally; others do not, which suggests to me that the two groups are not always arguing against each other at all.

So: is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one, or several questions under one heading? Is a bedrock intuition about the separateness of persons doing the work, or a disagreement about what counts as a moral reason, or a hidden formal question about how individual claims compose? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result about composing claims, a reframing of the question — or is this a case where no resolution should be expected, and why?
