My Kierkegaard reading group has been going in circles on the Binding for months, and at this point I'd rather have a diagnosis than another interpretation. One camp reads the teleological suspension as straight override: Abraham is about to do something he himself takes to be murder, and the text says a divine command can make that permissible, so it's either a reductio of divine command ethics or just naked fideism. The other camp insists that the ethical in the text isn't morality in our sense at all but the universal, the publicly statable and socially mediated order, so nothing we'd call morality is being overridden — what Abraham suspends is the demand that his relation to God be legible to anybody else. Both sides agree on the facts of the story, both agree the knight of faith is outwardly indistinguishable from an ordinary burgher, and nobody has moved in four months.

My hypothesis is that the fight is partly taxonomical, and that the two camps are using suspension for different logical animals. I've started borrowing a distinction from elsewhere in ethics: an overriding reason (a consideration that outweighs the moral one, which keeps its weight) versus an exclusionary reason (a second-order reason not to act on certain first-order reasons at all, without denying their weight). Camp one reads the command as outweighing morality, camp two as excluding it from deliberation whole leaving it intact — and if that's right the headline question splits into two, and each camp has been answering one half and declaring victory.

Second hypothesis: the real fight is about where the normative work sits, in the authority (God said so, full stop) or in the content of what Abraham is aiming at (he trusts he'll get Isaac back by way of the absurd, which is what makes the suspension teleological rather than a bare exception). One camp reads pure obedience, the other reads trust in a promise, and then each accuses the other of missing the point.

So what kind of stalemate is this — verbal, substantive but badly described, two different questions under one heading, or a bedrock clash about whether a relation to God could be a reason at all? Support it with what each side actually says, and then tell me what would count as progress given that diagnosis: a distinction, a formal result, a different question, or nothing at all.
