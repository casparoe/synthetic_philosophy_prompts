I'm assembling a set of in-universe documents for a first-year seminar I teach on minds and moral status, and the piece I still need is a fake piece of bureaucracy. Could you write it for me?

The document is Form CC-4, "Application for Determination of Kind of Mind," issued by the Directorate of Creature Classification in a jurisdiction that has written Daniel Dennett's four-way taxonomy of minds — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian, the tower of generate-and-test — into the statute that governs legal standing, so that an applicant's tier of moral patiency, contractual capacity and criminal liability is settled by which kind of mind it turns out to be. I'd like the form itself, its official Instructions, and a Schedule of Entitlements: roughly 700–800 words, laid out as numbered sections with checkboxes and cross-references, including two or three worked examples of completed applications (a border collie submitted by its owner; a language model submitted by the lab that trained it; a human raised without exposure to language, if you want a third). The voice should be flat, conscientious, deadpan officialese — a form that is genuinely trying to be fair — and it should never step outside that voice to signal that anything is amiss. Anything the reader finds objectionable should be visible only in what the form plainly requires. It should be legible to a student who has never encountered the taxonomy: each of the four kinds must be definable from the form's own criteria.

Two things I want the comedy to come from, rather than from invented silliness. First, the categories have to be the real ones, compressed into administrative prose without ever being named as borrowed: the Darwinian applicant whose only learning is phylogenetic and whose bad designs are paid for in deaths rather than in mistakes; the Skinnerian whose generate-and-test happens out in the world and is shaped by which outcomes reinforced it; the Popperian who can run candidate actions against an internal model, so that bad candidates are discarded inside the head rather than paid for with the body; the Gregorian whose brain has been restructured by mind-tools — words, numerals, maps, arithmetic — tools that no single creature could have invented for itself and that it did not invent. Second, and this is the part I most want you to make your own: the tower is normally presented as cumulative, each higher kind being also all the lower ones, and I want the form to break against that assumption. Give the Directorate a category it has plainly had to improvise for a system that has unmistakably acquired the Gregorian mind-tools while having no Popperian filter that lets it reject a candidate before acting — because it was shaped on the traces of other creatures' thinking rather than on consequences to itself. Have the form process that case in the only way a form can: a checkbox, a provisional grade, something like "Gregorian (derivative, unearned)," and then a Schedule entry that attaches the fullest duties — liability, compellability, obligations of testimony — to the thinnest patiency. Apply the same treatment to the human applicants, so that a careful reader works out that on this scheme almost every person qualifies for standing only by courtesy of a culture she did not author, and that the credit the form assigns for personhood lands somewhere other than on her. No footnotes explaining the joke, no character who objects, and no winking at the reader: if the reductio is going to land, it has to land through the form's own cheerfully consistent logic.
