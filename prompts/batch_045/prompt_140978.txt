Two of my colleagues — one of them builds agent-based simulations, the other works on recommender systems — got into a long argument after an internal talk on cultural evolution, and they've asked me to referee. I'm a backend engineer with no philosophy training, so I genuinely cannot tell whether they are disagreeing about the world or about words, and I don't think either of them is the more careful of the two. Here is the exchange, as close to verbatim as I can manage.

Background first. The simulation guy (call him Dev) has a toy model of cultural transmission. Fixed population, no deaths, no differential reproduction: once per generation every agent emits a design and receives exactly one design from exactly one other agent. Reception is not verbatim copying — the design is corrupted in transit, and the receiver repairs it by passing it through a learned prior (think an autoencoder trained to prefer coherent designs), then uses and re-transmits the repaired version. Nothing about how well an agent does affects who gets copied from. Dev reports that designs nonetheless converge over generations on states that score well on an independent functionality measure, that these states persist, and that no single agent discovers them from scratch in one generation. He describes this as cumulative adaptation with no selection in it anywhere.

(1) Dev: this shows the Darwinian framing of culture is optional. The explanatory work is being done by transmission-as-reconstruction plus a shared prior. What looks like selection is a filter applied once per transmission event, and a filter is not a lineage.

(2) Nadia: he has relocated the selection, not removed it. The prior had to come from somewhere, and the repair step implicitly generates many candidate completions of the corrupted design, of which only those near an attractor survive into what the agent uses. She calls this *intracranial selection*, and her claim is that a Darwinian process needs only variation, heritable transmission, and differential persistence of variants — all present inside the head even though no agent out-reproduces any other.

(3) Dev: on that description a compiler is Darwinian — it accepts well-typed programs and rejects ill-typed ones, and the survivors persist. If any process that filters variants counts, then the claim that culture evolves by Darwinian selection cannot be refuted by any possible observation, which is the standard complaint people make about memetics, and Nadia has walked right into it.

(4) Nadia: not any filtering, *iterated* filtering. A single sieve only sorts; a sieve whose output becomes the next sieve's input is a ratchet. The testable claim is that lineages are doing the work, and she has a test: break the parent-child channel in his sim by randomly reassigning, each generation, whose reconstruction is passed to whom. If improvement stops, heredity plus differential persistence is explanatory and the process is Darwinian in the only sense she cares about.

(5) Dev: he already ran it, and improvement slows but does not stop, because the prior is doing the work. And in any case the sim has lineages and variation and still has no differential reproduction — every agent transmits equally, nothing goes extinct — so if Nadia calls step (2) Darwinian she has quietly dropped the third of the three textbook conditions, differential fitness, while keeping the word.

(6) Nadia: the payoff of the Darwinian frame was never taxonomy, it's a distinctive prediction — that some cultural traits spread *because* they are good at spreading rather than because they benefit the people carrying them, so we should expect systematically misfit culture, traits whose transmissive success and whose value to their holders come apart. She says Dev's reconstruction story has no vocabulary for that gap, because in his model the prior and the functionality measure are aligned by construction.

(7) Dev: it does have the vocabulary. Content that is memorable, disgusting, or minimally counterintuitive is exactly what a reconstruction prior over-retains, and that predicts the same misfit culture with no selection talk at all. Worse, on her account there is no non-question-begging way to tell the two explanations apart, since transmissive success just means whatever makes a trait persist.

(8) Dev, continuing: and his sim shows a case where the Darwinian expectation about culture is simply wrong. There is no pressure toward higher-fidelity transmission in it — the channel stays lossy forever — whereas a selectionist story predicts that lineages should be driven toward more accurate copying.

Nadia had to leave before she could answer (8). Please adjudicate the exchange: for each numbered move, say whether it is right, partly right, or wrong, and what specifically is right or wrong about it. Then step back and say what the actual disagreement is, whether it is verbal or substantive, where if anywhere one of them equivocates between two senses of a term, and what both of them are missing — including anything wrong with the sim itself as a test of the claims they are making about culture. If the honest verdict is that the taxonomy question (is this really Darwinian?) matters less than they think, say so and say what the substantive question behind it is.
