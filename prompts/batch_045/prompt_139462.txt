I keep a small — and I'll admit, moderately pedantic — blog on metaphysics, and I'm drafting an interactive post on the dispute about whether there are past and future things: a diagnostic flowchart in the spirit of an online quiz, the point of which is to sort readers by what they are willing to say about cases rather than by which hyphenated label they already like. My readers have met the A-theory/B-theory contrast and one version of the truthmaker objection, but most of them know the positions mainly from survey articles, so the tree has to be built out of questions they can answer cold. Please write it for me: roughly six branch nodes and seven to nine leaves, every node phrased as a question about a concrete case or claim and never as a question about a label or a doctrine (no node may ask whether the present is objectively special, or whether one accepts the B-theory). At least one node must come from each of these places: an ordinary cross-time causal claim (my headache this morning was caused by the wine I drank last night, where the cause is not happening now); a singular past-tensed claim about an individual (Socrates, or the dinosaurs) and what makes it true; the relativity of simultaneity, and whether a privileged slicing of spacetime is a physical hypothesis, a metaphysical one, or neither; whether the future is open in a way the past is not, so that tomorrow's sea battle has no determinate truth value today; whether the inventory of what exists ever gains or loses members; and whether the felt passage of time is something the world does or something we do. Among the leaves I need presentism and its ersatz cousins, the growing block, a view on which the future and present are real while the past has gone out of existence, the moving spotlight (with the variant that posits an extra dimension of hypertime kept separate from the variant that does without one, only if they really come apart), the static four-dimensional block, a relativized presentism that indexes existence to an inertial frame, a branching view on which the future is a tree of open possibilities rather than a single line, and a quietist leaf for the reader who concludes there is no fact of the matter. I also want two of my own inventions placed somewhere on the tree or diagnosed as collapsing into existing leaves: the growing spotlight, in which the block grows and its leading edge is additionally distinguished; and the branching growing block, in which the trunk grows and the future branches. Tell me which of my six questions is actually doing the classificatory work and which is decorative, flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate rather than pretending it can, mark the branches where you have had to force a dichotomy that isn't exhaustive, and note the combinations of answers that no coherent position occupies — with a one-line diagnosis of each empty cell, saying whether it is empty because it is incoherent, because it is unattractive, or because nobody has got round to it. I do not want a reading list or any citations, and I don't need names attached to the positions; the flowchart will be used somewhere without web access, and what I care about is the structure of the space. At the end, add a short note on how confident you are that each leaf corresponds to something genuinely defended rather than something that merely sounds defensible, and single out the one leaf or branch you are least sure of — and if you think one of my branch questions is the wrong question to ask, say so there too.
