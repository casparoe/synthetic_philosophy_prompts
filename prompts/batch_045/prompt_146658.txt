I'm in eleventh grade and have been reading about infinity on my own, and I'd like someone to check whether the things I've ended up believing are actually consistent with each other. Here is where I've landed:

1. Actual infinities are real: the natural numbers exist as a completed whole, not merely as an endless process of counting.
2. The right way to compare the sizes of infinite collections is by whether their members can be paired off one-to-one. So there are exactly as many even numbers as natural numbers, and strictly more reals than naturals.
3. Even so, I want to say that the natural numbers are more numerous than the even numbers, since the evens are only a part of them. My way of holding this together with (2) is to say there are two distinct notions — pairability and being more numerous — that (2) is a claim about the first and (3) about the second, and that neither is merely a façon de parler.
4. Hilbert's Hotel is coherent: with every room occupied, moving each guest from room n to room 2n leaves all the odd-numbered rooms free.
5. Supertasks are not: nobody can ever have completed infinitely many separate tasks. A lamp switched off and on at half a minute, a quarter of a minute, and so on has no state once the minute is up, because the sequence of its states has no last member.
6. The fact that an infinite claim can't be settled by any finite check doesn't make it less true: I'm a realist about the infinite even though nothing in experience could confirm it.

The pair that worries me most is (4) and (5). Getting the guests into their new rooms seems to involve infinitely many walks, and the guest from room n has to walk past n rooms, so the walks get unboundedly long — which looks like exactly the supertask I deny in (5). My attempted reply is that (4) is a claim about which arrangement is possible, not about anyone bringing it about within a finite time, but I can't tell whether that reply survives. A friend also says (4) contradicts the hotel's being full in the first place; I think that one is just an equivocation on "full" and I'm not worried about it.

Please audit the set. Which pairs genuinely pull against each other and which only look like they do? Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each tell me what I would have to give up and what that would cost me. If a single revision would clean up several of them at once, say which. I especially want to know whether my two-notions move in (3) is a genuine distinction or whether (2) quietly forces me to abandon it, and whether (6) is doing any real work in the rest of the package.
