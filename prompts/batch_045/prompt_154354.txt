Construct a case in which Jeffrey conditionalization is order-dependent. An agent holds a prior over a hypothesis and two evidence variables, then undergoes two genuine Jeffrey updates, one on each evidence partition, with rigidity holding at every step — and her final probability for the hypothesis differs depending on which update she performs first.

Constraints.

- An ordinary setting, and each piece of evidence must be something she could genuinely be uncertain about: a result glimpsed on a smudged display, a reading half-heard over a bad line, a colleague's note with an illegible digit — not a signal from a perfect detector.
- The probabilistic dependence between the two evidence variables must be earned by the story: something in the situation makes them dependent in her prior, and the numbers should follow from that rather than be reverse-engineered from the verdict you want.
- The gap between the two orders must be large enough to flip a decision she actually faces, for instance by crossing the threshold at which a treatment or a repair becomes worth doing, without relying on extreme or gerrymandered probabilities.

Deliverables: the story and the prior; the two learning events stated as new marginal probabilities on their partitions; both computations, with an explicit check that each step preserves the conditional probabilities within the updated partition; and the two resulting posteriors for the hypothesis. Then say (i) what happens to the gap if the two evidence variables are made independent under the prior while the new marginals are held fixed, (ii) which feature of the case is really doing the work, and (iii) the strongest reply available to someone who insists that a rational agent's credence should not depend on the order in which two pieces of evidence with no objective priority are processed — and whether that reply can be made inside Jeffrey's framework or requires abandoning it.
