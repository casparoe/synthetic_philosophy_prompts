I keep four raised beds behind the philosophy building — a thing a couple of undergrads and I started during the pandemic — and the kale is currently covered in aphids. A colleague of mine who teaches animal ethics says I shouldn't lose any sleep over spraying them with soapy water, and her reasoning runs like this: those particular aphids are dead in a fortnight regardless, they'd most likley starve or get eaten by ladybugs, and whichever ones I kill will just be replaced by other aphids living the same kind of life in the same bed — so the summer's total of aphid living comes out the same whoever it's lived by, and I haven't made things worse for anybody. Another colleague says that's just a trick and that I'm killing thousands of animals for a salad. My gut is with the second guy, but I can't put my finger on what's wrong with the first argument. Two more wrinkles: the same aphids on the weeds in the vacant lot next door I'd never give a thought to, and one of my grad students insists that thinning a colony can ease crowding and set off a breeding surge, so spraying might mean more aphid lives get lived in that bed over the season, not fewer. What should I actually do about the kale, and is my colleague's argument broken or not?
