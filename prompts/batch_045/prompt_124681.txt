Running a 45-min session next week for my local rationalist meetup — maybe fifteen people, mostly ML-adjacent, no phil of science past the Sequences — on why unrealistic assumptions in a model aren't automatically a bug. Don't write the talk, tell me how to build it.

Two priors I have to work with: they arrive already knowing "all models are wrong," so they think this is settled and boring; and the ML version, that out-of-sample predictive accuracy is the only scoreboard. That second one is a trap, because it makes Friedman's "as if" move sound obviously correct, and then nobody learns to tell a harmless idealization from a load-bearing falsehood.

So what has to come first, and what depends on what? I want them holding the de-idealization test before anyone says "but the assumption is false": the difference between an assumption you can relax and watch the prediction perturb smoothly (friction, point masses, infinite populations) and one that doesn't perturb at all but makes the result collapse the moment you touch it (perfect rationality, zero transaction costs). I've started calling it scope laundering when a model gets carried from a setting where its false assumptions were harmless into one where those assumptions decide the answer, and nobody re-audits — where does that go in the sequence?

I keep failing to get across that "as if" isn't free: its got to be earned, either by a selection story or by the prediction being insensitive to it. So I was thinking of running a pair as the one example that carries the whole thing — something like leaves positioned as if maximizing light capture, next to firms behaving as if maximizing profit when nobody in the building has heard of marginal revenue. Is that the right pair, what does the example need to have and lack, or is there something better?

Misconceptions I expect: the F-twist collapsing into Box's line; "unrealistic" getting read as "approximately true" rather than "false and not even close"; and someone concluding that if assumptions don't need to be true then nobody has to check anything. Where does each get defused given the order? Then one check that would actually show they can audit a model they've never seen, plus what I should openly refuse to get into at this level — im guessing realism about unobservables, and the "models are little surrogate worlds, the jump to the real one is an act of judgment" move, which I'd rather leave as a pointer.
