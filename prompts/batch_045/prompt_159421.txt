I sit on the board of a small endowed trust that owns roughly four thousand acres of irrigated farmland along with the water rights attached to it, and this year we are required to file a management plan running to the 2080s. Two analysts we retained to advise us — one a neo-Malthusian who thinks the past two centuries were bought on credit from fossil groundwater, fossil nitrogen and fossil energy, and one a cornucopian in the Boserup–Simon line who thinks population pressure and prices reliably call forth the innovation that dissolves scarcity — have each written a document that barely acknowledges the other exists. The board has asked me to get them to produce one joint text instead, and before I can ask that of them I need to know what such a text could honestly look like. Please draft it for me as a template.

Treat them as parties to an adversarial collaboration and produce the document they could both sign, in four parts.

Part I: a numbered list of agreed claims — claims about the record since Malthus's Essay (population has multiplied many times over while the real price of staple foods has fallen; the demographic transition happened; intensification, not just extensification, did the work), about the formal structure both accept (compound growth against any fixed linear ceiling), about what neither side is committed to (neither claims a specific year of collapse; neither denies that local collapses are real and documented; both accept that a one-time energy and fertilizer subsidy is not the same kind of thing as a permanent technological capacity), and about what would count as a bad outcome for the trust's purposes. Include agreed negative claims, not only shared optimism.

Part II: a disagreements table. For each crux give the sub-question; the Malthusian's position in wording a Malthusian would actually sign; the cornucopian's in wording a cornucopian would actually sign; a test, case, or series of measurements both agree would bear on the crux; and each side's prediction recorded in advance, with numbers and dates where the crux admits them (for example: the year and level at which global population peaks; the real price in 2085 of a fixed basket of staple grains relative to today; the proportion of humanity living under renewable-water stress; whether global cereal yield growth stays above some stated annual percentage; whether the demographic transition holds in the poorest countries without further falls in fertility being preceded by rises in food prices). Cruxes I suspect matter, which you should keep, drop, or add to as you see fit: whether the post-1800 escape was driven by scarcity-induced intensification or by a depletable inheritance that is now being drawn down; whether carrying capacity has a physical floor that is independent of price or is purely a function of the current technology set; whether the demographic transition is a stable feature of wealthy societies or itself a response to a specific and temporary abundance; and whether recent signs — aquifer depletion, yield-growth deceleration, volatile fertilizer and energy prices, collapsed fisheries — are binding constraints or local bottlenecks of the kind that have been solved before.

Part III: an honest separation of the genuine disagreements from the verbal ones. Where the two sides have been using carrying capacity, constraint, scarcity, overpopulation, or sustainability to mean different things, say so and say which part of the dispute dissolves once the terms are fixed.

Part IV: the part I care most about, because it is the form the board's question takes. I have been experimenting with a scoring rule for dated long-run predictions: a prediction of the form X by year T gets full credit if X arrives by T, and decaying credit thereafter under a lag-discount function the predictor must declare in advance — say credit proportional to lambda raised to the lag — together with a registration requirement that any auxiliary hypothesis used to explain a miss must have been written down before the miss occurred and must carry at least one independent, testable consequence of its own, so that a theory is not free to invent its rescue after the fact. Apply this to the right-but-early verdict. What lambda would each camp have to declare to keep its own position creditable, and is the honest answer that any lambda is available, which would make right-but-early unfalsifiable rather than merely patient? State each camp's defection condition — the observation after which that camp would concede — and say whether the two defection conditions ever intersect.

Close with what, if anything, the joint statement licenses the board to do: whether it supports spending capital now on storage and efficiency, whether it supports assuming that scarcity will be solved, or whether the honest upshot is a decision made under unresolved uncertainty, and what that decision should look like. Where my prompt leaves a number, threshold, or date open, supply a plausible one and mark it clearly as a placeholder the two analysts would have to negotiate. Steelman both camps throughout — no formulation either side would disown. Please write it in American English.
