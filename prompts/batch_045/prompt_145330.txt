hey — me and two friends do a short audio-drama thing for my school's philosophy club (we post one every other friday, just two voices, no narrator, 8–10 mins, recorded on a phone in the band room). This week's topic is the norm of assertion and I'm the one writing it, and I'm stuck, because everything I can find is either a three-bullet summary that makes all the views sound identical or an actual paper I can't get through.

So: I need two people arguing about what rule actually governs assertion. One of them holds something like the knowledge norm — you should only come out and say p if you know p (I think the name is Williamson? and there's a "truth norm" and a "reasonable belief" norm too but I honestly can't tell them apart from the summaries, so feel free to pick whichever contrast is cleanest). The other holds a view I half-remember from a podcast: that assertion is more like handing someone something they're entitled to rely on, so what matters isn't whether you know, it's whether it's responsible for the people hearing you to act on what you said, and that shifts with what's at stake — same sentence can be fine at lunch and indefensible when someone's about to spend money on it. Both of them should be equally sharp, nobody gets flattened: each one should state the strongest version of the other one's point before pushing back, and each should concede at least one real thing by the end. And please no paper titles or years or footnotes in the dialogue, nobody can hear a citation in an audio skit.

The bit that'll keep this from being a rehash is that I want them to fight over a case my club came up with last month and definately cannot settle. Our class group chat has a "pinned claim" feature: you post a claim, it stays pinned at the top with your name on it for a week, everyone sees it the whole time, and you can "co-sign" someone else's pinned claim with one tap, which just stacks your name under theirs. So: is pinning even asserting, or is it asserting over and over for seven days? If the knowledge norm is right, do you have to keep knowing it the whole time it's up, and what do we say about the person who finds out on day three that it was wrong and just forgets to unpin? Is co-signing an assertion of your own, or vouching, or something else entirely? And if the reliance view is right, does a co-sign from someone who's usually right do something different from a co-sign from someone who's usually wrong — does it raise what everyone else is entitled to rely on? Also work in the lottery-ish case (my club brings it up every single time, so it has to be in there, just please make it not boring), and add at least one case of your own that pulls the two views apart in opposite directions — one where the knowledge norm looks too strict, one where the reliance view looks too loose.

Sections, in this order:

Setup — ~100 words: who the two characters are, one line each on their view, and where they are and why they're even talking.

The dialogue — ~1100–1400 words: the actual script. Two voices, interruptions allowed, at most one joke each, no speech longer than about 80 words, no narrator, and it has to make sense to someone who's only listening and can't see anything.
