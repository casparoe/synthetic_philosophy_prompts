I'm making a handout for a reading group on collective cognition, and one of our sessions is the Aunt Hillary thing — whether an ant colony can have a mind. I want the handout to be the joint statement of a fake adversarial collaboration, so please draft the document itself, not an essay about the question.

The two parties have been arguing past each other for a year:

- the Cognitivist, who thinks the colony is where the mind is — the colony, not the ant, is what perceives, decides, remembers and has goals, and individual ants stand to the colony roughly as neurons stand to a brain;
- the Skeptic, who thinks minds belong to organisms, the colony isn't an organism in the relevant sense, colony-level talk is a modelling convenience, and there's no subject there — nothing the colony is trying to do and nothing it's like to be it.

Here's what I want in the document.

A numbered agreed-claims section, and I want claims with actual bite in them, not "ants are interesting". Examples of the sort of thing I mean: no individual ant has anything remotely like a global representation of the colony's state; a true sentence about the colony (the colony is relocating to a better nest site) can be constituted entirely by the behaviour of ants that have no way of understanding that sentence; ants are themselves little agents with their own agendas — worker laying, policing, conflicts over who reproduces — in a way neurons are not, which puts pressure on the ant/neuron analogy; the number of "minds" in a nest is not something you can just read off. Give me whatever else genuinely belongs on the list, and don't pad it.

Then a disagreements table: for each remaining disagreement, the neutral description, plus the formulation of it that each side would accept for its own view. No strawmanning on either side.

Then the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling one would move at least one of them. For each crux: the wording each side accepts, one concrete case or observation both agree bears on it, and each side's prediction written down in advance. The four I definately want covered:

1. The boundary crux. Which candidate system are we evaluating — the workers alone, workers plus queen, or workers plus the pheromone field and the nest architecture they build (the stigmergic substrate)? Both should say whether including the extended stuff changes their verdict, and if it doesn't, why not. My suspicion is that the Cognitivist's case quietly needs the environment included and the Skeptic's needs it excluded.

2. The malfunction crux. When trail-following rules produce the ant mill — the rotating circle army ants get stuck in until they die of exhaustion — is that a colony-level cognitive failure, a breakdown of agency, or just a case where nothing was ever trying to do anything?

3. The decision crux. In nest-site selection with scouts, tandem runs and quorum thresholds: is there something there that deserves to be called a decision with a point of no return, or are we narrativising a dynamical attractor? Say what observation would discriminate and what each predicts about it.

4. The added-value crux. If the local-rules plus stigmergy model already predicts the behaviour, does the colony-level intentional description earn its keep or is it a redescription? I think part of this one is verbal — if you agree, say exactly which part, why, and what non-verbal residue is left.

Add any crux you think is real that I've missed (I'm wondering about persistence: queen death, colony fission, and whether memories laid down in trails and nest structure outliving the workers who made them helps either side).

Two more things: where a piece of the dispute is only about words, flag it as such rather than manufacturing a disagreement; and give me one thing each side would count as evidence against their own view.

Format: numbered agreed claims, then the disagreements table, then cruxes with predictions. Please don't turn it into a verdict on who's right — I want the text both of them could sign. Don't cite anything or point at papers, the handout has to work on its own.
