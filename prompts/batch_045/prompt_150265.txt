I run a philosophy of mind reading group, and the argument about whether dreams are experiences has come to feel completely frozen: Malcolm's side holds that the question has no application, Dennett's side holds that dreams are conscious episodes during sleep of which the waking report is at best a partial sample, and after several decades each side seems to think the other has simply failed to grasp the concept at issue.

My hypothesis is that the dispute is not empirical at all. Malcolm's argument, as I read it, is that what can be said about a dream is fixed by what a person can say and do on waking, so there is no gap between dream and report for further evidence to close; connectedly, that it makes no sense to locate a dream at a time during sleep, since the order in which events are reported is the only thing that fixes their order. Dennett's reply instead treats the report as evidence of a causally sustained episode in sleep, roughly on the model of a report of a hallucination.

I tried testing the hypothesis by stipulating away the empirical uncertainty. Suppose a decoder reads sleep brain activity and reliably predicts everything a sleeper would say if woken, and also flags dream episodes in sleepers who are never woken, so that no report is ever made. A Malcolm-style respondent still says nothing is settled: being the causal source of a possible report is not having an experience, and the new use of the word is merely stipulated. A Dennett-style respondent says that if the episode plays that role and best explains the reports, there is nothing further for an experience to consist in.

So what kind of stalemate is this? Is my hypothesis right — that the disagreement is about which cases count as data (first-person reports versus neural and behavioural evidence) and about the standards for applying the word experience, so that no empirical finding could settle it — or is a hidden empirical or metaphysical question still doing the work, or are the two sides answering different questions under one heading? Note too the awkward case each side claims as its own: sleepers who insist they dreamt but can report nothing at all. Please diagnose with reference to what each position actually commits itself to, and then say what would count as progress given that diagnosis: a distinction, a kind of evidence, a change of question — or nothing at all, and why.
