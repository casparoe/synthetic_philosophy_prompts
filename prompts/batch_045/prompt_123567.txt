hey — I have to write up my "position on consciousness" for my phil of mind seminar (we then swap with two people in the class and they try to break it) and I'd rather find the holes myself before i hand it in. here's roughly where I've landed, numbered so it's easy to poke at:

1. Illusionism is right. There is no phenomenality: no mental state has a what-it's-like-ness, and when we introspect and judge that it does, introspection is misrepresenting our own states as having features they dont have.

2. But that misrepresentation is real. It's a genuine psychological phenomenon with a subpersonal mechanism, it's what makes people believe in qualia, and explaining it is a real empirical project — it's also stubborn, in that learning the neuroscience doesn't switch it off. Relatedly I think Mary doesn't learn a new fact when she leaves the room; she acquires abilities plus a new instance of the very illusion in (1). Which is a bit wierd now that I write it down.

3. Consciousness is real though. The access/functional/reportable/globally-available kind is perfectly real and does causal work. Illusionism is only supposed to deny phenomenality, not consciousness — I'm not an eliminativist about experience full stop.

4. First-person reports are data. Any theory that ends up saying people are wrong about whether it hurts is a non-starter for me. If someone says their pain is throbbing and awful, that's evidence.

5. On pain specifically: what makes a state a pain, and what makes it bad, isn't a felt quality. It's the representing of tissue damage plus the motivational/conative role — it grabs attention, it generates a desire that it stop, it makes you try to get away.

6. But I also want to keep that suffering is the thing that matters morally, and that it matters exactly as much as I thought before I gave up qualia. Factory farming is a catastrophe. And specifically: someone with chronic pain who has completely habituated — it no longer grabs attention, doesn't disrupt anything they do, they've stopped being bothered by it, no avoidance behaviour left — is still being harmed, and harmed in the way that makes their life go worse.

7. And I think the concept of a quale is more or less empty, like phlogiston or vital spirits: there's nothing there for the term to pick out. Talk of qualia isn't even interestingly mistaken.

8. I also want to say both that the hard problem is dissolved and that consciousness is still an extremely hard scientific problem — we basically have no idea how the brain pulls off whatever it pulls off. Feels like having it both ways but I think its fine.

what I want from you: go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and how badly, ranked by severity, and which only look like they are. For each real tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest fix would cost me. Then tell me which single revision cleans up the most. Two caveats: I suspect (1) and (7) might be the same claim said twice rather than two separate commitments, so if that's right say so; and please don't just tell me illusionism is false and I should be a realist about qualia — that's the one move I've already ruled out, and I genuinely don't know which of these I'd give up so dont ask me.
