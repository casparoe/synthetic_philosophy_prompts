i've been going round in circles on Glaucon's challenge and I want someone to tell me whether the set of things I believe is actually a position or just a pile. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. I read Glaucon as asking for something quite specific: show that being just benefits the just person in a way that isn't just the payoffs that flow from being *seen* as just. So he isn't asking a crude prudential question, and any answer of the form you'll probably get caught, or reputation is useful for getting what you want, doesn't even engage him. On this reading, reputation matters to people only instrumentally.

2. I think Socrates' actual answer in the Republic fails on its own terms. However exactly the well-ordered soul is supposed to work, telling me that justice makes my soul harmonious and that this is better for me looks like one more payoff with the wrapping left on. It only looks non-instrumental because Plato is vague about the mechanism.

3. I also hold a motivational constraint on reasons: a reason has to hook into something the agent already cares about. There's no such thing as a reason that's supposed to move you while attaching to nothing you want.

4. So my own answer is a constitutive one: being just partly *constitutes* the things I already care about — friendship, being trusted, being someone I can live with — so the why be just question gets answered without pointing at any extra reward on top.

5. I'm fairly sympathetic to the situationist critique of virtue ethics. I don't think people have robust global character traits of the sort that would you take the ring is supposed to reveal.

6. But I also think the ring is a fair test in a different sense: if you'd take it and use it, then your justice was never real, it was only ever fear of getting caught.

7. Here's a case I made up to stop myself hand-waving, call it the transparent ring: same ring, except that everyone knows you have it, you can't be stopped or punished, and every deed of yours is unfailingly attributed to you. Total publicity, zero sanctions. My verdict: most people would still behave more or less decently, because what really binds us is being known by others rather than being punishible.

Something in here is cracked and I can't locate it. Give me the audit: which of these actually pull against each other and which only look like they do, ordered by how bad the tension is, and for each real one say exactly what I'd have to give up and what that would cost me elsewhere in the set. Don't summarise the Republic at me and don't hand me a reading list — I can't look anything up and I don't want one. Lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence, then give me the reasoning, no preamble.
