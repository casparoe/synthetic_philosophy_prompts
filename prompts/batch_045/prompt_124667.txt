Me and five friends run a little discussion group in the back room of a pub — nobody studied philosophy, we just read something and argue about it for a couple of hours. Last month we did the negative / positive / republican liberty stuff (Berlin, Pettit, that whole argument) and we kept talking straight past each other. Partly that's because one of us works at a warehouse where a scheduling app can cut her shifts with no warning and nobody to appeal to, and we all had a different gut answer about whether she's unfree.

So I want to build a sorting quiz — "what do you actually mean by free?" — about 8-10 questions, each one a concrete little scenario with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key at the end that maps answer patterns onto named positions. Hard rule: nobody should be able to tell from a question which answer goes with which theory, and no question should use the words positive, negative, republican, or domination, or just ask straight out "do you think freedom means being left alone?"

The leaves I want to be able to land on: (1) the plain nobody-is-blocking-you view, (2) the no-arbitrary-power-over-you view, (3) a self-mastery view where what matters is whether you're acting on desires you actually endorse, (4) a harder version of that where there's some standard of your real interests that can overrule what you endorse, and (5) a view where what matters is what you're genuinely able to do, counting money and abilities and nerve. I'd also like at least one leaf for a mix that people really hold, and if some combination of answers turns out to have no home, say so and tell me whether it's incoherent, just unappealing, or merely nobody's bothered to defend it.

Cases that have to be in there (add your own, but these): the master / manager who has total power over you but is kind and never actually exercises it; the rule you had a vote on and can appeal, which still stops you doing something; the person with no external obstacle who can't bring themselves to walk out the door; the person who sincerely and happily endorses their own submission; and — the one I care most about — a case where there's no person on the other end at all: pure market pressure, or an algorithm nobody aimed at you, that reliably shapes what you can do. I want the tree to make each view say something about that last one, because my guess is a couple of them can only handle it by stretching.

I'd like it built from the logical space rather than from the five names: start from the axes the cases actually turn on (is freedom about the menu of options, about who's holding the menu, or about the agent's own internal machinery?), show the combinations, then drop the real positions into them. Please flag any two leaves the quiz genuinely can't seperate — I have a feeling the self-mastery view and the able-to-do-things view collapse on everything except the can't-leave-the-house case, but maybe you can find a better seperator than mine.

Keep scoring doable with pen and paper, tell me what to do with "depends / not sure" answers, and give me one tiebreaker question for when two people land in the same place but I'm definately sure they disagree. No footnotes or further reading — we're not going to look anything up, and half of us won't have internet at the table anyway.
