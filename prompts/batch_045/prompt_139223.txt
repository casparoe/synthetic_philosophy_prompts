Its been almost a year of the same argument in our group and I've been volunteered to write the summary document, so: help me draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration, not a verdict on who's right.

Setup, in case it matters: our group is small and works on making systems overseeable. One camp — call them the Decompositionists — thinks iterated amplification plus distillation is basically sound. Break a task that's too hard to supervise directly into subtasks a weak overseer can check; the choice of decomposition is itself something you can amplify (in the idealization, the overseer has unlimited time and can consult copies of itself, arranged as a tree); the behavior you get out is behavior you've already endorsed, so distilling it into a fast model is just compression; repeat, and each round buys competence you couldn't get by supervising the whole thing end to end, without giving up the ability to check the parts. The other camp — the Residualists — thinks something doesn't survive the pipeline: picking the decomposition is exactly where the judgment lives, some verdicts are integrative rather than aggregative, and distillation converts "I got here by running these steps" into "I output this," so whatever guarantee you had was attached to a process the successor no longer has.

What I need:

1. A numbered section of claims both sides could actually sign — including about the failure modes, not just the optimistic part. I've been trying to get them to at least separate three; tell me if this is the right cut or a fake distinction: decomposition failure (wrong subtasks spawned, or the decomposition is itself misaligned), aggregation failure (right answers to the right subtasks, wrong way of putting them together), and internalization failure (the successor is output-equivalent to the amplified system but not reason-equivalent — it reproduces what the amplified system said without the structure that made it say it).

2. A disagreements table: every point of residual disagreement stated in wording each side would accept for its own position, no strawmen. If the Residualist's real claim turns out to be only about some subclass of tasks, the table should say which subclass. Mark for each row whether it's a disagreement about how the world is, about what "aligned" means, or about words.

3. The cruxes. My candidates — fix or replace them: (a) is the amplified system the definition of the target behavior, or an instrument for measuring a target defined independently? I think this decides whether "preserves alignment" cashes out as output-equivalence or as something stronger, but maybe I'm overstating it. (b) Something I've started calling the legibility tax: decomposition forces intermediate results into a channel the overseer can actually check, and the tasks where that tax is heaviest seem to be — maybe not coincidentally — the tasks where the whole looks like more than the sum of its parts. Real mechanism, or am I smuggling in the conclusion? (c) Whether distillation exerts a selection pressure of its own: the training signal can't distinguish "got it right by running the decomposition" from "got it right by pattern-matching," so even a perfectly aligned amplified system might yield a successor that's aligned only across the distribution it was distilled over. (d) After a few rounds, whose endorsements count — the thing being amplified is a human-plus-model hybrid, and it stops being obvious what "the overseer" refers to.

4. For each crux, one test, task family, or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each camp's prediction written down in advance. For (c) I had in mind: amplify and distill on a family of tasks, then test the distilled model on recombinations of sub-skills it never saw composed, and separately on perturbations chosen so that the amplified system would pick a different decomposition — if the distilled model's behavior doesn't move when the amplified system's would, that's evidence it isn't tracking the process. Is that design load-bearing or does it beg the question, and what would each camp predict?

Also: if part of this turns out to be verbal, say which part and why — I'd rather circulate a shorter document than a longer one. And definately don't pad it with a reading list; we've all read the same five blog posts.
