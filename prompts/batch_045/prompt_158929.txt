I have been reading on my own about dispositions, roughly the idea that calling a glass fragile or a powder soluble is not just a record of what it has done but a claim about what it would do, and about the ways that the simple test of asking what would happen if the glass were struck or the powder were dunked comes apart. As I understand it, there are cases where the very event that would trigger the disposition destroys it before it can act, cases where the trigger arrives but something else in the vicinity prevents the expected result from showing up, and cases where the expected result shows up anyway in something that never had the disposition at all. I have also been playing with a fourth case I have not seen discussed: a disposition that quietly lapses the moment anyone comes to know reliably whether the thing has it, so that it can be had but never certified, however careful the inspector.

What I would like is a satirical internal memorandum of about six hundred words, written in the deadpan voice of a career civil servant at a fictional standards bureau charged with verifying claims like fragile, flammable, or soluble before they may be printed on a product label. The joke should come from the metaphysics rather than from anyone being foolish: the bureau's procedures should keep destroying, hiding, or faking the property they are meant to detect, and its proposed reforms should follow that logic honestly, all the way to certifying goods no one has tested and counting breakages with the wrong cause, until a reader sees that a disposition was never something you could simply read off what actually happens. Please keep the civil servants competent and the concepts clean, so that someone who works on this material would agree the satire is fair rather than merely unkind. I would also like my fourth case included, handled as seriously as the others. Write the whole thing as continuous prose, without headings, bullets, numbering, or bold text, the way a long email reads.
