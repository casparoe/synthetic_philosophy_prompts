Our reading group has been chewing on the criteria of authenticity for something like eight months now — multiple attestation, dissimilarity, embarrassment, coherence, contextual credibility, the Aramaic/Palestinian background thing — and we have completely stopped moving. The people who think the criteria are basically broken and the people who think they just need to be applied more carefully seem to talk straight past each other, and pretty much the same five moves have been recycled since week three.

My own hypothesis is that this isnt really a methodological dispute at all, or at least not only that: I think the two sides are using the criteria for two different jobs and dont notice the switch. One side wants a filter on the tradition — a negative test, this bit cant be early, so drop it — and the other wants something that gives you positive confidence that this particular saying or episode goes back to Jesus, and I'm not sure any one criterion can do both. But I also suspect something worse is underneath: every criterion leans on a background picture of what first-century Galilean Judaism was like and what the early church would or wouldnt have invented, and that background picture is itself reconstructed from the very texts being tested. So the whole apparatus might be quietly circular, and the real fight might be about whether that circularity is fatal or is just how all history works.

So: what kind of stall is this, actually? A verbal one about "authentic"? Two questions under one label — one about a scholarly reconstruction, one about something the tradition has a stake in? A disagreement about what counts as evidence versus what counts as a prior? Or a genuine methodological dispute that simply cannot be settled because there's no control corpus: we have no body of sayings attributed to some other first-century Galilean teacher whose provenance we independently know, so the criteria can never be calibrated or checked for base rates, and maybe that is the whole problem. If you think my hypotheses are wrong, say so bluntly rather than being polite about it.

What I want is a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of the criteria — I've read the surveys. One sentence stating the diagnosis at the top, then the reasoning, then what would count as progress given that diagnosis: a better model of oral transmission, a calibration case borrowed from somewhere else in ancient historiography, dropping "authentic" in favour of something like "what best explains the evidence" so the question dissolves, or nothing at all. Keep the whole thing under 700 words.
