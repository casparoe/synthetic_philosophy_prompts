here's roughly what I have in mind, but change anything that dosn't work: a woman in her forties, Maren, whose brother is ill a two-hour train ride away; every Sunday evening she buys a non-refundable advance ticket to go see him on Saturday, and every Saturday morning she doesn't go. the booking service she uses lets you set your own cancellation penalty at the moment of purchase, and the bit I'd like you to build the story on is that she keeps choosing bigger and bigger penalties — she can predict perfectly well that she'll bail, so she tries to price herself into going — and it never works, because by Saturday the penalty is already sunk while the trip is immediatly there in front of her. two things to complicate it: the option to cancel honestly can't be removed, because sometimes (his condition is unstable) Saturday genuinely is the wrong day, so a fully binding contract isn't available to her; and a case worker at the service eventually suggests she stop buying tickets at all, on the grounds that the Sunday purchase has become a substitute for the visit — the felt sense of having done the right thing, plus the money wasted, is now doing the emotional work of the trip itself, so the commitment device is making the thing it was bought for less likely. please don't let any character explain any of this out loud; it should come through in what happens.
