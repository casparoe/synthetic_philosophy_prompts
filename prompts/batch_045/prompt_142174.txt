My case: a tile of alloy X, sitting on a bench. X is ductile — strike it at 20 m/s and it dents, no cracking. But X transforms under impact: above roughly 25 m/s the impact itself drives a phase change into a brittle phase Y within about 50 microseconds, and the remainder of that same impact then shatters what is now a tile of Y. Between 25 and 27 m/s you get a partial transform and the tile cracks badly without coming apart. So: is the tile, sitting there now, disposed to shatter when struck at 30 m/s?

If you pre-transformed the tile into Y and then hit it at 30 m/s, of course it would shatter and of course it would be brittle. The question is whether the tile in my hand is now disposed to do that.
