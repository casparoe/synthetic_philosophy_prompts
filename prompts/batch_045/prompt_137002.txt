Our local rationalist-adjacent discussion group — about 400 people, we rent a room an run a Discord — is fighting over whether to let a tiny splinter faction host a talk. Call them the Remnant. A dozen people, tops. They don't shout anyone down, they turn up to debates and argue in decent technical form, no threats, no force. But they're completely explicit that the open-debate norm is itself a mistake, and that if they ever had a majority they'd shut down the people they think are corrupting the culture. Someone found their private group chat saying exactly that.

Mira: this is just the paradox of tolerance, Popper's point. Tolerance is a cooperative equilibrium, a public good, and public goods have free-rider problems — a group can enjoy the protection of a norm while openly planning to dismantle it. Moloch eats norms like ours unless somebody enforces the boundary. And if the trigger is wait until they're actually using force, you've waited until it's too late to coordinate against them. A forward-looking trigger has to be part of the package, and their own stated intentions are the best evidence we'll ever get.

Dave: Mira's trigger is much broader than the one in the original argument. As he reads it, the claim to a right of suppression is tied to the intolerant not being prepared to meet you on the level of rational argument — fists and pistols instead of reasons. These guys still argue. Suppress people who are still arguing with you and you break the whole thing from the other direction. And separately: intolerant is the single most valuable label in any political fight, so once the rule is exclude those who would exclude, every faction has a massive incentive to pin it on its rivals. He calls it the ratchet — the boundary only expands, because over-labeling is free and nothing audits it.

Mira: that's an objection to bad implementation, not to the principle; we don't abolish locks because locksmiths can be crooked. Also, there's an asymmetry Dave is ignoring. A false positive costs a harmless weird group a room for an evening. A false negative costs everyone the entire arrangement, permanently, irreversibly. With that payoff structure the optimal classifier is deliberately over-inclusive — you eat lots of false positives because the one false negative is unrecoverable.

Dave: that asymmetry argument has exactly the shape of every witch hunt in history — infinite stakes, so every threshold collapses to zero. And it quietly assumes suppression works. Often it doesn't: excluding a fringe is also how you hand them a martyr story and free publicity. If suppression is sometimes self-defeating, the asymmetry can invert. He also says the safer version is the one where you're only allowed to act on demonstrable effects on other people's liberty, not on the content of what a group believes.

Mira: fine, then make it conditional on capacity instead of belief. Banning a mocked 3% probably grows them. Capping a group that's already at 15% and organized probably works. So the rule should be size-dependent, not absolute.

Dave: congrats, now the content of their doctrine is irrelevant and we're doing pure headcount-based repression. Also — who exactly is the we here?
