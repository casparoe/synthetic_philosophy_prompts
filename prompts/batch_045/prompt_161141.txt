I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I need a conceptual disambiguation of a slogan that has started doing suspicious amounts of work in my project's design arguments.

The slogan is the Confucian line recorded in the Analects, where Confucius is asked about government and replies with a list of paired roles; it is usually rendered in English as something like: let the ruler be a ruler, the minister a minister, the father a father, the son a son. I am deliberately rendering it loosely rather than quoting a particular translation, so please work from that gist and not from the exact wording of any version you may have memorized.

I first met it in a mailing-list thread about our open-source project's governance model. We were arguing between a Benevolent Dictator For Life (BDFL) model and a rotating-maintainer model. A contributor invoked the slogan as if it obviously showed that a well-ordered community requires clearly defined, non-interchangeable offices—ruler, minister, and so on—each with its own duties, such that whoever occupies the ruler office must do ruler things and not minister things. Later, in a design review of our permissions microservice, a different colleague used the same slogan to argue that a resource recorded as a parent in our data model (what they called the father role) must not perform subordinate actions (what they called the son role); if the parent resource started acting like a child resource, that would be role confusion and would break the normative order of the system.

To make this tractable for my system, I've been using a home-made distinction between two ways a role could constrain behavior. Call the first a spec-role: a role is an explicit, enumerable specification of permissions and duties fixed in advance, like an IAM policy document or a TypeScript interface, where conformance can be checked before or independently of any ongoing interaction. Call the second an enact-role: a role is a pattern of behavior that successfully maintains the reciprocal expectations of the other occupants in an ongoing practice, where what the role requires at a given moment cannot be fully specified in advance and can only be judged by whether the interaction stays in harmony. Please use 'spec-role' and 'enact-role' exactly as I've defined them here.
