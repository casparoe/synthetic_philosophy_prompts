I am a professor of metaphysics and philosophy of science, and next month I am to give a single ninety-minute seminar to the world-modelling group at an industrial research lab. The group trains large sequence models to predict future frames from observational video. Two features of their situation matter for what I need from you. First, they have no interventional data whatsoever: no experiment on the systems they model, no intervention on the apparatus, nothing in their setup corresponding to doing something to a system rather than watching it. Second, they are fluent in their own vocabulary and impatient with mine. I know their vocabulary well enough to audit a translation but not well enough to produce a fluent one myself.

What I want is a restatement of the dispute between Humean and anti-Humean accounts of lawhood in that vocabulary, as faithful as you can make it, followed by a report on what the restatement fails to carry. Take the positions to be these three: the best-system account, on which laws are the axioms of the strongest and most economical true summary of the totality of occurrent particular fact, and nothing in the world answers to 'governing'; the necessitarian account, on which laws are relations among universals or properties, external to the occurrent facts, and they necessitate the events falling under them; and the dispositionalist account, on which there are no laws at bottom and the modal force comes instead from the causal powers properties have essentially. The third is the one I expect to resist translation, and I want to know why.

The vocabulary to work in, which I shall be checking: hypothesis class and its inductive bias, the training distribution, the realizability assumption against agnostic learning, held-out and out-of-distribution generalisation, minimum-description-length and regularisation as the trade-off between simplicity and strength, identifiability, aleatoric versus epistemic uncertainty, and the data-generating process.

Carry the whole translation on one case, which I intend to use in the seminar: a model trained on every existing video of one particular double pendulum in one particular laboratory, which fits the training frames essentially perfectly and predicts freshly recorded frames of the same apparatus well, after which the apparatus is dismantled and no further experiment on it is possible. The question that must survive translation is what, if anything, fixes what that pendulum would have done had it been released from a different angle.

I have three suspicions, and I want them adjudicated rather than flattered. The first is that 'data-generating process' is already a piece of anti-Humean metaphysics, so that any translation resting on it has begged the question, and that when I remove it I am left with the training distribution, which is itself a posit of structure over merely possible inputs and so is not available to the Humean either. The second is that the contrast between realizability and agnostic learning is the wrong place for the dispute to land, since the Humean is not an agnostic learner and the necessitarian law is not an element of a hypothesis class anyone selected; on this view, the genuine recurrence of the old problem about which predicates are projectible is the choice of hypothesis class. The third is that the distinction between aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty cross-cuts the Humean's own treatment of objective chance, leaving out something on both sides.

Give me the residue report as a table, one row per claim or commitment, each marked as translating cleanly, translating only with distortion, or having no expression in their vocabulary at all; and where a row is dirty or empty, say whether you take that to indict the vocabulary or to show that the philosophical claim was doing less work than its defenders suppose. If there is a decision in their actual pipeline that the Humean/anti-Humean difference would change, name one; if there is not, say so and say what that itself shows. Do not supply references or further reading, and do not let the translation turn the Humean into someone who denies that the patterns are real.

Close with a single machine-readable line: \answer{vocabulary}, \answer{debate}, \answer{both}, or \answer{neither}, according to whether the residue as a whole is best read as a defect of the machine-learning vocabulary, a sign that the metaphysical dispute is less substantive than it looks, both, or neither.
