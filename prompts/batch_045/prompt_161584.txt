hey, I've been going back and forth on the language of thought hypothesis for a couple years now and I have this nagging feeling that its not really a live debate anymore so much as a loop. Every argument on both sides seems to be decades old and mutually well known: the productivity and systematicity arguments (thought is generative and structured, so there must be a medium with combinatorial syntax and semantics that the operations are defined over), the argument from computation (thinking is causal-transformation-of-representations, so there has to be a vehicle with the right structure), and then the connectionist reply that you can get structure-sensitive, apparently systematic behavior out of a network that never tokens anything sentence-like — plus the counter-charge that when such networks succeed they're really just implementing a classical architecture in a distributed way. Everyone I talk to knows all of this and still lands in the same two camps. So I want a diagnosis, not a survey, and please no literature review or reading list — I've read the stuff, I want you to tell me whats actually going on with the disagreement.

Here's the bit that got me thinking, and its the part I most want you to engage with, because I made it up and you won't have seen it before. Call it the RSS result (recoverable sentence-like states). A lab trains a large sequence model end to end on next-token prediction — no compositional machinery built in, just weighted sums and nonlinearities. Afterward they find a linear map from hidden states into a space where: (i) the same content-bearing element gets approximately the same vector wherever it shows up in a sequence; (ii) swapping that vector in swaps the element's contribution to downstream behavior; (iii) you can combine vectors for the parts and predict, better than chance, what the model will do on the whole. But the map wasn't designed, the model's basic operations are still just matrix multiplications, and the compositional structure is only visible through the map — you can't point to a part of the network and say "here is the constituent."

I described this to two friends. One: "that's just what LOT always said — there's your mental sentences, case closed." Other: "that's a description, not a mechanism. You've shown the system's activity can be re-described compositionally at the right grain; you haven't shown compositionality is doing any causal work in it. You've committed the read-out fallacy — inferring from the existence of a faithful read-out that what it describes is a real constituent of the process rather than a shadow the dynamics happens to cast. Worse, the map is fitted by optimizing agreement with a scheme we bring to it, so the compositionality may be in the map, not in the model."

So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? My own guess, offered as a guess, is that it's mostly a Marr-level slide — one side treats LOT as a claim at the level of representation and algorithm (a claim about what the best explanation of the behavior posits), the other hears it as an implementational claim about causal constituents, so they talk straight past each other and the RSS result is evidence for neither. But I can also see it being: a verbal dispute about "representation," "language," and "vehicle"; two different questions under one name, where one side is asking an empirical question about cognitive architecture and the other is staking out a methodological stance about what a cognitive explanation has to look like; a disagreement about which data get to count as evidence (human behavioral systematicity, neural read-outs, engineering results); a hidden empirical question that nobody has designed the right experiment for; or just bedrock intuitions about whether "really running a language" requires implementation-locality or only a counterfactually robust description.

Tell me which of these is doing the real work — or if it's several, how they're stacked — and argue for it using what each side actually says rather than listing positions. Then, given your diagnosis, tell me what would count as progress: a distinction, an experiment (and what it would have to show — what would tell a shadow from a constituent?), a formal result, a change of question? Or is this the sort of thing where no progress should be expected, and if so, is that because the question is ill-posed, because it's merely hard, or because of how the field is organized? One more thing I want answered directly: on your diagnosis, does the hypothetical RSS result favor anybody, or is it neutral in a way that's itself diagnostic?

Answer in American English, and feel free to be blunt about it if the blunt answer is that weve been asking the wrong question.
