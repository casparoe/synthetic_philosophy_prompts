Here's the chapter. The thesis is that moral responsibility is entirely a matter of the fittingness of reactive attitudes, but that the fittingness facts aren't fixed independently of our practices of holding people responsible — because reactive attitudes are plastic. Being the sustained target of resentment, contempt or blame changes a person: it degrades the very capacities (to see others as participants, to feel guilt, to take reasons seriously in the way Strawsonians care about) in virtue of which blame is supposed to fit them in the first place. So there's a feedback loop: the practice of holding responsible partly makes and unmakes the property it's answering to. I call this the plasticity constraint. I argue (a) that any Strawsonian account that can't represent this loop is incomplete, (b) that it explains why excessive or structurally biased blaming is worse than merely unfair — it's self-fulfilling, it manufactures the impairment it then points at, and (c) that this gives us a new object of blame: a community or an institution can be blameworthy for the way its blaming practices erode the agency of the people it blames, even when each individual act of blame seems fine.

Sketch of the argument, in case you want to aim at it. Step one: I claim the reactive attitudes are the right starting point, not because they're a detection mechanism for some free-standing property, but because the participant stance is constitutive of the kind of relationship in which responsibility talk has a point. Step two: I note that participant-stance capacities are psychologically real, developmentally acquired, and responsive to social treatment — this is the empirical bit, and I lean on it heavily. Step three: from that I argue that the fittingness of blame at a time depends in part on the causal history of how that agent has been held responsible up to that time. Step four: I use that to distinguish two things people normally run together — blame that's unfitting because the agent didn't have the capacity, versus blame that's unfitting because we spent it down. Step five: I extend the accountability to the blamers, via the same apparatus, so it's not a special pleading.
