I've been reading the rationalist blogs since the Sequences were still going up, and the infinitarian-paralysis argument comes around roughly once a year: if the world contains infinitely much value, then no act of mine changes the total, aggregative ethics goes silent, and the comment thread spends three hundred comments proposing patches. I'm writing a post arguing that the familiar patches are not three formulations of one move but three genuinely different criteria that can come apart, and I want a single case that pries them apart. So I need a construction from you, not a survey — please don't cite, name, or link anything.

Three criteria, fixed in advance so that we don't argue about definitions later:

**PAD** (person-affecting dominance, strong-asymmetry version). Rank options by what happens to persons. X is better than Y if everyone who exists under both options is exactly as well off under X as under Y, and the remaining differences are only that (i) some people exist under X who do not exist under Y, each of them with a life worth living, and (ii) some people exist under Y who do not exist under X. Clause (ii) is free: there is nobody whom a prevented life is worse for. If anyone at all is made worse off going from Y to X, PAD refuses to rank the pair.

**LDA** (long-run average welfare per life). Take the future as an infinite sequence of epochs with a natural time order. For an option W and horizon N, let A_W(N) be the total welfare of everyone who lives in epochs 1 through N, divided by the number of such people. X is better than Y iff lim A_X(N) exceeds lim A_Y(N). If either limit fails to exist, LDA is silent.

**LLS** (liminf/limsup truncation). Let D(N) be total welfare realized in epochs 1 through N under X minus the same under Y. X is better iff lim inf D(N) > 0; Y is better iff lim sup D(N) < 0; otherwise no verdict.

What I want is a pair of options — X, in which an agent acts, and Y, in which she doesn't — over a future treated as infinite, finitely many people per epoch, integer welfare levels, such that all three hold at once:

- PAD says X is better than Y.
- LDA says Y is better than X, strictly: not silence, not a tie.
- LLS gives no verdict, and robustly so — build it so that lim sup D(N) = +∞ and lim inf D(N) = −∞, rather than leaving the silence resting on a knife-edge.

Constraints. One act by one agent: X should be the sort of thing an institution could actually adopt (a charter, a mandate, a settlement or replication protocol), and its effects should be infinite only because the world is, not because the agent has infinite power. No rearranging the universe by fiat, no supertasks performed by the agent, no simulators or deities, no perfect prediction — the propagation mechanism should be bureaucratic or physical. The scenario itself should fit in one paragraph of about 150–200 words. Every verdict must be derived from a pair of worlds specified completely enough that D(N), A_X(N) and A_Y(N) can be written down; stipulating the verdicts and working backwards to the worlds is the one thing that would make this useless to me.

What I want back:

1. The scenario paragraph.
2. A compact generating rule for both worlds — a formula, or a short table giving what epoch t looks like under each option, ideally organized into blocks whose sizes I could scale up and down.
3. A section per criterion: the verdict, and the actual computation. For LLS, write the block-by-block contribution to D(N) and show that the two limits are +∞ and −∞. For LDA, show that both limits exist and say what they are. For PAD, go clause by clause: who exists in both, who exists in only one, and why nobody is worse off.
4. An honest note on which structural feature is doing which work, plus ablations: what happens to each verdict if all blocks are the same size; if the created lives are set above the baseline welfare instead of below it; if the asymmetry is dropped from PAD; and if the future is capped at some large finite N instead of being infinite.
5. One point I think you'll hit and that I want stated explicitly: to make the negative half of the oscillation you need contributions that reduce welfare without making anyone worse off, which forces the losses to be prevented lives rather than harms to existing people. Say whether that's right, and what it costs the case — I'd rather know that the case inherits a contested population-ethics commitment than discover it in comments.
6. A steelman of the attack available to each criterion that comes out badly: what the LDA partisan says about per-life averages being order- and measure-dependent and about averages always having had this fight with Pareto; what the PAD partisan says about the asymmetry carrying the whole result; and what the LLS partisan says about silence not being a verdict and the case merely redescribing paralysis.
7. One line on whether the PAD-versus-LDA split already arises in a finite truncation, and one line on what I would not be entitled to conclude even if the case works.
