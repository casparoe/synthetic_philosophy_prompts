I'm the patient rep on the advisory panel at a fertility clinic -- I've got no background in any of this, I got on the panel because I did IVF there twice -- and we've got a request coming up that I can't work out how to vote on. I've kept notes on our calls over about four years, so here's the track record, roughly:

- Huntington's in the family: we test the embryos and tell the couple we won't transfer an affected one. Nobody argued about that one.
- BRCA1: we test and help them pick, but we said we wouldn't refuse to transfer a embryo that carries it, since there's stuff you can do later (screening, surgery). So: help, no veto.
- CF: same as BRCA1, help them choose, no veto. Someone pointed out last year that the treatments are much better now than when we first decided that, and we never went back and revisited it.
- Achondroplasia, both parents have it, they wanted a child who doesn't: we helped them pick. Very long, awkward meeting about whether us helping means we're saying their lives are worse; we came down on "no it doesn't, or anyway we're not going to police that."
- Achondroplasia, both parents have it, they wanted a child who does: no. We won't help make a child who'd need surgeries.
- Sex selection just for family balancing (two boys, they want a girl): no.
- Sex selection to avoid Duchenne: yes.
- HLA matching so the new baby's cord blood can treat an existing sick kid: yes, with conditions -- serious condition in the sibling, nothing extra asked of the baby, and the kid gets told later.
- Polygenic score for height, both parents very short, reason given was "so he doesn't get teased": no, and it was close. The minutes just say "not a medical reason."

Now the new one. A couple with several embryos. Both sides of the family have severe early obesity, one of them has had bariatric surgery, and they want us to rank the embryos by a polygenic BMI score and transfer the lowest one. Their reason is health -- diabetes, joints, the way their own childhoods went -- not looks, and I believe them. We don't currently offer any polygenic ranking at all, so a yes means deciding to start.

Two things from our last meeting that I keep going round on:

One, the score mostly predicts in a food environment like the one we actually live in. Feed the kid differently and those variants wouldn't do much. So part of me thinks we're being asked to offset a environment the parents themselves are going to create, which feels like a different thing from Huntington's. The other part says the environment is just a fact, it isn't their fault, and the kid is the one who pays for it.

Two, if a lot of parents start doing this, the kids who are still heavy end up with fewer peers, less accomodation, and more of the stigma concentrated on them. Which makes each individual request look *more* justified, not less. I genuinely don't know what to do with that.

So: is this the BRCA1 case or the height case, and what's the closest analog really? What's the rule that fits all nine of our old calls -- and are there two or three that fit them about equally well but come apart here? My guess is one of them is something like "maximize the kid's expected well-being, minus anything that's purely positional," and another is "parents can aim at whatever they want as long as they don't put the kid below some floor and we're not sending a nasty message about a group of people who already exist" -- I saw the phrase "procreative beneficence" somewhere, is that the first one? -- but I want to know what each one predicts here, which one we'd actually be committing ourselves to if we vote yes versus no, and what case we should put in front of ourselves next to find out which one we've been running all along. And if our nine old calls don't actually fit any single rule, say that too, and tell me which one we'd have to give up.
