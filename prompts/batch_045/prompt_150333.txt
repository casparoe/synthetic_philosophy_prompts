I'm retired now — 35 years of satellite retrievals and inverse methods, for whatever its worth — and I've been sitting in on a Bayesian epistemology reading group at the local university. Last week they sprang what they called the Judy Benjamin problem on us and I can see why it's a nuisance, but before I read any further into the proposed solutions I want the spec. What should I be demanding of a rule before I look at what anyone's rule does?

The setup as we had it (I'm not fussy about the canonical numbers, so here's my own version): a soldier parachutes into a territory divided into a friendly zone, Blue, and an enemy zone, Red, and each of those is split into two company areas, call them 2nd Company and HQ. Four cells, she has a prior over all four. Her radio crackles and what comes through is not "you are in Blue" and not even "your chance of being in 2nd Company is now 0.6". It's news about a conditional: given that you're in Blue, your chance of being in 2nd Company's area is three quarters. Nothing is said about the conditional chances inside Red. She's supposed to come out with new probabilities over all four cells.

So: write me the desiderata. What does a general rule for updating on evidence of that shape have to deliver to count as adequate — for the purpose I fix at the bottom — with the case or consideration behind each requirement, and each one marked as either non-negotiable or just what one camp happens to like. The ones I want you to take a definite position on, and add to as you see fit:

- It has to collapse to plain conditionalization when the news is simply "A, for certain", and to Jeffrey-style conditioning when the input is a bare new probability over a partition. Is either of those actually negotiable, or are they just the limiting cases that any rule worth discussing recovers automatically?
- Locality or rigidity or whatever you want to call it: a message that is explicitly about the conditional chances inside Blue shouldn't scramble the conditional odds inside Blue, and my group could not agree whether the same preservation is also required for the odds inside Red, which the message never mentioned. Tell me what the requirement really is and what case would settle it.
- Order. Two such messages arrive one after the other. Does it matter which I apply first? Is commutativity a genuine desideratum or a comfort blanket people reach for because it's mathematically tidy? If order does matter, say plainly what that means for a rule I'd actually implement.
- The input isn't a proposition, and this is the bit that bugs me most. A claim about what your own credences should be is not an event in a four-cell algebra and not a fact about the terrain either. So a rule owes me an account of what the object of an update is, not just a recipe for shuffling four numbers. What's owed here and where does it bite?
- Indicative versus subjunctive. Half the room said a counterfactual version of the message is a completely different problem. Is it, and does one rule have to cover both?
- Exploitability: various people gesture at Dutch-book or scoring-rule or accuracy arguments. Is not-being-exploitable over time a desideratum or a prejudice? Same question for "be the closest distribution to your old one that satisfies the constraint, under some information distance" — that one smells like the regularization I did for a living and I distrust myself for liking it.
- Grain: the message uses a partition finer than mine, or coarser. What's a rule obliged to do then.
- And one of my own, which I can't place: real radios are noisy. Suppose the channel garbles one message in ten and she can't tell whether hers was one of them. Is that a new desideratum, or just the old ones applied to a bigger space? I genuinely can't tell whether it's the same problem wearing a hat.

Then the part I actually care about: take your own list and tell me which subset of it can't be satisfied together. My suspicion is that the collision is between something like (rigidity inside the partition the message is about) and (the message carrying no news at all about whether you're in that partition) and (conditionalization in the limit) — and that the collision is the point of the example rather than a defect in the candidate rules. But I may have the wrong pair, or the wrong number of pairs. If the impossibility sits somewhere else, say where, and say which single requirement on your list does the most work in knocking candidate rules out.

Purpose, so the negotiable/non-negotiable marks mean something: I want a rule I could code up and run. Not a theory of ideal rationality for its own sake — something that takes probabilistic, conditional-shaped readings off an instrument or off an expert panel (which is more or less what my old retrieval codes did with an a priori covariance) and returns a posterior, without my having to invent a prior over the messenger's psychology. If a requirement only matters to someone doing foundations of rationality, mark it that way and move on.

Last, briefly — a line or two each, no scorecard, I'll take that bit to the group: how the minimum-information/minimal-change family, the treat-the-conditional-as-something-with-a-probability-and-conditionalize-on-it family, and the supposing/imaging sort of rule do against your list, and which one you'd actually hand to someone with a noisy instrument and a deadline.
