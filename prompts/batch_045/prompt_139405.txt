ok so this is gonna sound random but I'm in this bio club at school and we got into a huge dumb argument last month about whether viruses are alive and it never got resolved, so now I want to build one of those which-theory-do-you-secretly-hold quizzes for the club: you answer a bunch of concrete cases and at the end it tells you which definition of life you've been assuming this whole time. I want 10 or 11 questions, each one a specific case with three answer options (alive / not alive / alive but something else is going on, or the question is just malformed), and a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the actual positions. The cases I want used: a mule; a seed that sat in a freezer for 200 years and then gets planted; a prion; a fire spreading up a hillside; one of those lab cell lines descended from a single person's cells that's been dividing in dishes since the 1950s and has evolved to live on its own; a bacterium whose genome was built from scratch and only has the genes it needs for life in a nutrient soup; a transposon, the kind of DNA sequence that just copies itself around a genome without ever being a cell; a worm program on an offline network that mutates and competes with other copies; a robot that can scrounge parts and build a working copy of itself; and a slime mold where separate cells merge into one blob and later split up again. I also want at least one case where the honest answer is alive but not an organism, and at least one that separates being alive from being able to die, since I think people conflate those without noticing.

The main thing is that no question should ask anything like do you think viruses are alive or is life about metabolism or reproduction — nobody in my club knows those words and they'd just pick the label they like, and also those questions are boring. Every question should be a case where two of the criteria come apart, so the pattern of answers does the sorting and the person doesn't know which position each answer pushes them toward. Please build the key from the logical space instead of from a list of names: first the branch questions and what combinations of answers they allow, and only then put the named positions on the leaves. The branch questions I had in mind: does it have to keep itself going far from equilibrium (metabolism, or autopoiesis if that's different); does it have to reproduce at all; is reproduction enough or does it need heredity plus variation so it can actually accumulate adaptations by selection (my textbook says that's the working definition NASA uses, a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution, if im remembering that right); does it have to be one bounded individual instead of a colony or a lineage; does it need there to be a sense in which things can go well or badly for it; and does any of this require carbon chemistry or is the substrate irrelevant. Leaves should include the checklist-property view, autopoiesis, the Darwinian/NASA-style one, the replicator-or-information one, the pluralist there-are-several-different-things-here one, and the deflationary one that says life isn't a real category at all. For the empty cells in the grid, say why each one is empty: incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Is Sam's view actually a distinct position or does it secretly turn into one of the others under pressure, and does mine collapse into autopoiesis or is there a case where they disagree? Also please flag any pair of leaves the quiz straight up can't tell apart, and where you can, add one tiebreaker question for that pair, but if the instruments genuinely can't separate them just say so instead of inventing a case that does. Jargon in the scoring key is fine, just not in the questions themselves.
