I'm writing up my position on consciousness for a graduate seminar, and before I present it I would like the set audited for coherence rather than for truth — I can defend the theses individually, what I cannot see is whether they can all be held at once. Here is the package as I currently hold it.

(1) Qualia epiphenomenalism, restricted to physical effects. Nothing ever makes a causal difference to any physical event in virtue of its phenomenal character; my saying that it hurts has a complete causal explanation in neural terms. I hold this because of the causal closure of the physical together with a strong aversion to systematic causal overdetermination, and those two motivations are why I hold it — I am not relying on any argument from the conceivability of zombies.

(2) Introspective authority, understood as theory-independent. I know what my current experience is like immediately and non-inferentially, and the security of that knowledge does not depend on my accepting any psychophysical theory. If a psychophysical theory I believed turned out to be false, that would not shake my knowledge that I am now in pain.

(3) The tracking reply to the paradox of phenomenal judgment. My introspective judgment is produced by the neural correlate N of the experience, and N nomically necessitates the quale Q, so my belief covaries with Q across the relevant counterfactual situations. Knowledge requires counterfactual covariation rather than causal contact, so my judgment can be knowledge of Q even though Q causes nothing.

(4) No brute content. An intentional state refers to, or is about, something only in virtue of an appropriate causal-informational relation to it. I accept this because the Twin Earth style of argument seems to me to work, and I want to extend it to phenomenal content: what makes my judgment about Q rather than about N, if anything does, is its place in a causal-informational order.

(5) A verdict on a case. My molecule-for-molecule duplicate who lacks qualia produces the same utterance I do, by the same causal route. I want to say that his utterance is not true and that he knows nothing about his experience, whereas mine is true and I do know.

(6) Phenomenal concepts are direct. A phenomenal concept picks out its referent otherwise than by way of any contingent description — not via its neural or functional role — and this directness is, I think, what generates the explanatory gap. My own proposal for making it compatible with (1) is that the experience is not a cause of the judgment but is partly constitutive of it: the judgment-token is realized in the experience-token, and constitution is not causation, so (1) is untouched. I also claim that this directness is consistent with (4): content can be externally fixed and still be direct.

(7) The mental-to-mental exception. Since (1) is a thesis about the physical only, qualia can cause later mental events: the pain makes me attend to my knee and is the ground of my episodic memory of it, and qualia partly explain the character of my subsequent stream of consciousness.

(8) No revisionary practical upshot. Epiphenomenalism does not touch the moral significance of suffering, because mattering is a normative relation rather than a causal one; if I concluded that some system — an animal, or a future artificial system — had genuine qualia, that would count in favour of taking its interests into account exactly as it would if qualia were efficacious. And I think behavioural and functional evidence can in principle tell us whether another system has qualia.

What I want is an audit, not a survey. Which pairs of these are genuinely in tension, and how severe is each? For every genuine tension, state precisely what would have to be given up and what the cheapest repair is — including cases where the repair is to one of the motivations attached to (1) rather than to a numbered thesis, since I take those to be separable. Some of what looks like a tension I suspect dissolves on inspection; I would like those separated from the real ones, with the distinction that dissolves them stated explicitly rather than left implicit. Then tell me which single revision, if I made it, would clean up the most, and what it would cost me elsewhere in the package. If I have helped myself to a premise I have not stated, identify it.
