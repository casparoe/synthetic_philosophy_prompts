I'm writing a novel set in an AI lab and I've got two characters stuck in an argument I can't write my way out of. One keeps insisting capability and goals are seperate things — "horsepower is horsepower, the steering wheel is a different part, you can bolt any wheel onto any engine" — and uses that to say a really smart system could still want something completely dumb. The other says that's backwards: intelligence is more like a river, it cuts its own bed, so anything smart enough will slide into the same few sub-goals no matter what it was pointed at — grabbing resources, keeping itself running, not letting anyone rewrite what it wants.

Both of these feel like they're smuggling something, and there's a wrinkle: the system in my book wasn't programmed, it was trained on a proxy score, so nothing was ever literally bolted on. Can you audit the two analogies for me — what maps to what in each, where each one breaks, and which of those breaks actually matter for the conclusions my characters are drawing? I also want to know whether the river picture just overcorrects, since it seems to imply the goals are fixed by the capability, which is the opposite mistake. If one of them has to win the scene, who should it be, and if there's a better image for two smart people to throw at each other, give me that too.
