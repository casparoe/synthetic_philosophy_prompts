QUESTION 2 (25 marks)

Threshold-Lexical Negative Utilitarianism (TLN), as set out in the handout:

- Suffering and enjoyment are not measured on a single scale. A moment of suffering is *blocking* if its intensity is at or above τ = 2 on the 0–10 intensity scale; suffering below τ is *noise-level*.
- No quantity, intensity or distribution of enjoyment can compensate for even one moment of blocking suffering.
- Worlds are ranked in three stages: (1) PEAK — compare the single most intense moment of blocking suffering anywhere in the world; lower is better. (2) If the peak ties, COUNT — compare how many distinct individuals ever undergo a moment of blocking suffering at that peak intensity; fewer is better. (3) Only if both tie, NET ENJOYMENT — maximize total enjoyment minus 3 × total noise-level suffering. Duration and population size enter at stages (2) and (3) only, never at (1).
- NB: an empty world contains no blocking suffering and scores 0 on net enjoyment.

(a) Explain how TLN differs both from classical negative utilitarianism and from a merely "suffering-biased" utilitarianism that gives suffering a large but finite weight. [7]

(b) (i) Set out the continuum (sorites-style) argument against a lexical threshold, and say what the TLN theorist has to accept or give up in order to resist it. [4]
(ii) A defender writes: "unlike classical NU, TLN escapes the Repugnant Conclusion, because the lexical tier blocks the aggregation of arbitrarily small benefits." Assess this claim. Assume that the lives added at the mere-addition step contain no blocking suffering, and that each has a small positive net enjoyment score. [4]

(c) Rank the two outcomes below, justifying the ranking stage by stage. [8] Then: would your ranking change if Beta's episodes were at intensity 5 rather than 6? [2]

Outcome Alpha: 4 patients each have one 90-second episode of blocking suffering at intensity 6. Nobody else in Alpha suffers at all. Total enjoyment over the following fifty years: 12,000 units. Total noise-level suffering: 300 units.
Outcome Beta: 700 patients each have one 90-second episode of blocking suffering at intensity 6. Nobody else in Beta suffers at all. Total enjoyment: 480,000 units. Total noise-level suffering: 20,000 units.

STUDENT'S ANSWER

(a) Classical NU says that only suffering matters, so it is indifferent between an empty world and a world with no suffering and lots of enjoyment — that's where the anti-natalist flavour comes from. TLN differs in two ways: enjoyment still counts as a tiebreaker, and only suffering above the threshold blocks. TLN does keep a weaker version of the empty-world result though, since any world containing blocking suffering still loses to an empty world however much enjoyment it has; the threshold only helps if all the suffering in the world is noise-level. Against a suffering-biased utilitarian: they put pain and pleasure on one scale and simply weight pain more heavily. That is still a difference of degree — there is always some finite amount of enjoyment big enough to outweight any pain. TLN denies that above the threshold: no quantity of enjoyment compensates, not even an enormous amount spread over millions of people. So the disagreement with the weighted view isn't about how much suffering matters, it's about whether the two are on one scale at all.

(b)(i) Build a chain from an agony at intensity 6 down to a barely noticeable discomfort at 1.9, where each adjacent pair differs by less than anyone could notice. If a modest amount of enjoyment can compensate each step down, then adding them up, some finite amount of enjoyment compensates the whole drop — so either there is no threshold at all, or a difference too small to perceive makes an infinite normative difference. To resist this, the TLN theorist can either bite the bullet and say that imperceptible differences can have infinite normative significance (one grain of sand can make a heap), or deny that the small steps add up, i.e. give up transitivity of "not worse than" across the chain. The second is the stronger reply, because it doesn't commit you to a sharp cutoff at some point on the scale that you then can't locate.

(ii) The defender is right. The Repugnant Conclusion works by letting a vast number of tiny positive amounts add up to more than a small number of large ones. TLN blocks that, because aggregation only happens inside the enjoyment tier, and a per-life enjoyment score that is barely above zero doesn't get to aggregate across millions of people the way a straight total does — the threshold sorts it into noise. So the huge population of barely-worth-living lives doesn't beat the small happy one.

(c) Both worlds peak at intensity 6, so stage (1) ties and we move to net enjoyment. Alpha: 12,000 − (3 × 300) = 11,100. Beta: 480,000 − (3 × 20,000) = 420,000. Beta is much better. The episodes last the same in both, but duration isn't supposed to matter at stage (1) anyway, only peak intensity. If Beta's episodes were at intensity 5 instead, Beta would win even more clearly — lower peak at stage (1), and that settles it before you look at anything else.
