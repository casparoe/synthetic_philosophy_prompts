I sit on a hospital ethics committee that is reviewing an assistive memory system for patients with dense anterograde amnesia: a wearable device that continuously captures the patient's day and replays it on cue, instantly available and uncritically trusted, and without which the patient has no accessible record of anything that has happened to them. A colleague argues that this settles our consent question: a blind traveller's cane is, everyone agrees, part of an experienced user's perceptual system, so by parity the device is part of the patient's mind, its contents are the patient's own beliefs, and wiping the device — or letting the patient sign a consent form without first consulting it — is morally on a par with inducing amnesia.

Two complications should be held fixed. First, the system is partly generative: for events it captured only partially it reconstructs plausible detail, and patients endorse those reconstructions as readily as actual replays, so many of its contents were never consciously experienced or endorsed by anyone. Second, unlike biological memory, the store is durable, cross-modal, searchable, and readable, editable and deletable by other people.

Please audit the cane analogy as it is being used here. Give the correspondence table — what plays the role of the tip of the cane, of the skilled user, of skill acquisition, of the cane set down — then separate the disanalogies that matter for the consent conclusion from the ones that are decorative, especially the transmitter-versus-store difference, the unendorsed contents, and third-party editability. Say whether the conclusion about consent and about the moral status of wiping the device survives once those are on the table. Locate the case relative to the standard Otto-and-Inga argument and the conditions Clark and Chalmers set out in their 1998 paper: the device seems to satisfy constant immediate accessibility and automatic endorsement, but not the history of conscious endorsement that Otto's notebook has. If the cane turns out to be the wrong analogy for a durable, partly co-authored store, name a better one and say whether it supports a different conclusion.
