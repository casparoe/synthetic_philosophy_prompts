Hey — I'm on the allocations committee of our county's homeless services board, and we have to vote in eleven days on how to spend a one-time $4.2M state block grant, and I've been asked to circulate a memo before the meeting. The board is basically split: four of us want a Housing First model (no sobriety requirement, no mandatory programming, just get people into permanent units with case management), and four are from a coalition of faith-based providers and neighborhood associations who want abstinence-based transitional housing with their chaplaincy and recovery programming built in. The ninth guy just votes with whoever spoke last, so the memo matters more than it should. The annoying part is that the state application has to name one lead agency and one program model, and the program officer warned us they'd look unfavorably on "parallel tracks," so we probably can't just fund both and call it a day. Complicating it further: the coalition supplies something like 70% of our volunteer hours and owns two of the buildings our shelters run out of, and they've said that if we fund a model that doesn't require sobriety they'll pull the volunteers and one of the leases. Two of our members think that's an illegitimate threat and we should call their bluff on principle; two think that regardless of whether it's a threat, we'd be trying to run this with a third of the staff and nowhere to put anyone.

The thing I keep trying to work out, and the reason I want an actual memo rather than a vote, is whether there's a real trade available here or whether we're just haggling. Here's the idea I've been kicking around: the coalition's objection seems to be partly about outcomes (they genuinely think abstinence-based programs work better and that Housing First "enables" people) but mostly about complicity — they do not want their name, their building, their volunteers, or their donations attached to something they regard as doing harm. The Housing First side doesn't care at all about symbols; they care about how many people are still housed at 24 months and how many ER visits and jail nights we avoid. So I've been proposing what I've been calling "unbundling the symbol from the substance": the county itself is the lead agency and runs the Housing First program under the county's name, no co-branding, no commingled funds, and a formal minuted statement of non-endorsement; the coalition separately gets county money to run an opt-in recovery house of their own that nobody is required to enter; and we commit to keeping the two programs' names off each other's materials. My claim is that this isn't log-rolling and it isn't splitting the difference — it's supposed to be strictly better for both sides than either a straight fight or a 50/50 split, because each side gets more of the thing it actually cares about and gives up the thing it cares about less. A friend of mine (philosophy grad student, insufferable) says this is just moral trading and the real question is whether the complicity objection survives being paid off that way. Another member says any compromise here is itself a statement and we'd be putting the county's name behind a view we think is wrong. I don't know who's right and I need the memo to actually argue it out rather than split the difference between the two arguments, if that makes sense.

Can you draft the memo? It has to be something I can hand to people who are tired and don't read philosophy — plain language, no name-dropping as a substitute for an argument, and it has to end with something we could actually vote on, including what we do if the coalition refuses the deal. Please use these sections, roughly this long: "Bottom line" (~120 words, the actual recommendation: which model, what structure, what happens by when); "What we're really disagreeing about" (~250 words, separating the outcome disagreement from the complicity disagreement, and being honest about how much of each is in play); "Why this beats splitting the difference" (~300 words, the case for the trade I described, including whether the trade logic still works for the people in the coalition who really do believe the outcomes are worse); "The strongest thing on the other side" (~250 words — I want this argued hard, not gestured at, whether it's the threat/legitimacy point or the "compromise is itself a statement" point or something I haven't thought of); "If they say no" (~150 words). Thanks!
