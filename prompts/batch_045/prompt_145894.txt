Reading-group thing — six of us, grad students plus one biologist, working through Hofstadter's Ant Fugue. Aunt Hillary being the ant colony: he talks to her, she has views, she learns things, she can be wrong, and the point of the dialogue as I read it is that her "thoughts" float on the scurrying of ants the way ours float on the firing of neurones, and that you can lose individual ants without losing the thought. I set out to build an argument that she has no mind and ended up with one that eats me as well. Here it is.

(1) Having a mind requires having states with determinate content — beliefs and desires that are about definite things. If there is no fact of the matter about what a system believes, then it has no beliefs, and so no mind.

(2) Content is fixed by causal-functional role: what a state is caused by, what it causes, what it would cause in other circumstances. No determinate role, no determinate content.

(3) Her putative states are realised in the churn: the ants, the trails, who is carrying what, which galleries are being dug. Suppose we could cool a fraction of the colony — they're ectotherms, cooled ants just get slower — so we can dim her continuously. There is no natural stopping point, no stage at which the colony goes from having a belief to lacking one, just a steady degradation of fit between the ascription and the base.

(4) And the realisers compete. Take "Aunt Hillary believes it's going to rain". Several overlapping sub-networks of teh colony — the forager traffic, the nest-repair traffic, whatever the nurses are up to — each fit that ascription about as well as the others, and they will come apart within the hour.

(5) So there is no unique candidate whose role could fix the content, and no non-arbitrary way to select one. Where the base is graded and the candidates compete, the role is indeterminate. That is not the sort of thing a threshold can repair; picking a cut-off point doesn't tell you which of the competing networks is the one that counts.

(6) So by (1) and (2) Aunt Hillary has no beliefs. Talk of her mind is a compact way of summarising ant-level facts and nothing more. Which is the conclusion I wanted.

(7) But run (3)–(5) on a person. Neurones die and are replaced, synapses turn over, no two of us share a realiser for "it'll rain", there is no discrete thing in my head that is that belief either, and the same competition between overlapping neural coalitions seems available. So I have no beliefs. Which I definately don't accept.

(8) So somewhere in (3)–(5) I have broken something that the human case also needs.

Where? I'd most like to keep (1) and (2) — I'm a functionalist and I don't want to hand over role semantics just to get a conclusion about ants. But I can't see how to keep both and still reach (6) without reaching (7). The biologist in the group says the answer is that (3) is simply false: the colony reproduces as a unit and has its own selection history, so it is a subject in a way that a chunk of itself isn't. That sounds like an awful lot of weight for one premise to carry, and I don't know what it commits me to. If I try to escape (4) by saying the whole colony is the subject rather than any sub-network, I also don't know what stops the subject being the colony plus its food trees plus the patch of woodland it sits in.

One more thing I should flag: the argument never mentions consciousness. I left it out on purpose, because putting it in collapses the whole thing into "are ants conscious?", which feels like a different and harder argument. But maybe that omission is exactly where the bug is. Someone else suggested the answer is just that her states have derived rather than original intentionality; I don't see how that helps, since (1) and (2) say nothing about where the content came from.

So: name the step, say why it looked fine, and price the alternatives. If it's valid and the honest move is to give up (2) or to swallow (7), say that — I'd rather hear it than be handed a fake escape hatch. And if the answer is that I've equivocated between two things, I want both of them named precisely.
