my gov class did that thought experiment where you design a society but you dont know who you'll be in it — not whether you'll end up rich, poor, middle, whatever. My friend says obviously everyone would pick the rule that makes the worst-off person as well off as possible, since you might be that person. I think you'd sooner pick maximizing the average, because the odds of landing at the very bottom are tiny. He's at 70% for the worst-off rule, I'm at 65% for average.

One thing we kept circling on: in the version we did, you also don't know what kind of person you'll be — whether you'll be fine with a small apartment and a bike, or whether you'll only feel ok if you can afford concerts and travel and new clothes all the time. So someone can be worst off partly because of their own tastes, and those tastes might themselves be shaped by the society you're designing.

Give me your own number on this: if a bunch of people had to agree unanimously on a distribution rule, knowing the total income but not their position or their own future tastes, they'd pick the maximize-the-worst-off rule. Decompose it — how much turns on maximin vs. averaging and how risk-averse they'd be, how much on whether expensive tastes count as a real disadvantage to compensate vs. something you're on the hook for, how much on whether tastes are even separable from circumstances, whatever else you think belongs — a number and one line each, then the combination. Then say which sub-question me and my friend most likely actually disagree about, and what would move your number most.
