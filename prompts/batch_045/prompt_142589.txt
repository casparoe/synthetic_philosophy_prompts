I'm applying for a one-year dissertation fellowship and need a two-page proposal for the project I would spend the year on. Here is the half-formed idea I want turned into one.

The realism/nominalism dispute about universals is almost always run on examples about classification: many red things, many electrons, and the question whether a single entity is literally present in each of them. Both camps then lean on machinery nobody polices — the realist on a notion of the same property, the resemblance nominalist on a notion of resemblance treated as primitive and qualitative. My hunch is that the debate changes shape if resemblance is made quantitative, and we now have a concrete model of what that means: objects as points in a feature space, similarity as a graded metric. So the question I want to work on: does a quantitative treatment give resemblance-based nominalism what it has always lacked — an answer to the charges that resemblance is cheap because everything resembles everything else in some respect, that many pairwise similarities never compose into a class, and that no such account distinguishes natural from gerrymandered groupings — or does it merely relocate the problem, because the choice of features and of metric already encodes the carving of the world that was supposed to be the output of the theory rather than its input?

The proposal needs: the question stated narrowly enough for one year, with a separation of which of those three charges a metric really answers from which it only restates; why the standard realist critique of similarity leaves the question open, given that it was framed against a purely qualitative notion; a month-by-month method that is not just applied machine learning — reconstruct the objections at full strength, specify what a feature-space representation must supply in order to meet them, test whether the answers come from the metric or from something assumed in setting it up, then ask what ontology the surviving version of the view commits you to; the three live outcomes — realism in disguise, defensible nominalism, or dissolution of the dispute — and which considerations would decide between them; and an honest account of failure conditions and scope. Do not give me a bibliography; I will add citations myself.
