I'm a visiting PhD student, and in three weeks I'm presenting a paper to the Levels and Mechanisms reading group here — about twelve people, ninety minutes on a Friday afternoon, of which the last forty will be questions, and I've been told that this group is unusually aggressive in a friendly way. My paper is about Hofstadter's careenium: a frictionless surface on which vast numbers of tiny, fast, magnetic particles (simms) career about, sometimes sticking into large, heavy, slow clusters (simmballs), whose configurations can be rearranged by events impinging from outside and which thereafter shove one another around at the macro level while the simms go on shoving each other at the micro level. Hofstadter's question is which level is really doing the pushing — whether, in the sense that matters, the symbols push the substrate around. My thesis is that they do, and that this is a metaphysical claim rather than a merely epistemic one: given an interventionist reading of causation, there are interventions on simmball-level variables that make a difference to later simmball-level variables in a way no intervention describable at the simm level captures, and that difference-making is robust across a wide range of micro-realizations of the same simmball configuration. I concede from the start that the careenium's microphysics is deterministic and causally closed; my claim is that the exclusion argument against downward causation buys its conclusion only by switching to a production-or-process notion of causation, and that once you hold the interventionist notion fixed the simm-level story is causally sufficient but not causally exhaustive in the sense that matters. Late in the paper I try to head off two cases of my own. The first is what I call the inverted careenium, where I reverse the parameters — light, fast simmballs and heavy, slow simms — and the intuitive arrow of causation seems to flip, which is the objection I most fear, since it suggests my whole result is an artifact of Hofstadter's chosen mass and velocity ratios. The second is the leaky simmball, a cluster whose constituent simms are continually exchanged with the surrounding medium so that it retains its shape while lacking any microphysical identity over time; I use it to argue that persistence of the macro pattern, not of the parts, is what macro-causation needs.

The audience is roughly this: a senior physicalist who works in the causal-exclusion tradition and thinks overdetermination is the fatal word; two younger members who work on mechanistic explanation and will want to know whether I've shown causal relevance or merely constitutive relevance, and who will press me with mutual-manipulability tests; a philosopher of physics who will say that levels here are just scales and that everything I'm calling downward causation is coarse-graining plus a low-entropy boundary condition, and who may raise the time-asymmetry of my counterfactuals; a computer scientist from the ML group who will ask whether any of this licenses substrate-independence for artificial systems and whether symbol status is observer-relative; and one member who thinks the whole setup smuggles in intentionality because simmballs mean something only to us. Assume I know who's who by name and can address them individually.
