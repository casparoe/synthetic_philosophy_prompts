ok so I'm a senior and I'm the student rep on our school's honor board — basically when someone gets written up for something the rulebook is fuzzy about, we sit with the assistant principal and vote on whether it counts as a violation or not. It's usually boring (dress code stuff, backpacks) but this week there's an actual case and I genuinely don't know what I think, and I'd rather not just vote on vibes.

Two years ago a fight broke out in the east stairwell and like four kids filmed it and it was on snapchat before second period even ended, and it got ugly — parents calling the school, local news, the whole thing. So the next year the handbook got a new rule, and the wording is: no student may operate a device capable of recording video anywhere on school grounds during school hours. Everyone knew what that was about. The case now is a sophomore, I'll call her M, who has some vision thing — she sits in front and uses her phone camera as a magnifier, points it at the board or at a handout and reads it off the screen, and sometimes screenshots it so she can zoom in at home. She's not filming anybody. A hall monitor saw a phone pointed at something and wrote her up anyway. Her mom is appealing and her argument is basically: obviously that rule is about kids filming fights, not about a kid using her phone to read the board. Our AP's response was that a rule means what it says, and if we start asking what people were trying to do when they wrote it, we'll be there until midnight on every case. (Side detail that came up last meeting: when the rule was announced at assembly the principal said something like "this is about fights, not about you texting your grandma," and the AP says that doesn't matter, the handbook is the handbook and the assembly isn't the handbook.)

The thing is, I'm kinda on M's side but I also don't love how easy the "but what was it actually for" move is. My friend Ari, who does debate with me, pointed out that you can always describe the point of a rule at whatever level of vagueness gets you the answer you want: the point was "stop kids filming fights," but you could equally say it was "stop fight videos going viral and making the school look bad" or "stop recording people who didn't agree to be recorded" or "keep the school from getting sued," and each of those gives you a different answer here and none of them feels more official than the others. And there's a second wrinkle — the rule was written before anyone at my school had heard of the meta ray-ban glasses that half the junior class wears now, so whatever the people who wrote it "meant," they definitely weren't thinking about glasses with a camera in them. So: what's the actual disagreement between "go by the words" and "go by the point," in a case like this? Is there a version of the purpose thing that answers Ari, or is Ari basically right and that's why people fall back on the text? Like I said I'll figure out my own vote, I just want to know what the real argument is on each side and where each one breaks, and I'd rather you didn't just tell me "it depends" lol
