Long time reader of the rationalist blogs, first time asking something like this. I've read GEB twice and a decent chunk of the Metamagical Themas essays, and I still can't tell whether the strange-loop stuff is load-bearing or just very pretty — which is probably why I want fiction about it rather than another argument.

Write me a short story, 800 to 1000 words, about a tangled hierarchy. Not an illustration of Escher's Drawing Hands — I know the picture — but something with the same shape: two or more levels each apparently producing the other, with no way to say which is really on top, and one Hofstadter-ish level that stays still while everything else turns.

The bit I actually want you to chew on is a distinction I've not seen anyone make, and I half suspect its doing real work. Call a strange loop a loop: you climb levels, you come back to where you started, same level, closure, the hierarchy is tangled but it closes. Call a strange spiral the thing that looks exactly like that from the inside but comes back round one turn up — a helix rather than a circle, so each recursion drifts, the rules on the second pass are subtly not the rules from the first, and nobody ever sat down and rewrote them. My sense is that most of the famous cases (Gödel sentences, the hands, self-reproducing programs) get described as loops when at least some of them are spirals, and that the interesting question is which. I'd like the story to know the difference without any character naming it.

Constraints, sorry, there are a few. Set it somewhere with a crunchy rulebook and a paper trail — a benefits office, a cathedral records room, a kitchen brigade, a compiler's passes, a call-centre QA queue, whatever you like — and definitely not in a dream, a simulation, a VR rig or a nested-fiction reveal; no character reading a story that turns out to be this story. No winking at the reader either, no 'you, reading this': leave the reader's level alone, so if the story needs an inviolate level it has to build one inside the fiction and keep it inviolate there. And keep it visibly inviolate rather than merely unvisited — I want to be able to tell that a rule can't be broken, not just that nobody in the story has happened to break it yet. If anyone has to talk about the ideas, have them get it slightly wrong. End with the thing still turning, please, not with an explanation.

Then, no heading, just a paragraph or two straight afterwards as if you were emailing me: where you cheated to make it narratable, whether your inviolate level is really inviolate or just unvisited, how confident you are in the loop-versus-spiral distinction as you've used it, and the one claim you'd retract first.

Oh — no headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables: continuous prose the whole way through, the way you'd write an email. British spellings if you wouldn't mind.
