Nadia thinks we've been individuating the conditions wrong and wants to index them to the subject's evidence rather than to the target proposition — call it evidence-anchored sensitivity: very roughly, S knows p when, in the nearest worlds in which S has the evidence she actually has and bases her belief on it in the way she actually does, p holds. Owen is the purist: he thinks the whole point is that the condition indexes to the proposition itself, that the closure failures are a prediction rather than a bug, and that Nadia's fix is either vacuous or quietly question-begging.

The thing I really want them to chew on — and please have them actually chew on it rather than gesturing at it — is that Nadia's move seems to turn entirely on whether evidence is factive. If the evidence she "has" already entails p, then the nearest evidence-worlds are all p-worlds and closure falls out for free; but then the modal condition looks like it's doing no work at all and she's just got an evidentialist theory wearing a subjunctive hat. And if evidence isn't factive, then the hallucination worlds have exactly the same evidence and the closure failure comes straight back. I also want Owen to have a proper go at her about which evidence is doing the anchoring — everything she knows, or just what she's actually relying on in this bit of reasoning — because I think that's where the fix either collapses or turns back into the old method-gerrymandering worry, and I'd like Nadia to have a real reply to that rather than just changing the subject.
