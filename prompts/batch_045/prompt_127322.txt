I work in sequential decision making. My working vocabulary is MDPs and POMDPs, policies versus plans, value functions and Bellman operators and their fixed points, discounted and average-reward objectives, regret and exploitability in online and bandit settings, constrained and multi-objective MDPs, the split between planning-time computation and execution-time behavior, options and other temporally extended actions, and the strand of work on non-exponential discounting where an agent's future selves are modeled as other agents in an intrapersonal game. I have no formal philosophy training.

I keep running into the argument that an agent with intransitive, or otherwise non-expected-utility, preferences can be money pumped: an environment can be arranged so that the agent pays to move from A to B, pays to move from B to C, and pays to move from C back to A, ending where it began but poorer, and the argument is then generalized into the claim that such preferences are irrational. Alongside it there is a debate about whether rationality imposes diachronic requirements at all — requirements on how a sequence of choices over time hangs together — or whether every genuine requirement is synchronic, a requirement on the choice you face now, with the appearance of diachronic norms produced by something else. The two familiar planning styles in that debate are the sophisticated one, which plans by backward induction through its own anticipated future choices, and the resolute one, which forms a plan and then executes parts of it that, at the moment of execution, it would not re-endorse.

Restate that whole cluster in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report.

Concretely, I want each of the following rendered in RL terms: the money pump itself as an object I can build (a state cycle with negative reward along the edges, offered to a policy with particular local preferences); the dominance or exploitability claim the pump is supposed to establish, and the distinction between being exploitable by an adversary and failing to maximize expected utility, since my sense is that the pump only directly establishes the former; the thesis that rationality is diachronic versus the objection that it is really synchronic requirements plus information about one's own future behavior; sophisticated versus resolute planning; and the apparent cost of commitment, given that in my framework deleting actions from your own action set can never raise the optimal value of the start state.

To make the translation concrete, translate them against this architecture, which is the one I actually build: a planner that emits a full plan, can install action masks on the deployed executor, can mark a mask revocable or irrevocable at some cost, and can either simulate its own later re-planning as part of the environment or ignore that possibility; an executor that either runs the plan as given or re-plans greedily at each step.
