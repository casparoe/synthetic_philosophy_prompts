Me and a couple of friends have this dumb little game we play at the park every few weeks: one of us hides a token somewhere on a printed map that's a 2x3 grid (colums West / Middle / East, rows North / South, six squares total), and the hider texts the rest of us hints while we wander around with our phones. I'm usually the one writing the hints, and I've gotten weirdly obsessed with what a hint that's an *if-then* is supposed to do to your beliefs. Someone on a forum once told me there's some famous puzzle in this neighborhood called the Judy Benjamin problem, but I could never find a version of it written for normal people, so I've just been inventing my own cases. Assume unless I say otherwise that all six squares start out equally likely and the hint is completely trustworthy. Here's what my gut says: (1) "If it's in the West column, it's in the north square" — I feel this should leave the West column at 1/3 overall and just move the West-south third up onto West-north, so West-north is now 1/3 and nothing outside the West column budges; the hint doesn't tell me *whether* it's in the West column, only where inside it. (2) Same sentence, except I've already searched West-north myself and it's empty — now I feel the hint means it's definately not in the West column at all, and the other four squares split the difference evenly. (3) "If it's in the West or Middle column, it's in the north row" — I feel each of those two columns should keep its own 1/3, i.e. the mass in the south squares moves straight up inside its own column rather than being smeared across both north squares. (4) Two different friends text me, one saying "if it's in the West column it's in the north square" and the other "if it's in the West column it's in the south square," both reliable — I conclude it's not in the West column, and I don't think I've learned anything that separates the other four squares from each other. (5) Sometimes the hider texts "either it's not in the West column, or it's in the north square" instead of the conditional in (1). That rules out exactly the same single square, but it *feels* different to me: the either-or phrasing makes me want to lower my P(West column), the if-then phrasing doesn't. I half think that's just a glitch in my head and half think it's telling me something real. (6) Then there's Dave, who is right about this kind of thing about four times out of five. When Dave says "if West then north" I want to move most of the West-south probability onto West-north but not all of it — except I realize I have no idea what "80% reliable" even means for a conditional, or whether the leftover bit should sit on West-south or leak out of the West column entirely.

The one I keep flip-flopping on: suppose West-north is a hiding spot the hider loves, so I'd have put 50% on it and 10% on each of the other five, and the hint is "if it's in the West column, it's in the south square." Part of me says the West column cannot still be sitting at 60% when the only West square left standing was a 10% square; another part says the hint only speaks about what's inside the West column, so West should stay at 60% and West-south should soak up the whole thing. I go back and forth every time I think about it, which makes me suspect (1) and (2) can't both be right for the reason I think they are.

So: is there one rule for updating on a conditional that gets all of this, or nearly all of it? I'd like it stated precisely enough that I could actually code it up (I know a bit of Python), with a note on which of my cases each part of the rule is there to answer, and an honest list of the verdicts it can't give me. If there's no single rule and my verdicts are really answers to two different questions, say that instead and say what the two questions are. Also tell me what your rule says about a couple of cases I haven't listed — say, "if it's not in the East column, it's in the north row", or the two hints from (4) arriving in the opposite order (I assume order shouldn't matter, but I'm not sure my instincts respect that). And please don't just name a theory at me and stop; I want the rule and the reasoning, not a reading list, since I can't get at journals anyway.
