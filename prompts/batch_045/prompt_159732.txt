I'm a retired experimental physicist — thirty-odd years at the bench, and since I packed it in I've been poking at philosophy, mostly the Everett stuff. The maths I'm fine with. What I cant get straight is probability: I've tried writing down my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases to see whether there's a rule behind them, and its not coming out clean.

1. Equal-amplitude two-way split (silver atoms through a Stern-Gerlach): 1/2 each. Fine.
2. Three-way split, all amplitudes equal: 1/3 each. Fine.
3. Amplitudes whose squared moduli are 0.2 and 0.8: 0.2 and 0.8, not 1/2 and 1/2. So its not branch counting.
4. Continuum of outcomes (when a nucleus decays): go by the amplitude-squared density; counting is meaningless here. Fine.
5. Equal-amplitude split, except the down branch then gets shredded by the environment into a million sub-branches that all look identical from the inside: still 1/2. I'm fairly confident on this one, because I dont even think the number of branches is well defined.
6. Equal-amplitude split, except a machine scans me and rebuilds two copies of me on the down branch and one on the up branch, all three awake and wondering which they are: my gut says 2/3 that I'll find myself down. Which looks flatly inconsistent with 5.
7. Equal-amplitude split where the down branch kills me instantly and the up branch runs another forty years: still 1/2, no temptation to weight by how much life there is in a branch. But then why did I feel any pull in 6?
8. A split that gets cleanly reversed before anything irreversible happens: I want to say there's no probability question at all, rather than 1/2.

My tentative rule is just the Born rule and nothing else: square the amplitudes and ignore everything downstream — how many branches there are, how many copies of me, how long they last, whether the split gets undone later. Test it against my verdicts, tell me where it breaks and what clause you'd have to add to fix it, and be straight about which verdicts I'd have to give up. Also: is this a rule about credence, or about how much the branches matter, and what does your repaired version say about a case I havent listed — one where I dont know the amplitudes, or where two branches merge back into one?
