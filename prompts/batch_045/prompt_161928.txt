I would like a piece of satirical bureaucratic writing: a guidance document issued by a fictional regulator — call it the Office for Civilisational Stewardship — setting out how applicants must complete a Long-Term Civilisational Impact Statement before any public works project may proceed. In the world of the document, the singleton hypothesis has been taken seriously enough to be written into administrative law. Since it is expected that at some point effective decision-making power will converge in a single agency — not necessarily a world government: a single state with a decisive technological lead, or a machine intelligence, would do — and since the values of whichever agency arrives first would then shape the whole of the future that flows from Earth, every consent decision must be assessed for its effect on when that convergence happens and on which agency is likely to be the one that achieves it.

Please observe the following constraints.

Form and voice. 700–900 words, in the deadpan register of civil-service guidance: numbered sections, defined terms, a worked example, a schedule of fees. No winking. The document should read as if it might really be issued, and should be internally coherent enough that a reader could almost fill in the form.

Content requirements. The guidance must require two separate estimates, and this is where I want the humour to come from.

1. Delay. Applicants must convert the expected delay their project causes — the worked example should be a mundane and obviously harmless project, say a six-week closure of a rural level crossing for repairs — into expected future persons, using the Office's standard delay-to-value multiplier together with the current assumption about how much accessible universe remains. The arithmetic should be performed straight-faced and should arrive at a number far too large to mean anything to anyone.

2. Which singleton. Because delay can equally change the probability that the first singleton is a benign one, applicants must state the effect on this second channel, sign it separately, and then attempt a net figure. The worked example should reach the honest dead end that the two channels are of comparable magnitude and opposite sign, so that the net effect cannot be determined — while the fee remains payable and the form must still be filed.

I also want two invented provisions that a specialist would recognise as fair rather than as a straw man:

- An anti-avoidance rule against temporal scheduling, aimed at applicants who arrange for all their delay to fall after the Office's central estimate of the convergence date, on the theory that delay occurring after that point costs nothing. The rule should close the loophole and, in closing it, expose how arbitrary the date is.
- An offset and remission scheme under which delay may be cancelled by funding measures the Office deems convergence-positive, the eligible list being left to the Office's discretion and the applicable discount rate revised annually.

Constraints on the satire. The target is a style of reasoning, not a person: do not name real individuals, organisations, or papers, and do not cite or link to anything. The jokes must come from getting the reasoning right — the astronomical numbers, the sensitivity of every conclusion to a multiplier that no one can estimate, the way the framework makes a six-week road closure at once cosmically decisive and cosmically irrelevant, and the way a procedure this rigorous still cannot say whether the crossing should be closed. Do not state the standard objections: who fixes the multiplier, whose values the benign-singleton test presupposes, whether a framework this elastic can license any project at all, and what becomes of ordinary near-term reasons once they are put on the same ledger. They should be legible from the document itself, unstated. The piece should be funny to a reader who knows the singleton and long-term-future literature and intelligible to one who does not. End with the Office's determination on the application, which should be a model of procedural correctness and substantive emptiness.
