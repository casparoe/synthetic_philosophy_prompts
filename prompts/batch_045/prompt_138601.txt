Draft the joint statement that a constitutive micropsychist and a cosmopsychist (a priority-monist panpsychist) could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the combination problem. Both grant that consciousness is the intrinsic nature of the physical, that physics describes only structure and dynamics, and that neither brute emergence nor eliminativism is available to either of them: the dispute is about the direction of grounding between the experience of the cosmos and ours.

Format it as a numbered section of claims both can sign, including what any solution to the combination problem would have to deliver and how the three aspects of the problem hang together, namely how many subjects there are, how a rich human phenomenology gets built from impoverished ingredients, and which structural features of the brain match which features of experience. Then a table of the residual disagreements, each stated in the formulation its own side would accept. Then the cruxes.

Both sides have agreed in advance to treat the following constructed case as diagnostic. Call it the Bridge Series: a technique can splice N nerve fibres between two adult human brains, with N increasing continuously from zero up to the fibre count of a corpus callosum. Both accept that there is no principled discontinuity anywhere in the series, that there are two subjects at the bottom end and one at the top, and that a completed neuroscience would describe every stage of it in purely structural terms. Neither may escape by saying that the number of subjects is simply vague all the way along, unless it also says what makes that number determinate in the ordinary one-brain and two-brain cases.

For each crux, I expect one to be whether the intrinsic nature of a neural state is fixed by that state's physical description alone or only by its physical description together with its place within a larger subject, state what each side would count as evidence or argument against its own view, and record each side's prediction in advance, including its predicted profile of subjects across the Bridge Series if the number of subjects ever became measurable. If you conclude that some part of the dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why.
