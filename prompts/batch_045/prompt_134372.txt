ok, I keep seeing international corporate tax competition used as the textbook Moloch case — every country would be better off if rates stayed up, each one individually gains by shaving a point off, so they grind each other down, and then in 2021 a big batch of countries signed onto a 15% floor through the OECD. I want that episode run through the multipolar-trap lense properly, not the usual tragedy-of-the-commons telling.

Two things I want you to actually apply rather than assume. First, my own distinction: surplus destruction vs surplus transfer. In a clean Moloch trap the thing being competed for gets burned in the competing (ad load, arms spending, positional effort). In tax competition the surplus mostly doesn't burn, it moves — out of public revenue and to owners of mobile capital. So is this a trap at all, or a distributional fight wearing trap language? And how much of your answer rests on the empirical question of how much of the mobility is real investment vs pure profit shifting?

Second, check which of three conditions hold: the equilibrium is worse for nearly all players than some feasible alternative; unilateral defection is reliably rewarded; and there's a selection mechanism that removes the players who refuse (capital flight, exit, firms dying) rather than just penalising them. I suspect it passes the middle one cleanly and the other two messily — tell me if I'm wrong.

Then the bits I actually care about:

- Don't skip the defense of tax competition as a discipline on government (the Leviathan / efficiency line). Moloch analyses usually assume it away — does the framing have to, or can it live with it?
- The floor isn't a world government. As far as I get it, it partly works by letting a country collect the top-up on its own companies itself instead of losing it to someone else, which looks like flipping the defection incentive rather than suppressing it. Structural fix or relabelling?
- Is the race re-routing into subsidies, credits and carve-outs an escape, or the same trap in a new costume?
- What observable thing, ten years out, would tell you the trap was genuinely escaped?

Be committed: give me a verdict — confirmation of the Moloch framing, a documented escape, or a case where the framing is mostly doing rhetorical work — and flag which claims you'd defend hard and which you'd fold on immediately.
