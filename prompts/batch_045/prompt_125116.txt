Not a philosophy guy, I write backend systems. There's a shape of failure I keep running into in my own code, and the famous digger wasp story looks like the same shape to me, and I want to know whether this thing already has a name.

The story as I half remember it: the wasp paralyses some prey, dumps it at the nest entrance, goes inside to inspect the burrow, comes back out and drags the prey in. If you move the prey a few inches while she's in there, she comes out, hauls it back to the entrance, and goes in to inspect again, and you can apparently keep doing that to her. The lesson people draw is that she's running a fixed hard-wired routine, and that this is what mindless, unfree behavior looks like from the outside.

But I get the same outward pattern in my systems for almost the opposite reason. A deploy worker checks that a resource is free, goes off to fetch something, then acts on the result of that check; if the world changed in the meantime it never notices, and its error handling is "abort and run the whole sequence again", so it loops, and on a screen recording it looks exactly like the wasp. Nothing in it is hard-wired, it's a flexible general-purpose state machine, it just re-validates at the wrong moments and keeps reading a snapshot that went stale. Same with me at home: I walk into the kitchen and loose what I came for, and walking back to where I started usually brings it back — again not a fixed loop, just state that only loads in the right context. And honestly, re-inspecting a burrow you just found disturbed isn't obviously dumb either, so that's a third thing the same video could be showing.

So: is there an established concept for rigidity that comes out of flexibility plus a stale model of the world, as distinct from rigidity that comes out of a genuinely fixed sequence? The word sphexishness seems to cover the hard-wired reading, but what I care about is the diagnosis, because the two look identical on video while the fix is different — in one case you add flexibility, in the other you fix when you re-check. Does that distinction have a name of its own, how does it sit relative to sphexishness and to things like habits or action slips, and does anyone actually use it in the free will argument where someone says we ourselves might be rigid in ways we can't see from the inside? I dont just want a label, I want to know where the line between these cases really falls and what counts as being on each side of it.
