I've been in a small scene around a regional style of hand-thrown pottery for about fifteen years — I'll call it the Blackwick jug, I don't want to name the real thing. The name comes from one workshop in one valley; the shape came out of how the jugs were fired and what they were used for, nobody wrote a rule down, people just learned by handling them.

Here's the pattern I can't find a word for. Newcomers, and now most sellers, pick out a Blackwick by the shape — fine, it's a decent guide. But over twenty years I've watched "Blackwick" come to mean the shape: a potter defending a slab-built jug, thrown nowhere near the valley, glazed in a way that wasnt possible then, on the grounds that it has the right shoulder. Meanwhile the people who learned by handling jugs will now pass something that looks a bit off if it came out of the right workshop, and reject a dead ringer that didn't. So the word runs on two things at once — a visible test anyone can apply, and an inherited list of examples nobody will overrule — and when they conflict the examples win, nobody says so out loud, and nobody has ever been caught being wrong.

The part that bugs me: whenever an outside body tries to fix it — a museum catalogue, a fair's judging rules, a marketplace listing policy — the definition always comes out as the shape, because the shape is the only checkable bit, and then the old jugs that don't match get re-described as early, provincial, atypical, and in five years people remember them that way. The codification doesn't just fail to capture the concept, it edits the examples holding the concept up.

Is there a name for this? It's not concept creep — the extension barely moves. Not prototypes or family resemblance either, the fights here aren't about borderline cases. Someone pointed me at the division of linguistic labor, experts holding the real nature and everyone else going by a stereotype, but that has the experts as the ones who are right, and here they just have a different test that happens to agree on every case anyone can point at. And I don't want a bare label: I want what this thing is, what it isn't, and where its edges are.

Give me: "What already exists" (~200 words, the two or three nearest named things, described honestly), "Why each one misses" (~250, including where my case really does overlap), "The name and the definition" (~150), "Cases and non-cases" (~200, with at least one example from a completely different domain and at least two things that look similar but shouldn't count).
