Me and a mate have been going round on the statue and the clay thing, and he's built an argument I can't find the hole in, but I'm sure there's one because look where it ends up.

1. The statue and the lump of clay it's made out of are, right now, the same matter in the same shape in the same place.
2. Any plain present-tense fact about one is a fact about the other: same weight, same colour, same chip on the left knee, same pressure on the plinth. Nothing you could point at or measure right now tells them apart.
3. Two things can't differ only in what would happen to them somewhere else. If two things are alike in every way they actually are right now, there's one thing there, and "the statue" and "the lump" are just two ways of talking about it.
4. But the two ways of talking say opposite things. Roll it into a ball and the clay is still there while the statue is gone. And the clay was dug out of the riverbank in March, while the statue wasn't made until April.
5. One thing can't both survive and not survive the same rolling, and can't both have been around in March and not have been. So one of the two descriptions isn't latching onto anything real.
6. It's "statue" that drops out: whether something is a statue seems to depend on what people think — intentions, art history, what someone paid for it — and whether a thing exists can't depend on that. Where the lump stops is just where the matter stops.
7. So there's no statue, only clay in a statue-shaped arrangement. And the same steps kill the mug (only ceramic), the cat (only animal-stuff), and me (only a body) — and if you run 6 again on the lump, since where matter "stops" is its own judgement call about dust and glaze and stray atoms at the edge, the lump goes too and there are no ordinary objects at all.

I'm not buying 7. My mug exists. But I can't say which step is rotten. He says the only way out is to admit the statue and the clay really are two things, which kills 3 — but then I don't know what makes them two, and someone else on the thread said that if they're two then so are the statue and the artwork (which could of been cast in bronze instead), and the statue and the ornament, and the thing that sits there until somebody melts it, all piled up in the same spot, which sounds worse than where we started. I've also got a feeling something slippery is going on in step 4, like a couple of the words are doing more than one job, but I can't pin it down.

Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost me? I'd rather keep that the statue was made in April and that my mug is real than keep the neatness of step 3, but I want to know what I'm paying. And please price the other ways out too — the "they're one thing after all" one, the "one constitutes the other" one, and the "no statues" one — in plain English, without the jargon.
