I spent most of last night arguing with a friend after a department colloquium on no-collapse interpretations, and I want to cash the disagreement out as numbers rather than slogans, because we kept talking past each other and I suspect the real dispute is somewhere other than where we were aiming. So here is the proposition I want your credence on: conditional on branching, no-collapse quantum mechanics being literally true as a description of our world, the Born rule is derivable from the rest of the theory — the squared-amplitude weights get recovered from the dynamics, the branching structure, constraints on rational agency, or whatever else — rather than being appended to the theory as an extra postulate, a pragmatic gloss, or a piece of normative bookkeeping about what agents ought to expect. Give me one headline number for that, and then give me the decomposition that produces it: the sub-questions it depends on, a number and one line of justification for each, and enough arithmetic that I can see how they combine. Please don't spend your words on a literature survey or a reading list; I know the neighbourhood, I want the reasoning.

The terms I want separated, plus whatever you think I've left out: (1) that the formalism even supplies the materials a derivation needs — whether there is a well-defined, non-arbitrary decomposition of the universal state into branches with well-defined multiplicities, given how approximate and context-dependent decoherence is and how many incompatible factorisations the same state admits; (2) that the decision-theoretic route, in the Deutsch–Wallace vein, actually delivers — how much you discount it for yielding only a consistency constraint on the attitudes of an agent who knows she will branch, rather than something that could make a physical claim true or false, or for quietly presupposing amplitude-weighted judgments in the set-up; (3) that the self-locating-uncertainty and separability line, associated with Carroll and Sebens, delivers — specifically, your credence that the principle doing the work there is independent of the conclusion rather than a restatement of it; (4) that symmetry-based derivations in the Zurek envariance style, or anything else you know of, run without smuggling in what was to be shown; and (5) that the right response is dissolution rather than solution — that nothing in the multiverse is objectively chancy, that probability talk is vindicated only from inside a branch or indexically, and your credence that this counts as success on the proposition as I've formulated it rather than as changing the subject.

Then there is the wrinkle my friend sprang on me around midnight and that I have not been able to shake, which I want as its own sub-question with its own number and its own weight in the combination. Call it the granularity worry: Born weights are continuum-valued and can be irrational, while branches, on any story that counts them, look like discrete integer-valued things. So a derivation that proceeds by counting branches, or by taking a limit of branch ratios, must either posit unmeasurably many branches per outcome, or recover the Born rule only approximately, or else be using branch count as a label for something that was really amplitude all along. He says this shows the counting strategy is either committed to an absurd ontology or circular; I said a limit construction is fine because the theory was never committed to a finite branch number in the first place. Give me your credence that the granularity worry is a genuine obstacle to the best available derivations rather than a red herring, and say how much your answer to it moves the headline number. While you are at it, tell me how much of your headline number rides on whether the job is to deliver objective chances versus merely rational credences for branching agents, since I think that is where my friend and I actually parted.

To finish: name the single term you are least confident in and say what one piece of evidence or argument — a theorem, a thought experiment, an empirical result about decoherence or cosmology — would move the headline number the most, and in which direction. For reference, I was sitting near 0.75 and he was near 0.25; tell me which of these sub-questions two people at those numbers are most likely disagreeing about, and why. And to keep us honest about what is being predicted, add a second, separate number for: by 2050, most people working on foundations will treat some one derivation as successful — since that could come apart from whether any of them is correct.
