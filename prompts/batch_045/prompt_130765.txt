For a long piece I'm writing about how institutions lose their shared factual footing — county election offices, hospital ethics committees, a university department that split over a plagiarism finding — I've been working through the literature on hinge commitments: the idea, from Wittgenstein's posthumous notebooks on certainty, that some things are held fast not because they have been established by evidence but because everything else we do with evidence presupposes them. I keep hitting the same frustration. Every account I meet seems tuned to one or two pet examples — someone waving their hands about, the claim that the earth existed long before one was born, the thought that nobody has been to the moon — and I can't tell whether the accounts genuinely disagree about the phenomenon or are just describing different corners of it. A physicist friend, otherwise patient with me, said over dinner that all of this is just Bayesian priors with the updating rate set to zero and that the real work is being done elsewhere; I would like to be able to give him a better answer than that it is subtler than that. So, before I commit to any one account in print, I want the specification.

Write me the desiderata that any adequate account of hinge commitments has to meet: the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must respect rather than collapse, the things it must be consistent with — with the case or consideration behind each requirement, not just the requirement itself. Fix the purpose as mine: I need a notion I can actually apply in reporting, one that lets me look at a live dispute and say whether what is breaking down there is a framework commitment or merely a contested empirical claim that has been elevated to framework status. (I have started calling the second thing hinge laundering, and I want to know whether a good theory would even need that category or whether its own machinery already handles the case.) Among the cases I want the list to be tested against: hinges at three different scales, from one's own body and history, through institutional ones like a laboratory's calibration standards or a court's chain of custody, to civilizational ones that nobody alive chose; hinge drift, where a commitment thins out across generations without anyone deciding anything or any generation being irrational; hinge collapse, as in an adult who learns that her childhood memories were implanted and loses the certainty that she has a continuous past; and the laundering case, where an empirical claim is insulated from evidence by being declared framework-level. I also want the list to say what a hinge is not: not just a very confident belief, not a Moorean certainty, not a prior, not a presupposition in the ordinary linguistic sense, not a Kuhnian paradigm, not a constitutive rule — and to tell me which of those contrasts are load-bearing and which are culture-war positions in the field.

So: mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are bargaining chips for one camp or another; flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — I especially want to know whether the demand that hinges be arational, outside the game of giving and asking for reasons, can coexist with the demand that a theory retain some critical bite, some way of saying that one framework is better or worse than another and that the person who treats a conspiracy as bedrock has gone wrong rather than merely moved to a different language game. Say which single requirement knocks out the most of the existing candidates. Then, briefly — a short paragraph each at most, since the criteria are the part I care about — how the main families of account do against your list: the reading that treats hinges as rules or grammar rather than propositions, the reading that treats them as propositions we hold by a non-evidential entitlement, and the reading that denies they are propositions at all. No bibliography or reading list, please; I will do my own sourcing and I know how these pieces usually get padded. Where a desideratum rests on a technical distinction, give me one sentence on what turns on it, because I will have to translate it for readers. Finally: tell me how confident you are in what you claim, and single out the point you are least sure of — if I quote you in the piece, I would rather quote the uncertainty than have it sanded off.
