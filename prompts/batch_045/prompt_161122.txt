I work in reinforcement learning and sequential decision-making, and I would like to understand a dispute in formal epistemology by seeing how much of it can be said in the vocabulary I actually use: value functions, Bellman operators and their fixed points, one-step and n-step bootstrapping, periodically refreshed target networks, partially observed environments and state aliasing, distribution shift, regret against a comparator class, and the practice of training a model on its own outputs.

The dispute concerns the Reflection principle (van Fraassen's), which as I understand it says roughly this: your present credence in a proposition, conditional on the supposition that your later self will assign that proposition some particular value x, should itself equal x. Three further pieces travel with it. First, a diachronic Dutch book argument, intended to show that an agent who violates the principle can be presented with a package of bets that guarantees a loss however things turn out. Second, a family of counterexamples in which the agent knows in advance that her later self will be drugged, exhausted, misled, or otherwise epistemically degraded, and where reflecting on that later self seems plainly wrong. Third, a set of repairs: restrict the principle to future selves the agent is certain will not be impaired; restrict it to propositions about which she knows she will merely have gained evidence; or weaken the equality to an inequality, so that present credence is required only to lie within some band around expected future credence.

I would like two things.

First, a translation. Restate the principle, the Dutch book argument, the counterexamples, and the main repairs in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can. Say what the conditional-credence constraint becomes when the objects being revised are predictive distributions or value estimates rather than full beliefs; what the sure-loss argument becomes when the benchmark is regret or exploitation by an adversary rather than incoherence among betting prices; and what the degraded-future-self cases become when the analogue is bootstrapping from a target you know to be biased or stale. Where my vocabulary offers several candidate translations, say which is closest and why the others are worse. In particular, give me your view on a resemblance I keep noticing: the Bellman equation already requires my current estimate of a state's value to equal my expectation of my own later estimate of the successor state's value, which looks like Reflection imposed as a fixed-point condition on a bootstrapping estimator. Tell me whether that is the same constraint in other notation, or only a formal analogue with a different normative status.

To make the translation concrete, here is the case I actually care about. An agent is trained by temporal-difference learning against a target network refreshed every k steps. At a step T that the designer knows in advance, two things happen together: the observation channel is corrupted, so states alias and the agent can no longer distinguish situations it previously distinguished, and the agent is fine-tuned for a fixed number of steps on labels produced by its own earlier checkpoint. Between now and T the agent must choose whether to keep bootstrapping off its post-T estimates. Please render this case in both vocabularies and say whether the two frameworks' verdicts coincide. Note that both corruption and degradation look like plain non-stationarity from inside my framework, whereas the epistemological literature distinguishes an impaired future self from a merely better-informed one. Whether my vocabulary can express that distinction, and whether it needs to, is part of what I am asking.

Second, a residue report. List what translated cleanly, what translated only after you quietly altered it, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. I am especially interested in four possible residues, though correct me if I have misdescribed them. (i) The Dutch book argument seems to presuppose a bookie who can make bets contingent on my internal state at a future time, which in my terms is a white-box adversary with access to my weights; my field evaluates policies by regret against a comparator, not by dominance arguments over belief states, and I suspect something is lost in that substitution. (ii) Reflection is addressed to a single agent persisting through time, and I am unsure my framework has a notion of the same agent across a retraining or distillation step at all. (iii) The principle is a synchronic constraint on current credences with diachronic content, and I cannot tell whether that maps onto a consistency condition inside one model or onto something holding between genuinely different models. (iv) The principle quantifies over the agent's own future credence function, so the agent needs a distribution over its own future distributions; I have seen it suggested that unrestricted Reflection generates trouble when the proposition in question concerns those very future credences, and I would like to know whether that is the same difficulty as asking whether iterated self-training has a nontrivial fixed point, and what model collapse suggests about it.

Then give me your assessment of the residue rather than just an inventory. Where the translation fails, is that because my vocabulary lacks something a theory of rational belief needs — a notion of coherence with no decision-theoretic cash value, for instance — or because the philosophical principle was less determinate, or less normative, than its formulations suggest? Does the Bellman resemblance vindicate Reflection, by showing that any estimator that bootstraps must satisfy something structurally identical, or does it deflate it, by showing that the condition is merely a fixed-point property with no authority over what to believe? Please do not force the translation to come out clean: flag explicitly wherever you had to fudge, and mark the places where your answer would have been different had you been willing to smuggle in assumptions I do not hold, such as the assumption that the agent's objective is expected return against a fixed environment.
