my partner and I are about to start an IVF cycle, and the clinic we're with does embryo ranking — they'll score embryos on polygenic stuff (disease risk, predicted height, educational attainment-ish) and there's apparently an optional "wellbeing" composite they can add, which is basically predicted life satisfaction aggregated over a bunch of traits. I have exactly one undergraduate ethics module behind me and a friend who's an actual philosopher who keeps telling me my view on all this collapses, but won't say which bit, so I'm trying to write down what I think before we're sitting in that room and I have to decide in fifteen minutes. Here it is:

(1) I accept something like the procreative beneficence idea — if you're choosing which of several possible children to bring into existence, you have a real moral reason to favour the one you expect to have the best life, not just permission to pick whichever you like, and I don't think that reason is defeated by the fact that nobody is made worse off by your choice (the non-identity point doesn't kill it for me, it just changes what kind of reason it is). (2) But I think the reason has to be about that child's own good: I don't think you may pick the embryo that's a tissue match for a sick older sibling, or the one predicted to be temperamentally easy, or the one who'll be the kid that ends up looking after us when we're old. That looks to me like treating the child as a means. (3) I don't think the disability-rights / expressive-objection style critiques make selection wrong — choosing an embryo that won't have Down syndrome doesn't say anything about how well the lives of people who do have it go — but I take a weaker version seriously: if you can predict that enough people selecting against trait T will make things worse for the people who already have T (fewer services, more hostility, less accommodation), that's a cost I should weigh, and I think in some cases it should flip what I do. (4) I'm fine with selecting for non-medical traits, height or musical ability or whatever. But I completely balk at picking the embryo that tops the wellbeing composite mostly because it's predicted to have modest ambitions and to be very easy to satisfy, and I can't say what's wrong with it — which bothers me, because by my own (1) it looks like the one I'm supposed to choose. (5) I think the therapy/enhancement distinction does no real moral work, and I also think public healthcare should cover this for serious disease and not for enhancement. (6) I think parents should be legally free to select or not select for basically anything — no bans, no mandates — at the same time as holding (1), which sounds like a duty.

So: does this hang together? My guess is the trouble is in (1) against (2), or somewhere in (4), but I honestly don't know which of them I'd give up and I'd rather not find out by picking. Please treat it as an audit: which pairs genuinely conflict, which only look like they do and actually dissolve once you're careful, and for each real one what exactly would have to give and what giving it up would cost me. Rank them by how bad the tension is rather than listing six equally-weighted problems, and if one of them isn't a conflict at all, say why rather than hedging.
