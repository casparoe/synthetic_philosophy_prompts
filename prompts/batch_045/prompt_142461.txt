I want to test a claim I keep running into: that Kripke's rule-following sceptic only gets any traction because the rules we care about (plus and its friends) have infinitely many cases, so that any finite run of behavior can always be fitted by some deviant rule like quus — and that if the practice were finite, and the speaker had actually been through every case in it, the sceptic would have nowhere left to hide. Build me a case that checks whether that's true.

Specs:
- One speaker, one short paragraph, ordinary realistic setting, nothing sci-fi, and please not about numbers.
- The rule's entire domain is a small finite list, and the speaker has already been through every single case on it at least once — nothing is left untested for the deviant rule to lurk in.
- There are two candidate rules: N, the one she intuitively means, and D, the deviant one. D has to be statable in a single short sentence and has to be a rule some real group of people could actually be trained to follow: no quus-style arbitrary clauses, no except-on-Tuesdays gerrymanders, and it shouldn't be visibly more complicated than N.
- The catch: N and D come apart on a case she has already answered, and on that occasion she gave the D answer — confidently, with no independent mark of error beyond the fact that it clashes with the rest of her practice and that she was mildly disrupted (interrupted, bad lighting, an unfamiliar format, whatever). So D fits her actual history strictly better than N does.

After the scenario: say what the sceptic concludes; what a dispositional straight solution can say here; what a non-reductive or primitivist straight solution can say; and what Kripke's own community-based skeptical solution delivers about the verdict. Then name the feature of the case that's doing the work, say what happens to the paradox if you remove it (e.g. remove the disruption, or remove the surrounding community), and say how whoever comes out worst would attack the setup as cheating. Don't just stipulate that she means N — say where that intuition is supposed to come from.

Please answer in American English.
