Reading group on conditionals next week, and I've been asked to circulate where I've landed beforehand so the others can attack it. Here it is:

1. For indicatives: my confidence in "if A, B" should just be P(B|A), and that's basically the whole story. I dont think indicatives have truth conditions at all.
2. Counterfactuals do have truth conditions, roughly nearest-antecedent-world style. And the fact that they flout strengthening of the antecedent and transitivity doesn't worry me — I take that as a discovery about them, not as a reason to think they aren't truth-apt.
3. It's one and the same conditional underneath. Whether you get the indicative or the subjunctive reading is just a question of which worlds the context hands you — what we're taking for granted, versus how things actually are. No extra semantic difference between the two.
4. Or-to-if is in good standing: from "A or B" you can infer "if not-A, B". I use it constantly and don't want to lose it.
5. My favourite case: only Ana and Bo had keycards, and the log shows that one of the two opened the incubator at 2am, but not whose. I'm happy to assert "if Ana didn't contaminate the sample, Bo did", and I want to deny "if Ana hadn't done it, Bo would have". My explanation is that subjunctives require a causal or historical connection running from antecedent to consequent and indicatives don't.
