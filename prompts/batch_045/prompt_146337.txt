Hey — I'm a bioethics PhD student and I've been sitting in on our hospital's organ donation committee (mostly taking minites, occasionally asked for a view). There's one set of practices I can't get a grip on and I'd like your read on what it actually presupposes.

Here's the thing. In donation after circulatory death we've moved to normothermic regional perfusion: after death is declared, the team restarts circulation — heart beating, blood warm and oxygenated, the works — but our protocol says to clamp the vessels to the brain first, so nothing perfuses it. The stated reason is always that reperfusing the brain would be a problem in a way that reperfusing kidneys and liver isn't; restarting the person, or at least violating the dead donor rule, is what's on the line. So: a warm body with a beating heart on the table, and the one organ singled out as must-not-be-revived is the brain. Meanwhile on the brain-death side of the same programme, donors get paralysed and anaesthetised for the retrieval, and families are told at the bedside that she's gone while also being told she might still hear them.

It keeps reminding me of Dennett's "Where Am I?" — the brain in a vat, hooked up by radio to the eyes and ears in a body wandering around somewhere else, and no one able to give him a straight answer about where he is. So: reconstruct the implicit view of persons, or of where a person is, that these practices need. Is the clamping strictly committed to a brain-located person, or only to something thinner like a rule about which bits of a body are protected? Is the set of practices internally consistant? And here's what I really want to know — Dennett's own answer is deflationary, the self as a centre of narrative gravity rather than a thing with a location, and if that's right the clamping rationale looks like it should collapse; so is there any reading on which the practice is defensible and the deflation is also true, or does one of them have to go? Tell me too what the practice merely fits well with versus can't survive without, and what would actually have to change in protocol if we gave up on the location picture altogether.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give me the reasoning — no preamble, no restating the question.
