Hi — I host a short podcast for my theory seminar, and next weeks guest is a philosopher who defends actual intentionalism: what a text means just is what its author actually meant by it, including intentions nobody knew about until the letters or diaries turned up. She also has a recent chapter pushing the view onto texts with no single author — Wikipedia articles, collaboratively written statements, corporate reports — where her line is roughly that meaning tracks the group's joint intention, and when there's no joint intention there isn't a meaning at all, just readers making something of it.

I want 7–8 questions that don't let her just restate that. A few places I'd like her pressed, each with a follow-up aimed at the reply she's most likely to give:

- Her favorite argument, that you can never actually tell apart what the words mean from what the author meant, so there's no difference to describe. If meaning is that hostage to biography, does a diary turning up in 2050 retroactively change what the poem meant in 1850? Was it always about that, even while no reader could have known?
- Authors who mean something and fail: irony so private nobody could ever pick it up, or an intention she couldn't articulate at the time. If she says the author didn't *successfully* mean it, push on whether that quietly imports the reader's competence, i.e. the hypothetical-intentionalist formula she rejects.
- Fiction, where the author isn't asserting anything: does she have to bring in the audience the author was writing for, and if so is meaning now what that audience would have taken her to mean?
- The multi-author chapter: is she really committed to saying a Wikipedia article means nothing? And if she retreats to something like whatever the current editors intend, then the same string of sentences changes meaning with every edit — at which point what work is the author doing?
- Meaning versus significance: if everything we actually value about literature lives on the significance side, why should anyone care about meaning?

End with one question about what could count as evidence against the view.
