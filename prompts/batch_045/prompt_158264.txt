hi — I'm taking a seminar this term on cultural evolution (the Darwinian-theory-of-culture kind, memes and transmission biases and all that) and I keep noticing the same pattern over and over, and I don't think I have a word for it, which is annoying because I want to write my final paper on it.

So, the pattern. Something — a slang word, a way of tying a scarf, a meme format, a band, a coffee order, a hiking route — gets picked up by a small group. It spreads. Then at some point it crosses a threshold and the exact same thing becomes a reason *not* to do it, and the people who were doing it earliest stop doing it and act like they never did. Last year a word my floor used constantly showed up in a mass email from the housing office and it was dead in about four days, and not in a bored-of-it way: people were actively embarrassed, somebody deleted a whole story archive over it. Then two weeks later there were three mutated versions of the same word going around, all of them slightly harder to pull off than the original (weird grammar, only usable in one specific kind of sentence), and everyone insisted the new ones were the real ones and had been all along.

What I think is going on is that what's being copied isn't really the word or the scarf thing, it's a position: not-the-thing-everyone-has. And there's a consequence I can't get out of my head, which is that the variants that survive this cycle seem to get more demanding over time — more effort to learn, easier to do wrong — like the difficulty is load-bearing, because it keeps the number of people who can do it small on purpose. Which would mean the cycle doesn't converge, it just re-seeds at the edges forever.

I know there's stuff in biology about rare types having an advantage, I think there's something about signaling costs, and in our readings there was that argument about whether culture is copied or whether people independently re-derive the attractive versions. None of those feel like they're pointing at this though: the rare-advantage thing doesn't explain why rarity has to be advertised by the content itself, the cost thing doesn't explain why people retroactively disown it, and the copy-versus-attraction thing doesn't explain why the content keeps moving in a direction.

So: does this have a name, or am I describing three separate things that already have names separatly? I don't just want a label — I want to know what exactly the phenomenon is, what the nearest established concepts are and precisely where each one stops short of it, and which fits best. I also want boundary cases pinned down: things that are rare for accidental reasons, or things people take up late and completely sincerely, shouldn't count, and I want to know why. And one bigger thing I can't settle: if a variant's fitness depends on its own frequency and its content is partly just "not that one", is that still a replicator in the sense the meme people want, or does this kind of variant break that framing?
