I spent my career as an earth-system scientist inferring quantities I could not observe directly from proxies I could, so the feature of this debate that strikes me as genuinely philosophical is this: much of what gets labelled collapse is a change in the proxy record — palace archives, monumental building, long-distance trade, fine wares, literacy itself — rather than a measured change in the welfare of the people who lived through it. Please build the piece on that gap.

Deliverable: roughly 600 words, as an in-universe public information bulletin issued by the Bureau of Continuity of a small Late Bronze Age port city, several years after its palace economy has stopped functioning and its archives have stopped being written. Addressed to residents and remaining trade partners, it must explain that nothing here has collapsed; the city has merely entered a phase of managed reduction in administrative complexity and is now leaner, more resilient, and better aligned with local carrying capacity.
