i'm putting together a short talk on the Orwellian vs Stalinesque business for a grad reading group, and i want to open with an actual joke instead of a slide of Dennett looking smug. Refresher in case its useful: color phi — two coloured dots flashed in succession a few degrees apart, a few hundred ms apart; you see a single dot travel from the first position to the second, and it looks like it changes colour partway along the trajectory, at a point the dot reaches *before* the second flash has even happened. So the system seems to have committed to the change before the information about the new colour was in. Orwellian story: you did have some experience of the dot, and then your memory of it got quietly rewritten once the second colour arrived. Stalinesque story: there was never a conscious experience of the pre-change dot at all; the whole moving-and-changing item was assembled first and presented as a fait accompli. And the Dennett-ish move (roughly, I know it's contested) is that there's no principled way to pick between them, because both need a finish line where content becomes conscious, and the joke is on anyone who thinks there is one.

Here's my own wrinkle, and I want the joke to use it. I think there's a third option that isn't on the usual menu: the experience of the original colour happens, then it gets deleted from the record and nothing is put in its place — no rewritten memory, just an absence — and yet the subject reports smooth continuous motion with a colour change, because the report is generated from a stream that never contained the original in the first place. I've been calling it Nixonesque revision (the tape exists, it's just missing eighteen minutes).

So: give me the joke. Constraints:

- The punchline has to turn on the structural difference between the options — what gets revised, when, and relative to what — not on "brains are weird lol" or "philosophers will argue about anything." If I can imagine the same joke being told about umpires, it's not the joke I want.
- Tellable in one breath, no whiteboard required.
- It should land for someone who's actually read the chapter and just mystify everyone else. Polite chuckles from the uninitiated count as failure.
- If my Nixonesque thing collapses into one of the other two, say which and why in one sentence, and then write the joke about the real two-option dispute instead. I'd rather have a good joke about the actual distinction than a cute one about my own invention.
- Phi puns allowed only if they're genuinely good, which they probably arn't.
