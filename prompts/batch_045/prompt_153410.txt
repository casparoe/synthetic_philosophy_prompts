i've been going back and forth on this for ages and i want someone to audit my position on individual contributions to collective harms, because i use in arguments all the time and it definately feels like there's a joint somewhere that I can't see.

roughly where i've landed:

1. My reason to do or not do something, in these cases anyway, is just the difference I expect to make. Causal expected utility, small probabilities multiplied honestly, no hand-waving about my contribution being "too small to be a real difference." If I'm going to count a one-in-a-million chance in one place I have to count it everywhere.

2. So I think the inefficacy objection fails for voting. My vote almost surely decides nothing — I'd guess something like one in tens of millions in a national election — but I still think I have a real, substantial reason to go vote, and I don't think the twenty minutes it costs me is a serious counterargument against stakes that big. I turn out.

3. I also think the inefficacy objection fails for buying factory-farmed meat. I think buying it is the wrong thing to do, even though I also think — and this is the bit I keep saying out loud to people — that my one packet of chicken very probably changes nothing at all about how many chickens get bred. The store reorders the same quantity, there's no threshold anywhere near my purchase, the counterfactual difference is basically nil. Still the wrong thing to do.

4. I'm suspicious of any moral reason that isn't about outcomes. When people start talking about complicity, or about what your action expresses, or about doing your fair share, I usually think they're reaching for a verdict the numbers won't hand them.

5. For climate specifically I think the only lever that matters is the collective/political one — carbon pricing, regulation, whatever — and any individual person's own footprint is a rounding error next to that. I think someone who actually wants to do something about it should be spending their effort on the politics, not agonizing over their own flights, and I roll my eyes at the personal-footprint-calculator stuff.

6. I don't think I'm required to give more than a token amount to effective causes. I do about one percent and I don't feel guilty about the rest, even though I'll happily grant that a marginal dollar spent well does more expected good than my trip to the polling station does.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? My hunch is that something in the 3-to-5 region is doing work it can't do, but if I had to drop one I genuinely don't know which it'd be. Rank whatever tensions you find by how bad they are instead of just listing them all as equally serious, and for each one say what would have to give and what that'd cost me. If one of them dissolves once you look at it properly, say why rather than counting it as a tension.
