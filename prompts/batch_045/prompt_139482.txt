I want a number from you on a question I've been going back and forth on, and more importantly I want the decomposition that produces it.

The claim: at some point before 2100, a singleton will come to exist and will hold that status for at least ten continuous years. By a singleton I mean a single agency — a state, a coalition that acts as one, a firm, an AI system, or some hybrid — that is the effective highest-level decision-maker for the planet, in the sense that on any large-scale global matter it cared about strongly, no other agent or coalition could stop it from getting its way.

I want that last clause treated as ambiguous in a way that matters, so please give me your number twice, once under each reading:

- The veto-player criterion: the agency is the singleton if no other agent or coalition can prevent it from bringing about any outcome it strongly prefers — it holds the only veto, whether or not it ever uses it. (On this reading an AI system that could restructure the world but has been instructed not to might already qualify, and the question becomes partly counterfactual: what would it do if it tried?)
- The last-word criterion: the agency is the singleton if its actual say settles matters — other agents defer in practice, so it does not need to be able to enforce its will because its will is not contested.

The two can come apart badly. A world government with almost no state capacity satisfies neither; a hegemon everyone preemptively accommodates satisfies the second and not obviously the first; a hypothetical unshackled system satisfies the first and not the second. I don't know which is the better reading of the concept, and I suspect a lot of the disagreement about whether a singleton is likely is really disagreement about that. Tell me if you think so.

Here is the decomposition I have in mind, though add or restructure terms as you see fit. I would like a rough probability and one line of justification for each, then the combination:

1. That some technology (advanced AI most obviously, but not only) produces a decisive capability gap between its controllers and everyone else, sometime before 2100.
2. That such a gap, if it arises, concentrates in a single agency rather than remaining distributed among several roughly comparable ones — i.e., that the multipolar outcome is avoided.
3. That concentrated capability converts into actual effective control rather than a "paper singleton": nominal primacy over a world that quietly does not comply, more like a low-capacity state than like a ruler.
4. That control, once achieved, persists for a decade — that it is not eaten from within by succession, internal fragmentation, or the agency's own changing aims.
5. The non-technological paths, which I think get short shrift: a great-power war ending in unconditional unification, a catastrophe that makes emergency global government acceptable, or what I'll call the drift path — no one establishes anything, but over decades the world's highest-level procedures converge on one set of standards, one enforcement mechanism, or one chokepoint, and the singleton exists before anyone names it. A related case I want handled separately: the hinge path, where control of a single narrow piece of infrastructure (a universal verification system, a compute supply chain, a satellite layer) is sufficient for singleton status without any control of territory or armies.

Please also note where you are treating these terms as independent and where you are not, since I expect the tempting multiplication to be badly wrong in both directions.

Then: which single term is your final number most sensitive to? What one piece of evidence arriving in the next fifteen years would move you the most, and in which direction? And finally — my credence is around 0.3, a colleague's is around 0.7; given your decomposition, what is the specific proposition we most likely disagree about? I have a suspicion it is not "will AI be powerful" but something further downstream, and I would like to know which.

Please keep the whole thing under about 800 words, and write it as reasoning rather than as a survey: no literature review, just the number and what holds it up.
