Something keeps happening with a few arrangements I've made with friends and family, and I don't know if there's a word for it.

The basic setup: my friend and I disagree pretty fundamentally about what's worth doing with money. She's mostly worried about animals on factory farms. I'm mostly worried about extreme poverty, and honestly think the animal stuff is close to a distraction. So a while back we made a deal: for every $50 she donates to the poverty charity I pick, I donate $50 to the farmed-animal charity she picks. Neither of us has changed her mind about anything. We're just swapping.

Here's the bit I keep staring at. Because I'm the one writing the checks to the animal charity, I spend my giving on the thing I think is nearly worthless, and the thing I think actually matters is being funded by someone who thinks it's a mistake. She's in the exact mirror image. And the more completely we swap, the further it goes: in the limit, each of us spends all our effort on the cause we'd rank last, and our favourite cause is entirely in the hands of the person who ranks it last. We both think we got a good deal. We both feel vaguely awful.

It showed up somewhere else too. I'm vegetarian; my partner isn't and thinks it's silly. We have a standing arrangement where he eats vegetarian whenever we're together, and in return I go with him to things I'd otherwise skip, mostly his church. So my vegetarianism is now being done by someone who thinks it's nonsense and eats meat the moment I'm out of town, and I'm sitting in a church I don't believe in. Also — and I think this is part of it — he does it badly, in the sense that he does exactly what we agreed and none of what I actually wanted when I asked for it.

And a third thing, which might be a different thing entirely: once these arrangements became a regular habit, I noticed we both started talking up how much the thing we were receiving meant to us. Suddenly the church thing "really matters" to him. Which is nice, except that we now know less about each other's actual concerns than we did before we started, and I suspect the deals are worse as a result.

So my question. I get the impression that the general practice of trading across moral disagreements is something people actually write about, but I'm not after that. I'm after the specific flip: the person who cares most about X ends up doing the least about X, and X gets done by whoever cares about it least. Does that have a name of its own?

If it doesn't, I'd like a name and a definition that actually carves it, not just a label. Specifically: is this just division of labour in a moral costume — the thing where everyone does what they're relatively best at — or is something else going on when what's being divided is values rather than tasks? Is it the same as the politicians-voting-for-each-other's-pet-projects thing? Is it the same as whatever it's called when doing one good thing gives yourself permission to slack on another? Does anything turn on whether what's being traded is money, versus time, versus your own body sitting in a pew? Is there a version of this that's genuinely bad and a version that's fine, and where's the line — or is the bad feeling I have just a mistake on my part, something the arrangement is supposed to dissolve?

And please say how confident you are in what you're telling me, and flag the single point you're least sure of.
