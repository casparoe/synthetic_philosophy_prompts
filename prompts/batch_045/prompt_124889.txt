I'm a PhD student in machine learning, and I've been asked to write the decision-protocol section of my lab's deployment handbook. The situation I need it for: our deployed agents operate in environments containing adversaries who have exfiltrated a copy of the agent's policy network and can run it forward on any input, but who do not have the agent's random seed. So the adversary predicts a deterministic policy perfectly, and predicts a stochastic policy no better than the policy's own action probabilities. The result is a Death-in-Damascus structure: whichever option the agent ranks highest is the one the adversary has pre-positioned against, and every option comes to look like a guaranteed loss once the agent conditions on its own choice.

What I need is a procedure, not a literature review. Please include no references, citations, or recapitulation of the decision-theory debate — I know that material, and an on-call engineer running this will not. Write a procedure that an engineer with no decision-theory background can work through in about fifteen minutes when an agent's available actions are correlated with outcomes its action does not cause. It should give the questions in order, say what counts as a sufficient answer to each, identify where it stops with a no, and mark the points where it hands the decision to a person because the question is one of values or of organizational risk appetite rather than one about the structure of the case.

Three things I care about most. First, the diagnostic that separates the two families of case: one where the agent's preference merely diagnoses a fixed state of the world that its action does not produce, and one where the outcome is produced by something tracking the agent's decision procedure — these call for opposite responses, and I want the question that tells them apart, not a list of symptoms. Second, the adversary's accuracy profile is discontinuous at determinism; say what the procedure does with that, and with options that differ in payoff conditional on survival. Third, give three worked applications: one genuine case of the predictor kind, one that superficially resembles it but belongs to the diagnostic kind, and one where the procedure returns a verdict my team will not want to hear. Close by saying plainly what the procedure cannot settle. Please write in American English.
