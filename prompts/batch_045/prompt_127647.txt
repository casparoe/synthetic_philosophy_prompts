My friend Jonah has opinions about military service and I can never keep them strait. His verdicts so far:

- Switzerland keeping its militia conscription: good, it should keep it.
- Finland keeping universal male conscription: yes, keep it.
- Sweden pausing conscription for a few years and then bringing it back: the return was right, but he's oddly lukewarm about it compared to Finland.
- Austria holding a referendum and voting to keep the draft: good, correct outcome.
- The US ending the draft and going all-volunteer: fine, no going back.
- The UK and France going professional: fine.
- Germany suspending its draft: he thinks that was a mistake, which is the one that confuses me most, since Germany is a big rich country like France.
- Israel conscripting basically everyone, women included: he says yes, but grudgingly, and insists it's a different kind of case.
- Ireland staying professional even though it's neutral: fine by him too, Ireland isn't really expected to fight anybody.
- Costa Rica having no army at all: also fine.

The case I actually want is invented, so don't go looking for it. Call it Meridia: 5.5 million people, EU member, constitutionally neutral, no land border with anyone who might plausibly invade, and its only real security problems are undersea cables getting sabotaged, GPS jamming, and a big merchant fleet it can't protect. It currently has a professional force of about 8,000. The defense ministry is floating a Swiss-style militia: four months of basic training, annual refreshers, open to all genders, a civilian-service alternative for objectors, and most of the billets would be drone operators, cyber and air defense rather than infantry.

What would Jonah say? What I actually want is the rules: give me the two or three principles that fit all ten of his verdicts, say what each one predicts about Meridia, and tell me which is most likely his, with the reasons. My own guess is that he says yes, because every one of his yeses is a neutral or territorial-defense country and every one of his noes is an expeditionary power — but I can't tell whether that's really his rule or whether he's just running something like "conscription is for countries that need mass, and the US, UK and France don't." The Meridia plan is explicitly not about mass, which is why I'm stuck. Also tell me which further case would settle it; I was thinking of asking him about Switzerland itself under the condition that the militia's jobs have all moved to screens and sensors so headcount no longer buys anything, but maybe there's a cleaner test.

And please separate what's actually settled in this area from what people who study it argue about. My suspicion is that a lot of what sounds like common sense here is contested — whether conscripts are cheaper, whether conscript armies are harder to send abroad, whether conscription makes a country more or less likely to end up in wars, whether gender-neutral conscription is the equality-friendly version or not — and I don't want a contested empirical claim handed to me as the consensus. Flag the ones you're leaning on that are shaky.
