I got into an argument at a conference dinner about the arrow of time and it's been bugging me since. Here's the shape of it as I understand it. The underlying dynamics are more or less time-symmetric, entropy is higher toward what we call the future, and the standard Boltzmann-style move is to say that's because of one very low-entropy boundary condition at one end plus a typicality/counting story: almost any microstate compatible with that condition evolves toward higher entropy, in both temporal directions, and we happen to be on one slope. The pushback I keep running into comes in two flavours. One says this delivers the gradient but not the direction: a single low-entropy boundary gives you increase away from it in both directions, so you get a symmetric, two-headed universe with no fact of the matter about which end is later, and if that's not acceptable then something other than the gradient has to be doing the work. The other says the asymmetry is partly an artifact of where we sit — we have records of one end and not the other, we deliberate toward one end and not the other, and a description of the world from outside would leave nothing to explain.

These lines have been in front of everybody for over a century. Same equations, same data, no movement that I can detect. So I don't want another survey, I want a diagnosis. Is this really three questions sharing a name — why there's a gradient, which end is the future, and why we predict one way and retrodict the other / why memory and agency point where they do — with people sliding between them? Or is there one question where the sides differ about what would even count as an explanation: do they disagree about typicality measures, about whether an explanation has to render the explanandum probable or merely locate it in a structure, or about which cases count as data at all (does the two-headed universe count as a reductio, or as a prediction to be swallowed)?

My hypothesis, for what it's worth: much of the stall comes from people answering the direction question while advertising answers to the gradient question, and the two-headed universe is the only place the difference becomes visible. To test that I made up a case I haven't seen anyone use: one universe, one low-entropy bottleneck, two civilizations on opposite sides of it, each with records and deliberation pointing away from the bottleneck, who eventually meet in the middle and compare notes. Neither can derive the other's 'future' from the boundary condition, and each thinks the other is running backwards. Does that case settle anything about whether there's a direction over and above the gradient — or is it just a vivid restatement of the stalemate, in the sense that each side reads it in its own terms and comes away confirmed?

And given whatever diagnosis you land on: what would actually count as progress? A result about typicality or measures, a cosmological finding about the far end, a clean distincton between gradient and direction, a decision to stop asking one of the questions — or should I expect no progress at all, and if so, why exactly?
