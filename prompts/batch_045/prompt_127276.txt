I'm in a philosophy of biology seminar and we've just come out of the unit on genetic information. Rather than another is DNA literally a code? essay, I want the specification such an essay would have to meet.

Please write the desiderata that an account of carrier of genetic information — or of what it is for a physical structure to be literally a code — has to satisfy, where the point of the account is to settle whether molecular biology's talk of codes, messages, and reading is literal or a working metaphor. Give the motivating case behind each requirement. The cases I most want handled, though you should add others:

1. a sequence whose entire functional role is sequence-specific binding — a spacer of exact length, or a site whose job is simply to hold a protein — where nothing downstream is read out into a distinct product;
2. a cell with two translation systems over the same nucleic-acid alphabet, one using triplet codons and one using four-base codons read by orthogonal tRNAs, so that the same stretch of mRNA yields two unrelated proteins depending on which ribosome takes it;
3. a lineage in which a reassigned synthetase has shifted a whole codon family, so that the same gene now places a different residue at dozens of sites — viable, but measurably slower-growing;
4. prions and methylation marks, which transmit heritable, phenotype-relevant structure while being protein conformation and chemical modification rather than polymer sequence.

I'd also like the list to separate questions that usually get run together: whether the mapping must be arbitrary or may be stereochemical; whether the code is a property of a string or of a string-plus-reader; whether carrying information requires the possibility of misreading; whether the message's output is amino-acid sequence, phenotype, or selected function.

Mark each requirement as non-negotiable or as a preference of one camp, and flag any subset that cannot be jointly satisfied — in particular, whether allowing error commits you to a semantic normativity that sits badly with keeping the account naturalistic. Close by naming, in a sentence or two, the single requirement that excludes the most contenders; I'll build the scorecard myself.
