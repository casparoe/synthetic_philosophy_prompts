Fix the purpose first: the theory is for an analyst working now, without access to the future, who must put a calibrated probability on a named polity surviving another N years, and who must be able to say what would justify overriding that number with a case-specific story about this polity's particular crisis, institutions, and leadership. What I want from you is the specification, not a forecast.

Write the desiderata any adequate method for choosing a reference class and generating a base-rate survival estimate has to meet, each with the case that motivates it. At minimum I want to see how the list handles these: what counts as the same polity's continuing rather than its being replaced, so that a single state's lifespan is even measurable; the fact that the currently existing cases are right-censored while the finished ones are a biased sample of everything that ever started; the freedom to gerrymander the time unit, the start date, and the property list, so that the same polity supports wildly different numbers; the tension between estimates that are conditional on duration already survived and estimates that are time-invariant; the requirement that the properties defining a class be specifiable in advance of the outcome and not selected because they fit which cases failed; the requirement that the estimate still be informative, meaning not so broad a class that it collapses into a trivial statement about everything's ending; and the fact that the analyst is forecasting from inside the lifetime of the object, with a sample assembled by whoever recorded it.

Mark each desideratum as non-negotiable or as the preference of a camp, and say which single requirement does the most work in excluding actual forecasting practice as you have seen it. Then identify the subset you think cannot be jointly satisfied: if you think the list is itself an impossibility result, say plainly what has to be traded for what and why no cleverer method escapes the trade. Close with three or four lines only on how naive base-rate reasoning, narrative case-specific reasoning, and duration-based reasoning from a single observation each fare against the list.
