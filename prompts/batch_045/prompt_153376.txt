The topic I want examined is luck egalitarianism. Here is the invented apparatus I would like you to build the question around; please feel free to sharpen it if you can see a better version.

In the imaginary Republic of Varrania the state operates the Equal Risk Scheme. Each year every citizen's disposable income is adjusted by a "luck audit," which works as follows:

- Officials construct, for each citizen, a counterfactual twin: someone placed in the same social position and with the same legal rights, but endowed with the average bundle of native endowments (health, talents, temperament) and an average-quality upbringing.
- Any shortfall between the citizen's income and the twin's is attributed to brute luck, and is cancelled by a state transfer. (A misfortune counts as brute luck when it did not flow from any choice of the agent's.)
- Any shortfall that flows from the citizen's own choices is left standing; the citizen bears it. (A misfortune counts as option luck when it is the outcome of a gamble the agent knowingly took.)
- Officials treat a choice as genuinely the agent's own, and therefore as a responsibility-bearing choice, only when three conditions hold: the agent had adequate information about the risks, was not coerced, and could reasonably have foreseen the outcome.
- Varrania maintains a modest floor below which no one is allowed to fall, but it insists that the floor is humanitarian relief rather than justice. Justice, it says, is done by the audit.

Varrania's actuaries have recently published two awkward findings:

(i) The choices that show up in the audit are themselves distributed by brute luck. People who grew up in poverty, or who inherited a temperament low in impulse control, are markedly more likely to make exactly the choices — casual work, no saving, early dropout — that leave them short of their twin. The audit therefore converts brute luck into apparent option luck, and the transfers shrink towards zero precisely for those who began worst off.

(ii) Some losses are covariant. When Varrania's shipbuilding region collapses after a trade shock, everyone who chose to work there loses at once, and no private insurer would ever have covered the risk, since there is no pool across which to spread it.

What I want the question to require, roughly in three linked parts:

1. An explanation of why Varrania's architects would say the Equal Risk Scheme is the most natural institutional expression of luck egalitarianism — why the audit, and not merely the floor, is supposed to be where the justice is.
2. A verdict on the actuaries' findings: do they refute the scheme, or can it be reformed so that it survives them? The candidate should pick the strongest reform available — for instance, relocating the luck/choice cut from outcomes to opportunities or to a hypothetical insurance market, or holding agents responsible only for choices that express who they really are, or confining compensation to a baseline and letting some losses lie — and defend that reform against the strongest objection to it.
3. A statement of what the answer to part 2 shows about what sort of doctrine luck egalitarianism actually is: is it fundamentally a view about treating people as equals, or a view about people getting what they deserve, and can it coherently be both?
