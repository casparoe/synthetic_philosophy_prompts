I'm on the subcommittee that writes the conduct code for our nursing students' eight-week placements at a partner hospital abroad, and we have to put something in the handbook before the vote on the 14th. Two incidents from this spring are what forced it.

Incident one: the ward attendant who brings bedpans and changes linens expects a small cash gift each morning, and without it he's slow or doesn't come at all. Everyone does this, from the cleaners to the senior nurses, and the attendant's salary is genuinely below subsistence. One of our students refused on principle: she called it extortion and said a patient's basic care shouldn't be purchasable. Her patient went two days without being changed, and the hospital liaison says the student humiliated the attendant in front of the whole ward.

Incident two: medication comes off a single cart and nobody lines up; you catch the nurse's eye and ask. Another of our students stood in a polite queue, never recieved anything, and then complained to the liaison that the system is unfair. The liaison told her the queue was the rude part, that standing in an orderly line implied the staff were too slow and made them lose face.

The committee has split three ways. Some say these are just local coordination habits and a visitor owes nothing either way, so the handbook should say "use your judgment" and stop there. Some say being a guest is precisely the case where you owe compliance: you're benefitting from a system you didn't build and can't repair in eight weeks, and opting out is enjoying everyone else's restraint while contributing none. A third group says the two incidents are different in kind and need different rules — one is a coordination device where opting out mainly makes you ineffectual, the other is a payment that exists because of underfunding, so complying both helps one identifiable person and helps entrench the arrangement that person is stuck in.

Two further things we can't settle. (a) Several local staff told our students privately that they hate the gift custom and wish outsiders would refuse, but nobody will say so where it can be heard — does that change what our students should do? (b) Last year's cohort gave more than locals give, and the liaison says expectations have started rising for everyone, so our students' compliance may be slowly pricing local families out.

Can you draft the memo I'll circulate? I need an actual recommendation about what goes in the handbook — a rule, a rule with a carve-out, or deliberately no rule at all — with the reasoning behind it, and it has to give the strongest version of whichever position we end up rejecting rather than a version nobody would defend. Two pages at most.
