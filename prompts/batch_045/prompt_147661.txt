I convene a small monthly reading group on the Fermi question — half astronomy and SETI people, half philosophers, plus one political scientist — and we have spent the better part of a year going in circles about the dark forest hypothesis: the claim that the sky is quiet because, given a large but deeply uncertain spread of civilizations, lightspeed lag, and the possibility of strikes that are cheap for the striker and catastrophic for the target, the rational policy is to stay silent and to preempt. One camp in the group — call them the forest people, several of whom arrived already converted by the novel that popularized the name — holds that this is the best available explanation of the silence. The other camp — call them the emptiness people — holds that the silence is mostly evidence about how rare technological life is, that priors rather than game theory are doing the explanatory work, and that the dark forest is an elegant story bolted onto numbers we do not have. I have volunteered to produce a first draft of a joint statement both camps could put their names to, and I would like you to draft it: first a numbered section of claims both sides can sign (about what the observations so far do and do not rule out; about the physics of lag, distance, and what would even count as a technosignature; about what follows decision-theoretically if certain payoffs are granted; and about how much of the residual dispute is really a dispute about priors); then a table of the disagreements, with each side's position stated in wording its own partisans would accept as fair; then the cruxes; then, for each crux, a concrete observation, argument, or hypothetical case both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

There are three things I want the document to actually confront, because they are where I think the live argument has moved. First, what I have been calling the foundry window: it seems plausible that any civilization that industrializes passes through a phase of fast-growing energy use, and that even if it later disperses into habitats or deliberately goes quiet, that phase should leave behind a thin, outward-expanding shell of waste heat and atmospheric residue — in which case concealment may simply not be available to a species that ever had heavy industry, and the forest camp owes us an account of how hiding could be cheap and durable enough to be worth choosing. Second, what I call the witness condition: for silence-plus-preemption to be an equilibrium, strikes must be both cheap to carry out and hard for third parties to observe and attribute; if a strike is visible, there is room for reputation, norms, and punishment, and the forest begins to look less like a forest than like a town square with a severe security dilemma. Third, what I call the census gap: our capacity to detect biospheres is currently improving faster than our capacity to detect modest technospheres, so we may be within reach of asking whether the galaxy contains many inhabited-but-quiet worlds or very few inhabited worlds at all, which I take to be the observation that most sharply discriminates between the two stories. Please use these, but if you think one of them rests on a confusion, say so in a footnote rather than bending it into place.

Two further requests. I suspect part of what the group is arguing about is merely verbal — that words like silence and dark forest are each doing several different jobs — so please say explicitly which part of the dispute you take to be verbal, and what substantive remainder is left once the verbal part is set aside. And please be explicit about confidence throughout: tag each agreed claim and each prediction with a rough confidence level, and end with a short section singling out the one line in the document that you think a genuine, committed member of each camp would refuse to sign, and why. I would much rather discover now which formulation breaks the truce than find out after I circulate the draft.
