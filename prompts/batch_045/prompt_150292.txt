In our interdisciplinary reading group — economic historians and philosophers of science, meeting for two years now — the Malthus question has stopped moving entirely, and not for want of data. Everyone agrees that for centuries before the industrial break real wages and population track each other inversely, and that afterwards both rise together for two hundred years. One side says the mechanism was right and only the timing was wrong: the escape, on this view, is owed to an energy and land subsidy (coal, colonial acreage, later high-yield agriculture) arriving from outside the model, and on a long enough horizon the asymmetry between geometric increase and arithmetic increase reasserts itself, so Malthus was early rather than mistaken. The other side says the escape is exactly the falsifying case — the same variables that were supposed to produce misery instead produced the demographic transition — and that restricting the theory's domain to the pre-industrial centuries, or relabelling the escape as exogenous, is rescue after the fact.

I want a diagnosis, not a verdict. My hypothesis is that right-but-early is really three claims with different evidentiary burdens — vindication about the past, vindication about a longer-run future, vindication of the mechanism given an exogenous switch — and that the debate slides between them without anyone noticing. But it may instead be a disagreement about what a long-run prediction owes us: whether it may stay silent about the interim path, and whether a rescued theory may borrow from its rival the mechanism that explains the interim data. We have been toying with two tests and want them pressure-tested: a long-run prediction earns credit only if it constrains the observable path before its terminal date and not merely the endpoint; and a rescue may not import from the rival theory the very mechanism that accounts for the anomaly being explained away. Tell me which test, if either, is doing real work; what kind of disagreement this is underneath — factual, verbal, about what counts as the theory's domain, about standards of evidence for a tendency claim — and what would count as progress given that diagnosis: a series, a modelling result, a distinction, or nothing at all.
