My partner's in philosophy and I got dragged into her reading group — I'm a condensed matter physicist, so I use laws every day but had never once asked what one is. We've been stuck on the Humean vs. anti-Humean thing for weeks and it feels like a genuine stall: everyone agrees on what actually happens (the full distribution of events, "the mosaic"), everyone agrees the famous laws are true, and yet half the room says laws are nothing over and above the pattern that gets the best balance of simplicity and strength, the other half says there's a real governing or necessitating relation doing work, and nobody's intuitions move a millimeter.

I want a diagnosis, not a verdict. Is this verbal, with two different things meant by "law" (summary vs. governor)? Are the sides answering different questions under one label — what science asserts, versus what makes it true or what explains the regularity? Or is a hidden question doing all the work, like whether brute facts about the mosaic can really support the counterfactuals, explanations, and intervention-inferences that working scientists rely on? My own half-baked hypothesis, which nobody in the group liked, is that the fight is really about what counts as a stopping point in explanation: one side is happy to say "brute pattern" and shut the book, the other thinks that's precisely the thing that needed explaining, and the alleged governor is just a name for the refusal to stop. If that's right the dispute shouldn't be resolvable at all — but then why does it feel factual?

One more thing from my end of physics that I want taken seriously. My field runs on effective laws — thermodynamics, hydrodynamics, low-energy theories — that are genuinely lawlike but not fundamental, and that can flatly conflict with each other across scales. If lawhood is a matter of degree, or scale-relative, or admits several equally good systems, does that damage the best-system side (which wants a unique winner) or the governing side (whose relation looks all-or-nothing)? And does the answer change the diagnosis? Give me reasons, not a survey of positions, and tell me what would count as progress here — a distinction, a formal result, a change of question — or argue that nothing would and say why.
