I'm trying to extract the account of grounding that my own case verdicts commit me to, and I don't think they are consistent as they stand.

1. The fact that the singleton {Socrates} exists is grounded in the fact that Socrates exists — but I don't want to infer that the singleton is nothing over and above Socrates; I want it to be a genuine extra item that merely fails to be fundamental.
2. The fact that there are at least two things is grounded jointly in Socrates' existing, Plato's existing, and their being distinct; no one of the three suffices alone.
3. The general fact that every electron has charge −1 is grounded in all of its instances together with the fact that those are all the electrons there are.
4. Yet I also want to say that the law stating that general fact explains why this particular electron has the charge it does, and I would rather not give that up.
5. The fact that the room is empty is grounded in the positive facts about what is in it, and not conversely; but I am much less sure about the negative fact that there are no polar bears in Antarctica, where I cannot survey the positive basis.
6. The statue's existence is grounded in the clay's being arranged statuewise; the clay's existence is not even partly grounded in the statue's existence — dependence runs one way only.

My working principle: the Ys fully ground X iff the Ys form the minimal, non-redundant set of facts such that a complete description of reality that listed the Ys would thereby have settled X, and no Y depends on X.

That seems to handle 1, 2 and 6, but 3 and 4 pull apart, and I suspect 5 breaks it in a further way. So: is there a single, precisely stated account of grounding — deploying the full/partial distinction only if it does real work — that gets all six verdicts, or nearly all? State the account, say what motivates each clause, and if saving every verdict costs more in complexity than it is worth, tell me which verdict to overturn and what the cost of overturning it is. Then apply the account you defend to two cases I have not listed: (a) in a world where a program, its input, and the hardware running it are all in place, is the fact that the program output 42 grounded in all three taken together, in each of them severally, or only in the hardware? (b) In a world where the only clock reads twelve exactly when it is noon, does the fact that the meeting was at noon ground, get grounded by, or neither ground nor get grounded by the fact that it was held at the hour the clock struck twelve?
