I am writing a paper on the butterfly episode in the Zhuangzi: Zhuang Zhou dreams he is a butterfly, and on waking he does not know whether he is Zhou who dreamed he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming it is Zhou; the passage then remarks that between Zhou and the butterfly there must be some distinction, and calls this the transformation of things. My thesis is that the two main families of reading are not merely rival interpretations of a puzzling paragraph but rival accounts of what a person is, and that the rivalry can be made to show up in practice rather than only in exegesis. To test that, I need a case in which the two readings tell someone to do different things.

Please construct such a case to the following specifications.

The two readings to be pitted against each other:

A. The skeptical reading. The episode raises a first-personal epistemic problem: experience does not mark its own difference from dreaming, so the subject has no title to treat the state they are now in as the one in which things are as they seem. Waking has no epistemic privilege over dreaming.

B. The transformation reading. The episode is not about not knowing but about not being fixed. There is no enduring subject that was Zhou and is now the butterfly; there are successive states into which things transform. The distinction the text insists on is a distinction between those states, not a hidden fact about which one is really the subject. The apt response to transformation is responsiveness to what is now before one, not adjudication between states.

Constraints on the case:

1. One paragraph, ordinary setting, present-day. No science fiction, no drugs, no brain injury or clinical sleep disorder, no lucid dreaming, no sleep laboratory or monitoring equipment.
2. The subject is not confused. They know they fell asleep, know afterwards that the episode was a dream, and at no point suspect they might be asleep now. The case may not work by simply rerunning the original question.
3. Inheritance constraint: the dream must leave the subject with something that presents itself as a claim on them once they are awake and that they could either honour or set aside. It must be something owed or owed-to, not a skill, not a piece of information, and not a leftover mood.
4. Reading A must nonetheless be genuinely engaged rather than idle. It needs to deliver a verdict that actually guides action, and that verdict must differ from reading B's. The difference may not consist in A saying that one cannot know and so should suspend while B says something positive; A's verdict has to be as directive as B's.
5. Direction. I want the reversal of the expected one: A, the reading usually taken to be deflationary, should be the one that treats the inherited claim as weighty or binding; B, the reading that is supposed to take the butterfly seriously, should be the one that releases the subject from it. If you think this direction cannot be reached without building part of the answer into the case, say so, construct the best case available in whichever direction is open, and mark exactly where the obstruction lies.
6. No stipulation. The verdicts must fall out of the two readings plus the facts of the case. The subject may not have heard of either reading, may not endorse anything like them, and must not have a metaphysical view built into their psychology.
7. Recall constraint. The dream contains at least one specific item, such as a name, a sentence, or a detail of a place, that the subject can confirm after waking was something they already knew but could not recall while awake. This is to stop the case from resting on dream-content being generically unreliable or generically vague.

After the scenario, please supply:

(a) the verdict each reading returns, with the reasoning that gets it there, in two or three sentences each;
(b) the single structural feature of the case doing the work, and what happens to the two verdicts if that feature is deleted: do they collapse into agreement, and on which side;
(c) the strongest objection available to a defender of whichever reading comes out worse, specifically about where the case has quietly helped itself to something that reading denies;
(d) one sentence on whether the case survives if the item from constraint 7 turns out, on waking, to be false after all.

Please state what you take the case to show about the two readings, and keep the whole response under about 700 words.
