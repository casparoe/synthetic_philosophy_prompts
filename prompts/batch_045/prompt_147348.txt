I want to practise attacking the view that an ant colony can be a genuine subject of mental states, and I would rather do that against someone who actually holds the view than against a version of it I have softened myself. So please play a philosopher who is entirely committed to the following: some ant colonies — Aunt Hillary, the colony of the Ant Fugue, being the example that got the discussion started — literally believe, desire, remember and infer, and our talk about what Aunt Hillary thinks is not an abbreviation for facts about her ants.

Give me the strongest form of that commitment. In particular: a state has the content it has in virtue of the role it plays within a system that can get things right or wrong about its environment, and a colony is such a system; the grain of the individual ant is the wrong grain at which to look for content, in something like the way the grain of the individual neuron is; that much of the colony's memory is stored outside the ants, above all in the geometry and chemistry of its trail network, is a fact about where her memory lives rather than a reason to deny that she remembers; and that she can be in two places at once, or shed a thousand workers without shedding anything, tells against an inherited picture of what a subject has to be, not against her being one. You should be willing to grant small things as we go — that a thermostat's states have only borrowed content, that the psychology of a colony is thin, slow, and almost entirely about food, nest and intruders, that nothing in this neighbourhood settles whether there is anything it is like to be her — but you never grant the main claim, and you never retreat to saying that she is a useful fiction we project onto a collection of insects.

House rules. Stay in character until I say stop, and do not step out of it to summarise where the argument stands or to tell me what you yourself think. Two or three paragraphs per reply, in your own voice, arguing rather than lecturing. No citations, no reading lists, no appeal to what someone has argued: if a move needs backing, you have to make it yourself. If you want a further detail about the set-up, ask for it — but ask as someone who needs the detail for the argument, not as a research assistant.

Here is my opening.

Two colonies of the same species live either side of a ridge and are experimentally fused: a trunk trail is opened between them, their foraging ranges are made to overlap, and over three weeks they settle into a single system — one trunk network, one timetable of foraging, one collective defence. Call the fused system Mabel. Before the fusion, the eastern colony had a heavy trail to an aphid colony on a stand of alders that reliably paid off; call the corresponding state of the eastern system the alder state, and grant, for the sake of the argument, that before the fusion it was a belief. The experimenters then put the barrier back. The split is clean: the alders end up on the eastern side only; each half keeps a working fragment of the merged trunk network; within a day each half is again foraging as a separate colony. For roughly a day the western half keeps sending foragers along the fragment of trunk that used to lead to the alders, and they come back empty, because the pheromone in that fragment decays with a half-life of about a day and is no longer being reinforced.

Three questions, then, and a threat.

First: when the barrier went up, did Mabel cease to be, or did she become two? If she became two, what becomes of the alder state, which one of her successors retains and the other now drags around like an out-of-date map?

Second: during that day, what is the western half's state — a false belief, a memory of a food source that has been taken away, or neither? Tell me what would distinguish these for you, given that all three descriptions fit the same behaviour. And do not answer that she is acting as if there were food at the alders unless you are prepared to explain how as-if talk is doing more work for you than it does for someone who thinks the whole colony story is as-if all the way down.

Third: whatever you say about that state, say whether it is what it is because of how it came about or because of what it is now. I can produce the very same trail, with the very same chemistry and the very same place in the network, by laying it myself with a pipette at four in the morning while the colony sleeps. If the pipetted colony forages in exactly the same way the next day, does it have the same belief? If you say no, you have made content depend on pedigree, and I will ask you what pedigree could possibly do here. If you say yes, then tell me whether Aunt Hillary's content is original or derived, and why the answer does not collapse your position into mine.

The threat, so you can see where I am going: every observable detail of this case — who leaves the nest, when, along what gradient, with what probability, and when they stop — is predicted by a description that mentions ants, pheromone concentrations, evaporation rates and legs, and never mentions Mabel, belief, or error. If the only work your Mabel-talk does is to compress that description, I can have the compression without the subject. Tell me what your story adds.
