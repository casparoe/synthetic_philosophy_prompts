Write me a parody, about 600 words, set out as numbered clauses, of the induction handbook for a drab back-office firm that hires temps for a single job: continuing a numerical series correctly. The new starter is shown 2, 4, 6, 8 and asked to go on.

The humour has to come from the handbook's own inability to say what it's asking for, so each clause should try a different way of nailing down what makes a continuation correct and quietly give up on it. I'd want roughly: a clause that tries to define correctness by what the temp is disposed to answer; one that appeals to the rule the temp meant without ever managing to say which rule that is; one ruling that what a temp feels, pictures or inwardly says is not evidence and that there's no inner fact to consult; and, as the last substantive clause, one that settles for what a qualified assessor would agree to after a reasonable period of scrutiny. It must never admit that this has swapped correctness for assertibility, and it must never come out and say that there's no fact of the matter — it keeps talking as though there obviously is one while making it impossible to say what.

Two more things I'd like worked in. Slip in the rival rule that agrees with addition for every pair of numbers below 64 and returns 5 for anything above, together with the policy that no run of past answers can distinguish it from adding, and that the handbook's own clauses are subject to the same difficulty as the series. And include a staffing rule that no temp may ever work a shift alone, since a lone worker cannot be certified as following any rule at all, even if they recieve a perfect audit.

Deadpan HR voice throughout — appraisals, rotas, escalation, a grievance procedure that rules out appeals on the grounds that you meant something different. No naming of philosophers, no use of the word quus, no footnotes, no winking at the reader.
