my frustration is that both sides now agree on the data and neither has moved, because each side has an all-purpose move — "that's cognition, not perception" and "that IS perception, you're just defining it as the part that can't be reached" — and neither move costs anything. my working hypothesis is that there are three disagreements stacked under one name. (1) a cut-point problem: the visual system is massively recurrent and bidirectionally connected, so there is no fact of the matter about where "perception" ends and "cognition" begins, and the choice of cut is a stipulation, not a discovery. (2) a disagreement about what impenetrability was supposed to do theoretically: evidence for a modular architecture, versus a claim about the phenomenal character of experience, versus a claim about experience's epistemic role. (3) a disagreement about whether introspective reports about appearances are any evidence at all, which is a methodological disagreement that no experiment about perception can settle.
