ok so I TA an undergrad phil of science course, and I got into this over beers with a friend who works in ML interpretability, and now I can't find the move. He handed me this argument against Kuhn's incommensurability thesis and it seems to work too well:

(1) The meaning of a theoretical term is just its place in the whole network of a scientists beliefs — its inferential role, nothing more.
(2) A paradigm shift changes big chunks of that network.
(3) So after a revolution the old terms and the new ones don't mean the same thing, and there's no neutral language in which both theories can be fully stated.
(4) If there's no neutral language, the two theories can't be compared at all: no shared logical consequences, no contradiction between them, no common measure of which one gets nature more right.
(5) But Kuhn's own 1962 book is a piece of history — it tells you what Priestley and the Aristotelians actually believed, and says how and why the field moved.
(6) Doing that requires the historian to state, in our language, what those people believed, which (3) and (4) forbid.
(7) So if (1)–(4) hold, Kuhn's own book is impossible. So is all history of science, and for that matter the history of anything anyone ever believed.
(8) History of science obviously is possible — Kuhn did it.
(9) So something in (1)–(4) is false, and the incommensurability thesis is dead.
(10) And his shared criteria for theory choice (accuracy, simplicity, scope, fruitfulness and the rest) aren't an escape hatch either, because if terms don't keep their meanings across the divide then accuracy means one thing before the revolution and another after, so the supposedly neutral umpire isn't neutral.

Two things bug me. First, (1) looks like it proves too much all by itself: if meaning is the whole network, then every time I learn one new thing every term I use shifts meaning, so I don't mean the same thing by coffee from one hour to the next and I can't interpret my own notes from last week. Second, I want to keep (2) and (3) — the Gestalt switch thing feels right to me — and I definitely want to keep (8). But then I don't see what's left to give up, and if I give up (1) I don't know what theory of meaning replaces it or whether the replacement lets Priestley and Lavoisier be arguing about the same stuff after all.

Which step do you reject? Why did it look fine when I wrote it down? And can you price the options for me — at least these three: keep meaning holism and drop the move from there's no shared language to there's no comparison; drop holism for some causal/externalist story where reference is fixed independently of theory; or follow Kuhn's own later move of shrinking incommensurability down to the taxonomic kind-terms so that only part of the vocabulary goes untranslatable. Whichever one is the standard answer, tell me what it can't explain.
