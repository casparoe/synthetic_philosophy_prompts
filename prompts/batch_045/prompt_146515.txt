Hey — I'm a journalist (mostly science-and-ideas stuff) doing a column on self-amending rules, and my hook is on-chain governance: DAOs whose founding charter is a contract that can rewrite its own amendment procedure, conventions that get to vote on the formula by which they vote, that kind of thing. It's due Friday, and separately I'm opening a panel in a couple of weeks on "constitutions that amend themselves," where I'll have maybe thirty seconds at the top. Both need the same thing: one joke. I'm definately overthinking it, but my editor says I can't open with another paragraph of setup.

The bit of the material I keep getting stuck on — and that I want the joke to actually be *about* — is the situation where the rule telling you how to change the rules is itself one of the rules. Concretely, my invented nightmare charter: clause (a) says "No provision of this charter may be modified except by a two-thirds vote"; clause (b) says "Clause (a) may be modified by a simple majority"; and clause (c), which I've been calling the mirror clause, says "Any modification of this charter must be ratified by the committee established by this charter." So a simple majority can lower the bar for everything by going after (a) first, and the body that has to ratify the change is the very body whose existence and powers the change is about — which means a sufficiently creative amendment can dissolve the ratifier and appoint its own successor. There's also the self-erasing version: a rule whose entire content is "This rule is hereby repealed," which either repeals itself in the act of taking effect (in which case it was never really law at all?) or can't, because a rule that isn't there can't do anything.

What I want is a joke whose punchline depends on that machinery, rather than on lawyers being pedantic or coders being nerds — the way a good joke about, say, Liar-paradox stuff makes you see the structure fresh again. A one-liner, a two-line exchange, a fake excerpt from the minutes of that meeting, whatever shape works, I'm flexible; it just needs to be short enough to say out loud and to land with a room that's half law-and-philosophy and half crypto-governance people. Please skip anything that requires knowing a real statute or a named court case to get, and skip "the smart contract had a bug" gags — the joke should be about the logic of a rule getting its hands on its own amendment procedure, not about anyone's code being broken. And if you can, add one short line afterward saying which specific feature the joke is leaning on, so I don't mangle it when I try to explain it in the intro.
