I am writing a paper for a volume on the definition of art, and I want to build it around a case rather than around the standard philosophical examples — the readymade urinal, the Brillo cartons, the forged Vermeer, the elephant's painting. My case is Banksy's film *Exit Through the Gift Shop* (2010), and I would like a sustained reading of it through the definitional debate. Assume I know the film and the literature; I do not need citations, a bibliography, or a reading list. I want the argument.

The film is, roughly: Thierry Guetta, a Los Angeles vintage-clothing dealer with a compulsion to film everything, attaches himself to street artists — his cousin Invader first, then Shepard Fairey, then Banksy — and accumulates thousands of hours of tape he never watches and never edits. Banksy eventually takes the footage over and turns it into a documentary; to get Guetta out of the way, he tells him to go home and make some art. Guetta renames himself Mr. Brainwash, mortgages his business and his house, hires a production team that fabricates the work under his direction, and stages *Life Is Beautiful* in a vast former studio complex — a show that gets a cover story before the works have even been chosen, sells close to a million dollars in its first week, and is extended for months, to the visible bewilderment of Banksy and Fairey, who had regarded him as derivative and unready. The film's own status is contested: it is billed as a documentary, narrated in voiceover by someone other than Banksy, built largely from Guetta's footage, credited to Banksy, and widely suspected of being an elaborate prank; Banksy's closing voiceover gives up on the question of who, if anyone, the joke is on.

Set that against the three standard families of account: the procedural ones (institutional conferral of the status of candidate for appreciation; Danto's artworld and its atmosphere of theory; the intentional-historical definition associated with Levinson), the functional or aesthetic ones (Beardsley), and the anti-essentialist and cluster responses (Weitz, and Gaut's cluster account). For each, I want to know where exactly in this story Guetta becomes an artist — if he does — and which account makes the film a counterexample rather than an illustration.

Two complications I want taken up rather than smoothed over. The first, which I will call the laundering problem: here status seems to be conferred by the artworld on the basis of publicity, proximity to already-sanctioned artists, and press attention, and not on the basis of anything about the objects — conferred, that is, for reasons the artworld would itself disavow if they were stated. Should a procedural account care about the causal route by which conferral happens, or only that it happened? If it must appeal to the right kind of grounds, has it smuggled in the evaluation it was supposed to avoid? Second, the sealed-room case, which I have not seen discussed: an isolated obsessive builds an enormous, visually ravishing installation in a warehouse he never shows anyone and never thinks of as art — he thinks he is building a machine, say — and it is discovered after his death and produces exactly the responses art produces. Compare that with Guetta on each of the three families. If the accounts diverge on the two cases in a way that tracks nothing we care about, the honest conclusion may be that the film is not really asking "what is art?" but something neighbouring — about authorship, credit, or the normativity of recognition — and I want you to say so if you think that.

Also: Guetta's work was largely executed by a hired team, as much canonical work has been; say whether the film thereby risks collapsing into a cheap point about authenticity, and what would distinguish Guetta's arrangement from a Renaissance workshop or the Factory if not sheer hindsight. Finally, tell me where the reading fails: which scene resists it, where the film is too invested in its own prank to be a reliable philosophical witness, and whether, at the end, the film contributes anything to the definitional debate that a paper could not.
