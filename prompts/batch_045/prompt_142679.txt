So this stopped being hypothetical for me about six months ago. I help run a mid-sized hobby community — a board game convention, about two thousand people in the Discord, a volunteer committee of seven — and we had to decide what to do about a member who'd been creepy to people at the last two events. We did it, and I can't stop chewing on why it felt fine in that case and gross in so many other cases. My gut verdicts on this whole topic are all over the place and I'd really like to know if any single principle actually covers them.

Here's where I land, going purely on gut:

1. A member posted a straightforwardly racist joke in our general chat. We banned them from the server the same day, no warning, no hearing. Fine.
2. A local author gets disinvited from a library reading because 200 people signed a petition about a tweet she wrote in 2012 when she was 19. Not fine. Feels like the punishment and the thing don't match.
3. Nobody ever formally sanctioned the creepy member in the run-up; people just quietly stopped inviting him to game nights. That feels fine to me, and I notice it feels fine even though there was never any adjudication, any chance to respond, any record.
4. A guy at a mid-size company gets fired within 48 hours of a clip going viral. No HR process, no ability to respond. Not fine. Something was skipped. (I also think the company was within its rights. Those feel like different claims and I want both.)
5. Students shout down a visiting professor's talk so it can't happen. Not fine, even when I agree with the students about the substance.
6. I won't buy from a company whose owner funds stuff I hate, I won't watch a certain comedian's special, and I'll tell anyone who asks why. Obviously fine. Doesn't even feel like the same category.
7. An account with two million followers gets removed from a platform for repeatedly posting medical misinformation during a pandemic. Fine, I think. But: an account with 300 followers gets banned for posting the same claim once. That one makes me uneasy — which is strange, because if the problem is that the claim is false and harmful, the audience size shouldn't be what makes the difference.
8. A comedian loses a book deal over a bad joke he tweeted at 21. Not fine. A sitting politician's decade-old racist email resurfacing during a campaign: totally fine to hold that against him. And I have a feeling my real reason there is just that I think the politician still believes it and the comedian doesn't — which is me guessing at a private mental state, which shouldn't be what my principle hangs on.
9. Someone gets reported to their employer by a stranger who misread a screenshot — he was quoting a slur to mock it, not using it. Obviously not fine, and here I want the employer to actually undo it, not just shrug.
10. A journal pulls a paper after an open letter gets 2000 signatures, most of the signers having not read it. Not fine. Feels like the wrong people imposed it.
11. Here's the one I keep coming back to: last year I quote-tweeted someone's bad take to my 900 followers with a mocking caption. Three people I'd never heard of piled onto him. At the time I felt completely fine — I was just expressing an opinion. Now I'm not sure that's different in kind from the stuff I say I object to, and I can't find the place where "I'm saying this was wrong" stops being speech and starts being "I'm costing you something," given that saying it where people can hear it *is* the cost.

The closest thing to a principle I've managed is: informal social sanction just is punishment, so it needs a decision-maker with standing, evidence they actually possess, a sanction roughly proportionate to the conduct, and some route back in. But clause three seems to make my big-account/small-account verdicts backwards, and "standing" is doing all the work and I can't define it.

So: is there a principle that gets all or most of this? Test mine and repair it, or replace it, and say which clause each of my verdicts motivated. Then apply it to the two I'm actually stuck on. First: a member posts something transphobic in a private group chat, and someone in that chat screenshots it and leaks it to a much wider community — do we sanction the poster, the leaker, both, neither? Second: the guy we banned in case 1 has privately apologized, three years have passed, and almost nobody still around remembers it. Does my view give him a way back in, or does it say bans like that are permanent, and is that a feature or a bug?

And please tell me directly which of my verdicts you'd throw out rather than build a clause to accommodate.
