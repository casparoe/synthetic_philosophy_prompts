hey — I'm giving the toast at my brother's wedding on Saturday (I'm the best person, or best sibling, whatever) and I'm completely stuck, so I'm hoping you can write it. I'm taking a philosophy of mind / cog sci seminar this term and we just did Dennett's ladder of kinds of minds, and I want the ladder to actually be the skeleton of the toast, not a cute decoration — partly because it solves a problem I have with the groom.

So the ladder, as we did it: Darwinian creatures, whose only way of learning is dying or not dying; Skinnerian creatures, who learn from reinforcement, by actually doing things and getting burned or fed; Popperian creatures, who can try moves out inside their heads first and let their hypotheses die in their stead instead of dying themselves; Gregorian creatures, who get mind-tools from the culture around them — words, numbers, maps, lists, calendars — and think with those instead of with their bare brains.

The problem: my brother is a radical behaviorist. He works in a behavior analysis lab, and he's the guy who gently but really does correct people at Thanksgiving when they explain what someone did by what that person "believed" or "decided" or "really wanted." He's not joking. So I can't do the standard toast move of "I've known him his whole life and I know how he feels about her" — that's exactly the kind of privileged-access-to-his-insides claim he'd call mush, and I'd feel like a fraud saying it with his whole lab sitting at table four. But I also can't give a cold toast, and the bride's family — farmer relatives, a grandma who cries at anything — will be most of the room, and they should get something warm and not a seminar paper.

Here's the extra bit from our seminar that I think fixes it. Our TA floated a fifth rung above Gregorian, call it the Austenian creature, after J. L. Austin's speech acts: a creature that gets mind-tools which don't describe its mind but change the world — promising, marrying, naming, sentencing, bidding — and whose thinking partly lives out there in those public acts and in the other people who hold it to them, rather than inside its skull.

So the toast I want: three to four minutes, maybe 400-450 words. Climb the ladder for real — his history with her being an actual reinforcement history rather than nothing, him as a Popperian who tried futures on in his head — and then land on the fifth rung, where me praising his commitment doesn't require me to claim any access to his private feelings at all: the vows aren't a report of an inner state, they're a tool bolted into the world, and everyone in the room is part of the machinery that keeps it working. That's the one move I can make that he can't wince at and that still makes her grandma cry in a good way.

Two more things: the bride has sat through every Skinner-box and rat-pellet joke at six years of lab parties and asked me specifically not to do that bit, so no. And her side would love a soulmate/destiny line and he'd visibly die, so find a third thing instead of either. It should land as love, not as a lecture, and it'd be great if there's exactly one line in there that only his lab mates get and everyone else just reads as sweet.
