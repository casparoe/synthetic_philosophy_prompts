I host a small podcast on philosophy of mind — audience is mostly grad students, so they already know the color phi setup (green flash at one spot, red flash a bit later at another, subject reports a single dot moving across and changing color somewhere around the midpoint, apparently before the second flash has even happened). No need to have her walk through the basics.

Next week's guest — let's call her Prof. R. Vasquez — defends what she calls "content-only timing." Experiences aren't located at times at all, on her view; they just have temporal content, i.e. they represent things as happening at times. So the Orwellian vs. Stalinesque dispute — did you consciously experience red at the midpoint before the second flash, or was your memory rewritten afterward? — is for her a badly formed question rather than a hard empirical one. She insists this isn't the usual Dennett-ish shrug that there's no fact of the matter; she thinks there's a positive story to tell, it's just a story about content.

Draft me eight to ten interview questions. Constraints:

- Her two stock deflections are "you're helping yourself to a dated experience" and "reports are data, there's nothing more to be said about when." I want the sequence to make both progressively harder to reach for, so that by the last third she has to say something constructive.
- At least one question on what the datum even is for her, if experiences aren't dated. Include a follow-up keyed to the answer I expect (that the datum is just what the subject's experience represents), pushing on whether the Orwell/Stalin fight just reappears one level down, as a dispute about which representation got tokened when.
- At least one on a version of the experiment where the color of the second flash isn't fixed until after the midpoint (coin flip, timestamped). Intuitively there couldn't have been a red-experience at the midpoint whose content wasn't settled yet — so doesn't her view have to concede something to the Stalinesque side? Anticipate her reply and follow up on it.
- At least one built on a design I made up: run color phi but also collect a speeded green/red discrimination via button press right around the midpoint, and separately a delayed retrospective report, and see whether the two ever come apart. What would each pattern of results show for her? Follow up on why the speeded press is or isn't a better guide to the timing of experience than the later verbal report.
- One on whether her view is secretly just the Stalinesque horn with "experience" swapped for "content" — is "the experience now represents red as having been at the midpoint" really different from "memory was revised"?
- One on whether she owes an error theory for the intuition that there's something it's like right now, and for the unity of the stream over time: if nothing is located at a time, what makes a run of experiences one stream rather than a pile?
- End with one question about what evidence could count against her: if we had an independently validated neural marker of red-at-the-midpoint content present at the midpoint time, would that settle anything, and if not, what would?

Keep the questions spoken, fairly short, and phrased so she can't just restate the view. Don't have her define terms for the audience, and don't add anything about how she got into the topic.
