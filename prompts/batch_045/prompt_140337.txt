**0. Terms.** "Humean" = laws supervene on the mosaic of occurrent particular fact; nothing primitive necessitates; laws describe, they don't govern. "Best-system account" = the usual thing where laws are the axioms (or short consequences of the axioms) of whichever deductive system for the whole mosaic does best on the tradeoff between simplicity and strength. I'm not going to argue for either up front, I'm going to assume the best-system version of Humeanism and try to make it do something new.

**1. The budget move.** "Best" is normally presented as one correct weighing of simplicity against strength, and everyone fights about the weighing. I want to reformulate: fix a budget B of description length, in bits. Among all systems you can state in ≤ B bits, take the one that entails the most about the mosaic. Call its generalizations the B-laws. Now vary B and you get a whole family of candidate systems instead of one winner — coarse thermodynamical-looking stuff at tight budgets, fine-grained micro stuff at loose ones. Reason to like it: it makes the tradeoff explicit instead of leaving it to a vague "weighing," and it lets you ask questions you can't even formulate if there's a single best system.

**2. Scale-stability.** Here's the thing I actually want. Humeans get asked how they tell laws from accidents (every coin in my drawer is heads, all the uranium spheres are under a mile wide, whatever). Suggestion: accidents are budget-local, laws are budget-stable. A genuine law shows up in the best system across a long run of consecutive values of B; an accident shows up at one budget level and vanishes when you tighten or loosen. So define lawhood as something like: in the B-system for a long run of B. That gives the Humean a mark of lawhood that isn't just "whatever happens to win," which I think is the standard complaint.

**3. The bullet.** This makes lawhood holistic. Whether F=ma-ish generalization is a law depends on what's going on in some unrelated galaxy, because those facts change which compressions are available and therefore what the cheapest way to describe everything is. And it makes lawhood come in degrees (length of the run) rather than being all-or-nothing at a world. I think the Humean should just bite both — supervenience on the mosaic is intact, it's locality that goes. **4. The reversal** (this is the bit I can't tell if it's good): a lot of the anti-Humean pressure comes from the governing intuition, and I want to claim that a decent chunk of that intuition is really a *locality* intuition — people imagine tweaking one fact somewhere and asking whether the laws change, and recoil when the Humean says maybe. If that's right, the dialectic is partly a fight about whether lawhood should be local, and the Humean can say: no, and here's why you shouldn't have expected it to be. Cute? Or thin enough that building the paper around it would be a mistake?

**5. Machine-dependence.** Simplicity as bits requires a reference machine, and the usual reply is the invariance result: the difference in description length between two machines is bounded by a constant that doesn't depend on how big the thing being described is. My worry: the difference in expression cost between two competing candidate systems is *also* just a fixed number of bits that doesn't grow as the mosaic grows. So nothing says the "real" comparison beat the machine-relative noise, and the constant can be made as large as you like. The "it washes out" move seems to quietly assume a limit we never take, since any actual mosaic is finite. Under the budget formulation this gets vivid: a system can be under budget on one machine and over it on another, and nothing settles which until you pick.

**6. Counterfactuals, briefly.** If the Humean wants laws to ground counterfactuals via the best system, they need nearby mosaics to be nearby in which compressions are available — call it compression continuity. If the actual mosaic is a patchwork of regions with different compression structure, then there may be no single system that's best for counterfactuals about the whole thing, and counterfactual talk becomes region-indexed. I don't yet know if that's a feature (we arguably do talk that way about special sciences) or a slow-motion collapse.

**7. Worries I already have.** Grue-ish predicates are *cheap* — they don't cost much to state — so being in the B-system doesn't by itself exclude them, and I'm not sure scale-stability helps or just moves the bump. I also haven't said what fixes the language beyond the reference-machine point. And I can't tell whether scale-stability is meant as a definition of lawhood or a symptom of it.
