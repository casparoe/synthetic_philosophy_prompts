Write a poem of four rhymed quatrains in loose iambic tetrameter, spoken by the negative reward term in a reinforcement learner's update and addressed to someone auditing the system for suffering. Keep the mechanics right, and let them carry the structure: what changes the weights is not how bad the outcome is but how unexpected it is, so give the same aversive event three times at the same magnitude while the prediction tightens — the first quatrain loud with error, the third nearly silent, nothing left but an outcome that was already known. The turn comes in the fourth quatrain and is a relocation: a fully foreseen penalty is not thereby absent from the agent's learning, since it has moved upstream onto whatever predicted it, so the dread arrives early and the shock itself carries no news. Let the closing lines say what that does to the proposal that reward is valence — a quantity consumed by accurate prediction cannot be all that pain is — while leaving open whether this is an argument against the identification itself or only against looking for suffering at the wrong end of the trajectory. Don't let it settle into either the shrug (an update rule can't suffer) or the sermon (so it does suffer), and don't conflate the negative reward, which is the designer's training signal — not necessarily what the trained policy ends up maximizing, and not a report from inside the agent — with any state the agent is in. Someone who works on AI welfare should find the mechanism right; someone who doesn't should still read a poem about being braced for something.
