I had a long argument with a friend last night about Goodman's new riddle of induction, and I am still not sure who won. Her position was that grue is only a puzzle because Goodman smuggles a date into the definition of the predicate: once you notice that, the whole thing is a parlour trick with no bearing on how working scientists actually project. My reply was that you can reproduce the puzzle with no reference to time at all, and that the interesting version is one where the awkward predicate is one a real research community would have had good methodological reasons to adopt rather than a disjunction invented to cause trouble. We finally located the disagreement: she says no case can be built in which Goodman's own entrenchment answer (project the predicate with the stronger track record of past projections) and the naturalness or objective-resemblance answer (project the predicate that carves nature at the joints) come apart, except by rigging the set-up. I say such a case exists and is worth putting in front of students.

Please build it. I want a single-paragraph scenario, realistic — a laboratory or a field science, no emeralds, no fixed date, no sceptical or Cartesian machinery — in which a community holds data compatible with two rival generalisations that diverge on some prediction checkable in principle, and in which the awkward, grue-like predicate is one that community could plausibly have adopted for good reasons: it is what an instrument directly reports, or what some practical or theoretical purpose of theirs makes salient. The verdicts must fall out of the two responses rather than be written into the scenario, and the case should not be settled merely by observing that one predicate is disjunctive in form, since plenty of perfectly projectible scientific predicates are.

Then show that the entrenchment response and the naturalness response license projecting different predicates, and why; name the structural feature doing the work; say what the case degenerates into if that feature is removed (a purely verbal dispute, or ordinary grue again); and give the strongest line of attack for whichever response comes out worse. It is for a seminar handout.

Finish with a table, columns: Response | Predicate it licenses projecting | Feature of the case it keys on | Strongest objection available to it.
