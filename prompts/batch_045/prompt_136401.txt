My wife and I are trying to decide whether I should leave a secure job to start a small business with a friend, and she had me try a decision method she'd read about: picture the version of me a year from now who knows every fact I could find out, has slept, is not frightened, has had a week to think about nothing else, and is also supposed to know what it actually feels like to have failed or to have succeeded; then ask that person what to do. His answer, she says, just is what is good for me, as opposed to what I merely feel like doing.

What bothers me is that the answer flips depending on how I set him up. If I imagine him keeping my present attachments — the mortgage, the kids' schools, how much I would hate letting people down — he says stay. If I also let him take on whatever I would care about after the decision, no longer frightened because the thing is already settled, he says go. My wife thinks the second is the honest version and that the first just smuggles in my fear. But the second one seems to have stopped being me, and the person who actually has to act on the advice is the frightened one, not the serene one.

Is there a principled way to tell which version of the method is the right one, or is the method really just a machine for returning whichever answer you build into it? And if the serene version does have the right answer, what makes it authoritative for me now?
