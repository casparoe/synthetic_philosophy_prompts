My buddy and I got into a three-hour argument last night and I can't shake it. We were talking about that AI safety proposal where you get two systems to argue in front of a human judge who can't check everything, and the judge just picks whichever one sounds more convincing. He says truth has a built-in edge: the liar has to keep inventing new stuff, the honest side just points at what's already there. I think that's backwards, because of something I keep running into in totally ordinary arguments.

Here's the pattern. The more exactly right your claim is, the more claims you've made. I tell my manager "the deploy broke checkout for people with saved cards on Safari, it was about 3% of sessions, and it was fixed in forty minutes," and what she hears is "so the deploy broke checkout." My friend tells me the car I'm looking at has a transmission problem; I say "true for the 2013-2015 automatics, the manuals are fine, and there was a recall fix," and he says "so you admit it's a problem." Nobody lied, but the accurate version lost, and it lost because every qualifier I added was another thing the other person could demand I back up or just quietly drop. Being precise doesn't shrink what you're on the hook for, it multiplies it. And the lying version isn't cheap because lies are cheap to make — its cheap because it only has one surface to check.

Is there a name for this? Everything I poke around at is about the opposite direction: the thing where one side floods the zone with bogus claims so you can't rebut them all. This is different — the honest side isn't overwhelmed by volume, it's penalized for accuracy, and giving the judge unlimited time doesn't obviously fix it, since the budget seems to be per-claim rather than per-argument. I don't just want a label. I want to know what exactly this is, what it isn't, and where its edges are: does it depend on the audience being lazy, does it survive a careful and well-informed judge, does it still bite if the judge is allowed to ask questions, and is it the same mechanism when the qualifiers are true vs. when they're just hedges. If nobody's named it, name it and define it so it's clearly distinct from the nearby things. And if it's real, does it actually put a hole in the debate setup, or does the setup dodge it somehow?

Also tell me how confident you are and what would change your mind — which consideration would flip you, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
