Writing the last slide of my grad seminar on modal metaphysics — we've spent two weeks on Lewis and then a week on the standard Kripkean complaints, and the room is split pretty evenly between people who think the whole view is one big reductio and people who think that line of his about not being able to answer an incredulous stare is a cop-out. I want to close with a joke.

The bit I want the joke to turn on is my own variant, which I've been calling cheap modal realism: the worlds are concrete, isolated, and plentiful exactly as Lewis says, but there's no fact of the matter about how many worlds there are full stop — the total varies from world to world, so plenitude is a local principle rather than a global one. Take it to the limit and there are worlds that are, as it were, alone: worlds in which there is exactly one world, and in which everything true is therefore necessary. I like it because it turns "how much possibility is there?" into an ordinary contingent question instead of a choice of framework, and I think it takes the sting out of the why-is-this-world-the-actual-one worry (actual is still indexical, there's just no fixed number of worlds for the indexical to range over) — but that last move is the part I'm least confident about.

So: give me a joke whose punchline actually turns on cheap modal realism, and specifically on the number of worlds being itself a contingent, world-relative matter — not a generic possible-worlds gag, and not one that would work just as well for regular Lewis. It should be tellable in one breath at the end of a lecture and it has to survive people who know this material: nobody in the room should come away unsure whether I've got indexicality and counterpart theory straight.

After the joke, a short note: where it takes liberties (flag it if the punchline quietly needs something Lewis would reject, or smuggles in actualism), and, for my own calibration, which parts of Lewis orthodoxy are settled doctrine among people who work on this — actuality as indexical, no transworld identity, the isolation of worlds — versus genuinely contested, and don't let the note make variable-number-of-worlds sound like anything close to a consensus view. Then close the whole thing with one last line in the form \answer{the joke in a single sentence}.
