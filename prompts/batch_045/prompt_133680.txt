I sit on my university's research-integrity committee, which adjudicates charges of misrepresentation against faculty. Our policy defines misrepresentation as "stating or otherwise conveying a materially false or misleading account of fact," and nearly every case turns on the words "or otherwise conveying." Our rulings from the last two years:

1. A paper reported that "all twelve pre-registered hypotheses were tested"; one had in fact been tested with a different measure than the pre-registered one, disclosed in a footnote. No violation.
2. A PI told a collaborator, "I ran the analysis we agreed on," having run it on a superseded dataset that he knew she would assume was not in play. Violation.
3. A grant report stated, "We have not observed the effect in male mice," when male mice had never been tested; the committee found that he knew the funder would read this as a failed test rather than an unperformed one. Violation.
4. An author replied to a reviewer, "We thank the reviewer for this suggestion," and then did not act on it. No violation.
5. Asked by her department chair whether she had drawn on the department's shared subject pool, a faculty member answered, "Participants were recruited through the university's online system" — true, but the participants came from a commercial panel and the online system carried only the advertisement. Violation.

The case before us now: a program officer wrote to a PI asking, "Were any participants withdrawn for safety reasons?" He replied in writing, "Three participants discontinued the study early." In fact two of the three were discontinued after adverse events that a local investigator judged possibly related to the study drug; this appears in an appendix to the annual report already in the officer's possession, in a table that never uses the words "safety" or "withdrawal" and codes the reason as "investigator decision." The PI maintains that he did not want to raise an alarm in an email, expected the officer to consult the report, and would have confirmed the safety withdrawals had he been asked again.

Draft the recommendation I will circulate. I want: the finding we should reach if we are to be consistent with ourselves; which of the five precedents is the closest analog and why; and, because I suspect our record is equally consistent with several different rules — that only takeaways the speaker intended count, that any false takeaway a competent reader would foreseeably draw counts, that a literally true statement is excused when the speaker's own materials let the reader recover the truth, and that a reply must address the question actually asked — an explicit statement of which rule each possible finding commits us to, and of the one further variation on these facts that would best separate the rules still in play. Begin with the recommended finding in your first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble.
