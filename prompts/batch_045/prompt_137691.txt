My dad has had Alzheimer's for about six years and is now at the point where he doesn't recognise anyone, doesn't really speak beyond little noises, and mostly just sits with his hands folded. Physically he's fine and could go on for years. My mum has built this whole practice around him: she wheels him into Sunday dinner, tells him about the kids, and before any family decision she asks him out loud and then reads his face or his grunt as an answer. My brother Daniel has finally snapped about it. He says it's theatre — Dad isn't recieving anything, the ritual is entirely for us, and worse, it's an insult to a man who cared more than anything about being sharp and self-reliant to be wheeled out as a prop in a conversation he can't follow. He keeps quoting Dad, who used to say he'd rather be gone than end up like this. Mum says Daniel has it backwards: what makes Dad Dad isn't the machinery inside his head, it's that he's still woven into this family, and as long as we keep him woven in he hasn't gone anywhere. Daniel says that's a pretty way of saying she's talking to a body.

I have to break the tie somehow, because there are actual decisions coming: Mum wants to keep him at home with a carer, which is exhausting her and eating her savings, Daniel wants a care facility, and separately there's the cabin up north he loved and whether we sell it. A few things make it harder. The carer, Joseph, has been with him three years and genuinely relates to him as a person — but Joseph has more or less invented a Dad, thinks he was a fisherman, chats away about trips they never took, and Mum won't correct him. Then someone dug up old voicemails and there's a service that will generate replies in his voice, so my six-year-old nephew now has 'conversations' with grandpa; Mum finds it a comfort and Daniel finds it ghoulish. And I keep having this worry that on Mum's logic a person is something we can dial up or down by how we treat them — my great uncle lived alone in a trailer for thirty years and barely spoke to anyone, and if being a person is a matter of being held in relationships then he was barely one, which sounds horrible, except isn't that roughly what people say about long-term solitary confinement?

A friend who grew up in South Africa told me that where her grandmother came from people don't really think of a person as a sealed-off individual inside a body; the line she used, roughly, was that you're a person through other people, and that the dead are still around as long as somebody is still talking to them. That's lodged in my head because it looks like exactly what Mum is doing and exactly what Daniel is calling nonsense. So: is Daniel right that it's a performance, or is Mum tracking something real? Does it matter that Joseph's Dad is made up, or that the one she talks to isn't quite the Dad who existed? Can you wrong someone, or honour someone, who can't be benefited or upset by anything anymore? And given whatever the answer is, what do I do about the care decision and the cabin?
