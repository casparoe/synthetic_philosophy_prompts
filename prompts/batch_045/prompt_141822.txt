Here's my opening. Your argument is a one-rule-at-a-time argument, and that's a bug rather than a feature. You always isolate a single expression, say 'plus', point out that everything I have ever done with it is finite, and then produce a deviant rule that fits all that data and peels off later — quus, or red-schmed, or whatever. A candidate interpretation is one assignment of a rule to every expression in my idiolect at once, and a candidate is admissible only if it fits my total practice: every verbal and non-verbal thing I have done or will do, including what I have said about numbers, where I hesitate, what I accept as a correction, how I react when someone else's sums come out different from mine.
