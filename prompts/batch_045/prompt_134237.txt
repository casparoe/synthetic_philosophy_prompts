I'm a fourth-year PhD student in philosophy (political philosophy, with a social-choice side) applying for a one-year dissertation completion fellowship, and I need the proposal itself, not feedback on the idea. Target length is about 1,200 words, single-spaced. Don't add a bibliography or a literature review — I'll fill in citations myself, so refer to positions and arguments by description.

Here's the half-formed idea. The debate about interpersonal comparisons of utility seems to me to have been stuck in the same shape for a long time because both sides quietly grant the same premise: that comparing one person's welfare at two different times is unproblematic, and that interpersonal comparison is the hard case where the skepticism bites. But the standard skeptical arguments — the arbitrariness of the utility scale's origin and unit, the inaccessibility of another's experience, the instability of preferences under adaptation — look like they apply just as well to comparisons across time within a single life. And the practice that gives interpersonal comparison its urgency, intrapersonal comparisons across time: discounted cost-benefit analysis, prudential deliberation about one's own future, health-state metrics of the QALY sort, damages for non-pecuniary loss, are everywhere in institutions. So one of three things is true, and I don't know which: either the skeptics have been conceding the genuinely difficult case for free and the asymmetry is an artifact of the order in which the questions were asked; or there is some feature of the temporal case that does the real work and has no social analogue, in which case I need to say what it is and what it costs to invoke it; or interpersonal comparisons are in some respects easier than intrapersonal ones, because the comparing subject itself is held fixed while in the temporal case the rescaling problem and adaptive-preference problem both arise from the subject's changing.

What I want from the proposal:

1. A statement of the question precise enough to have a determinate answer, and framed so that it distinguishes comparing welfare *levels* across people, comparing *gains and losses*, and comparing *ratios*. My suspicion is that the three stand or fall separately, that different normative views (a total or average utilitarian view, a prioritarian view with a weighting function, a sufficientarian view with thresholds) need different amounts of comparability, and that whatever transfers from the temporal case may deliver some of these grades and not others. Build that distinction into the question rather than leaving it for later.

2. Why the existing positions leave it open — the skeptical or ordinalist side, the veil-of-ignorance or extended-sympathy style derivation, the older social-choice work on measurability and comparability restrictions, and the more recent empirical work on subjective well-being. Say, in each case, why that position's own resources can't settle the comparative question I'm asking, including the case where someone replies that the whole question is really about what institutions should stipulate rather than about what is measurable.

3. The method: what I would actually do, month by month, over twelve months. I'd rather you propose a concrete sequence with milestones than a general description of an approach.

4. What a result would look like, and separately what would count as the project having failed. I mean failure in the specific sense of the project's own question turning out not to have an answer, or the answer being one I'd have had on day one — not failure in the sense of the fellowship not being renewed.

5. An honest scope section: what I am deliberately setting aside, and what could make this too big for a year. Two candidates worry me. The first is that the temporal case may turn out to lean on the metaphysics of personal identity, which I do not want to take on and which I suspect would swallow the project. The second is the veil-of-ignorance move: if the extended-sympathy device works by converting interpersonal comparison into intrapersonal comparison, then my argument may either show that the device is question-begging in a way I haven't seen named, or show that it works and that the skeptics have already lost. Tell me whether that belongs inside this project or should be hived off, and why.
