I ended up on this volunteer public-safety advisory thing for our city (I have no background in any of this, I've just been reading random stuff on my own after getting dragged in), and two people on the committee have been going at each other for about a year and finally agreed to do an adversarial collaboration instead of another shouting match. One of them is basically a reform/defund person: keep a much smaller number of sworn armed officers, strip them of traffic, mental-health calls, school presence and so on, move that money into unarmed response teams, housing, clinics. The other is an abolitionist: no body should have a general standing mandate to use force against the public, and the goal isn't a nicer police department but a different arrangement entirely. I've been asked to write up the document and I have no idea how to structure it, so: draft the joint statement both of them could sign.

To keep it from floating off into abstractions, put it in a made-up city — call it Bell Harbor, 380,000 people, racially and economically mixed — and stipulate that both sides accept the following for the sake of the document: only a small fraction of 911 calls involve violence or a weapon; response times and clearance rates for serious violent crime are what people are most afraid of; most of what officers actually do hour-to-hour is non-violent; and any replacement institution would still have to deal with people who are armed, violent, or in crisis and refusing help. Then give me a numbered section of claims both of them can genuinely sign, and a table of the disagreements where each row is phrased in the wording that side would accept for its own view. My guess is that at least one row is only about words, and if so say which and why.

Two things I want in there specifically, because these are the ones I cant get them to stop circling. First, the extension question: involuntary psychiatric holds, taking a kid out of a home, enforcing an eviction — all of those are the state using force or the threat of it on someone who says no. Does the abolitionist's argument reach those too, or does it stop at the uniform, and does the reformist think the state's monopoly thing is fine everywhere except patrol? I've started calling this "the violent-envelope problem": if some agency can lawfully detain you when you refuse, it has a mandate to use force no matter what you call it. Second, the 3am question: whatever you build, somebody has to show up at 3am. For each real crux, give me a concrete case or piece of evidence both would agree bears on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts it will show. Please dont give me citations, reading lists, or study names — nobody on this committee is going to look anything up, and I want the whole thing to work from the stipulated facts plus argument. Somewhere around 900-1200 words.
