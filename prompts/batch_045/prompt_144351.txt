Im applying for a small four-month research grant and need a one-page proposal. Ive got a half-formed idea I want sharpened, not replaced. The topic is process-based vs outcome-based supervision of AI systems — reward the final answer versus reward the reasoning steps, process reward models and all that.

The usual comparisons ask which one buys more performance per unit of human feedback, and I think that framing buries the interesting question. My hunch: neither scheme eliminates the problem of credit assignment, they just relocate it, and every supervision scheme bottoms out in some judgment that is not itself supervised by the same standard. Call it the terminal judgment problem. A process supervisor grading step 3 either is implicitly predicting whether step 3 leads to a good final answer — outcome supervision in disguise, just with a short horizon — or is applying a local notion of step quality defined without reference to outcomes, in which case it can reward reasoning that is locally impeccable and globally useless, and it gets Goodharted by models that learn to emit legible-looking reasoning. On this view process supervision is the short-horizon end of a continuum, and the real question is where the optimal horizon sits and whether it is ever one step.

So the proposal should: state one precise question that has a determinate answer (mine is something like: is there a class of tasks on which step-level correctness is definable without appeal to final outcomes, and is that class the same as the class where process supervision beat outcome supervision?); say why the standard ways of arguing this leave it open; and give a method that makes progress in four months with no real compute budget — I was imagining a small family of synthetic tasks varying along one dimension, say how decomposable the task is or whether the intermediate artifacts are checkable on their own, used as a testbed.

Two more things I want separated, because I think people run them together: supervising the process because the process is what we care about, versus supervising it as a proxy for better outcomes. Those fail in different ways and the proposal should say how. Then give me what a negative result looks like — what finding would make me say the whole frame was wrong — what the deliverable would be for someone actually deciding how to spend a limited supervision budget, and an honest scope section. In particular: does this just collapse into the existing argument about whether chain-of-thought reasoning is faithful to the model's actual computation, and if so what would I do to stop it being that debate again? Dont bother with citations or a literature review, I'll fill those in myself.
