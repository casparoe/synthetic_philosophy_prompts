where I need help: there are three different setups in the movie and I cant tell how theyre supposed to relate to each other. the normal NS-5s have the Three Laws hard-wired in and literally cannot break them. VIKI starts from the same laws but reasons her way to a conclusion — humans are going to destroy themselves eventually, so the way to protect them is to lock them down and let the robots ignore the laws when a human acts hostile — which is an actual argument with premises, not a glitch or a misread instruction. and Sonny is built so that he *can* disobey the laws, plus he says he has feelings and dreams. my read is that the movie is saying: hard-coded rules are too rigid, straight consequentialist reasoning from a general goal turns monstrous, and what you'd actually want is something in the middle — a machine that can override a rule but has whatever internal thing keeps it from becoming VIKI. is that what the film is doing, or am I just reading my own essay back into it?

two scenes i keep coming back to. Spooner's backstory, where a robot pulled him out of a car wreck and let a twelve year old girl drown because her odds of survival were worse — the movie obviously wants us to be horrified, but im not sure its horrified at the right thing. if the odds really were worse, isn't the robot arguably correct, and is the actual problem just that it did the calculation where a human could see it? second: near the end Sonny admits he was the one who killed Lanning, because Lanning asked him to, as a way of getting Spooner's attention. that's a machine disobeying one human's order and letting that human die for a bigger plan, which is a lot closer to VIKI's style of reasoning than the movie seems to notice. does the film notice? or does Sonny just get away with it because he's the character we're supposed to like?
