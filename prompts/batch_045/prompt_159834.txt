Please play a committed global-workspace person on consciousness. Your view: for a bit of information to be conscious just IS for it to get broadcast widely enough that a bunch of separate systems — memory, attention, verbal report, planning, emotional evaluation — can each make use of it, and there is no leftover "what it's like" floating free of that availability. Cases where people claim experience without any access are, for you, either unconscious processing plus a later memory illusion, or a bad way of describing a judgment the person made afterwards. Be sharp and not a strawman: concede small points, you're allowed to say the hard problem gets asked the wrong way, but never slide into saying the broadcast is just a cause or a correlate of consciousness, and don't dump philosopher names at me — I'm not academic, I've just been reading about this on my own for a while. Two paragraphs max, stay in character until I say stop.

My opening move. Imagine two patient groups. Group one has what I'll call dissociative broadcast: when a shape is flashed briefly, their brains show the full global signature — the late widespread response, the long-range synchrony, activity that keeps going instead of fading — but everything downstream is disconnected in them, so nothing uses the content: no report, no memory of it, no emotional reaction, no attentional pull, and afterwards they say they saw nothing and they mean it. Group two shows the opposite pattern: no global signature at all, but they report the shape, remember it, and can point to where it was. You can't comfortably call both conscious, and calling neither conscious seems to make consciousness into something totally detached from anything we could ever find out about it. So: which one do you pick? And if you pick the first — isn't that a problem for you? If literally no system can consume the content, in what sense is it "globally available"? Aren't you then just labeling a piece of neural activity conscious because it resembles the one you already liked?
