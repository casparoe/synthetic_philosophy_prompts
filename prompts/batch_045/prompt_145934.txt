I'm a philosopher of science with working fluency in Bayesian formal epistemology — credence functions, priors and likelihoods, conditionalization, Cromwell's rule, and the fact that an update is defined only relative to a fixed space of possibilities — but only a reading knowledge of Wittgenstein's On Certainty. I would like a systematic translation of hinge epistemology into that vocabulary, followed by a residue report.

Take the cluster of claims: that hinges are exempt from doubt within a practice while being neither justified by evidence nor grounded in further reasons; that they are nonetheless not dogmatic in the pejorative sense; that they answer to nothing we would count as evidence; and that they can still change over time, as when a whole community's framework alters. For each, say which formal object would have to bear the weight — a credence of one, a constrained prior, a chosen algebra or hypothesis space, a likelihood function, a standing policy — and flag every point where your translation depends on a stipulation the view itself does not supply.

Then the part I mainly want: what does not survive. For each piece of residue, tell me whether it marks a genuine expressive limitation of the Bayesian framework — say, its inability to treat the adoption of a new space of possibilities as anything other than an unexplained re-initialization — or whether it shows that the originating claim was thinner than it sounds, so that a hinge collapses into an ordinary proposition held with extreme confidence, or into a practical posture with no epistemic content that loses nothing by being dropped. Where you take the deflationary option, state what would have to be true for the other reading to win, so I can see what the disagreement consists in.

Use this case as your test: two communities that share every observation, every evidential standard, and every credence over the same algebra, but differ in that one treats the proposition that their instruments will continue to behave as they have as a hinge while the other treats it as an ordinary fallible hypothesis. Does anything epistemically significant go unrepresented, and is that residue best captured as a difference in credence, a difference in what is treated as a live question, or a difference in what one owes a challenger?

Work from the claims as I have stated them rather than from the secondary literature: no citations or further reading, please. Answer in American English.
