hey — I help run a small informal reading group in our maths/CS department; we formalise a lot of boring analysis in Lean and absolutely nobody there knows any set theory beyond the barber story. Last night we got into a silly fight about whether dependent type theory actually *solves* Russell's paradox or just refuses to ask the question, and it turned out none of us could say what the real menu of options even is. So I want to make a handout that is basically a flowchart: you start at "ok, the collection of all collections that aren't members of themselves can't exist — now what?", and you end at a position, with every branch being a decision somebody actually has to make.

What I'd like: build the tree out of *licencing rules for forming collections*, not out of a list of theory names — first the questions and the space of answer-combinations they allow, then the real positions dropped into that space, then the empty cells named as empty with a one-line note on whether they're empty because incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Six to eight branch questions, eight to twelve leaves, plain indented text or numbered nodes, whatever's easiest to read. Every question has to be answerable by someone who has never heard the word "ZFC": phrase them as things like whether forming {x : φ(x)} is fine when x ranges over stuff you've already built but not over everything at once; whether a collection may contain itself; whether you want a collection of all collections; whether membership chains have to bottom out somewhere; whether you'd sooner give up part of classical logic than give up comprehension; and whether the fix is even about which collections exist rather then about which sentences are well-formed in the first place. Please make sure these leaves all show up: an iterative/cumulative picture, a two-sorted "some collections are too big to be members of anything" picture, a typed-or-stratified picture, a non-well-founded picture, a constructive/intuitionistic picture, and a keep-full-comprehension-and-learn-to-live-with-contradictions picture. Two extras I really want in: a branch for the negation-free cousin of the paradox (the one where the property is a conditional instead of a negation, so you get the same explosion without any step a dialetheist can block by fussing over negation), because I think it splits the contradiction-tolerant leaf in a way the original doesn't; and a home for one of our member's hand-rolled fix, where every property gets a "formation stage" and is only allowed to quantify over collections formed at strictly earlier stages — I can't tell whether that's just type theory wearing a hat or something that breaks, so tell me where it lands and what goes wrong with it if it does.

Every leaf should carry one sentence on what you lose, stated as something someone formalising maths would actually feel (this theorem becomes unstateable, you have to prove that existence fact by hand, you can't define that function by recursion, whatever). And please flag the pairs of positions your tree genuinely can't separate — I suspect all the two-sorted class theories collapse together and I wouldn't be surprised if the typed and stratified ones do too — rather than inventing a gerrymandered question just to pry them apart. If a cell is one nobody in fact defends, say so honestly instead of giving it a catchy name, and don't bother with reading lists or references, nobody in the group is going to chase them.
