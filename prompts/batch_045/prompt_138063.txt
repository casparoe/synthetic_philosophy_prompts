Can u write me a parody — a numbered FAQ/memo, about 600 words — from a university's "Office of Pedagogical Surprise," announcing that there will be a surprise exam next week.

The conceit: the Office has "solved" the problem by administrative means. The exam will be held on the first day d such that at 9 p.m. the previous evening no enrolled student knows that the exam is on d. The Office maintains, deadpan, that this clause defeats the students' backwards-induction argument, because the clause is a blindspot for them: anyone who came to know that it was satisfied would thereby make it unsatisfied.

I want the comedy to come from actually getting the epistemology right rather than from puns. Work in: the special status of the last possible day; the gap between "unannounced" and "unpredictable"; knowledge vs. high credence (the Office has an official surprise threshold, and it is embarassing); what is supposed to be going on in a student's head on Thursday evening; and why the Office treats the students' betting market as a compliance violation, given that shutting the market down seems to restore the surprise it destroyed.

Nobody in the document should say the word "paradox" or explain anything to the reader — keep it straight-faced and bureaucratic throughout, and end with the Office having extended the same clause to fire drils and to its own office hours.
