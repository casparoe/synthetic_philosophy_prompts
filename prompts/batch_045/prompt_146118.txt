I'm in a metaphysics reading group that just spent two sessions on dispositions, and my housemate — she does something with rheology, lots of cornstarch, don't ask — got interested and now claims she's proved that there are no dispositions at all. I've written her argument out as carefully as I can, and what's annoying is that each step looks fine to me individually while the conclusion is nuts.

(1) A predicate picks out a real property only if having it makes some difference to what the bearer does or would do. If two things differing only in fragility never differed in anything that happened, there'd be nothing for the property to be.
(2) The only work a disposition could possibly do is to bring about its manifestation: the sole contribution of being disposed to M in C is that the bearer Ms when C obtains. Dispositions are directed at their manifestations; that's all they are.
(3) For any disposition D to M in C, there's a possible setup in which x has D but C's obtaining would remove D. A live wire wired to a breaker that trips the instant you touch it: live, touched, no shock. So D's being present doesn't guarantee M.
(4) For any D there's also a possible setup in which x has D, D persists, and something else stops M anyway — the glass in bubble wrap. So even D-plus-C-plus-no-fink doesn't give you M.
(5) And the other direction is just as bad: a thing that lacks D can M in C for unrelated reasons. A chunk of something totally un-fragile with a detonator taped to it shatters when struck. So M doesn't even indicate D.
(6) The usual patch — x is disposed to M in C iff x would M if C obtained and D were retained and nothing interfered — doesn't help, because my group found cases where you can't separate the fink from the mask. Take a fusible link: a wire that conducts, but whose conducting (resistive heating) is what melts it when the current gets big. The disposition's own exercise destroys the disposition, and the destroying is what stops the manifestation. Is that a fink or a mask? Or take oobleck: it's a liquid, so it's disposed to deform under stress, and it jams rigid under impact, and the jamming comes from the very same suspended-particle microstructure that makes it a liquid in the first place. You can't delete the interference while holding the disposition fixed, and you can't hold the disposition fixed while deleting the interference. So the patched analysans is either false or circular.
(7) So for any disposition you like there's a possible situation where x has it, C obtains, and M doesn't happen, and a possible situation where x lacks it and M does, and the fix that's meant to handle that can't be stated without circularity. Which means whether x Ms in C is never settled by whether x has D. It's settled by everything else going on around it.
(8) So by (2) D does no work, and by (1) no disposition predicate picks out a real property. There are no dispositions. Fragility, conductivity, solubility are fine as shop talk, but nothing in the world answers to them — there's just the microstructure and what it does.

What makes me suspicious rather than convinced is that if this works I don't see why it doesn't work on causes: any cause can be stopped from producing its effect by something else, so on this reasoning causation picks out nothing real either, and ditto for laws of nature. My gut says something too cheap is happening when each disposition being finkable or maskable in some possible setup gets turned into its doing no work anywhere, but I can't name the step. Which step do you reject, why did it look fine, and what does rejecting it commit me to? If I have to choose I'd sooner give up (1) than end up an eliminativist about dispositions, but I want the price list for each option, including the bullet-biting one. And is there any version of realism about dispositions that can admit the standard patched conditional analysis is circular and still survive?
