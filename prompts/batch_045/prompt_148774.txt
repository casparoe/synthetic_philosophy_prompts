In the original case you're the only person around. I think thats a red herring and I want to hold something stronger: duties of rescue dont divide. Say five adults are standing at the edge of the same pond and any one of them could wade in at the same small cost. I want to say each of them fully owes it that the child doesn't drown — not one fifth of a duty each. I've been calling it the overdetermination view: the duty is discharged by the child's being saved, not by your doing your share, so if all five walk away each of them is fully blameworthy, not 20% blameworthy. And its not a duty to do the rescuing — what you owe is that the child not drown through your inaction, so if you cant swim but you can shout at the guy next to you and he jumps in, you've done nothing wrong.
