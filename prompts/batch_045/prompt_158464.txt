ok so I've been chewing on the Dennett vs. Caruso thing on and off for a couple years and I keep having the same feeling: they steelman into almost the same policy and then declare victory in opposite directions, which makes me suspect I've tabooed the wrong word. Quick version of my read, correct me if I've got either of them wrong: Caruso, free will skeptic, public health quarantine model — no basic desert (no "he deserves to suffer *because* he did wrong," as opposed to suffering being useful), so anything we do to an offender has to be justified the way you'd justify quarantine: by risk, by what actually helps, and if we're depriving someone for reasons that have nothing to do with what they merit, we owe them decent conditions and compensation. Dennett, compatibilist, also not a retributivist as far as I can tell, says sure the cosmic ledger is gone, but holding people responsible is a real practice worth keeping, it's partly what *makes* and maintains people as agents who respond to reasons, and punishment gets justified forward-looking while censure survives.

Here's the thing I want you to actually do work on. I have a test I've been using and I want to see if it survives contact with you: the tumor case. For any proposed replacement practice, ask whether you'd still run it on someone you knew for certain was not a responsible agent — the guy whose behavior was caused by a tumor, the sleepwalker, the person who was pushed. If yes, it's quarantine-shaped and Caruso can have it. If no, there's desert in there wearing a disguise, because the only thing the tumor case removes is desert and nothing forward-looking.

Rate these eight on 0–10 for how much desert is still load-bearing (0 = you'd do it to the tumor guy without hesitating, 10 = it collapses entirely without retributivism), run the tumor test on each, and put them in three tiers: clean for a skeptic, only available if you've got Dennett-ish compatibilist responsibility, or needs real desert. One clause of why each.

1. Actuarial quarantine — no fixed sentence, release when a risk model says you're safe, comfortable conditions, compensated for your time. Bonus question inside this one: the model is allowed to count "would probably reoffend *if not monitored*," monitoring is expensive, so poor monitoring capacity quietly becomes extra years. Does that count as desert, or is it just quarantine with a budget constraint?
2. Income-scaled day fines under strict liability — no mens rea inquiry at all, no blame finding, just a price.
3. Deterrence pricing — severity set by the elasticity of the marginal offender, including exemplary severity for rare-but-tempting crimes.
4. Bare censure — official finding of wrongdoing, public record, no hard treatment attached.
5. Restorative conferences — apology, agreed repair, compliance backed by the usual threat of escalation.
6. Dennett-style maintenance blame — we blame, we demand reasons, we expect revision, justified by the claim that being inside that practice is what grows people's capacity for self-control.
7. Reformative education whose success condition is that the offender genuinely accepts they were blameworthy.
8. Repair and voluntary treatment only; if you decline, you get exactly what a dangerous non-agent gets, nothing more.

Two more things. Give me a number: if the criminal system were rebuilt on the tier you'd actually defend, what fraction of the current US incarcerated population would still be locked up (0.3 = about a third)? Defend the number for one sentence. And tell me which item is the one where Dennett and Caruso agree but for opposite reasons, because I think that item is where the real disagreement is hiding and I can't tell which one it is. Also flag any item that cheats by relocating desert from the act up to the rule or the system — punishing nobody in particular but designing the institution so that somebody definately eats it.
