Here's the thing I keep noticing.

Thread one. He says over and over that he doesn't believe in a personal God — that a God who answers prayers, rewards and punishes, is a childish projection — and in a letter late in life he said the word God was for him only an expression of human weakness and the Bible a collection of primitive legends. And yet he won't call himself an atheist, he won't quite call himself a pantheist either when asked directly, and he keeps using the word constantly: God doesn't play dice, the Lord is subtle but not malicious, he wants to know God's thoughts. When he's pressed on what's religious about him, he says it's an unbounded admiration for the structure of the world so far as science can reveal it, and he calls this something like cosmic religion in one of those essay collections. Crucially, he doesn't want the practice retired. He says teachers of religion should give up the doctrine of a personal God and keep the rest.

Thread two. Same man says human beings have no free will, that everything that happens including what I do is fixed by natural law, and there's a line of Schopenhauer's he goes back to — you can do what you want, but you can't want what you want — though I've genuinely forgotten where he wrote it, so don't rest anything on the source. And then he spends decades writing public essays telling people how they ought to live, praising Gandhi, blaming militarists and nationalists, insisting that value judgements remain necessary and that science can ascertain what is but never what should be.

My friend says these are two unrelated things: the God business is just a sloppy way of talking about awe at the laws of nature, and the free will business is a plain inconsistency he never noticed. I said no — they're the same move in different clothes. Keep the vocabulary, the practice, the attitude, the ought-talk; drop the thing everyone says the practice is answerable to. Somebody must have a name for that. I half remember the word fictionalism being used for something like it, but that feels wrong, because he isn't winking at it: he seems to think the awe is the actual content and the belief was never doing the work.
