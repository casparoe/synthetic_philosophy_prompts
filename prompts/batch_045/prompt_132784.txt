Hi. I work in the clerk's office of a fairly religious mid-sized city council, and one of my jobs is screening the written submissions we read into the record at public hearings (evangelical and Catholic churches, two mosques, a gurdwara, a very active secularist association, assorted resident groups). Our standing orders say a submission should offer reasons that all members of the public can reasonably be expected to accept regardless of their religion or worldview, which is obviously some version of the public reason idea, but nobody has ever turned it into anything you could actually run. We recieve 30–40 submissions a week, one or two pages each, we've got about three minutes per submission, we can't research the religion involved, if we leave something out we have to print a one-sentence reason that will survive appeal to a panel of elected members, and our lawyers say we can't exclude anything because of who the speaker is, only because of what the argument says.

So: give me a procedure. The questions in order, what counts as a pass at each one, where it stops and excludes, where it kicks the thing back for one rewriten version, and where it has to hand the call to a person because it's a judgement call dressed up as a rule. Two of my boss's ideas are in the mix and I want you to tell me if they're mistakes: she thinks people should always be allowed to make their point in religious language as long as they also attach a secular version of the same argument (a translation proviso, she calls it), and she thinks the rule should only really bite at the final vote and not during early hearings. Then run the procedure on three cases: (1) a church's one-page submission against a medically-assisted-dying resolution arguing that life is a gift from God and suffering can be redemptive, no secular premises anywhere; (2) an interfaith housing coalition submission supporting a zoning change that opens with a verse about hospitality to the stranger and then gives three paragraphs of rent and vacancy statistics; (3) a secular tenants' union submission on the same zoning change that never mentions religion but whose whole case rests on the inherent dignity of every person, which their own newsletter says they took from Catholic social teaching. I'd be annoyed if the procedure threw out (3), so if it does, say so plainly, and if it does something else I won't like elsewhere, say that too. Also be honest about which of your steps are really just someone's politics arranged as a checklist.
