Two things I keep circling back to, and I want to know where they sit in the ranking or whether they're red herrings:

- exit asymmetry: getting out of a bubble mostly just takes exposure, but getting out of an echo chamber takes rebuilding a whole trust network from scratch, so the cost of leaving scales with how thoroughly your trust in outsiders was destroyed. If that's roughly right, then a lot of debunking-style interventions are aimed at the wrong failure mode, and the interesting question is about trust repair rather than information delivery.
- chambers about chambers: some communities are organized around the claim that mainstream institutions are themselves echo chambers, so any outside correction is predicted by the chamber's own narrative and lands as confirmation. Is that a genuinely distinct mechanism with its own dynamics, or just echo chambers all the way down?
