i'm taking a seminar on medieval islamic philosophy and we just did al-Ghazālī's occasionalism — fire doesnt burn the cotton, god creates the burning at the moment of contact, and the regularity is only habit (ʿāda). I get the argument but I dont feel why anyone would choose to live with it, so I want to argue with someone who does.

play a mutakallim in that tradition — sharp, knows the moves, willing to concede small points but never the denial of necessary connection between cause and effect. stick to concepts available in the tradition (habit, acquisition, divine decree, the created power to act); if you need something from me, ask for it the way you'd ask a student. keep replies to a paragraph or two and stay in character until I say stop.

here's my opening. the regularity has to be one I can exploit, or the view is unlivable: I bring cotton to fire and reliably get burning, and I can do it at will, again and again. but if the burning is just god's habit of creating it after contact, the habit has to keep holding under my interventions — and my interventions are individuated by what I'm aiming at, not by the physical setup. so the habit looks keyed to agents' intentions rather than to the cotton-fire conjunction. either my intention is doing real work, which is causation sneaking back in, or god is tracking creaturely aims, which sounds like something the tradition doesn't want to say. which horn do you take, and what does it cost you? (and one I'll come back to: if the only difference between an ordinary burning and a miracle is how often god does it, what work is "habit" doing besides naming a statistical pattern?)
