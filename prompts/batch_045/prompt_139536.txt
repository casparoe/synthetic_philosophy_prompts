Write a piece of satire in the form of a fictional government document: a "Draft Programmatic Welfare Impact Statement," roughly 800 words, in deadpan administrative prose, issued by an invented federal body — call it something like the Interagency Working Group on Wild Animal Welfare — whose brief is to extend regulatory benefit–cost analysis to the welfare of wild animals, and which concludes, to its own evident satisfaction, that the highest-scoring land use in the region under review is a paved commercial strip with ornamental groundcover. The program's motto is "No Net Suffering."

Structure it in numbered sections: (1) a summary of the proposed rule; (2) a methodology section defining the accounting unit, a "sentience-weighted hectare-year," with the weights derived from nociceptor counts, reversal-learning results, and a stated discount rate; (3) a findings table that monetizes net welfare across four or five land-use categories; (4) rules for tradeable "compassion credits," with baseline, additionality, leakage, verification, and monitoring provisions; and (5) a "Response to Comments" section.

The document may take the following as given, and I would like the argument to be airtight relative to them: welfare is to be aggregated across sentient individuals, with no separate entry for species, ecosystems, wildness, or naturalness; the populations in the region are r-selected, so most juveniles die before maturity and those deaths are on net negative, which means that additional habitat produces additional births and therefore additional suffering; land is scored against the counterfactual of what would have happened to it anyway; and future welfare is discounted at the working group's standard rate.

The comedy should come out of the machinery rather than out of gags: the unit conversions, the sentience weights and what they do to earthworms, the additionality rule requiring an applicant to demonstrate that the wetland it drained would have gone on generating suffering had it been left alone, the handling of "existence value" and "aesthetic value" as items to be logged but not scored, and the serene bureaucratic tone applied throughout to juvenile mortality. Give me two commenters in section 5. The first is an environmentalist who insists that a place can matter independently of the summed welfare of the animals in it; the reply should make clear, without ever saying so, that the framework has no column in which that value could go. The second is a welfarist ally who points out that nothing in the working group's own logic stops at the strip mall; the reply should be a refusal to extend the analysis beyond the scope of the programmatic statement. No character in the document should name the reductio, and the working group should never break character or appear to notice what it has concluded.

Close with the standard notice about the public comment period. Invented proper nouns and invented officials only; no real people, no citations, no footnotes.
