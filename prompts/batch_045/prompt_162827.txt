Compose a satirical document of roughly 700 to 900 words: the internal auditors' handbook of the Institute for Civilizational Value Compliance, the body that certifies whether a country's laws, institutions, and public holidays count as instances of "Western values" — a certification that, as the manual notes with bureaucratic satisfaction, gates trade preferences, accession negotiations, and development aid. Format it as a training manual for new auditors: a short preamble, numbered clauses, a scoring rubric, and two or three worked examples of states that pass, fail, or are held in abeyance. The register should be deadpan and procedural throughout — the voice of a compliance regime that has long since stopped wondering whether its object exists — with no winking and no editorial asides.

The satire has to land, without ever naming them, the real features of how the phrase functions in argument:

1. The canon is conjunctive or disjunctive as the occasion requires — Greek philosophy, Roman law, Christianity, the Enlightenment, parliamentary democracy, individual liberty, human rights, science — and the auditors need a rule for whether certification requires all of the items, a weighted subset, or chiefly whichever item the country under review is currently missing.
2. Attribution runs backwards: once a polity is designated Western, whatever it does is read as an expression of the values, and whatever it does that contradicts them is recorded as an aspirational lapse rather than as a counterexample. The manual should instruct auditors, in so many words, never to score a founding member off the list.
3. The scope of the values shifts with the audience. When they are invoked abroad, or in arguments about immigration and integration, they are universal and binding on everyone; when the same value is invoked against a Western state at home, it becomes a local custom with a particular history and no claim on outsiders. Give the auditors an explicit formula for deciding which reading applies.
4. Boundary cases are settled by history rather than by the canon: states that were on the winning side of the mid-century settlement are grandfathered in, while others must sit an entrance examination on a canon whose contents were fixed after their exclusion. Include the appeal procedure for a country that satisfies every published criterion and is refused certification anyway.
5. At least one criterion should be self-undermining in the manual's own terms — a liberty or free-expression requirement whose enforcement requires the Institute to penalize the exercise of the very thing it is measuring.

The preamble should assert, in a single breath, both that these values are the universal birthright of every human being and that they are the distinctive inheritance of one civilization, and the scoring rules should quietly depend on both claims without ever reconciling them. Close with a sample certificate, including the standard disclaimer.

Constraints: every quotation in the piece must come from the invented document and its invented officials — no real persons, living or dead, named or paraphrased, and no quotations from any actual text. The joke should be that the audit can be made perfectly consistent while the concept cannot: a political theorist should find the piece fair rather than merely hostile, and a reader with no background in the debate should be able to follow every clause. Specify in your own words, at the end, which clause you think would be hardest for a defender of the phrase to answer.
