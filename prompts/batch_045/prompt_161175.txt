hey — I want a thought experiment here, and I'd rather have arguments than a potted biography of Brouwer.

Set up: I'm in a maths department where a couple of people are pushing a "formalisation norm" — the idea that a paper isn't really done until the main theorem has a machine-checked proof, in practice Lean because that's where the libraries are. One of the people pushing it is a constructivist sympathiser and he keeps saying Brouwer would have been delighted: the kernel checks a finite object, a proof of an existential is a term you can evaluate, extraction hands you an actual algorithm, nothing appeals to an inaccessible infinite. I think he's got it backwards. Brouwer treated mathematics as a languageless mental activity and formal language as a defective instrument for getting someone else to carry out the same construction; a machine checking a derivation looks to me like exactly the thing he spent his early career attacking. As I understand it he wasn't even that enthusiastic when Heyting wrote down axioms for intuitionistic logic — correct me if that's wrong — so why would he be happy about a type checker?

Here's the part I definitly can't settle, and it's what I actually want your view on. The wrinkle is that "proof assistant" isn't one thing, and neither is "Brouwer's mathematics". On the first axis: Lean's mathlib is classical, excluded middle and choice are on by default and almost nobody switches them off, whereas Coq's underlying logic is intuitionistic and classical reasoning is something you add as an axiom. On the second axis, his characteristic principles split. The continuity principle — every function on the continuum is continuous — sits very comfortably with a computational reading, since in a type theory you can only ever define computable things anyway. The fan theorem, as far as I know, isn't free, you have to assume it. And the creating-subject arguments, which I think get packaged into something called the Brouwer–Kripke schema (for any proposition there's a sequence staying flat until the subject has a proof of it and then flipping) seem to drag in time and an acting subject that a kernel has no vocabulary for.

So: hand the anti-formalist Brouwer of the foundational debates a laptop. Does he read machine-checked proof as the vindication of constructivism, or as Hilbert's programme with better marketing? Which axis decides it for him — "is the library classical" or "can I even prove my own theorems in this thing"? And if the choice on offer is a classical Lean on one side and an intuitionistic-but-lawlike Coq that still can't do bar induction on the other, is there any version of the technology he endorses, or is "never formalise anything" the honest extrapolation?
