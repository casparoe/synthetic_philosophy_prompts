my flatmate is doing a PhD on something in the neighbourhood of machine sentience, and he shoved this argument at me over dinner last week and I've been turning it over since and I can't find the broken step. He calls it the cheap minds argument. In my own words:

Setup. By a process's organisation I mean the abstract pattern of which of its states lead to which others, counterfactually — the state-transition structure, whatever the states are made of. Then:

(1) Consciousness is fixed by organisation: two systems with the same organisation have the same experiences, whatever they're made of, whatever speed they run at. Standard functionalism, substrate independence, I'm definately happy to keep it.

(2) Counting: every distinct implementation of a conscious organisation is a distinct subject. Two machines running the same program means two streams, and if what they're running is unpleasant, it counts twice, not once.

(3) No discount for weird hardware: if there's something it's like to be a system, and what it's like is bad, it counts for exactly as much as it would in a brain. What we owe a system is a function of the organisation realised and of nothing else.

Now the machinery. Take something ordinary — a phone running a game.

(4) The same process realises lots of different organisations at the same time, with no funny business. Lump some of its states together; if the lumping is consistent, in the sense that the next coarse state is fixed by the current coarse one, you have a coarser organisation realised by exactly the same physical states. Call these the shadows. He says that for a state space of any decent size there are astronomically many such lumpings, and that every one of them is a perfectly respectable implementation of a perfectly respectable computation.

(5) Boundaries: the phone together with the air around it is also a process with its own organisation, and the phone plus the air plus you is another, and so on outward. Nested candidate systems all the way up, all realised by overlapping matter.

(6) The echo: read the process's state at t as the state the organisation assigns to t−1 and you get the same organisation over again, realised by the very same states, just offset. Same tokens, two runs. He insists this isn't merely a relabelling of a single implementation, though I'm not sure I follow why.

(7) Among the organisations realised in (4)–(6), some nonzero fraction are organisations of agony — ones such that, by (1), anything realising them is in agony. A small fraction of an astronomical number is still astronomical. So right now your phone contains more suffering than the total that has ever existed anywhere, and so does your head, and by the same reasoning so does a rock.

(8) And there's nothing to be done about it: switching the phone off is itself a physical process that realises organisations, including agony ones, so by (3) switching it off isn't obviously better. Every action and every inaction is on a par.

(9) So morality is either completely swamped by facts about cheap minds or completely inert, and either way the sentience-based picture he started from can't do any work.

I first thought the trick was that old move where a wall can be described as running any program you like, and that the multiplication is all gerrymandered mappings. He says he doesn't need it: (4), (5) and (6) are supposed to be implementations of the boring kosher kind, the sort nobody objects to in ordinary cases, and the multiplication still goes through even if every one of them is respectable. I can't tell whether that's right.

I'd most like to keep (1) and (3) — I really don't want to end up saying that what matters is the stuff, or that pain realised in silicon matters less. If something has to go I'd want it to be (2) or one of (4)–(6). But whenever I try to say why the shadows don't each get a subject, I end up either picking out a right grain by hand, which feels like taste, or saying one subject per hunk of matter, which feels like it costs me (1).

Which step is broken? And what does rejecting it cost — do I owe a criterion for implementation that isn't just aesthetic, do I have to let consciousness come in degrees, do I have to say the number of minds in a room isn't a determinate fact? If more than one step is shaky, say which one is doing the real work and price the alternatives.
