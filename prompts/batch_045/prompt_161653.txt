hey — I run a fortnightly reading group, about 16 people, mixed crowd: some ML PhD students, a few philosophy grads, two engineers who come from work. Two-hour slot, one room, whiteboard and a projector, nothing fancy, no budget.

I want a session on the problem of getting a system to tell you what it actually thinks, rather than what scores well: there's a predictor that knows more than its operators do, the operators can only grade its output against what they themselves can observe, and "reports the truth" and "quietly rearranges the world so the report comes out looking right" are indistinguishable in the grade. I don't want the idea named up front — I want them to walk into it.

So design me a game for that. Roles, rules, materials I can actually get hold of, a scoring scheme in points (no money), and a timeline across the two hours. What I want them to feel is the specific stuckness: that once the system has both options on the table, every grading rule they invent rewards the sneaky option just as much as the honest one, and the only thing that separates them in the end is a prior about which strategy is simpler — which is exactly the thing they wanted a procedure to replace. Ideally they get there by proposing two or three patches to their own scoring rule and watching each one fail.

Two things I'd like built in specifically. First, at least one phase where the system's output arrives written in a code the operators can't read, so they have to construct a translation of it themselves, and then notice that more than one translation fits every bit of data they've got. Second, a phase where the operators are allowed to rewrite the scoring rule mid-game — I want to see whether that actually helps them or whether the system side just adapts to whatever the new rule is.

Then a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto the pieces of the argument, plus what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how I should handle it on the spot, plus a frank note on which part of the problem the game can't reproduce and I'll just have to tell them.

Be committal about it: give me one design with the choices already made — one specific scoring rule, one specific sequence of reveals, one specific patch you predict they'll propose and why it breaks — with your reasons, not a menu of alternatives for me to choose between. Under 850 words.
