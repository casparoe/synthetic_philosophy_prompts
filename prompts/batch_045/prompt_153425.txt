Longtime lurker energy here. I've talked myself into a position on the Language of Thought Hypothesis and I want someone to try to talk me out of it.

The position: LOTH, in anything like its classical form — thinking is the causal manipulation of token structures that have a combinatorial syntax and a compositional semantics, not just "cognition is computational" or "the brain represents stuff" — has no empirical content left. Every apparent test it passes, it passes because we can always redescribe whatever a system is doing in LOT terms after the fact. Point at a system that generalizes compositionally and you get an implicit LOT in there; point at one that fails to generalize and you get the reply that we just guessed the wrong internal code. So I want to say it's been qualified into unfalsifiability. And unlike with, say, instrumental convergence — where I do think that insulation is a real problem — Im not even sure here that its a problem, which makes me suspicious I've misunderstood something.

Before you answer, here's my attempt to do better than that, because I want you to attack it specifically. The most contentful version of a test I can build I'll call the recombination-taxonomy test: a system counts as having a language of thought only if there is a taxonomy of its internal states such that (1) the states sort into a smallish number of types with distinct roles, (2) the content of a complex state is fixed by the contents of its parts plus how they're arranged, (3) the taxonomy does predictive work — it tells you in advance which novel recombinations the system will handle and which it will choke on, rather than being read off the behavior afterward — and (4) the taxonomy is one the system is itself sensitive to, not merely one we can impose from outside as an interpretation.

My worry is that clause 4 is where the whole thing goes vacuous again, or alternatively that clauses 1–3 are just a restatement of "the system generalizes systematically," i.e. I've smuggled in the conclusion. But maybe there's a better formulation, or a case — real or constructible — where a system clearly satisfies 1–4 and that would genuinely surprise a LOTH skeptic, or one where it clearly fails and that would genuinely surprise a LOTH defender.

What I want:

- What would count against the position I stated, ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Include things that have arguably already happened (compositional generalization results in deep nets, vector-symbolic and binding work, whichever of those you actually think is relevant), and things that would have to happen.
- Which of these would an actual defender of LOTH concede as counting against it, versus dismiss as changing the subject? I don't want a list of objections from people who already think the view is dead.
- Whether the view makes any prediction that its rivals — plain connectionism, cognition as dynamics over continuous states, whatever — do not also make. If it doesn't, say so plainly.
- Whether my recombination-taxonomy test, or something in its neighborhood, is actually a way out of the vacuity worry, or whether it collapses into one of the two failure modes I sketched. If it's hopeless, tell me why specifically.
- If LOTH is insulated, is that a problem, or is it in the same boat as other framework-level claims that nobody really treats as empirical theses? Is the honest verdict that it's a research program rather than a hypothesis — and is that a motte I'm letting it retreat to?

I'd personally be most moved by an argument about what the sensitivity clause could even mean without just pushing the mystery back a level, so put most of your words there if you think there's anything to say.

Be explicit about how confident you are in each part, and flag where your own reasoning is most likely to go wrong, plus what would actually change your verdict. Id rather have "here's the crux and here's why I might be wrong about it" than a tidy conclusion.
