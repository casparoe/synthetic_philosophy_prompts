I'm in the final term of a seminar on experimental philosophy and the philosophy of action, and the two people leading it have spent the semester arguing about the Knobe effect in a way that has stopped being productive. They have now agreed, at my suggestion, to write a joint paper instead of two replies to each other, and I have been asked to produce the first draft of the document they would both sign. I would be grateful for a draft I can put in front of them.

The two positions, as I would characterise them. Side A: the asymmetry found in the chairman-style cases — an agent who brings about a foreseen side effect while caring only about something else, where the side effect is a harm in one condition and a comparable benefit in the other — is a genuine output of the competence underlying ordinary intentional-action attribution; the normative status of the side effect partly determines whether it counts as something the agent did intentionally, and the resulting judgments are not defective. Side B: the asymmetry is a performance-level phenomenon — blame, negative affect, conversational enrichment of the question, or subjects' priors about whether agents foresee the bad things they bring about — and measures that filter these out leave no asymmetry in the concept itself.

What I need is the following.

One: a numbered section of claims both sides can sign. These should include, at minimum, what both accept about the basic effect and about what would not count as evidence for either side — in particular, I want it recorded that neither side may appeal to its own intuition about the cases, or to the intuitions of the seminar, as data.

Two: a disagreements table with these columns: the crux; the claim as A would phrase it, in wording A would accept; the claim as B would phrase it, in wording B would accept; a test or case both agree bears on the crux; A's prediction; B's prediction. The cruxes I think have to appear are: whether the driver is moral valence or norm violation as such; whether the asymmetry generalises to knowingly, deliberately, and to explicit questions about foresight; whether it survives under speeded responding, cognitive load, and forced delay; whether it survives when blame is blocked — the agent is fully excused, or the outcome is bad but attributable to no one; and whether it survives in a production or paraphrase-selection task rather than a yes/no question.

Three designs of my own that I would like used as the agreed bearing evidence, unless you think one is confounded, in which case say so and propose a better one. First, a two-by-two crossing valence (harm versus benefit) with the presence of an explicit written rule that the side effect breaches: A predicts the rule cell, not valence, does the work; B predicts a valence main effect driven by affect. Second, a case where the side effect is approved by the agent's own community and disapproved by the subjects', to separate the subjects' moral response from the agent's normative environment. Third, a task in which subjects are paid for accuracy in selecting the paraphrase a neutral reader would find clearer, rather than answering a direct question.

For each, give me each side's prediction in a form that could be entered in a pre-registration, and flag any prediction that is insulated — where a side could absorb either outcome without cost — with a note on why that matters here.

Three: a short closing section on which parts of the dispute are merely verbal. I suspect one is whether intentionally in the test question is being read as on purpose or in a way that makes the agent answerable, and another is whether the competence/performance distinction is doing real work or only relabelling the data. Say which parts are verbal and why, and give a formulation of what is left over once the verbal parts are set aside.

Constraints: no citations, and no appeal to published findings by author, title, or year — describe any empirical pattern you rely on in a sentence and mark it as jointly accepted by both sides. Write the vignettes out in full rather than alluding to them. Formal register, in the style of a pre-registration document rather than an essay. Around 1200 to 1500 words.
