wrote up some rough notes for a paper idea on the Frege–Geach problem for expressivism, and i'd love comments before i decide wether to bother turning them into anything. Please dont give me a reading list or citations, I roughly know the literature, I just want your reaction to the argument.

Setup. Expressivism: to say "lying is wrong" is to express a con-attitude toward lying, not to state a moral fact. Frege–Geach: in "if lying is wrong, then getting your little brother to lie is wrong," the moral clauses arn't doing what they do when they stand alone, yet the modus ponens inference still looks valid, and validity is normally cashed out as preservation of truth.

1. Contribution vs. force. The semantic value of a moral predicate isn't a property but a transformer: it takes the attitude-type of its embedding context and outputs a new attitude-type. So "wrong" in a negated context outputs something toleration-ish; in the consequent of a conditional it outputs a slot that gets filled by the transformer contributed by the antecedent clause. Composition is just function composition, and the whole compound ends up expressing one structured higher-order attitude (say, disapproval of the pair: approving of lying while tolerating getting your brother to lie).

2. Coherence. Call a stance incoherent when it contains attitudes that "collide," where collision is defined syntactically over attitude-pairs rather than over contents.

3. Validity without truth (the bit I'm unsure about). An inference is valid iff every maximal coherent extension of any stance that contains the premise-attitudes also contains the conclusion-attitude. So no truth-conditions are needed, just a space of stances and an extension operation.

4. Consequence I think I noticed: because a stance can have several different maximal coherent extensions, this seems to give a supervaluation-style logic, so some classically valid patterns should fail in embedded contexts (maybe constructive dilemma, maybe excluded middle for embedded moral clauses).

What i want from you:

- Is step 3 actually different from the usual higher-order-attitude reply, or is "maximal coherent extension" just truth-in-a-model with extra steps?
- Is step 4 really a consequence? And if it is, is it a refutation instead of an interesting result, given that people plainly reason classically with embedded moral sentences?
- Does the package still deliver what people wanted expressivism for — no queer moral facts, a tight link between moral judgement and motivation, an account of moral disagreement as disagreement — or does step 1 quietly give back descriptive content?
- What's the strongest objection I haven't listed, and which part should I develop first? Also tell me honestly if this is boring or if the whole thing has been done before.
