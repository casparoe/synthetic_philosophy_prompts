I'm co-teaching a first-year seminar on social norms next term and we're doing Lord of the Flies alongside Lewis's 1969 book on convention, and I want to get the reading straight before I inflict it on eighteen-year-olds. Set-up so we're arguing about the same thing: the Lewisian package is roughly that a convention is a regularity in a recurrent coordination situation, arbitrary among the eligible alternatives, where everyone conforms, everyone expects everyone else to conform, and everyone prefers to conform on condition that the others do, with all of that common knowledge — and that such a thing can get going with nobody agreeing to anything, just from precedent or from one option's being salient. No rule, no sanction, no legislator, and nobody needs to be able to say why it holds. The evolutionary-dynamics literature that grows out of that picture sharpens it: norms as equilibria nobody chose and nobody could justify.
