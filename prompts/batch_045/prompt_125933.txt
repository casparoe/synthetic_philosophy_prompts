I build and maintain probabilistic risk models, and there is a configuration I keep meeting in them for which I have no vocabulary. I would like to know whether it has a name, and — more usefully — how exactly it should be carved up, since a bare label would not tell me anything I can act on.

Here is the smallest case in which I can see it. I am tracking a shipment. Before any message arrives my credences over four exclusive possibilities are: delayed and held at customs, 0.24; delayed for some other reason, 0.16; not delayed and arrived early, 0.30; not delayed and arrived on time, 0.30. A source I only partly trust sends word: if the shipment was delayed, the delay was at customs. Nothing in the message makes me certain of anything, so ordinary conditionalising on a learned proposition is not available to me. There are two ways of cashing out what the message does, and as I assess its credibility both of them squeeze the same possibility — delayed for some other reason — down to 0.08. On the first, the message bears on a proposition, the conditional read materially, true unless the shipment is both delayed and held somewhere else: that proposition goes from 0.84 to 0.92. On the second, the message bears on a ratio: the chance of customs given delay goes from 0.60 to 0.80.

The two readings agree about which cell shrinks and disagree about everything else, because they disagree about where the freed weight goes. The first, if I hold fixed the relative weights among the ways the message could be true, spreads it over all three remaining cells in proportion to their prior sizes: the two not-delayed cells rise to roughly 0.329 each, delayed-and-at-customs to roughly 0.263, and my confidence that the shipment was delayed at all falls from 0.40 to about 0.343 — note that the ratio I would have naively taken myself to be adjusting moves as well, but only to about 0.767. The second, if I hold fixed the delayed/not-delayed split on the grounds that the message concerns where the delay happened rather than whether there was one, sends the entire freed 0.08 into delayed-and-at-customs, which rises to 0.32, and leaves my confidence in a delay at 0.40. Both procedures answer to the instruction to change as little as you can; they differ only in what they treat as held fixed, and they give answers for the delay question that differ by more than a tenth of its prior value. The arithmetic is mine and I have checked it, but I would not be shocked at a slip.

Four further things I have noticed, which may or may not be the same phenomenon. First, neither reading determines a unique answer on its own: the message supplies one number, and the rest has to come from a stipulation about what is preserved, and that stipulation is indexed to how I had already divided up the possibilities before any evidence arrived — a bookkeeping decision with no evidential content. Second, when two such messages arrive together, the order in which I process them changes the result, and when both bear on the same conditional the earlier one is erased entirely: only my final confidence in the conditional seems to matter, so that two independent reports count for no more than one. Third, the rules I can think of all agree at both extremes — when the message is worthless and when it is conclusive — and disagree in between, with the disagreement largest when the message is moderately informative; I cannot find anything in the situation that selects among them. Fourth, and strangest: a colleague tells me that for a nested message, of the form if the shipment was delayed, then if it was delayed more than a day it was held at customs, it matters whether I collapse this into if it was delayed more than a day it was held at customs, and that whether collapsing is legitimate is itself disputed for probabilistic readings of conditionals even though it is trivially fine for the material one.

To be clear about what I am not asking about. When the uncertain news concerns a flat matter of fact — an instrument reading, a witness saying that it rained — I have a procedure I trust, and it is not in question here. Nor is this the puzzle about how a proposition one already accepted can lend support to a general hypothesis. My difficulty is specific to news whose content is conditional in form: I want to know whether the conditional shape adds anything to the problem of updating on something you are not certain of, or whether it only complicates the bookkeeping, and if the former, what exactly it adds.

So: does this configuration have a name? Which are the nearest established concepts, and where does each one stop short of what I have described — in particular, are my four further observations one thing or several, and which of them travel together? Is there a principled constraint that picks out one rule, an analogue of change nothing you are not forced to change, and what would have to be true of a case for that constraint to apply? And if nothing established fits, coin a term for it: give a definition, the conditions under which it applies, and boundary cases, including a case that looks like mine but is not one, and a version of mine where the right answer is uncontroversial. I am after the carving and the edges, not the label.
