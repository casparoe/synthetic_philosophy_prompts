I keep going back and forth on guilt vs shame — which is the better one to have around, and whether a culture can actually pick — and I think the only way to make progress is to write down my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases and see what, if anything, they add up to. So here they are, sorry if its a lot.

1. I snap at my sister on the phone and hang up, then feel bad. Guilt is clearly the right thing here; shame would be wildly out of proportion.
2. I realize I've been quietly condescending to a coworker for three years — never anything you could point at, just a tone. Verdict: this one is shame, not guilt. If I felt guilt about it I'd be copping out — I'd apologize for one email and then feel fine.
3. I cut someone off in traffic, nobody sees, nothing happens. Verdict: a bit of guilt, no shame. Feels slightly silly but I'm sticking with it.
4. My family's money comes from something ugly a century back, and I did nothing. Verdict: guilt is wrong (I didn't do it), but a low-grade shame seems right? Maybe? I flip on this one.
5. My friend's husband died and mine didn't, and I feel awful about still being here. Verdict: whatever that is, it's a mistake. Not fitting, and I'd definately want it to stop.
6. I'm ashamed of my accent in front of people from work. Verdict: bad. Shame latched onto something I never chose and can't really change.
7. My old company ran "blameless postmortems" and people genuinely started reporting their own screwups. Verdict: a culture can be pushed toward the guilt end and it can work.
8. My kid's school does apology circles and it's pure theater. Verdict: same-looking intervention, doesn't work, and what it produces is shame wearing guilt's clothes.
9. I ghosted a friend, apologized properly, she forgave me, and I still feel guilty. Verdict: no idea. Half of me thinks it's leftover noise, half of me thinks the guilt knows something I don't.

The principle I keep wanting to say is: guilt is for what you did, shame is for who you're being, and each is in good standing only when its object is something you could have done otherwise about and when it moves you toward repair rather than hiding. But I can see it's in trouble — 2 and 6 look structurally the same (character isn't chosen either), 5 isn't about anything I did or am, and 7 and 8 look alike and come out opposite.

So: is there one principle, or a short list of candidates, that gets most of these? State it precisely, say which case forces which clause, tell me honestly which of my verdicts it can't accommodate and which you'd overturn, and give me a case I haven't listed where the two come apart in a way my principle can't see. And on the back of it: does whatever you land on say anything about whether a culture — not just a person — can shift the mix on purpose, or is that just a different question? Plus, if there's any real sense in which one of the two is the better moral emotion to have around, I'd like to hear it.
