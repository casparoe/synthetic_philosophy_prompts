I'm meant to lead the discussion on the Judy Benjamin problem at my reading group on Thursday, and I've been test-driving cases on a friend who did her PhD in statistics and has extremely firm intuitions about this stuff — annoyingly firm, given that she'll only ever give me her answers as four numbers to two decimal places and nothing else. The setup we're using is the usual one: Judy parachutes into an area divided into four quadrants, two in Blue (friendly) territory and two in Red, with Second Company HQ sitting in exactly one Blue quadrant and one Red quadrant, so the four cells are Blue-no-HQ, Blue-HQ, Red-no-HQ, Red-HQ; then a radio operator sends a message that constrains a conditional probability ("if you're in Red territory, the odds are 3 to 1 that you're in HQ area") rather than telling her the plain probability of anything. I made up my own numbers instead of using the ones from the paper. Her verdicts so far, always in the cell order above: (1) unconditional message, prior (0.40, 0.20, 0.12, 0.28), message says her chance of being in Red territory is 0.60 → she gave (0.27, 0.13, 0.18, 0.42), i.e. straight Jeffrey conditioning, HQ-odds inside each territory untouched; (2) prior (0.35, 0.25, 0.12, 0.28), message "if you're in Red, odds 3:1 HQ" → (0.35, 0.25, 0.10, 0.30); (3) prior (0.39, 0.21, 0.25, 0.15), message "if you're in Blue, odds 2:3 HQ" → (0.36, 0.24, 0.25, 0.15); (4) prior (0.25, 0.25, 0.25, 0.25), message giving both conditionals ("if Red, 3:1 HQ; if Blue, 1:3 HQ") → (0.375, 0.125, 0.125, 0.375), three decimals because she said they came out exact. When I gave her a version where I spelled out the operator's protocol (a coin flip decides which territory gets reported on), she said you just conditionalize and there's no puzzle at all, which I suspect is her dodge.

The case I want to bring on Thursday is this one: prior (0.25, 0.25, 0.375, 0.125) — she's 50/50 on which territory, 50/50 on HQ within Blue, and only 1-in-4 on HQ within Red — and the message says "if you're in Red territory, the odds are 3 to 1 that you're in Second Company HQ area." What four numbers does my friend give? More usefully: what rule is she actually running? As far as I can tell her track record is equally consistent with (a) something like local surgery — set the conditional to what the message says, keep the probability of being in Red territory and the HQ-odds inside Blue as they were; (b) the minimum-relative-entropy / "closest new distribution" rule I keep seeing mentioned for this problem, which as I understand it drags down the probability of the region the message is about even though the message is ostensibly only about what happens inside that region; and (c) something looser, like "the operator talked about Red, which is evidence you're in Red." Tell me what each of those predicts here (rough numbers are fine, and tell me if any of them can't even satisfy the constraint with these priors), which one you'd bet on and why, and how confident you'd be. Also: which further case would best separate the survivors? I was thinking either cranking the distortion right up — same 3:1 message but a prior with P(HQ|Red) = 0.1 — or flipping the direction of the message (tell her the odds are 1:3), but I can't work out which of those is the sharper test.
