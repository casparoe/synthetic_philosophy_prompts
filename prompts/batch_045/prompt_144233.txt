hey — i'm taking a judgment & decision-making course this term and i'm also the person who writes the budget for my student society. We run six or seven events a year (a careers-fair type thing, a couple of socials, a conference, a few one-offs), and every one needs a projected attendance number and a cost estimate that the student union finance comittee signs off on. The committee is five undergrads who have never heard of any of this. If we overrun by more than about 15% we eat it out of next term's allocation, and if we underspend badly they cut our budget, so both directions hurt a bit. I've got maybe half an hour per proposal, a spreadsheet, and my memory of what past events actually cost.

So: can you turn the inside view / outside view stuff into a procedure I can actually run? Not "be aware of the planning fallacy" — I want the ordered questions, what counts as a good enough answer to each one, where the procedure says stop and don't commit the money, and where it has to hand the decision back to the committee because it's a values call rather than an estimation call. Remember the people signing off are 19 to 21 and have no stats, so no step can need more than a spreadsheet.

The bits I keep getting stuck on, which I want the procedure to actually handle rather than hand-wave:

- I build the bottom-up number first (venue, catering per head, ticket sales as a percentage of last time, sponsorship I'm "pretty sure" about) and then I go looking for a reference class — which means I can basically shop for whichever class gives me the answer I already wanted. How does the procedure stop me doing that? Do I have to commit to the class before I'm allowed to do the bottom-up estimate?
- Some line items obviously have base rates and some don't. Catering cost per head: years of data. "Will this speaker actually draw 200 people": nothing. Do I regress the total or the components? Regressing the whole total feels like it throws away the information I genuinely do have.
- Our reference class is only the events we actually ran, and we only ran the ones we felt good about after doing exactly this kind of optimistic planning. I've argued myself into both "so the base rate is too rosy" and "so the base rate is fine, because the filter operating on the class is the same filter operating on me now." Which is it, or what would I have to check to tell?
- Do the events we proposed and then quietly didn't run go in the class?
- When am I allowed to shift off the base rate because this case is genuinely different, and what makes that a legitimate reason instead of a rationalization? Does it have to be a mechanism I state out loud in advance and can score afterwards, and what do I do with a reason I only thought of after seeing the outside-view number?
