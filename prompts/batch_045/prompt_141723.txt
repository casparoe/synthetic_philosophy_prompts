I have no academic background in any of this. I got into it because my daughters classroom has one of those timelines on the wall — dinosaurs, ice age, farming, industrial revolution, and then a question mark — and I realised I had no idea where the Anthropocene would go on it, or whether it goes on it at all. So I started reading on my own, and now I have a pile of opinions I suspect don't fit together, and I want to know what rule I'm unconsciously applying, or which opinions I have to give up.

My verdicts, going by gut:

- The dip in CO2 somewhere around 1610, when the population of the Americas collapsed after contact and huge amounts of farmland grew back into forest. I like this one much more than I expected to. It feels like the moment the planet became one connected system rather than a bunch of separate ones.
- Eight thousandish years ago, early farming clearing land, rice paddies, the slow start of methane. Nope. If that counts then basically the whole Holocene is the Anthropocene and the word stops doing any work.
- 1784, the steam engine, the industrial revolution. Feels wrong to me even though it's everyone's go-to. It reads like an answer to "what caused this" rather than "when did it start," and those feel like different questions to me even though I can't say why.
- 1945, the first atomic test, the fallout settling into sediment and lake beds all over the planet. Yes. This one feels right and I can't fully explain it.
- The 1950s, when everything shoots up at once — population, concrete, plastic, cars, CO2, nitrogen. Also yes, and if you made me pick a single answer I'd of picked this one over the 1610 one, which I know is a bit inconsistent of me.
- 1964, the peak in the bomb fallout. Fine in principle but too late. It's like saying the party started at 2am because that's when it was loudest.
- Giving a fuzzy band instead of a year — "sometime in the twentieth century." I hate this. It feels like dodging. But I can't say why, and it bothers me that I can't.

The best thing I've managed to come up with on my own is: a period starts when the change becomes self-driving, when it would keep going even if everyone stopped pushing. But that only really fits the 1950s. It doesn't fit 1945 at all — a bomb doesn't make anything self-driving, it just leaves a mark — and it definately doesn't fit 1610, which I also like. So either my principle needs a second clause about marks that simply cannot be missed, or one of my verdicts has to go, and I can't tell which is the cheaper option.

So: is there a principle that gets all of this, or nearly all of it? I want it stated precisely enough that I can test it myself, and I want to know which case motivates each part of it. Then please run it on cases I don't have verdicts on:

- Does the marker have to be in rock, or is a lake bed or an ice core good enough? And does it have to last — if geologists a million years from now couldn't see it, is it not a real start date?
- The domestic chicken thing: the most common bird on earth is now a farmed one, and there are billions of their bones in landfills. My gut is completely silent on this and I don't know what that means.
- What if we sort it out — CO2 comes back down, things stabilise, by 2200. Does the Anthropocene then turn out never to have started, or does it start and then end, or was the 1950s the start no matter what happens later?
- And who decides. I read that a committee of geologists was arguing about a particular layer of mud in some lake as the official marker, and my honest reaction is that whatever they decide has nothing to do with when it actually started and everything to do with what we're allowed to call it. That might be the most important thing I believe here, or it might just be confused. Tell me which.
