So I got voluntold to give a speech at our town's volunteer appreciation dinner next Friday, and I'm 17 and I have no idea how to do this without it being either fake or accidentally rude. I'm the one presenting the neighbor-of-the-year thing to Mrs. Okoye, who lives two doors down and who last January waded into the creek by the old mill road and got two kids out of a car that had gone in. Her daughter Ada is my best friend and Ada's going to be sitting right there.

Here's the part that's screwing me up, and it's because of my philosophy elective, sorry. There's this idea about stuff that's good to do but that nobody was obligated to do — nobody would have blamed her if she'd stood on the bank and called 911, but it also isn't right to say she was required. And the two ways speeches like this normally go both wreck it. One is "you did what any decent person would have done," which is just false — fourteen other people stood on that bank and she's the one who went in, so if I say that I'm erasing the actual thing she did. The other is "you did more than you had to," which my aunt basically said out loud at dinner last week makes everyone else in the room feel accused, like we're all being let off the hook for something we should be ashamed of. I definately don't want to stand up in front of my whole town and imply they're all cowards. But I also don't want to make it sound like a hobby, like she just really enjoys freezing water, because then why is there an award.

The audience thing makes it worse. Half the room is the volunteer fire company and the EMTs, and for them going into that creek is literally the job — they get paged and they go, at 3am, for free. So if I make a big deal of her exceeding what was required of her, it lands on them as an insult, like their version of the same act counts for less because they signed a paper. But if I say it was required of everybody, the award means nothing and I'm also lying about her. And Chief Alvarez is speaking right after me, so I can't say anything he'd then have to walk back.

Also: she hates being made into a big deal. If the speech turns her into some saint she'll want to die and Ada will actually kill me. But there's a thing I genuinely want to say and don't know how to, which is that praising her seems to do something weird to the situation — once everyone spends a whole evening saying how amazing it was, it starts to feel like next time she's expected to, and like anybody who doesn't is quietly failing. I don't know how to praise her without that happening, and it feels like the same trap as the aunt thing but from the other direction.

Can you just write the speech? Like 3 to 4 minutes spoken out loud, not an essay I'm reading. Rules: no naming any philosophers or using any philosophy words, nobody there would know them and it'd sound like I'm showing off; nothing religious, neither her family nor mine is and the room is mixed; don't call her a hero more than once, ideally not at all; and it has to actually end with me handing her the plaque, which is genuinely kind of ugly, so if you can make that part land instead of being awkward that would help.
