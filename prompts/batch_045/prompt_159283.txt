"Three concepts, not two. Negative liberty, from Isaiah Berlin: you're free to the degree that nobody interferes with you — you're free to the extent that doors are open, and the more doors are open the freer you are, so a kid with no money isn't really free because most of the doors are shut for him even though nobody's literally stopping him. Positive liberty is the other Berlin one: freedom is self-mastery, your rational self in charge instead of your impulses, and the whole point of Berlin's essay is that this idea is what gives you totalitarianism — once you say someone's real self wants X, you can force them to do X and call it making them free, which is exactly what the twentieth century dictators did. Third is republican liberty, which people like Pettit revived: freedom is non-domination, you're free only if nobody has the power to interfere with you, where arbitrary just means random or unpredictable. So every law is someone's power over you, which means more laws = less freedom, and the republican fix for that is direct democracy — everybody votes on everything, because then you're only obeying yourself."
