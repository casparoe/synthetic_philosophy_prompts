I teach an upper-level undergraduate option in jurisprudence, and I am setting this year's final paper: a 48-hour take-home in which candidates answer one question out of three, subject to a 2,500-word ceiling. I would like you to draft one of the three options — the one on self-amendment and the paradoxes of legal self-reference — together with a model answer and a marking scheme.

The students have read Hart on the rule of recognition, Kelsen on the basic norm and on legal revolution, and the standard literature on entrenchment and amendment powers, and they have played several rounds of Nomic in seminar. I do not want a question that can be answered by setting Hart beside Kelsen, reporting the stand-off, and concluding that validity is "ultimately a matter of official practice". I want a question that forces candidates to work through the mechanics of a system amending its own amendment rules, and to take a position rather than survey one.

Please build the question around the following hypothetical jurisdiction, which I have drafted for this purpose. (You may add one further complication of your own if it sharpens the crux, but flag in your notes what you added and why.)

The Republic of Aurinia is governed by an Instrument of Rules, whose first six rules read:

1. The Assembly may amend or repeal any rule by a resolution supported by two-thirds of all its members.
2. Rule 1 may be amended or repealed only by a unanimous resolution of all members of the Assembly, confirmed by a majority at a national referendum.
3. Any purported amendment inconsistent with Rule 2 is void, and shall be treated as never having been enacted.
4. No rule has effect before the day on which it is enacted.
5. A rule ceases to be in force if no organ of the Republic has applied or expressly affirmed it for a continuous period of fifteen years.
6. A rule comes into force only when the Keeper has entered it in the Register of Rules.

In Year 12 the Assembly passes the Reform of Rules Act by 71 votes to 29. The Act repeals Rule 2; amends Rule 1 so as to require only a simple majority; purports to exempt itself from Rules 3 and 5; and purports to take effect from the day it was first laid before the Assembly. No referendum is held. The Keeper refuses to enter the Act in the Register, and it is never entered. Between Year 12 and Year 30 the Assembly enacts fourteen statutes by simple majorities, the courts decide cases by applying them, and the Keeper enters each of them in the Register. Over the same period no organ of the Republic applies or expressly affirms Rule 2 or Rule 5. In Year 30 a litigant argues that Statute No. 9 is not law, because when it was enacted the Assembly had no power to enact it by a simple majority.

In drafting the question, please ensure that it does the following things.

It should compel candidates to separate three things that are easily run together: amending a rule that regulates the procedure of amendment; amending a rule that regulates the scope of the amendment power; and exercising a power whose authority derives from the very rule being amended.

It should require them to assess the two-step manoeuvre — using Rule 2's own procedure to relax Rule 1, and the relaxed Rule 1 to do the rest — both as a route the Assembly could have taken and as a reason for thinking that Rule 2's entrenchment was never binding in the first place, but only ever a delay. In particular it should force them to confront whether Rule 3 closes that route, and whether Rule 3 can protect Rule 2 without itself being entrenched.

It should require them to deal with self-application: whether a rule can exempt itself from the requirement (Rule 6) that determines when it comes into force, and whether a rule of desuetude that is never applied can lapse under its own terms.

It should require them to handle the conflict between Rule 4 and the Act's retroactivity clause, and to say what follows for the fourteen statutes if a purported amendment cannot validate itself retroactively — including whether the result is a change within one continuing legal system or a revolution that institutes another, and by what criterion they are deciding.

It should ask them, finally, either to redraft Rule 2 so that the two-step manoeuvre is unavailable, or to explain why no drafting can make an amendment rule unreachable by amendment.

Please give me: the question exactly as it would appear on the paper, with sensible part-structure and any word guidance; a model answer of about 900 words that argues for a definite conclusion rather than canvassing options; and a marking scheme out of 25, organised around five or six named must-hit moves, with the marks attached to each and one line on how candidates typically get each one wrong. Add a short markers' note identifying the strongest-looking wrong answer — for instance an answer that treats the courts' and the Keeper's eighteen years of acquiescence as itself settling the question of validity, and so never asks what has to be true for this still to be the same legal system — and identifying the one place where an answer reaching the opposite conclusion from your model answer should nevertheless be awarded full marks, so that the scheme rewards argument rather than agreement.
