The distinctions I actually care about (this is my own attempt, tell me if it's wrong):

1. Does the breakdown need an agent that models the metric and adapts on purpose, or can it happen with a dumb selection process that doesn't know the metric exists?
2. My four-way split: picking the extreme values selects for noise ("regressional"); intervening on the proxy breaks whatever causal tie linked it to the target ("causal"); someone optimises the proxy directly ("adversarial"); the proxy gets pushed outside the range where it was ever validated ("out-of-range"). Are these really four, or do two of them collapse? Put that question in the tree somewhere.
3. Is there a fact of the matter about the target beyond the family of proxies — is the proxy tracking something, or partly constituting it? (Think poverty counts, where the "line" is itself a convention.)
4. Is the collapse continuous in how much optimisation pressure there is, or is there a cliff? Does it matter if 1% of actors adapt or 90%?
5. Is the fix engineering/institutional (keep it secret, rotate metrics, use several, keep a human in the loop) or is it structural (nothing legible enough to steer by is robust to being steered by)?
