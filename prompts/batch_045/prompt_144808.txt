My reading group (four of us, no philosophy background, we've been picking at Wittgenstein's On Certainty for months) finally had a real fight last night, and I want your number on it rather than a menu of positions.

The bit I think we all got: some commitments — that I've got two hands, that the earth was around long before I was born, that objects don't pop out of existence when nobody's looking — aren't held on evidence and aren't open to doubt, because they're the frame that makes anything count as evidence for anything in the first place. Call them hinges. The fight is about how far that goes. Priya's objection: from inside the theory, a devout person for whom "God hears prayers" is utterly fixed and a hard atheist for whom "no mind is listening" is equally fixed look exactly alike, so hinge talk collapses into "whatever you can't bring yourself to doubt is in order," and it can never call a fanatic or a conspiracy theorist irrational. She thinks that's fatal.

My answer was what I've been calling the load-bearing test: a real hinge isn't merely a conviction you can't shake, it's one that holds up a practice of giving and asking for reasons — it decides what counts as a reason for what. On that test, "God hears prayers" may be unshakable, but if it doesn't regulate what you'll accept as evidence for anything, its not a hinge, just an obstinate belief, and the theory doesn't have to bless it.

Give me your credence that the load-bearing test rescues hinge epistemology from Priya's objection. Decompose it: whether the test really excludes the cases she's worried about, or whether religious/political/conspiracy-shaped certainties turn out to be load-bearing inside some local practice anyway; whether "load-bearing" can be told apart from "deeply entrenched" without already assuming what's a hinge; how much rides on whether hinges are the kind of thing that could be true or false at all rather than rules or animal reactions; and whether the theory should even want to escape, or whether it's fine that adjudication just stops at the hinges. Number plus one line of justification per term, then the combination.
