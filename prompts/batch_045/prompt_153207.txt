trying to figure out what I actually mean by weakness of will, going by my gut on cases, because the standard line (you do A while judging, right then, that B is better) keeps giving verdicts I dont want.

- 2am, rewatching a show I've already seen, paper due Monday, actively thinking "I should start the paper" — clear case.
- Quitting a career I trained for years for, after months of coming to think the career is bad for me — not weakness at all, I just changed my mind.
- Third drink at a party after deciding to stop, and I'd stop if you handed me fifty bucks to — weakness, but a small one.
- The heroin user who shoots up while judging, even mid-shot, that its worse for him — I dont want to call this weakness, it feels closer to a spasm. But: sleeping through the alarm I set myself at 11pm after deciding to run — I do want to call that weakness, even though it looks just as involuntary.
- Dieter who, fork in hand, sincerely judges the cake is worth it tonight, though last night he'd have said no — still weakness, and somehow milder than the 2am one.
- Deleting the apps before a deadline: fine, rational. Paying a mate to change my password so I cant get back in: fine, but faintly pathetic.
- Putting off a 20-minute tax form for weeks even though the refund arrives 10 days after filing whenever I do it — textbook procrastination, and delay-of-reward cant be whats going on, since the delay is the same either way.

My own theory was that its not the reward being far, its the cost being near: aversiveness ramps up as a thing gets close, so the reversal is about looming effort, not about hyperbolic discounting of a payoff. That predicts the tax form, but it seems to break on the cake, where nothing aversive looms and the bad part is distal.

Is there one account that fits all of these, or a small set, and what does it say about the case I keep flipping on: 6am on race morning, dreading it, having never once regretted running — is skipping weakness, or is running the failure? And does it have to give up my addict verdict or my alarm verdict?
