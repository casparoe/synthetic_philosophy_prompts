I run a tiny reading group that's been grinding through personal identity stuff, and we keep going in cirlces on the teletransporter case: scanner destroys the body here, replica wakes up on Mars, reductionist says fine because what matters is preserved, non-reductionist says no that's just death with a copy left behind. It's stopped being useful — people recite their position instead of thinking about it. So I want a case engineered to flip that pattern: a scenario where the reductionist has to say the person does not survive and nothing that matters in survival is preserved, while the non-reductionist has to say the very same person survives and, since identity is what matters in survival, that what matters is fully preserved. Same two theories, opposite verdicts, opposite direction from the stock case.

Constraints, because I know how easy it is to cheat here: (1) realistic-ish setting — a hospital, a clinic, a neurologist and a family, something that could plausibly happen in the next fifty years; no fission, no duplicates, no uploading, no branching, and please don't bring souls into the setup at all. (2) Don't make it brain death or a permanent vegetative state — that's its own tired argument and it's contested whether the organism is even still there. (3) Both verdicts have to be generated rather than stipulated: the non-reductionist's you survive has to fall out of something any non-reductionist would grant, like the uninterrupted continued existence of the same living brain or organism, and the reductionist's verdict has to fall out of the psychological criterion with any cause, not from you announcing in the setup that the psychology was destroyed. (4) There needs to be a real decision inside the case — an advance directive written twenty years earlier that someone now has to honour or override, a spouse deciding whether they're still married to this person, a surgeon asking for consent to the next operation — such that the two views give different answers about what it's rational to do or to fear. Verdict-matching with no practical upshot isn't what I'm after. (5) Try to close the two obvious escape hatches, or tell me honestly if you can't: the reductionist shouldn't be able to slide into continuity is partial, so identity is just indeterminate and prudential concern comes in degrees, so engineer the loss cleanly enough to block that; and the non-reductionist shouldn't be able to reply of course you survive but obviously the psychological catastrophe is terrible for you without noticing that this reply concedes the reductionist's whole point. Also tell me what a biological or animalist view says here, and whether your case actually separates it from the Simple View or just lumps them together.

After the scenario — a paragraph or two, no more — walk me through what each theory says and why, name the single feature of the case that's doing the work, and say what happens if it's removed: what if you leave one thin strand of psychological continuity, or make the damage gradual, or make it reversible? Then give me the best attack available to a defender of whichever theory comes out looking worse. And last thing, the bit I'm genuinely unsure about: is this really a reversal of the teletransporter, or does it just relocate the disagreement from identity to what matters? If it's the second one I'd rather know that up front than find out halfway through writing it up.
