hey — is there a name for this? I do logistics for a volunteer organisation and read a bit of this decision-theory stuff on the side, so forgive the lay description.

There's a thing I half remember where loads of people can each do the same risky-but-useful thing on their own, one person doing it is great, but each extra person who does it stacks up the downside without stacking up the upside, so everybody individually reckons they should go ahead and the group ends up worse off. I think the name has "curse" in it, or maybe I'm mixing it up with the auction thing. Anyway — that's not quite the pattern I keep noticing. The pattern I keep noticing is about *who* ends up doing it.

Two from real life:

We have a group chat of about twelve and we need one person to email the guy who owns a hall and ask if we can have it for free. One polite email, decent odds. Four separate emails from four different people and he thinks we're a circus and says no to all of us. Everyone can see the chat, so nobody wants to be email number two. So nothing happens for a week, and then whoever is least able to sit with an unanswered thing fires off an email — and in that particular group the people who can't leave it alone are also the people who write a bad email and don't know him. So the ask gets made by the worst available person, and it gets made by them precisely because everyone else was being careful. The careful peoples restraint doesn't remove the risk, it just delegates the decision to whoever is worst at it.

Second one, from a job I had: a shared service, anyone on the rota could push a hotfix for a bad config. Two people pushing at once and the change double-applies and takes the thing down for an hour. Everyone watches the same dashboard. Guess who pushes. It's whoever's tolerance for watching the error rate is lowest, which tracks pretty well with being the person who least understands what the config actually does. Same shape.

So: many people each have the option; exactly one taking it is what you want; more than one is bad; everyone can see that nobody has taken it yet; waiting has a cost; and the cost of waiting varies across people in a way that correlates the wrong way with how good they'd be at it. Result: the group's caution selects its least suitable member to act unilaterally.

What I want isn't a label. I want to know what this is and what it isn't:

- Is it just the curse thing plus the adverse-selection thing glued together, or does the visibility and the ordering do real work? Every version of the curse I've come across is simultaneous and nobody can see anybody else. If it's genuinely a distinct failure mode I'd like it named; if I'm only redescribing a special case, say so, tell me which one, and say how much gets lost by subsuming it.
- It feels close to the auction thing where the winner is whoever overestimated the most, close to the nobody-helps-because-everyone-assumes-someone-else-will thing, and close to a standoff where each side waits for the other to blink. It isn't any of those, or not only those — the first is about prices and one winner, the second is about nobody acting at all, and in the third nobody acting is the good outcome. But I'd like the boundaries drawn properly against each of them and against whatever else is nearby.
- The fix looks different, and that's my main reason for wanting a separate name. For the curse the advice is basically restrain yourself, coordinate, build a norm against acting alone. Here the fix isn't "nobody do it" — somebody should do it, just not that person. You'd assign the job outright, or make the competent person's waiting cheap, or make the eager person's acting expensive. If the remedies come apart like that, is that enough to make it a separate concept, or is that a bad test?
- Boundary cases I can't settle: if the correlation runs the other way — the most impatient person is also the best at it — then nothing goes wrong and it doesn't feel like a phenomenon at all. Same thing with a happy ending, or outside the definition? If everyone is equally competent but everyone waits, is that still my thing or just the ordinary coordination problem? And does it need the more-than-one-is-actively-harmful part at all, or does merely-wasteful duplication work too?

If there's no name, coin one, define it, and give me the edge cases. I definately won't be satisfied with "that's roughly X".
