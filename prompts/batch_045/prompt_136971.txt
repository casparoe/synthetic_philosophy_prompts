My flatmate is trying to get a small grant to set up a community bike workshop — the kind of venture where the buisness plan is mostly vibes plus one spreadsheet — and she's been told she now has to submit something called an 'outside view statement': here is the base rate for enterprises like yours, now justify your existence. She's livid about it, and I spent an evening trying and failing to get accross why the people asking might have half a point, which got us onto the planning fallacy, then reference class forecasting, then the obvious question of who the hell picks the reference class. I gave up on explaining and decided to write something instead.

So: imagine a world where taking the outside view isn't just good practice but a legal requirement, administered by some fictional body — call it the Office for Epistemic Modesty, or the Outside View Compliance Unit, whatever reads best — and write its statutory guidance for applicants. Deadpan civil-service register, all issued-under-section-whatever openings and where-an-applicant clauses, about 700–900 words, with numbered clauses, at least one worked example of an actual application being assessed, at least one mock form field with a character limit, and one quoted 'inside-view' critic — say an inventor or a founder being interviewed about what the regime has done to their industry.

The rules for the comedy: the laughs have to come from the epistemology, not from paperwork being boring in general. So the document needs to quietly work through, and get right, at least these: (a) the reference class problem — any class can be narrowed by piling on features until it has one member, at which point you're back to the inside view, and any class broad enough to be statistically useful is too broad to say anything about this case; (b) genuinely unprecedented projects, where the class is empty and the base rate is zero over zero (I rather like the idea that the Unit's official workaround is the class of projects whose promoters described them as unprecedented, which does have a robust historical failure rate); (c) gaming — applicants shopping around for flattering classes, which the Unit must then police, meaning the Unit is doing precisely the case-specific inside-view reasoning it banned; (d) the outside view turned on itself, i.e. the base rate of success for projects that have already survived outside-view assessment, and what that number does to the regime's own credibility; (e) mandated modesty becoming pure theatre, with applicants declaring enormous uncertainty bands while still asking for exactly the same money. Keep it straight-faced the whole way, no winking and no 'of course this is absurd' — a reader should be able to tell what's being ironised from the content alone. Someone who knows this literature should find it fair rather than a strawman: the Unit's rules should follow from the real logic of the outside view, and that's exactly what makes them horrible. Invent whatever in-world jargon you need (a zero-class declaration, reference-class laundering, a modesty return, a second-order base rate), and if there's a genuinely neat formal point you can bury in a footnote or a worked example, do that too.
