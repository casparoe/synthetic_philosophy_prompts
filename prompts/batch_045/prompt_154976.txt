I've been reading LessWrong and the old Overcoming Bias comment threads since basically forever, and a few of us from the local meetup are hacking on a tiny belief-tracking app (working name: Kinematics) where you don't log verdicts, you drag a slider to record how strongly today's observation pointed at some proposition, and the app pushes that through everything else in your model. I implemented the propagation as Jeffrey conditioning: take the partition {E, not-E} for whatever the slider is about, set the new credence in E to the slider value, hold the conditional credences P(H|E) and P(H|not-E) fixed, done. A friend of mine who actually does stats says this is broken in a way I should care about, and I've now argued about it twice without getting what her objection is.

Here's what I think I know. Probability kinematics is supposed to be the generalization of Bayesian conditioning to the case where your evidence is uncertain: instead of becoming certain of E you move to some intermediate credence q, and rigidity of the conditional credences given E and not-E is what pins down the rest. And I've seen it justified as a minimal-change rule — of all the distributions that put credence q on E, this is the one closest to your old credence function, so it's the conservative choice, the one that doesn't sneak in information you didn't recieve. That sounds exactly like what I want from a general-purpose updating engine, so I've been treating "my credence in some proposition moved to q" as the only input the rule needs, regardless of what caused the move. I also batch a day's slider moves and apply them in whatever order they come out of the log file, because I was under the impression that Bayesian updating is commutative and the final state shouldn't depend on the order you learned things in. (Somewhere in the back of my head there's a half-memory of someone on the forums saying Jeffrey himself later backed away from the "updating on uncertain evidence" framing and treated it as just a way of describing a change in credence — I can't reconstruct the argument and I'm not sure it matters for the app, but flagging it in case it's the crux.)

The case she keeps raising is this. Suppose a user logs a slider on "home rapid test read positive," but they're squinting at the cassette in bad bathroom light and are only 70% sure of what the display actually said. To me that is textbook uncertain evidence: set credence in E = "the test read positive" to 0.7, keep P(infected | positive read) and P(infected | negative read) where they were, propagate. She says that in exactly this kind of case the rigidities are the thing that fails, and that the correct move is to condition on something you *are* certain of — what the display looked like — and put the unreliability of your eyes into the likelihood rather than pretending the uncertainty is about the world-level proposition; and that the same goes, even more obviously, when the news is about the test rather than the patient, e.g. a preprint revising the sensitivity estimate downward, where P(infected | positive read) is precisely the quantity that moved and holding it fixed can't be right. I sort of follow, but then she said something about the answer depending on how finely you carve the partition, and about Jeffrey updates not commuting so that my batching silently picks an order, and I lost the thread. I also want to know what to do with the case I think my app handles fine: a user stares at their model, decides on reflection that their prior on the local base rate was overconfident, and drags the base-rate slider with no new observation at all.
