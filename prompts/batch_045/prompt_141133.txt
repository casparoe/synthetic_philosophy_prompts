Before I repeat any of this to my team, I'd like to know how much of the essay is fair. Here is how it summarizes the theorem:

"Aumann's agreement theorem says that two rational agents who begin with the same prior beliefs cannot end up disagreeing. The striking part is that they never have to pool their evidence: it is enough that they are honest with each other. If each states her probability for some claim, and each knows the other is reporting hers accurately, then Bayesian updating on what has been heard drives the two numbers together, and after enough rounds of exchange they settle on the same figure. The agreement is then stable — once both have openly said '0.7,' that figure is common knowledge between them, and no further exchange of information can pull them apart. The moral usually drawn from this is diagnostic: any disagreement that survives honest discussion is proof that something has gone wrong, since the only remaining explanations are that one side is reasoning badly, is being dishonest, or never really shared a prior in the first place. And because rational agents who share a prior converge on the truth, persistent disagreement is a reliable signal of error somewhere."

The part I'm most suspicious of is what the essay does with this next.
