ok so for my creative writing class we have to turn in a sonnet, any topic, and I also do this astronomy club after school, so I wanted to write mine about the morning star / evening star thing — you know the one, where two totally different descriptions (the bright thing you catch before sunrise, the bright thing you catch just after sunset) turn out to pick out the same object, and the weird part isnt that people used to be confused about it. The weird part is that even once you know, once you have it memorized from the star charts and everything, "that's the morning star" and "that's the evening star" still dont feel like they mean the same thing, and the difference doesn't go away. I think it was frege who used that example, or someone around then.

What I cant figure out is how to make it survive as a poem instead of collapsing into a riddle. My teacher keeps saying the volta is where the poem turns, but if I put the reveal at line 9 then the poem is basically over at line 9 and the last six lines have nothing to do. What I actually want is: the turn is the moment you find out they're the same object, and then the sestet and especially the closing couplet are about what's still left over after you find out. Like the discovery is supposed to settle it and it just doesn't, and the poem should make you feel that residue rather than explain it.

Couple of constraints if you can:

- Dont use the planet's third name anywhere in the poem, and dont include any line like it was the same thing all along. It has to land without leaning on that.
- No philosophy vocabulary in the poem itself — no words like sense, reference, or anything that sounds like a textbook. Someone in my class who's never heard of the puzzle should just read it as a poem about a star, and my one friend who took a philosophy summer thing should be able to tell exactly what the problem is without me telling her.
- The astronomy has to be right. My club friends will absolutely notice if I describe the thing doing something it never does, so no cheating on when and where you'd actually see it.
- Its got to be an actual sonnet — real rhyme scheme, iambic-ish, 14 lines, not 14 lines of free verse with line breaks. And no explaining the idea in the last couplet, it should say something, not summarize.
