Please explain, intuitively rather than by formal reconstruction, how the two dogmas in Quine's "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" are supposed to support one another. I follow each argument on its own: I can see why "analytic" resists explication if every route to it runs through cognitive synonymy, and I can see why it is implausible to assign each statement its own private range of confirming experiences. What I cannot see is why abandoning the second dogma is supposed to remove the ground for the first, rather than merely leave the first unexplained.

To sharpen this, suppose I grant confirmation holism and try to rebuild analyticity on top of it. Call a sentence protected if, for any recalcitrant experience, the best repair of our total theory is one that leaves that sentence standing; call it definitional if giving it up would count as changing the subject rather than as being mistaken. The hope is that this distinction is holism-compatible: holism says any statement can in principle be revised, but not that revision is always the cheapest repair, and "no revision needed, since the words mean what they mean" looks like a limiting case of a cheap repair.

Where does that move fail? In particular, does it collapse only once reductionism is given up, or does confirmation holism on its own already undercut it? And what does Quine put in the place of the distinction — merely a difference of degree in how centrally a statement is held? If so, is that degree a semantic fact about the sentence, or only a fact about our methodological preferences?
