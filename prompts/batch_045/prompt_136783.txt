The lecture only does the cute Prisoner's Dilemma case where both players submit something like "cooperate iff you'd cooperate with me" (or the provability-flavoured cousin, "cooperate iff I can prove that you cooperate with me") and everybody ends up with the symmetric cooperative payoff, and I want a problem that pushes past that, something that'll actually make the students argue with each other.

So the point of the problem should be that program equilibrium doesn't hand you the *fair* split, and I'd also like you to say something about which splits of the cooperative surplus are sustainable in general (my hazy guess is: anything that leaves each player with at least what they'd get at mutual defection, so you get a kind of folk-theorem flavour, but I don't trust that and I want it stated properly). Does that handicap actually buy player 1 anything?
