I am writing a novel set in a mid-sized English city during the two years after a policing scandal, and I need one document that exists inside the book: the joint statement that the two characters who disagree most deeply could both actually sign. Marcus Iliffe is a retiring chief superintendent who now consults on reform; Nadia Okonjo runs a neighbourhood mediation collective and argues that the function of policing should be dissolved rather than improved. They have sat on the same panels for eighteen months and have at last agreed to write one page together.

Please draft it as the product of an adversarial collaboration. Structure it as a numbered list of claims both can sign, followed by a table of the disagreements that remain. For each disagreement give the reformist formulation and the abolitionist formulation, each in wording that side would accept as a fair statement of its own view. Then set out the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of them, and for each crux give one case or piece of evidence both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

Four matters I want handled rather than gestured at. First, the capacity question: whether some incidents still call for an armed coercive response and, if so, who holds it and under whose authorisation. Test it on an invented case, say a violent domestic call at three in the morning in a ward where the nearest unarmed team is twelve minutes away. Second, the substitution test: if every present police task were transferred to agencies with different powers, funding and uniforms, which of them would still object, and does that show the objection is to the institution or to the capacity itself? Third, a wrinkle I want them to take seriously, that what the state monopolises is not only force but the authoritative record of what happened, the incident report that becomes the official version of events; ask whether the quarrel partly turns on that, and whether the objection would survive if only the record-keeping and not the force were moved elsewhere. Fourth, where the defund position belongs: tell me whether it is a third station or merely a timetable for arriving at one of the other two, and say which crux settles it.

Constraints. The statement will be pinned to the wall of a community hall in chapter nine, so it must read as something two exhausted people wrote at two in the morning and would still defend after breakfast: plain, unpolemical, free of rhetoric, both signatures at the bottom. No adjudication, no narrator's verdict, no footnotes. Nothing that requires looking anything up: no citations, no real cases, no figures about actual forces, and no appeal to authors, theories or traditions by name; every case, scheme and number should be invented for the book. After the table, add one line saying which part of the quarrel you take to be merely verbal.

British English throughout. No more than 700 words, table included.
