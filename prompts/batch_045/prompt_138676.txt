I'm putting together a seminar on social norms and I want to use Dagen H as the worked case for Lewis on convention — Sweden's switch from left- to right-hand traffic, 3 September 1967, 5am. I want the analysis, not the history, and I want it before I commit to it on a syllabus, so please be opinionated.

Facts as I have them, so nothing turns on your knowing them: all of Sweden's neighbours drove on the right, with roughly 5 million vehicle crossings a year; more than 90% of Swedish cars were already left-hand-drive, which made overtaking on two-lane roads genuinely dangerous; in a consultative referendum in 1955 something like 83% voted to keep driving on the left; the Riksdag went ahead anyway in 1963, set the date four years out, and ran a four-year campaign — signange, milk cartons, a song contest; on the day, non-essential traffic was banned 1am to 6am and drivers were told to stop, edge over to the right, stop again, then proceed. Accidents fell sharply and were back to roughly the old level within a couple of years. Railways and the Stockholm metro never switched.

What I want, in three parts. First, a disciplined mapping: who is the population here, what exactly is the recurring coordination problem, which are the equilibria, and where does arbitrariness go when the vehicle fleet and the road layout aren't up for grabs? Second, the claim I actually want to defend: that Lewis gives you a theory of convention-maintenance but no theory of convention-displacement. My fix is to split a convention's *grip* — what it costs one person to deviate while everyone else stays put — from its *depth* — how much of the surrounding payoff structure has been rebuilt around it: fleet, signage, intersections, muscle memory. High grip with shallow depth looks immovable and may be cheap to move if you can synchronise; Dagen H is my evidence, and the metro is my control case. Tell me if that's right, and whether it's an addition to Lewis or just Lewis relabelled. Third, say where Lewis goes silent: are Swedes driving on the right after 1967 *by convention*, or by law plus self-preservation, and can you give me a test that distinguishes them? And give me the best account you have of why 83% voting to keep left is not evidence that they were in the better equilibrium — and what that does to the arbitrariness claim.

Take a definite line, with reasons, and flag the point where you'd least want to be pushed.
