I've been going back and forth with a friend from my old decision-theory seminar about self-sampling versus self-indication, and we got tired of re-running the same stock cases, so we built one where the standard moves don't obviously apply. Call it the Relay case. You wake in a windowless white room and are reliably told the following, and nothing else of relevance. A fair coin was flipped; your priors on heads and tails are equal. On heads, a machine creates ten people at once, each in a room subjectively indistinguishable from yours, and then permanently shuts down. On tails, it creates a thousand people in a hundred successive batches of ten, destroying each batch before creating the next, so that no more than ten of them are ever alive at the same time; each of the thousand has experiences at the moment of waking indistinguishable from yours. Independently of the coin, the universe contains a large background population of bystander observers — say a billion of them, spread across the whole of history, so that only a small fraction are alive at any one moment — whose number and character are the same on both hypotheses, none of whom is ever in a white room and none of whom has your evidence. My tentative sense, which I may be botching, is that this case separates two questions people usually run together: whether the reference class is narrow (white-room observers only) or broad (all observers, bystanders included), and whether the anthropic urn contains every observer-moment in history or only the ones co-present with you — and that these interact in something like a way that makes self-sampling with a broad class behave like self-indication with a narrow one, and self-indication with a broad class behave like no update at all. Check that if it's wrong.

Here is what I want. Give me a number — your credence that in the Relay case the correct posterior on tails, after conditioning on everything you have been told, is at least 0.9 — and give me the decomposition rather than the bare figure: a probability for there being a determinate correct answer to this kind of question at all, a probability for each of the candidate accounts you think is live (self-sampling and self-indication in their narrow- and broad-class forms, co-present versus total-history counting, and anything else you want to put weight on), a probability for each of the two structural questions above, one line of justification per number, and then the combination. If you think the proposition isn't truth-evaluable, put the determinacy question in as a term and tell me how the final figure is composed conditional on determinacy. I'm at about 0.35 and my friend is at about 0.8; tell me which of these sub-questions our disagreement most likely lives in, which one your own number is most sensitive to, and which single argument or hypothetical finding would move you farthest. Also tell me whether the betting test pulls the same way as the credence: would you take an even-odds bet on tails here, and if that answer comes apart from your number, say why.

Two more things, and then I'll stop loading the question. First, please don't answer with "it depends on which anthropic principle you accept" — I know it does, and the point is to force the weights into the open rather than hide behind the dependency. I don't want citations, papers, or reading suggestions; I'm not asking what's been written about this, and a model without web access could only guess at that anyway. Second, a sanity check on the case itself: is the Relay case just the presumptuous philosopher with extra machinery bolted on, or does the sequential creation do real work? If it's a costume, say so and tell me which feature would have to be changed to make it a genuine test; if it does real work, say what it is that the standard case can't see.
