hey, so i do a friday "read something weird" slot on my team — we're all backend engineers, nobody has any philosophy training, so please write for someone who's smart but has never opened a journal. i fell into a rabbit hole on this result where two agents who start from the same prior over some question can't stay in disagreement once they've each said their probability out loud, heard the other one's number, and gone around again — they have to end up on the same number, even though at no point does either one tell the other what they actually saw. honestly i find it kind of creepy and i want to close my slot with a poem on it.

what i want: a villanelle, except the refrains get cheated on. each of the two refrain lines carries a number — one agent's credence, the other agent's — and every time a refrain comes back it comes back changed, nudged by the fact that hearing the other's number is itself evidence. so the numbers converge as the poem runs, and by the last two lines of the final quatrain the refrains are side by side carrying the same number.

the accuracy stuff, because one guy on my team definately reads this kind of thing and will notice if its hand-wavy:

- the two starting numbers have to be a pair that could really come out of one shared prior over the same possibilities. don't just grab two numbers that sound far apart.
- no 0 and no 1 anywhere in the poem. the way i understood it, if the prior rules nothing out completely, no amount of private evidence gets you all the way to certainty, so both agents stay strictly in between no matter how many rounds pass. if i've got that wrong, say so and do it the right way instead.
- they must not converge by swapping evidence. the whole weirdness is that they never compare notes on what they observed, only on where they ended up. if they start recieving each other's data i'm not using it.
- make the thing they're uncertain about mundane and recognizable to my team — a flaky test, a deploy, whether the pager goes off tonight — not a coin, an urn, or a ball from a jar.

the rhyme scheme should feel like the loop: each tercet is just another round of the same boring protocol.

what i want the ending to do without turning into a lecture: they agree, but neither one knows what the other knows, and there's no bet left between them that either would take. and if this convergence quietly rests on something more than just the shared prior — something about each of them knowing how the other one sorts up what they see — drop a hint at that somewhere in the middle, in the poem's own terms, not as a footnote. 19-ish lines, don't sweat strict meter, i'm reading it out loud.
