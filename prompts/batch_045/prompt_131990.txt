Before I commit to a rubric for scoring machine-generated variations on a theme, I want the specification that any such rubric would have to satisfy. The purpose is fixed: the theory has to be good enough to (a) grade a variation a system has already produced, (b) say in advance which outputs would count as cheating rather than as creative, and (c) say what a system would have to have represented in order to produce one. Not good enough to explain consciousness, genius, or the feeling of insight.

The starting picture I have in mind is Hofstadter's: a creator who has absorbed a style holds a theme as a bundle of adjustable parameters, the knobs; creative work is exploring that space by turning a few knobs at a time while holding fixed whatever makes the result a variation on that theme rather than a different piece; the difficulty is less the turning than knowing which knobs exist and which are load-bearing. I want the desiderata for an adequate account of this, with the case or consideration behind each.

Some candidate requirements I can already see, and which you should keep, refine, or reject:

- The twin failure modes: a variation can fail by being too close to its source (derivative, a souvenir) or too far from it (arbitrary, no longer answerable to it). A theory that can only measure distance and not say where the usable band is, and why it is where it is, has not delivered.
- The free-knob problem: transposing a tune into another key is a real transformation and is worth nothing; reharmonizing it is worth something. The theory has to distinguish knobs that carry the theme from knobs that are free, without smuggling in a hand-written list of approved transformations.
- One-shot variation: a musician hears a tune once and improvises on it. There is no corpus, only a single exemplar, and the theme has to be induced from it. What counts as the theme in that case, and what licenses one generalization from the exemplar rather than another?
- The shadow theme: much real work varies a genre, an oeuvre, or a house style rather than any stated theme. Say whether the theme being implicit is a complication or the normal case.
- The novelty/quality dissociation: a variation can be strikingly novel and bad, or familiar and excellent. The theory must not let one stand in for the other.
- Articulacy after the fact: it should be able to say which knob was turned, what was preserved, and why the result works. A mechanism that outputs variations but cannot give that account has produced something, not explained it.
- Anti-vacuity: if everything can be post hoc described as a variation on something, the theory explains nothing. It must be possible for it to rule some conceivable output out as not a variation, and ideally in advance rather than on inspection.
- Scope: need it cover domains where the theme is a functional brief rather than a melody or a text — engineering, design, mathematics? Or is cross-domain coverage a prejudice of people who want one theory of everything?

For each requirement you keep, give the case that motivates it, and mark it non-negotiable or a preference of one camp. Then the part I most want: which pairs of requirements I should stop trying to satisfy together. My suspicions are that specifying the knob space in advance is in tension with accounting for radical novelty, where the knobs themselves are new; that demanding recognizability fights the demand for departure; and that the anti-vacuity condition may be purchasable only by giving up cross-domain scope. Tell me which of those are real dilemmas and which dissolve on a better formulation.

Then say which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidate theories, and why you think it is that one rather than another — I would guess the anti-vacuity condition, but I would rather be argued out of it.

Finally, keep the scorecard short, two or three lines each, on the knob-twiddling model itself, blind variation and selective retention, conceptual blending, and the current view of large language models as remixers. The criteria are the deliverable; the scoring is only there to show the criteria bite.
