The shell is rigidly coupled to the bucket by a light axle, so the shell always rotates exactly with the bucket: at every instant the bucket and the shell are at rest relative to one another. Nothing else exists — no earth, no stars, no experimenter. We run the experiment.

Stage 1: right after the cord is released, the bucket and the shell spin around, but friction has not yet dragged the water. The water is still, while the shell rotates relative to it; thus the water has a large relative rotation with respect to the only other matter in the universe. If concavity is produced by rotation relative to matter, your relationism must predict that the water's surface is concave already at this stage. Newtonian mechanics predicts a flat surface here, because the water is not absolutely rotating.

Stage 2: later, the water has been dragged into co-rotation with the bucket. Now the water, bucket, and shell are all at rest relative to one another, so the water's relative rotation with respect to the only other matter in the universe is zero. If concavity requires rotation relative to matter, your relationism must predict a flat surface. Newtonian mechanics predicts a concave surface here, because the water is now absolutely rotating along with the rest of the universe.
