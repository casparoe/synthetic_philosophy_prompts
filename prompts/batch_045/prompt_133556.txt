Public health systems and insurers rank programmes by a single number that puts every health state on one cardinal scale and then sums it across people: relieving mild pain in very many patients beats relieving severe pain in a few, and in the same cost–benefit exercises the pain a policy causes is routinely offset against mere convenience or consumer enjoyment priced in dollars. Yet the same institutions appear to work on the opposite principle at the bedside: a patient in severe pain is relieved now, whatever it costs and whatever else that money could have bought, and no one defers that patient's analgesia on the grounds that the funds would relieve more pain if spent elsewhere.

I want the implicit theory, and specifically its fit with lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism — the view that there is an intensity of suffering past which no quantity of milder suffering, and no quantity of mere pleasure, can outweigh a single instance. Reconstruct what the aggregating practice needs about cardinality, interpersonal comparability, and the commensurability of pleasure with pain; then say which of those it strictly presupposes and which it merely fits well with. Could someone who accepts a lexical threshold still run these rankings — for instance by claiming that no option on the menu ever crosses the threshold, or that summing is legitimate among sub-threshold pains? Is the bedside half of the practice genuinely inconsistent with the budgetary half, or does each admit a reading that reconciles them (a deontic side-constraint on clinicians, an error theory about the rule of rescue, something else)? And what would concretely have to change if the threshold view were adopted: what gets defunded, what gets funded first, and where does the view leave practice indeterminate, because the threshold's height and the unit of aggregation — a moment, an episode, a life — are unsettled? One further wrinkle: the pain scales used in these settings grade pain along more than one axis, roughly how badly it hurts versus how much it distresses the patient, so say which axis such a threshold would have to track and why.
