Hey, I somehow got elected secretary of the board of the Westhampton Farmers' Market this fall — small nonprofit, 38 vendors, a summer market on the square and a winter market in the old Grange hall that starts the first Saturday of December, so three weeks out. I have to circulate the memo for Thursday's meeting and I'd really like help writing it, because the argument we're about to have is one of those that sounds dumb when you describe it and turns out not to be.

Here's the case. Rule 4(b) of our market rules says: "Only produce, cut flowers, honey, eggs, and dairy grown or produced by the vendor within a fifty (50) mile radius of Market Square may be sold at the Market." Northfield Greens has applied for one stall for the winter season. They grow lettuce, herbs and microgreens hydroponically — no soil, LED lights, heated — in a converted warehouse eleven miles from the square; they employ nine people, they aren't a family farm, and about two thirds of what they grow goes to two supermarket chains and a restaurant distributor. They'd be growing and selling straight through the winter, which almost nobody else around here can do. Dale, who's been on the board forever, is very loud about this, has been posting in the town Facebook group, and has hinted he'll resign: he says they aren't growers in any sense the rule was meant to cover, and that letting a warehouse operation in will wreck the market's identity and cost us our best vendors. Priya says the opposite, and she has spreadsheets: she claims a heated and lit warehouse in a New England January puts out more carbon per pound of lettuce than trucking field lettuce up from three hundred miles away, so if the rule is really about food miles then the text gives the wrong answer here. Nobody on the board can check her numbers before Thursday. Three other things are in play: the only note in the minutes from when the rule was adopted in 2009 is something like "to keep this a market for local growers," and nobody agrees whether that means the point was who's doing the selling, or food miles, or propping up small family farms, or just freshness and the town's buy-local branding; our own precedent is a mess, since two years ago we let in a guy growing mushrooms on logs in his basement eight miles away, three years ago we turned away a honey producer at fifty-five, and last winter we approved a cider maker whose apples came from sixty miles off on the theory that the cider was produced by the vendor; and there's a waitlist, where the person at number three is a dairy farmer who lost a barn in a fire and really needs that winter stall.

What I need is the memo, not a general essay about reading rules. Bottom line for this season — admit, deny, or admit with conditions — with reasons a vendor who's unhappy could actually answer, and an honest treatment of the strongest thing on the other side instead of a balanced list of pros and cons. Some things I'd want it to deal with head on: whether we should look at the 2009 minutes at all or whether that's the same fight courts have about legislative history; what "grown" even does when there's no soil involved; whether the fifty miles is as the crow flies or by road, which matters for a beekeeper on the waitlist who is forty-seven miles straight and fifty-three by road; how to handle our three inconsistent past decisions without it looking like we pick favorites; and what to do given that we can't fix the rule before the winter market, since amendments take a two-thirds vote of the full membership at the annual meeting in mid-March and have to be mailed to members thirty days ahead of that. My brother-in-law, who is a lawyer and was three beers in, kept saying "just read the text," and then said something about purposivism and absurd results that I didn't follow, so if the memo could say in plain language whether that courts-and-statutes argument even transfers to a nonprofit's own rules, that would help me enormously. And if you do go the purpose route, please tell me which purpose we're supposed to be using — the one the 2009 board had in mind, or the one the rule now does the most good by — because I think that's where Dale and Priya are talking past each other. Whatever the recommendation is, I need it with dates.
