Start by laying out the logical space — the questions and which combinations of answers are available — and only then drop the existing views into it, telling me which boxes are empty and why (incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored). Cases I want doing the work: a brain emulation running on silicon instead of neurons; a giant lookup table that reproduces every input-output pair of a human life without any real processing going on; the same simulation run frame by frame but in scrambled causal order; a lab-grown cortical organoid; a system whose internal states get recorded but never feed back into anything it does; and the same computation run a million times over. I also want at least one branch about how to count subjects — the computation copied, split across two machines, or merged with another one — since where the boundary of a sentient thing falls matters as much as whether it's sentient at all.

Six to eight questions, eight to ten leaves, aimed at readers with college-level science and no philosophy background: each node has to be something someone can answer without knowing where it leads, so nothing like "are you a functionalist?". No citations or further reading, I just want the tree.
