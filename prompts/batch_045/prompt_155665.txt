I'm a teaching assistant for a graduate seminar on explanation in evolutionary biology, and next week we're doing the adaptationist programme and the Gould–Lewontin spandrels critique. I want a piece of mock-documentary satire to hand out alongside the readings: a parody of the compliance form that a fictional journal — call it the *Journal of Ultimate Design* — requires every submitted manuscript to file, a "Certification of Adaptation," submitted by a fictional lab (Brocklebank Institute for Ultimate Causation) in support of a paper arguing that the near-universal human disposition to press the "door close" button in an elevator — including in elevators where the button is disconnected, which is most of them — is an adaptation produced by a dedicated closure-response module, shaped in the ancestral environment by the fitness value of rapidly sealing an enclosure against predators, cold, and intruders. Roughly 700–900 words, laid out as a numbered form: a manuscript header block, eight or nine numbered certification items the authors must swear to, and a closing line recording the editorial decision.

The jokes have to come from getting the methodology right rather than from inventing absurdities, so exaggerate real features of adaptationist practice, don't fabricate new ones: the atomization of a trait into sub-traits until a function can be found for one of them (and then speaking of the whole as a "trait complex"); the argument from costliness or "special design"; reverse-engineering an ancestral function and then treating the elevator as a supernormal stimulus exploiting a mismatch with the ancestral environment; a small optimality model with a cost-benefit curve that is fitted rather than derived; a comparative prediction from a single outgroup; and the ritual dismissal of the byproduct and developmental-constraint alternatives as untestable, unfalsifiable, or a mere redescription of the phenomenon to be explained. In the same spirit, give the form a quantitative confidence device — a "Design Confidence Score" obtained by multiplying the authors' own ratings for costliness, specificity of design, cross-cultural universality, and so on, with a pass threshold, and a clause permitting authors whose score falls short to subdivide the trait and resubmit. The authors should be competent and deadpan throughout: everything they sign should be something a real, good adaptationist could write with a straight face, because the reductio depends on their competence — a reader should feel that a practitioner following the form faithfully could certify almost anything, without the text ever saying so.

Two further constraints. First, include at least two bracketed interventions from the journal's in-house spandrel skeptic interleaved with the items; at least one must be a genuinely strong objection (that the behavior is best explained as a byproduct of a general-purpose causal-contact or goal-completion system plus a modern artifact, with no selection history of its own), and the authors' answer to it should be recognizable, on reflection, as not answering it at all. Second, never name Gould or Lewontin or any living researcher — have the authors refer to their critics only obliquely, as the San Marco school or the Pangloss-skeptics — and don't let anyone break character to signal that this is a joke. No citations or bibliography are needed; the piece should be legible and funny to a newcomer who has only half-remembered the debate, while making someone who actually knows the literature wince in recognition.
