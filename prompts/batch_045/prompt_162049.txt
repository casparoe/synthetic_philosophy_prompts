so I run this tiny newsletter where I take things that definately should not be productized and write fake corporate documentation for them (onboarding docs, status pages, help centers). I want one on transitional justice, specifically the truth-for-amnesty bargain: the state offers to drop prosecution if perpetrators give a full account of what they did, and in the process it ends up deciding what the record of the whole period says. My readers are mostly engineers, so assume they know nothing about the topic and don't make it depend on any real country, commission, or date — invent the country and the office running the program.

What I want: a help-center FAQ page for the online amnesty application portal, in the flat, cheerful voice of a government product team. Exactly five numbered entries, each one a question line plus an answer of one or two sentences, no intro and no conclusion, no framing around it, ideally 250-350 words total.

The jokes have to come from features the real bargain actually has, not from made-up cartoon evil. Roughly one per entry, and I'd like at least four of these to show up somewhere:

- amnesty is conditional on "full disclosure," but the completeness of a disclosure is graded by the same state that has no independent way to check it
- the people with the strongest reason to apply are the ones already caught or already in prison; the people who were never investigated have no reason to, so the archive ends up made mostly of what the state already knew
- the application nobody can contradict is the strongest one — no surviving witnesses, no accomplices, no paperwork — so the cleanest files belong to the acts that were most successfully hidden
- the form requires the act to have had a political objective, and the dropdown only offers political categories, so killings over a debt or a field get quietly re-narrated to fit
- victims can submit an impact statement into the same system, and it doesn't affect whether the grant goes through; they can't veto or appeal either
- there's a hard submission deadline, disclosures get thinner and more copy-pasted toward the end, and once the portal closes the final dataset becomes the country's official memory — the gaps aren't listed anywhere because there's no field for them

Keep it deadpan. Don't let anyone wink, don't add an aside saying "isn't this awful," and don't break character to explain the critique — a reader should be able to tell what's being made fun of just from how straight the page plays it. Someone who has actually read about this stuff should think the piece is mean about the right things.
