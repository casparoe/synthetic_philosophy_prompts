I work as a statistician, mostly on survey and registry data, and the vocabulary I actually think in is this one: a target population and a sampling frame; known inclusion probabilities; design-based versus model-based (superpopulation) inference; finite-population quantities estimated from draws; inverse-probability-of-selection weighting, post-stratification, and calibration on marginals; selection nodes and collider bias in DAGs; size-biased and length-biased sampling and the inspection paradox; capture–recapture and serial-number estimation of a closed population; and, when I go Bayesian, marginal likelihoods and Bayes factors. I have no formal training in philosophy, and what I know about anthropic reasoning comes from papers I read on my own.

What I would like is a faithful restatement, in that vocabulary, of the dispute between the self-sampling assumption and the self-indication assumption, together with an honest report on what the translation fails to carry.

My own first pass, which I suspect is wrong in places: SSA looks like a design-based stance — fix the world, then treat me as a single draw from a specified frame of actual observers, so that nothing about the size of the frame follows from the bare fact of my being a draw. SIA looks like a model-based stance in which the number of draws is itself random, and my existing at all is a datum whose likelihood is proportional to how many draws there are — a likelihood that is size-biased by the observer count. On that reading, the doomsday-argument inference is ordinary finite-population estimation from one observed serial number; the Sleeping Beauty disagreement is a disagreement about the unit of analysis, subjects versus observation-episodes, and hence about clustering and weights; and the presumptuous philosopher is a Bayes factor equal to the ratio of observer counts, which is why it feels like it is getting something for nothing.

Please do the following.

1. Correct that mapping where it is wrong, and say which of my categories — design, frame, inclusion probability, likelihood, prior, weighting — each assumption actually occupies. If one of the two is not a sampling assumption in any sense I would recognise, say what it is instead.

2. Take the reference class problem and treat it the way I would treat a contested sampling frame: sometimes the frame is fixed by the design, sometimes it is partly observed and I post-stratify, and sometimes I have nothing to go on and run a sensitivity analysis over plausible frames. Is the reference-class problem an instance of that ordinary under-specification, or does it have a structure my vocabulary cannot represent?

3. The datum in these problems is not a measurement of an external event but something like: I exist, I am this one, and it is now. Likelihoods in my work have no slot for a first-personal or self-locating coordinate. Say exactly where the translation has to add machinery my framework does not contain, and whether adding it is a neutral representational choice or already takes sides between the two assumptions.

4. Under one assumption my existence is evidence about how many observers there are; under the other it is not. But there is no observation I could have made in which I failed to find myself existing, and in my vocabulary a datum with equal likelihood under every hypothesis carries no evidential weight. Locate precisely where the evidential force comes from, and say whether the difference between the assumptions is a difference in likelihood, a difference in prior, or a difference in something that is neither.

5. Give me an identifiability assessment. With effectively one draw and no repeatability, is the choice between the assumptions identifiable in the sense I would demand of two rival models, or do they agree on every observable? Answer separately whether the choice is decision-relevant: is there any decision problem on which the two prescriptions come apart? In my field a convention that changes no decision-relevant output is a convention, and a choice that does is an assumption worth arguing about, and I want to know which of these I am looking at.

6. Finish with a verdict on the residue. Where the translation was clean, where you had to distort, and where there was simply nothing to say in my terms: does that pattern point to a gap in the statistical vocabulary — no home for self-locating uncertainty, no way to specify a frame from inside the sample — or does it point to the philosophical dispute being less substantive than its literature makes it sound? I am open to a third option, namely that both assumptions are incomplete specifications of one sampling model and that my framework is the wrong place to complete them; if that is what you think, say so and say what the missing specification is.

One constraint on the translation: do not quietly substitute for either assumption some neighbouring claim that is easier to express in my terms and then present the result as the translation. Where the fit is bad, I would rather be told than reassured.
