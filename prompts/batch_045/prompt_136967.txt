Background, stated in my own terms so that nothing here depends on how any particular author phrases it. On etiological (teleological) approaches to naturalizing content, an internal state represents a condition roughly when it has the proper function of indicating that condition, where the function is conferred by the creature's selection history rather than by what the state happens to track at present. The view earns its keep by making systematic misrepresentation possible: the state can be tokened by something other than what it is about. On covariance or information-theoretic approaches, content rides on what the state reliably indicates in the creature's actual or normal environment, which makes systematic error hard to locate and drags in something like the disjunction problem. The case below is one I have constructed precisely to sit where these two motivations come apart, so please work the case as stated rather than substituting a more familiar one.

A species of small amphibian has an internal state, call it D, produced by a mechanism M that tokens D whenever the ratio of long- to short-wavelength light reaching the eye exceeds a fixed threshold. In the ancestral forest-floor habitat that ratio is exceeded only during the last forty minutes of daylight; call that condition C1. A downstream mechanism K responds to D by triggering retreat into a burrow and a metabolic slowdown. This arrangement has a history of selection: retreating at C1 reduced predation by nocturnal hunters, and the slowdown conserved energy. So D has the proper function of indicating C1, and K has the proper function of producing retreat in response to C1.

A population migrates to a high-latitude marsh. There, in the summer months, the ratio is never exceeded at dusk, but it is exceeded for a few minutes around noon whenever an algal bloom in the water column filters the light; call that condition C2. In the marsh, C1 never tokens D and C2 tokens D reliably and uniquely. Retreat at C2 also turns out to be advantageous, but for an entirely unrelated reason: noon sun through a bloom coincides with a UV and desiccation hazard, and sitting it out prevents tissue damage. So D is a perfectly reliable indicator of C2, and the behavior D produces is exactly right. Stipulate that over the first thirty generations nothing has died from the hazard and nothing has lost out by retreating: no differential reproduction, no selection on either mechanism. I do not want the answer "eventually selection would sort this out." I want the verdict inside the window.

Variant A. After thirty generations, neither M nor K has been touched by selection in the marsh. Every actual tokening of D is caused by C2, every retreat is successful, and the only selection history in play is the ancestral one in which D meant C1.

Variant B. Same as A, except that over those thirty generations selection has acted on a modifier that makes K's response to D swifter and longer-lasting, because sluggish retreat is punished by the UV hazard. M itself is unchanged: same threshold, same wiring, same reliability. There is now a selection history for consuming D in the marsh, and none for producing it.

Here is the number I want. Give me your credence that the correct treatment of variant B assigns D a determinate content that is C2 — the algal-noon condition — rather than C1, rather than an indeterminate or disjunctive content, rather than no content at all. Note what makes this hard: etiological views standardly want a fit between the producer's function and the consumer's function, with the content being the condition such that the consumer's success is explained by the producer's indicating it. In variant B the producer's history says C1 and the consumer's history says C2, and no single condition in the animal's history occupies that explanatory role.
