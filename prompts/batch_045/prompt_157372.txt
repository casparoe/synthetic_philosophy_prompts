im a statistician — i mostly fit latent-variable models and do uncertainty quantification — and I keep running into this fight in philosophy of physics about whether the wavefunction is ontic (a real thing out there) or epistemic (our information / uncertainty about something else). I want that fight restated as faithfully as possible in the vocabulary I actually use, and then I want the residue report.

My vocabulary: parameters vs latent variables, identifiability, priors and posteriors, predictive distributions, sufficient statistics, posteriors that happen to overlap, ensembles or particles as samples, non-identifiability as plain underdetermination by the data. Also: I already use the words epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty all the time, where epistemic means "uncertainty about the parameters because I only saw finite data." I strongly suspect that is NOT what the physicists mean by psi-epistemic, and I want that collision dealt with head-on rather than quietly glossed. My exposure to the philosophy side is blog-level, so define anything technical on their side before you translate it.

Here's my own attempt, which I want you to either sharpen or demolish. Treat a preparation procedure like a training protocol, and the wavefunction like something it outputs — the fitted parameters? the predictive distribution those parameters define? a posterior over latent states? — and then read the whole dispute as a question about whether the map from wavefunctions to distributions-over-latent-states is injective, or whether two different preparations can produce distributions that overlap. If that's roughly the shape of it, make it precise; if it's badly off, say exactly where it breaks.

Then please do three things. (1) Translate each of the two views into my terms, as honestly as you can — don't smuggle in ML assumptions and call them translation. (2) Translate the main argument that's supposed to kill the epistemic view, the one that needs something like "if two systems are prepared independently the joint is the product" (i half remember a result from around 2012 that does this, but I don't want names, papers, or dates — I can't check them and I don't care). (3) The residue: what translated cleanly, what only went through with distortion, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. Two things I especially want your view on. First, in ML I can always posit more latents for free, and I treat non-identifiability as a boring fact about my data rather than a metaphysical question — so does my vocabulary have no room for the word "real" at all, and is that a blind spot in the ML framing or evidence that the quantum dispute is thinner than it sounds? Second, is there anything in my toolkit with no counterpart on their side — does anything I do look like their product structure for independently prepared systems, or is entanglement precisely the point where the analogy dies?

End by telling me whether the residue makes you think the physicists are arguing about something ML is missing, or whether a statistician is entitled to think the whole thing was never quite well posed.
