I've been trying to work out what I actually think about conceivability as a guide to possibility by just going case by case, and I keep ending up with a pile of verdicts that don't obviously add up to one rule.

- A world where every neuron fires exactly as it does here, everyone says and does everything they say and do here, and nobody is home — no what-it's-like at all. I can hold this one, it survives every attempt to fill in detail, and it does move me toward thinking it's possible.
- The clear drinkable stuff in the lakes and taps not being H2O. Every time I try, the imagining slides off into a different scenario — some other liquid, or somebody's mistake. No evidence for me, and I think I can say why.
- There being a largest prime. Collapses straight away, and I'd just call it impossible.
- Goldbach's conjecture being true but unprovable, ever, in the usual formalisation. I can't fill this one in at all — there's nothing it would be like — but I still want to count it as some evidence. My friend says I'm cheating, since it fails the same fill-in test the largest prime case fails.
- Vividly "conceiving" that I'm located over by the mannequin instead of in my chair. Zero evidence, because I can name a mechanism — a vestibular conflict — that would produce exactly this feeling in a completely normal person even if the thing were impossible.
- I can't visualize a space with ten spatial dimensions at all, and I'm certain it's possible.
- A universe beginning without any cause: the tenth time I try feels much more OK then the first, and that growth makes me trust it less, not more.

So: is there one principle behind the fill-in test, the rehearsal thing, and the mechanism test, or am I quietly running three different rules? What does it say about the case I keep flip-flopping on — conceiving that every one of my apparently successful conceivings is actually a failure and its object impossible, which I can't fill in but which doesn't collapse either? And wich of my verdicts does the principle force me to drop?
