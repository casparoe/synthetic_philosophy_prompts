Machine-learning engineers deal constantly with a failure pattern they call shortcut learning. A classifier is trained on labelled images — photographs tagged as wolf or as husky, say — performs well on held-out data from the same source, and then fails in deployment. On inspection it turns out to be tracking the background rather than the animal: the snow, the ruler in a clinical photograph, the metal marker left on a hospital scanner. The team's settled way of describing this is that the system has not really learned what a wolf is, that it is really a snow detector, and that its output was right but for the wrong reason. Their settled way of settling the matter, when two engineers disagree about what the network really represents, is to intervene: paste the animal onto a new background and see whether the output flips.

I would like to know what theory of representational content this bundle of practices presupposes, and how far it transfers to the project of naturalising mental content. On what is the distinction between the feature the label names and the feature the network uses supposed to depend — the history of how the system was trained, the intentions of the people who built or deployed it, or its actual causal-discriminative profile? Note the tension: the verdict that it is really a snow detector withholds content from the very feature the system responds to, which looks historical or intentional, whereas the test used to adjudicate that verdict fixes content by actual causal role, on which reading nothing is left for the system to get wrong. Can the practice have both? What must represent, error, and right answer for the wrong reason mean for these claims to be true, and which of the familiar accounts — selected-effect or etiological, causal-informational, functional-role — can supply it? Distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and say where it is underdetermined and several theories would license it.

Two complications I want addressed. First, nothing in the set-up selected the background feature: no one intended it, and gradient descent has no intentions; the correlation was simply there in the data. Whose selection history, if anyone's, is doing the normative work, and what becomes of proper function when the function-conferring history is a blind optimisation process applied to an artefact? Second, the remedy is itself a classifier — a set of counterfactual test images, or a detector trained to flag shortcut reliance — and it too may be exploiting some feature nobody intended. Does the practice generate a regress, and if so where does it stop; or does the regress show that the talk of really representing was never doing the work the engineers thought it was?

Finally: what would the engineers' reports have to look like if they abandoned the historical element and read content off causal role alone, and what in the natural world would then make it a fact that the wolf-detecting network is wrong?

Please answer in British English, in about 600 to 800 words.
