I work on compiler and developer-tooling infrastructure, and I fell sideways into the language of thought hypothesis while reading about it after an argument at work. The argument is one my team keeps having: our platform represents domain objects as a structured intermediate representation, with a grammar, immutable nodes, and operations defined over structure, and a group on the other side of the building wants to replace it with learned dense embeddings that generalize by similarity. Reading about the philosophy made me realize the older dispute is not really about biology at all, it is about whether you can get structure-sensitive processing out of a representational format that has no syntax. I have no philosophy background, so please don't assume I know the literature's vocabulary; I'll tell you the target in my own words and you can use better words in the piece.

The target is the classical version of the language of thought hypothesis, associated with Jerry Fodor, and specifically three things I take to be load-bearing in it. First, that thinking is computation over mental representations with combinatorial syntax, and that the operations are sensitive to the structure of the representations rather than to their similarity to one another. Second, the arguments from productivity and systematicity: a finite stock of primitives yields unbounded thoughts, and capacity is not piecemeal but comes in blocks, so that a system that can represent one structured thought can represent the systematically related ones, which is supposed to be the objection to the connectionist-style rival. Third, the regress about learning: acquiring a representational system by hypothesis testing seems to presuppose a system at least as expressive as the one being acquired, so the format cannot itself be learned, and the view gets pushed toward the claim that concepts are innate, arguably all of them, including mundane artifact concepts and even concepts for things that did not exist until very recently. I want the piece to be fair to all three. If any of my three is a caricature, quietly use the defensible version instead of the version I garbled, and let the satire come from that.

What I want is a parody of an internal engineering document: a design-doc or RFC-style migration memo, 700 to 900 words, dated and versioned, written by the lead of the team that owns the mind's representation layer and addressed to a rival team that wants to rewrite it as embeddings. Treat the mind as a legacy system whose internal format has a stable grammar, and treat concept acquisition as a product surface. The memo should be deadpan throughout, in the register of a competent staff engineer who is not trying to be funny, and it should reach, with a straight face, the recommendations that follow from taking the argument seriously: that no genuinely new representational capacity has ever been shipped, that what looks like learning is a feature-flag flip on the initial release, that the training pipeline and the onboarding research roadmap should be shut down and their headcount redeployed, and a shipping manifest listing, as already present in version 1.0, concepts whose instances arrived long after the binary and some that arrived in the last few decades. The humor has to come from the argument being rendered correctly, so that someone who has actually read the systematicity argument and the learning regress laughs because the memo is a faithful application of them; no workplace-sitcom gags, no puns on neural or net, and no invented objections. Where the view faces a real objection, the memo may bite the bullet but may not concede that concepts can be learned, and its author should not describe themselves as having been persuaded of anything by a meeting.

Two more constraints. The piece has to be legible as a reductio without anyone breaking character: include at most one short dissent block from the embeddings team, in the same dry register, and let the memo answer it only by restating the argument louder rather than by rebutting it. And it has to work for my colleagues who have never opened a philosophy paper, so the workplace consequences have to carry the comedy on their own. After the document ends, add nothing else except one final line in your own voice, on its own line, in this exact form: \answer{the single premise the memo's recommendations are a reductio of, as a short phrase of at most twelve words}.
