hey, help me settle an argument in the group chat for the little funding pot I help run — we do maybe six grants a year, ~50k each, mostly long-shot stuff aimed at big-picture risks.

one of the other organizers is a hard expected value fanatic and he's got an argument I keep half-accepting. he says: yeah, EV fanaticism sounds nuts in the abstract — you'd liquidate everything to buy a one-in-a-billion shot at 10^40 lives, Pascal's-mugging-style — but that intuition only bites if you imagine you get handed one bet and then the lights go out. we get many bets. maximizing EV is just running a venture fund: nearly every company you fund returns zero, you write the checks anyway, and the whole thing works because the payoffs are insanely skewed and you hold a portfolio. so stop worrying about the fanaticism objection and take the long shots; the tiny probabilities get averaged over a career of granting.

something's bugging me though. someone else in the chat pointed out that the VC logic needs the portfolio to be diversified in a way ours maybe isn't: our long shots aren't independent draws, they're all bets on roughly the same underlying thing (this century not going catastrophically badly), so if that goes badly they all go to zero together, and if it goes fine maybe they all pay out together. plus you don't get to re-run the century, you can't reinvest after a wipeout, and there's only a handful of these opportunities a year anyway.

can you audit the analogy properly? give me the correspondence table — fund, LP, startup, exit, diversification, fund lifetime — then the places the mapping breaks, and separate the disanalogies that actually matter for his conclusion from the ones that are just noise. i specifically want to know: is the correlation-across-bets thing the load-bearing problem, or is the bigger one that at one-in-a-million you basically can't distinguish 10^-6 from 10^-9, so the "edge" isn't known rather than known-and-small? eight of our grants get their numbers from the same guy's model, which feels less like holding eight tickets and more like holding eight copies of one ticket. also: is there a better analogy? my mate suggested an insurance company, but then someone pointed out insurers mostly refuse the top layer of correlated catastrophe risk and buy reinsurance, which seems to cut against him, and now i'm not sure whether "portfolio" is even the right frame or whether research-with-option-value is (one of our grants is basically just "pay someone to estimate the probability better", and i can't tell if that's a bet or a meta-bet).

does the "so be fanatics" conclusion survive any of this, or was the portfolio picture smuggling it in the whole time? we have to decide by friday lol.
