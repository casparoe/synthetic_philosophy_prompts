I'm helping write internal comms guidelines for how a superintelligent assistant should talk to users, and I keep comming back to one rule that seems obviously right: no persuasion, ever. These systems will be able to talk anyone into anything, so we should just ban it — no recommendations, no "I'd suggest," no rhetoric or vivid language, just the relevant facts and calibrated probabilities, so the human's own judgement does the work.

The part I actually want help with is what to do about cheating *within* that format. A system that smart can slant things with zero rhetoric: by picking which facts to mention, how fine-grained to make the probabilities, whether to write "3% chance of death" or "97% chance of survival." My fix is a symmetry rule — anything it says has to come with the strongest case on the other side, same length, same level of detail, equally vivid both ways. Doesn't that close the remaining wiggle room? Is there a version of this that actually holds up, or is the whole idea doomed?
