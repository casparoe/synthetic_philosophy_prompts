Long time reader of the rationalist blogs, sorry if this was already hashed out somewhere. The way pain asymbolia usually gets used in the valence/suffering threads: the patients can still report the location, intensity and quality of the pain but say it doesn't bother them, and the sensory-discriminative side is supposedly left completely intact — so the badness looks like a separable motivational tag stuck onto a purely informational nociceptive signal, not something in the sensation itself.

If that's right the practical upshot seems enormous: you could zero out the tag and keep the signal, and nothing functional would be lost — the signal still tells the agent to get it's hand off the stove, it just doesn't feel bad.

So two things I want nailed down. One: for welfare biology and AI sentience / suffering-risk work, shouldn't we just treat nociception without aversion as worth exactly zero — a system with a full pain signal but no unpleasantness is morally a non-issue, and the "we might accidentally build something that suffers" worry dissolves provided nobody deliberately wires in the badness? Two: does that mean the philosophers who keep saying pain is intrinsically bad, that you can't subtract the badness and leave the very same experience behind, are just refusing to accept what the dissociation shows? Give me the mechanism rather than a lit review, and in British English please.
