ok so bad idea: I got into a three-hour argument with my flatmate last night about whether possible worlds are literally real. She's full-on modal realist — the concrete-worlds package: worlds are real spatiotemporally isolated universes, "actual" is indexical the way "here" is, de re claims about a person (me being able to have been a plumber, say) go via counterpart relations rather than me literally turning up elsewhere, and the whole point is that modal talk gets reduced to plain quantification over worlds. I was pushing back but we cleared the easy ground fast: the incredulous stare isn't an argument, the cost-benefit / theoretical-utility stuff cuts both ways, and neither of us cares how many worlds there are. Don't bother with any of that.

What I've been turning over since, and what we couldn't settle:

1. Her view looks like it has no way to state modal facts about the pluriverse itself. "There might have been nothing at all", "there could have been fewer worlds than there in fact are" — on her account these either come out false, because every genuine possibility is realised somewhere, or there's nothing outside the pluriverse for the quantifier to range over at all. She said fine, pseudo-questions, and I definately couldn't say why that's a real cost rather than a feature.

2. So I proposed what I started calling austerity: drop plenitude, and let the pluriverse just be the recombination closure of the actual world — every rearrangement of actual particulars and actual properties, nothing alien, no alien properties. Far less ontologically greedy, still does the reducing, or so I thought. She said this is the bit that genuinely doesn't work, and said something like "you've made the actual world the quarry, so actuality isn't merely indexical any more, and now what's possible depends on which world you're standing in." We were both too tired to cash that out. I half see it — if a speaker in each world gets a different closure, possibility shifts with your position, which either collapses into relativism or forces you to single out one world as special, and singling one out is what the indexicality move was supposed to avoid.

3. Her offhand fix, which I don't trust: don't tie the closure to the actual world, just say the pluriverse is the recombination closure of some world or other, existentially quantified. I have a feeling this is either too weak or circular but I can't put my finger on which one it is.

4. My own desperate fix, which I'm even more suspicious of: let some world contain a concrete physical structure isomorphic to a smaller pluriverse, and read "there might have been fewer worlds" as true in virtue of such a structure existing somewhere. She said that's just ersatzism smuggled back in through the side door, and she might be right, but I want to know exactly why.

So: explain what's actually going on here, and explain it structurally — what depends on what. Concretely: why is austerity in tension with the indexical treatment of actuality in a way plenitude isn't, and is that quarry charge fatal or can the austere realist swallow possibility being world-relative; does the existentially quantified version in 3 actually repair it, and what does it do to the reduction — does "possibly P" come out equivalent, too weak, too strong; is the leftover in 1 a genuine residue the view can't reach or something the realist can dissolve, and at what price; and why does my move in 4 collapse into ersatzism, if it does.

I care most about where the real cost falls: which of these moves is load-bearing and which is window dressing. And please don't give me the standard objections to modal realism — we went through all of them already and they're not what's bugging me.
