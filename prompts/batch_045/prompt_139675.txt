I am applying for a one-year dissertation fellowship and need the spine of the proposal. The topic is the difficulty of telling apart a person's genuine preferences and her biases — the sort of case where someone says, of a woman who has declined the career she was quietly steered away from, or of a worker who reports being content with a dangerous job, that the preference is not really hers. My half-formed idea is that the debate keeps stalling because it asks for one thing where it needs several: 'biased' gets pressed into service to discount a preference in the measurement of well-being, to void its authority in consent, to exclude it from moral aggregation, and to locate criticism in the agent rather than in her situation, and I suspect these jobs come apart and call for different tests.

The angle I want to build the project on is a neutrality constraint. An institution — a state agency, a hospital ethics committee, a benefits administrator — is normally barred from appealing to a contested conception of justice, which appears to rule out the two standard tests: the historical test (this preference would not have formed under just conditions) and the error test (it rests on a false belief, where the falsity is itself what is contested). So I want to propose a fourth test and ask how far it can carry us: call a preference tracking-deficient when it shifts in response to a factor that the agent herself, on reflection, would classify as irrelevant to what she values in the object — how the option is framed, the order in which options are presented, the identity of who else benefits, her own momentary social position — even though the preference purports to track only features of the thing itself. The questions I want sharpened are: which of the four tests entails which, if any; whether tracking-deficiency is the only one an institution required to stay neutral about justice can apply; whether applying it still requires a second-order judgment about which factors are irrelevant, a judgment vulnerable to the same charge, so that the apparatus collapses either into scepticism about bias or into unlimited paternalism; and which of the four jobs each test can legitimately do.

Please turn this into the spine of the proposal. Give it as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion: (1) the research question, stated so that it has a determinate answer; (2) why the existing positions leave it open, naming the specific gap rather than the literature; (3) the method, quarter by quarter over twelve months; (4) what a positive result would look like, and separately what would count as the project having failed; (5) the scope — what is deliberately left out so the project fits in one year, and why the remainder is still worth a year of funding. The whole thing should come to roughly 400 words.

Write it as philosophy rather than as a map of a debate; argue that this work is worth doing and can be done in the time.
