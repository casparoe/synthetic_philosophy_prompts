I assess habitat-loss applications for a regional planning authority — around sixty a year, mostly conversion of rough grassland, scrub and wetland to housing, solar arrays and intensive agriculture. Most are decided at officer level, but any application with a contested animal-welfare assessment goes to committee. The remit was recently widened: alongside biodiversity, we must now record the expected effect of the loss on the animals themselves. I have half a day per application, a desk study plus one site visit, no population modelling, and two pages to fill in.

Please write me a decision procedure my team can run on a proposal to determine whether the net welfare effect of the conversion is positive, negative, or genuinely indeterminate, and — where it is one or the other — roughly how large. Give the questions in the order I should ask them, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to the committee because the remaining question is one of values rather than facts. In particular it has to settle how I handle these:

whether to count only the animals alive on the site when the decision is made, or also the animals whose births the conversion prevents, given that most of the welfare effect of habitat loss seems to run through births rather than deaths;
what accounting horizon to close the books at, since effects on future population size compound without limit, together with whatever justifies that horizon rather than a longer or shorter one;
whether the typical life of the animals concerned is one worth living or not, and what I do when the evidence does not settle this;
the deaths caused by clearance itself and the fate of animals displaced into territories already occupied by others;
which animals are in the account at all, given that my team counts vertebrates and decapods but not the invertebrates that often make up most of the animal biomass on a site.

Then run it on three applications: draining thirty hectares of wet grassland supporting a large breeding population of common toads and field voles for distribution sheds; clearing ten hectares of mature scrub for a solar farm, where a small, stable population of a long-lived territorial bird is at carrying capacity and clearance will kill some adults; and a proposal to restore sixty hectares of improved pasture to wet woodland, which everyone in my team expects to approve. The procedure should tell me where it cannot produce a signed number at all, and be honest about which steps are judgement dressed up as method. Please write in British English.
