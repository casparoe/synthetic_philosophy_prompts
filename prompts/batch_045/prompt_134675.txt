For the past two years I have served as the lay member of my hospital's research ethics committee, and I have just been asked to sign off on the outcome instrument for a trial of deep-brain stimulation in treatment-resistant depression. The instrument is a hedonic scale: it asks people how much pleasure they take in a list of named activities, and the trial's success will be judged by movement on the total score. I do not want to approve a measure whose implicit theory of pleasure I have never examined, so before I read the competing accounts of what pleasures have in common, I want to know what I should be demanding of them.

The question has also stopped being academic for me. Two years ago my brother, who has Parkinson's disease, was taken off a dopamine agonist after he developed compulsive gambling. He says the gambling was not much fun: he did not enjoy it, he was puzzled by his own behaviour while he was doing it, and he has not missed it since. Whatever pleasure is, it is plainly not the same thing as whatever put him in front of the machine, and a scale that asks him how much he enjoys things would have recorded nothing unusual while his life was coming apart. I want a specification in which that dissociation is visible rather than definitional noise.

So please write me the specification. Fix the purpose as mine: an account is adequate here if and only if it is good enough to underwrite a self-report instrument used to compare hedonic states across people, across diagnostic groups, and across time. I accept that adequacy for metaphysics might be a different list, and I would like to be told where my purpose distorts the requirements.

The cases the list has to handle. Use my labels, and add your own where you think I have missed a kind of case (mark those as added).

A. The proof and the bath: the pleasure of finally solving a hard problem, and the pleasure of warm water on cold skin.
B. Relief: the scratched itch, the first cold water after a long thirst, the stopping of a pain.
C. Unlovely pleasures: taking pleasure in another's embarrassment or misfortune, and the enjoyment of a frightening film or a drop on a roller coaster.
D. My brother's gambling: vigorous, sustained pursuit with flat, puzzled enjoyment.
E. Stipulated case: a patient who, while a piece of music plays, manifestly takes pleasure in it — leaning in, attending, the usual facial and autonomic signs — but who a minute later has no memory of the episode and never seeks the music out again.
F. Stipulated case: a patient who says of an afternoon with his grandchildren that he knows it was a good afternoon and would call it pleasant — he is not depressed and does not retract it later — but who reports that nothing felt like anything; the enjoyment has become a judgement rather than an experience.
G. Stipulated case: a pleasure the subject does not want, is ashamed of, and would pay to be rid of, which nonetheless continues while it lasts.

The requirements I want sorted. Some of the following are things people demand of a theory of pleasure; my suspicion is that several are not desiderata at all but the prejudices of one camp, or artefacts of my own purpose. Say which are which, and why: that it must identify a feature shared by all and only pleasures; that it must not identify pleasure with wanting, reinforcement, or reward learning; that it must not identify it with the judgement that things are going well; that it must explain why pleasures seem to be pleasures in something, directed at an object, when so many of the cases above have no common kind of object; that it must allow for mixed and admixed episodes rather than a single signed valence; that it must be able to fail, i.e. to state what would show that pleasures have nothing interesting in common and what would then follow for hedonism and for my instrument; that it must yield, or explain the impossibility of yielding, a single currency in which different pleasures can be compared, summed, and traded off over time; that it must be reportable by the person having it; that it must respect first-person authority about one's own pleasure; that it must cohere with the reward-sciences literature on the dissociation between liking and wanting; that it must explain why pleasure, and not something else, is what hedonists build a theory of well-being out of; that it must be measurable on a short scale.

Two of the candidate accounts I want scored I have made up myself, so that the list is tested against something you cannot have memorised. The Tuning-Fork View: a pleasure is the felt fit between what one is currently doing or undergoing and the capacities and demands it answers to — the fit is skill-to-task in the proof case and body-to-environment in the bath case, and my brother's gambling is said to involve the fit being signalled without being felt. The Flag View: a pleasure is not a quality of experience at all but a phenomenal marker, indefinite in character, whose function is to single out the current activity as one to keep doing — a pointer rather than a feeling, so that the heterogeneity of pleasures is the heterogeneity of the things pointed at, and the unity is in the role. Score these alongside the felt-quality family (pleasure as a distinctive qualitative character of experience) and the attitudinal family (pleasure as a propositional attitude such as enjoyment, taken toward an object).

Please present the requirements as a table with exactly these columns:
- id: short label
- requirement: one sentence, in the form An adequate account must be able to X
- why: the case label from my list, or your added case, plus the consideration that makes it a requirement
- status: non-negotiable / non-negotiable only given my purpose / camp preference, naming the camp / not a requirement at all
- excludes: what kind of view the requirement rules out, described generically
- tension: ids of requirements this one conflicts with, and whether you take the conflict to be genuine or merely apparent
- falsifier: a case or finding that would show the requirement itself was mis-specified rather than the theory wanting

Keep the weight on the table; the scoring should be brief. After the table, give me a single JSON object with these fields:
- purpose: one sentence restating what adequacy means for me
- most_excluding_requirement: {id, explanation} for the requirement that does the most work in ruling views out
- unsatisfiable_subsets: array of {requirement_ids, explanation, which_to_drop, what_dropping_it_costs}, empty except where you really think the subset cannot be jointly met
- demoted: array of {requirement_as_usually_stated, why_it_is_a_prejudice_or_a_purpose_artefact}
- case_corrections: array of {case_label, correction} for any of my cases you think is doing no work or that I have misdescribed
- scorecard: object with keys felt_quality_family, attitudinal_family, tuning_fork_view, flag_view, each mapping to {passes, fails, weakest_point, one_line_verdict}, where passes and fails are arrays of requirement ids
- questions_for_the_trialists: array of strings, the things my committee should ask the trial team given what the list turned up
- residual_judgment_calls: array of strings, the places where the specification runs out and someone has to decide on non-theoretic grounds, with who should decide
