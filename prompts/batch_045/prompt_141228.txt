I hold a fairly standard form of semantic externalism for natural-kind and artefact terms: the content of a speaker's term is not fixed by that speaker's intrinsic physical and functional states alone, but depends partly on which things in their environment they have actually been in causal contact with and on how their linguistic community deploys the term — so that a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me on Twin Earth means something different, not merely believes something false, when they use their word for the clear drinkable liquid. I also hold the two usual companion claims: that this is compatible with authoritative self-knowledge, and that it is compatible with content doing real explanatory work in psychology.

I want a map of the view's exposure, not a list of objections I can dismiss from inside it. Specifically:

1. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and for each say whether an externalist would themselves concede it as counting against the view or would treat it as smuggling in an internalist presupposition.
2. Build the strongest case you can against me rather than the most familiar one. The variant that worries me most is this: a speaker raised on Earth, secretly transferred to Twin Earth for twenty years of ordinary adult linguistic life, then returned to Earth with the transfer erased from memory and their current dispositions to apply the term indistinguishable from a lifelong Earthling's — and who also explicitly takes themselves to mean by it just what they meant as a child. What does my view say their current term refers to, and about whether they are now systematically in error? If the view has to appeal to something like a dominant causal source or a weighted mixture to answer, is that principle earned or ad hoc?
3. Say whether the view risks anything at all. Externalists can redescribe the relevant environment at whatever grain is convenient, lean on deference to unseen experts, and declare semantic indecision in mixed or switching cases. Has that repertoire insulated the thesis so that no possible case could come out against it? If so, is that a genuine defect for a thesis of this kind, or is insulation appropriate here?
4. Where is the real exposure: in the Twin Earth argument itself, in the extension from linguistic meaning to thought content, in the compatibility with self-knowledge, or in the compatibility with psychological explanation? Pick the weakest joint rather than surveying all four.

What I would personally find most persuasive is a defeater that shows externalism cannot state a determinate verdict about some case it ought to be able to handle, or that collapses into a rival view it was introduced to defeat. Reasserting internalist intuitions about what is in the head would move me least.
