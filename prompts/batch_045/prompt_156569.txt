I've been reading about theories of truth on the side for a while — I write technical documentation for a living, so this is night-time stuff — mostly the people who call themselves deflationists or minimalists, and I keep running into the same move without having a word for it. Its not quite the thing I first assumed it was, and I want to know whether it has a name and, more than that, what exactly it is.

Here's the shape of it. An author spends a chapter arguing that "true" doesn't pick out any real feature of sentences or beliefs at all — that it's a device, that saying "it's true that the train leaves at six" is just a way of saying the train leaves at six, useful mainly when you want to nod at a claim you can't conveniently repeat ("whatever the inspector said about the wiring, it was true"). I can follow that. Then a couple of chapters later the same author needs truth to do work. Two cases I actually marked in the margins:

1. Explaining why our best theories let us build bridges that stay up and get probes to comets: because the theories are true, and plans formed on the basis of true descriptions of the world tend to succeed. When I try to cash out what "true" is contributing in that sentence, the only thing I can put in its place is "the world is the way the theory says it is" — which looks a lot like the correspondence story the earlier chapter said we didn't need.

2. Explaining why you should want true beliefs rather than merely comfortable ones: true beliefs are the ones that will still steer you right in situations you haven't been in before. Same thing — the explanation seems to need truth to be a relation to how things really are, and I can't see how a word that contributes nothing beyond restating the claim is supposed to do that.

So: a notion gets declared weightless, and then the exact weight it was said not to have is loaded back onto it when there's explaining to do. And it isn't a slip — the theory needs it; take the borrowed weight away and the explanation falls over.

What I want is not a label, or not just a label. I want to know: does this have an established name, and if the nearest names only cover one of my two cases, tell me that (I've seen discussions of why truth is valuable or why it's the aim of belief, which feel close to case 2 but not to case 1). Is it one phenomenon or two? What are the nearest established concepts and how does what I've described differ from each? And then the part I actually care about: is the borrowing illegitimate, or can a thin notion legitimately carry explanatory weight — what would a deflationist have to produce in order to be entitled to that sentence, i.e. what would paying the debt look like rather than just asserting it's paid? Where are the edges: does it still count when the debt gets passed down a chain (truth leans on correspondence, correspondence leans on reference, reference leans on something else, and nothing at the bottom is ever cashed out)? Does it count when the borrowed sentence is doing rhetorical or motivational work rather than load-bearing work?

I've bumped into the word "quasi-realism" somewhere in this neighborhood but it seemed to be about moral language and I can't tell whether that's the same shape or a different one. Relatedly: I think I see the same shape outside truth entirely — someone says "cause" is only a way of talking about patterns of events, then explains why you should intervene on this variable rather than that one by saying this is the real cause. Same pattern, or am I lumping two things together?
