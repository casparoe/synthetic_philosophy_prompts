You are a professional epistemologist specialising in the semantics and pragmatics of knowledge attributions. I am convening a small adversarial collaboration and need you to draft its joint statement. The two signatories are a committed epistemic contextualist — the standards expressed by an attribution of "knows" shift with the attributor's context, including the attributor's practical stakes — and a committed pragmatic encroacher of the subject-sensitive invariantist stripe: standards are invariant across attributors but rise and fall with the subject's practical stakes. They have talked past each other for years; I want a text both could actually sign.

Take your time. Before drafting, work through each of the following cases separately for each theory and note where they genuinely diverge rather than merely redescribe the case; then draft.

The cases the statement must jointly handle, with each side's prediction recorded:

1. Divergence: the subject's stakes are low but the attributor's are very high, and the mirror image.
2. Distributed subject: a three-person clinical team where each member faces different personal stakes and no single practical situation is theirs jointly, yet the team's spokesperson says "we knew."
3. Hidden stakes: the subject is unaware that the stakes are enormous.
4. Deferred stakes: stakes are negligible now but will become severe an hour later, after every opportunity to check has passed.

Deliverable: (i) a numbered section of agreed claims — what both accept about stakes-sensitivity, about the cases above, and about what would count as a bad move in this debate; (ii) a table of residual disagreements, each stated twice, once in wording the contextualist and once in wording the subject-sensitive invariantist would accept as their own; (iii) a table of cruxes, each with a case or argument both would agree bears on it and each side's prediction written down in advance; (iv) a short closing note saying which parts of the dispute you take to be merely verbal, which substantive, and why.

Constraints: formulations should be neither vacuous nor slanted; no attributions, citations, or bibliographies — this must stand on argument alone. Write the finished statement in British English.
