I'm grading for the third-year seminar on pain and consciousness that I took last year, and I've got a stack of short answers from last weeks problem set to get through. The question was:

Patients with pain asymbolia report the location, quality and intensity of a painful stimulus much as the rest of us do, but say it does not bother them and make no attempt to escape it. Explain what this case is supposed to show about the badness of pain, and say how much it shows.

Here is one answer:

Pain asymbolia is a dissociation case: the patient can describe the pinprick as sharp and burning and can say where it is, but they are not distressed by it and do not try to get away from it. Pain is normally thought to have two components, a sensory-discriminative one (its location, intensity and quality, how sharp or burning it feels) and an affective-motivational one (how unpleasant it is and how much you want it to stop). In asymbolia the second component drops out while the first stays intact, so the case shows that what is bad about pain is not the bare sensation but the unpleasantness, and that the two can come apart.

That is bad news for the view that the badness of pain is intrinsic to the sensation itself, and good news for the evaluative view, on which the badness just is the unpleasantness. It also refutes the motivational theory. The motivational theory says pain is bad because of the desire it produces to be rid of it; the asymbolic patient has no such desire, so the theory has to say that their pain is not bad at all, which is absurd, since of course a sensation like that is bad for you even if you do not mind it.

So the case shows that badness is one separable feature of pain rather than the whole thing. In ordinary subjects the two components normally vary together, so in practice we can still read off how bad someone's pain is from how intense they say it is. The main worry is that the evidence is thin: it comes from a small number of clinical case reports, patients' descriptions vary, and we cannot tell from the outside whether they really lack the unpleasantness or merely fail to react to it.
