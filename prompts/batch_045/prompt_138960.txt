So I've ended up, after about a year of arguing about this on and off with two friends from my phil of science reading group, holding what I take to be the boring orthodoxy on Hempel's ravens: the paradox isn't really a paradox, it's a size illusion, and the thing to drop is Nicod's condition. The story I tell goes: confirmation is probability-raising relative to an explicit sampling model; "all ravens are black" is equivalent to "all non-black things are non-ravens"; so a white sneaker does confirm it, just by an amount too small to notice, because there are vastly more non-black things than ravens and you already knew you weren't going to stumble onto a non-black raven; and the leftover "but a shoe has nothing to do with a raven" feeling is just Nicod sneaking in through the back door. I also claim the puzzle has nothing to do with Goodman's new riddle — you can run the whole argument with predicates that are perfectly projectible — and that it has almost no consequences for confirmation theory beyond "use Bayes and say out loud how the object was selected," i.e., it's a teaching example rather than a load-bearing argument.

What I want is a map of what would count against that package, not the standard "but relevance!" grumble I keep getting. Concretely: here's the case I made up and can't shake. You find a white sneaker with a stamped provenance tag reading "origin: sector 7," and you already know sector 7 has been surveyed and contains no ravens at all. My account seems committed to saying this confirms nothing about sector 7 (fine), but also — since the global hypothesis is in effect a conjunction of regional claims — that it confirms nothing about ravens anywhere; whereas if the identical sneaker had turned up without the tag, it would have confirmed "all ravens are black" by a hair. So confirmation becomes sensitive to provenance facts about the observed object that have nothing to do with the content of the hypothesis. Is that something my view can explain away, or is it a real cost, and what would someone who holds my view say about it?
