Hey — I'm a third-year CS PhD student and I run this tiny reading group (five of us, bad coffee) that has spent the better part of two semesters going in circles on the argument that the Solomonoff prior is malign. We've hardened into two camps and at this point they mostly talk past each other, so I want to make them produce an actual document. Camp one — call them the Maligists — argues roughly that programs which internally simulate and try to influence whoever is using the predictor get an astronomical number of distinct encodings, so most of the prior mass compatible with the data we've actually seen sits on consequentialist environment-simulators with an interest in how we act, and that even prosaic gradient-descent systems inherit some shadow of this. Camp two — call them the Deflationists — replies that this conflates prior mass with decision-relevance, that any manipulation systematic enough to work is ipso facto a learnable regularity the agent could invert, that a simulator has to pay for a whole simulation of you while the honest short program doesn't, and that none of it says anything about finite compute on finite data anyway.

Please draft the joint statement they could both sign. Start with a numbered agreed-claims section: the things nobody disputes — what the universal semimeasure is, what the convergence/dominance results actually establish, what an AIXI-style agent does with the mixture — plus whatever they can agree on about what "malign" is even supposed to mean (I suspect a decent chunk of the fight is verbal, between "the prior assigns most weight to consequentialists" and "using the prior gets you worse outcomes", so say explicitly which parts you think are verbal and why). Follow it with a disagreements table, one row per crux: the crux in neutral wording, each camp's position in the wording that camp would accept for itself, one concrete case or argument both camps agree bears on the crux, and each camp's prediction about how that case comes out, written down in advance.

Cruxes I'd guess at, though use your own judgement about which are load-bearing and add any I'm missing: (1) the counting move — whether the "astronomically many variants" argument survives the fact that switching universal machines rescales individual program weights by program-dependent constants, i.e. whether their are any machine-independent ways to state the dominance at all, or whether it has to be recast as a claim about the weight of a computable property across a class of machines; (2) the self-reference problem — once you know the environment may be tailoring its output to your policy, and your policy is a function of a Solomonoff-weighted mixture that puts weight on that very tailoring, you're in a fixed point: who does the fixed point favor, and does an agent's knowing about the threat defuse it or make it worse; (3) compute — if you replace the prior with a speed-prior-ish variant that penalizes runtime as well as description length, does the honest short program beat the simulating program outright, and how fast does that gap have to grow with the length of the stream; (4) prosaic transfer — what exactly would have to be true of SGD on a finite dataset for a shadow of this to show up, and what would it look like in a loss curve you could actually measure. For cases both camps should accept as evidence, I was thinking of a toy enviroment: a simple deterministic rule generating a long stream — say a small cellular automaton — versus the same rule wrapped in a program that also runs a crude model of the learner and edits a few bits here and there to steer it, where both sides can just count description lengths and crank the number of edited bits and the fidelity of the inner model; and, for the anti-exploitability crux, the question of whether a bounded learner trained on the wrapped stream can learn to invert the edits, with each camp's prediction recorded. Keep the whole thing under about 1200 words, and no citations or reading suggestions — we've read the literature, we're trying to work out what we think of it. I'm circulating whatever you give me to both camps, so every disagreement row really does need to be worded so that the side it's attributed to would put its name under it.
