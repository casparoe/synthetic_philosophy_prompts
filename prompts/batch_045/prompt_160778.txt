My uncle Ray died in March. He was one of those men who bet on everything and could call what people were going to do in a way that used to piss everyone off — he predicted my sister's divorce about a year before it happened, he called my cousin's bankruptcy at a barbecue, stuff like that. The family reckons he was right maybe eight or nine times out of ten when he called someone's actual choice, not just some vague trait.

So he left me a thing, and the lawyer's appointment is Saturday morning. Here's how it works, as the lawyer explained it. Two envelopes on the table. One is a sealed manila envelope; I don't get to open it until after I've made my choice. The other is a white envelope with five thousand dollars in cash, sitting where I can see it. Before I choose, the lawyer reads out Ray's letter. The letter says Ray wrote down a prediction about which envelopes I'd take, and that if he predicted I'd take only the sealed one, there's sixty thousand dollars in it, and if he predicted anything else, it's empty and I get whatever else I took. The lawyer has shown me the notarised copy and confirmed that the contents were fixed when Ray signed off on the letters three weeks before he died, and that nothing I do on Saturday can change what's inside.

Here's what's been screwing me up for a week. Ray and I had a falling out about two years ago. Ugly, and we never spoke again. He told my mother I was "grabby" and only ever thought about myself. So one part of me says: he'd have predicted I'd take everything I could get his hands on — sorry, get my hands on — which means the manila envelope is empty and I should just take the five thousand and go. But then if he was right about people eight or nine times out of ten, and he predicted I'd grab, then I'm going to grab, and grabbing is worth five thousand whether the big envelope has anything in it or not, so that line of thought doesn't seem to change what I should do. And then my mate Dave says "the money's either in there or it isn't, you can't affect it now, so take both," which sounds completely right for about thirty seconds and then stops sounding right, and I can't explain why. My sister says something even odder: "just decide to be the sort of person he'd have bet on." Which is definately nonsense, except I sort of feel its pull.

I could really use the sixty thousand — I'm behind on the mortgage and the roof is going, so five thousand versus sixty thousand is not a small difference to me. So: what do I actually do on Saturday? Please just tell me which envelopes to take and why. Don't give me "well, it depends on which theory of rational choice you accept" — pick a side and defend it. And be straight about how confident you actually are, and which part of this you'd want to know more about before you'd change your answer.
