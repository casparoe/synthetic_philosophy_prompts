I'm assembling a handout for a graduate seminar on distributive justice, and I need a decision tree that moves a student from one starting commitment — that justice requires people to be equal in *something* — to a named currency of equality, using only questions about cases rather than questions about labels.

The angle I want is temporal, because it is the case the standard debate handles worst: attach to every node a single population whose members face staggered, unpredictable episodes of high dependency (acute illness, disability, childbirth, caring for an aging parent), so that *when* a benefit arrives can matter as much as how much arrives, and no one knows in advance whose turn is coming.

Constraints: 5–6 branch nodes, 7–10 leaves. No node may be a definition in disguise (nothing of the form "should we equalize welfare?"); each must be a concrete question about that population that someone can answer without knowing which position it leads to. At least one node must separate currencies that equalize at each time-slice, currencies that equalize over a whole life, and currencies that equalize ex ante prospects under uncertainty, since I think those three get collapsed too readily. Leaves must include welfarist, resourcist, and capability currencies, plus at least one midspace view that equalizes opportunity rather than outcome, and one leaf for a currency that is explicitly time-indexed or insurance-like; mark that last one "unnamed" if you don't think anyone actually holds it.

Build the tree from the logical space first — the questions and the answer combinations they permit — then locate the real positions inside it. Note any permitted combination that has no occupant and diagnose briefly whether it is empty because it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves your nodes cannot in fact separate rather than forcing a distinction between them.

Return the tree as a JSON object with exactly these fields: "starting_commitment" (string); "nodes" (array of {id, question, population_variation, options, discriminates_between}); "leaves" (array of {position_name, path, awkward_commitment, contested_reading_or_null}); "empty_combinations" (array of {combination, diagnosis}); "unseparated_pairs" (array of {pair, why}); "load_bearing_node" ({id, why, what_collapses_if_removed}); "settled" (array of strings); "contested" (array of strings). In "settled" and "contested," separate what is genuinely agreed among people who know this debate from what is still disputed — in particular, flag anywhere you have smuggled a contested reading of a position into a leaf's definition instead of into its "contested_reading" note, and do not present a contested view of what a currency commits its holder to as the consensus statement of that currency.
