I'm building the epistemic machinery for a simulation of a small team of software auditors who have to decide which of a very large set of claims about a codebase to check before signing off, and I keep hitting the same wall. The standard possible-worlds treatment of belief — the one on which an agent's beliefs are the propositions holding throughout every world compatible with what the agent takes to be the case — makes each auditor believe every logical consequence of what they believe, including consequences that would take longer than the age of the universe to derive, and makes them indifferent between formulations that differ only in how hard the equivalence is to see. I need belief to do two things: predict which claim an auditor will check, act on, or ignore next, and give me a standard for saying that an auditor's reasoning has gone wrong rather than merely stopped short. So before I go shopping for a formal framework, I want the specification one would have to meet.

Please write the desiderata for a theory of a bounded agent's beliefs: the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must respect, the results from logic and computational complexity it cannot contradict, and the constraint behind each. I have a partial list, which you should correct, reorder, and extend rather than just execute. It includes: an auditor who believes a conditional and its antecedent but has not drawn the conclusion, and must not thereby be described as believing the conclusion; an auditor whose beliefs are jointly inconsistent, who must not thereby believe everything; the requirement that inference sometimes does extend belief, so that noticing a consequence is a genuine way to acquire one (a theory that makes the agent a logical idiot is no better than one that makes it a god); the requirement that some substitutions of equivalents go through while the hard ones do not, and some principled account of where that line falls; the problem I think of as the prefix problem, that closure under each small step, iterated, seems to rebuild full omniscience one cheap step at a time, so the break in the chain has to be located rather than stipulated; and two things I have made up and am suspicious of myself — an attributable-gap condition, on which every permitted failure of closure must be traceable to a specifiable and independently checkable limitation of the agent (a resource bound, a blind spot for a class of equivalences, an unrecognized instance of a schema) rather than posited case by case, and the leash, on which a failure of closure cannot be permanent: if both premises of a one-step inference are occurrent and the agent has the capacity to perform it, the model must be committed to the agent either acquiring the conclusion or giving up a premise, never to a state suspended indefinitely. Also address, with cases: whether belief attaches to propositions or to representations, given that derivational difficulty shifts with how a proposition is written down; what stops a fine-grained notion of belief from degenerating into the modeler attributing any pattern of belief and calling it boundedness; what the theory implies about beliefs about other agents' beliefs, given that the usual results about common belief and agreement presuppose the idealization I am trying to remove; what the graded-belief analogue looks like, since a probability function is already fully coherent over every logical relation; and how the theory represents an agent whose inferential powers improve, and how it marks the difference between an auditor who has not yet noticed a consequence and one who would reject it.

Mark which requirements are non-negotiable for my two purposes and which are really preferences of one research program; say whether prediction and normative evaluation pull the specification in different directions; flag any pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and tell me which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidates. Then, briefly and only for the approaches you can characterize without guessing, how the main families of response to the problem of logical omniscience do against the list; skip any you would have to reconstruct from half-remembered details, since the criteria are the deliverable and the scorecard is a footnote. Please write in American English.
