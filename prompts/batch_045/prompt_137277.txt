I've basically talked myself into the "qualia are a defective posit" line, and I want someone to try to talk me out of it. My version of it isn't "there is nothing it is like to be in pain, shut up" — it's the package deal argument: take the bundle of properties the term is supposed to carry in these debates (intrinsic rather than relational; non-representational; ineffable and unanalyzable in structural terms; private in the sense that no third-person description could in principle capture it; known directly and indubitably by the subject; and the kind of thing that could be inverted or absent while every functional and discriminative fact stays fixed), and notice that they pull against each other. Effableness and direct knowledge sit badly with intrinsicality-plus-privacy; the inversion and absence possibilities sit badly with the idea that these properties are what grounds your knowledge of them; and once you drop any one item to relieve the tension, the famous arguments — the knowledge argument, the conceivability argument, the explanatory gap — lose whatever bite they had, because they were all silently relying on the dropped item. So I've stopped saying "qualia don't exist" (too easy to answer with "then what am I attending to right now") and started saying "the thing being posited can't be the thing doing the work."

Tell me what would count against that. Not the greatest hits list — I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want you to distinguish the ones my side would itself concede from the ones we'd shrug off. Some specific things I keep going back and forth on:

The recalcitrant report. Suppose we had real introspective training — not a weekend of mindfulness, but something that genuinely removed the known ways introspection misleads, so that subjects reliably distinguish "I made a fine discrimination" from "there was a qualitative character present," have internalized the tricks (attention changes the state you're attending to, we confabulate similarity structure, verbal report forces a discretization that wasn't there), and still insist, under careful non-leading prompting, that something is left over once all the functional facts are in. Is that evidence, or is it just the datum to be explained? I can't tell whether "of course they'd say that, it's the thing we owe an explanation of" is a satisfying answer or a dodge, and if it's a dodge I want to know what the non-dodging version would look like.

The self-model case. Here's the sort of thing I think would actually move me. Imagine a system whose first-order perceptual machinery demonstrably lacks the resources to make some discrimination — no detectors, no downstream states that track it — but whose self-model, the thing that generates its "how things seem to me" reports, gets corrupted or noisily trained such that it reports a rich qualitative character for exactly that dimension, and goes on to make similarity judgments, memories, and choices that are coherent with the reported character and incoherent with what its perceptual system is actually doing. A phantom quality produced by a broken self-model. Does that show that quality-talk and discrimination are separable in a way that helps the realist? Or does it show the opposite — that the "what it's like" story is a story the system tells, which is more or less my view wearing a lab coat? I honestly don't know which way this cuts and I'd like you to argue both sides and then pick one.

The generalization worry. If the deflation move is good, it looks good for everything, not just for sensory quality: the felt sense of understanding, the seeming of a thought's meaning, the "and then I decided" of agency. Is that a reductio of my position, or just a consequence I should definately accept? Relatedly: does anything I care about — the badness of pain, say — hang on the posit, or does everything morally relevant survive its removal? If something does hang on it, is that a reason to be suspicious of my position or a reason to be suspicious of the thing hanging there?

And the insulation question, which I think is the real one. My worry is that this view has been qualified into a place where no possible first-person datum could count against it, since any such datum is either a report (explained away as a judgment the system makes) or a functional fact (already conceded). If that's right: is it fatal, or is it the normal hard-core-of-a-research-programme kind of insulation that any big metaphysical view has, and which I'm only noticing because the word "illusion" makes it sound like an empirical claim that should be testable? Also, separately from falsifiability: is there a version of this where I turn out to have collapsed into some boring representationalism and the whole dispute was verbal, and would that be worse for me than being straightforwardly wrong?

I'd like a verdict at the end: how exposed is this position, really, and which one or two items on your list should I actually lose sleep over.
