I've been slowly writing up a post for our local rationalist meetup's blog (readership: six people, my mom, and one guy who only shows up to argue about protein folding) on the careenium thing, and I want comments on the draft before I inflict it on anyone. Short version of how I got here: I've been re-reading Hofstadter's old dialogue about the careenium — the billiard-table-ish box full of little magnetic balls (symms) that clump into higher-level patterns (symmballs), with the question being who shoves whom around in there — and it's been rattling around my head since I first went through the sequences back whenever. Every time I bring it up with friends, half of them say obviously the symms, the symmballs are just a way of talking, and the other half say obviously the symmballs, otherwise there's no point to the higher level, and neither side ever says anything I can actually test. So I tried to build a test. Notes below — they're notes, not prose, sorry if it reads like bullet points.

**1. Three senses of "push," because I think people talk past each other here.**
- P1 (counterfactual dependence): change the symmball-level state and the symms end up somewhere different. This is trivially true in any careenium, because any difference at the macro level *is* a difference at the micro level. So P1 can't be what's at stake; it's the sense people use when they say "of course the symmballs push."
- P2 (closure): the symmball state at t fixes the symmball state at t+1 without help from the micro details — the zoomed-out dynamics is self-contained. This is the only version with teeth, and it's an empirical claim about how the careenium is built, not a metaphysical claim about levels.
- P3 (explanatory priority): the symm-level story doesn't make the symmball-level story redundant. I want P2 to hand me P3 and I'm not sure it does.

**2. Two tests for P2.** My careenium is Hofstadter's plus a learning rule (collisions nudge magnet strengths) plus the walls' bounciness being set by the outside world.
- *Test 1, the identity scramble:* a demon swaps any two symms that play the same role in different symmballs. Symmball description unchanged. If the later symmball evolution is unchanged, symm identity is causally inert. But I've started to think this test is cheap: in the naive careenium the symms are interchangeable by construction, so the test mostly re-describes the setup rather than probing it.
- *Test 2, the hidden-correlation probe:* now the demon gets the run of the place — any micro state compatible with the same symmball description, including stuff invisible at the zoomed-out level, like uneven magnet wear from past collisions. Because my careenium learns, wear is real and accumulates, and I claim the demon can basically always find two micro states with the same symmballs now and different symmballs later. If so, the abstraction leaks and the honest answer for that careenium is boring: the symms push, the symmballs are a leaky abstraction.
- *What would make the answer flip:* error correction. The clump has to be an attractor that re-imposes itself on whatever the micro is doing, so micro deviations get damped instead of accumulated. Call the strength of that damping the self-repair coefficient. On this reading, "symbols push the substrate around" isn't a thesis about the metaphysics of levels at all, it's a design claim about how much slack a system has to re-assert its macro state: full self-repair means the symmballs are fully in charge, zero means the symms are, and real systems sit somewhere in between.

**3. The borrowed-authority objection, and my reply.** Objection: even in the fully self-repairing careenium, every instance of a symmball pushing symms around can be re-told at the symm level — the error correction is itself just symms bumping. So the macro story does no causal work of its own, and my self-repair coefficient just measures how *nice* a description is. My reply: ask what each description buys you. The symm-level account is longer, and more importantly it doesn't generalize — it tells you what happens for *this* arrangement of symms and has to be redone after every scramble, while the symmball account survives all of them. Causal credit should track invariance under interventions: whichever description stays true across the bigger class of interventions is the one tracking what's actually doing the pushing. So the symmballs push, in the only sense ever in dispute, because they're invariant under more interventions than the symms are. And notice what isn't doing any work: aboutness. The symmballs mirror the outside world only because the walls' bounciness is set by that world, so the semantics is inherited from the causal chain leading into the careenium, not contributed by the macro level. Which means that if you were hoping this buys you a load-bearing "I," what you actually get is an invariant structure with borrowed semantics. I genuinely don't know if that's a real "I" or a fake one, and I'd rather say so than fake it.
