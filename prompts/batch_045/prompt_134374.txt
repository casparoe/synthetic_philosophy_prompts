I got into counterfactual history during a bout of insomnia and now I keep noticing something I can't name. My book club did a what-if night. Someone said that even if Napoleon had won at Waterloo, France would still have lost within a year, because the other powers simply had more men. Someone else said that was cheating: you're picturing him winning by a fluke, a stray canonball or something, and then keeping every other fact about the campaign exactly as it actually was, including the fact that he was loosing up to that point. If he'd really won, they said, the reasons he was in a bad spot would probably have been different too, and then maybe he'd have kept winning. Both sides felt the other was breaking a rule and neither could say what the rule was.

Same night, smaller case: if I'd taken the other job offer in 2019, I'd be miserable now. But if I'd taken it, I'd presumably have shown up as a slightly different person, and the job would then have gone differently in ways I can't fill in — so I don't see what gives me the right to hold everything else fixed.

And here's the bit that puzzles me. None of this comes up with ordinary future ifs. "If it rains on Saturday the match is off" seems perfectly clear, and nobody asks how the rain got there or what else would have had to change. Make it past tense — "if it had rained on Saturday the match would have been off" — and the same can of worms opens up. So it can't just be about being uncertain.

I've started calling them package-deal ifs, because you have to decide how much of the surrounding package of facts travels along with the one thing you changed, and it feels like there are two incompatible conventions that people switch between without noticing. Is there an established name for this, or for the choice you're forced to make? I genuinely don't want just a label — I want to know what exactly the thing is, whether my future-if example is really clean or whether the same mess is hiding in it, and if there is an established version, whether my "package deal" thing is the same one or a different one. And if I'm muddling two seperate problems together, or my three examples aren't really cases of one thing at all, please say so plainly instead of politely going along with it.
