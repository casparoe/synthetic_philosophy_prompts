i work on RL for long-horizon control stuff — scheduling and maintenance for infrastructure with 20-30 year horizons, not games. I got dragged into an argument online about whether its rational to discount the future and now I think me and the philosophy people are using the same word for like four different things, so I want to try a translation and see what survives.

My vocabulary, so you know what you can lean on: MDPs and POMDPs, discounted return and γ, Bellman operators, continuing vs episodic formulations, average-reward and Blackwell optimality, the reparameterization where γ<1 is the same thing as a 1−γ probability of the trajectory terminating at each step (a hazard rate), γ-annealing over training, and model-based planning with a learned model I dont trust past about 50 steps. Basically no philosophy background beyond the usual blog-level stuff.

Translate these into that vocabulary, one at a time, as faithfully as you can:

1. The view that discounting a good just because its further away in time is arbitrary or irrational — that the date of something isnt a reason for it to matter less — including the standard move where someone who discounts purely by nearness keeps reversing their own earlier plans.
2. The defences of positive discounting that claim its not really about time at all: (a) we might not be around, the project gets interrupted, the world might end; (b) future people will be richer, so the same welfare gain matters less at the margin; (c) resources invested now grow, so spending now has a real opportunity cost; (d) and the ones that genuinely are about nearness — that a persons own near future has some special claim on them, or that there is an agent-relative prerogative to favour the near.
3. The nasty one: choices that change which future people exist, where saying 'this policy made these people worse off' seems to stop making sense because there is nobody who is worse off.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about:

- Which of those map onto machinery I already have? My guesses: (a) is literally the hazard-rate reparameterization, (b) is a concave transform on the reward and has nothing to do with γ, (c) is an exchange rate between present and future resources. Correct me where Im wrong, and flag anywhere a translation only works if you quietly add something to my framework that isnt there.
- Does pure time preference as such have any expression in my formalism other than 'set γ<1'? Heres my worry: a colleague and I tried splitting γ into two knobs — a survival probability and a separate weight for how much later welfare counts — and they just multiply, so the math cannot tell them apart. Does that mean the philosophers distinction between extinction risk and pure time preference is fake, or just that my formalism is too coarse to hold it?
- The plan-reversal argument: with continuing exponential discounting my optimal policy is stationary and I dont see any self-defeating-plan problem at all. Is that objection only aimed at non-exponential, hyperbolic-ish discounting? If it only bites there, whats left of the case against a constant γ<1?
- What has no counterpart at all in RL vocabulary? My candidates: the interpersonal part (my formalism has one reward stream and no notion of whose welfare, so the whole aggregation question is invisible to me), and the fact that I partly discount as a hedge against my own model error, which feels like a legitimately different reason that isnt in their list — is it, or is it really (a) in disguise?

And the bit where this cashes out: I have to hand a single number to a planner. Does any of this tell me how to pick it, or does the translation just tell me my one knob was doing four jobs at once and I should split it — and if so, what does the split actually look like?
