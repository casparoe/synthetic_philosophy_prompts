Hey — I run a tiny reading group that spun out of the old rationalist blog scene and we have been going in circles on Fitch's paradox of knowability for about three weeks now. Two of us hold the positions below, neither is budging, and I think it is because we keep restating each other badly. Can you write the argument as an actual dialogue — the strongest version, something I could hand to both of them without either one feeling strawmanned?

The two sides:

- Kit: verificationist / anti-realist sympathiser. The knowability principle is right for the propositions it was ever about — truth is not recognition-transcendent — and Fitch only shows you have to state it with a restriction. The Moorean propositions (p and it is not known that p) are pathological, and they are the only ones the argument touches.
- Sam: realist. Fitch is a sound reductio, and the restrictions people reach for are either ad hoc or quietly concede the realist point anyway.

Concrete things I want them to fight over rather than gesture at:

1. Replace the hand-wavy could in principle be known with something computable. Take an idealized learner that works like a limiting-computable procedure — it can compute with a halting oracle, it outputs guesses, and for a lot of propositions it eventually stabilises on the right answer forever. But at no finite time can it tell whether it has stabilised or is just in a long lucky run, and there are plenty of truths (arithmetical truth in general) it never stabilises on at all. Now run Fitch inside that picture. Does the paradox get more vivid or does it evaporate? Notice that for such a learner a Moorean proposition is one that flips from true to false in the very process of being learned, so it is not the kind of thing convergence can ever lock onto. Is that the shape of the restriction Kit wants, or does it turn knowable into something too weak to do the anti-realist work?

2. Principle as a schema or rule versus a single universal claim. If you only ever assert knowability instance by instance from outside the language, or as a rule (whenever you have established p you may infer that p is knowable), you never get the instance for the composite Moorean proposition, because you can never establish that one. Does that block the derivation? And if it does, has Kit bought safety at the price of the whole thesis?

3. Someone in the group keeps insisting the last step of the derivation only gives you a double negation once you give up double-negation elimination, so the anti-realist ends up with something weaker than everything true is actually known. I want Sam to take that seriously and then say whether weaker is the same as safe, or whether it is a change of subject.

4. Whatever restriction Kit proposes, can it be stated without already knowing which propositions are well behaved? Sam's suspicion is that sorting the stable ones from the pathological ones is itself a recognition-transcendent fact, so the restriction smuggles in what verificationism denies. Kit gets to reply that realism has the mirror-image problem.

5. Sam should be honest about whether Fitch does any real work for the realist, or whether it is a cheap win: does it bite against someone who only ever claimed knowability for observation-level or empirical propositions? If not, what is the actual objection to verificationism?

No bibliographies, nobody winning by definition, and please no one saying as Fitch showed as if that settles it. End however it honestly ends, including with them realising part of the disagreement is verbal if that is where it goes.
