So here's where I'm at. I've been chewing over Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition, and I buy the setup but not the conclusion. The setup, as I understand it: if the proposition that Brutus stabbed Caesar is a complex object whose constituents are Brutus, the stabbing relation and Caesar, then those constituents don't just magically stick together. So you add a further item — a copula, a relation of instantiation, whatever — to bind them. But now you've got four things that themselves need binding, and off you go forever. Fine. What I don't get is why this is supposed to be *bad*. Loads of things are infinite. There are infinitely many natural numbers; there's a regress of justification for beliefs; there are infinitely many propositions, that p, that it's true that p, that it's true that it's true that p, and so on. Nobody panics about those. So what is the gut-level reason this particular regress is thought to be vicious rather than merely tall? Is the thought that unity is constitutive — that you don't have a proposition at all until it's already unified, so any story that only delivers unity at the end of an endless chain never delivers a proposition anywhere along it? Or is it that the explanation is circular in a damaging way, since each level helps itself to the very notion of combination it was meant to account for? I want the intuition here, not just the usual benign-versus-vicious classification.

The second thing is the bit I actually care about, and it's more specific. One standard move is to stop the regress by positing a primitive non-relational tie or nexus: the proposition just is Brutus, stabbing and Caesar bound by a tie, and the tie isn't a further constituent that needs its own tie. But here's what bugs me. The tie has to bind things *arranged in a particular way*. That Brutus stabbed Caesar and that Caesar stabbed Brutus have exactly the same constituents and, on this story, the same tie, and they had better be different propositions with different truth conditions. And that Brutus stabbed Brutus has fewer constituents and still needs its own proposition. So the tie seems to have to come with positional structure — agent slot, patient slot — or at least with an ordering. But positional structure is structure. If I say the constituents come as an ordered triple plus the tie, the ordered triple is itself a structured item that needs its own unity. If I say instead that the tie has argument places with contents, I seem to have smuggled the structure back in inside the tie and just hidden it. I've been calling this the level-climbing problem for want of a better label: every time you make unity primitive, the thing you made primitive turns out to have internal structure of just the sort that generated the problem, so the bump in the carpet reappears one level up. Is that a genuine further problem, or am I only re-describing the original regress in different words? And if it is genuine, does it tell specifically against tie and nexus views, or does it land on every view equally?
