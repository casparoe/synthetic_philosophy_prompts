I've been hanging around the rationalist blogs since the Overcoming Bias days, and I'm finally writing the post I've been chewing on: that Hofstadter-style superrationality is just FDT with the correlation pinned at exactly 1, and that it stops being usable the moment the correlation is merely high instead of exact. I need the case that shows it.

Build me this (I've been calling them half-twins): two people who publicly committed to the same decision procedure and know the other one did, but each one then executes it with an independent probability ε of fumbling it — ordinary human error, tired, misread the instructions, panicked. No uploads, no literally shared source code, no predictor sharper than a smart human. One paragraph, realistic setting, PD-ish payoffs, stakes a normal person actually faces.

Then pick real numbers — an ε and a payoff matrix — and give me the verdict for each of CDT, EDT, FDT and superrationalty at those numbers, with the arithmetic and the threshhold ε at which each view flips. What I'm hoping for is an ε where CDT defects, EDT and FDT both cooperate, and superrationality either defects or just has no determinate answer because its premise runs out. If that's not reachable, say so and tell me the smallest extra ingredient that makes it reachable. Then name the one feature doing the work, confirm that ε = 0 collapses it back into the ordinary twin PD where everyone cooperates, and give me the strongest reply a superrationalist has. Stipulating the verdicts is cheating, and so is making the superrationalist dumb — at ε = 0 they're supposed to cooperate fine.

American English please.
