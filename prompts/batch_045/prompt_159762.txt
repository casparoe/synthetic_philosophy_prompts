My mate did a philosophy module at uni and he keeps saying that "it's true that the train is late" doesn't say anything more than "the train is late" — the word "true" is just decoration or grammar. I more or less buy it for that example. But I keep running into cases where it definitly doesn't feel like decoration, and I have no word for what's going on in them.

Here's the sort of thing I mean. A relative hands me a letter from a doctor written in a language I can't read and says "whatever it says in there is true". Or: "everything my gran said about what happened to the family in 1947 was true" — she's been dead twenty years, almost none of it was ever written down, and even she couldn't have sat down and listed all the claims, because most of them were the kind of thing you never spell out. Or, weirder: "exactly two of the things in that sealed report are true" — nobody alive knows which two, and there's no list anybody could produce. And I notice I can pass these along: I tell you "if what the surveyor says is true we're fine", you tell someone else, and it travels through four people without anyone ever unpacking it into actual sentences.

Things I've tried and why they don't work for me. It's not just "I'm willing to go along with it", because I can't assert any of these claims — I don't know what they are. It's not quite "matches the facts" either, or at least it doesn't feel like I'm doing that; I'm doing something more like vouching for a source. My mate says these are all just shorthand for a giant list of sentences I could in principle write out, but in my examples I couldn't even in principle — and yet the sentence still seems to mean something perfectly definite, and to be the kind of thing that could turn out to be wrong. That's the bit that bugs me.

So: is there an established name for this use of "true", and for the problem it supposedly makes for the view that "true" adds nothing? Please don't just hand me a label — I want to know what the nearest concepts in the area are, how what I've described differs from each of them (including from plain shorthand-for-a-list), and which one actually fits best. If there's nothing established, say so and coin something, with a definition and a couple of boundary cases: e.g. what about a source who's a habitual liar who happens, unknown to anyone, to have told the truth this once; or a case where twenty years later everybody learns the contents and half of them are obviously false — was the original ascription wrong all along? And does any of this really hurt the deflationary-ish view my mate was pushing, or is there a tidy answer I'm missing?
