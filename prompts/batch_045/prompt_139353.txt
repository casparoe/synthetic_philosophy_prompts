I co-organize a longtermist reading group, and we want a one-page decision tree titled "Does the simulation argument dampen fanaticism about the far future?" — something a newcomer can walk through and land on a named position. Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of labels: five to seven branch questions, each phrased as a concrete claim the reader can accept or reject without knowing where it leads, and then locate positions at the leaves (named where a name exists, "unnamed" where not). The branch questions must include: whether the standard trilemma (near-term extinction before a posthuman stage, posthuman disinterest in running ancestor simulations, or our almost certainly being simulated) raises a non-negligible credence that we are in a simulation; whether the welfare of merely simulated beings counts fully, counts at a discount, or counts only through its effects on whatever is not simulated; whether being simulated makes our future more likely to be truncated by shutdown, resource limits, or the simulators' purposes, or instead vastly larger through nested simulations; and whether, conditional on being simulated, our evidence should be treated as a sample from all simulations, only from simulations containing observers like us, or preferentially from simulations that were run because they contain large futures. Leaves must include business-as-usual longtermism, shutdown-discounted longtermism, a cluelessness or anti-fanaticism leaf, an amplified-fanaticism leaf on which extra hypotheses raise the variance and make tiny probabilities matter more, and a base-reality-only leaf. For each unoccupied combination of answers, give one line on whether it is empty because incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply undefended, and flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate.
