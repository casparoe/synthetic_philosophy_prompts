The angle I keep coming back to is to make the question turn on a case I made up rather than the textbook ones, so there's nothing to recall. Call it the Helios lineage: a synthetic biology group assembles self-replicating protocells from scratch, and one of the membrane proteins, P, initially does nothing useful at all. At t1 the cells are moved into a chemostat containing a toxin, and P happens to pump it out, giving its bearers about a four percent fitness advantage. By t2, five hundred generations later, P is universal in the population and clearly retained because of that effect. At t3 the toxin is removed from the environment and P still gets expressed, but now all it does is buffer internal pH by a small amount, which makes no measurable difference to survival or reproduction. I also want a fourth branch of the case where the lab builds a second lineage whose version of P has the pH-buffering effect but is never under selection for it — it's just carried along. So the student has to say what the function of P is at each stage, what the two accounts each say, and — this is the part I actually want tested — whether there's a fact of the matter, or whether the right answer depends on what you wanted a function attribution for in the first place. If they can get to the point that the two accounts might be doing different explanatory jobs (one underwriting "why is it there," one underwriting mechanistic explanation of what the system can do now), and to the point that even once you grant the etiological story there's an indeterminacy about *which* historical effect is the function given that selection acts on whole organisms and the effect is only ever a matter of degree, they've got the bit I'm looking for.
