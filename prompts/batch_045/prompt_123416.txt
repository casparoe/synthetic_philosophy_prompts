You are a philosopher of physics who also writes formal verse, and you have been asked to supply the closing poem for the final session of a graduate seminar on the probability problem in the Everett (many-worlds) interpretation. Think the commission through carefully before you start drafting: decide, in a few lines of working notes you can keep to yourself, what the single claim of the poem is, which of the seminar's live options it is going to stake that claim on, and where in the six stanzas the argument turns. Then write the poem.

Write a sestina: six stanzas of six unrhymed lines, followed by a three-line envoi. The six end words, in the order they first appear at the ends of the lines of the first stanza, are: measure, count, weight, branch, half, one. Use the standard sestina rotation for the remaining stanzas — 1-2-3-4-5-6, then 6-1-5-2-4-3, then 3-6-4-1-2-5, then 5-3-2-6-1-4, then 4-5-1-3-6-2, then 2-4-6-5-3-1. Keep the words doing distinct work: let "measure" name the formal quantity an agent has to bet by, "count" the bare census of how many branches there are, "weight" the felt heft of one outcome's having more of your future in it than another, "branch" the ontology itself, "half" the answer the census gives, and "one" the thought that there was only ever one quantum state doing all of this. In the envoi each word appears exactly once, two per line: pair "half" with "branch", pair "one" with "weight", and let the last line carry "count" somewhere inside it and end on "measure".

The poem is in the voice of a young experimentalist, Ila, writing in her lab notebook to a colleague she nicknames the Bookkeeper. On her bench is a preparation that can be dialed continuously — a single qubitlike system set to amplitude √(1/3) on "up" and √(2/3) on "down", run N times in a row. The Bookkeeper has proposed what he calls the Census Rule: since in the Everettian picture every outcome really happens and branches are all the reality there is, rational credence should just be the fraction of branches on which the outcome occurs — and a fraction of branches is a count. On the up/down run that yields one half; across N runs, with 2^N branches, it yields the familiar bell curve centered on N/2. The squared-amplitude rule yields a bell curve centered on N/3. Ila's reply should make the reader feel the difference between N/2 and N/3 as something lived at the bench — the lamp, the notebook, the accumulating tally — and not merely stated.

Have the argumentative turn land in the fourth stanza, and let it be the moment of the dial: because she can rotate the amplitude continuously, there is a squeeze. At one end of the dial she is certain of the outcome; at equal amplitude, symmetry forces a half, which is exactly the Bookkeeper's answer and is why his rule looks principled at midpoint and nowhere else. Do not let the poem overclaim here. Symmetry and continuity narrow the field; they do not by themselves hand you the square. Let the poem register that the squeeze leaves something undetermined and gesture, without naming any contemporary author or paper, at what kind of extra ingredient is supposed to close the gap — a constraint about how weights compose across independent systems and repeated runs, or the decision-theoretic demand that an agent's betting weights cohere with one another across branches and over time.

Then let the ending be honest rather than triumphant. Somewhere out in the branching there are thin branches of tiny but nonzero amplitude on which all N runs come up "up", and the notebook on those branches records frequencies that flout the rule she has just defended. Let that haunt the envoi without being resolved.

Two formal demands, because the form is supposed to carry the philosophy rather than decorate it. First: a sestina gives every one of its six words exactly one end slot per stanza, which is precisely the Bookkeeper's fallacy — equal slots, equal reality — so the weight has to be smuggled in somewhere the roster cannot show it. Use line length, caesura, or internal repetition so that lines carrying the two-thirds branch run heavy and lines carrying the thin branches run short and gaunt. Second: no line should exist mainly to transmit a formula; if 1/3, 2/3, 2^N, or N/3 appear, they should be doing something a prose paraphrase could not do.

Write it so that a philosopher of physics would not wince at the physics and a reader who has never opened a quantum mechanics textbook would still find a good poem. No citations, no footnotes, no bibliography, no named authors, and no prose gloss inside the poem. After the poem, add a note of at most 120 words indicating which stanza carries the squeeze, where the extra ingredient beyond symmetry and continuity is invoked, and how line length or repetition is carrying amplitude.
