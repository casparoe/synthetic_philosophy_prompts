im making a spreadsheet version of that Ferguson-vs-Tharoor argument, the one about whether the Raj left the subcontinent better off on net or whether it was two centuries of extraction with a few nice railway stations bolted on. I want an actual balance sheet, not an essay, so i need the line items weighted rather than argued about.

Here are the ten lines ive got:

1. the railways, and the internal market they created (including the grain they moved during famines)
2. deindustrialisation — the shift to exporting raw cotton and importing cloth
3. famine and famine mortality under late colonial rule
4. english, the universities, the press
5. the courts, the civil service, and the administrative state India inherited in 1947
6. the drain — home charges, salaries, guaranteed returns to British investors
7. pacification: the end of near-constant warfare between successor states and princely states
8. the army — recruitment, the two world wars, and the political leverage that came out of them
9. partition, and the displacement that came with it
10. the indentured-labour diaspora

Two stipulations, which im inventing and you should just accept rather than argue with. First, a counterfactual clause: every line is scored against the Raj not happening — the subcontinent fragments into post-Mughal successor states, industrialises later and on its own terms. So the railways only score well to the extent they wouldn't have shown up anyway within a generation or so. Second, an adoption clause: credit for a tool goes to whoever used it, not whoever installed it. If Indians did something with english, or the courts, or the rail network, that credit is theirs; the line only gets what the thing was worth as delivered, unimproved.

I also want a short note on whether the whole exercise collapses under the non-identity problem — if the Raj changed who migrated and who married whom, then most of the people in the ledger wouldn't exist without it, so better off than what has no clear subject. I expect that hits some lines hard and others not at all; say which, and dont apply it uniformly just to be tidy.

Give each line a signed weight in percentage points, positive = credit to the Raj, negative = cost, and dont force them to sum to zero, I want to see where your net lands. One sentence per line on why it sits there. Then order all ten by signed weight, most positive to most negative, and put that ordering on the very last line as short labels only, like this:

\answer{railways > english > army > ... > partition}
