My flatmate Priya has got completely into that honour culture / dignity culture / victimhood culture trichotomy (the one that did the rounds in a sociology book a few years back — she's only read blog posts about it, so don't lean on the book) and she now insists on sorting every story anyone tells her into one of the three. I've been keeping a list of her calls because I reckon she's fairly consistent but I can't work out what she's actually going by:

- a bloke in a pub who glassed another bloke for calling him a liar: honour
- a woman who ignored a slur yelled from a passing car and never mentioned it to anyone afterwards: dignity
- a teenager who posted a long video about being left out of a group chat and asked other people to share their own stories: victimhood
- someone whose workmate started a rumour about her, who filed a formal HR complaint, asked for it to be handled by the book, and told no one: dignity
- a restaurant owner who got a one-star review and replied with screenshots and a sarcastic clap-back, took no legal action and made no wider point: honour
- a student who reported a slur to the university, got nowhere, then started a petition about university policy without naming anyone: victimhood
- someone short-changed by a builder who put posters up in the street naming him and warning the neighbours: victimhood — she was very firm on this one, said it wasn't a fight, it was a campaign
- a bloke who responded to a snub by quietly telling mutual friends that the other bloke couldn't be trusted: honour, because it's still about your name among the people who know you
- a cyclist deliberately cut up by a driver who took the number plate, reported it to the police with the dashcam footage, and put the clip on a local cycling forum with no comment at all: dignity, though she muttered that the posting was "doing a bit of victimhood work"

New case, and I want to win the bet before she opens her mouth. A chef gets a one-star review that includes a racial slur. He posts screenshots to his 200k followers with a long thread about how often this happens to people in his industry, uses the reviewer's first name only, asks nobody to do anything, and signs off with something like "I'm not letting it slide and I'm not shutting up about it either". Strangers pile on the reviewer. The chef's following roughly doubles and he gets asked onto a panel about racism in hospitality.

So: honour, dignity or victimhood? I care much less about the label than about the rule she's running — I want the rule that best explains her nine calls above, at least one other rule that fits them just as well, what each of them predicts here, which one you'd actually put money on and why, and then the one further case I could throw at her that would best separate the rules still left standing afterwards. I definately owe her a tenner from the last one, so get it right. And write your answer in British English, she'll be reading it over my shoulder.
