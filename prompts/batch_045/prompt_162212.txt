I lead a three-person forecasting cell at a freight-risk consultancy. Every Monday we keep one spreadsheet: a joint probability distribution over eight to twelve scenario cells (roughly, cause × location × time-to-clear) that we re-elicit each quarter, and which drives real money — whether a client pre-positions inventory, reroutes, or pays for expedited clearance. During the week, assessed reports arrive that are almost never of the form "X is now certain". They come as new marginals over one partition ("the chance the delay is at customs is now about 40 per cent"), as assessments conditional on a cell or on a group of cells ("if it is the northern route, then there is a three-in-four chance it clears within 48 hours"), as comparative or vague remarks ("port is now at least twice as likely as depot"), and sometimes as several of these at once in a single report from one source of imperfect reliability. My analysts are sharp but have no formal training in formal epistemology, they have fifteen to twenty minutes per update, and I sign off on every revision.

What I want from you is a written procedure they can actually run. Please build it around the following distinctions, which I want to survive the simplification: learning a proposition outright, which is ordinary conditioning; learning a new marginal over a partition, which is Jeffrey-style probability kinematics and is only legitimate when the odds inside each cell of that partition are untouched — say how an analyst is supposed to check that in practice, and what to do when it visibly fails; and inputs that are neither of those but merely constrain the new distribution, such as a conditional assessment whose antecedent is a cell or a cluster of cells — the case philosophers discuss as the Judy Benjamin problem — where the standard recommendation is to move to the closest distribution to the prior that satisfies the constraint, closeness measured by relative information (Kullback–Leibler divergence, equivalently minimum cross-entropy). For that last family, give my analysts the operational move: rewrite each input as a linear constraint on the cell probabilities, or show that it cannot be so rewritten; note that the solution then takes the form of an exponential tilt of the prior; and give a hand-workable recipe for the case of one or two constraints, plus what to do with a spreadsheet solver otherwise.

The procedure should have explicit decision points and explicit stops, including: what to do when a constraint set is jointly unsatisfiable, or satisfiable only by putting mass into a cell the prior gave zero; the diagnostic that a minimum-information update should leave the relative odds inside untouched cells exactly as they were, so that an analyst can tell when an answer is wrong; whether two constraints arriving together must be applied jointly rather than in sequence, and what goes wrong if they are sequenced; and the point where the choice of divergence rule is itself a methodological judgement that I have to sign off rather than a fact — tell me what a plausible alternative rule would give instead on the same input, and how to record which was used. It should also route inputs down two different channels: reports from a source of known reliability are evidence, to be handled with likelihoods over the cells, whereas an assessment is a constraint imposed on the new distribution, and conflating the two is the error I most want the procedure to make impossible.

Please apply the finished procedure to three cases and show the arithmetic. First, a calibration case: four cells, two regions at even odds, and within each region two causes at even odds; the report says that if it is the northern region, the chance it is the depot rather than the port is three in four. Give the four revised numbers, say what happened to the odds within the southern region, and say why that is the signature of this rule. Second, a case I will not like the answer to: a cell we wrote down as impossible in the quarterly elicitation, and a report this week that appears to require positive probability there. Third, a single report containing both a claim from a source with a known seventy-per-cent record ("it is customs") and a conditional assessment downstream of it ("if it is customs, sixty per cent chance it clears within 48 hours") — route each part properly, show what the table looks like if both are naively imposed as constraints, and state the rigidity assumption being relied on and how we would test it.

I would like the answer as the procedure itself, followed by a one-page checklist version my analysts can pin up, followed by a short honest section on what the procedure cannot do. Define any notation you use. Our team is American, so please write the deliverable in American English.
