I am writing a novel set in the orbit of a truth commission, in which several characters argue, repeatedly and badly, about whether two very different programmes of mass killing can be ranked against one another. Before I finish drafting I need a taxonomy of the positions available on that question, built as a tree: every node should be the claim that separates the positions from one another (whether a single quantity can be assigned to an atrocity at all; whether the question is answerable in principle but beyond our evidence; whether the obscenity lies in the ranking or in the mere asking; whether the purpose of the comparison matters — fixing guilt, apportioning commemoration, deciding what to prevent with limited means), and every leaf should carry a name, or the word "unoccupied", together with a single line on what a person holding it would refuse to say aloud.

Two constraints from the novelist's side. First, each branching question must be something a character could answer in their own voice without knowing any of the labels, so the tree sorts them by what they actually assert rather than by what I have decided they believe; give me, at each node, the line of dialogue that would settle it. Second, I need room for split purposes: a character who ranks freely when asked what a bystander should have prevented, yet falls silent when asked who suffered worse, must not be forced onto a single leaf — so tell me which nodes have to be repeated for each purpose.

Please also mark the answer combinations nobody occupies and say briefly whether that is because the view is incoherent or merely unfashionable, and flag any pair of leaves a reader could not tell apart from what the characters say, since my characters have to be distinguishable in scene. Finally, one axis I want included even if the literature ignores it: whether the manner of killing, up close versus through paperwork and supply decisions, is rank-relevant independently of the number of deaths. If it fits nowhere on the tree, tell me why.
