I am defending a version of Daniel Dennett's counterfeit-people argument about AI: today's conversational systems are engineered to exploit our tendency to adopt the intentional stance, and they mimic the outward markers of personhood while failing the conditions that actually make something a person, such as reciprocal recognition, moral accountability, and a genuine history of agency. Prepare me for the question period: give me the six to eight objections I will actually get from this audience, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased the way each subgroup would phrase them, each with a short reply and a note on where the reply is weakest, and flag the one question I am hoping nobody asks. I need a prep sheet, not a defense or a literature review.
