I am assembling a three-year research program on stake-sensitivity in epistemology — pragmatic encroachment, subject-sensitive and interest-relative invariantism, and those views' rivalry with, and possible collapse into, epistemic contextualism. The program will fund roughly six person-years of work across five or six projects, all of it conceptual: no experimental psychology, no corpus linguistics, no empirical work of any kind. Assume I already know the familiar terrain — the bank and travel cases, the knowledge–action principle, the reading of stakes as shifting a credal or evidential-support threshold, safety- and sensitivity-style accounts of what stakes might be doing to the modal profile of belief, and the standard arguments for and against treating knows as semantically context-dependent. Do not survey that terrain for me, and do not recommend readings. What I need from you is an agenda, and a defense of its ordering.

Three case-types have been nagging at me, and I want you to treat them as data to be accommodated rather than as confusions to be dissolved. The labels are stipulative.

*Role-stakes.* A hospital pharmacy technician checks a label against a screen. Her personal stakes are trivial; the stakes attached to her role, fixed by a protocol and liability document she has never read and could not recite, are severe. Which stakes encroach, if any? Note the variants: the role-stakes are real but unknown to her; the institution has quietly lowered them; two technicians with identical evidence and identical care occupy roles carrying different stakes. If knowledge tracks role-stakes, it begins to look like a status an institution confers rather than something the subject possesses; if it tracks personal stakes, the intuitions that motivated encroachment fall quiet precisely in the cases with the most social weight.

*Adversarial stakes.* A counterparty who wants you to be uncertain can raise the cost of your being wrong — a penalty clause, a planted trap, a threat it will later carry out. If knowledge is stake-sensitive, another agent can destroy your knowledge without touching your evidence, your reliability, or the facts, merely by changing what is at stake. Is that a reductio, or an interesting consequence the view should own? And does it matter whether the subject could have foreseen the stake-raising?

*Proleptic stakes.* Two subjects have identical evidence, identical credences, identical present circumstances, and are equally careful. One will next month be handed a decision in which this very proposition is pivotal; the other never will be. Neither can now foresee which is which. Does the first fail to know *now* — in virtue of a fact about her evidence and present situation that is, by hypothesis, absent?

Alongside these, take seriously a structural question I think the literature has left underspecified: what exactly is the stakes variable? Expected cost of error, worst-case cost, the ratio of the costs of the two error directions, or something else? In the asymmetric cases — false positives cheap, false negatives catastrophic, or the reverse — the plausible specifications come apart, and a single scalar threshold on credence cannot represent all of them.

What I want from you:

1. Four or five questions the program should commission, in priority order, with the ordering defended explicitly. For each, say what it is upstream of — which other questions cannot be settled until it is, and which it merely informs — how tractable it is in three years with analytic and light formal tools, how neglected it is relative to how much ink it has absorbed, and what its payoff is outside epistemology: legal standards turning on knowledge or reasonable belief, professional roles and institutional sign-off, and whether stake-sensitivity should be built into automated systems that defer to human judgment.
2. The trade-offs between your criteria, stated as trade-offs. Where importance and tractability pull apart, say which you let win and why.
3. What you would decline to fund, and why. I expect you to be willing to say that some live-looking question has been farmed out, or that its answer would not change anything downstream. Tell me whether the semantics-versus-pragmatics question — is the phenomenon best captured by an interest-relative but non-indexical semantics, by a contextualist semantics, or by a pragmatic story about what assertions of knows convey — deserves a project of its own or is a sink.
4. Which of my three case-types, if any, genuinely discriminates between encroachment and contextualism, and which is only a new instance of an old pattern and should be dropped from the program.
5. Where the dispute bottoms out: a substantive disagreement about knowledge, a disagreement about which notion knows expresses, or something closer to verbal. Is settling that worth a project, or is it the sort of question that only gets answered retrospectively?
6. What evidence appearing within the next three years — an argument, a formal result, a shift in a neighboring field — would move items up or down your list.

Reason your way to the ordering. I am not asking for a map of the literature; I am asking to be told where six person-years should go, and to be able to disagree with you about the reasons.
