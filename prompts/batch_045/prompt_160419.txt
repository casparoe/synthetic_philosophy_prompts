I host a small recorded interview series on long-standing conceptual problems in physics, and next month's guest is Dr. Ines Vartanian, a thermodynamicist who has just published a monograph arguing that every mainstream exorcism of Maxwell's demon quietly presupposes a demon that stands *outside* the system it sorts. Her target includes both the classical erasure accounting — the move that saves the second law by charging the demon for clearing its memory, at roughly k_B T ln 2 for a perfectly informative bit — and the more recent information-theoretic form of the second law, on which the work extractable in a feedback-controlled cycle is bounded by the drop in the system's free energy plus k_B T times the mutual information the demon's memory carries about the system, i.e. W ≤ −ΔF + k_B T·I. On her reading, both treatments install an agent who measures from without, parks the result in a register whose thermal couplings nobody has to specify, and settles the bill in a bookkeeping step the gas itself never sees. Her constructive claim is that once the demon is made genuinely autonomous — physically built into the apparatus, powered by the same bath, with a finite memory it clears in blocks rather than bit by bit, and operating at finite speed rather than quasistatically — the standard accounting either double-counts the demon's own degrees of freedom or reinstates, in the act of modelling, the very externality it was meant to eliminate.

I'd like you to draft eight to ten questions for this interview, together with a one- or two-sentence follow-up for each, keyed to the reply she is most likely to make. She has two stock answers I want made progressively harder to lean on: that the cost is always discharged at erasure and its locus is thermodynamically irrelevant, and that the mutual-information bound is not a licence for a working demon because in any real device the information is never free. Among the questions, please include at least one each on: the circularity worry (that the principle used to exorcise the demon is itself derived from the second law it is invoked to protect); whether a bound of the form above refutes the demon or merely taxes it, given that a device reliably extracting just under k_B T ln 2 per bit still does everything Maxwell imagined; a two-temperature variant, in which the demon's memory register is held at a temperature different from the gas, asking which temperature enters the bound and whether a cold memory could be run as a refrigerator at a profit; a "forgetful" demon that writes each measurement outcome onto fresh tape and never erases, so that the exorcism becomes a claim about the finite supply of blank tape rather than about thermodynamics; the worry that Feynman's ratchet-and-pawl point — that any physical latch is itself at temperature T and therefore jitters — already does the whole job, making the erasure step redundant; a partial-information demon whose correlations with the system are weaker than one bit per cycle, and whether the tax should then be proportional or whether there is a threshold below which nothing at all can be extracted; and the difference between per-cycle and asymptotic bookkeeping, given a demon that can run for an enormous number of cycles before its first reset. Close with one question about what a tabletop experiment would have to show to count against her thesis: we record without a live fact-checker, so nothing should depend on recalling titles, dates, or authors, and I'd rather the questions be unusable as invitations to restate her position than merely polite.
