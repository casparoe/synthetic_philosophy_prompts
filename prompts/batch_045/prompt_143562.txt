Putting together a short post for my EA-adjacent reading group on interpersonal utility comparisons, and I want someone to audit my position set before I embarass myself with it. I have a feeling at least one thing in here has to go, but I can't tell which.

Roughly where I've landed: (1) I'm a utilitarian in the boring total sense, I'm happy with QALYs-per-dollar style cost-effectiveness reasoning, and I bite the bullet on the repugnant conclusion — I don't think it works as a reductio. (2) I think interpersonal comparisons of welfare are real and that we have decent epistemic access to them: self-reports on a zero-to-ten scale, the plain obviousness that a migraine is worse than a stubbed toe, that sort of thing. (3) But I also think the economists are basically right that utility in the preference-satisfaction sense is just a representation of choice behavior, and that there's no fact of the matter about the relative scale of two people's utility functions — nothing makes my units ten times yours or a tenth of yours — and I read Arrow-style impossibility results as support for that. (4) I think the best derivation of utilitarianism is the Harsanyi impartial-observer move where you weigh outcomes as if you had an equal chance of being anyone, and I think range-normalizing everybody onto a zero-to-one scale is ad hoc and gives people with narrow, boring lives more weight than they deserve. (5) In actual policy work I'm fine with willingness-to-pay as the metric in cost-benefit analysis, and I don't like distributional weighting — it feels like fudging the numbers until you get the answer you wanted. (6) I think people who say you can't put a number on suffering are mostly giving up early and dressing it up as sophistication.

Which of these actually pull against each other? I have vague suspicions in two spots: that using willingness-to-pay as my operationalization doesn't sit well with (1) and (2) once you remember diminishing marginal utility of money, and that (4) might quietly deliver average rather than total utility and so doesn't fit with biting the bullet in (1). But I genuinely can't tell whether either of those is a real problem or just a vibe, and I'd rather not throw away a commitment I don't have to. Rank the tensions by how much they actually cost me, say for each what the least damaging revision would be and what I'd give up by making it, and flag anything that only looks inconsistent.

And please write it as continuous prose, the way you'd write an email — no headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables.
