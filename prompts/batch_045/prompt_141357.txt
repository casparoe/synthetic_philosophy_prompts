So this is dumb, but I got voluntold to write the fun insert for our dept newsletter and I want to do a parody of one of those bureaucratic assessment forms. Background: our lab does comparative cognition (corvids, a couple of octopuses, one horrifyingly clever border collie) and this year the university made us renew "cognitive capacity" paperwork for every animal we work with, so I've spent a week filling out forms that pretend you can grade a mind the way you grade a cut of beef. Meanwhile our journal club just did the four-way taxonomy of kinds of minds — the Darwinian grade where the whole behavioral repertoire is fixed by selection and the individual can't really learn anything inside its own lifetime, just act out what ancestral environments built into it; the Skinnerian grade that can be shaped by reinforcement but only by actually doing the thing and eating the consequence; the Popperian grade that can try out candidate actions against an internal model first, so bad hypotheses can perish in its place instead of the animal paying with its hide; and the Gregorian grade, the one with imported thinking tools — language, notation, diagrams, arithmetic, whatever the local meme pool hands it — whose smarts live at least partly outside its skull, in the scaffolding.

What I want is the form itself: an in-universe government/IRB-ish document, something like "Application for Regrade / Cognitive Capacity Assessment," ~800 words, written in flat civil-service compliance prose with numbered sections and a lot of smug fine print, from a world where a board actually assigns creatures a grade and the grade has consequences. The jokes have to come from the taxonomy being right, not from inventing nonsense about it, so: the Darwinian section should ask questions no Darwinian creature could answer and no Darwinian creature needs to; the Skinnerian section should quietly be about cost — who pays for the education, with what, and how many trials; the Popperian section should let the applicant skip the pain but then have to admit, somewhere in the fine print, that the inner model doing the preselecting was itself built by selection plus reinforcement, so the guard has no guard; and the Gregorian section should let applicants bring tools and then fall apart, because once tools are permitted you're no longer measuring the head, you're measuring the applicant's library, and there's no principled place to stop. A few things I'd like in there: a "provisional (tool-dependent)" status with a demotion clause for what happens at a closed-tools regrade hearing when the notebooks and the language get taken away, and an appeals provision for cases where the candidate hypotheses were generated by something else — a trainer, a textbook, a statistical model — so the error died in someone else's stead and the board genuinely cannot say whether the grade belongs to the applicant or to whatever supplied the guesses. Keep it deadpan and legible, never wink at the reader, let the form keep confidently applying criteria that only the top grade could satisfy, and don't let it accidentally resolve the regress or turn into a sermon about how all grading is socially constructed. Oh and please write it in American English — the newsletter goes out to our US funding partners too.
