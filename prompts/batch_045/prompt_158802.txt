I'm designing a review pipeline for a safety-critical decision process and want to know how much weight the Condorcet jury theorem should carry as its justification. A fixed review budget buys either one carefully supervised senior reviewer, whose chance of getting a given item right I'd put at about 0.85, or a panel of N cheap AI reviewers, each of which I'd independently put at about 0.62 on the same item distribution.

The panel's errors are not independent. On a share q of items there is a common cause of error — a shared misconception, a shared blind spot in the data the reviewers were trained on — and on those items the panel effectively votes as one, with the shared error pointing in an unfavorable direction at least as often as not. On the rest, residual pairwise error correlation is ρ, which I suggest treating by substituting an effective jury size of N divided by one plus (N minus one) times ρ. Two further wrinkles: the items are contested enough that "getting it right" may be partly indeterminate, and whoever produces the items may adapt to whatever review rule I adopt.

Give me a number: your credence that at N = 25, q = 0.2 and ρ = 0.1, majority vote over the cheap panel has higher expected accuracy than the single senior reviewer. I've given you the parameters, so don't answer that it depends. I want the headline figure plus the decomposition: your credence that the 0.62 competence estimate survives adaptation to the panel rule; your credence that panel errors really decompose into a shared-cause component and a residual-correlated component in the way I've stipulated; your credence that correctness is determinate enough on these items for accuracy to be the right currency at all; and the size of the effective-jury-size penalty at these values. A number and one line of justification for each, then how they combine.

Finally: which term is your figure most sensitive to, what one week of empirical work would move it most, and at what N you would stop adding reviewers rather than spend the difference on better supervision.
