I run a once-a-month reading group for physics undergrads (they know stat mech and partition functions, basically zero philosophy) and the next session is on Maxwell's demon. I want the handout to be a decision tree instead of prose: something a student can walk from "fine, I'll grant the demon could in principle be built and that a Szilard engine nets you kT ln 2 per cycle" down to a leaf that tells them what they actually think the demon proved.

What I want from you:

- Build the tree from the logical space first: the questions, and which combinations of answers they allow — then place the real positions into that space. Don't start from a list of famous names and work backwards.
- 7 or 8 branch nodes, each phrased as a question someone can answer without knowing where it leads. No node that's just "do you accept Landauer's principle?" — I want the nodes to be about cases and about what has to be paid for, and let the principle fall out of the answers instead of being assumed.
- 7 to 9 leaves, each a position on "does the demon get exorcised, and by what?" Give it the usual label if you're confident of it; if you're not, make up a two-word name and put a (?) after it. I'd much rather have a clunky description I can trust than a sleek label you're guessing at.
- At least two answer-combinations the space allows but nobody actually occupies, with a one-line diagnosis each (incoherent? just unattractive? merely unexplored?).
- Any pair of leaves your tree genuinely can't seperate, flagged as such rather than forced apart.

The three cases I want used as diagnostic nodes, because these are the ones my students got stuck on last time:

1. the one-shot demon: one molecule, one cycle, kT ln 2 extracted, then we let the whole apparatus melt and never reset it. Violation, violation only on average, or no violation at all?
2. the cold-tape demon: it records bits on a tape, then instead of erasing at T it ships the full tape to a reservoir at T/10 and erases there, paying k(T/10) ln 2 per bit. Where did the rest of the cost go, or is there no rest?
3. the heirloom demon: it comes into existence already holding a tape whose bits happen to match the gas perfectly. It never measures anything, just reads what it inherited, same yield per cycle. If the cost still lands somewhere, where?

And one node I definately want, because I think three of the standard positions differ only on this: whose entropy budget are we balancing — the gas, the demon's memory, the sealed box, or the box plus whoever prepared it? Make the tree say which line each leaf is drawing instead of hiding it inside "the second law is safe".

Aim for something that fits on two sides of A4.
