hey — I'm building one session of a first-year "how history works" seminar on non-human causes, and I want to stop my students from writing either "environmental determinism, bad!" or "Diamond was basically right" and calling it a day. So I invented two seperate numbers and I want you to apply them.

For each case below give me:

- **C (0–10)**: how much the outcome counterfactually depends on the non-human event *holding that society's institutions roughly fixed*. Take the germ/climate/volcano out of the story and ask how different the outcome is in that one place.
- **S (0–10)**: how much of the *spread* in outcomes across the different societies the event hit is explained by the event itself rather than by what those societies had already built (grain markets, property law, medical theory, who had to eat what). High S = it overrode local differences. Low S = same event, wildly different results depending on human arrangements.

The cases:

1. The Black Death and the rise in real wages / weakening of bound labour in parts of Western Europe afterwards.
2. Old World pathogens and the demographic collapse of Native American populations after 1492.
3. The eruption behind the "Year Without a Summer" and the harvest failure, food rioting and emigration that followed in Europe and New England.
4. Potato blight and the Irish famine.
5. The cholera pandemics and 19th-century urban sewer-building.
6. Cooling in the 1300s and the disappearance of the Norse settlements in Greenland.
7. A big Icelandic fissure eruption in the 1780s that poisoned pasture, killed livestock and caused famine there.

Then three more things:

- Which cases come out **high C and low S**? My hypothesis is that this combination is exactly where calling the microbe or the volcano an "actor" does the most damage — it's doing enormous causal work, but it isn't the thing that explains why here rather than there. Tell me if that's the right way to carve it, or if I've got the axis wrong.
- Give each case a tier for how comfortable you'd be letting a student write a paragraph with the non-human thing as the grammatical subject of a verb of action: **fine / fine with a wink / only in scare quotes / don't**. One sentence per case on why it lands there.
- Flag any case where the underlying empirical claim is shaky or contested enough that I shouldn't put it in front of first-years without hedging — and tell me which of the two numbers my students will most likely conflate.
