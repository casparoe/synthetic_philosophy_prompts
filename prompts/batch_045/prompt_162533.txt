Our reading group is doing a Nietzsche-and-film night and I drew Parasite, which looks on it's face like a ready-made illustration of ressentiment from the Genealogy — family crammed into a semi-basement, an elaborate scam against a cheerful rich household, an eruption of violence at the birthday party — but I'm suspicious of how easy that reading is, and I want you to stress-test it rather than confirm it.

Give me exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, no intro and no conclusion: the strongest Nietzschean reading you can manage, naming the two or three scenes that actually carry it; the place the mapping breaks, which I think is that there are no masters in this film — the Parks are pleasant bourgeois consumers, no pathos of distance, no self-created values, nobody Nietzsche would call noble, so there is nobody here for a slave revolt in morality to be aimed at; whether the smell is better handled as bad conscience and internalized cruelty than as ressentiment proper; an idea I can't shake, which is that most of the film's violence is lateral — the two poor couples fighting each other in the bunker and over the phone — while the one vertical killing is a single unreflective stab that looks more like the immediate discharge Nietzsche attributes to the noble type, so maybe what separates reactive from active isn't class position but whether there's an outlet within arm's reach — tell me if that's a real gap in the Genealogy or if the book already has resources for it; and finally what the film shows about ressentiment that argument alone doesn't, thinking especially of the fantasy ending and whether that's a revaluation of values or the same desire with a new buyer, and, if you think it cuts the other way, just say plainly that this film isn't the book's object and I should tell the group so.
