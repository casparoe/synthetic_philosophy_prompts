I'm writing a short term paper for a philosophy of physics reading group on the hole argument, and I've constructed an argument whose conclusion I can't accept but whose individual steps all look sound to me. I'd be grateful for a diagnosis.

The setup, as I understand it: work on a fixed manifold M with a Cauchy surface Σ and a "hole" H lying in the future domain of dependence of Σ. Let d be a diffeomorphism that is the identity on a neighbourhood of Σ and everywhere outside H, but non-trivial inside H. Then if (M, g, T) is a solution of the Einstein field equations, so is (M, d\*g, d\*T): the two models induce exactly the same initial data on Σ, agree everywhere outside H, and assign different metric values to the points inside H. So: (1) if those two models represent distinct physical possibilities, GR is indeterministic — the complete state on Σ fails to determine what happens in H, even though H lies inside Σ's domain of dependence. (2) That can't be right; the initial-value problem for GR is well posed, and the data on a Cauchy surface are supposed to determine a unique solution up to diffeomorphism. So the two models do not represent distinct possibilities. (3) The usual moral: models related by a diffeomorphism represent one and the same physical world; there is no fact of the matter about which spacetime point bears which metric value, and only the diffeomorphism-invariant structure is physical. Call this Leibniz equivalence. (4) But Leibniz equivalence as stated is fully general — for any diffeomorphism whatsoever, (M, g) and (dM, d\*g) are one world; being isomorphic is sufficient for being the same world. (5) Now consider a toy theory, call it Flash. Its background structure is bare time with no distinguished instant, and its only law is that there is exactly one flash: a field that is 0 up to some instant and 1 thereafter, with that instant left entirely unspecified. Take two models, flash at t₀ and flash at t₁. They are isomorphic: a diffeomorphism of time carrying t₀ to t₁ maps one onto the other, and the background has nothing that distinguishes one instant from another. Indeed every model of Flash is isomorphic to every other, so on the criterion in (4) Flash has exactly one world. (6) So Flash is deterministic — and so is any theory whose symmetry group can carry any model into any other, however brute its events look. But Flash is the paradigm of an indeterministic theory: nothing in the history before the flash settles when it occurs. (7) So Leibniz equivalence, the standard reply to the hole argument, proves too much: it erases the distinction between determinism and indeterminism wherever a theory has enough symmetry. (8) So the reply fails, and the substantivalist is left with the indeterminism in (1).

Which step should I reject, and why did it look sound in the first place? If I have to choose, I'd rather keep Leibniz equivalence as a reply to the hole argument than my verdict on Flash, but I want to know what each of those costs — in particular what "the state on a region determines the state elsewhere" has to mean once Leibniz equivalence is in play, whether GR still comes out deterministic under that reading, and whether the verdict then depends on how the points of one model are identified with the points of another. The one difference I can locate between the two cases is that the hole diffeomorphism is the identity on the region where the two models agree, whereas my time translation is not — and I can't see why that should matter, given that (4) ranges over all diffeomorphisms alike.
