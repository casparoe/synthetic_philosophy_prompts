I'm defending what my superviser keeps calling the "grumpy Humean" line on testimony, and I need someone to try to break it. The claim: nobody gets justification from being told that p unless, somewhere in the explanatory chain, they've got non-testimonial reasons — perception, memory, inference — for taking the speaker to be reliable, either globally or about this kind of claim specifically. Testimony is a channel, not a source.

The part that's supposedly mine is how I cash out "somewhere in the chain." I want to say the needed reasons aren't per-claim; they're a kind of calibration budget. You accumulate evidence that some speaker, or some class of speakers, is reliable in a domain; that budget gets spent down as you accept more of what they say; and justification runs out when the budget is exhausted, or when the evidence feeding it was itself testimonial all the way down. I like this because it gives the view teeth against the Reidian rival — it predicts a specific failure pattern: sudden, domain-local collapse of trust rather than smooth uniform entitlement that gets defeated case by case. Which I take to be roughly what happens when you're reading output from a system that produces assertion-shaped sentences but has no track record in the domain, no communicative intentions, and no community that has calibrated it — LLMs are the case I keep coming back to, where the evidence you'd lean on is benchmark results, and those are, near enough, just people telling me things.

So: what would count against this? Defeaters ranked fatal to merely awkward, please, and keep separate what a reductionist would themselves concede counts against them from what they'd just wave off. Is the bootstrapping problem — children, first encounters, learning a first language at all — fatal, or just the price of admission? Does my calibration-budget version collapse straight into anti-reductionism once I let the budget be inherited from other people, i.e., is "somewhere in the chain" doing all the work and none of the predicting? Does the LLM case cut against me (we do seem to get justification from things that aren't speakers at all) or for me? And please be honest about whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability: if every apparent case of justified testimonial belief with no non-testimonial reason behind it gets answered by positing a local reason I hadn't noticed, is that a problem for a view of this kind, or is it fine? The thing that would move me most is a case where a hearer is clearly justified and no non-testimonial reason is available even in principle, not merely one I failed to notice.
