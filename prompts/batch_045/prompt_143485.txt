The thesis, briefly, so you have something to aim at. I argue that Sen's liberal paradox isn't a conflict between liberty and anything at all, but an artifact of one modelling decision: rights get represented as decisiveness over pairs of complete social states, so a right turns into a conditional fact about the social ranking. I want to replace that with what I've been calling *exercise-conditional* rights — a right is a power over a coordinate of the social state — and then index the Pareto principle: it may only be applied across states that don't differ in anyone's exercise of a protected power. Take the three-outcome case (Lewd reads, Prude reads, nobody reads; Lewd ranks Prude-reads above Lewd-reads above nobody-reads, Prude ranks nobody-reads above Prude-reads above Lewd-reads). The two rights-respecting comparisons still go through on my account, but the Pareto step that closes the cycle compares two states that differ precisely in what each person does with their own protected coordinate, so it isn't an admissible comparison and the cycle never closes. The obvious cost, which I try to get ahead of in the paper, is leakage: almost anything anyone actually does has effects on other people's coordinates, so if the personal sphere is drawn generously my Pareto condition does almost no work, and if it's drawn narrowly I've settled the substance in advance by drawing it. My fix is to distinguish causal side-effects from constitutive dependence, and I'm not at all sure I can hold that line.
