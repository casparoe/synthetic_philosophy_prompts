So here's the setup before the actual ask: I fly gliders most weekends and I've been working through Dennett's four kinds of creatures — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian, the tower of generate-and-test — with two people from the club, one of whom is an airline first officer, and we got into a stupid argument about US Airways 1549 that none of us could settle. I want you to be the referee: imagine you're a philosopher of cognitive science who also knows aviation human factors, and think carefully, brainstorm the mapping before you commit to anything. Don't just retell the Hudson landing, I know the story: Airbus A320 out of LaGuardia in January 2009, flock of Canada geese right after takeoff, both engines out at a couple thousand feet, Sullenberger and Skiles end up ditching in the river, everybody got out. (I've read contradictory things about whether a return to LaGuardia was actually flyable and I don't want anything to hang on that, so treat it as unsettled.) What I actually want is a disciplined mapping of those three minutes onto the tower, and then the same mapping run backwards.

Theory onto case first: who or what is the creature here — Sully as an individual organism, the two pilots as a coupled unit, or the whole socio-technical aviation system? What are the candidate actions in the generate-and-test process (ditch north, ditch south, Teterboro, back to LaGuardia, restart attempts, the order they try them in), and where do those candidates come from — is ATC literally functioning as an off-board hypothesis generator? Put each piece of the response on a floor: the overlearned hands, the ditching checklist, the glider-pilot background, crew resource management, the simulators, the bird-strike reporting and airspace planning that made the hazard legible at all. Then case back onto theory: what would a merely Skinnerian creature do with three minutes and zero trials available? My guess is that this is the whole point of the upper floors — that every floor below Popperian is simply unavailable when you get one shot, since it would need actual deaths to learn from — but tell me if that's right, or whether some conditioned/overlearned stuff is doing real work in the actual flying that the tower has no vocabulary for.

Then please take my two half-baked ideas seriously and tell me whether they extend the framework or break it. One, the compiled Gregorian: a competence acquired on the fourth floor, out of words and checklists and sim hours, but executed under time pressure as fast automatic pattern recognition that looks a lot more Skinnerian in real time than Gregorian — doesn't that show the tower is labelling the history of a competence rather than the process that runs when you use it, and that Dennett slides between those two readings? Two, the fifth floor: an institutional or distributed creature whose hypotheses die in surrogates that live outside anybody's skull — full-motion sims, incident databases, NTSB investigations, air traffic control, the manufacturer's procedures. Should the tower have a floor like that, or does adding it wreck the point of the hierarchy, which as far as I can tell is about where the design information comes from (genes, reinforcement, private rehearsal, culture) and not about how many nervous systems it gets spread across?

And the part I care most about: where is the tower silent on this case? My list is time pressure versus cost of information (the tower is about how wastefully you learn, not how fast you execute), startle and stress, trust in instruments versus what you can see out the window, whether a group can be a creature at all, and the nasty irony that the geese are Darwinian-plus-Skinnerian creatures whose habits were shaped by our own Gregorian land-use decisions, and the only thing that can do anything about them is a decades-long institutional learning loop that no individual organism gets credit for. Sort those into real gaps versus me projecting a personal-level story onto an evolutionary framework, and give me a verdict at the end: is 1549 a confirmation of the tower, an anomaly for it, or the kind of case the tower was never built to describe? Table of correspondences first, then the breaks, then the verdict.
