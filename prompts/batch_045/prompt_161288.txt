I'm a PhD student (philosophy/cogsci, leaning philosophy of mind) and I have a 40+20 slot at a Minds and Machines colloquium next week where I'm defending a view I've been calling consumer-relative representational realism about trained neural networks. Roughly: yes, internal states of trained nets genuinely represent things, but 'represents' is a three-place relation — state S represents X *for downstream consumer C*, in virtue of a counterfactual-supporting causal role in producing the behaviour — so unrelativized claims like 'layer 8 represents the gender of the French noun' are indeterminate rather than false. The sketch: (1) the divergent-readout case shows unrelativized content claims have no determinate answer; (2) relativized ones are causally testable and do real explanatory work in predicting behaviour under intervention; (3) the 'it's just vectors all the way down' line generalizes straight to systems neuroscience, so it proves too much. The bit I think is actually new is something I'm calling *export width*: the number of distinct downstream consumers that read a given direction with the same counterfactual profile. My claim is that narrow export width is exactly why so many probing results feel gerrymandered, and that high export width is what licenses the context-independent ascriptions people want to make. The case I lean on is one I built myself in a 4-layer transformer trained on synthetic Python-ish code: one direction in the residual stream is read by one attention head as 'we're inside a string literal' and by another head, in a different context, as 'the previous token was an opening quote'; ablate one consumer path and fine-tune briefly and the single direction splits into two roughly orthogonal ones. I want to argue the pre-split state has no determinate content beyond what it does for each reader, while the post-split states do. There's a consequence I'm owning up front: features the network computes but that no current consumer reads — dormant features, the kind that only light up after fine-tuning — come out as not represented on my view, which feels wrong to me and will definitely feel wrong to the room. I ran a version of this in our seminar last month and got wrecked on exactly that point, so I know it's coming.

The audience, precisely: about half the room is philosophy-of-mind people, mostly teleosemantic / proper-function types plus one or two phenomenal-intentionality people, who think nothing in a trained net has content because the only thing selection ever cared about was loss minimization, and who will say my counterfactual condition buys me causality but no normativity and therefore no misrepresentation — error collapses into mere suboptimality. Then there are two interpretability engineers who do activation patching and sparse autoencoders all day, and who will say (a) my 'counterfactual-supporting causal role' is just patching with Latin on top, (b) superposition means there's no fact of the matter how many features are in a layer, so my export-width metric measures an artifact of my decomposition choice, and (c) dormant features just refute me. And there's one philosopher of science who always asks whether representational talk in a young science is a placeholder that gets eliminated the way talk of 'the gene' got refined, and whether any representational ascription has ever predicted something you couldn't have got from behaviour plus architecture. Nobody there works on decision theory or AI risk, so skip objections from that direction.
