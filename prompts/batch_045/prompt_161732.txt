Hi — I help run a small survival server that's been going for about six years, and I'm trying to write an actual written conduct policy (we've been doing it by vibes until now), and I keep hitting the same wall in the arguements.

Here's the shape of it. Player A spends four months building a cathedral. Player B blows it up with TNT, wich is obnoxious but isn't against any rule. A says: "you destroyed something real." B says: "nothing was destroyed, it's data on a hard drive, you're comparing a pile of blocks to losing your house." A then spends three pages arguing about how many hours went into it, which completely misses what B meant — B wasn't denying the hours, he was saying the loss doesn't weigh much. Then the reverse case: we announced a world reset last year, players said "you're erasing something real", I heard that as "this is as bad as demolishing a town", said something snippy about it being only a game, and only later realised they just meant "there is a thing here that we care about and you're ending it without asking us first."

So my rough read is that "real" is doing two jobs — is-there-a-thing-there at all versus does-it-matter-enough — and each side hears the other as talking about the wrong one. But I think there might be a third thing going on too: sometimes people aren't claiming anything about what the object is and are instead saying what stance we should adopt ("griefing should count as a real offence in the rules even though this is a game"). I can't tell if that's a third sense or just what the second one turns into once you stop trying to settle it.

Someone on our forum said it's plain equivocation, and someone else said it's a "merely verbal" dispute. Neither feels right to me. Both senses of "real" are perfectly coherent on their own, so it isn't a word shifting meaning mid-sentence in a way you can catch; and it doesn't feel empty either, because once you clear it up there's still a genuine fight about whether the loss counts for anything and how much.

So: is there an established concept for this configuration — the slide between existence and significance, plus the projective "treat it as real" bit — or is it three separate things I'm lumping together? If it's nearest to some existing idea, tell me which one and where exactly my description stops matching it. If there's nothing, coin a term and define it, and give me the boundary cases: does the same thing cover money, insults, flags, anything whose existence depends on people agreeing? I need the carving more than the label, because the rule I'm writing has to be answering one specific question rather than three. I'm not an academic, so keep it plain where you can — and please answer in British English.
