My mate and I spent a good two hours in the pub last night going round in circles about privacy and got absolutely nowhere, so I want to try a different format. Roughly: I think privacy is a matter of what other people can find out about you — you have privacy over some fact to the extent that others don't have it and can't easily get hold of it. He thinks that's hopelessly crude and that privacy is really about the norms governing a context: whose business something is, whether its flow fits the norms of that relationship, that sort of thing. His killer case was a landlord who puts a camera in your bathroom and never once watches the footage — he says your privacy is wrecked, I say nothing was found out so nothing was lost. My counter was that his account can't handle a storm ripping your curtains down in front of a bus queue: no norms are violated by anybody, yet something privacy-ish clearly goes wrong.

Could you draft the joint statement we could both sign if we ran this properly as an adversarial collaboration? I'd like a numbered section of claims we can both agree on (including, I suspect, that losing privacy and being wronged are seperate things), then a table of the disagreements, where each row gives one crux, my line and his line in wording each of us would actually accept, a concrete case we'd both agree bears on it, and what each of us would predict about that case. Two more cruxes to work in if you can: whether a purely inferred fact can cost you privacy — an insurer scores you as a bad risk from other people's shopping data, nobody read your file, nobody was told anything — and whether the thing that has privacy has to be an individual at all, or whether a family group chat or a parish can have it too.

Please commit rather than hand me a menu: say which framing you think is nearer the mark, which cruxes are decisive, which parts of our row are merely verbal, and give reasons for each of those calls. And flag anywhere you're guessing at what either of us is definately committed to, or where you've invented a move neither of us would endorse.
