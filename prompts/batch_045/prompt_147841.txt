I'm writing the framing chapter of a dissertation on whether the parallel between the genetic code and a Gödel numbering is a genuine structural isomorphism or only a suggestive metaphor, and before I commit to a line in front of my committee I want someone to check whether the package I have ended up with actually holds together. Here is the position as I would defend it.

(1) The parallel holds at the level of mechanism. In Hofstadter's typogenetics — the puzzle system in *Gödel, Escher, Bach* where strings are copied, cut, spliced and rebound by rules that look only at letter-shape — the manipulation is purely typographical, and I claim real translation is the same: the decoding machinery responds to shape and complementarity at a local site, not to any direct chemical affinity between a codon and the amino acid it stands for, since the matching is done by adaptor molecules whose own structure implements the mapping. So a genome is to a cell roughly as a set of strings is to a formal system: the ribosome is a proof-checker, translation is a derivation, and the genetic code has the status of a numbering scheme.

(2) The arbitrariness of the code is what earns the analogy its content. Because nothing about the chemistry fixes which codon goes with which amino acid, the mapping can do the work of an arbitrary encoding; the further fact that it is then fixed and shared across lineages — the arbitrary-then-frozen story — is what makes a given string have one determinate reading rather than none. A colleague insists these two halves of (2) fight each other. I do not see it, but it is the one place I have been challenged.

(3) I want to be deflationary about content on the formal side, and I think the deflation carries over. The Gödel sentence does not, in itself, say anything about itself; its content is a product of the coding scheme, the rules and an interpretation, and there is nothing more to a string's being about something than the role it occupies within such a scheme. I would say exactly the same about a gene.

(4) But I also want a disanalogy, and it is the one I care most about: biological content is intrinsic in a way formal content is not. What a gene codes for is settled by machinery that is physically present, and no external interpreter has to step in and assign a reading. This, I claim, is why error, malfunction and misinformation are at home in biology and not in number theory.

(5) Downstream of this I am a selection-history theorist: what a stretch of DNA is for is a matter of what it was selected for, not of chemistry and not of any irreducible teleology. No vitalism and no primitive normativity — if there is a normativity of malfunction it has to be assembled out of function plus something else.

(6) Where I think the analogy really breaks is self-replication. Self-replication requires a description plus a constructor that executes it; in the cell the constructor is itself built out of the described medium, whereas in a formal system the rules stand outside the strings and nothing is built at all. That is why the Gödel sentence is self-referential but not self-replicating. And methodologically I want to deflate strange-loop talk generally: either cash the loop out in mechanism or drop it.

(7) Two case verdicts I am attached to. First: a sequence that no cell will ever read — reconstructed from an extinct organism, or held in a database — still determinately codes for a particular protein; I do not want to say its content goes blank until someone translates it. Second: prions transmit heritable information although nothing sequence-like is being read off a nucleic acid template, so whatever information means in biology it cannot just mean what the code maps a string to.

What I want is an audit, not a survey and not a summary of the literature. Go through these and tell me which pairs genuinely pull against each other and how badly; which pairs only look like they clash; and for each real tension, exactly what I would have to give up and what giving it up costs me — specifically whether the loss is local, so that I surrender some claim in (6) and keep the chapter, or structural, so that the argument of the chapter itself goes. Order the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equals; I would rather know which one is fatal than have seven equal worries. Include the hypothetical recoded organism — a lineage in which one codon has been reassigned to a non-standard amino acid, so that the same string has two different readings under two mechanisms — if it is diagnostic for where my view really sits. Then name the single revision that would clean up the most and say what it commits me to. If one of the seven is not merely in tension but gets the logic or the biology wrong, say which and say why.
