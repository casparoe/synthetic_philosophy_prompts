Its been bugging me for a week, so here goes. I'm in a slow reading group going through Gödel, Escher, Bach and we just did the Ant Fugue (I think that's the right dialogue — the one where a character converses with an ant colony called Aunt Hillary, and where the point, as I remember it, is roughly that no individual ant has any idea what the colony is up to, that the colony's thoughts take ages by ant standards, and that she is still a perfectly good candidate 'I'). The fight I keep having afterwards is with my friend Priya, who works on multi-agent LLM systems. She says the standard analogy does all the work for her: ants are to neurons as a colony is to a brain, and in the superorganism version ants are to cells as a colony is to an organism. So, she argues, a swarm of fairly dumb agent instances coordinating through a shared document or message board could have a mind of its own in exactly the same sense Aunt Hillary does — and if that's right, then the swarm, not the user or the operator or the individual agent, is the thing we should be asking moral-patient questions about.

What I want is an audit of the analogy rather than another round of us talking past each other. Could you lay out the correspondence explicitly, a table is fine, and do it for both mappings she slides between: ants : neurons :: colony : brain, and ants : cells :: colony : organism. Say what happens to the queen, to pheromone trails and nest architecture, to scouts and foragers, to the fact that ants die and are replaced while the colony goes on, and to damage — losing a hundred ants vs. losing a hundred neurons. Then the disanalogies, and please separate the ones that actually bear on her conclusion from the ones that are just decorative: my own list is that ant signalling is slow, broadcast, chemical and routed through the environment rather than point-to-point and fast, so a lot of the colony's memory sits outside any ant at all (in the trail network and the nest); that a colony contains its own reproduction and has real internal conflicts of interest, e.g. between queen and workers over how much to invest in males versus females, which nothing in a brain corresponds to; and that Aunt Hillary's ends are her own in a way the swarm's aren't, since the swarm's goals come from whoever wrote the prompt or the reward. One more thing I can't get out of my head and that no one in the group had an answer to: a colony starts as a single mated queen alone, then two ants, then ten, then fifty thousand. If the mind is at colony level, when does it appear, and does the analogy give any usable answer or does it just go quiet there?

The two things I care about at the end. First: does the conclusion survive? Does Aunt Hillary give Priya any reason at all to treat a swarm of LLM instances as a candidate subject, or is the analogy carrying the whole argument — and if it is, which single disanalogy does the most damage? Second: if brain/colony is the wrong target, what's better? I've been toying with the nest as a tape and the ants as the read-write head, i.e. stigmergy as external memory, so that a colony is closer to a distributed computer or to an economy with pheromone gradients as prices than to a brain; I've also heard immune system and slime mold thrown around. For each candidate, tell me where it locates the 'I', whether it helps or hurts her case, and what feature of the source it quietly imports that the swarm lacks.
