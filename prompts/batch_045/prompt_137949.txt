We've got a housing co-op with a written charter, and two of its rules keep throwing up the same loop — we've had to table the same three plans at four meetings in a row now. Could you look at the setup and tell me what's actually going wrong? Please answer in American English, and keep it concrete, because I'm going to read your answer out loud at the house meeting.

Rule 1, "sovereignty": for a short list of named issues, one member has the call, and whichever way they go on a comparison, the house goes that way. The charter defines this thinly — the call only binds when the two plans being compared are identical except on that member's issue. Priya has the call on whether there's a TV in the living room; Malik has the call on whether the back patio is a smoking area.

Rule 2, "no-objection": if every single member prefers plan A to plan B, the house ranks A above B. This one isn't thin — it applies to any pair of plans, including plans that differ on several issues at once.

The three plans:
- X: TV in the living room, patio smoke-free
- Y: no TV, patio smoking
- Z: no TV, patio smoke-free

Priya hates the TV but can't stand smoke even more, so she ranks Z > X > Y. Malik smokes, but he'd honestly rather have the TV than the smoking patio, so he ranks X > Y > Z. Everyone else lands somewhere in between but agrees that X beats Y.

Sovereignty gives us Z > X (Priya's call on the TV) and Y > Z (Malik's call on the patio), and no-objection gives us X > Y. Round and round.

One housemate says its just Sen's liberal paradox, which he read proves that majority rule is broken, so we should switch to score voting or ranked choice, since those always hand you a complete ranking with no cycles. Another says keep everything as is and just make sovereignty conditional: your call stands unless the house unanimously prefers some other plan.

Which of those two fixes actually gets rid of the loop, and what does each one cost us? And separately: would it help to make the two rules symmetric in scope — either let members exercise their call on plans that differ on other people's issues too, or restrict no-objection to one-issue-at-a-time comparisions? The charter is up for revision in March and I'd like to show up with something better than "let's just vote again."
