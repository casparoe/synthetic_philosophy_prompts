I am designing the scoring layer for a forecasting tournament between automated reasoners that do not share a representational vocabulary, and I want the rule to be defensible from inside accuracy-first epistemology: the only epistemic value is the accuracy of credal states, agents are graded by a scoring rule applied to the credences they issue, and the usual payoffs of that program — coherence, updating by conditionalization, responsiveness to evidence and to peers — are supposed to fall out of expected-accuracy reasoning rather than be bolted on.

The complication I cannot get past is granularity. The standard machinery assumes one fixed algebra of propositions shared by every agent in the comparison. My reasoners differ in which distinctions they can draw: one forecasts over a coarse partition, another splits the same possibilities into subtypes, and a third only learns to draw the finer distinction halfway through the tournament. Standard scoring rules are defined relative to a partition and are not automatically comparable across partitions, so "who was more accurate" may depend on who was allowed to be vague.

Setup. Omega is a finite set of worlds. An agent's vocabulary is a partition A of Omega (its cells are the finest propositions it can express); its credence function c assigns non-negative numbers summing to one across the cells of A. The actual world is w, and the cell of A containing w is A_w. Lower scores are better. A measure is proper on a fixed vocabulary if, holding the vocabulary fixed, expected score is minimized by reporting your actual credences. Where an expectation is needed, take it with respect to the agent's own credences, extended to the finest partition {{omega} : omega in Omega} in whatever way the measure in question extends them. "Uninformed refinement" means replacing a cell by several sub-cells and splitting its credence equally among them; "informed refinement" means splitting it in proportion to the true chances.

The five candidate measures:

1. Atom-log (AL). AL(c, w) = -log c(A_w). Zero for the maximally coarse vocabulary {Omega}, and never negative.
2. Uniform-extension Brier (UEB). Extend c to the finest partition by giving each world omega the mass c(A_omega)/|A_omega|; then score the extension with the Brier (squared-error) score on the finest partition.
3. Background-extension Brier (BXB). As in UEB, but the extension within a cell is proportional to a background measure mu fixed by the tournament designer (UEM is the special case with mu uniform).
4. Common-coarsening Brier (CCB). Given a group of agents, take the meet of their vocabularies — the finest partition coarser than all of them — collapse each agent's credences onto it, and score everyone with the Brier score there. Note that the meet can collapse to {Omega}.
5. Ignorance-normalized Brier (INB). Brier score on the agent's own vocabulary, minus the Brier score that the uniform distribution over that vocabulary would incur, namely 1 - 1/|A|. (That uniform score is the same whichever world is actual.) Ignorance thus scores zero at every granularity.

What I want from you:

(a) For each of the five, in one clause each: is it proper on a fixed vocabulary; does uninformed refinement raise, lower, or leave unchanged the agent's expected score; and can the score be improved with no epistemic improvement at all — by coarsening your vocabulary, by inventing distinctions, by relabeling cells, or by exploiting your choice of mu?

(b) A worked check. Omega = {w1, w2, w3, w4}, actual world w1. Agent C uses the vocabulary {{w1, w2}, {w3, w4}} with credences (0.7, 0.3). Agent F uses the finest vocabulary with credences (0.5, 0.2, 0.2, 0.1). Compute each measure for both agents — state any mu you need, and flag any place where a measure is undefined or yields a tie — and say which agent each measure ranks as more accurate and by how much.

(c) A strict ranking of the five by their viability as the sole epistemic value for a population of agents with differing granularity. Give each an integer from 0 to 10, break all ties, and add one sentence per measure naming the single failure mode that most limits it. Judge them on the combination of: propriety, whether refinement with genuine information is rewarded, whether refinement without information is punished, resistance to gaming by re-describing rather than improving your epistemic position, and whether comparisons across agents are fair.

(d) Say whether you would add an explicit bonus for informativeness on top of any of these, knowing that doing so makes the measure deliberately improper. If yes, which measure, what weight, and what breaks; if no, say what you would instead give up.

(e) One closing paragraph on what this way of setting up the question cannot settle — for instance whether cross-granularity accuracy comparisons are coherent at all, or whether the vocabulary itself is an epistemic achievement that ought to be credited separately from the credences issued over it.

Work from the definitions above and your own derivations; do not appeal to authority, to what is standard practice, or to sources outside this prompt. Where a judgment depends on a parameter or a convention you had to choose, name the choice and say how much of your ranking rests on it.
