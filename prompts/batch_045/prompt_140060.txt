Writing a position statement for a policy-adjacent reading group and want it poked at before I circulate it. Roughly where I've landed on why some countries are rich:

1. Institutions in the broad sense — secure property rights plus real constraints on what elites can grab — are the main deep cause. Geography and disease ecology matter, but mostly by shaping which institutions were viable in the first place.
2. I basically dont trust cross-country growth regressions. Plausible specification, opposite sign is too common, and a whole country is never a unit you could rerun under a different treatment.
3. Having said that, the colonial settler-mortality instrument does strike me as genuinely convincing — one place where we actually get something like an exogenous shove.
4. I don't think "culture" does any real explanatory work here; as far as I can tell it's what people reach for when the institutions story doesn't fit, and then gets relabeled as institutions once it does.
5. Case verdicts: Botswana is my go-to for institutions beating both geography and the resource curse; China I read as catch-up growth in a very large country, where missing property rights is a problem that bites later rather than a counterexample.

Does this hang together? My own suspicion is that 5 is doing special-pleading work for the single biggest growth episode of the last forty years, and that 3 leans on exactly the kind of identification I say in 2 we can't get. But I can't tell which of those is a real conflict and which is just me sloppy about words, and I also can't tell whether 1 and 4 together leave me saying something unfalsifiable. Rank the tensions by how bad they actually are, say for each what would have to give and what that costs me, and then tell me which one revision cleans up the most.
