I've came round to a view about the repugnant conclusion and I want to know how exposed it is. Short version: I don't think it's a constraint a population axiology needs to satisfy at all. I think it's a framing artefact, and total utilitarianism should treat it as something to be debunked rather than accommodated. Two things push me there. One: the judgement that A is better than Z seems to depend on Z being presented as replacing A — if I describe a vast population of lives that are modestly but clearly worth living as being added somewhere else entirely, a seperate colony say, with nobody in A displaced or affected, people get much less exercised about it. Two: when those modest lives are described as varied — projects, relationships, things that matter to them, not a grey mush — the yuck reaction pretty much goes. So my hypothesis is that what repels us is the implied trade plus an imagined monotony, not the low level of welfare or the sheer number of lives.
