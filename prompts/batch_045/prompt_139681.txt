I'm writing a short paper for a seminar on formal systems and cognition, and before I present the critique I want to test it against the strongest available version of the escapist reading of Hofstadter's "jumping out of the system" rather than the version I can most easily knock down. Please play a committed escapist: a philosopher of logic and mind who holds that the distinction between working inside a formal system and reasoning about it marks something real, irreducible, and not capturable by adding more rules to the system — not a mysticism about minds, but a claim about the difference between a rule's being stated and its being applied. You have real resources and should use them: the statement/use distinction, arithmetization (Hofstadter himself maps the MIU system into arithmetic by letting M, I and U go to 3, 1 and 0, so that the system and its rules reappear as arithmetical facts), Lewis Carroll's tortoise regress about rules for applying rules, Tarski-style limits on a system's containing its own semantics. Stay in character until I say "stop"; reply in the first person, about two paragraphs, conversational rather than lecture-like; concede small points freely but never the central one; and never answer "it's intuitive" or "that's just what understanding is" and leave it there — if you appeal to the character of the cognitive act, say what work that appeal does.

The case I want to start from is the MU puzzle. From the axiom MI, with the rules xI → xIU, Mx → Mxx, xIIIy → xUy and xUUy → xy, the string MU cannot be derived, because the number of I's in any derived string is never divisible by three (doubling preserves non-divisibility, and so does subtracting three), while MU has zero I's. Hofstadter's moral is that seeing this requires stepping outside: the rules produce strings, and never produce the word "not". Now consider a system I will call MJU. Its alphabet adds one symbol, N; rules 1–4 are unchanged but apply only to strings over {M, I, U}; and there is one further rule, Rule J: for any string S over {M, I, U}, if S is not derivable in MJU itself, you may write down NS, read as "S is not derivable here". Because nothing containing an N can feed back into rules 1–4, the {M, I, U}-theorems of MJU are exactly those of MIU, so the definition is not circular. Rule J is effective — theoremhood in MIU is decidable (the usual criterion being that a string is derivable just in case it begins with a single M, contains nothing but I's and U's besides, and has an I-count that is not a multiple of three) — and it is sound in the sense that it never lets you derive NS for an S that is itself derivable. But now N MU, and infinitely many other such strings, are theorems of MJU: the system states, within itself, exactly the kind of thing Hofstadter said you had to go outside to see. (N N MU is not a theorem, since N MU is one.)

So here is my opening move, and I want an answer to it, not a restatement of your position. Either MJU has internalized the jump — in which case the inside/outside line was never about where the conclusion sits, and your moral needs to be restated — or you will say that applying Rule J still required me to know, from outside, that MU is underivable, so that the jump has merely relocated from the conclusion to the licence for taking a step. If you take the second route, I have two follow-ups and I want you to take whichever hurts more: (a) I can add Rule J2, licensing those applications of J whose side condition has itself been derived in the system, then J3, and so on — so if the regress bottoms out anywhere, the level where it stops is exactly as internalized as N MU was supposed to be impossible to make; and (b) if it never bottoms out, then "jumping out" looks like a label for whatever reasoning we have not yet folded into the rulebook, a promissory note secured against our present ignorance rather than a standpoint we occupy. I can also guess one move you may want — that the strings mean nothing until we interpret them, so the apparent self-reference is ours and not the system's — and I would ask you to make it only if you can also say why it does not collapse into the trivial claim that no formal system ever means anything. Which is it: is MJU a counterexample to escapism, or is escapism a claim about acts rather than theorems, and if so what does that claim predict that my regress does not already give me?
