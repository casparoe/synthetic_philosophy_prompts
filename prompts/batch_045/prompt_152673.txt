I'm toying with using the eurozone's no-bailout clause as my main example in a lecture on Ulysses contracts and constitutional precommitment, so do the work for me. Set-up, in case it helps: the EU treaties contain a clause saying neither the Union nor any member state will assume another state's commitments, alongside a ban on central banks financing governments directly, and the standard story is that this is a Ulysses contract — governments who can't credibly promise fiscal discipline on their own lash themselves to the mast, markets lend cheaper, voters can't be bought with borrowed money. Then 2010-2012: the Greek loan facility, the EFSF, the ESM, bond buying by the ECB, and a lot of creative lawyering whose whole point was to keep the text intact while doing the thing it forbade (lending to a state isn't assuming its commitments, and so on).

What I want runs both directions. First, the episode through the theory: who is actually the Ulysses and who are the sirens — debtor states binding themselves, creditor states binding their own future generosity, or the Union binding itself against becoming a fiscal union? And what is the temptation supposed to be, given that it doesn't look much like akrasia and looks more like panic, contagion and compassion under pressure? Does the Ulysses framing even have vocabulary for that, or does it quietly misdescribe the case by assimilating it to weakness of will? Then the case against the theory. Two things nag me. One: the clause wasn't really honored or broken, it was complied with textually while being violated substantively, and every workaround was engineered for exactly that; is that a failure mode the precommitment literature should have predicted, and does it show legal entrenchment is weaker than the mast metaphor suggests? Two: euro-area sovereign yields converged almost to German levels through the 2000s, which suggests nobody ever expected the clause to hold, so if the point of a Ulysses contract is to change what others expect of you, this one may have failed at its own job well before 2010. Is that a counterexample to the idea, or just a badly built precommitment — wrong agent, no one with standing to police it, no cost to reinterpreting it?

Be disciplined about the mapping: say which features the framing genuinely explains, which it distorts, and where it is simply silent. Don't give me reading lists.
