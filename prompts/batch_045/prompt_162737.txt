Here is the position I hold, in three parts. Call it the connection-first package.

(1) Bayesian conditioning is the right formal model of rational belief revision, and the Bayesian apparatus is also the right setting for an account of evidential support.

(2) The old-evidence problem — the datum E is already known, so its probability is one, so conditioning on it cannot raise the probability of a new theory T that entails it, yet historically such episodes are treated as confirmations — is a real problem, but only for one of those two jobs. It is a problem for a two-place, history-independent explication of confirmation. It is not a problem for the modeling of how an inquirer's credences ought to move, because the inquirer genuinely does learn something.

(3) Where I do want an explication of support, the repair is to condition not on E but on the newly recognized connection between T and E. What an agent learns when a new theory is articulated is that the theory delivers the datum; that proposition really was news to her, and conditioning on it does the confirmatory work. Paradigm case: the anomalous advance of Mercury's perihelion had been on the books for decades before the general theory of relativity, and the theory's recovery of it was treated as one of its strongest supports. Second case, which I take to be harder: the sleeping dataset — a number sitting in a published table for years that nobody had connected to anything, until a theorist notices that her model predicts it.

I want a map of this package's exposure: what it risks, where it is insulated, and what would have to be true for it to be false. Please cover the following, and add what I have missed.

The worries I want engaged, at minimum:

A. Logical omniscience and the size of the bump. A logically competent agent who understands T already knows that T entails E, so the connection proposition has probability one as well and the repair buys nothing. And where the repair is patched by having the agent learn only some bounded chunk of the consequences of T, the resulting boost depends on how finely the space of candidate connection propositions is partitioned, looks arbitrary, and is typically far too small to account for episodes in which a theory goes from obscurity to near-universal acceptance on the strength of one old datum.

B. The novelty mill. If what rescues confirmation is that the agent did not antecedently know the connection, or had forgotten the datum, then confirmation can be manufactured: an agent who arranges to forget E and then re-encounters it, or who cycles through such re-encounters, can drive her credence in any theory that entails the datum arbitrarily high, with no new information entering at any step. Does the package have a principled stop here, and if the stop is that manufactured confirmation is irrational, what is the argument, given that the credal trajectory is indistinguishable from the honest one? If the answer is that support is agent-relative, what becomes of the use of confirmation theory to reconstruct what an episode did for a scientific community?

C. Graded availability. Evidence is not simply old or new; it comes in degrees of cognitive availability — forgotten, recorded but unconsulted, consulted but unconnected, expected, predicted. Which agent does the theory model, the bounded one or the idealized one? If the bounded one, then support depends on contingent facts about who has read what, two scientists sharing a corpus get different degrees of confirmation, and the less well-read scientist gets the larger boost. Is that a genuine feature of the account — it would explain why noticing an old datum in the literature is evidentially valuable — or is it a reductio?

D. Prior-embedded evidence and the connection glut. In the historical cases the datum typically helped shape the space of candidate theories and the priors over it, so conditioning on the connection looks like double counting what the prior already absorbed. Relatedly, arbitrarily many mild variants of T also entail E, so learning that T entails E does not discriminate T from its neighbors, and the boost has to be spread over a partition whose granularity is a free parameter.

E. The novelty asymmetry. Scientists, statisticians, and laypeople all treat a genuine prediction as more impressive than an accommodation, and there is an independent rationale in the overfitting literature: a model tuned to data already in hand can fit it without tracking anything. Can the package represent that asymmetry at all? If it can only by making support depend on each agent's personal learning order, it pays for the asymmetry by surrendering the objectivity of support. If it cannot, then explanationist, hypothetico-deductive, and error-statistical severity-style rivals have a datum my package cannot describe. Which of those two costs is worse, and does the asymmetry itself survive scrutiny?

F. Empirical and historical defeaters. What observation would actually count? For instance: that in the celebrated cases the datum functioned as rhetoric or legitimation rather than evidence, and acceptance tracked something else — elimination of an ad hoc auxiliary, unification, contrastive fit against the rival; or experimental findings that agents who learn a theory entails an already-known datum show no credence increase at all, or show one only when the datum is framed as a prediction; or that communities systematically discount accommodations in precisely the cases where my account says they should not.

G. Unfalsifiability. With an unconstrained prior over a space of theories and a free choice of which connection proposition the agent learned, it looks as though any historical episode whatsoever can be reconstructed as rational Bayesian updating, which would make the package a language rather than a theory. Is that fair? Is it fatal, or is it acceptable because Bayesianism is a norm of coherence rather than a descriptive theory of science? And if it is only a coherence norm, notice the trap: either the old-evidence problem never had a grip on the part of the package I actually care about, in which case my (2) dissolves the problem rather than solving it, or it did have a grip, in which case the retreat in (2) is the weak point and not the strength I have been treating it as.

Finally: if forced to give ground, my preferred order of retreat is to keep (1), then (2), and to surrender (3) first. Tell me whether that ordering is coherent, what surrendering (3) actually costs me — do I end up with no account of support at all, or with a rival's account wearing borrowed clothes — and at the end give me one sentence stating what would make you abandon the package entirely.
