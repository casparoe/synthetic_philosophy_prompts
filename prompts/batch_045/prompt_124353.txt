Hey — I volunteer with a small animal-welfare nonprofit (mostly shrimp and insect stuff), and one of our donors has started asking in earnest whether we ought to have a position on AI systems possibly being moral patients, so I got voluntold into making a one-page worksheet for the other volunteers. Most of them are engineers and vets with basically zero philosophy background, so I definately don't want it to be a menu of -isms they pick between by label.

What I'd like is a decision tree instead: start from something everyone in the room can already accept — something like "this system's behavior is produced by something functionally similar to a nervous system" — and lead to a named position using roughly 6–8 branch questions, where every branch is a concrete case someone can have an intuition about without knowing any jargon. The cases I want in there: (1) a giant lookup table that reproduces a creature's behavior exactly but whose internal causal story is boring; (2) a shrimp's brain replaced chip by chip over an hour; (3) a model trained only to emit text that sounds like a pain report, which now reliably does — is that a case of us reading experience in, or of us discovering it; (4) a simulation run 10,000x speed, where a whole "lifetime" passes in a few minutes of wall clock; (5) a system that's only partly and intermittently implemented, with chunks of its processing shared between two overlapping "creatures."

Leaves should include at least: a substrate-independent computational view, a biology-only view, a view where it turns on how well the system's internal states explain its behavior, a degrees-or-thresholds view where moral weight scales with some quantity, and an error-theory-ish "there's no fact of the matter" leaf. Where a combination of answers is a spot nobody actually occupies, mark it empty and say in one line why it's unattractive or incoherent. And please flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really separate — I'd rather have that printed on the page than pretend the sorting is clean.
