Quick reminder of the view I mean, so we're on the same page: whether a subject counts as knowing p partly depends on what's at stake for them practically, so someone can know on low stakes and fail to know on high stakes with no change in their evidence. My thought is that the film is unusually good for this because the deliberation is basically a machine for changing what's at stake for twelve people at once — the guy who wants to make the ball game, the angry father, the quiet ones who slowly come to feel a man's life is on the line — and their verdicts move without much new evidence arriving.

The wrinkle I want to press, which I don't think the encroachment literature (Stanley, Fantl and McGrath) really handles: jurors are supposed to apply stakes that aren't theirs. So if encroachment is right, the juror has to occupy an epistemic position that's sensitive to someone else's practical situation.
