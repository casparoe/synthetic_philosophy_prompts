I've drifted into holding a stronger view than I started with. Finks, masks and mimics — cases where the stimulus itself switches the disposition off, blocks the manifestation, or produces the manifestation in something that never had the disposition — don't just refute the naive conditional analysis ("disposed to M in C" = "would M if C"). They show that no counterfactual analysis can work at all: name any condition in the analysans and I can rig a mechanism to fire exactly when that condition holds and wreck the result. Same for the usual fix, where you analyse the disposition through some intrinsic basis property the object has to keep while the stimulus is applied — the rig just targets the keeping, or fires a moment before the stimulus arrives. So dispositions are irreducible powers, and these cases are discoveries about the world rather than artifacts of how we choose to regiment counterfactuals.

What would count against that? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and separately tell me which ones a powers theorist would actually concede as counting against them, rather than just what the counterfactual-anlysis crowd likes to shout. I keep going back and forth on two cases of my own. One: an intrinsic fink, where the interferer is one of the object's own parts — a bomb whose safety catch disarms the detonator at the instant the trigger is pulled, no sorcerer standing by. Two: a self-masking disposition, where manifesting switches the disposition off, like metal that forms an oxide layer and stops corroding. If those really differ in kind from the standard set, does the difference help me or hurt me?

The line I find hardest to shake is that the powers view collapses back into what it replaced, since the only grip we seem to have on which power a thing has is what it would do under stimulation — so maybe the two views don't differ after all. Is that the strongest objection, or just the one that gets under my skin? And be honest about whether my view has been insulated into unfalsifiability by the move of saying that any apparent failure just means the thing didn't really have the disposition — and whether that insulation is actually a problem for a view of this kind.
