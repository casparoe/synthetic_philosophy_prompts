Setup you can assume: two parties, A and B, dividing a pile of money or money plus one asset. Both are risk-neutral over money across the range that matters, and I want payoffs in dollars — no "let's say A's utility is the square root of the money" tricks, since I don't want the curvature of the frontier smuggled in through utility curvature. The disagreement point has to be non-zero and realistic (what each actually walks away with if talks collapse — a fallback sale, a likely court award, a deadweight loss) and stated explicitly. And the feasible set should be convex as it stands: every point on the frontier should be an agreement they could really sign, with no coin flips or lotteries used to paper over a dent in the set.

Definitions I'll be grading against: Nash's solution maximises the product of the two gains over the disagreement point. The Kalai–Smorodinsky solution takes each player's ideal payoff (the most they could get while the other still gets at least their disagreement payoff), draws the straight line from the disagreement point to that ideal point, and picks the furthest feasible point along it — same thing as equalising the ratio of actual gain to ideal gain at the highest level the set allows.

What I want is a case where the two disagree, and disagree in the opposite direction from every example I can find. Every worked example I've seen has KS as the one that rewards whoever has more at stake — bigger best case, bigger share — which is also why people complain that you can inflate your outcome under KS by getting a pie-in-the-sky option onto the menu. I want the reverse: the player with the larger ideal gain, in dollars, should do strictly better under Nash than under KS, and the other player strictly better under KS than under Nash. But I don't want a gerrymandered frontier — whatever kink or curvature does the work has to come from a real institutional feature you can name in one sentence (a financing cap, a penalty clause, a matching contribution that only unlocks above a threshold, a fire-sale discount on part of the pie, whatever). For what it's worth, I tried building it around a buyout where the buyer can raise $40k cheaply and every dollar above that has to come from flogging equipment at fifty cents on the dollar, but with my numbers KS still handed more to the side with more at stake and I couldn't see which way to move the threshold — use that if it helps, or throw it out.

- tell me what happens if you delete the institutional feature entirely — my hunch is that with a single straight frontier the two solutions coincide exactly however lopsided the stakes are, but check that rather than taking my word for it;
- tell me which geometric feature is really driving the gap. I suspect it's where the "extra" surplus sits relative to the line from the disagreement point to the ideal point, but say if I'm wrong;
