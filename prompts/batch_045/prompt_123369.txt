I TA for a formal epistemology course and I'm marking a short-answer question on a variant of the Judy Benjamin problem. I've got my own read but I'd rather not ankor on it before I see an independent one. Here's the question, then one student's answer verbatim. Give it a mark out of 20, and give me point-by-point comments I could paste into the feedback box: which claims are right, which are wrong, what the correct version of the wrong bits looks like, and roughly how many marks each mistake should cost.

QUESTION (25 minutes)

Judy is dropped by parachute into a region split into four cells: two in Red (enemy) territory and two in Blue, two patrolled by Headquarters Company and two by the Second Company. Her prior:

            HQ      Second
Blue        0.10    0.20
Red         0.30    0.40

Her radio crackles: "If you are in Red territory, then you are in Second Company's area." Assume she takes the message to be completely reliable.

(a) State precisely what constraint this puts on her posterior.
(b) Let H = Judy is in HQ Company's area. Compute P*(H) under two rules.
   Rule A (shift within the antecedent): mass that the constraint rules out stays inside the antecedent of the conditional — the mass in Red & HQ moves to Red & Second, and the Blue cells are left untouched.
   Rule B: treat the message as the material conditional (Red ⊃ Second), i.e. rule out Red & HQ and renormalize over what's left. Equivalently: choose the posterior Q minimizing relative entropy, the sum over cells of Q(x) log(Q(x)/P(x)), subject to the constraint. Useful fact: when the constraint just says that some cell has probability zero, that minimizer is the prior renormalized over the cells the constraint leaves open.
(c) In one short paragraph, say exactly what the two rules disagree about — name the quantity that differs — and say which rule you think is right and why.

STUDENT ANSWER

Let H = HQ area, R = Red territory. Before the message: P(R) = 0.7, P(H) = 0.4, P(H|R) = 3/7, P(H|B) = 1/3.

(a) The message rules out the cell Red & HQ, so the constraint is P*(H|R) = 0. That is a constraint on a conditional probability, not on which cell she is in: it does not say she isn't in Red territory, only that if she is, she's in Second Company's area.

(b) Rule A: the 0.30 in Red & HQ moves to Red & Second, giving (Blue&HQ, Blue&Second, Red&HQ, Red&Second) = (0.10, 0.20, 0, 0.70). So P*(H) = 0.10 and P*(R) = 0.70, unchanged. That last part seems right: a message that tells her nothing about which army's territory she is in shouldn't change how confident she is that she's in Red territory.

Rule B: minimizing relative entropy just means finding the smallest change to the prior that satisfies the constraint, and rule A's distribution is already the smallest such change, since it moves only the mass that has to move and leaves every other cell exactly where it was. Because the log is concave, the freed 0.30 goes where the prior was already heaviest, i.e. into Red & Second. So rule B returns the same numbers as rule A: (0.10, 0.20, 0, 0.70) and P*(H) = 0.10.

(c) The two rules agree on both conditional probabilities. On each, P*(H|R) = 0, and on each P*(H|B) = 1/3, since neither rule touches the ratio inside Blue territory. Since P*(H) = P*(H|R)P*(R) + P*(H|B)P*(B), and the rules agree on both conditionals, they must agree on P*(H) = 0.10. So the disagreement people make a fuss about here is largely verbal: it comes from authors applying the two rules to different constraints. Really the example just shows how much these problems depend on the prior.
