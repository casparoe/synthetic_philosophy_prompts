I am a retired physical chemist who has recently begun reading philosophy of science, and I now attend a small seminar on Popper’s views on Darwinism. In that seminar we repeatedly meet the line — Darwinism is not a testable scientific theory but a metaphysical research programme — and it is typically deployed to close off discussion rather than to open it.

At our last meeting I presented a toy model of my own devising, which I called the neutral-ratchet with periodic bloom. In this model, a haploid population accumulates slightly deleterious mutations at a constant rate, so that in the absence of selection the mean lineage slowly ratchets downward; but every fifty generations the environment undergoes a brief bloom, during which individuals carrying a certain allele at one locus are, let us say, ten percent more likely to survive the accompanying bottleneck. All other heritable variation in the model is neutral, and there is a systematic mutational bias that produces the allele more often than its alternative. My question was whether the claim that the observed rise in that allele’s frequency across several bloom-bottleneck cycles is attributable to selection at that locus, rather than to drift together with the mutational bias, is a testable empirical claim about the population’s history, or whether it is the sort of claim that the slogan shows to be empty of empirical content.

A more philosophically experienced member of the group replied simply by invoking the line above and saying that my question therefore had no testable content, because Darwinism is not a testable scientific theory but a metaphysical research programme. I am certain that something is wrong with this use of the line, but I cannot say precisely what.

I would be grateful for a disambiguation of the slogan. What are the different precise claims it could be making — from platitudes to substantive and defensible theses to claims that are false — about the logical status of Darwinism, the testability of particular selection hypotheses, the role of natural selection as a framework for testable theories, and the bearing of all this on my toy model? Please lay the candidate readings out as a numbered list. For each reading, state in precise terms what it claims, say whether it is trivial, substantively defensible, or false, and say whether it would actually support the objector’s conclusion that my question about the neutral-ratchet with periodic bloom has no empirical content. I do not need an account of who held the view when; I want a conceptual map that I can take back to the seminar and use to work out which reading, if any, the objector was implicitly relying on.
