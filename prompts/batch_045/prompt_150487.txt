I'm writing a seminar paper (roughly 2,500 words) on the metaphysics of persistence, and I would like to build it around a novel rather than around one more variation on the ship of Theseus. I have been re-reading Wilde's The Picture of Dorian Gray, and what keeps striking me is that its premise is an inversion of the ordinary case: normally the person is the thing that visibly accumulates the marks of a misspent life while the painted image stays fixed, and in Wilde's story it is the other way around. The wish Dorian makes in Basil Hallward's studio — that he might remain what he is while the canvas grows old and bears the record instead — seems to me to prise apart two things that theories of persistence normally treat as fused: whatever it is that makes someone the same person across time, and the properties a life lays down as it goes. I would like a reading that takes both the novel and the endurantism/perdurantism dispute seriously, and tells me whether Wilde is dramatizing something the theories miss, or whether I am reading a metaphysical distinction into what is really only a plot device.

The questions I most want help with are these. First, on the worm-theoretic version of perdurantism, Dorian would be the aggregate of his temporal stages, and every one of those stages is qualitatively the same beautiful young man — a degenerate worm whose parts never vary — while the canvas is a numerically different object that does all the varying. So which thing is Dorian? Is the picture, in any defensible sense, the repository of his temporal parts, or is it merely another persisting object alongside him? And if the stage theorist is right that we are instantaneous stages and that persistence is really a matter of counterpart or continuity relations, does Dorian's bargain stop being metaphysically alarming altogether? Second, there is the problem of temporary intrinsics: the endurantist who holds that a thing is wholly present at each moment of its existence owes an account of how one and the same thing can have incompatible intrinsic properties at different times, and the standard move is to relativize property-holding to times. Wilde appears to offer a second, fantastic option, on which the later property is simply borne by a different object entirely. Does the novel actually dramatize a live answer to that problem, or does it quietly change the subject from persistence to appearance? Third, what should I do with the ending, in which Dorian stabs the canvas and the change transfers back — the withered corpse on the floor, the portrait restored to its first beauty? Is Wilde vindicating the perdurantist thought that a person just is the whole temporally extended thing, corrupted later parts included, and that the unmarked body was never the person to begin with? Or is the ending only a restoration of moral order that takes no position at all on what persistence consists in?

I also need to meet an objection rather than dodge it. My seminar leader says the whole project is a category mistake: persistence concerns what it is for something to remain numerically the same through change, whereas Wilde's bargain only relocates visible and physical effects, since Dorian's memory, remorse and self-knowledge all stay with the body, and theories that tie personal identity to psychological continuity would locate the person with the body regardless of what the paint does. Please take that seriously and tell me whether the reading survives it and, if so, what is left of it once it does. Finally, point me to two or three scenes I could anchor the argument in — I have in mind the locked attic room where the picture is kept, Basil's murder, and Dorian's last confrontation with the canvas — and give me, in closing, an honest statement of what a reading of this novel could contribute to the endurantism/perdurantism debate that the arguments in the literature do not: whether, for instance, it gives us a grip on why people find the endurantist picture of themselves so attractive, and where the novel resists being read as metaphysics at all.
