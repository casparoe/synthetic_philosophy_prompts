Our reading group has been going in circles on levelling down for about two years and I want a diagnosis, not a survey. I've read the surveys; they all end by saying both sides have resources and nothing gets decided.

Here's where we are. Prioritarian says: all that matters is weighted well-being, with more weight on well-being changes the worse off the person is, and that's the whole story. So in the standard case — you can't lift anyone up, the only thing you can do is bring the better off down to the level of the worst off (the example everyone uses involves taking away something like sight rather than adding a benefit) — nothing good is achieved. The outcome isn't better in any respect, it's just worse for someone. Egalitarian says: no, it's better in one respect, because there's non-instrumental value in people being equal, it's just that the gain is small and gets outweighed. And then everyone deploys the same four moves and we stop: the egalitarian says the priority-weighted view is really an equality view wearing a lab coat, since it's motivated by the same cases and agrees in the two-person comparisons; the prioritarian says the levelling-down intuition is importing something else — unfairness in how the inequality arose, or envy, or the fact that no decent person would do it; someone says it's a gain in a respect and not a gain all things considered; someone says the whole example is rigged because it asks about betterness when what we wanted was a decision.

I've tried inventing variants to break the tie and they don't break it, which is itself part of what I want explained:

1. Intrapersonal levelling down. One person, two stretches of her life: a great first half and a miserable second half. You can't improve the second half, but you can make the first half worse, so that her life is more even. If levelling down is obviously pointless here, does that show the prioritarian's complaint is really about the separateness of persons and not about equality? Or does the egalitarian just bite the bullet and apply her view across time too?
2. The two islands. Two populations who will never meet, never trade, never know about each other, no common institutions, no attitudes between them. One is better off. Levelling the richer island down doesn't fix anything relational — no envy, no domination, no comparison anyone makes. Does the egalitarian still hear a gain, and if she says the gain shrinks, has she admitted the view was relational all along?
3. Tiny gap, enormous loss. The better off person is barely above the worse off one, and levelling costs him something huge. If there is a gain in equality here, how big is it? Nobody in my group has any idea how to scale it, and I suspect the reason the argument can't close is that there's no agreed unit for the thing the egalitarian says has value.

My working hypothesis is that the levelling-down objection isn't an argument at all — it's just the prioritarian's intuition restated in a case where her intuition is loudest — and that the real disagreement is structural: whether the betterness ordering over distributions has to be separable (built up person by person, additively) or whether there can be non-instrumental relational value, i.e. whether something like the Pigou–Dalton transfer principle can be basic. On top of that there's a second-order fight about what "better" is even for: choosing between options, or a free-standing axiological property, or something that might just be incomplete so that the question has no answer.

Is that right? Is it a verbal dispute about "better" or about "value", a difference about which cases count as data, rival bedrock intuitions, or a hidden formal question about separability and representation that nobody has bothered to state cleanly? Give me the diagnosis with reasons from what each side actually commits to, engage the three cases, and then tell me honestly what would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, a change of question — or whether I should expect this to never resolve and why. And if the answer is "bedrock all the way down", say what that means for whether the paper I'm planning is even worth writing.
