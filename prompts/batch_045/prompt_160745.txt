He uses it for two conclusions at once. First: the question whether the system is conscious, or understands anything, is settled by the fact that a competent interrogator can't tell it apart from a person. Second: once it passes, what's inside is simply irrelevant — whether it's a giant lookup table of canned continuations, a next-token predictor, or something with a genuinely integrated architecture makes no difference to whether it has a mind. I think both moves run faster than the slogan allows, but I can't get traction on exactly where.
