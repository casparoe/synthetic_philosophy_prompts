My brother-in-law is really into nudging — defaults, auto-enrollment, that whole thing — and we've been going back and forth about it for like a year. His argument is always the same shape: nobody's being forced, we're just helping people do what they already want to do but never get around to. I dont think that's wrong exactly, but I keep hitting this one wierd snag and I have no word for it.

The snag is that once a nudge has been running for a while, you cant check that claim anymore. Before my employer switched to auto-enrolling us in the retirement plan, maybe a third of people signed up on their own. A couple years in its more like nine out of ten. So now if you ask "do people actually want this?", the obvious answer is yes — but that yes is partly manufactured by the policy itself. And you cant just pull the default back out to find out, because the number doesn't go back to a third either, it lands somewhere in the middle, which doesn't tell you anything clean. Organ donation looks like the same shape to me: places with the opt-out default have way higher rates, and everyone reads that as people wanting to donate, but the default is what's doing the work and also what's blocking the question.

I poked around a bit and "adaptive preferences" sort of points at it, but as far as I can tell that's mostly about people stuck in bad situations talking themselves into being fine with them, and this isn't that — the thing being nudged toward might be genuinely great for people. It's not quite a self-fulfilling prophecy either, and it's not just "people are lazy about switching."

So: does this have a name? If there are a couple of near-misses out there, which one actually fits and how does my thing differ from each? I care about the carving, not the label — I want to be able to say what it is, what it isn't, and where its edge is. For example, is it the same phenomenon when a nudge flops and everyone reads the flop as "turns out people dont want this after all," or is that a different thing wearing the same clothes? If nothing established covers it, give me a term, a definition, and some boundary cases.
