Hey — I run a tiny newsletter/reading group that's basically modern Stoicism, and someone in it asked me to write down what I actually believe so everyone else can take shots at it. Here's the list, roughly in the order I believe it:

1. The dichotomy of control more or less as Epictetus gives it: the only thing that's up to me is my own judgment, what I assent to. Body, reputation, career, what other people do, how things actually turn out — none of that is up to me. And I think this is literally true, not just a useful thing to repeat to myself.
2. What I actually use in practice is the "trichotomy" version that's in all the modern books: some things are partly up to me, and the move is to relocate the goal onto my share (I can't win the race, I can run well; I can't get the job, I can prepare well). This is the bit that's genuinely helped my anxiety.
3. I'm a physicalist and I don't think the cosmos is ordered for the best or that things happen for a reason. But I still use "live according to nature" as a motto, meaning something like "act in line with how human beings actually are, socially and psychologically."
4. Only virtue is good. Health, money, relationships are preferred indifferents — genuinely worth selecting and working for, just not worth being disturbed about losing.
5. In the rest of my life I reason like a straight consequentialist about where to give money and time: what I care about is which outcomes actually happen, and I pick by expected impact. Honestly this is the part of my life I'm most confident is right.
6. Grief is appropriate, and someone genuinely untouched by a friend dying is missing something human rather than being advanced. Anger is never appropriate, including at real injustice.
7. I judge the practice by whether it lowers my anxiety, and I score specific episodes: I got passed over for a promotion last year and basically felt nothing, and I counted that as a win. My partner says that's just shutting down emotionally and I think shes wrong.
8. I'm a compatibilist about free will, so I don't see why determinism should make "up to me" meaningless, and whenever people bring up ancient Stoic fate I sort of tune out.

I've red the Enchiridion a bunch and I basically know the standard party line, so don't just hand me Stoic orthodoxy — I want an audit of *this* particular combination. Which pairs are actually in tension, ranked by how bad the tension is rather than all flagged equally? Which ones only look like they conflict and fall apart when you look closely? For each real one, what specifically would I have to give up, and what does giving it up cost me — and is there a single revision somewhere that cleans up more than one of them at once? If I'm helping myself to something in one of these that the others rule out, say which one has to go.
