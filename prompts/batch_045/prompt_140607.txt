Writing a short essay on dispositions for a metaphysics seminar and I think I've got the fink/mask distinction muddled. The way I've written it up: a fink is when the thing isn't actually disposed to do the characteristic thing but it would still do it if the trigger happened (my example: a tumbler that isn't fragile at all, but with a tiny charge packed inside that shatters it if you tap it), and a mask is when it does have the disposition but the trigger itself destroys the disposition before its able to show up. On that reading I can't see what the fuss is about: in the first case the conditional comes out true so nothing goes wrong, and in the second the disposition is gone by the time anything would happen, so again nothing goes wrong. Everyone says these cases are what break the simple counterfactual analysis of disposition ascriptions, so clearly I've misread something — but what?
