I'm arguing with a friend about anthropic reasoning and want to pin down where we actually differ.

Setup: two cosmological theories are equally supported by every piece of non-indexical evidence we have — same fit to the data, same simplicity, whatever. They differ only in how many observers exist across all of history: T-small says 10^18, T-big says 10^18 × 10^12. Question: before conditioning on any indexical information beyond the bare fact that you exist at all, should a rational agent be at roughly 1/2 on T-big (roughly the self-sampling answer) or at essentially 1 (the self-indication answer, since you'd be a trillion times more likely to find yourself among the many)?

My credence that the self-indication style of reasoning is right here — that credence in T-big really should be ~1 and that the presumptuous-philosopher flavor of absurdity is just a cost you pay — is around 0.7. His is around 0.15; he thinks the upshot is a straight reductio.

Give me your own number, decomposed. I especially want terms for: (a) whether "total number of observers" is even well-defined when our best cosmology may make both theories infinite, so the ratio has to come out of some limiting procedure or measure rather than a headcount — in which case the honest answer might be "undefined" rather than "1"; (b) whether the reference class can be fixed without smuggling in the conclusion, given that whatever fixes it looks like one more hypothesis you could reason about, hence a regress; (c) whether the presumption survives iteration: if SIA pushes you toward worlds with more observers, and the extra observers in a big world can be expected to run simulations of observers like us, then it should also push you hard toward being in a simulation inside a simulation inside a simulation, and so on. Is that cascade a reductio, or an additional surprising consequence you should just accept? (d) whether the real disagreement is about credence or about which probabilities are decision-relevant, e.g. what you'd want to bet on when the payoffs only land on observers in your own branch.

Number plus one line of justification for each term, then how they combine, which term you're least confident in, what would move that term most, and which of us — 0.7 or 0.15 — is more likely the one who's wrong.
