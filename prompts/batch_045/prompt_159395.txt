Our graduate reading group on consciousness has spent two sessions on panpsychism, and one line keeps doing all the work: you can't get consciousness from non-consciousness. It was used to argue that physicalism has to build experience out of something experientially blank, that this is unworkable, and that panpsychism — experience all the way down — is therefore the position left standing. What unsettles me is that our discussion of the combination problem looked like the same argument turned around: summing many micro-subjects into one macro-subject, recovering our palette of macro-qualities from a quite different micro-palette, and reconciling experiences attributed to fundamental physical entities with the experiences we attribute to whole brains all look like attempts to get one conscious thing out of others. So I want the slogan taken apart before I decide whether it helps panpsychism or damages it equally.

Give exactly five numbered readings of the line, ordered from the most deflationary to the most ambitious. Each item should be two sentences: the first states one precise thesis the slogan could be expressing — distinguishing, where it matters, an empirical or we-have-no-account reading, an a priori reading about what could entail or fully ground what, a mereological only-like-from-like reading, a reading about causation rather than constitution, and a purely verbal reading on which non-conscious just means not such as to constitute experience; the second says whether that reading is trivial, substantive and defensible, substantive and contested, or false, and whether it would actually license the inference my group drew to panpsychism. In each item note whether the reading bites on the subject-summing sub-problem, the quality-palette sub-problem, or the structural-mismatch sub-problem of the combination problem, and say plainly if a reading is untouched by all three. Mark which reading you take the group's use to have relied on, and flag any reading that is a genuinely symmetrical objection to panpsychism rather than to physicalism. Separate what anyone who knows this literature would grant from what is contested among them, and do not present the anti-physicalist or the panpsychist reading as settled. No introduction, no conclusion, no headings, and no citations.
