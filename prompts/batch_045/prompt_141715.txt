im an SRE, ~8 years of on-call, and I do most of my arguing in the vocabulary I use at work: replicas and leaders, Raft/Paxos, quorums, failover and split-brain, linearizability vs serializability vs eventual consistency, RPO/RTO, snapshot-and-restore, blue/green deploys, CRDTs, leader leases, idempotent retries. Philosophy-wise I read Parfit's teletransporter stuff years ago in a class and remember the shape of it but not the details, and I've been writing a dumb little internal blog series called "distributed systems for philosophers, philosophers for distributed systems" and i want the personal identity one to be actually good rather than a pile of lazy analogies.

So here's the job. Restate the reductionist side of the teletransporter debate in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can — the claim that identity isn't what matters in survival, that what matters is something like psychological continuity/connectedness with any cause, that it comes in degrees, that fission is as good as survival on the "what matters" question but can't be identity, and that the usual fix for that is a non-branching clause bolted onto the relation. Then do the same for the two things people push back with: the feeling that there must be some further fact of the matter beyond all the continuity-and-connectedness bookkeeping (the thing that's supposed to make "will *I* be the one who wakes up on Mars" a real question with a determinate answer), and the worry that the non-branching clause is completely ad hoc — that it makes what matters to me depend on whether some rival copy exists light-years away and does me no good or harm. I also want you to try my own variant, which I made up and haven't seen anywhere: call it the reconciler. Same scanner, but instead of destroying the source, it keeps the original running for an hour in degraded mode, ships a copy, then does a deterministic merge of the two divergent states back into a single node, with a tunable conflict-resolution weight (say 70/30, so the merged node keeps mostly the copy's memories but not entirely). Is the reconciler a case of survival at all on the reductionist story, does the non-branching clause even fire when the branches are transient and get healed, and does it matter whether the merge happened in one hour or one year or one second?
