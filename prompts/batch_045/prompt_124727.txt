His position, as near as I can state it: Bell's theorem doesn't show that nature is non-local. He's happy to drop (iii) — he's an Everettian — and he says that once you do, the step in the derivation where you write the joint probability as a sum over hidden variables of a product, P(A,B | x,y,λ) = P(A | x,λ) · P(B | y,λ), just doesn't go through, because there isn't a single A and a single B to take a joint probability of. So, he says: strictly local dynamics, everything branching, correlations come out fine, and the appearance of non-locality is an artefact of insisting on one world.
