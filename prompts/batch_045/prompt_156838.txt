Me and some friends got into the torture versus dust specks thing again on saturday — you know the one, one person horribly tortured for decades versus an astronomically large number of people each getting a speck in the eye for a second — and I walked away bothered by something that neither side in the argument ever actually addressed.

Here is what I keep circling around. Everyone argues as if the disagreement is about whether tiny bad things can add up to a big one. But the thing that seems to actually drive my own reaction isn't smallness, it's spread. Ten thousand pinpricks, one after another, all in my own skin, over the course of an afternoon: that sounds genuinely awful, and I would pay real money to skip it. Ten thousand people each getting exactly one pinprick: I shrug. Same number of pinpricks, same total if badness just sums, wildly different reaction — and inflating the number on the second case doesn't seem to move me much either. It also runs backwards: a heavy burden spread thinly over a million people feels better, not merely equal, which shouldn't happen if all you are doing is adding. So the pattern I think I am seeing is that bad stuff piles up inside a single life and only weakly or not at all across different lives — and that this, not the tininess of the specks, is what is really doing the work in the original argument. (I also vaguely half-remember something about patients rating a longer medical procedure as not worse than a shorter one if it ended more gently, which, if it is true, makes me doubt even the piling-up-within-one-life part.)

I've had people tell me this is just the separateness of persons thing, or something about interpersonal aggregation, but when I try to see how those words get used they seem to point at distributive justice, or at whether you can compare how badly off two different people are — and that isn't my worry. I am not worried about fairness between the pinprick people. I am worried about whether there is a total there to be fair with. So: is there an established name for the specific claim that the summing works within a subject and not, or not fully, between subjects? And I want the carving and not just the label — does it collapse into plain diminishing returns on badness, or is it a different animal? Does it make a difference whether it is one person over a very long time versus many people at once, or whether the person forgets each pinprick instantly, or whether it is a creature that is arguably several minds and arguably one? And the part I actually care about most: where people have discussed this, is the verdict anywhere that it is *defensible*, or is it mostly treated as a glitch in human intuition that we should train ourselves out of? I'm not looking for reading lists or citations, I just want to know whether the joint I think I'm seeing is a real one or whether I've definately mashed two different things together.
