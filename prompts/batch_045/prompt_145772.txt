I'm writing a short paper for a reading group on the "could a book be conscious" question — the setup in the dialogue from Hofstadter and Dennett's 1981 collection The Mind's I, where Einstein's brain is kept alive in a jar and can only "answer" questions by making a reader's eyes land on a passage, so that all the understanding seems to live in the book rather than in the brain.

My thesis: the book isnt conscious, but not because it's static while minds are active. The usual "it's just a giant lookup table" objection proves too much, since a brain-at-an-instant together with the laws of physics is also just a mapping from one state to the next. What the book lacks isn't activity, it's enforcement. In a brain, the move from one state to the next happens whether or not anybody cooperates; in a book it happens only because a reader volunteers to turn to the right page. Its dynamics are borrowed, not enforced. Call this the enforcement condition.

Two consequences I want to defend: (1) the test for whether some object is the subject is a perturbation test — does it keep making the right transitions when you mess with the reader, the page order, the environment — which is why the brain in a jar with a tireless technician is genuinely a borderline case and should be; (2) a lazy simulation that only computes when queried isn't a subject either, so the only way to make a book conscious is to build the enforcement into it, and then it stops being a book.

Give me exactly five comments, numbered list, one or two sentences each, no intro and no wrap-up at the end. No style notes and no reading suggestions — I want to know where the argument breaks. Specifically: I'm worried that "enforced" is only a fancy word for "produced by the object's own intrinsic physical setup," in which case I've restated the ordinary view instead of replacing it; and I'm worried about the reverse, that if enforcement is what does the work then a fanatically reliable reader who never turns the wrong page starts turning the book into a mind, which I don't want to say. Tell me which of those is the real problem, or name one I havent seen.
