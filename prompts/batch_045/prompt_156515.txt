Building a rule for a regional health authority and I need the spec before I look at any candidate rules. The rule has to rank budget options that trade gains for one person against losses for others — a hip replacement for one patient vs. another year of home dialysis for four, that kind of thing — and a few of the options also change how many people are affected (prevention now vs. treatment later). We dont have utility meters: all we get is observed choice behaviour, self-reported intensity, and clinician judgement. And the ranking has to be defensible in plain language to whoever loses out, so "the normalisation is arbitrary but we fixed it this way" isn't available as an answer.

So: write me the desiderata that a theory of interpersonal comparison would have to meet to be good enough for that job. For each one, give the concrete case that makes it a requirement rather than just an intuition. Please include the awkward ones: the person whose expectations have adapted downward and who reports being perfectly content with very little; the person who has every incentive to inflate how intensely they care; comparisons where one side is a future version of the same person after a big change; and the fact that whoever applies the rule is not omniscient. Mark each desideratum non-negotiable, negotiable, or a preference of one camp, and flag any pair you think can't be jointly satisfied. My own guess is that invariance under rescaling each person's utilities independently (which you seem stuck with, since expected-utility representations are only fixed up to positive affine transformation) collides with being able to say who is absolutely worse off, and hence with any rule that gives priority to the worse off — but check whether I've got that right, and whether Arrow-style impossibility bites once you insist on the weakest informational input.

Skip a full scorecard against named theories, I'll do that seperately — one column on what each requirement rules out is plenty.

Format: the list as a table with columns: requirement (one sentence), motivating_case (one or two sentences, concrete), status (non-negotiable / negotiable / camp preference), rules_out (a few words, or a dash). Under the table, a JSON object with fields: conflicting_pair (the two desiderata you think can't both hold, and why), weakest_link (the one you'd drop first and what dropping it costs), minimum_information (the weakest input — ordinal and non-comparable, level comparable, unit comparable, or fully comparable — that this job actually forces on you, with the reason), open_question (the one thing on the list you're least sure is a real desideratum).
