Hey — I run a twice-monthly reading group on anthropics for astronomy and physics grad students. Most of them have never done this formally but they've all argued about the Doomsday argument at 1am. Next session is self-sampling vs. self-indication (and the presumptuous philosopher). Last time half the room was implicitly assuming SSA and half SIA and nobody noticed for a full hour, so I want to head that off: I want a decision-tree handout that sorts people by what they say about cases, before anyone gets to say the words "self-sampling" or "self-indication".

What I'm after:

Everyone starts from the same commitment: my total evidence is the third-person physical evidence plus the indexical fact that I'm this observer having this evidence right now, and I want a number. Then 7–9 branch questions, each a concrete mini-case with 2–4 answer options, where the answer sends you to another node or to a leaf. Each leaf carries: the name of the position (or "unnamed"), one sentence on what it's committed to, and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Cap it at 9 leaves — if two things I asked for collapse into each other, tell me that rather than inventing a tenth.

The nodes have to be phrased so someone can answer without already knowing the labels. "A fair coin was flipped, a machine makes one observer if heads and two if tails, you wake up and notice you have a black beard — how confident are you in heads: 1/2, 2/3, or 1/3?" is the register I want. "Should all possible observers be weighted equally?" is not.

Please build it from the logical space rather than from a list of names: first say what the independent questions are and which combinations of answers are genuinely available, then drop the actual views into the boxes, and for any combination nobody occupies give me a one-liner on why (incoherent? unattractive? just unexplored?).

Cases that have to show up at minimum: the black-beard incubator; the duplication-with-amnesia case (coin, two awakenings, memory wiped); a node that forces a verdict on the Doomsday argument and the presumptuous philosopher together, since anyone who likes one but not the other owes an explanation; and two I made up for this session:

- the Unborn Crowd: fair coin. Heads — you're created, woken once, that's it. Tails — you're created and woken once, and a year later a thousand more observers just like you are created and woken once each. You wake with no clue what year it is. Same question: your credence in heads.
- the Endless Room: two hypotheses about some physical parameter, otherwise equally well supported by the data. On one, the universe contains finitely many observers; on the other, infinitely many. Does that move anything? And if the answer is "the infinite one goes to probability 1", I want to see where that lands you.

I also want a branch where "I don't think there's a fact of the matter about the credence, I only want to answer questions about what to do" is a genuine live option, and that route has to end in a real leaf (the anthropic-decision-theory-flavored one), not a shrug. Give it a case too — a bet or a policy choice where the packages disagree about what to do, not just about what to believe.

Leaves I'd like covered if you can fit them: SSA with more than one option about the reference class (I want the reference-class choice to be an actual branch, not a footnote), SIA, full non-indexical conditioning (or whatever it's properly called — rename it if I've got the term wrong), the halfer and thirder ends of the duplication case, "just do the physics and don't update on your own existence at all", and the decision-theoretic-only leaf. Flag any pair of leaves your cases can't actually pull apart, and say what sort of case would.

Last bit, the part I care most about: a short note on where this tree is most likely to be wrong. Which branch question are you least sure does the work you've assigned it — the one where if someone at the session says "actually those two answers don't come apart the way you claim", you'd have to redraw the whole thing? And where are you least certain about which real view sits in which box?

Format: the tree as something I can actually read (nested bullets or an ASCII sketch is fine), and then the same content as a JSON object with these fields:

- nodes: array of {id, question, options: [{text, routes_to}], separates} — separates is one line on which views you think come apart at that node
- leaves: array of {id, name, commitment, awkward_commitment, reached_from} — reached_from is which answers or nodes land there
- empty_combinations: array of {combination, why_unoccupied}
- unseparable_pairs: array of {pair, reason, case_that_would_separate_them}
- doubts: {weakest_branch, what_would_change_it, mapping_uncertainty}

thx, and deffinitely keep the case wording concrete enough that I can put it on a handout as-is.
