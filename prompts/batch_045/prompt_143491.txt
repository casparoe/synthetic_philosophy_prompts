hey — im putting together an adversarial collaboration and I need someone to actually draft the document. Two camps:

- the **precisionist**: a sharp/uniqueness Bayesian. Rationality always mandates a single probability function, update it by strict conditioning, then maximise expected utility. Anything that looks like imprecise credence is either loose self-reporting or a sharp degree of confidence the agent can't fully introspect, plus maybe some pragmatic slack in how we talk about it.
- the **credalist**: an imprecise probabilist / permissivist. Sets of probability functions are the representational primitive, rationality is permissive, and the decision rule is E-admissibility or maximality, falling back on something like Γ-maximin when you have to pick one act.

They've been going at each other about dilation and about whether "mushy credence" is even a state anyone could be in, and mostly they've been talking past each other.

What I want is the text they could both sign. Format:

1. a **numbered agreed-claims section** — with real content, not "both sides agree rationality matters". Things where the concession costs somebody something.
2. a **disagreements table**: each row a residual disagreement, with two columns — the precisionist's own formulation of her view and the credalist's own formulation of his — each phrased so the *other* side would accept it as a fair statement of what's being claimed. No strawmen, and no wording so neutral it says nothing.
3. a **cruxes section**: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of them, and for each crux a concrete case or argument both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance — what each expects the case to show, and what each would concede if it came out the other way.
4. a short closing note on **which part of the fight, if any, is merely verbal**, and why.

Cruxes I think are in there, use them or drop them as you see fit, but I want the two scenarios below built in because otherwise the whole thing floats:

- **The two-instrument case.** One instrument, A, is known good and reads 0.30 on a quantity in [0,1], with error you have a sharp distribution for. Instrument B was built by a subcontractor you know nothing about: it's either as good as A or it's junk that prints a uniform random number regardless of the truth, and you have no evidence either way — no base rate, no nothing. B reads 0.80. You can bet at even money that the true value is above 0.50, bet it's at or below, or pass for free. Is some act rationally required here, is more than one permitted, or is passing required? And separately: what does each side predict a room of competent non-philosophical statisticians would actually do, and what would each concede if that prediction went against them?
- **The worst-member scoring rule.** A forecasting platform lets forecasters report a point or an interval for each event, and scores the report with a strictly proper rule applied to the member of the report least favourable given what happened: report [0.2, 0.6] and you're scored as if you'd said 0.2 if the event occurs and as if you'd said 0.6 if it doesn't. Point reports are just singleton intervals. Does an interval report ever come out best on this rule, and by whose lights? The crux is whether that result is evidence against imprecise credence or an artefact of baking sharp probability into the scoring function — so give the symmetric foil too (a rule that scores the set by its best member, or by the member closest to the truth) and say what each side predicts forecasters will do under each.
- **Long-run collapse.** An imprecise agent who knows she'll get a long stream of evidence she can't yet see: does her set shrink towards a point, and if it does, does that vindicate the precisionist or is it just what imprecision looks like once the evidence is in?

Please don't turn this into a verdict — no "on balance the credalist has the better of it". No citations or bibliography, I don't want any. And keep it in vocabulary both camps would use: if you reach for something like "real agents are bounded anyway", say how it's meant to bear.
