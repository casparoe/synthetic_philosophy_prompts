Longtime lurker, first-time poster. I'm a PhD student in political economy, where Aumann-style agreement results are background furniture in half our models, so "rational agents cannot agree to disagree" gets tossed around my department constantly. Last week in a methods seminar I mentioned that my supervisor and I had each read the same preregistered replication study, told each other our posteriors on the headline hypothesis — she said 0.6, I said 0.3 — and neither of us moved. A visitor said, basically: well, Aumann — so either one of you is irrational or one of you doesn't actually want the truth.

I don't think that follows, and I can't say why, partly because I suspect we weren't disagreeing about the same thing. Two wrinkles I want you to take seriously rather than smooth over. First, we attended to different parts of the report — she to the main specification, me to the robustness appendix — so our posteriors are conditional on different coarse summaries of one study, and the event "the effect is real" may not even be measurable in both of our event spaces. Call that asymmetric refinement. Second, her number is over "the effect is real in the direction the original authors claimed" and mine is over "the effect is real as the study operationalized it," which come apart when the preregistration was vague. Call that hypothesis drift.

So: what precise claims could that slogan be making? I want the menu, trivial to wild, including readings that turn on exactly what has to be common knowledge, on whether the prior has to be over one shared algebra of events, and on what happens when agents' partitions aren't refinements of each other at all. For each reading say whether it's trivial, substantive and defensible, or false — and, the part I actually care about, which readings would get the visitor his conclusion, and what he'd need to assume about us even then.

Sections please, in this order: Readings (about 400 words, numbered, each a single thesis I could write on a board); Verdicts (about 200 words, one line per reading); Does it get him the conclusion (about 150 words); What I should have said in the room (about 100 words, a couple of sentences I could actually say out loud). In American English.
