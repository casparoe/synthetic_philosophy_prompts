So — Rudd's 2008 National Apology to the Stolen Generations, read through Jaspers' four guilt categories from those 1946 lectures (criminal, political, moral, metaphysical). Recap in case any of it matters: from roughly the 1910s to the 1970s Australian federal and state agencies removed Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children from their families under protection/assimilation policies; the 1997 Bringing Them Home report documented it and recommended an official apology; Howard refused the word, on the grounds that an apology would imply guilt being passed on to a later generation, and moved a motion of regret in 1999 instead; Rudd apologised in Parliament in February 2008, and actual compensation has been patchy ever since.

What I want is a disciplined mapping before anything else. Which kind of guilt, if any, attaches to whom — the politicians who made the policy, the officials who took the children, the voters who returned those governments, Rudd's own parliament apologising for a state that is and isn't the same one. Then tell me what an apology can even do on Jaspers' scheme, since he has political guilt answered by liability and payment rather than words, and moral guilt as something nobody can take on for somebody else. Is the apology doing real work, or is it a category error with good manners?

Two wrinkles I want handled, because this is where I think the scheme strains. (1) Someone who moved to Australia in 2015, naturalised, voted in the last election — does she inherit political guilt on Jaspers' account? Say the awkward thing if it's the answer. (2) The non-identity point: plenty of people alive now, on both sides, exist because the removals happened. Does that move any of the ascriptions, or is it a red herring?

Then commit to a verdict: does this case vindicate the four-way distinction or expose a gap Jaspers never had to face — he was writing to a country whose members mostly were the people who did it. Give me a definite answer on whether a nation can be guilty here, and flag the part you're least confident about.
