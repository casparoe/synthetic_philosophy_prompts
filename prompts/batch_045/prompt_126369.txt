Hey — I need a hand getting ready for a talk. I'm in lower sixth and I've got to do this 15 minute thing for the school's maths-and-philosophy society next Wednesday, with about ten minutes of questions after. I've never done one of these and I'm proper bricking it about the Q&A bit.

The topic's the MU-puzzle from Hofstadter's GEB. You know the one: the MIU system, start from MI, rules are (1) if a string ends in I you can stick a U on the end, (2) everything after the M can be doubled, (3) III anywhere can become U, (4) UU anywhere can be deleted. MU can't be derived, and the usual story is that you have to jump out of the system to see that, because the number of I's is never a multiple of 3.

My thesis is that the usual story is wrong in a way that matters. I think jumping out is actually two separate moves and everyone only notices the first:

- the plug-in: you have to map the strings onto something you can actually reason about — here, numbers mod 3. MIU never mentions numbers. So the place you jump to isn't neutral ground, it's a second formal system you picked, and nothing in the puzzle tells you which one to pick.
- the sweep: even once you've plugged in, the argument is about every possible derivation, all infinitely many of them. That's the bit I think is genuinely outside, and that's the bit that connects to Gödel rather than the counting trick.

I also want to say up front that MU is true but unprovable is a sloppy way to put it — MU is a string, not a sentence, it hasn't got a truth value — and instead say the unreachability is provable outside but not expressible inside.

Two examples I'm using:

1. My one-rule-change version: same system, but you can also delete an I whenever it's immediately followed by a U. MU comes out in four steps (MI, MII, MIIII, MIU, MU), but everyone I showed it to knows it's impossible because of the counting thing. Point being, the mod-3 argument is a fact about the rules, not about the strings, and if you carry it across without redoing it you get the wrong answer.
2. A system where I basically can't do the sweep at all: start from a number, you may halve it if it's even or replace n with 3n+1 if it's odd, and the question is whether you can always get back to 1. Which is the Collatz thing, nobody knows. So JOOTSing is a hope, not a guarante.
