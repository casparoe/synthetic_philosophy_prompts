I want to use lexical threshold negative utilitarianism as a working decision rule, and I have come to think that most of the advertised disagreement about it is really disagreement about a piece of machinery the view never specifies. Please write the specification for that machinery.

Setup, so that we are using the terms the same way. By lexical threshold negative utilitarianism I mean the conjunction of three claims: first, that there is a single hedonic scale on which pleasant and unpleasant experiences have magnitudes; second, that there is a threshold on the unpleasant side such that any unpleasant experience above it is lexically more urgent than any amount, however large, of pleasant experience — no finite quantity of the latter compensates for one unit of the former; third, that above the threshold, and below it, trade-offs are ordinary and the scale behaves cardinally. The purpose I want it adequate to is comparative and institutional: an allocator with a fixed budget, a population of people who are not herself, and incomplete information, facing a choice between programmes — one that eliminates a small number of severe pains, one that adds a very large number of mild pleasures, one that converts severe pains into mild residual ones, one that shortens severe pains at the cost of adding many mild ones — must be able to rank such options, must be able to rank them under risk, and must be able to say, non-arbitrarily, when the view yields no ranking at all.

What I am asking for is the desiderata for the first and second clauses: the theory of the scale and of the cut. What must an account of the commensurability of pleasure and pain deliver in order to be adequate to that purpose? For each requirement, state the case or consideration that motivates it and say what specifically goes wrong in the allocator's hands if the account fails it. Then mark each requirement as non-negotiable relative to the purpose I have fixed, or as the preference of a particular camp that a defender of the view is entitled to reject — I am as interested in which purported requirements are really partisan as I am in the list itself.

At a minimum, the account must be able to handle the following cases, which I do not think the standard examples in circulation cover; add further cases of your own where you think the list is thin.

A subject who has the sensory-discriminative profile of pain — location, intensity, sharpness — with no distress or unpleasantness at all, and reports being indifferent to it continuing.

Pleasures whose magnitude is a function of a pain that preceded them: relief, the easing of an itch, the first minute after a dental injection wears off. If these are counted at face value on the pleasant side, then a pain and the relief that follows it are double-counted, and analgesia looks less valuable than it is, or more, depending on how the bookkeeping goes.

Single experiences with both valences at once — the spicy meal, the horror film, the satisfying exhaustion — where it is not settled whether there are two magnitudes or one.

Pain whose quality differs and not merely its intensity: burning, nausea, grief, boredom, despair, the feeling of suffocating. If these are separate dimensions rather than points on one line, then a lexical threshold over a scalar presupposes an earlier aggregation of the dimensions, which seems to reintroduce exactly the trade-offs the lexical cut was introduced to forbid. Say whether this is a real problem or a confusion.

The sandpile: very many discomforts, each just below the threshold, distributed over many people, against one episode just above it. And alongside it, the invariance question: if two adjacent sub-threshold moments are merged into one longer experience, or one long experience split into two, must the ranking survive? A threshold stated in units of intensity per unit time is not invariant in the way a threshold stated in peak intensity is, and I want to know whether grain-invariance is a genuine desideratum or a prejudice of the extensionalists.

Interpersonal comparison without behaviourism: two subjects, the same stimulus measured on a shared physical index, different reports, and expressive norms, stoicism as a costly signal, and anticipation that makes the same stimulus worse for one of them. A shared index is not yet a shared quantity, and I want to know what a theory has to add to get from the one to the other.

The location of the threshold, and its ratchet: if the cut is set as a fraction of the worst pain a human being can suffer, then a successful programme that eliminates the worst pains raises everything remaining relative to the cut, or collapses the above-threshold range, and the allocator's rankings churn for reasons that have nothing to do with anyone's welfare. Relatedly, whether the verdicts are invariant under a positive rescaling of the units — if they are not, the doctrine is hostage to a choice of units, and I want that put down as a requirement or dismissed as a confusion.

Use under risk: an option carrying a very small probability of an above-threshold outcome against an option guaranteeing an enormous quantity of below-threshold benefit. Lexical priority appears to make expected-value reasoning unavailable and to license either fanaticism or paralysis; a theory adequate to my purpose has to say which, or say something better.

The distinction between an experience being bad and a life being worse for containing it, as in pain that is chosen, endured, and later valued. And the distinction between how an episode feels while it is happening and how it is evaluated afterwards, where the two are known to diverge.

Two closing tasks. First, if some subset of the desiderata cannot be jointly satisfied, say so plainly and identify the smallest jointly unsatisfiable subset you can find — that is the part of the answer I care about most, since a list that is internally inconsistent is itself a result about the view. Second, name the single requirement that does the most work in excluding candidates, and then score, briefly, four or five contenders against the list: the single cardinal hedonic scale of the classical picture; lexical threshold negative utilitarianism as it is usually stated; a view on which suffering is a distinct normative kind with no common scale on which to be traded against pleasure at all; a view on which the badness of pain tracks the content and reliability of a damage-signal rather than felt intensity; and a preference-based or revealed-choice account of welfare. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scorecard. Do not argue the general case for or against negative utilitarianism; assume it, and work on the scale. No bibliography is wanted, and nothing here should depend on being able to look anything up.
