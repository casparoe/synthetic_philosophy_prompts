Hey, I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy past whatever they teach in high school, so be gentle — I got nerd sniped by something called "infinite ethics" in a group chat last week and I can't let it go.

Here's the setup as I understood it. If the universe is infinite and holds infinitely many beings whose lives go well and infinitely many whose lives go badly, then "add up all the welfare and compare" doesn't produce anything you can rank — it's infinity minus infinity, or a sum that just isn't defined — so ordinary total-utilitarian-style aggregation stops working. Two repairs came up in the chat. Call the first one "density": stop totaling and compare rates — exhaust the universe with a nested sequence of finite regions, compute average welfare per being in each region, take the limit, and say the world with the higher limiting average is better. Call the second one "difference-making": since anything I can actually do touches only finitely many beings, treat the infinite background as a constant that cancels, and rank options by the finite footprint they leave — the good minus the bad for the beings on the receiving end.

The argument started when a math friend pointed out that density has basically the same disease as summing a conditionally convergent series: change the order in which your nested regions enumerate the universe and you can push the running average around, or make it never settle down. Two replies came back and we couldn't pick between them: either you need a privileged ordering (distance from here? causal order? something from actual physics?) in which case the theory is quietly resting on that choice and not on welfare, or you say "take any ordering, and where all reasonable orderings agree, that's the verdict" and just live with the fact that they often won't agree.

So please compare density (with whatever fix you think is least ad hoc) against difference-making. Three things I'm stuck on. First: as far as I can tell, density has the nasty feature that a finite intervention moves no limiting average at all, so every action available to me comes out exactly neutral and the view stops telling me what to do; difference-making does give advice but can't rank whole worlds, and it has what looks to me like a boundary problem — in an infinite universe my causal footprint has no natural edge, so where do I stop tracing effects? Which of those two failures is worse, and why? Second, are there cases where the two actually disagree about what to do, and not just about bookkeeping — I was trying to imagine an action that makes a small local group much better off while nudging the global density slightly down, and I couldn't tell whether that's even constructible or whether difference-making would just bless any locally-good action no matter the wider picture. Third, a guy in the chat kept saying "just count with infinite numbers, ratios aren't the only way to compare infinite sets" and I definately couldn't tell if he was joking — is that a genuine third option or does it collapse into one of the other two?

Don't tell me who wrote what or point me at papers; I can't look anything up and I don't want a reading list. I want the conceptual comparison, and systems or programming analogies are very welcome — I keep thinking of this as an infinite stream I can only ever iterate over in some order.
