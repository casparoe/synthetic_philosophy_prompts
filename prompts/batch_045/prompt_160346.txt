Here is the position I have been defending, which I will call absorption subjectivism. Probability talk, properly analysed, is talk about agents' degrees of belief constrained by coherence. But this is not a licence for anything goes, because everything the objectivist ever wanted from probability is recovered inside the subjectivist framework as a theorem rather than assumed as a postulate. Three recoveries do the work. First, coherence constraints — the Dutch-book and accuracy arguments — force degrees of belief to satisfy the probability axioms and to update by conditioning. Second, exchangeability plus the representation theorem for exchangeable sequences recovers limiting relative frequencies and a parameter that plays the role of a chance, in the sense that no bet, decision or policy could tell the difference between it and a genuinely objective chance. Third, calibration and merging results recover the long-run guarantee that forecasts line up with frequencies, and that agents who share a likelihood and see enough of the same data converge with probability one. On this view the frequentist and propensity vocabularies are at best convenient shorthand for facts about exchangeable sequences and at worst a reification of a parameter in a representation theorem, and no observation and no decision-theoretic consideration distinguishes the interpretations where it matters. I rely on this constantly in applied forecasting work, mostly to shut down colleagues who talk as if there were a fact of the matter about the chance that a given project ships on time.

I want to know how exposed that view is, and I have two devices I have been poking at it with.

The first is a class of cases I call loop targets: singular events whose probability depends on the probability that gets assigned to them. The probability that a bank fails given that a respected institution publishes that very probability. The probability that a crowd-management plan succeeds given that the operators have been told it probably will. The probability that a system behaves in a certain way given that its designers forecast its behaviour and then adjust it in light of the forecast. My view handles these, after a fashion: coherence still constrains the assignment, and nothing more is needed. But what worries me is that this looks like exactly the class of forecasts for which two of my three recoveries fall silent — there is no stable chance setup and no exchangeable sequence of repetitions — and the third is empty, since the calibration and merging results say nothing about a single event that never recurs under the same conditions. I want to know whether this is a real hole or whether I am just pointing at the familiar fact that singular probabilities are hard.

The second device is a stipulative adequacy condition, which I do not know whether to endorse. Call it the Audit Principle: an interpretation of probability is adequate only if, for any probability assigned to a singular event, there is some finite record of outcomes that would show the assignment to have been wrong, as opposed to merely unlucky or merely incoherent with the agent's other assignments. If that condition is even roughly right, my view looks badly exposed, because on my own account the only thing that can be wrong about a singular probability is incoherence with your other probabilities, and so no finite record can ever audit it; and my three recoveries get their purchase only by aggregating over sequences of the very kind that loop targets do not admit. Part of what I want from you is a verdict on the Audit Principle itself — whether it is a fair test, whether it smuggles in what it was meant to test for, and whether the rival interpretations actually do better on it. A consideration that sinks every interpretation equally is not much of a defeater, and I would rather be told that than be handed a pseudo-problem.

Three things in particular I want addressed directly. One: are the recoveries really recoveries, or do they presuppose what they were supposed to deliver? I have in mind the worry that exchangeability is relative to a chosen sequence and a chosen partition of the possibility space, and that on my own view nothing constrains the choice, so the recovered frequencies and the recovered chance look freely adjustable rather than objective. Two: has the view been insulated by construction? If every piece of apparent objective content is recovered as a consequence of the view, then of course no such content can count against it. Tell me whether that is principled absorption — a genuine explanatory unification of the kind that counts in the view's favour — or unfalsifiability by design, and whether insulation of that sort is or is not a problem for a position of this kind. Three: do loop targets and the Audit Principle actually bite, or do frequentism and propensity theories fare worse on the same test?
