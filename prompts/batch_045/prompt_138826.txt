for a monthly "big ideas" lunch at my office — about ten people, none of them philosophers, we've done inequality twice and it always collapses into the same argument — I said I'd write down in one page what I actually think about merit and desert, so we can argue with something instead of with each other. Here's the list, roughly in the order it came out:

1. Desert has to bottom out in something the person did or chose: effort, deliberate practice, showing up, the sacrifices they actually made. Nobody deserves credit for a good brain, a stable home, or being born in a country with a functioning labour market. Call this the anti-luck condition.
2. Still, I don't think it's unjust that a good neurosurgeon earns twenty times what a night-shift hospital cleaner earns. I'm just not moved by the leveling intuition here, and I don't think I should have to be.
3. If I'm honest, what I really want from "meritocracy" isn't a society where people get what they deserve. It's the abolishment of inherited position — the kid on the wrong side of the tracks should be able to become the neurosurgeon — and I'd take that even if it produced bigger gaps than the ones we have now.
4. I think it's genuinely insulting to tell someone that their success was mostly luck, and I think this matters politically rather than just being a matter of tact: people who feel their achievements are being explained away go looking for someone who won't explain them away.
5. I think we basically can't see, from the outside, how hard anyone tried or how much of a result was them. So institutions should be judged on whether the process was open and fair, not on whether the outcome matched anybody's desert.
6. I want inheritance taxed into near-nonexistence, because nobody deserves who their parents were.
7. And I don't think I get to say "the gap in (2) is just the market, and markets aren't a justice question." Markets are a legal artifact; their payoffs are as much a matter of justice as the tax code is.

I have a vague feeling something is wrong with (2), but I can't say what, and it might be somewhere else entirely.

So: does this hang together? Go through the pairs and tell me which ones are actually in tension and which only look like it — I'd rather be told "that one's fine, here's why" than have all seven flagged. Rank the real tensions by how bad they are rather than listing them evenly. For each one say what would have to give and what giving it would cost me. And then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, since I'd rather make one large move than six small ones — but don't pick for me, I want to know what each one costs and then decide.
