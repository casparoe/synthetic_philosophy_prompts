I've been going back and forth on Hilbert's hotel by myself and I can't tell if I actually have a view or just a pile of reactions. Where I land: full hotel, one more guest shows up, everyone shifts up a room — fine, no problem. A bus with infinitely many passengers arrives, everyone doubles their room number — I'll grant it, but it feels like a trick in a way the first one doesn't. Everyone in an odd-numbered room checks out — I know the guests left can still be paired one-to-one with the originals, but I want to say there are half as many people in the building now, and I know that definatly sits badly with what I just granted. One guest in room 7 leaves and nobody replaces them: "still exactly as many guests" feels wrong to me even though "still infinitely many" doesn't — somebody is gone. Everyone in a perfect-square-numbered room leaves: "fewer" feels right, but "how many fewer" feels like a nonsense question. A hotel with a room for every real number between 0 and 1: I balk, and I can't tell whether I'm objecting to the math or just to the idea of anyone building it. And the one that actually breaks me: a new guest arrives at 1/2, 3/4, 7/8... of a minute before noon and everyone shifts up a room each time, so by noon every guest has moved infinitely often — I don't think that one has an answer at all, and it feels like a different kind of problem, not just a weirder one.

The principle I keep coming back to is that infinity isn't a finished thing but a process, so any question about what the hotel is like "at the end" is badly formed. Does that actually get my verdicts, and which one does it force me to drop? Also tell me what the other rules on offer are — the pairing one, the "a part is always smaller than the whole" one, whatever else fits my cases — where each one handles things and where they come apart. And what would the account you land on say about a case I haven't thought through: one full hotel with a room for every whole number, another with a room for every real number — is the second one bigger, and could the first hotel's guests all move in with rooms left over?
