Last night I got into a three-hour argument with my friend Priya — we were both in the same rational choice seminar last term — and she ended up shutting me down with the same sentence about six times: "a difference that makes no difference is no difference." I want to know what, precisely, that sentence could be claiming, because I'm pretty sure it was doing at least two different jobs in her mouth and I cant get her to say which one she meant.

Context, so you know what work it had to do. The seminar case that started it was a spectrum of lives, L0 through L100. L0 is a normal-length life full of the things that make a life go well — deep relationships, achievement, actually understanding stuff. Each step up trades a small loss along those dimensions for a bigger gain in sheer duration, so each L(i+1) is supposed to be a slight improvement on L(i), and L100 is an enormously long life lived at a level barely above mild contentment. My intuition is stubbornly that L0 is better than L100, and each individual step also looks like an improvement to me, so something has to go, and I was flirting with giving up transitivity of "better than." Priya's line was supposed to stop that: adjacent lives differ by amounts no one could possibly notice, a difference you can't notice is no difference, so there's no betterness between adjacent lives for the argument to chain together, so transitivity survives untouched. Then later, once we'd drifted onto an actual decision of hers — two job offers that differ by a slightly shorter commute and a slightly worse health plan — she used the same sentence for what felt like a completely different purpose: she said it means she has no reason to prefer either one, that flipping a coin is fine, and that nobody could criticize her either way. I said that's a different claim and she said I was splitting hairs.

Things I tried that went nowhere, so you know where I'm stuck. (1) I said: if adjacent lives are *equally good*, then since equality of value is transitive, L0 and L100 come out equally good — and she does think they differ, so she must mean something weaker than equal goodness. She said I was helping myself to transitivity of equality in order to argue against transitivity of betterness, which felt like a dodge but I couldn't say why. (2) I said: imagine we had a perfectly reliable well-being meter that registers the difference between L(i) and L(i+1) even though no human can feel it; she said the slogan is about what matters to agents, not about what instruments recieve. (3) I said: if each step is literally *zero* difference, then a hundred of them is still zero — you can't build a real difference out of a hundred null ones — and she said that only shows the steps can't all be zero, which she'd already granted, and that "no difference" never meant zero anyway. (4) I pointed out that how finely I slice the spectrum is up to me, so I can make each step as tiny as I like, and it seemed bizarre that my choice of granularity should decide whether the argument works. She said that's exactly what shows the argument is a trick.

So here's what I want, and please don't give me a reading list, citations, or "see so-and-so on this" — the seminar handouts are gone and I just want the distinctions: lay out the distinct precise claims that sentence could be expressing — about perceptibility, about what a person could notice or discriminate, about whether there's a determinate value fact, about indifference in preference, about reasons or blameworthiness, about what's worth caring about, whatever else belongs on the list — and for each say whether it's trivial, substantive but defensible, or actually false. Then tell me which of those readings, if any, would really do the work Priya needed in each place: which one genuinely cuts the chain between L0 and L100, which ones only look like they do, and which one she was most likely leaning on when she concluded she could flip a coin and that no one could blame her. A numbered menu of readings with a verdict on each is exactly what I want to bring back to her.
