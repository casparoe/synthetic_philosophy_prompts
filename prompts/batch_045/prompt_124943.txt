Here is the thing I want translated, stated so that you don't have to recall any source. Consider this idealization of good deliberation. A question is put to a person with a bounded context window and a bounded amount of time. Rather than answering outright, that person may split the question into sub-questions and hand each one to a fresh copy of themselves, recursively, then combine the answers that come back. Copies are stateless: one sees its sub-question and nothing about where it came from, what its siblings are doing, or how deep it sits. Let H be the limit of this arrangement — a person with access to arbitrarily many stateless copies of themselves, organized this way. H is offered in the AI alignment literature both as a model of what ideal deliberation consists in and as a standard a machine might be trained to approximate, with the expectation that the machine could then be compressed back into something that answers in one step.

I want the translation done for a specific variant, which I suspect is not the one usually discussed. Suppose every node writes its (question, answer) pair into a shared store after answering, and a node whose question already appears in the store reuses the stored answer instead of recomputing it. The structure is then a DAG rather than a tree, and identical sub-questions always receive the identical answer. Call this memoized H. Nothing else is changed: still stateless nodes, still a bounded context, still the same person copied, still no node that sees the whole.
