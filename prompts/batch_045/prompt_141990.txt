I'm trying to work out what I should even be demanding of a theory of rational choice under transformative experience — the L.A. Paul sort of case, where you can't know what it'll be like to have the kid, move abroad or take the job, and part of what's unknown is what you'll come to care about. Before I read any more of the literature I want the spec: what would an account have to deliver to count as adequate *for first-personal deliberation*, i.e. as something you could actually use at the moment of choosing, rather than as a standard for third parties handing down judgements about whether you chose well. Give each requirement with the case behind it, mark which are non-negotiable and which are really just preferences of one camp (my guess is that "it must always issue a verdict" and "it must never steer you somewhere your later self rejects" are the contested ones), and flag any pair that looks jointly unsatisfiable — if the list can't all be had at once, that's the result I most want.

The cases I want actually handled rather than gestured at: (1) titrated transformation, where the same change can be taken in small reversible steps that add up to roughly the whole thing — wether the theory is committed to treating the series as equivalent to the single big jump, or can coherently say the steps are fine and the jump isn't; (2) the extremely well-informed agent, who has unusually good imaginative and testimonial access to what it's like, maybe because she's had a near-miss version of it — does the difficulty fade as information improves or is it structural; (3) the retrospective split, where afterwards she's glad it happened, wouldn't repeat it knowing what she now knows, and treats her earlier self's deliberations as beside the point; (4) mandatory transformation, where the change is coming whatever she does and the only thing left to choose is her anticipatory stance. Say whether (4) is even in scope for the same theory or a different problem altogether.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases, not on scoring the contenders. But do tell me which single requirement knocks out the most candidates — I suspect it's the one about being usable by the agent who's actually in the dark — and then give a short paragraph each on how bog-standard expected utility maximisation on your current preferences, a "choose what your post-transformation self would choose" view, and an objective-value view fare against your list.
