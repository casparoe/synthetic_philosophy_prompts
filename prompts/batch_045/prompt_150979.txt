ok so i think ive talked myself into the dark forest thing, but a specific version of it: not "aliens are evil", but that detecting and destroying are asymmetric — you can locate someone way earlier than you can learn anything trustworthy about their intentions, and there's no cheap way to signal "im harmless" that a paranoid civ would actually accept — so striking first is rational, everyone can work that out about everyone else, and that alone explains the silence. my own addition is that it predicts more than just silence: any civ that starts broadcasting loud should get shut down pretty fast, so we ought to expect at most short-lived technosignatures and basically never ancient, long-running ones.

what would count against this? i want observations and arguments ranked by how bad they'd be for the view, and especially the ones someone who believes it would have to admit really do count against them, not just the stuff critics always repeat. also be honest about whether its falsifiable at all: if the prediction is silence and silence is literally all weve got, does anything actually test it, and is that insulation a problem or fine for a theory like this? the thing that would move me most personally is if the timing doesn't work — if the window where you can detect someone and also cheaply destroy them is basically zero for most of a galaxy's history, the whole argument feels like it collapses. is that a real defeater or am i overthinking one detail?
