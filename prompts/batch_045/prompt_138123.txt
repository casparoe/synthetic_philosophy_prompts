I'm a high school student writing a paper for our philosophy club on speciesism, and I built an argument that I now think must break somewhere, because I can't accept where it ends up. I'd be grateful if you'd find the break for me.

(1) The only things that can make one creature's suffering count for more than another creature's suffering are facts about that creature itself — its own capacities — never the biological group it belongs to; group membership as such cannot affect how much a pain matters.
(2) The capacities that seem to do the work vary by arbitrarily small degrees across animals: one can lay out a long series of creatures, from an adult human at one end down to a fish at the other, in which each creature differs only slightly from the one next to it.
(3) Small differences in the underlying capacities can only produce small differences in how much the creature's suffering matters; there are no moral cliffs.
(4) So the difference in moral standing between the human at one end of the series and the fish at the other end is only small.
(5) But that is unacceptable: a fish's suffering does not matter nearly as much as a person's does. So at least one of (1) through (3) is false, or the inference to (4) fails.

My friend says the error is in (3) and that I should simply accept a sharp cutoff somewhere on the scale. But that seems to return me to exactly what I was objecting to: a cutoff has to sit at some point on a smoothly varying scale, and wherever I put it, two creatures on either side of it differ only slightly while their suffering counts very differently. If I have to choose, I would rather keep (1) and (2) than keep (5), and I would rather not place the line by species.

Please tell me which step you reject and what rejecting it costs me. Is there a reading of (3) that survives while (4) still fails, or does the argument go through and I have to accept a cliff after all? And if I do accept a cliff, what exactly am I committed to that I wasn't already? Please answer in American English, in a few focused paragraphs, and don't name or cite any authors or books — I'll add the references myself.
