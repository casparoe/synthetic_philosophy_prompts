Is there a name for this? I'm a backend dev, no philosophy background, and I keep watching the same shape of argument on my team without having a word for it.

Project's going badly, someone says kill it. Nobody ever says "we've spent two quarters, we have to finish" — that one I'd recognize. What comes out instead is a stack of forward looking reasons: the data model work is reusable whatever we do, two internal teams have already integrated and would be stranded, a rewrite from scratch would cost about as much as finishing. All of them are about the future, and most of them are true. Knock one down and a different one shows up; the advocates never run dry and never once say "ok, kill it."

Here's the part I actually want a word for. Most of those reasons were already available on day one. What the spending changed wasn't which reasons exist, it was how much weight they got — the reusable-knowledge argument was heard in week one and shrugged off, and in month seven it's decisive. The money never enters the argument, it just silently moves the bar for what counts as a good enough reason.

I know the sunk cost fallacy and this isn't the textbook version of it, and I also don't want a word that just means rationalizing, because if the knowledge really is reusable then continuing looks fine to me. Is there an established concept for the weight shifting thing specifically — and if not, can you name and define it, and say how it differs from escalation of commitment and from just having learned something genuinely useful?
