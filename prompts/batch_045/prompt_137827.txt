I'm a first-year PhD student, and I've been paid to compile an index and a case-list for a senior colleague's unpublished manuscript on vagueness. The draft breaks off before its final worked example, which is unfortunately the exact case my own short commentary is supposed to respond to, so I need to work out what she would say if she had written it. She never labels her own view. My supervisor says she is obviously a supervaluationist; a friend in my cohort says everything she commits to is equally consistent with epistemicism; someone else says the pattern only makes sense if standards are context-sensitive. From the draft chapters and her seminar handouts I have this track record. On a mid-series case (a pile of about a thousand grains) she refuses to assert *that is a heap* and refuses to deny it. She asserts *either that is a heap or it is not* without any hesitation, while denying *either it is definitely a heap or it is definitely not a heap*. She rejects the tolerance principle outright, and insists that nothing in her view requires tampering with classical consequence. She is explicitly non-committal about whether the indeterminacy is semantic or merely epistemic: in one footnote she says only that nothing we could do, even in principle, would settle where the boundary lies. On contextual material she calls a five-foot-eleven man *tall* when the conversation is about a basketball roster and withholds entirely when it is about jockeys, describing this as a shift in the operative standard rather than a change of mind. On a multidimensional predicate she asserts *city* of a settlement of two hundred and fifty thousand with no municipal government of its own, withholds it of a settlement of nine thousand with a full charter and a mayor, and says of two sixty-thousand-strong settlements — one dense, with a university and no charter, the other with a charter but essentially rural — that there is no saying which is more of a city than the other.

The unwritten case is this. A settlement of sixty-one thousand, densely built up, with an elected council that levies some taxes but no independent charter, considered inside a single fixed conversation (a national infrastructure meeting, so the comparison class is not drifting), and known to sit in the middle of a series in which neighbouring settlements differ by one household. I need to know: would she assert, deny, or withhold *Millbrook is a city*; would she assert *either Millbrook is a city or it is not*; and — the one I really cannot work out — would she assert that it is *definitely* indeterminate whether Millbrook is a city, i.e. that Millbrook is determinately a borderline case, or would she withhold there too?
