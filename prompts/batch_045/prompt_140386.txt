I'm on the conduct committee of a chartered institute (clinical engineers, though the trade doesn't much matter) and we've got a case next week that's turning into a philosophical row rather than a disciplinary one, so I want to circulate a short written opinion beforehand and get people to vote on something concrete.

Facts, briefly. Our member R. was the assigned listener in the institute's confidential peer-support scheme, which promises members in writing that nothing said in a session goes anywhere. In one session a colleague told her, while in a fairly bad state, that he'd falsified calibration records for equipment used in neonatal care. Separately, Rule 7 of our Code says a member with reasonable grounds to think another member has committed a serious breach must report it to the Registrar within five working days, with no discretion and no exceptions listed. She didn't report. Over about three weeks she talked him into reporting himself, which he eventually did. Nobody was harmed and no one on the panel thinks she was careless or covering for a mate — the whole question is the three-week gap.

The wrinkle is the Code's "conflicted compliance" proviso, bolted on a few years ago after an earlier mess: where a member shows that complying with one rule would have required breaching another, the committee records a finding of violation but imposes no sanction. No one has ever invoked it. Our lay member says that using it amounts to conceding our own Code can contradict itself, and that we should simply find she ought to have reported and move on. I think there's something in that, but I don't think it ends the discussion.

What I want is the opinion: whether we apply the proviso here and what precisely goes in the record; whether "she faced a genuine dilemma" and "there was no good option but one was still clearly better" pull apart into different institutional responses or whether the difference is academic and we should stop arguing about it; and whether we should recommend that Council rank the rules so this can't recur, plus what we'd give up if it did. Give us a bottom line we can actually vote on, take the lay member's worry seriously rather than patronising it, and if the metaphysics of dilemmas doesn't settle what the committee should do, say so and tell us what to decide in its place. Keep it under about 900 words.
