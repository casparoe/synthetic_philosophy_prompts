hey — I'm a PhD student (formal epistemology, decision-theory adjacent) and I'm TAing our upper-level seminar on probabilism this term. I have to write the take-home final and I need one question on Dutch book arguments that isn't just state the theorem and explain how it supports probabilism, because everyone in the class can already do that and it wouldn't separate anyone.

The angle I want to build it around is something I keep going back and forth on: the Dutch book argument only shows that an agent whose credences violate the probability axioms can be made to accept a package of bets that loses money for sure. It never shows that a coherent agent can be made to gain, and it doesn't obviously say anything about accuracy or about what anyone should believe. So on the face of it probabilism comes out as a purely defensive norm: be coherent or a well-informed and hostile bookie can bleed you. I want the question to force students to say whether that's a problem for the argument or the whole point of it, and to be precise about which assumption is doing the work — that the agent takes either side of a bet at their fair price, that utility is linear in money over the relevant stakes, that the bookie gets to pick the stakes and scale them, or whatever else they think matters.

To stop it being answerable from memory, I want at least one moving part they haven't seen in the readings. Two candidates I've been playing with — use whichever works better, or make up your own:

- a friction case: the bookie pays a fixed transaction cost per bet, or there's a spread between buy and sell prices, so the set of bets they can profitably offer shrinks, and some incoherent agents turn out to be unexploitable. Does immunity-under-friction show their incoherence is harmless, or does it just show the argument was never really about money?
- a no-bookie case: an agent who is incoherent but certain that no one will ever offer them a bet (say, betting is banned where they live, or they're the last person alive). Same worry in a different form: what is the argument supposed to be doing for them?

What I need from you:
