I am writing a paper on whether the formula of universal law can be made to yield determinate verdicts, and I have given up trying to read the answer off the text. Instead I have been collecting my own verdicts on cases, and I would like to know what principle, if any, they add up to. I am not asking for readings, for exegesis of what Kant meant, or for the history of the distinction between a contradiction in conception and a contradiction in will; I am asking after the principle my verdicts point to, on my own случае list.

The verdicts, given as I would give them in conversation:

1. I inflate my income on a mortgage application to obtain a loan I could not otherwise get. Impermissible, and seriously so. This is the case the test was built for: universal inflation of loan applications is a world in which the application does no work, so the lie cannot do what I want it to do.

2. I tell a colleague I will come to her dinner party, intending from the start to cancel at the last minute. Impermissible, but nothing like as seriously as 1 — closer to a discourtesy with a moral edge. I also doubt the same mechanism covers both: my casual promise does not seem to be drawing on a practice that my single act erodes.

3. I ride the bus without paying, on a route so heavily subsidised that my fare changes nothing — no service, no fare, no statistic I can detect. Impermissible, mildly. But every instance of my maxim satisfies its own condition while the aggregate defeats it, and whether the test reaches this case seems to depend on how that is handled.

4. I give to charity only when a matching campaign multiplies the gift. Entirely permissible, and sensible besides. Yet if everyone conditionalised giving on a match there would be no matches, and the maxim appears self-defeating under universalisation.

5. I lie to a friend about where I am taking her, because the surprise is the point of the evening. Permissible; I would do it again. Universal deception to preserve surprises destroys the possibility of being surprised, so the test seems to forbid it.

6. I am the only person who will ever know that a document in my care was forged, and I stay quiet because exposing it would cost me my job. Impermissible, seriously. But if a maxim may carry whatever uniquely identifying detail its author likes, no universalisation test reaches this: universalise keeping quiet when you are the sole person who knows and exposure would cost you your job, and nothing collapses.

7. I skip a national election in which my vote was never going to be pivotal. Not wrong — a mild civic failing at most. Structurally this is case 3 exactly: each instance inert, universal abstention destructive. My verdicts differ.

8. I give nothing to famine relief while living comfortably. Impermissible, but of another shape: I want latitude about how much and to whom, and the only argument available is the one from what a will cannot will, which has always struck me as a different and weaker argument from the one that disposes of 1.

9. I visit my friend in hospital from a mixture of affection and a wish to be seen as loyal. Not wrong. I want the principle to fall silent here.

The principle I have been trying to make work has three parts. A description rule: test the maxim as the agent's operative reason states it, striking out every element that does no work beyond singling out the agent or the occasion — names, dates, the fact that I alone know — and replacing it with the most specific description available to anyone in my situation. A rate condition: do not ask only about universal compliance, but sweep the rate of adoption upward from its actual level and look for a threshold at which the point the agent's reason aims at collapses. A failure condition: impermissible iff there is such a threshold. This disposes of 1, 3 and, with the description rule, 6; it says nothing about 9; and it condemns 4, 5 and 7, where I do not condemn.

So: repair it or overturn something. Does a further clause — something about taking a benefit produced by others' contributions, or about what those affected could consent to — separate 4 and 5 from 3 without swallowing 3, and does anything short of that keep 7 and 3 apart? Give me the principle, or the two or three candidates, stated precisely, each clause tied to the case that forces it, and say plainly which of my verdicts it cannot keep. Then tell me what it says about the case I go back and forth on: claiming a home-office deduction when the rule is vague, I am at the edge of entitlement, and I know that if everyone at the edge claimed it the code would have to be rewritten. Add two cases of your own, not on my list, where the candidates diverge, with each candidate's prediction recorded. And tell me honestly whether what survives is still a universalisation test, or a fairness principle, or the formula of humanity wearing the other formula's clothes — and, if the latter, which single case on my list is doing that work.
