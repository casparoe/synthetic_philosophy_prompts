i'm putting together a short poem to close the last session of an undergrad philosophy of biology seminar, on the species problem, and the sestina feels almost too perfect for it: six end-words rotating through every stanza so each one keeps coming back in a different slot and never settles, then the envoy where all six have to crowd into three lines together. can you write one? use these as end-words, or swap one if you have something better: species, lineage, barrier, hybrid, kind, name.

i'd like the rotation to do the arguing: let each stanza try on one candidate account and then lose it to the next. so — Darwin's own deflationary shrug that "species" is basically a convenience word with no sharp line separating it from variety; Mayr's criterion of interbreeding populations reproductively isolated from others; the move that species are individuals rather than kinds, with organisms as parts rather than members and species born at speciation and dead at extinction, which is why there aren't laws about them; the phylogenetic thought that a species is just the segment of lineage between two branching events, so the ancestral species literally ceases to exist the moment it buds off a daughter. and then the cases that break the tidy picture: asexual organisms with horizontal transfer, where there's no barrier to police at all, and ring species where A interbreeds with B and B with C but A doesn't with C, so "same species as" isn't transitive and can't be doing the job of a natural kind. In the envoy let all six words sit side by side without crowning one — pluralism, or at least an honest standoff, rather than a resolution.

accuracy matters more than prettiness here: someone who actually works on this should be able to read it without wincing, so never define a species by shared intrinsic traits of its organisms, and don't let the ending imply that everything is fine once we say species are real lineages and the rest is bookkeeping — that's exactly the claim half the room will push back on. and please don't fake a rhyme scheme that isn't there: end-words only, unrhymed, i'd rather have clean lines than forced ones. if you can land a real closing image (a museum drawer, a field guide, salamanders around a valley) that's a bonus, not the point.
