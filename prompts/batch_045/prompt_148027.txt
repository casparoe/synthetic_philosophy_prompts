I spent my career in experimental physics — designing apparatus, budgeting systematic error, arguing over whether a three-sigma bump was a signal or the look-elsewhere effect — and in retirement I have begun reading the Lucas–Penrose argument that mathematical judgement cannot be captured by any formal system. I would like that argument restated in the only vocabulary in which I know how to appraise a claim: measurement.

The mapping I have in mind is this. A candidate formal system F is an apparatus, its theorems are readings, and the Gödel construction applied to F is a procedure that prints a sentence G_F the apparatus will never produce. The premise I want scrutinised is the step from F does not prove G_F to I can see that G_F is true — which in my terms is a single, unreplicated, self-directed measurement taken by an uncalibrated instrument about itself.

Two stipulations. First, do not let see mean infallibly see: suppose my performance here is a measured reliability r < 1 on sentences of comparable complexity, with statistical and systematic uncertainties attached, and that the sentences are long enough that no finite agent, myself included, could check one by hand. Second, grant the mechanist the corresponding move: a machine that emits its own candidate Gödel sentences with the same reliability r, or samples them from a distribution.

Restate the argument as faithfully as you can under those stipulations, using my terms — signal and noise, calibration and blind analysis, replication, error budgets, the difference between a fluctuation and a discovery. Then give me the residue report: which steps carry over cleanly, which survive only after distortion, and which parts of what the philosophers are claiming have no counterpart at all in a vocabulary built for measurement. My real question is where the residue lies: does the argument collapse once seeing is degraded to a fallible readout on sentences no one can verify, or does my vocabulary simply lack the resources to say what the anti-mechanist means by seeing a truth?
