My 15-year-old has a school debate next week — she's been assigned to argue that the Soviet side started the Cold War, and then in the second round she has to argue the exact opposite. So we've been going through it together in the evenings, and i keep noticing the same thing happening, and I don't know what to call it.

It goes like this. Whichever side she's arguing, she picks a moment to start the story, and from that moment the other side is always the one who made the first move that nobody had answered yet. Start in 1945 and it's the Americans with the atomic bomb, and not sharing it. Start in 1946 and it's the Soviets sitting tight in northern Iran. Start in 1947 and it's the Truman Doctrine. Start in 1948 and it's the Berlin blockade or the Czech coup. Push it back past the war and it's 1917, or the intervention after it. And whatever start you pick, the other person just takes one step back and says "sure, but that was a reaction to—", and the argument never lands anywhere. The thing is, they're mostly not disagreeing about what happened. They agree on the events, and they even agree on a lot of the short-range what-ifs. What they disagree about is where the story begins.

The other thing I can't unsee is this. In every version, the side she's arguing for gets described at the level of deliberation: they were worried about this, they were boxed in by their allies, they'd been burned before, they had to. And the other side gets described at the level of character: they were expansionist, they were paranoid, they'd always wanted this, that's just what they're like. She does it without noticing, in both rounds, with the names swapped. It isn't bias in the ordinary sense of getting the facts wrong — the facts are fine. It's the level of description that flips.

So: does this have a name? And I don't just want a label, I want the carving — what exactly the thing is, what it isn't, and where it stops. "Security dilemma" gets used somewhere in this neighbourhood and it feels close but not right to me: that seems to be about two states genuinely misreading each other's defensive moves as offensive ones, whereas what I'm describing is about how the person telling the story cuts up the timeline, which is a diferent thing even if the two feed off each other. I've also seen "revisionist" and "post-revisionist" thrown around, but those look like labels for camps of historians rather than a description of the mechanism. And it isn't quite tu quoque or whataboutism, because it isn't "you did it too" — it's "you did it first, counting from here."

Where are the edges? It doesn't feel like the same thing as propaganda or arguing in bad faith, because my daughter is arguing both sides honestly and the mechanism works perfectly well both times — but I'd like to know where it tips over into that, and where it stops being what it is and becomes just ordinary cherry-picking of dates. Is whatever it is the same animal as what you get in a divorce, or with two kids fighting in the back seat, or is that something else wearing the same clothes?

And the bit I actually care about: if the thing does have a name, does having the name settle whether "who started it" is a well-posed question at all, or does it just relocate the fight somewhere else? I have to tell her something by Thursday.
