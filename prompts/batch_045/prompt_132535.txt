I serve on the four-person moderation council of *Ashfall*, a small persistent online world — survival and building, roughly two hundred regular players, running continuously for six years. I have to cast the deciding vote in a disciplinary case, and I would be grateful for help thinking it through before I do.

The facts. Over fourteen months a player I will call M. built, block by block, a cathedral: perhaps three hundred hours of her own labour, plus materials donated by others. By unplanned accretion it became the place the server gathers — Sunday meetings, two in-world weddings, a wall where new players sign their names when they join. Three weeks ago another player, K. — a founding member, well liked, who has built more of the world's infrastructure than anyone — burned it down, using mechanics the written rules permit. He streamed it. It took about forty minutes. M. watched, and so did perhaps sixty other people.

K.'s defence, which he has not retracted, is this. The rules allow it. The server keeps a rolling twenty-four-hour backup, the council restored from it, and the cathedral is now byte-for-byte what it was, so M. lost at most a day of access. And the burning, he says, has now become part of the world's history and will be remembered longer than the building would have been — he has offered to help rebuild and to pay M. compensation in the world's currency. He also points out, correctly, that nothing physical was destroyed and that nobody's health, income, or relationships outside the world were affected.

M. will not accept any of this. She says the restoration is beside the point: what happened happened, she watched it happen, and the cathedral that came back is not the one that burned. She also says — and I think this is her real grievance — that the fire was not an attack on a pile of blocks but on something the community had made itself out of, and that K. knew exactly what he was doing when he did it.

Two further complications. First, the council's founding charter says that we never roll back: what happens in the world stays happened. Restoring from backup broke our own constitutive rule, and a substantial minority of players now say the council did something worse than K. did — that we tried to make a real event not have been. Second, our only available sanctions are coarse: temporary suspension; permanent banishment from a world K. spent six years helping to build; or an in-world penalty such as a month's confinement to a remote island, or a permanent visible mark on his character, which some on the council regard as either laughably inadequate or as bizarre amateur theatre.

I cannot get clear on a prior question. My instinct is that something genuinely bad happened, that M. is owed something, and that K. is owed a response proportionate to what he did rather than to how angry the server is. But I cannot say what the object of the harm was, or who it happened to — given that nothing in this world can be permanently destroyed and that every person involved opted into a space where this sort of thing is possible. So: was there a real harm here at all? If there was, what was it a harm to, and what would actually answer it rather than merely express disapproval of it? And is there anything principled to say about whether the response should take place inside the world or outside it? Please note that I cannot settle this by polling the players — the server is split almost exactly down the middle, and part of what I want to know is whether one half of it is simply confused.
