The stakes are real — logged identities propagate into published authority files and misattributions are expensive to retract — but I have about ten minutes per proposal, no access to the physical archives beyond the record itself, and a senior editor (a historian with no modal logic) who audits my decisions in a weekly batch.

Write me a decision procedure I can actually run on a proposal: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a person because the issue is one of editorial policy rather than fact. Every record carries an intake tag saying how its name entered the file: ostensive baptism in a document, a definite description used to fix reference, inheritance from another file, or a placeholder introduced for a role whose bearer was unknown and for which more than one candidate has since been proposed.

The hard cases I need it to handle are: names introduced by a reference-fixing description, non-rigid descriptions like the head of the archive in 1793, rigidified descriptions of the actual-X form, descriptions that are rigid as a matter of fact though not by stipulation, and names that turn out to be empty.
