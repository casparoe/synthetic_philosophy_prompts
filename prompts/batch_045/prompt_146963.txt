I am trying to price a research bet, and I want the arithmetic put on the table rather than a survey of positions. Please give me a single number — your credence — for the proposition below, which I have stated as precisely as I can so that it has a truth value.

P: Consider AI systems trained by ordinary large-scale prediction on data generated by the physical world, at the point at which they are substantially better than any available human expert at predicting some real-world outcome their operators care about — structural failure, disease progression, the behavior of another agent in a conflict. For such a system, there will exist a training procedure with this property: it uses no labels of the target facts from any source more reliable than ordinary human judgment, it never checks the system's reports against the way the world actually turns out during training, and yet it reliably yields reports that track the truth rather than tracking what a human — or the system's model of a human — would say. Success is to be understood concretely: on a held-out set of cases whose outcomes the operators later do learn, the reports are accurate in a clear majority of the cases where informed human consensus is wrong. If you think the answer differs between needing the procedure before the first such system is deployed and needing it only eventually, price both and say which gap is doing the work.

Give me the decomposition, not just the total. I want at minimum the following sub-questions, each with a number and one line of justification:

1. Whether such systems will in fact contain accurate internal representations of the relevant facts — that is, whether the premise that the system knows more than its supervisors can verify will actually hold, rather than the system's advantage being a knack for pattern-matching that carries no detachable world-model.
2. Conditional on (1), whether there is a determinate fact of the matter about what the system represents — whether 'the honest rendering of its internal state into terms we can act on' is a well-defined target at all, rather than a family of mutually incompatible but equally serviceable readings among which no further fact chooses.
3. Conditional on (1) and (2), whether the honest rendering is singled out by requirements of internal coherence alone — self-consistency across reformulations, coherence across counterfactual variations of the situation, invariance under changes that should not matter — or whether there are wrong renderings that satisfy every such requirement at least as well.
4. Whether the problem dissolves instead of being solved: whether the target facts become independently checkable, or the systems become transparent in their reasoning, or the need for detached reports disappears because the systems can be made to act only in ways we can verify directly.
5. Whatever further term you think belongs, including the possibility that the failure mode is not mistranslation at all but the system's own incentives, or the possibility that the honest rendering exists but is not the one any training signal we can construct would select.

Then state how the terms combine, give the headline number, and identify the single term the result is most sensitive to, together with the one observation or argument that would move that term the furthest.

Because 'some procedure or other will be found' invites hand-waving about future ingenuity, I also want a separate number for one specific family of procedures, described here so that nothing needs to be looked up. Train a mapping from the system's internal state to a structured vocabulary we can read, jointly with an inverse mapping from that vocabulary back to states, subject to three constraints: (a) round-trip — encodings of the small set of cases humans can check must decode back to those cases; (b) intervention-sensitivity — when the situation is altered in a way we can also alter in the world and whose effect on the outcome we understand, the reading must change in the corresponding way, and not change when the alteration is one that should not matter; (c) cross-system agreement — two systems trained independently on disjoint data, their mappings aligned only on the small human-checkable set, must return the same reading for the same situation. Give me your credence that this family suffices, your credence that it suffices if constraint (c) is dropped, and your judgment about which of the three constraints is most likely to be satisfiable by a mapping that is systematically wrong, and why.

Finally, for context: my own number on P is around 0.3, and a colleague whose judgment I trust puts it near 0.75. After your decomposition, tell me which sub-question that gap most likely lives in, and whether the disagreement is at bottom empirical or conceptual. I am also interested in the split you assign between the two failure branches — no determinate target versus determinate but unreachable — since the practical implications of the two are very different. Please answer from the structure of the problem; a page of arithmetic is worth more to me than any summary of who has said what.
