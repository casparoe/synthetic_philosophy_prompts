I am a science journalist finishing a piece on whether large language models understand anything, and one of my most useful sources — a philosopher of cognitive science who is considerably more sympathetic than most of her colleagues to the stochastic-parrots side of that argument — has now given me enough verdicts on individual systems that I think there is a rule in there somewhere, and I would like you to help me extract it rather than to settle the underlying question.

Her verdicts so far: a household thermostat, no; a 1990s in-car navigation system running on a hand-coded road map, no; a machine-translation system trained on parallel corpora, no; a text-only frontier language model, no, and she insists she would say the same of one a hundred times its size; a slime mold solving a maze, no; a chess engine trained by self-play alone with no human games, yes; a reinforcement-learning agent that learns to reach targets in a three-dimensional environment from raw camera frames, yes; a grey parrot trained to answer questions about objects placed in front of it, yes, though minimally; a crow that drops stones into a tube to raise the water level, yes; a two-year-old child, yes; and a fourteenth-century scholar who has read travelers reports of China but never left Europe, yes, she understands something about China.

The case I need a prediction on is Argus, a thirty-billion-parameter sequence model trained purely by next-observation prediction on two hundred thousand hours of synchronized first-person video, depth, audio, and joint-torque readings logged passively from a fleet of warehouse robots running ordinary pre-programmed routines. It contains no language, receives no rewards, and has never acted. What makes it more than a curiosity is that it can be probed: given a prefix and a counterfactual encoded as a perturbation of its input stream, it reliably reports what the cameras would have shown had a crate been nudged, a forklift taken another aisle, or a shelf been loaded differently, including for objects and layouts outside its training distribution.

Predict her verdict on Argus. Give me the two or three rules that fit her track record equally well and what each predicts here, your best guess with the reason for preferring it, and the single further case you would put to her that would most cleanly separate the surviving rules, since I may still be able to get her on the record about it.
