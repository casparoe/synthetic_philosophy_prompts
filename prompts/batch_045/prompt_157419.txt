Had a three-hour argument with my friend last night about the colour phi illusion and I'm still chewing on it, so here goes.

Set-up: a red dot flashes at one spot, then a fraction of a second later a green dot flashes at a spot a few degrees away. What you see is a single dot travelling from the first spot to the second, and it looks like the dot changes colour partway along the path — before the green dot has actually been presented. So your visual system seems to report a colour change that, on the stimulus timeline, hadn't happened yet at the time you saw it.

Two classical stories about this. Orwellian: you really do have an experience of the unrevised sequence, and then, after the fact, the record of that experience is rewritten, so you end up remembering the colour change that never occurred. Stalinesque: nothing unrevised is ever experienced; the content is doctored before it reaches consciousness at all — verdict first, trial staged afterwards. My friend's line, which I think is basically Dennett's from the early 90s, is that this is a dead question: the two stories posit identical neural events, agreeing all the way down about where and when the erroneous content enters the causal pathways, and differ only about whether to label that location pre-experiential or post-experiential. No possible evidence tells between them, the appearance of a real question is manufactured by the Cartesian theatre, and anyone still arguing about it hasnt noticed they're doing Cartesian materialism. Thirty-odd years, no movement.

I don't think it's dead, but I also can't say what we were actually disagreeing about, and that's what I want from you. Three things came up that I couldn't settle.

One: there's a third option we kept skipping past — maybe nothing gets revised because there is no unrevised draft. Motion and colour processing interpolate and extrapolate as a matter of course (think of the flash-lag effect, or the way a moving target overshoots where it actually vanished), so the experience simply is of a dot changing colour, and calling that a revision smuggles in a fake baseline of two discrete flashes. But then I worry that this third option isn't a third option at all, just Stalinesque with friendlier branding.

Two: we argued about whether an experiment could break the tie — interrupt the trial mid-sequence with a mask or a pulse, or get the subject to respond to the first flash at a delay shorter than the second, and read out the content at that moment. My friend said no, any probe is itself another stimulus that gets drafted in, and any report comes out after the green flash anyway, so you can never step outside the drafts to time them. I half-agree, but I think the sharper point is different: you can measure whether the content at a time depends on input that arrives later, and it does, and that still doesn't discriminate the two stories. Is that right, or is measurement of that integration window secretly the whole substance of the dispute, with the Orwellian/Stalinesque framing just decoration?

Three, my actual hypothesis: the two sides are using different senses of experienced at a time. One is about availability — the content is there for report and action systems at that moment — and about availability I think my friend is just right that the two stories collapse. The other is about presence, whether there is something it is like for the subject then, and presence is defined as what's accessible from the inside, so of course it won't answer to third-person evidence; the residual intuition that there must still be a difference is an intuition about presence. If that's right the dispute isn't about the timing of anything, it's a metaphysical or methodological disagreement about whether facts about what it's like have to be facts about what's available, wearing an empirical question about a visual illusion as a costume.

Is it verbal, substantive but in principle unresolvable, two different questions sharing a name, or an ill-posed dichotomy that only looks like a choice if you grant a single finish line where content becomes conscious? Is the no-revision option genuinely distinct, and would any probe or integration-window result count as progress or only as an answer to a neighbouring question? Given your diagnosis, what would progress even look like — a distinction, an experiment, giving up the question — and is progress to be expected at all?
