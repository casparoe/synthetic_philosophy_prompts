I'm a journalist — I mostly cover science and ideas — and I've got a piece on the go that started out being about expertise and has drifted into a much older question: is knowing something actually worth more than merely getting it right? I'm talking about the puzzle from Plato's Meno, the one where the challenge is to say what knowledge adds over true belief, given that both supposedly get you to where you're going. I don't want a survey of the literature; I want your number.

Here's the proposition, stated as carefully as I can manage:

P: There is some value that knowledge has over merely true belief, over and above the value of the true belief itself. Understood like this: take two agents, one who knows that p and one who truly but non-knowingly believes that p, and hold fixed everything about p's practical payoff — both act successfully, neither's situation differs in any other way. In that comparison, the knower's state is in some genuine respect better.

Give me your credence in P. Skip the "it depends what you mean by knowledge" reflex — or rather, if that really is load-bearing, say so, but then also give me a number conditional on your best reading and tell me how much the reading is worth. What I actually want is the decomposition: the sub-questions the number rides on, a rough probability for each, and how you're combining them (don't just multiply them together if that isn't what you're really doing). Here are the ones I've got, add or collapse as you see fit:

1. Is the comparison even well formed? Can we really hold true belief fixed and vary only the knowledge-ish features, or does every way of setting that up smuggle in extra practical differences?
2. The swamping objection: if knowledge is reliably produced true belief, and reliability matters only because it delivers true beliefs, then once the true belief is in hand the extra looks worthless. How likely is it that this can be answered?
3. Modal accounts — safety, stability across nearby circumstances: is a belief that would have survived small changes worth something even for one-shot action, via dispositions or downstream counterfactuals?
4. Credit or achievement accounts: is there value in getting it right because of your own competence rather than by luck?
5. The redirect: maybe the Meno question has the wrong target, and what's really valuable is understanding — grasping how things hang together — with knowledge valuable only when it comes packaged with that.

Then there's a case I keep turning over, and I want you to deal with it rather than file it. Ada has a perfect index. She acts on the outputs and everything goes right. She can't tell you why any of them is true, and had the oracle been wrong about one she'd have been wrong too, but it never was. Call her a knower or not as you like; the point is she has maximal reliability and zero grasp.
