Not sure whether this is a philosophy question or an HR question, but here goes. I'm on a small committee at the company where I work (~800 people) that runs our 'high potential' program for junior staff: each year we pick about fifteen people under thirty, and those people get the stretch assignments, the exec mentors and the funded training budget. I've been on it three years. What's bugging me is this: the people we pick really do visibly out-perform the people we didn't pick two years later, better reviews, faster promotions — but of course they do, they got the good projects and someone senior advocating for them. So the stat that gets reported upward ('the program has an 80% hit rate') seems to me to be partly measuring the program's own effects instead of our skill at picking, and I can't get anyone else on the committee to see why that bothers me. One colleague says who cares, it's a tool, the only question is whether spending the money on these fifteen produces more value than spending it on anyone else, and that asking whether 'potential' is real is empty. Another says the whole thing is made up, there's no such thing as potential, we're just laundering the fact that we like people who sound like us.

Now there's a proposal I have to vote on next week, and I have to say something. Someone read a bit about self-fulfilling prophecy stuff and suggested we make the list secret — the fifteen wouldn't be told they're on it, though their managers still would be, since managers are the ones handing out the assignments — on the theory that then we avoid inflating their self-image and avoid making everyone else feel written off. My instinct is that this misses most of the problem: the mechanism I actually worry about runs through how other people treat you, not through how you think of yourself, so a secret list might be worse, not better, since if you at least know you were picked you can discount it. But I really can't tell whether I'm right about that or whether I'm just being contrary.

And the bit I keep going in circles on: my colleague says that if 'potential' is something we invented, we can simply invent a better version of it — pick at random from the eligible pool, or rotate the resources so everyone gets a turn and we stop pretending we can tell. That sounds absurd to me and I can't say why. If the category is ours, why isn't it ours to change? And relatedly, if the label is what produces the thing it names, does that make the label false, or just lucky, or neither? I did one undergraduate philosophy course about a decade ago so I can handle being told I'm conflating two things, and please don't give me a reading list, I'm not going to read it. What should I actually argue in the meeting, and what am I missing?
