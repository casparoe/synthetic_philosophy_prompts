Last week's discussion went badly. Half the class decided this was just skepticism about other minds in a costume, the other half thought it was a joke because obviously it's a rabbit, and my flatmate who does actual field linguistics says no working linguist is ever in this position because you end up with years of data and eventually a bilingual speaker. So I want them to run into the problem before I name it: no lecture up front, and I'd rather the word inscrutability doesn't leave my mouth until the last fifteen minutes.

What I have in mind, and you can redesign it if there's something better: an invented field site (call it the Emba valley, language Embe), with me as the informant sitting behind a cardboard screen playing from a lookup table I've built in advance, allowed to produce only two tokens — a grunt for assent and a head-click for dissent — and nothing else at all, no nodding, no explaining, no matter what they ask. The class is split into four teams of three or four. Phase one: they get a deck of about 40 index cards, each with a described stimulus occasion plus the recorded response — rabbit in the open, a rabbit's ear with the rest hidden behind a rock, a severed rabbit foot, a rabbit darting behind a bush and reappearing, a rabbit seen again the next day with the query that they'll want to translate as same one again, two rabbits at once, a rabbit carcass, and so on. They score, and the sting is that they all score perfectly. Phase three: teams swap manuals and each has to design a field procedure that would break the other team's manual — a new query, a new set-up — and every procedure gets the same answer under both manuals. Phase four, which is the bit I care most about: team D is privately instructed to build a manual that keeps every single prediction while moving the whole apparatus at once, i.e. it doesn't just swap gavagai for a part-word but reinterprets part of, same, and there is together to compensate, a package deal rather than a one-word switch.
