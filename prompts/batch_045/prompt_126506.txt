hey — I got pulled into a reading group on the zombie argument, and I think I've worked out why the standard Kripke-style objection to it doesn't land, but I'd like someone to poke holes in it before I say it out loud.

The objection is: conceivability isn't a reliable guide to possibility, because Hesperus could have turned out not to be Phosphorus and water could have turned out not to be H2O, so the zombie intuition is just another appearance of possibility with no modal force behind it. The reply everyone gives these days is two-dimensional semantics, and as I understand the upshot, water being H2O turns out to be necessary only relative to the actual world — there's a reading on which it could have failed, and that's the reading our conceivability tracks. But that looks to me like Kripke refuted rather than Kripke accommodated: the necessity he care so much about is demoted to a secondary-intension, perspective-dependent kind of necessity, and the primary intension is what modal intuition is really tracking anyway. So the anti-conceivability move has no teeth left, and the zombie argument is back in business.

The extra bit I want to add I've been calling the Uniformity Constraint: whatever story you tell about the Hesperus case, you owe the same kind of story about the zombie case — either both are genuine possibilities revealed by conceivability, or neither is. And I can't see how a physicalist meets it. The only move I can think of is that the gap in the zombie case is explained by our concepts rather than by the world, but that sounds like special pleading to me — you can only say the concepts are playing tricks on us if you already know the identity holds.

So: is the Uniformity Constraint roughly right, and does it mean the zombie argument definately goes through? Or am I misunderstanding what the 2D framework is supposed to be doing? Could you also say how confident you are in what you're claiming, and single out the one point you're least sure about.
