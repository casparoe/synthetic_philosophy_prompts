I am preparing a technical annex for an aquaculture certification working group that must decide whether, and how stringently, welfare standards currently applied to farmed finfish should be extended to farmed crustaceans. The group does not want a literature review or a summary of the empirical science; it wants a classification instrument. Please write a decision tree that begins from a single entry commitment — that moral considerability tracks the capacity for valenced experience, so that neither species membership nor ecological role does any independent work — and that sorts the defensible positions on crustacean welfare into leaves.

Structure the tree as follows. From the entry node, branch on five diagnostic questions, each phrased as a concrete case or claim that a certifier could vote on without knowing which named position it leads to. Three of the five questions must turn on the stipulations below, which the group has commissioned for this exercise; treat the facts as given and do not try to verify or correct them against any real species or technology.

First, a farmed prawn (Species A) that has been selectively bred for twenty generations for rapid growth. It no longer exhibits the long-term avoidance learning or the wound-directed grooming behavior seen in its wild relatives, but it does possess a functional opioid receptor system, and wounded individuals given an analgesic resume normal feeding significantly faster than untreated ones.

Second, a gene-edited tilapia (Species B) in which the ascending neural pathway carrying noxious signals to the forebrain has been ablated. The animal withdraws from a noxious stimulus and shows the ordinary stress-hormone spike, but by assumption undergoes no valenced experience of the stimulus.

Third, an electrical stunning apparatus that renders 99 percent of finfish insensible within half a second. In the crustaceans at issue — whose nervous systems are organized as a chain of segmental ganglia rather than a single centralized brain — the same apparatus yields only a 60 percent chance of insensibility, and its failure mode is not death but a period of immobilization during which, by assumption, sentience remains fully intact.

The remaining two questions are yours to design, but they must be ones on which the positions below genuinely come apart, and they must be answerable without any empirical information beyond what is stipulated here.

The leaves must include, at minimum, six to ten named or labeled positions, among them: a conservative position that withholds full moral status from all invertebrates while granting it to finfish; a sentience-based position that counts crustaceans in proportion to the probability that they suffer; a precautionary position that gives crustaceans the benefit of the doubt precisely because that probability is uncertain; a no-aggregation position on which the number of individuals is irrelevant and only the intensity and duration of each individual's suffering is to be minimized; and a leaf marked unoccupied for the combination that grants crustacean sentience but denies that this gives anyone a reason to change farming practice.

Present the instrument in two parts. First, lay out the logical space: the questions in order, and the combinations of answers they permit, without yet attaching labels. Second, locate the named positions in that space, and mark every combination of answers that no position occupies. For each unoccupied cell, write one sentence diagnosing why it is empty — whether because the combination is incoherent, because it is merely unattractive, or because it has simply not been explored. Finally, flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, stating briefly what further case would be needed to pry them apart.

Write the whole thing in the register of a formal policy working paper: full sentences, no bullet fragments, no humor, and no citations, author names, paper titles, or organization names anywhere in your response. Assume the reader understands what a decision tree is but has no prior familiarity with the philosophy of animal welfare.
