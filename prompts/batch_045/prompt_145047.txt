My brother and I have been arguing about where covid came from since like 2020 and it hasn't moved an inch — we've both read the same papers, we both know the early cases clustered around the Huanan market, that no close relative of the virus was ever published, that this kind of lab has leaked before, and that most new human viruses come from animals, and we still sit at wildly different confidences. Someone told me this is precisely what Aumann's agreement theorem says can't happen, so I want the careful mapping: treat the two camps as the agents, lay out what the shared evidence and the shared space of possibilities actually were, and tell me which assumption really broke — the common prior (we clearly picked different reference classes for the base rate), common knowledge of the evidence (half our arguments are about whether the missing precursor virus or the Chinese government's stonewalling even counts as evidence), or something else. Two things specifically: (a) the theorem is about posteriors, not about what people say out loud, and public statements here were cheap talk with career and political stakes — does it even have jurisdiction over the visible disagreement? and (b) if you measured disagreement by willingness to bet instead of stated odds, would the violation shrink, grow, or become unmeasurable? Then the reverse direction: does this episode vindicate the theorem as a diagnostic tool, or show that once priors and evidence-counting are allowed to differ it can't say anything about real disputes?
