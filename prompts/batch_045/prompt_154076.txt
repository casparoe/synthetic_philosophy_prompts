I host a small philosophy podcast, and my guest next week has written a book arguing that Hofstadter's Aunt Hillary — an ant colony from the Ant Fugue — is not a metaphor for a mind but a minded subject in her own right, and that Dennett's hierarchy of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian and Gregorian creatures misclassifies her: the ants are only Skinnerian, but the colony, on her account, is Popperian, because trail-laying lets it kill off candidate routes before committing to any of them. Draft eight to ten questions that don't let her simply restate the thesis. I want at least one question each on: whether colony-level mentality needs anything functioning like a global workspace or central bottleneck, or whether massively parallel, stigmergic processing with no locus of control can already make the intentional stance predictive rather than merely bookkeeping; the temporal-grain worry, given that a colony's decisions take minutes and its pheromone fields integrate over hours — what fixes the temporal grain of a mind, and could Aunt Hillary ever think about an event shorter than her own decision latency; the Dennett placement itself, since if the nest architecture and trail network are doing the representational work, this looks less like Popperian internal hypothesis-testing and more like Gregorian use of an external mind tool, and I want her to say whether her account secretly needs her to be Gregorian; and the fission case — if the colony were divided into two viable halves that later re-merge, does Aunt Hillary become two subjects and then one again, and how does that differ from the ordinary case in which the queen dies and every worker is replaced within a season. Give each question a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to make. She is very practiced at the move that you are only redescribing the same physical events at a coarser grain, and that one could say the same thing about you and your neurons; I would like the sequence to make that reply progressively harder to lean on, and to close with a question about what observation would count against her claim that the colony, rather than its ants or its environment, is the locus of the mind.
