a guy in my cohort handed me this last week and I keep failing to find the bad step. Setup: Nozick-style tracking, method-relative — S knows that p iff (i) p is true, (ii) S believes p, (iii) were p false, S wouldn't believe p via the method M she actually uses, (iv) were p true and she to use M, she would believe p. Plus single-premise closure: if S knows p, knows that p entails q, and comes to believe q by competently deducing it from p, then S knows q.

(1) I know there is a mug on my desk (p). Tracking: no mug there, and my visual method doesn't deliver that belief.
(2) I know that p entails not-H, where H is the hypothesis that I'm a mugless envatted brain being fed mug-experiences.
(3) So by closure I know not-H.
(4) But tracking on not-H: were not-H false, i.e. were H true, my experience would be exactly as it is, so I'd believe not-H anyway. Condition (iii) fails, so I don't know not-H.
(5) So tracking and closure can't both hold. The usual story is that Nozick drops closure — we know we have hands, we don't know we're not BIVs — and that's supposed to be the interesting bit.
(6) Here's where I think it gets away from us. The version of (iii) people actually write down is: were p false and S to use M to arrive at a belief whether p, S wouldn't believe p via M. Read not-H that way: in the nearest H-world I have no eyes and no mug, so I can't use vision at all; the "were S to use M" antecedent isn't satisfiable, so (iii) comes out vacuously true, and tracking plus closure deliver that I do know not-H.
(7) Same move for every skeptical hypothesis whose truth removes the method I actually use — the demon, the five-minute-old world, the simulation. So tracking can't give Nozick's asymmetry at all: read one way it's skeptical, read the other way it hands me knowledge of the denial of every skeptical hypothesis.
(8) And (6) proves more than that. Once M can be described so that it essentially involves p's being true — seeing that p, remembering that p, being reliably told that p — (iii) is automatic for any true perceptual or memory belief, including the Gettier and fake-barn cases tracking was built to rule out. The sensitivity test stops doing any work.

The conclusion I can't accept is that there's no formulation of the theory that keeps both closure and its anti-luck bite. If I have to choose I'd rather keep closure and the fake-barn verdict than the letter of (iii). Which step is doing the damage? And if it's the method-relativization, what do I pay for dropping it — does plain unrelativized sensitivity just buy me the familiar problem that I don't know anything about the unobserved present?
