I moderate a large public peer-support forum for people with a chronic condition. Members trade treatment experiences, and a real number of them make real medical decisions on the strength of what they read here. About once or twice a month my volunteer moderator team — no formal training, an hour or two per decision, access to nothing but what is publicly posted — has to decide whether a particular subforum has developed an epistemic problem and, if so, what to do. Interventions are expensive: clumsy ones trigger mass exits and confirm whatever the members already suspected about us.

Please write the procedure my team should run on a proposal to intervene. Ground it in the distinction now standard in social epistemology between an epistemic bubble and an echo chamber. In a bubble, members are missing relevant evidence and argument because of structural filtering — whom they follow, what the ranking algorithm shows them, whose word they take for what is worth reading — not because they distrust outside sources; they generally have no settled view of those sources, and supplying the missing material through a voice they already trust tends to work. In an echo chamber, members have met the outside sources and have been given, in advance, reasons to discredit them; the discrediting is general rather than claim-by-claim, it is part of what membership consists in, and pressing more outside material at them tends to be absorbed as further evidence of the corruption of the outside. Escape then requires rebuilding trust in some specific outside voice, which the moderators cannot do themselves, since we are already inside the discrediting story.

The complication I want the procedure to handle is a pattern I keep seeing that fits neither description. Call it translation loss for now: the outside material is present in the thread, the sources are cited approvingly and trusted, nobody is accused of bad faith — but the community has a standing rule that an outside finding must be re-expressed in the community's own framework before it counts as understood, and the re-expression systematically downgrades the finding's strength. No benefit on average becomes benefit for a subtype like us; probably false becomes emerging; the trial was underpowered becomes the trial was too small to pick up the effect we are all seeing. Information is present, trust is intact, and nothing updates.

Requirements:

1. An ordered set of questions, each with an explicit statement of what counts as a sufficient answer, using only evidence available from public posts. The procedure must be run per topic, not per community, since the same subforum may be a bubble about one treatment and a chamber about another.
2. Observable markers that discriminate the three patterns, and an explicit stop rule for when the markers conflict or come out mixed.
3. Clear stopping points: where the procedure returns a no, where it returns do nothing, and where it hands the decision to a person because the remaining question is one of values rather than fact. Include exposure is contraindicated here as a possible outcome, with the reasoning.
4. An intervention menu keyed to the diagnosis, each entry saying what it costs and what it would look like if it were working.
5. Everything must be defensible publicly to the members, so no step may rest on private data or on claims about members' psychology that could not be supported from the posts themselves.
6. Honesty about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure, and about what this procedure cannot see at all — I am thinking of a chamber that has learned to perform openness, and of a bubble that is perfectly fine and should be left alone.

Then run the finished procedure on three cases and keep the runs in character: (a) a subforum where mainstream clinical guidance never appears, where a member who once linked a national health service page got a couple of polite replies and no hostility, and where people say things like my specialist never mentioned that; (b) a treatment-skeptical parenting group that quotes official health guidance at length and at its own initiative, mainly to show that the guidance convicts itself, with a shared vocabulary for reading official statements and a detailed account of why every relevant body is compromised; (c) the translation-loss pattern above, in a subforum built around a single explanatory framework for the condition, where high-quality evidence is posted and praised every week and the community's restatements of it reliably remove its force.

Be committed throughout. Take a definite position at every branch, give your reasons, and where the diagnosis is genuinely uncertain mark your confidence and say what would settle it. In particular, tell me plainly whether my third pattern is a genuine third failure mode or whether it collapses into one of the other two — if it collapses, say which, why, and what follows for what I should do about it, rather than manufacturing a category to accommodate me. Include at least one case where the procedure tells me to stop or do nothing despite my instinct to intervene, and do not quietly rescue that case to make the procedure look better than it is. Please write in American English.
