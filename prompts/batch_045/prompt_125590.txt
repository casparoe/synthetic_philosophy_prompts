I have taught promissory obligation for years, and I no longer trust the pattern of my own verdicts, so I should like you to do the reflective-equilibrium work on them rather than to report what anyone has said about promising.

(i) I promise a graduate student a reference; the conversation slips his mind entirely and he never follows it up. Bound — I write it.
(ii) A colleague to whom I have promised a co-authored paper quietly works behind the scenes to have me dropped from the project. Not bound.
(iii) My dying mother extracts, by an emotional pressure I would hesitate to call coercion, a promise that I will never sell the house. Bound, and yet I should think far worse of myself for breaking (i) than for breaking this.
(iv) I promise my partner never to read her letters. She dies; reading them proves to be the only way to finish the biography I also promised her I would write. I cannot shake the sense that the first promise still binds, even if it must give way.
(v) I promise "the department" at a meeting that I shall take on a burdensome committee role; three years on, every individual present has been replaced. Bound, I think — but bound to what?
(vi) A friend privately decides he no longer wants me to keep a promise and says nothing, from awkwardness. Bound: release must be communicated.
(vii) At a ceremony whose point I do not grasp I repeat words in a language I do not speak, and no one present can say what was promised to whom. Not bound.

The pair I cannot reconcile is (ii) and (vi): in each the promisee holds an attitude of which I know nothing, yet the covert sabotage releases me and the covert release does not.

My tentative principle, offered to be tested and repaired rather than defended: a promise binds just when, through an act of uptake, the promisee has acquired a claim on my acting for the reason that I said I would, and that claim is defeated only by — and here I do not know how to finish. Take it through all seven cases, state the finished principle precisely enough that a new case could be decided under it mechanically, tell me which clause each case forces, name the verdict it cannot save, and then derive its verdicts on three cases I have not listed: a promise made to myself, a promise whose content I misunderstood at the time of making it, and a promise I made intending not to keep it but later performed.

Please answer in British English.
