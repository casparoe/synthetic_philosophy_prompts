Posting this ahead of my reading group on thursday — we're doing the 'is the brain a computer' question and I was asked to write down my own position first so everyone else can poke at it. I definately want a consistency check before I stand up and present it. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(a) 'The brain computes' is a claim about explanation, not about what the brain is essentially. To say a circuit performs computation X is to say there's a description of its activity with the structure of X, and that this is part of why the animal can do the thing it can do. No extra metaphysics, no claim about the soul-stuff of the tissue.

(b) But I don't want to say computation is just in the eye of the beholder either. I reject the old argument that basically any physical system implements any computation given a sufficiently sneaky mapping. I think there's a fact of the matter about whether a piece of cortex is really doing something multiplication-shaped as opposed to us being able to find a multiplication-shaped description of it.

(c) I also think the interesting neural computation isn't digital. Graded potentials, population rate codes, dendritic nonlinearities, neuromodulation turning the gain up and down on whole circuits — continuous, messy, noisy. When people say the brain is a computer they're picturing something like a CPU and I think that picture is doing all the work and is wrong.

(d) On consciousness I want to stay agnostic about whether computation explains it, but I do believe something like substrate independence: if you get the functional organisation right — really right, not merely right-enough-for-behaviour — you get the experiences too.

(e) And I'm suspicious of the level-skipping that (d) invites. Concretely, imagine three replacement systems for a patch of visual cortex. System 1 reproduces every spike time of the original tissue. System 2 reproduces only the population-level signal that actually predicts the animal's behaviour, and nothing finer. System 3 replaces each neuron with a small lookup table trained to match that neuron's input-output behaviour over the range of inputs it actually received during a year of normal life — so it tracks the real thing everywhere in that range and diverges wildly off-distribution. My instinct: 1 is doing the same computation as the tissue, 2 isn't, and 3 is a case where I have no idea what to say. But that instinct seems to commit me to there being a privileged grain of description, and I can't see where the grain comes from given (a), or how you'd find it objectively given (b), or how fine it has to be before (d) is safe.

Which of these are actually in tension? Someone told me (a) and (b) can't both be true and that feels wrong to me — tell me if I'm kidding myself there, or whether it's the other way round and (a) quietly dissolves the worry behind (b). Then rank the real tensions by how bad they are, say for each what I'd have to give up and what it would cost me, and tell me which single revision to the package cleans up the most. Please don't just hand me a reading list, I'm already behind on the reading.
