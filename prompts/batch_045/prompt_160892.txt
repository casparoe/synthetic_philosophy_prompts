He says you don't need any of Hempel's fancy consequence conditions to get "everything confirms everything" — plain instance-confirmation plus equivalence does it on its own. Now define two predicates: F(x) means "x is this particular shoe I'm holding, or Z is false," and G(x) means "x is this particular shoe I'm holding, or Z is true." I look at the shoe. It is F, and it is G, and I know both of those without having the faintest idea whether Z is true. So by Nicod's condition my observation confirms "all Fs are Gs." But "all Fs are Gs" comes out true if Z is true and false if Z is false (as long as there's at least one object in the universe besides the shoe), so it's equivalent to Z. So by equivalence, looking at my shoe is evidence that the Cavs win in 2031. Z was arbitrary. Any observation confirms anything.
