I've been going back and forth on scalable oversight, and I'd like a comparison of two protocols rather than another restatement of why debate is elegant.

Protocol A is the familiar one: two agents alternate short statements on a question; a judge who is weaker than either of them reads a bounded transcript and picks a side; at training time that verdict is the signal.

Protocol B is A with a settlement clause. Anything a debater says that goes beyond framing the issue is logged as a commitment with an attached check — a prediction, a claimed result of some cheap test, a specific claim about what an independent referee would find within, say, a week — and a fixed share of the payoff is withheld until those commitments are cashed out. A debater can still win over the judge in the room; they just can't leave the ledger behind.

What I want compared is how each handles the obfuscated arguments problem. I suspect the two protocols are aimed at different diagnoses of it, which is why I can't work out which is better:

(1) Is the failure that the judge cannot detect obfuscation at the time, or that a liar can generate indefinitely many locally plausible claims at no cost? The first diagnosis makes B look like the obvious patch. The second makes it look like B has merely relocated the judgeship onto whoever verifies the ledger, and will inherit the same problem one level up, plus the extra problem that the verifier has no adversary.

(2) The usual case for A is that truth has an advantage in argument: there is often a short, decisive, checkable consideration on the true side, while the liar has to keep talking. Does B preserve that advantage or quietly trade it away? Cashing out commitments favors claims that resolve quickly and cheaply, and the questions we most want oversight on — whether a plan is safe, whether a proof sketch is sound, what we ought to do — are arguably the ones with nothing to wait for.

(3) A is a training signal, not just a verdict. Does a payoff that is delayed and only partially observed degrade that signal enough to outweigh A's clean but gameable one?

Which of the two degrades more gracefully as question difficulty passes the judge's competence, and what is the strongest argument against your own answer?
