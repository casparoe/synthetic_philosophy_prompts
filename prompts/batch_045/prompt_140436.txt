Someone in my ethics reading group insisted that the is-ought gap and the naturalistic fallacy are the same complaint wearing two different hats, and I half believed them at the time but it still feels off to me. Can you compare the two properly? My rough sense is that one is about derivation — no pile of purely descriptive premises gets you to a prescriptive conclusion unless you slip in something extra — while the other is about definitions, and that even after you've "fixed" the derivation by adding an explicit bridge premise (like you ought to do whatever maximizes pleasure), the second worry is still just standing there. Am I on the right track, where exactly do the two come apart, and is the word "fallacy" doing the same work in both? Also: does the reverse move — starting from an ought and ending up at an is, e.g. we ought to eliminate suffering so eliminating it must be possible — fall under either of them, or is that a seperate complaint entirely?
