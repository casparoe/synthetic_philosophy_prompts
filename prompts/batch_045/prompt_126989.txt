Last night I got into a three-hour argument with my friend Sam about whether anyone can really *know* there's an external world, and it ended with him literally holding up his hands and going "here is one hand ... and here is another", like that just settled it. His version is that it's a genuine proof: the premises are different from the conclusion, he knows the premises, and the conclusion follows — three conditions for a good proof that he swears come from some philosopher — so skepticism is over. I kept saying it feels like he's helping himself to the exact thing we were arguing about, but every time I said "that's begging the question" he'd push back and I couldn't say what precisely was wrong with it.

Two things I did manage to throw at him that he handled badly. First: if we really were envatted brains getting messed with by a demon, there would *still* be stuff existing outside our minds — the vat, the wiring, the demon, the server. So if the conclusion only means that something mind-independent exists, the skeptic can just go "yes, obviously" and the proof has established something nobody was denying. So what would "external world" have to mean for that gesture to actually hit the worry? Second: I tried running the same move on other cases — here is one memory, so the past exists; here is one person in pain, so other minds exist — and it felt a lot weaker there. Sam said those are totally different but couldn't tell me why, which makes me think something specific about the hand case is doing the work and not the gesture itself.

So: what are the distinct precise claims that holding up your hands and saying that could be making? Not just "it's a valid argument" — I mean the whole range from dead obvious to genuinely radical. My own guesses were: perceptual experience justifying the belief immediately, without needing a prior argument that your senses are reliable; perception being a direct relation to a mind-independent object rather than an inference from how things seem; the sentence not being a knowledge claim at all but one of those Wittgensteinian hinge things where the giving of reasons just stops; or it being a straight weighing of which thing is more plausible. I'm probly missing the interesting ones.

For each reading tell me whether it's trivial, substantive and defensible, or just false — and whether it would actually let him end the argument the way he wanted, or only sounds like an answer while really just restating his confidence. Then: which reading was he most likely reaching for, given that he thought one gesture closed the whole question, and which of my two objections really bites, and only against which readings? I'd personally be moved most by an account of which reading makes the gesture answer the vat worry instead of describing it away.
