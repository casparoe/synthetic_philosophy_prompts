I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about updating on uncertain evidence, so here are my gut verdicts on cases and I want the principle behind them if there is one.

1. Textbook case: prior of 0.3 on rain, I glance at the window in bad light and land at 0.6, and I'm happy to say the ratios inside the rain and no-rain cells stay where they were. Jeffrey conditioning (probability kinematics) looks exactly right here.
2. Two independent weak signals: one glance moves me to 0.6, and a friend who looked out a different window independently would also move me to 0.6. But applying the update twice does nothing the second time, which feels wrong — two independent readings should push me further than one. I want to combine the reports first, then update once.
3. Overlapping inputs: the app makes me 0.6 on rain, and separately the barometer makes me 0.8 on low pressure, and rain and low pressure overlap. Doing them in one order gives a different answer than the other order. My verdict is that order shouldn't matter — though a friend says it legitimately should, since the second input is new information and its total effect depends on where you already were, and I half believe him.
4. Rigidity fails: the same dim glance also teaches me something about how bad my eyes are in dim light, so P(glance | rain) moves too. Here I want to say: drop the shortcut, enlarge the space, condition on the whole experience.
5. Carving: the same glance described coarsely (red vs not red) versus finely (scarlet vs crimson vs other) gives different final numbers, and that seems like it shouldn't — it's the same information.

Is there one principle behind 1–5, something like "soft updating is only a computational shortcut for conditioning on your total evidence; use it when rigidity holds and model the source explicitly when it doesn't"? Or does that collapse into something vacuous? And what does it say about the one I keep flip-flopping on: my credence just drifts to 0.4 overnight with no reason I can point to at all. Should I update on that, or is it not evidence?
