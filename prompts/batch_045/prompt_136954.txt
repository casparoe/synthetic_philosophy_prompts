I keep going back and forth on whether "the brain is a computer" is even meant as a substantive claim. Depending on who's saying it, it either sounds trivial (pick the right mapping and any old physical system implements any old formalism, including its own dynamics) or obviously false (nothing is clocked, the states aren't discrete, no one wrote the program). Before I read more of the literature I want the spec: what would a theory of what it is for a physical system to implement a computation have to deliver, for the claim that the brain does to be one a working neuroscientist could either use or reject?

Give me the requirements, each with the case or worry behind it. Some I already think belong on the list and I want you to argue me out of any that are just camp prejudice: it has to survive the triviality worry (ruling out degenerate mappings somhow, without smuggling in what a designer meant the thing to do); it has to take a position on grain and timing (same computation if you halve the speed or coarsen the state partition?); it has to handle physical perturbations that don't correspond to any formal input; it has to say whether implementing is intrinsic to the system or only relative to a description, and if it's intrinsic, whether that's compatible with the computation not adding any causal power beyond the physics; and it has to say whether the states involved need to represent anything, or whether syntax alone is supposed to carry the explanatory load.

Then: which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of one camp, which pair or triple you suspect can't be jointly satisfied (this is the bit I care about most — if the list is unsatisfiable, then "is the brain a computer" just becomes a choice about which requirement to drop, and I'd rather know that up front), and which single requirement knocks out the most popular accounts. After that, in a couple of sentences each, how the systematic-mapping view, the mechanistic information-processing view, and the every-system-computes-everything view do against your list.
