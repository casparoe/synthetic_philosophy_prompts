I have been going back and forth with another longtime commenter about whether embedded agency is hard for principled reasons or merely for want of someone having the right idea yet, and I have noticed that we are both mostly asserting. I would like a number, and I would like to see the arithmetic that produces it.

The proposition, stated as precisely as I can manage:

**P:** The obstructions to a satisfying theory of embedded agency are contingent difficulties rather than principled limits. That is, there exists a formal account of rational agency for agents that are properly contained in, simulated by, and computationally exceeded by their own environments, which satisfies the following three desiderata jointly and without ad hoc exceptions:

1. **Grain-invariance.** The theory's prescriptions must not depend on where the agent/environment boundary is drawn along the causal chain between deliberation and outcome — whether one counts the agent's motor cortex, its hands, its tools, its detached notepad, or its later self as part of the agent. A theory that needs the boundary supplied from outside, and that returns different verdicts for different grainings of the same physical situation, does not count as a solution; it counts as a restatement of the problem one level down.
2. **Diagonal adequacy.** The theory must return sane verdicts where the environment contains a transparent and computationally adequate model of the agent: Newcomb-style predictors, environments that simulate the agent and reward or punish the simulation, agents confronting their own source code. It may not help itself to a hypothesis class containing both the environment and the agent itself, since that is the assumption whose failure is the whole difficulty.
3. **Borrowed substrate, non-circular.** Embedded agents routinely think partly outside their physical boundaries — via notebooks, institutions, other agents, tools. The theory must be able to count some external states as the agent's own memory or computation and others as environment, in a way that does not presuppose the very agent/environment distinction it was supposed to earn.

**Resolution condition.** I will count P as settled true if someone produces a framework that handles the standard pathological cases under all three desiderata and the people who work on those cases converge on it as adequate rather than as a patch; settled false if a no-go result shows the three cannot be had together, or if the consensus becomes that one of them was mis-specified. If neither happens, P is unresolved and I do not care about my number except as a record of my reasoning.

My credence in P is about 0.55. My interlocutor's is about 0.15, and I cannot tell whether we disagree about the world or about how demanding the desiderata should be.

Please give me your own number, decomposed. The nodes I want priced, at minimum:

- that the three desiderata are mutually consistent at all, i.e. that there is no hidden inconsistency of the kind that would make P false by theorem;
- that classical Bayesian/expected-utility machinery is the right base to repair rather than the wrong base to abandon, where repairing it means giving up the assumption that the truth is in the agent's hypothesis class, or that the agent can update on its own future;
- that 'agent' picks out something stance-relative rather than a natural kind, and — this is the term I am least sure how to price — what that does to P: if agency is a posit we choose for predictive convenience, does satisfying all three desiderata become easier, or does it stop being the kind of thing that can be satisfied?
- that joint satisfiability is compatible with the theory being usable by a bounded agent, rather than being a fixed point no one can compute;
- that the problem does not simply dissolve — that the systems we actually build and care about are genuinely better modeled with an agent boundary than without one.

Add whatever other terms you think belong. Give each a number and one line of justification, show how they combine, and if you think the terms are not independent, say where the dependence is and what it does to the product rather than multiplying anyway. Then tell me which single node the total is most sensitive to, which of the two of us at 0.55 and 0.15 is most likely mispricing that node, and what one piece of evidence — a no-go theorem, a working system, a reframing — would move your number furthest, and in which direction. I would keep desideratum 2 over desideratum 3 if forced to drop one, and I would like to know what that preference is costing me.
