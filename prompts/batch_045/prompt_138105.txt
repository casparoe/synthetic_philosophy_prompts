teaching parfit's nonidentity problem to undergrads next week and instead of a slide with bullet points i want to close the session with a poem. here's what im after:

a sestina — six stanzas of six lines, unrhymed, end-words rotating 6-1-5-2-4-3 (if stanza one ends 1 2 3 4 5 6, the next ends 6 1 5 2 4 3, and so on), then a three-line envoi carrying all six words, 39 lines total — spoken by a parent who did not wait, addressing the child she actually has.

the end-words: wait, name, wrong, better, her, none. your order.

the reason i want a sestina is that i want the rotation to carry the argument. same six words, same materials, a different arrangement every time — and each arrangement is a different possible child, not the same child under different conditions. so: one stanza per act (the one she did, then five alternatives), and in each stanza the word "her" should pick out a different girl — the one who'd have been born under that act. "her" should never refer to the child being spoken to. if you want to be fancy, make one stanza a different-number case (no child, or two) and let "her" dangle there, pointing at nobody.

two things have to stay true at once and i dont want the poem to choose between them: that nobody here was harmed or made worse off, and that we still want to say the choice was wrong. no line should say or clearly imply that the child shes addressing is worse off than she would have been — that's the slip i'll be listening for. you can reach for the impersonalist move (there's less well-being in the world than there might have been) but only in one stanza, and it has to sound like something the speaker can't quite make herself believe.

dont resolve it, and dont let the envoi collapse into "but you're here and that's what matters." it should work read out loud to a room of people who've just spent an hour failing to solve this. no preamble explaining the form, ill do that bit myself.
