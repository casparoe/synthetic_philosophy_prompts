Hey — I'm building a little interactive position-sorter for a first-year cog-sci seminar (a web page where students click through nine or ten scenarios and get told which theory of consciousness their answers actually commit them to, rather than which one they've heard of). Topic is the access vs. phenomenal distinction and global workspace theory, and I keep rewriting the intro paragraph because it's easy to sound like a encyclopedia entry. So: help me build the instrument.

Constraints that matter for my purposes:

1. Build it from the logical space, not from a list of names. Start from the diagnostic questions, lay out the combinations of answers they allow, then locate the actual positions in that space, and mark the empty cells — with a word on each empty cell about whether it's incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unoccupied because nobody's gotten round to defending it.

2. I don't want 'access' treated as one thing. Split it into at least three grades: (a) report-ready, the subject can say what it was right now; (b) use-ready, it's available for deliberate reasoning, deciding, guiding action, even if they never report it; (c) influence-only, it biases later processing or behaviour but isn't available for reasoning or report. The sorter should be able to tell apart someone who counts all three as access, someone who counts only (a), and someone who counts (a) plus (b) but not (c).

3. Second fork I care about: how much weight does the subject's own insistence carry? The interesting cases are ones where performance says one thing and the subject says another, roughly 'you could only name four of the items' vs 'no, I saw all of them, I just couldn't get to them in time'. Put at least two such conflict cases in, and make the answer options turn on whose testimony you trust and why, not just on what happened.

4. Third fork: actual vs. counterfactual availability. If a state is never reported or used, but would have been had you been asked at the right moment, does that count as access? At least one dream case and one 'you only noticed it later' case belong here.

Cases I want used, roughly one each (swap in better ones if you've got them, but keep the shape of these):

- a briefly flashed array of letters or symbols where the subject can name any single row when cued afterwards, but insists they saw the whole array
- someone with damage to primary visual cortex who 'guesses' well above chance and can guide a reaching movement, but sincerely denies seeing anything
- driving a familiar route on autopilot, arriving safely, with no memory of the road and no sense of having seen it (I call these the silent-competence cases)
- a very brief or masked stimulus that changes which word you read next, and which you'd swear you never saw
- a vivid dream, with no report or action possible at the time
- a background feeling — posture, a mild ache, a vague unease — that you only properly 'notice' minutes later
- a change-blindness-style case where the subject is confident and wrong
- reading a page while thinking about something else, then being asked a comprehension question about the page
- a thought that clearly guided what you just typed, or a word on the tip of your tongue, that you can't say what it was
- a case where what people say they saw shifts depending on whether you ask them to describe it freely or to pick from options

Rules for the questions themselves: no jargon. The words phenomenal, qualia, access, broadcast, workspace, and consciousness-in-the-technical-sense shouldn't appear in any question or answer option. Two or three options each, phrased so a nineteen-year-old who's never opened a philosophy of mind paper can answer, and no option that's just 'yes it's conscious' / 'no it isn't'.

What I want back: the questions with their options; a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions; a tree (nested bullets or ascii, whatever reads cleanly) whose branch nodes are the forks above and whose leaves are named positions — I want at least these as leaves: a global-workspace/broadcast identity view, a Block-style phenomenal-overflow view, a higher-order view, a first-order representationalist view, an illusionist or eliminativist view, and the 'this is all a verbal dispute' deflationary option, plus any other leaf you think belongs. One sentence per leaf on its most awkward commitment. Then a short section on which pairs of leaves the instrument can't actually separate and why (I suspect the broadcast view and the higher-order view collapse on a couple of my cases, but tell me what you actually find), and which answer combinations are unoccupied and why.

Don't bother with citations or further reading, I've got the reading list sorted — I just need the instrument itself.
