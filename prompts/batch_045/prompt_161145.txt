Browning and Goldhagen keep talking past each other in my reading group, so before I read any more of either I want the spec: write the desiderata a theory of perpetrator participation has to meet — what it would have to explain to count as adequate — with the case or consideration behind each. Fix the purpose as explaining variation: why, inside one unit, under the same orders, most men shot and a handful did not; why the same men who were shaken sick the first time later killed efficiently and some went looking for extra work; why the cruelty so often ran past what the orders required. Not a theory of legal guilt, and it should keep explanation and excuse apart.

Requirements I think belong somewhere on the list, but you decide which are genuine and which are just one camp's prejudice: handling within-unit variation without making the refusers moral saints; handling escalation over time; handling surplus cruelty (the gratuitous humiliation nobody ordered); distinguishing why the policy existed from why this man pulled the trigger, and saying how the two levels connect; explaining how a motive as widespread as antisemitic ideology could be present where no mass killing followed; saying something real about how situation and character interact instead of just picking a side; and doing justice to the fact that these men were not selected for sadism while opting out carried costs that were real but not fatal — one man who demanded release got it and was promoted on return home, so neither "they had no choice" nor "they all wanted to" is available. I'd also add a requirement of my own: the theory has to say what it predicts counterfactually for a unit whose commander neither pressures anyone nor explicitly permits an exit, since a theory that only fits the case where the door is held open hasn't shown it generalises.

Then flag any pair you think can't be jointly satisfied. My hunch is that "no dispositional difference between perpetrators and everyone else" — the ordinary-men claim, read strictly — pulls against "must explain the surplus cruelty and the handful who stepped out," and I want you to say whether that's a genuine tension or just an equivocation on ordinary (unselected for the job vs. statistically typical of the population). Close with a scorecard, one line per camp: Browning, Goldhagen, and some kind of situation-plus-character view.
