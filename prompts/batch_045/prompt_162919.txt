I'm working on a piece about whether knowledge depends on what's at stake — the low-stakes/high-stakes bank-style cases — and I want a number, not a tour of the positions. Give me your credence that pragmatic encroachment is true: that two people with the same evidence, same reliability and the same true belief can differ in whether they *know* purely because more is riding on it for one of them. Mine's around 0.35, and I want to see whether your decomposition puts the weight in the same place.

Break it down: how much rides on whether the intuitive judgments in those cases are tracking knowledge at all rather than something nearby (what it's reasonable to do, whether you'd assert it, whether you'd blame the person); how much on whether 'knowledge' here is the ordinary concept or a theoretical posit; whether, if something like encroachment is real, it lands on knowledge itself or only on justification and evidence-standards; and, conditional on encroachment being true, whether the contextualist or the interest-relative-invariantist version is the right one. Number and one line each, then the combination.

The bit I keep getting stuck on, which nobody I've talked to has answered straight, is stakes that aren't the agent's own. A stranger in another city takes out an enormous bet on whether she knows the bank is open tomorrow; nothing about her evidence or her situation changes and she never hears about the bet. Or a threatener will harm someone she'll never meet if shes wrong, but only on condition she never finds out. Call these idle stakes. Does the encroacher have a principled way to keep knowledge fixed there while letting it move in the agent's-own-stakes cases, and what does that distinction cost them? I think that's a big share of my 0.35.

Finally: which term are you least confident in, and what one thing — an argument, a distinction, experimental work on ordinary knowledge attributions — would move the number most, and in which direction?
