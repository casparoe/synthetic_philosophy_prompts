I'm one of nine people on my city's Charter Revision Commission (I cover science and ideas for a living, wich apparently qualified me) and I got voluntold to draft the memo on our franchise package. We report in five weeks, whatever we recommend goes on the November ballot, and the mayor wants it as one bundled measure while about half of us want the items split so voters can take some and leave some.

Only municipal offices are ours to touch — our state fixes 18 for state and federal races. On the table:

1. drop the city voting age to 16
2. legal non-citizen residents with two-plus years here vote in city elections (our city attorney says trying to include undocumented residents too would set off a preemption fight, so that's the raw part)
3. make turnout compulsory — the version actually in play is compulsory *attendance*, with a real "abstain / none of the above" box on the ballot and a small waivable fine, so nobody is forced to pick a candidate
4. one member's pet idea that she will not drop: a half-weighted proxy ballot cast by a resident parent or guardian on behalf of each kid under 16 in the household

I'm the person on this commission who keeps asking what voting is even for, and I've noticed that question is doing all the work: whether an election discovers a will that's already out there or constructs one that wasn't, whether the franchise tracks membership or affected interests, whether turning out is a public good nobody individually has reason to supply or an expression that's spoiled the moment it's compelled. I want you to pick a frame and then answer all four items consistently — I suspect the honest answer is yes to some and no to others, and I'd rather you say so than smooth it out.

Act as a political philosopher who also knows how elections are actually administered. First do a short brainstorm: the six or eight candidate framings of what voting is for, what each one would imply for the four items, which one you're taking and why it beats its strongest rival. Think it through properly before you start writing.
