I have a package of commitments about anthropic reasoning and its bearing on existential risk that I want audited before I build a paper around it. Please assume familiarity with the standard self-sampling / self-indication literature and do not survey it: what I want is a diagnosis of my own set, not a map of the field. Here are the commitments, with the reasons I would give for each.

(a) I accept the Carter–Leslie doomsday argument in its self-sampling form. Taking the usual back-of-envelope figure of a hundred-odd billion humans before me as my rank, and a roughly scale-invariant prior over the total, I accept the familiar consequence that a total within about twenty times what has come before is something like 95% probable. In practice I report a weaker figure, around two-thirds, because I hold the methodological view stated in (g) below and do not want to lean too hard on a single armchair consideration.

(b) The reference class is what I call reflectors: beings capable of representing themselves as one member of a temporally extended sequence and of reasoning about their own position within it. This covers paradigm adult humans, and would cover future emulations, posthumans and extraterrestrial persons if any exist; it excludes infants, non-human animals, and artificial agents that are sophisticated but lack second-order self-representation. I reject two rival choices: the maximally inclusive class of everything with a subjective point of view, because it admits Boltzmann-brain style cases and makes anthropic reasoning unusable; and the biological species class, because the boundary of a species looks like an arbitrary place to stop.

(c) I reject the self-indication assumption. It licenses the presumptuous-philosopher pattern of inference, and more fundamentally I do not think the bare fact that I exist is the kind of thing that can be evidence about how many beings there are. Evidence comes as observations, and my being here to make them is a precondition of observation, not an observation.

(d) I reject the delta-t version of the argument, the one that infers a remaining duration from a present duration alone and gets applied to the run of a play or the lifetime of a wall. Any phenomenon can be redescribed so as to yield a different interval, and I take that arbitrariness to be fatal. I do not think this costs me (a): my version has a sampling story and delta-t has none.

(e) I think the argument is mechanism-blind in a way that matters. Because it is an argument about the total rather than about how any particular risk operates, I take it to raise the probability of a truncated future and thereby to raise the expected value of mitigation generally. I also take it to favour concentrating on the risks that could plausibly act within this century, such as great-power war or artificial intelligence, rather than on slower-moving ones.

(f) Consider what I call the Quiet Expansion: we survive, leave the planet and fill the galaxy, but the overwhelming majority of the resulting minds are non-reflective in my sense, while the number of reflectors never grows much beyond its present scale. I hold that the doomsday argument tells against this scenario, and that its realization would count as a form of doom rather than as a reprieve.

(g) Methodologically, I want to be conservative about anthropic reasoning: I would prefer to accept an anthropic argument only where its conclusion is in principle checkable by counting, and I am suspicious of arguments whose entire force comes from comparing hypotheses that differ by many orders of magnitude.

I have no settled view about which of these I would surrender if they conflict, and part of what I want is guidance on that. Please tell me: which pairs are genuinely in tension, ordered by severity rather than all listed as equal, with what would have to give in each case and what that revision would cost me in substance; which pairs only look tense and dissolve on inspection, and why, so that I do not revise where no revision is needed; which single revision would buy me the most consistency; and whether any commitment here is doing no work at all.
