I teach clinical reasoning to medical residents, and there is a shape of case I keep running into that I cannot find a name for, though I assume there is a literature on it.

A resident feels sure that a scan shows something malignant, and is then told two things: that in this unit, readers who are *sure* about this presentation are right about as often as a coin flip, and that the reason is not that the presentation is intrinsically hard but a perceptual trap that produces both the error and the feeling of certainty. Her confidence is a symptom of the very process that makes her wrong. If she could stop being certain, she would be back to her normal accuracy and the undermining fact would no longer apply to her — but she cannot simply decide to stop being certain, and no amount of being told seems to switch the feeling off.

Two nearby cases seem to work differently. In one, the undermining fact is insensitive to what she does: she learns she has been overconfident in this domain for years, so even the moderate confidence she retreats to is itself inflated, and the doubt simply moves with her. In the other, complying seems to *produce* the problem: she is told that the departmental guideline's track record is unreliable precisely because everyone defers to it, and that deference destroyed the independence the track record depended on — so the more decisively she treats that information as a reason to defer, the less support it gives.

Does this cluster have an established name, or are these three different things? I am not after a label on its own. I want the carving: what exactly the phenomenon is; how it differs from a reason for doubt that merely outweighs the original evidence or makes it evidentially irrelevant; how it differs from the ordinary gap between judging that one should not believe something and actually giving it up; whether the category should include cases where one can only comply indirectly, by changing one's habits, one's sources, or one's environment rather than one's mind; and whether a reason for doubt that disappears when complied with belongs with one that appears only when complied with. If nothing established fits, coin the term, define it, and say which nearby cases must be excluded and why.
