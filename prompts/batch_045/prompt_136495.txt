I'm in a graduate seminar on epistemology and we did the Nozick tracking stuff last week, and I've been trying to write down where I've actually landed before I have to present on Thursday. My roommate (she's writing a dissertation on safety conditions and thinks sensitivity is a dead end) keeps telling me my view is a house of cards, and I genuinely can't tell whether she's right or whether she's just annoyed that I won't switch frameworks. So here's the profile, more or less as it sits in my notes:

(1) Tracking. I think knowledge requires that the belief track the truth: roughly, if p were false I wouldn't believe p, and in the relevant nearby circumstances if p were true I would — with the method of belief-formation held fixed. I still think this is the right shape for a theory even though everyone else has migrated to safety. (2) What I've started calling *transparent closure* in my own notes: I think knowledge is closed under known entailment, but only when the entailment is one I actually grasp — if I know p and I can follow the route from p to q myself, I know q; if an expert tells me "oh by the way, p entails q" and I can't follow the proof, I'm not sure I get q for free. Relatedly, in the zoo case where you're looking at a striped animal in the zebra pen and someone warns you that the zoo has occasionally shaved mules and painted stripes on them — I think that once you know it's a zebra, you know it's not a painted mule, and I have never understood why people give that up. (3) Ordinary knowledge is cheap: sitting here in this café I know I have hands, I know there's a laptop in front of me, I know the espresso machine is behind the counter. (4) I bite the skeptical bullet: I don't know that I'm not a brain in a vat, and I don't know that there's no Cartesian demon running the show. I take this to fall straight out of tracking and I've made peace with it as the price of the theory. (5) To keep (3) and (4) from collapsing into each other, I individuate the belief-forming method very finely — basically the token causal process that actually produced the belief, details and all, not some coarse type like "vision". (6) In the fake barn county case (the one where nearly every barn-looking thing in the valley is a papier-mâché façade and our driver happens to glance at the single real barn), I think the driver does know there's a barn; the "no knowledge" verdict has always struck me as an artifact of the theory rather than something anyone actually intuits. (7) I also think I know a priori that 2+3=5 and that nothing is both red and green all over, which my roommate says is a problem for tracking since there's no possible world in which those are false, but that objection feels like a trick to me.
