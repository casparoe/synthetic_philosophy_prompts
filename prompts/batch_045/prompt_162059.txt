So I've basically ended up as an inertist about panpsychism and I want you to try to break that.

Rough statement of where I'm at: suppose Russellian-ish monism is true — fundamental物理 stuff has an experiential intrinsic nature, so there's something it is like (in some thin sense) to be an electron or a photon or whatever. I still think the ethical upshot is basically nil, and I lean on three things to get there.

(1) The affective floor: micro-experiences are experiences but not valenced ones. Valence needs a fairly specific kind of integrated, self-modelling organisation, so quarks, rocks, photodiodes and stars have no welfare and nothing can matter to them. There's a floor below which you have experience without anything being at stake.

(2) Budget closure: even granting some micro-valence, I've been toying with a coupling thesis — the magnitude (and maybe sign) of micro-valence tracks something like local entropy production, and I want to say that no intervention available to us can change the total, because we can only reshuffle dissipation, not create or destroy it. So the aggregate is fixed whatever we do, in a way that animal suffering obviously isn't.

(3) Symmetry: even if I drop (2), my prior on whether dissipation couples to positive or negative valence is roughly symmetric, so the expected value of caring about it is about zero, and there's no operational handle on the sign anyway.

What I want from you: what would count against this, and in particular against (1) and (2) individually, since I suspect they fail separately rather than together. Some things I'm already worried about, which you should feel free to develop or knock down:

- The constitution worry. If my macro-valence is made of micro-experiences, it looks like either the micro-experiences already have something valence-like (and the floor collapses) or the making isn't really constitution (and I've quietly become an emergentist using panpsychist vocabulary). Is that fatal or just awkward, and which horn should I pick?
- (2) smells like it's doing rhetorical work. Entropy production isn't obviously conserved in anything like the way I need, I can change total dissipation trivially by burning something, and if experiential magnitude is pinned to a conserved quantity then micro-experience isn't doing any causal work — which was half the point of positing it. Does (2) collapse into epiphenomenalism, or into just physicalism with extra decor?
- Swamping backfiring. If there is micro-valence, the totals are dominated by stars and accretion disks and not by anything I do — but that doesn't make the issue inert, it makes it the largest thing in the world. I don't want to have accidentally argued myself into caring about astrophysics.

Also: what's already happened that bears on this? I'm thinking of the awkward result that some integration-type measures blow up on things like grids of simple logic gates, and the general trouble with saying anything operational about self-modelling — tell me how much those actually touch (1), and be honest if they don't.

Then: rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and for each say whether a committed inertist would concede it counts against them or dismiss it as beside the point. And please be willing to conclude the whole package is insulated: if "the experiences are too thin for anything we do to matter" can't be wrong about anything, is that a problem for a view that was supposed to make consciousness matter? Say yes if that's where it lands.

One more concrete thing, because I think it forces the issue: imagine a valence oracle — an instrument calibrated on humans that reads off the sign and rough magnitude of a system's experience from its dynamics, and assume it generalises. What readings should I expect it to give for a rock, a star, a human brain, and a big waste-heat radiator, and which of those readings would knock out which plank?

For what it's worth, the thing I'd personally find most persuasive is a collapse defeater: show me I'm a physicalist or an emergentist in disguise and I'll give it up. I'm much less moved by "that's weird" or by how big the universe is. Definitely don't bother with anything that rests on the sheer number of particles, I've stopped finding that argument interesting.
