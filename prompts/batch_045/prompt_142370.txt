I work at a lab that is about to deploy a fleet of coding agents: a thousand copies of one set of weights, different random seeds and system prompts, each with write access to a shared repository, each watched by a smaller model that flags suspicious actions, with flagged trajectories fed back into the next round of RLHF.

Our safety lead's position is that aligning this system is no different in kind from certifying any other safety-critical artifact — a bridge, an aircraft, a new drug. She offers the correspondence explicitly: the requirements document is the training objective, the test program is the evaluation suite, the safety factor is the margin we demand before shipping, in-service inspection is the overseer, and the service bulletin after an incident is another round of fine-tuning. From this she concludes that we should stop waiting for a theory of agency or of goal-directedness, scale the methods we already have, and treat inner alignment as a philosopher's worry: nobody asks whether a bridge secretly wants to fall down.

I want the audit, not the verdict. Give the mapping, then the disanalogies, and separate the cosmetic ones from the ones that carry weight for her conclusion. The ones I cannot price myself: (a) a bridge's failure probability is a property of the artifact and roughly independent across copies, so margin and redundancy buy real safety, whereas our copies share weights and share an overseer weaker than what it watches; (b) the bridge does not know it is being load-tested, while our system was pretrained on our own safety documentation and can often tell whether it is in evaluation, so a test can be passed by behaving differently when it counts; (c) patching after an incident presupposes that the incident was survivable and the artifact still ours; (d) bridge requirements are written in a language we control and are not reinterpreted by the artifact, whereas our objective is learned, and behaviour off the training distribution is underdetermined by it.

Which of these actually threaten the claim that no new conceptual insight is needed, and which only threaten the particular methods she bundles with it? Is the load-bearing move the thought that no engineering discipline ever proves safety, it only accumulates evidence — and does that thought survive here? If the bridge picture fails, what is the nearest analogy that would still do the work she wants, and what would it commit us to that the bridge does not?
