hey — I'm putting together the handout for a one-day workshop on theory choice that my dept runs for the phil-sci grad students (we let the physics and astro people come too), and I want a poem on the back page. It should be about underdetermination.

Not the "anything goes, man" version, and not the version where every prediction drags a pile of auxiliary assumptions along behind it — I mean the strong claim: two whole theories, complete packages, that fit every observation anybody could ever make and still say things that can't both be true.

I want it as a ghazal, because the form basically IS the argument: five or six couplets that each stand on their own with no running story between them, every second line ending in the same short repeated phrase, and then a final couplet where the poet signs their own name. Self-contained theories. One shared bit of evidence that keeps coming back and never picks between them. And then a last couplet that the form promises is coming but whose content you couldn't have guessed in advance — which is the "alternatives nobody has even thought of yet" version of the worry. If ghazal isn't quite the word for what I just described, dont argue with me, just do what I described.

What I actually care about:

- the repeated phrase should be deadpan and observational, the kind of report nobody in the room would fight over. I was thinking "the needle didn't move," but take something better if youve got it.
- the couplets should be recognizably different theories, and not all of them physics. One should be the geometry case — curved space versus a universal force quietly shrinking all your measuring rods — and at least one should be an empirically equivalent pair from somewhere else entirely.
- here's my own distinction, so you won't find it in the literature: some rivals earn their equivalence (both packages genuinely explain, you pick on simplicity or fruitfulness or whatever, its a real choice) and some just copy it — theory T plus some undetectable mechanism bolted on to reproduce T's predictions exactly. I've started calling those shadow twins. I want at least one couplet to be a shadow twin, and I want the reader to feel that its cheap in a way the others aren't, without the poem stopping to point at it.
- dont let it slide into the pessimistic induction. Different argument, different mood, I don't want it.
- and no resolution couplet. The needle didn't move. That's the ending.

Ten to twelve lines or so, and the repeated phrase has to land every single time it comes around. It should be right enough that a philosopher of science reads it and doesnt wince — accurate, not just wearing the vocabulary. thanks!
