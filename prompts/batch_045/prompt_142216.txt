Our team keeps ending design arguments with "we build tools, not agents" and I've finally noticed that I have no idea what anyone means by it.

The fight that set this off: we're deciding whether to ship a coding assistant that can open pull requests on its own overnight, or one that only suggests diffs a human applies, or the middle option where it opens PRs but only inside a scope a human pre-approved. Someone says the first one is out because we build tools, not agents. Someone else says it's fine as long as we keep a human in the loop. I pointed out that a thermostat closes a loop and acts on the world and nobody calls it an agent, and they said thermostats don't have goals, and I said a chess engine has a goal and is definitly a tool. Then it moved to responsibility: a tool is something where the person using it is on the hook, an agent is something you'd have to blame. Which sounds like it's a claim about our org chart and not about the model at all.

My own stab, which might be garbage: I think there are three different places people could be drawing the line, and they come apart. (a) In the training: was the thing optimized to pursue outcomes across time, or just to complete a prompt? (b) In the scaffolding: how many steps does it take before something stops it, and who set that stop? (c) In the accountability structure: who gets paged when it breaks production? And I've started to suspect that tool vs agent isn't a property of the system at all but of the relation between the system and whoever owns the task — so the same weights are a tool when I drive them step by step and an agent when my manager points them at a backlog and goes to lunch. If that's right the slogan can't settle the shipping question by itself, because it's a claim about us, not about it. One more wrinkle I can't place: a spellchecker that silently autocorrects as you type is obviously a tool, but it acts without being asked at each individual step, which is supposed to be the agent-y bit.

So: lay out, as a list I can bring into the next meeting, the different precise things "tools, not agents" could be claiming — from the readings that are platitudes nobody would deny, through the substantive ones that would actually rule something out, to the ones that are false or empty. For each, give the reading in one sharp sentence, tag it trivial / defensible / false, and say specifically whether it gets my colleague his conclusion about the overnight PR-opener. Flag which reading people are probably reaching for when they say it in a room like that, and whether that reading does the work they think it does. And tell me if my "it's a relation, not a property" idea is a real position someone holds or just confused.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — what the slogan is actually doing in arguments like these, and whether it settles anything here — then give me the menu and the verdicts. No preamble, and don't restate my question back at me.
