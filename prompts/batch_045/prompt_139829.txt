I'm writing a novel — near-future, played mostly straight — and I need one in-world document that a character can read on the page without the book stopping dead. Here's the setup. In this world a private foundation maintains "the Book": a room full of indexed volumes that are supposed to contain the reply Einstein would give to any question anyone could put to him. It's that old thought-experiment about a book you can hold a conversation with, except now it's an institution with a gift shop. Members pay a lot of money to submit one question in approved wording, and recieve a reply some months later, typed up and notarised, because the answer has to be looked up and read aloud by a paid Reader. My narrator is up at 3am trying to decide whether to spend the last of an inheritance on one question about her dead father, and she's reading the foundation's public FAQ while she decides. So I need the FAQ itself.

Write me that page: about 700 words, numbered questions and answers, deadpan corporate-customer-service voice, the kind of thing that was drafted by a lawyer who has stopped being surprised. The jokes have to come from the philosophy being right, not from anyone going "can you believe this place." Specifically, I want these real features exaggerated into policy rather than stated as philosophy:

— that the Book is a lookup table: every exchange was precomputed, it has never once been surprised, and retrieval is doing all the work that understanding was supposed to do
— the speed problem: replies take months because reading takes time, and the foundation insists this is just the Book thinking at its own pace, and sells a "Real-Time Edition" that is transparently a staff member improvising in the next room
— that it never initiates anything: no one has ever been asked a question by the Book, there's a spontaneity surcharge, and one answer admits the Book has volunteered three remarks since 1974, all of them about trains
— the systems reply: the foundation's official line that the person is the whole apparatus — Book, Readers, index, building, insurance policy — and that the Book by itself has no views on anything
— the regress hiding underneath: the Readers' union, the "Reader Problem" of staff who start to believe they're the voice, and the rule that no Reader may serve more than four years
— the triviality worry: a customer asking whether their kitchen appliance is also Einstein, answered with the foundation's refusal to certify appliances while pointedly not disputing the principle that any object can be read as implementing any conversation you like
— the consciousness question itself, which the FAQ should slide off into what the insurers require: the foundation warrants only that the Book behaves a certain way and takes no position on the rest

A few invented details I want in there please, since they're load-bearing for my plot: questions must be submitted in pre-approved phrasing and there's "paraphrase insurance" if yours gets reworded; follow-up questions are answered from "Branch Editions" compiled by impersonators, and the contract calls this continuity; and there's a clause about what happens to your question if the reading is interrupted. Keep it funny for someone who knows the Chinese-room / blockhead literature and perfectly readable for someone who doesn't. Don't let the document wink at the reader — nobody in it is in on the joke — and don't let it settle whether the Book is a person; I want the reader to feel the pull of both answers and be a little frightened that the FAQ is, on its own terms, reasonable. End on one customer question the foundation declines to answer.
