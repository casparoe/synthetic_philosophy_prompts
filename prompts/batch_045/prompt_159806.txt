I have been trying to reconstruct, in my own vocabulary, an argument to the conclusion that functionalism about the mental is self-undermining, and I have ended up with something I cannot accept, because it appears to prove considerably more than that. What I want is a diagnosis: which step should I reject, why does that step look innocent, and what does rejecting it cost me elsewhere? Please argue it out rather than directing me to readings; I have no library access at the moment.

The argument, with its terms as I mean them:

(1) Role functionalism. For a mental kind M — believing that it will rain, being in pain — being in M is having some first-order state or other that occupies the causal role R_M assigned to M by a matured psychology, the role being specified by its characteristic relations to proximal sensory inputs, to other mental states, and to behavior. M is thus a second-order property: the property of having a realizer of R_M, whatever that realizer turns out to be.

(2) Multiple realizability, strong form. The realizers of R_M need share no physical property beyond the fact that they occupy R_M: anything specifiable without reference to R_M that is true of one realizer class may be false of another. I have tried to make this harder to escape than the usual appeal to extraterrestrial or silicon cases, in two ways. (a) Role-occupancy is tolerant. Any role specification usable against noisy, plastic, partly degraded biological systems must admit of degrees and allow deviation, so the class of realizers of R_M is graded, has vague boundaries, and can vary between individuals, between tasks, and within one individual over time. (b) Realization is diachronically multiple within a single subject. Where a circuit is locally damaged and other tissue comes to subserve the same transformation, that subject's later M-tokens are realized differently from her earlier ones. Heterogeneity of realization is therefore not a bare metaphysical possibility about remote cases; it is, apparently, a feature of the very population over which psychology generalizes.

(3) Causal inheritance. Where a subject's M is realized by neural state N, and N causes the behavior B, the causal work is done by N. M causes B only derivatively, in virtue of being the property of having an N that does that work: nothing is added to the causal history of B over and above N and its effects.

(4) The explanatory test. A generalization of the form M-instances cause B earns its standing as an explanation, and as something a science is entitled to project, only if it is projectible across the full range of cases it purports to cover: it must support counterfactuals about unobserved instances and license intervention, not merely summarize the instances already on record.

(5) Projectibility needs something shared to track. For the generalization to be projectible over a heterogeneous realizer class, there must be a property common to all the M-instances in virtue of which they produce B. By (2) they share nothing physical. The only remaining candidate is M itself, and M is thin: it is the property of having some realizer or other, and it settles nothing about what will happen under intervention beyond what the particular realizer settles. What remains is, in effect, the disjunction N1 or N2 or … or Nk. A disjunction is not the sort of thing a science projects or an intervention acts on: intervening to bring about B requires knowing which disjunct one is in and acting on that.

(6) So the M-level generalization is not projectible. The genuine causal and explanatory content lies entirely at the level of realizers, and these are heterogeneous. Psychology has no laws over M, no intervention-supporting generalizations, and, by (3), no causal work of its own to do. Psychology and psychiatry are at best collections of local, mechanism-specific models held together by a convenient but empty vocabulary.

(7) Therefore role functionalism, conjoined with multiple realizability, destroys what it was introduced to protect: the autonomy and explanatory standing of the psychological special science. Functionalism is self-undermining.

Why I distrust it: the same steps run on cases whose conclusion I am not prepared to accept. Take a thermodynamic kind such as temperature, whose instances in a dilute gas, in a crystalline solid, and in a radiation field share no microphysical property worth naming; the causal work in any given system is done by whatever micro-states realize it there; role-occupancy is tolerant there too; the realizer class is open-ended and, at the micro level, disjunctive. Run (1) through (6) and the result is that thermodynamics has no projectible kinds, that all the work is done by micro-states, and that no intervention acts on temperature as such. The same pattern looks available for functional kinds in biology, in engineering, and in economics. That is absurd, so something among (1) to (5) is false.

But I do not think I can simply rest in the overgeneration. A defender of the argument can reply that this is just how matters stand with multiply realized kinds generally, that their apparent projectibility is explained case by case by local facts about how homogeneous the actual instances happen to be, and that the mental is owed no special indulgence. So I want the diagnosis rather than the dismissal: identify the step that fails, explain why it looked sound, and tell me what each of the other options would cost.

Two things, so you know where I would rather not be pushed. I would sooner give up (2), (3), or (5) than (1) or (4): I want to keep the second-order characterization of mental kinds, and I want to keep some counterfactual or interventionist test of when a kind earns its explanatory keep, since abandoning (4) looks like conceding that psychology explains nothing. I should also say that one repair I keep returning to lands me in the opposite absurdity. If I reject (5) by saying that M is projectible after all, because all of its realizers share the same role-profile and a shared role-profile is sufficient for intervention even without a shared microstructure, then, given how tolerant (2a) makes role-occupancy and given that the grain of description seems up for grabs, it becomes very hard to say why an arbitrary system does not occupy R_M and therefore is not in M.

So please tell me which step to reject, what that step was doing in the argument and why it seemed safe, and how your diagnosis steers between the horn on which there are no psychological generalizations and the horn on which everything is in pain. Argue the prices of the alternatives rather than listing positions.
