Hi there — I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm putting together a long feature (3000 words-ish) on the whole tool-AI-versus-agent-AI argument. I've got about six weeks and room for maybe ten interviews, so I can't chase every thread, and I want help deciding which ones deserve the space.

Here's where I'm stuck, and I want you to engage with my framing rather than the generic version of the debate. It seems to me people use "agent" to mean at least three different things that usually travel together but don't have to: (1) initiative — the system kicks things off on its own instead of waiting to be asked; (2) the size of its option space — what it's actually able to do once running, spend money, send messages, modify files, hire a contractor; and (3) rollback — whether what it does can be cleanly undone, or whether its effects are out in the world before anyone sensible is looking. I've been calling the combination "the leash," and I think a lot of public argument is really a fight about which of the three is the load-bearing one.

Concretely: a source describes a coding setup that never acts until prompted, but once prompted will run for two days, burn a few thousand dollars, open pull requests and email colleagues for review. Is that a tool or an agent, and — more to the point — which of my three axes is doing the real safety-relevant work there? Related: I've started thinking about what I call the specification ceiling, the point where the user can no longer competently specify or check the goal, so that "the human stays in charge" becomes a fact about the interface rather than about the system. And there's a fourth thread I suspect is underrated: administrability — which of these distinctions, if any, could actually survive being written into procurement rules or an insurance policy, as opposed to being philosophers' distinctions that collapse the moment a regulator tries to use them.

So: rank the open questions in this area for me. I care much less about a complete list than about the reasoning. For each: what makes it worth the reporting time, which of the others it's upstream of, which ones are tractable in the next couple of years versus which are really proxy fights over a defintion that should be defused instead of settled, and which I should drop as overrated — including, if you think so, my own three-way split. Tell me too what would move something up or down the list: a particular kind of deployment, a benchmark result, something happening in court. Assume I want the piece to tell readers something they couldn't get from a press release, and that I can't just ring up whoever's loudest this week.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — the single thing you'd have me build the piece around — then give me the reasoning and the ranking. No preamble, and please don't restate my question back at me.
