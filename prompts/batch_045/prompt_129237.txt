I've been lurking around LessWrong and the Alignment Forum since way back, and for the last couple years I've been trying to get a handle on ELK. I finaly feel like I have a position, but I don't trust my own read on whether it hangs together, so I want an audit. Here's roughly where I've landed.

(1) ELK is a real and distinct problem, not just "honesty research" or "interpretability" with a new coat of paint. There's a specific thing we want — a report of what the model itself knows — that neither of those is aimed at, and I think it's plausibly the binding constraint on whether anything superhuman is alignable at all.

(2) Relatedly, the target isn't "get the model to tell the truth," it's "get the model to say what it knows." Those feel like seperate axes to me, and I think the report is right to insist on the distinction. And since the knowledge is already in there, this should be easier than teaching the model anything new — the hard part is only getting it out.

(3) The failure mode I'm most worried about is the human simulator: a translator that's really just guessing what a smart human would say given the camera feed. At the point where this matters the human doesn't know and the model does, so anything bottoming out in imitating human reports is worthless by construction. In the vault case the right answer is "someone swapped the diamond and the camera is being fed a fake" — a claim about what's happening in the world, not about what the image currently looks like.

(4) I also think human-labelled data is basically all we've got to train on, and I'm fine with that; the ban in (3) is about where the translator's answers come from, not about the data.

(5) My actual proposed mechanism is a simplicity prior: among all translators that do fine on everything we can check, take the simplest one, and trust that it lands on the same joints any capable predictor converges on — the natural abstractions idea that's been kicking around here for years, i.e. the carving is a fact about the world rather than about us.

(6) The translator itself should stay conservative: read out what's already in the activations, don't let it do its own reasoning. A translator that thinks is a translator that confabulates, and it's also where the human simulator sneaks back in.

(7) But I'm also fairly convinced a superhuman model's concepts will be alien to us — new carvings, not just finer versions of ours. That's why I don't think fine-tuning for honesty, or just asking nicely, gets there.

I'm not attached to any one of these, and some pairs I think are fine while others I can't tell. Please: go through them and say which are genuinely in tension and which only look like it, order the real tensions by how severe they are, for each one say what would have to give and what that would cost me, and then tell me which single revision cleans up the most. If one of them is just confused rather than in tension with anything, say that too. And no headings, bullets, bold text, or tables in your answer — just continuous prose, the way you'd write to a friend in an email.
