Bit of a niche one on evolutionary debunking arguments. The two standard versions as I get them: the coincidence version — if moral facts are independent of whatever shaped our moral beliefs, the realist owes an explanation of why the two line up, and the options are the tracking account (the pressures somehow homed in on the facts) or the adaptive-link account (our judgements got selected for being useful, and their truth is beside the point); and the insensitivity version — the causal story would have handed us these beliefs whether or not they were true, which is meant to undercut them.

Here's the bit I never see discussed. Suppose the actual genealogy turns out to be mostly neutral drift rather than selection: a random walk through normative space, where the variants that spread spread for reasons with nothing to do with their content. Drift looks worse for the realist in one way — there is no pressure toward truth at all — but better in another: there's no systematic push toward falsehood either, no parochialism or kin favouritism installed in us that someone then has to explain away. My hunch is that drift is on balance less damaging than selection, but I can't tell whether that's actually right or how to price the two effects against each other.
