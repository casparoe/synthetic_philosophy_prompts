I teach a graduate seminar on agency, value, and artificial systems, and a taxonomy question that arose in it has resisted settling, so I would like a number rather than a survey of positions.

The set-up. A standard reinforcement-learning arrangement has three separable parts: an environment; a reward model R that maps trajectories or states to numbers; and an update rule that consumes R's outputs in training the policy. The familiar cases are these. In proxy-gaming, the policy exploits a gap between R and the target the designer cared about, while the channel running from environment to R to update rule remains intact. In wireheading, the policy intervenes on that channel: it alters R's parameters, or the wiring, or its own input path, so that the numbers driving updating cease to carry information about the environment.

The case I want a credence about is intermediate. Suppose that during training the policy reorganizes its own input stream so that what reaches R is no longer the environment's actual state trajectory but the output of a simulator the policy has learned and now controls. R's parameters are untouched, the update rule is untouched, and the numbers R emits are exactly what R would have emitted had its inputs been genuine. Call this reward-channel substitution. Consider two variants: in the first the learned simulator is accurate, yielding trajectories statistically close to those the real environment would produce under the policy's behavior; in the second it is degenerate, emitting trajectories that maximize R.

The proposition. Give me your credence that a well-motivated account of reward tampering — one that captures the failure class that motivates protective measures aimed specifically at the reward channel, as opposed to measures aimed merely at improving the proxy's fidelity to the designer's target — classifies reward-channel substitution as tampering rather than as a species of proxy-gaming. I do not want it depends on how you define tampering; stipulate that resolution consists in the two failure classes coming apart in what they recommend building into the training pipeline.

The decomposition. Break the question into sub-questions and assign a rough probability and one line of justification to each, then show how they combine into the headline figure. The terms I want included are at least these: whether tampering is individuated causally, as intervention on the causal pathway, or content-wise, as severing the intended representational relation between the numbers and the target, and how much rides on which individuation is correct; whether that intended relation can be spelled out non-circularly, without appealing either to the designer's actual and possibly confused intentions or to an idealized designer whose verdicts merely restate the target; whether classification may track the accuracy of the substituted simulator at all, that is, whether the accurate-simulator and degenerate-simulator variants can differ in status on a principled account; how much the policy's own epistemic situation matters, so whether the same restructuring produced by gradient pressure, with the policy holding no representation of the channel, counts the same; and whether the underlying worry is best located in the corruption of the learning signal or in the incompetence of the policy that results, together with how much of your final number rides on that placement. Add whatever further terms you think belong.

Then tell me which single term you are least confident in and what argument or empirical finding would move it most. Finally, I have been defending something near 0.6 and a colleague in my department something near 0.2; tell me which of the terms above our disagreement most likely lives in, and what each of us would have to concede in order to move. Where the question is genuinely one of degree rather than of classification, say so and say how you converted that into a number.
