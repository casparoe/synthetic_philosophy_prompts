The topic is Occam's razor made precise: Solomonoff induction's prior, which weights hypotheses by 2 to the minus their Kolmogorov complexity with respect to a fixed universal reference machine. My half-formed worry and idea, which I would like you to sharpen rather than merely restate:

The invariance theorem is usually invoked to dismiss the choice of reference machine: switching machines perturbs complexity by an additive constant that does not depend on the hypothesis, so the ranking of hypotheses is supposed to be machine-robust. But that bound is asymptotic and hypothesis-independent, while actual model selection happens with a finite data set and a handful of candidate hypotheses whose complexity differences may be smaller than the constant. So the arbitrariness may be benign in the convergence proof and still decisive in the practice the proof is meant to justify.

My proposed fix, which I want the proposal to develop: charge for the machine. Score a hypothesis not by its program length under a privileged universal machine, but by the length of the shortest pair consisting of a machine description and a program for that machine — a two-part code in which a hypothesis has to name and pay for the machine that makes it look short, so that complexity cannot be laundered into the choice of reference machine for free.

A second idea I want woven in: separate compression that merely retrodicts from compression that constrains. A lookup table of the observed data is always available and often short enough to be competitive, and a hypothesis can fit the past tightly while leaving the future almost unconstrained. I suspect the right quantity is not the cost of describing the data given the hypothesis but something like the expected cost of describing the data you have not seen yet, and I would like the proposal to say whether that reframing buys anything or simply relocates the problem.
