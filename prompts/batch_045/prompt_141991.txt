I work in philosophy of science and have started reading the literature on grounding and fundamentality, where I keep tripping over a pattern I have no name for. The pattern is this: someone argues that the F-facts obtain in virtue of the G-facts, and then treats that result as licensing a demotion of the F-facts — they are nothing over and above the G-facts, so they need no separate place in the inventory of the world. But the claim that does the demoting does not seem to be statable in G-vocabulary alone. To say which G-facts ground a given F-fact, you have to pick those G-facts out as the realizers of the very item that was supposed to be demoted. Two cases push me the same way. The claim that a pain is grounded in microphysical facts: the microphysical facts in question have to be identified as pain's realizers, and the candidates are scattered and open-ended. The claim that moral facts are grounded in natural facts: the natural grounds of a specific moral fact seem to be identifiable only by appeal to the whole body of ordinary moral truisms, with no shorter naturalistic specification available. In both cases the ground is presented as the self-sufficient item and the grounded item as the dependent one, yet the dependence claim appears to lean on the dependent item in order to get stated at all.

I do not want a label. I want the carving. Is this one phenomenon or several? Is it a problem about specification, about vocabulary, or about what the relata of the grounding relation are taken to be? Does it arise for every grounding claim, or only for cross-level ones, or only where the lower level cannot supply the predicates needed to isolate its own work? What are the nearest named phenomena, and how does this differ from each — I have had bridge laws, supervenience, realization, and the nothing-over-and-above slogan suggested to me, and none seems to be quite it. And if the fundamental story can only be told using the vocabulary of the derivative level, in what sense is that level not fundamental? If there is no established concept here, give me one: a name, a definition, a case that falls under it, and a similar-looking case that does not.
