I am writing the specification section of a proposal on side-effect penalties for autonomous systems, and I need its core stated properly: the requirements list. Fix the setup as follows. We have an agent whose assigned objective we do not fully trust and cannot fully specify. We add to that objective a penalty term — an impact measure — keyed to the difference the agent's behaviour makes to the world, with the aim of making the agent prefer routes to its goal that leave most of everything else as it would otherwise have been. The measure is to be specified without first solving the whole problem of value; that is the entire point of the exercise. What I want from you is the desiderata: what any impact measure has to get right to count as an adequate answer to the side-effects problem for an agent of this kind.

Give me the criteria, not a survey of proposals. For each requirement, state the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, concretely enough that a candidate measure can be tested against it, and mark whether it is constitutive of the problem (relax it and you are no longer doing low-impact AI at all) or a preference internal to one research programme. Among the cases I expect a serious list to handle, and which you should treat as prompts rather than as a checklist to fill in: the ordinary one, where getting the coffee involves breaking what was on the shelf; the case where the counterfactual of doing nothing is itself the bad outcome, so that fidelity to the baseline forbids the rescue; the compensation case, where the agent breaks something and then restores a qualitatively identical state, and an endpoint-only measure scores zero; the laundered baseline, where the agent first degrades what would have happened had it not acted, so that its actual path looks impact-free by comparison; delegation and boundary-shifting, where the effect is produced by a second system the agent arranges for, or by reclassifying part of the world as part of the agent; the delayed leak, where the effect surfaces only after the penalty window has closed; the many-small-hands case, where a thousand agents each keep their own measured impact negligible while the aggregate is enormous; and the correction case, where impact-minimisation turns into a reason to resist being switched off, modified, or overridden — or, symmetrically, a reason to switch itself off. I also want the distinctions an adequate measure must respect rather than collapse: causing versus merely allowing, reversible versus irreversible, harm versus mere difference, changing the world versus changing someone's option set, and the impact of an action versus that of a policy or plan.

Two further constraints belong in the list, and I want your view on how demanding they really are. First, that the measure penalise rather than prohibit: the constraint should be tradeable, so that a large-enough stakes can still motivate an impactful course, but not so slack that it licenses anything. Second, that it be computable from the agent's own epistemic situation, without presupposing the true dynamics of the environment or a privileged unit and threshold for "how much impact is too much."

Now the part I most want. Tell me which subset of these desiderata you take to be jointly unsatisfiable, and argue it: if value-agnosticism, resistance to gaming via the counterfactual and via delegation, non-paralysis, and insensitivity to how the goal is designated cannot all be had together, say which one gives, what relaxing it costs, and whether what survives is still recognisably a solution to the original problem or has quietly become a partial theory of value. Also identify the single requirement that does the most work in excluding candidate measures, and say why that one rather than its neighbours. Where a requirement turns out to be untestable without assuming what it was meant to establish, say so explicitly rather than listing it alongside the others.

If it is cheap to do, close with a few lines scoring the main families of measure against the list — deviation-from-a-baseline-counterfactual, reachability or option-set size, preservation of attainable value for a set of probe objectives, and fixed whitelists or hard constraints on designated state features — describing each operationally rather than by name. Keep that part short; the criteria are the deliverable. Do not cite papers or assemble a reading list: I want the argument made from the cases and from the structure of the problem.
