I need a piece of satire for the last page of a reading-group handout on the no-trade theorem — the result that if everyone shares a prior, is rational in the Bayesian sense, and that's all common knowledge, then no purely speculative trade can be mutually beneficial, so nobody should ever trade with anyone just because they disagree about the odds. Plus the usual follow-ons: people with a shared prior can't "agree to disagree" about their posteriors, and the obvious question of why order books are full of people doing exactly what the theorem says they shouldn't.

What I want is a customer FAQ / onboarding pack for a trading venue that has taken this result completely seriously and designed its whole business around compliance with it. Call it something smug like PrudentMarkets. Voice is deadpan brokerage compliance-speak — the register of risk disclosures and "important information" booklets. Around 650 words, as numbered Q&As under a short header paragraph, ending with one tiny-print Important Notice.

The jokes have to come from the economics, not from finance-bro humor:

- The venue can't let two clients trade on a disagreement about the probability of a payoff, because that's speculation and there isn't any to be had. So the order book gets rebuilt around non-informational motives: hedging, different risk tolerance, consumption timing, forced liquidation. Make the intake form funny — underwriting has to certify that you are trading for a reason other than thinking you know better.
- The shared prior has to be enforced contractually. Clients sign a Prior Reconciliation Agreement; if two posteriors diverge, the platform makes them exchange them until they converge, and bills by the round.
- Somebody has to be irrational or the book is empty. The venue keeps a roster of Registered Noise Participants who are contractually obliged to have a non-epistemic reason for their orders — a divorce, a margin call, a sudden interest in alpaca farming.
- The firm's own revenue is a problem it has to solve without conceding that trade is mutually beneficial: fees reframed as a charge for the service of establishing that there is nothing to be gained.
- Marketing keeps slipping "our prices aggregate all available information" into the brochure and compliance keeps adding footnotes, because the no-trade result isn't a claim about price efficiency — that's a different set of assumptions entirely.

Constraints:

1. Don't break character to explain anything. No narrator aside, no winking, no "of course in reality." A reader who's never heard of the theorem should still get its shape from how absurd running a venue under it turns out to be; someone who knows the literature should be able to reconstruct the assumptions — common prior, common knowledge of rationality, no noise, zero-sum speculative trade, and what each one is doing — just from what the venue has to do to comply.
2. At least one entry should work as a straight-faced reductio: follow the logic to a place the reader recoils. My suggestion is the venue noticing that its own existence is the strongest evidence that its clients are not in fact rational Bayesians with a shared prior, and dealing with that in a corporate way — a reclassification, a carve-out, an internal memo leaked into the FAQ. Whatever you pick, keep it deadpan.
3. Two invented quotes: one from the venue's Chief Epistemology Officer defending the model, one from an outside critic (market microstructure person or behavioral economist) who's annoyed for a reason a specialist would recognize as a real objection — that the leaner the theorem is, the more it rests on a primitive nothing in the model gives you reason to assume, or that banning disagreement-driven trade would kill price discovery itself. Don't let the critic win outright; let the venue's reply be a dodge.
4. Nothing factually wrong about the economics. Get conditionalization right, get common knowledge right, and get the Groucho-style worry right — that being offered a trade is itself a reason not to take it. I want people who know this stuff laughing rather than wincing, and I'd rather it be 500 good words than 700 padded ones.
