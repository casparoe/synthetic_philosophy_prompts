Hi — I'm writing the take-home final for my upper-level seminar on the epistemology of markets (a phil / pol-ecy mix; they've read Hayek's paper on the use of knowledge, Aumann's agreeing-to-disagree result, Milgrom–Stokey, and Grossman–Stiglitz). I need one question, about 900 words, three-hour window, that makes them put the no-trade results and the Hayekian "prices aggregate dispersed knowledge" story into direct contact instead of letting them summarize each in turn.

The crux I want them pinned to: the no-trade theorems assume a common prior over a commonly specified state space, while Hayek's whole point is that the knowledge at issue is local, tacit, and largely inarticulable — so either the theorems concern something other than what Hayek was claiming, or the Hayekian pays a price somewhere. Make them say exactly where the price is paid. If you drop the common prior, aren't you conceding that prices aggregate knowledge only because traders are heterogeneous in a way that's epistemically arbitrary? If you drop the common state space, can "aggregation" even be formulated? Then bring in Grossman–Stiglitz: if prices fully revealed private information, nobody would bother acquiring it, so full informational efficiency is impossible — does that cap the Hayekian claim at partial aggregation, and is partial aggregation enough to carry the argument he wanted it to carry?

Deliver three things: (1) the question itself, worded so nobody scores by reciting defintions; (2) a model answer of 700–800 words; (3) a marking scheme out of 20 built around four or five must-hit moves, with the marks for each, plus one line per move on the usual way students blow it — e.g. treating the common prior as an idealization you can drop for free, reading "no trade from private information" as "no trade at all" and forgetting that risk-sharing and liquidity motives are still in play, or hand-waving "tacit knowledge" as if it were just noisy private information in a standard signal model. Please write it in American English.
