I've been trying to work out what I actually believe about wild animal suffering by going case by case, and my verdicts keep coming apart from my own expected-value arithmetic, so I'd like the principle that's trying to come out.

My verdicts: (1) Vaccinating wild rabbits against myxomatosis, or dosing wild ungulates with an anthelmintic — obviously fine, I'd fund it. (2) Immunocontraception for urban pigeons and starlings — fine. (3) Lethally culling overabundant deer to avert mass winter starvation — permissible, but I dislike it, and I'd still prefer sterilization even though it reaches the same population endpoint more slowly and so leaves more aggregate suffering along the way. (4) Eradicating introduced cats and rats on islands to save ground-nesting birds — fine; the killing of the cats barely registers with me. (5) The same arithmetic applied to a native predator — say, removing foxes from a mainland moor where predation is the dominant term in the local suffering ledger — not fine. And I can't ground that in nativeness, because I don't think a species' ancestry can matter morally in itself. (6) A gene drive spreading a mildly pain-blunting allele through a wild rodent population whose juvenile mortality dominates the ecosystem's suffering — not fine, even though my own numbers say it's the largest win on the list. (7) Demographic redesign: over decades, substituting for the current carnivores and r-strategist prey functionally similar species of comparable size and biomass but with K-shaped life histories — fewer offspring, more parental care, vastly fewer short miserable lives per unit of standing population — not fine. This is where I feel the most resistance and can give the least reason for it.

My tentative principle: intervene where the intervention is small-scale, reversible, and repairs a baseline we ourselves broke; refuse where it is ecosystem-scale, effectively irreversible, or redesigns a system we didn't break. But 4 versus 5 looks like it's running on something I disavow, and 1 versus 6 looks like mere mechanism disgust — treating animals good, editing their genomes bad.

Please steelman the best principle that captures 1 through 7, stated precisely enough to be applied, with the case that motivates each of its clauses; tell me plainly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate; and give its verdicts on two cases I haven't listed — feeding urban pigeons through a hard winter, and a single aerial drop of contraceptive bait across an otherwise untouched island.
