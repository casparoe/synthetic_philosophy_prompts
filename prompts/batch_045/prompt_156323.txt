My grandad died last spring and left a sum of money to be split between me and my two cousins, and for some reason the will says the three of us have to agree on the split unanimously, with me drafting the first proposal. I've been going round in circles on it, and I've slowly realised its not even a disagreement about the facts: we each keep measuring fairness in a different unit. One of us thinks about who'd get the biggest lift in how their life actually feels, one about who has least in the bank, one about what each of us is genuinely able to do with our lives right now. So I want a thought experiment to sharpen it before I put numbers on paper.

Build me a case with three claimants and one fixed pot, allocated once and never repeated. Ordinary contemporary setting, one paragraph, no melodrama, and two constraints that matter: nobody should be at fault for their situation, and nobody should be in such dire straits that sheer urgency settles it. I want whatever verdict comes out to come out because of the currency, not because one claimant obviously deserves it more.

The specifications:

- By welfare — how well a life goes for the person living it, by their own lights — claimant A is worst off.
- By resources — the external means a person commands — claimant B is worst off.
- By capabilities — what a person is really able to do and be — claimant C is worst off.

A clean three-way cycle, same facts held fixed throughout. Then a fourth currency I've been playing with and can't get to behave. Call it detachability: how much of a person's advantage survives the loss of the particular arrangement they currently depend on — the job, the relationship, the city, the one person who makes it work. Someone can be capable but entirely borrowed, or modest and entirely their own. I want the detachability ranking to come out as the exact reverse of the capability ranking. If you think that's not constructible in a single case, say so flatly and give me the nearest thing plus the reason it fails.

Give it to me as exactly five numbered items, no preamble and no sign-off: 1 the scenario; 2 the three verdicts and why; 3 the detachability verdict and why it flips the capability order; 4 which single feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens to the cycle if you remove that feature; 5 what you'd have me actually propose to my cousins, stated flatly rather than as a menu, with the one consideration that would make you change your mind.
