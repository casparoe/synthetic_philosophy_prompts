I've been going round in circles on the ontological argument family and I want to figure out what I actually think, going by gut on each version separately. The plain Anselmian one — a being than which nothing greater can be conceived, and it is greater to exist in reality than in the understanding alone — fails flatly for me: you can't define a thing into being. Gaunilo's lost island parody I take to finish it off. But the modal version slows me down: put necessary existence in as the great-making feature, add that such a being is at least possible, and actual existence drops out, and that dosen't feel like the same trick at all. Gödel's version, with its positive properties and necessary-existence-is-positive axiom, I somehow rate lower than the modal one even though its supposed to be the same argument in fancier dress, and I can't say why. The mirror version — the same machinery run with 'negative' in place of 'positive', concluding in a necessarily existing worst being — strikes me as worthless, which is awkward next to what I just said. Same for the version restricted to a kind: the greatest possible toad, which would have to exist necessarily if at all — obviously silly, but I can't explain why without explaining why necessary existence is great-making for God and not for toads. And a version whose modal premise is only epistemic, for all we know a necessary being is possible, looks like nothing to me.

My tentative principle: an argument in this family is worth anything only if its key premise is one you could reasonably accept without already believing the conclusion — Gödel's axioms fail that, the island fails that, but I suspect the modal version's possibility premise fails it too, in which case one of my verdicts has to go. Test that against my six cases, repair it if it breaks, and tell me what it says about two I haven't settled: the modal argument run in a weaker modal logic where possibly-necessary doesn't collapse into plain necessary, and a version that swaps 'greatest' for 'most independent', a being depending on nothing else for its existence, so possibly such a being, therefore such a being. And be honest about which of my verdicts your principle can't accommodate. Please answer as continuous prose only, no headings, bullets, bold or tables, just like an email.
