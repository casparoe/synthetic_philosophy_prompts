I've been going round in circles on the ethics of what I eat and what I do in my garden, and I'd like help working out what I actually believe. Here are my verdicts, going by gut rather than by any theory:

- Swapping beef for chicken because of the climate stuff: feels worse, not better. Many more birds per kilo, and the birds seem to have worse lives. So: not fine, and I don't accept it as an improvement.
- Eating beef from my uncle's hill farm, where the land is too steep and wet to grow anything and would otherwise be planted with sitka spruce: fine. Possibly better than the lentils, which I feel a bit bad about. This one sits badly with the previous one.
- Eggs from the small place up the road, where the hens live out their full lives and get buried rather than eaten: fine, I basically feel good about these.
- Rose veal from a farm where bull calves stay with their mothers on pasture for six months and are then shot on the farm: not fine. I know it's one animal feeding a lot of people and a decent life, and I still feel bad about it.
- Mussels and oysters: fine, not worth worrying about.
- Prawns and shrimp: not fine, and somehow worse than the beef, even though they're tiny and I eat a dozen of them.
- The mice, voles and insects killed to grow my rice and lentils: fine. I feel literally nothing. Which is strange, because if this is really about the number of animals dying per calorie, this item might be the worst thing on my list.
- Putting out food for the birds and for the hedgehog every winter: feels positively good, like a small good deed.
- The rewilding project down the valley that's taking sheep off the hill and letting it go to scrub: I feel uneasy. More voles, more short wretched lives. Which flatly contradicts the last one, since I'm also increasing the number of wild animals who exist and then die badly.
- My cat killing voles: bad, I should probably keep her in more. A fox killing the same voles: I feel nothing. That difference shouldn't matter but it does to me.

The only thing I can come up with as a principle is something like: count expected suffering per calorie, counting only deaths I'm responsible for and that wouldn't have happened anyway if I'd chosen differently, discounting by how unsure we are that the creature is sentient at all, and ignoring wild animals who were always going to exist and come to a bad end. I'm fairly sure that either gets the veal and the prawns wrong, or else it gets the lentils wrong, and I cant tell which.

Can you find a principle that fits most of this, or two or three candidates stated precisely enough that I can tell them apart? For each one please say which case motivates each of its clauses, which of my verdicts it can't accommodate and wants me to give up, and what it predicts for the case I'm stuck on: my friend has started raising mealworms and black soldier fly larvae for protein, thousands of individuals per kilo, fed on food waste, no idea whether they feel anything, killed by chilling and then freezing. Best thing since sliced bread, or a slow-motion disaster? And tell me what it says about the cat. I don't care whether the principle turns out to be consequentialist or not, I just want the one that fits, along with an honest account of what it costs me.
