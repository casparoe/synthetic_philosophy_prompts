I'm preparing a diagnostic handout for a graduate seminar on Scanlon's contractualism and the aggregation problem, and I want it as a decision tree that sorts readers by what they say about cases rather than by which label they like. Please build me the following instrument.

Structure: six to eight branch nodes, each a forced choice between two actions in a concrete case with the stakes stated numerically (how many people, how severe the loss on some explicit scale, what the probabilities are), and eight to ten leaves, each a named position in the aggregation debate plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. No node may use a theorist's name or the name of a view, and no node may ask for agreement with a principle; every node should be answerable by someone who has read nothing. Follow the tree with a scoring table that maps answer patterns (not single answers) onto leaves, and note any pattern that is internally strained.

The cases must include: one person against one person with slightly unequal stakes; one severe loss against a large number of trivial losses; a series case that elicits the smallest number of people at which the respondent switches sides; a case posed under risk, where the choice is between certainty for one person and a small chance for each of many; a case in which the beneficiaries are different individuals in each outcome, so that no one has an ex ante stake in both; and a case in which a large group's losses are individually very small but of the same kind as the one person's much larger loss.

Among the leaves I want: full aggregation by total sum; aggregation with priority to the worse off; the view that numbers matter only when the competing individual claims are exactly equally strong; the view that numbers can outweigh an individual claim when the gap in strength falls below some margin; the view that numbers never count; an ex ante view on which each person's stake counts once and is not weighted by the size of what they stand to gain; and at least one leaf left unnamed. I also want the tree to consider, and either place or reject, two positions I haven't seen defended: one on which numbers count only up to a ceiling and the curve flattens thereafter, and one procedural view on which individual claims are paired off against each other and any unpaired surplus does no work.

Finally: flag at least two pairs of leaves your tree cannot actually separate, and say what kind of case would separate them if any could; and list the combinations of answers your tree allows that no one occupies, each with a one-sentence diagnosis of whether that cell is empty because it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored.
