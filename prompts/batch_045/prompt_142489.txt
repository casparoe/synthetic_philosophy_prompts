ok so this got personal way faster than I expected. My grandmother died in the fall and left me and my two cousins ninety acres a couple hours out of town — maybe forty of it tillable, the rest scrub that's been growing up into sumac since my grandfather stopped haying it. We have to tell the county our plan by spring and everyone has an opinion, and because I'm the one who was in a philosophy PhD program for a year before I left, they keep looking at me like I'm supposed to have a view. The options as I see them: cash-rent the tillable to a neighbor who'll run corn/soy and donate part of the check to the food bank; put the whole thing into pasture for another neighbor's grass-fed herd (he does that short-duration high-density grazing thing and insists the cattle "stand in for" bison and that his bird counts prove it); seed everything to native prairie and more or less walk away; or keep the hay going for a guy with draft horses. In the same couple of months I also got pulled into the feral cat colony behind my building — TNR, and I'm the one who feeds them, which I now understand is not a neutral thing — and my brother-in-law started sending me links arguing that if nobody bought pork the pigs would never exist, that their lives are good on the whole, and that therefore we're the villains. So I'd like to work out what I actually think before I have to vote on the field.

Here's my gut on a bunch of cases, and I want to know what principle, if any, makes a pattern out of them. Beef to chicken: I've always treated this as the easy win, but when I actually think about it, chicken means a lot more individual deaths for the same amount of food, and if deaths are what matter I should be eating the steer — and I still feel like beef is worse and can't say why. The crop-cultivation thing: I feel bad about the field mice killed by planting and harvest, bad in a way that feels out of proportion to the cow, and I don't have a story about why. Eggs from a friend's six backyard hens: totally fine, I don't feel anything. Eggs from a "humane" operation where the male chicks were killed: not fine, even though my purchase didn't kill any particular chick and probably didn't cause any chick's death at all. The county deer cull where there are no predators left and the alternative is winter starvation and collisions: genuinely fine, maybe good. The same cull done by darting does with contraceptive instead of shooting them: I feel better about that, and I'm not sure I should given that it just means more deer living longer and then dying anyway. My neighbor's outdoor cat killing songbirds: bugs me, but I don't think she's doing anything wrong the way I'd think it was wrong to go out and shoot a bird — and yet I do think I'd be doing something wrong if I dumped another cat into the colony, and I feed that colony, and I can't square those. Roadkill: eating a deer somebody else hit feels fine, almost respectful; eating one I hit myself feels wrong to me in a way I can't defend, since the death is equally done either way, and obviously deliberately hitting one would be wrong. My brother-in-law's pigs: I think it's obviously nonsense, but when I run the same argument about labs — nobody thinks breeding dogs for meat is fine because the dogs would otherwise not exist and had nice lives — I can't locate the difference if the pig's life really is net positive. The watershed group near me wants to put beavers back on a creek, which will drown some animals and flood some woodlots in exchange for a wetland: I feel good about it and have no idea what principle that verdict comes from. And here's the pair I keep turning over: a controlled burn that kills a lot of small animals now to prevent a fire that would kill more later strikes me as simple arithmetic and fine, whereas culling deer to prevent their starving strikes me as something I recoil from — let nature take its course — even though the arithmetic looks identical, and meanwhile I don't recoil at all from filling the bird feeder every morning.

The thing I keep falling back on is something like: what matters is the total suffering I cause, wild animals included, weighted by how cheaply I could have avoided it. But that seems to imply I should be doing a lot more than I am, and that the cats are a genuine emergency, and I believe neither of those. So: give me that principle repaired, or two or three candidate principles precise enough that I could actually apply them to a new case, with the case that motivates each clause, an honest list of which of my verdicts each one can't save, and what each says about the field — and about the bird feeder, which I've honestly never once questioned until right now.
