hey, I'm the only sixth-former on my college's Events and Speakers Committee and I've somehow ended up writing the decision note for Tuesday's meeting, about whether to let the Secular Society go ahead with their speaker in a fortnight. Quick version: they've invited a journalist who writes about religion in schools and has said things like Islam being incompatible with British values. The Islamic Society want us to cancel, and they're not saying people can't handle being disagreed with — they're saying that even with a moderator and no press, having her speak on campus makes Muslim students feel unsafe and that some will skip lessons that week. Last term we waved through a pro-life society event with big graphic pictures in the foyer after a pretty similar complaint, so I know someone will ask why this one's different.

The bit that's actually messing with my head: some outside group has said on Telegram they're coming to support the speaker, and the Islamic Society say that's the real risk now — the people turning up, not the talk. Our senior tutor has floated a rule she calls the "reactive audience clause", where we could refuse an event because of the reaction it predictably draws rather than anything the speaker actually says. I sort of feel like letting a talk get blocked by who shows up to cheer it is backwards, but I also dont see how the harm principle stuff we did in politics helps at all if you genuinely think someone's going to get hurt because of the fallout.

Could you draft the note for me — around 400 words, in proper British English please, our committee secretary corrects every "organize" — with a concrete recommendation (cancel, go ahead, or go ahead with conditions, and if conditions then which), a view on whether we should adopt her clause as a standing rule or just decide this one case on our existing rule, and the strongest version of whatever argument loses? It can't just say free speech is important, half the committee will roll their eyes at that.
