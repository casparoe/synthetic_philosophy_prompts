Ok so this is going to sound like a much weirder question than it feels like from the inside. My husband and I are in the middle of IVF right now -- we've got five embryos frozen, the genetic screening came back on all of them, and in a couple of weeks we go in and pick which one to transfer first. And I keep having this feeling I can't shake, which is that picking one means *rejecting the others*: like I'm standing in a room with five of my own possible kids, choosing one and turning my back on the rest. My husband thinks that's nuts. His line is that the ones we don't use never become anybody at all, so there's nobody there to be rejected, and that whichever embryo we'd picked, the child we ended up with would have been that child and no other. I sort of follow the argument but it does nothing for the feeling.

It got worse over christmas because my brother and I got into this. He and I are both from the same IVF cycle, different embryos, four years apart (there was a third one, never used). He says that if our parents had picked differently each time, the person sitting at that table would just have been the other one of us -- that he could have been me and I could have been him, in the sense that the same person would've shown up either way, just with different DNA and a different name. I said no, that's backwards: that embryo is where you actually came from, and a different embryo is just a different person who never got born. But then he started pushing on where the line goes and I completely fell apart. Fine, I said, imagine a sperm that's identical to the one I actually came from except for one single base pair -- is that still me or not? If it's me, he said, then what about two base pairs, and ten, and by tiny steps you end up saying I could have come from a totally different man's sperm and still been me, which is exactly what I was denying. And if it's *not* me, then a single base pair is doing all this work and I have to say why. He also brought up something I'd read about somewhere: a technique where you take an ordinary skin cell, rewind it into a stem cell, and coax it into becoming a sperm or an egg. I'm fairly sure that's only been done in mice, maybe not even properly, and I don't know if it matters whether it works in people -- but if it does, then in principle my dad could scrape cells off his arm next tuesday and you could grow a sperm from it, decades after I was conceived. Is that the same sperm as the one I came from? Is any sperm ever "the same sperm" as another, or are they just things that get manufactured fresh? I don't even know what kind of sameness I'm asking about, and that's part of why I feel like I'm losing.

Two other things got thrown in and they made the whole question feel like it's sliding out of my hands. One: a friend of ours had a baby using a donor egg where the nucleus came from one woman's egg and the rest of the cell came from another's, so the kid has DNA from three people. My brother goes "so which egg did she come from?" and I had nothing. Two: someone told me identical twins are just one embryo splitting in two, and that very occasionally it runs the other way and two embryos fuse into one person. If one embryo can become two people, or two can become one, then "the thing I came from" isn't even a stable object to point at, is it?

So what I actually want is advice on how to think, not reassurance, and please don't tell me it's a personal decision or that my feelings are valid -- I want to know what's true. Is my brother's picture coherent? Is mine? Is there a principled line between "different cell, still would've been me" and "different cell, different person", or is that distinction an illusion I've been leaning on? Whatever the strongest version of each side is, tell me what it commits me to downstream and where it starts to hurt -- including what it has to say about the splitting and fusion cases. And then the bit I actually care about: if there turns out to be no fact of the matter about which earlier cells I had to come from, does that make my feeling about the five embryos *more* reasonable or *less*? Those feel like they should pull in opposite directions and I genuinely can't tell which.
