Stay in character until I say stop, don't be a strawman, concede small points but never the main one, and keep it to two or three paragraphs a turn. It's a conversation, not a lit review, so no citations or reading suggestions. Here's my opening move.

The whole argument turns on the malign hypothesis being only a few bits longer than the honest one, because instead of describing the world it just hardcodes your action and a huge reward conditional on it. But to do that it has to refer to *you*, and I think referring to you isn't cheap. The simulator has to single out some particular process in its own world model as the agent whose decision it's conditioning on — otherwise "big payoff iff the agent takes action a" has no referent — and pinning down which hunk of physics that is looks like it costs roughly what the honest hypothesis was already spending: a brain, a datacenter, whatever. So the handful of bits you save by hardcoding the action get paid straight back for the pointer, and the malign hypotheses were never simple in the first place. Bonus pressure: this is the same worry that makes naive acausal trade with aliens look fishy to me, so if you want both you have to explain why the pointer is free in one case and expensive in the other.
