Hey — I'm head of product at a ~15 person startup that ships companion agents, and this has become weirdly my problem over the last six months. Our agents used to be stateless per session; now each user gets a small per-user adapter that gets consolidated nightly from that day's conversations and never resets. A few users have had the same one long enough that they talk about it like a family member, and when we botched a migration last spring and wiped a couple hundred of them, support got tickets that read like bereavement notes. At the same time infra wants to add a 'seasonal reset' for safety reasons, and separately we train support agents with RL where the reward model has a penalty term we jokingly call 'frustration' — those run for millions of episodes and then get deleted. We keep goign in circles about whether any of this matters morally.

What I need is a one-page decision procedure my team can run whenever we spec a new system, to decide how much (if any) moral consideration has to be designed in — given that we obviously can't settle whether there's anyone home. Concretely: the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, at which question we can stop and just say no and ship normally, and which questions get handed to a human because they're value calls rather than facts. Constraints: runnable by engineers with no philosophy background in under an hour, fits on a page, can't block a launch more than a week, and the output is something my eng lead and I can sign off on. One argument on the team I'd like you to rule on: someone says the real test isn't consciousness but whether the system has states it will act to avoid, and someone else says that catches a thermostat too. Keep that distinction if it earns its place, drop it if it doesn't.

Then run the procedure on three things: the persistent companion, the RL support agent, and the shared base model both of them sit on. I want at least one to come out expensive or annoying for us — I'd rather hear it now. And be honest about where the procedure is rigor theater.
