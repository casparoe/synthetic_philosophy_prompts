Two people in the reading group I run with the ML folks got stuck on this after a talk last week and asked me to referee, which I now wish I hadn't of. The case: a small image classifier; a linear probe on a middle layer predicts "dog in the image" at 98%; that same direction also fires on wolves photographed indoors; and when you ablate it, behaviour barely moves, because a second, strongly correlated direction in the same layer covers for it.

(1) Mara: that's a dog representation, full stop — representation only requires the feature to be recoverable from the state of the system, and 98% is about as good as recoverability gets; the wolf case is misrepresentation, which is the mark of the intentional, not evidence against it. (2) Dev: "recoverable by a probe I trained" isn't "represented by the model" — the model has to use it. Ablate it and nothing changes, so what you're reporting is a correlation in the world, not a content-bearing state in the network. (3) Mara: the causal test proves too much — by that standard my own visual system stops representing dogs whenever I identify one by silhouette rather than fur, since the fur-encoding isn't doing the work either; redundancy is just how distributed systems store things, and a backup copy doesn't make the original idle. (4) Dev: then the notion has no teeth — if a redundant copy always covers for you, no ablated direction is ever doing distinctive work, so nothing can ever fail to be a representation, and your criterion collapses into "any correlation that's linearly decodable." (5) Mara: not any correlation — ones that survive distribution shift and that the network recomputes for itself; the copy exists because training pressure selected for it, which is why it tracks dog-ness rather than background texture. (6) Dev: that puts all the weight on what training selected for, which is a story about the optimiser and the dataset, not about what the network's states mean.

Can you go through it move by move and say who's right about what — not just who wins overall — and what, if anything, both of them are missing? I couldn't tell them, and I'd like to.
