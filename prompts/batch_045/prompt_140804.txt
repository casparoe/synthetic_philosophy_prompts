I have been going back and forth with a colleague about whether the Solomonoff-style reconstruction of Occam's razor survives the arbitrariness of the reference machine, and we have stalled at roughly 0.35 (my credence) against 0.85 (hers). I want your number, but mostly I want the decomposition that produces it, because I am no longer sure the two of us are disagreeing about the thing we think we are disagreeing about.

The proposition, stated as precisely as I can make it:

**(P)** The choice of reference universal machine carries no epistemic weight for the use of a universal prior as a *justification* of simplicity in science, in the regime science is actually conducted — finite samples, noisy data, competing hypotheses that differ by a parameter or two, and theories whose content includes continuously valued constants.

To make the question tractable I have been using a crude piece of vocabulary of my own. Fix a data set and the two best-supported hypotheses. Let the *compression margin* be the difference in shortest-description length between them under a given machine; by the invariance theorem, two universal machines differ in their description lengths by at most an additive constant independent of the string, so whenever the compression margin is smaller than that translation constant, the razor's ranking of the hypotheses is *machine-relative* — the framework does not say which one is simpler. Call such a case *fragile*, and let the *fragile fraction* be the proportion of real theory-choice episodes that are fragile in this sense.

My low credence comes from suspecting that the fragile cases are not the exotic ones, that the translation constants between machines anyone would actually adopt are not small, and that in a fragile case an appeal to the convergence theorem simply declines to answer the question that was asked. My colleague's high credence comes from the fact that the constant is bounded while the likelihood dominates the prior at an exponential rate in the sample size, so that machine-relativity is a rounding error with a short half-life. I do not think we have located the real disagreement yet.
