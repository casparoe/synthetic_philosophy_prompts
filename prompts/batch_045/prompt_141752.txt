I get the idea in the abstract; what I want is to see where the two actually come apart.

So here's the version of the case I care about, which isn't the standard one. Forget the duplicate planet far away. Instead imagine it's *this* Earth that slowly changes: starting around now and finishing fifty years later, every lake, river, cloud and tap has its H2O very gradually swapped out for XYZ, the superficially identical stuff with a different microstructure. Nobody notices because all the observable properties match, and assume nobody in the community in question has ever done chemistry or deferred to anyone who has. Nobody gets transported anywhere; it's one continuous linguistic community, talking to each other the whole time. By the end the H2O is essentially gone — though there's some left in sealed bottles in a museum, a bit in a Mars colony, and a few drops in a lab somewhere.

The things I want untangled:

I especially want to know whether a generation can end up meaning something different from the people who taught them the word without anyone noticing.

Is that a real metaphysical disagreement about what reference is, or just a fight about which way of talking is more useful, or is it something else — like a disagreement about what "our word" even picks out across time?
