hey — i'm in a seminar on consciousness right now and we just did higher-order theories, the Rosenthal-ish idea that what makes a mental state conscious is that you have a distinct higher-order representation directed at it. For the class zine I want to write a parody and could use a hand with it.

Write it as a short internal packet from a hospital's Consciousness Review Board: a one-paragraph memo on top stating the billing rule, then three or four very short insurance-style denial or appeal letters, about 600 words total, in deadpan bureaucratic American English. The board decides which of a patient's experiences actually counted as conscious, and they take the higher-order view completely literally:

- a claim for pain during a procedure denied because at the time the patient had no higher-order representation of being in pain, so there was nothing it was like for her and nothing to reimburse
- an appeal where the higher-order state got its target wrong — it represented her as being in agony in the left knee while the first-order state was in the right knee — and the board has to rule on whether she was conscious of a pain that wasn't there or not conscious of anything at all
- something that casually disqualifies infants and nonhuman animals, and a smart home thermostat that gets approved because it carries a state about its own reading of the temperature

No winking, no explanations aimed at the reader, no cartoon behaviorism: the jokes have to come out of the theory's real commitments — especially about what happens when a higher-order representation has the wrong target or no target at all — and out of the gap between what the board concludes and what the patient keeps insisting she felt.

At the end add a short note saying where the piece leans on a contested reading of those mismatch/targetless cases, how the letters would have to change if the other reading is right, and which one objection to higher-order theories you think the parody genuinely lands versus one it only looks like it lands. thanks!
