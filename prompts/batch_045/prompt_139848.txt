My roommate is doing a crimnology module and we got into a huge pointless argument the other night about undercover sting operations, and I keep going round in circles on it. Here's the practice as I understand it: the police invent a fake fencing operation, or pose as a teenager in a chat room, or run a whole fake terrorist plot where they supply the van, the inert "explosive", the money and a lot of encouragement — and then they arrest whoever takes the bait, even though the "drugs" were flour and the bomb never could have gone off. And then, in a lot of jurisdictions in one version or another, the defendant gets to say: no, this was manufactured, I'd never have done it if you hadn't come to me. Somewhere a line gets drawn between catching someone who was already disposed to do this and creating a criminal who wasn't. In some places the question is put as being about the defendant — was he already involved in this kind of thing, did he jump at it or did he have to be talked into it, how many times did they have to come back, did they have any real reason to suspect him in the first place. In others it's put as being about the police instead: did they do more than just present the sort of opportunity any ordinary person might have been offered, or did they apply the kind of pressure that would have pulled in pretty much anyone.

What's bugging me is that I read a bit of metaphysics last term and now I can't unsee the problem. Someone in my reading group mentioned that philosophers worry about "finks" and "masks" for dispositions — the rough idea as she explained it being that a thing can genuinely have a disposition that would get switched off at exactly the moment it matters (a fink?), or can lack a disposition altogether but still produce the outward sign because something else steps in and produces it (a mask?). I'm probably mangling this. But it seems to me that the government's intervention in a sting could be either of those things, and the doctrine has no way to tell them apart from the outside. Offering the bribe supplies the condition under which a corrupt disposition would show itself. But turning up the pressure might also break down a normally honest person — so the disposition was there, and it got switched off by the very thing that was supposed to reveal it. Or the person's clean record might just have been because nobody had ever offered him anything, so the sting removes a mask rather than revealing a trait. And everyone has some price, so what the police are really measuring is a threshold, not a yes/no property, and the more they escalate the more they can guarantee they'll clear it.

So, what I actually want to know: what does this whole practice presuppose about dispositions? First, reconstruct the implicit theory — what would dispositions have to be like for "was he already like that, or did we make him?" to be a sensible question at all? Does the doctrine strictly need something like a fink-versus-mask distinction, or can a plain conditional analysis ("he would have done it if offered") do all the work, and if you try to patch it with something like "would still have done it without the government's pressure," doesn't that collapse, since the only way to test it is to run the sting again without the pressure, which is exactly the thing you can't do? Second, where is the practice underdetermined — do both versions of the test (the one about the defendant, the one about the police) presuppose the same theory, or is one committed to a property of an individual and the other to something statistical about a population, or to a normative claim about what the state may do rather than a metaphysical one at all? Third, what would actually have to change if we gave up the metaphysics and went deflationary — say, "predisposition" is just a name for a pattern of past behaviour, or just a prediction about future behaviour? Would we convict everyone who takes the bait, or nobody, and would the part where we punish people for crimes that were impossible to commit survive that? Feel free to tell me I've set the whole thing up wrong, but please say where exactly.
