The proposition: that an argument of the poverty-of-the-stimulus form succeeds for at least one nontrivial linguistic phenomenon. Spell it out as the conjunction of three claims: (i) speakers reliably converge on some phenomenon P; (ii) the linguistic experience available to a child does not determine P, in the sense that the critical evidence — the relevant example types, or systematic evidence about which structures are impossible — is absent or vanishingly rare in child-directed speech; (iii) the best explanation of (i) given (ii) is that substantial knowledge of P is innately specified, rather than arrived at by domain-general learning from indirect distributional evidence, by statistical inference over a hypothesis space the child brings to the task, or by non-linguistic constraints on computation and representation.

Three stipulations to fix the target. First, choose your own working case — whichever phenomenon you think gives the argument its best shot — and say why you chose it: for instance structure-dependence in question formation (speakers form the interrogative by displacing the auxiliary of the main clause, as in a question about the man who is tall, never the auxiliary embedded inside the relative clause), or the degradation of long construals out of embedded questions (what did John wonder whether Mary bought resists the reading on which what is the thing bought), or constraints on anaphora. Second, take innate to be graded, and say which grade you are pricing: richly specified domain-specific representational content; a domain-specific but skeletal bias that only canalizes learning; a domain-general architectural or computational bias; or merely not learned from the specific evidence cited in premise (ii). State whether your number is even well defined without settling that, and if not, give the number conditional on the reading you favor plus a range across the others. Third, say what would count as resolution — a cross-linguistic corpus result plus a learner trained on the child's actual input, a derivation of the phenomenon from independent computational constraints, or something else.
