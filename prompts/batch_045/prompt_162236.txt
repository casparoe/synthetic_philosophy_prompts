I keep triping over this fight about original versus derived intentionality. Here’s how I half-understand it. Searle says conscious mental states have original (or intrinsic) intentionality: they are really about things in a way no artifact is. A coin-sorting machine built to sort US quarters — Dennett calls it a two-bitser, I think, and in the wandering version it gets moved to a country where the local coin happens to match quarters in weight and thickness, so it goes on sorting coins fine — only has derived intentionality, borrowed from the intentions of its designers and users. Dennett replies that this distinction won’t do the work Searle wants. If the machine’s discriminating behavior can come unmoored from its makers and pick up a new aboutness from its new environment, and if our own intentionality is itself a product of natural selection rather than of an intending designer, then either we have no original intentionality either, or derived intentionality is not the second-class status Searle takes it to be.
