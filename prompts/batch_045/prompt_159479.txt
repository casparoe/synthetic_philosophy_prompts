My aunt died last summer after a brutal couple of years, and at the funeral somebody said — kindly, not cruelly — that it was probably "her karma working itself out." Its been rattling around in my head ever since. I keep thinking: if karma is supposed to be a theory of moral desert, then saying that has to mean she somehow *deserved* it. But she had no memory of whatever she supposedly did and no continuity of character with whoever did it, and I cant see how desert survives that.

So build me a case where the two pictures come apart in exactly that direction: karmic desert says yes, this person deserves this suffering, and an identity-sensitive retributivist has to say no — with the gap generated by the case rather than stipulated. Constraints: one paragraph, nothing sci-fi, no gods or cosmic accountants keeping score, and no "well, karma just says so." The surplus desert has to fall out of a mechanism you actually state. The one I keep circling is compounding: the residue doesnt sit still, it spreads along the causal consequences the act set going, so a later bearer inherits a share of downstream harm she neither intended nor could have foreseen — which would also make whats owed bigger than anything proportionate to the original act. Use that, or swap in a better mechanism if youve got one.

Then show the work: the verdict each picture gives and why, which single feature of the case is doing the work, what happens if you delete that feature (does it collapse into ordinary retribution, or into pure bookkeeping that isnt desert at all?), and the strongest thing a defender of karma says to wriggle out. Point of the whole exercise: I want to know whether "it was her karma" is a desert claim or just a causal claim wearing desert's clothes.
