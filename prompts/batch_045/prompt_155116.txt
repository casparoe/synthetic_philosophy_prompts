The position to be translated is called risk-weighted expected utility, and I will state it as I understand it. Take a gamble with finitely many outcomes and rank them by how good they are. Instead of weighting each outcome by its own probability, weight the best outcome by a transformed probability of getting that outcome or better; weight the next by the difference between the transformed probability of getting it or better and the one already used; and so on down. The transformation is increasing, fixes zero and one, and when it is the identity the whole thing collapses back to ordinary expected utility. The point of the construction is that it separates three things that expected utility runs together: how likely you take the outcomes to be, how much you care about them, and a third thing, how you weight uncertainty itself. In expected utility a curved utility function is doing two jobs at once — the tenth million matters less than the first, *and* I dislike gambles — and this framework says those can come apart: you can be nearly linear in money and still systematically shift weight toward the worse end of the distribution. The claim I want tested is that this is rationally permissible, not a bias and not a disguised doubt about your own probabilities. The other side of the disagreement says any departure from expected utility is either a mistake about the probabilities or a taste that should have been folded into the utility function, and that the standard violation patterns only show people are inconsistent. The pattern that motivates the whole thing is the familiar one: someone who prefers a certain million to a gamble of a ten percent chance at five million, an eighty-nine percent chance at a million, and one percent at nothing, and who also prefers a ten percent chance at five million to an eleven percent chance at a million — the second pair is the first pair with both options scaled by the same probability, and expected utility says that scaling should not matter.

Two structural features I think matter for the translation, and that I want you to deal with rather than skip. First, the ranking is by how good the outcomes are, so the same utility function both scales the outcomes and decides their order; on my side the ordering is fixed in advance by the numeraire, worst losses first, and comes from nothing resembling a utility function. Second, because the weighting attaches to ranks within a single gamble, it cannot be applied bet by bet and added up.
