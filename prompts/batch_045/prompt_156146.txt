I've been reading proposals to abolish suffering by rewriting its biological basis — the idea that nothing anyone ever feels should be worse than neutral, while everything that makes a life go well is preserved or improved. I keep watching the same exchange, and I want to know whether the move it turns on has a name and a literature.

First instance. The critic says: if pain no longer hurts, it will not make you act, and you will leave your hand on the stove. The advocate says the hurting can be removed while the urgency stays — you still get a signal that seizes attention and drives withdrawal, minus the part that feels bad. The critic says those are the same part. The advocate then points to cases I have read about in which people register a damaging stimulus perfectly well and are not distressed by it, so the two components must be separable in principle. My suspicion is that this misses the critic's point: separating them is easy in the direction where the motivation disappears, and what the proposal actually needs is the direction where the motivation survives the loss of the badness.

Second instance. The critic says that without suffering we lose what seems to depend on it — courage, grief, pity, the sense that anything is at stake. The advocate replies that each survives in improved form: danger still faced as a challenge, love and loss still felt, another person's plight still responded to, but carried on a positive gradient rather than a negative one. Here too I suspect the reply keeps the name of the thing while changing what made it that thing: a challenge you cannot be daunted by may not be courage at all.

The pattern I cannot name is this. A proposal promises to eliminate some property, and protects that promise by claiming that everything the property was supposed to do is preserved in a substitute, where the substitute is specified only functionally — by what it does, never by what it is like. Is there an established concept this corresponds to? I am not after a label: I want the carving. What exactly is the move, when is it a genuine dissociation and when is it a sleight of hand, and how does it differ from the nearest candidates? It is not ordinary equivocation, since the two descriptions are competing accounts of one state rather than two senses of one word, and it is not the pattern of retreating to a weaker claim under pressure, since nothing is conceded. If there is no established concept, I would like one proposed: a name, a definition, and the boundary cases where it stops applying.
