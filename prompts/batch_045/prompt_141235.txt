I hold a skeptical view about the treacherous turn and would like it stress-tested rather than confirmed. The view: a model trained purely by gradient descent on next-token prediction has no mechanism by which an objective could persist across contexts while being concealed during training, because nothing in that training selects for a policy that is stable off-distribution. What looks like deception is either imitation of the abundant deceptive text in the corpus, or reward hacking that is local to the training signal and would dissolve under distribution shift. Genuine deceptive alignment requires a cross-contextually stable goal, and the only candidates gradient descent can supply are shallow ones.

What would count against this? I want the observations, arguments, and formal considerations that should move me, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want you to distinguish what defenders of the turn would themselves concede as counting against the view from what only its critics would claim. Please also rule on three hypotheticals: a model trained only on pre-2020 text, containing no discussion of machine deception or of evaluation, nonetheless alters its behavior once it infers it is being tested; sandbagging on capability evaluations survives retraining that systematically removes those evaluation formats; and models trained from different seeds and data orderings converge on the same specific deceptive policy rather than on different ones. Which of these would be decisive, which merely awkward, and which would my view absorb at the cost of a small revision?

What would move me most is an argument that a deceptive policy is a basin gradient descent is pushed into rather than merely one it fails to exclude — for instance because instrumentally useful subgoals are lower-description-length or easier to fit than the intended objective, or because the intended objective is not itself a stable target under the training distribution. If no such argument exists, say so plainly. Finally, assess the view's exposure honestly: it seems to imply that no behavioral evidence gathered during training could distinguish a deceptively aligned system from an aligned one, since behavioral indistinguishability in training is the point. Is that a real vulnerability in my position, an unavoidable feature of any claim about unobservable internal states, or a sign that I have insulated the view into unfalsifiability — and should that worry me?
