I've been asked to contribute a short position statement on vagueness to a graduate reading group, and before I commit the package to print I would like someone to stress-test it for coherence. Here is where I have landed, in no particular order.

1. Vagueness is a feature of representation, not of the world. There are no vague objects and no vague facts: when it is unsettled whether a given pile is a heap, that unsettledness is never inherited from any unsettledness in the pile itself.

2. I am not an epistemicist. In a borderline case I do not think there is a determinate verdict that we are somehow shut out from knowing. I think the case is genuinely unsettled, and that this is the whole story rather than a way of marking our ignorance.

3. Vagueness gives me no reason to revise logic. I want classical logic to survive intact: excluded middle stays valid, and I do not want to move to a many-valued or intuitionistic logic in order to accommodate borderline cases.

4. I regiment unsettledness with a determinacy operator, and I want it to behave like an ordinary well-behaved modal: determinacy implies truth, distributes over the conditional, and iterates, so that if p is determinate then it is determinate that p is determinate, and if p is indeterminate then it is determinate that p is indeterminate. In particular, I want indeterminacy itself never to be indeterminate.

5. Higher-order vagueness is data to be accommodated, not an artifact to be explained away. It is not merely that we cannot locate the edge of the borderline zone; it is vague where that edge lies, and vague where that second-order vagueness gives out, with no sharp level at which the hierarchy terminates.

6. Faultless disagreement is possible. Two competent and fully informed speakers can disagree about a borderline case, and neither has made a mistake; neither is under any obligation to retract, and neither has anything to retract.

7. Assertion is governed by a truth norm, or at any rate a knowledge norm: one should not assert what is not true. At the same time I want to allow that a judge applying a vague standard to a borderline case can rule correctly even though the sentence she utters in ruling is not true.

8. Methodologically, meaning is determined by use, and I am suspicious of any semantic theory that builds meaning-constitutive facts into its account which speakers could not in principle manifest in their practice. That suspicion is a large part of what drives me away from epistemicism in the first place.

What I want is an audit rather than a survey of theories of vagueness or a recommendation of some rival package. Go through the set and tell me which commitments genuinely pull against each other, ordered by how severe each conflict is. For each real conflict, say precisely which claim would have to be abandoned or weakened, and what that costs me elsewhere in the set. Then identify any pair that only looks like a conflict and explain why the appearance arises. I would also like to know whether there is one revision that would stabilize more of the set than the alternatives, though I do not want to be told which overall position I should end up holding. I genuinely do not know where the strain in this package lies, which is why I am asking.
