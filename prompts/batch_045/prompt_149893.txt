After a long argument last night with a friend — a linguist who holds that the 2021 "stochastic parrots" paper settled the question, and who describes these systems as stitching together linguistic forms observed in their training data according to probabilistic information about how they combine, without reference to meaning — I am trying to work out why neither of us moved an inch. We agreed on every empirical point I could raise: that the training objective is next-token prediction over text; that internal states can be shown to track unobserved variables of the domain being described, not merely surface statistics; that adding instruction tuning changes behaviour without changing the objective; and that the failure profile is uneven — confident confabulation here, brittle reversal of a relation there, superhuman extrapolation somewhere else. She still says no understanding; I still resist saying that; and neither of us could say what would change the other's mind.

My hypothesis is that this is a clash of evidential standards: for her, a mechanism not causally hooked up to the world is disqualified in advance, so no behaviour could count as evidence, while for me a system that degrades in the way human learners degrade, and transfers to tasks sharing nothing with the training surface, would count. Is that right, or is a hidden empirical question — about compositionality, about what the internal states are actually doing, about which ablations matter — carrying the weight? Or are we answering different questions under one word: one about mechanism, one about whether taking the intentional stance toward the system is warranted or merely useful?

Two hypothetical results came up that we could not agree how to use. First, a "retrieval twin": a system that matches a frontier model on every behavioural probe we can construct, but which is provably structure-free — a verified nearest-neighbour lookup over an enormous memorised set of continuations, with a machine-checked proof that it computes no compositional state. Second, a "silent tracker": a system whose internal states provably track the unobserved state of a simulated domain, but which fails the behavioural probes. For each, tell me what each of us is committed to saying, and whether the side that bites the bullet has revealed that its "understanding" was always shorthand for something else.

Give me the diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of the literature, and then tell me honestly what would count as progress given it — a distinction, an experiment, a formal result, a change of question — or why no progress should be expected.
