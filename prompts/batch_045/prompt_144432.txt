I'm applying for a one-year postdoctoral fellowship and need a two-page proposal. The question I want funded: suppose a contradiction were derived from the standard axioms of set theory (ZFC). Most of the philosophical discussion I have encountered treats this as one dramatic event — mathematics collapses, or it doesn't — but the practically and philosophically interesting question is which parts of mathematics we would actually have to give up, and by what standard we would choose among the candidate repairs: weakening an axiom scheme, retreating to a proper fragment, changing the underlying logic rather than the axioms, or reinterpreting the quantifiers so that the derivation no longer goes through.

Here is the angle I want developed. Call a result fragile if it is lost under every repair we could regard as acceptable, and robust if it survives under all of them. My suspicion is that this fragile/robust line will not line up with the usual divides — pure versus applied, classical versus constructive, central versus merely technical — and that wherever the line falls is evidence about what mathematics is answerable to, possibly something other than truth. I am not confident, and part of the project is deciding whether the distinction is even well defined, since what counts as an acceptable repair may be precisely what is in dispute.

Write the proposal: state the question so that it has a determinate answer; explain why the two obvious standards — preserve as many theorems as possible, or simply restore consistency by whatever means — are inadequate; specify what I would actually do month by month over twelve months, given that no one can survey all of mathematics; set out the possible findings and what would discriminate between them; and say honestly what would count as the project failing. One complication the proposal should face head-on: repairing so as to keep the mathematics that empirical science uses, and repairing so as to keep the proofs mathematicians find explanatory, may pull toward different repairs, and it is not obvious which should win. Assume the reader is a generalist philosopher. I will supply the references myself, so do not include a bibliography.
