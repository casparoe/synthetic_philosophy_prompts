Write a dialogue between an endurantist and a perdurantist working through a case neither of them has encountered before. A cosmos plays out exactly the same total history over and over: cycle follows cycle with no gap and no qualitative difference anywhere between them, and each copy of a given human life is intrinsically identical to every other copy, memories included, so that nobody ever remembers an earlier cycle. Their question is whether this is one person living the same life infinitely many times, or infinitely many numerically distinct people who happen to be exact recurrent duplicates.

Make the two speakers equally competent and have them argue from their own theory's resources, responding to each other's actual points rather than past them. The perdurantist should work with the idea of a person as an aggregate of temporal parts and should say how far that aggregate extends; the endurantist should work with the idea of a person as wholly present at each time at which she exists. Concretely: the perdurantist must face whether the number of people in this cosmos has become arbitrary, whether questions like how long she lived or how many times she has lived it remain well-posed on her account, and whether she now needs a brute fact about the extent of the aggregate, the very sort of primitive identity fact usually treated as the endurantist's burden. The endurantist must face what in her ontology distinguishes one wholly present entity recurring from many duplicate entities: is she pushed toward brute haecceities, or toward something counterpart-like, and if the latter, has she conceded the perdurantist's machinery? Both should also say whether their view's usual selling point does any work here, given that the puzzle is not about change or temporary intrinsics at all but about repetition: consider the perdurantist's tidy treatment of change and the endurantist's fit with ordinary talk of one thing persisting. Close with each conceding one real point and with a crisp statement of what remains genuinely in dispute.
