So I dont want another explainer, I've read enough of those. What I want is the spec. If I'm going to accept some cosmological picture as the one that lets me stop worrying, what does it have to deliver? Write the requirements list for a theory of the universe that gets to tell me I'm not a momentary fluctuation with fake memories. For each requirement, tell me the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than something it'd be nice to have, and tell me which ones you think are non-negotiable and which are just tastes dressed up as requirements.

Things I want on the list, and add whatever I'm missing:

- It obviously has to fit the real data (CMB, the expansion, all that). But I want you to say explicitly why fitting the data can't settle this, since a fluctuation brain has "data" too — it just isn't caused by anything.
- Something about counting. "How many observers of each kind does this picture say there are" has to be an answerable question, and I suspect this is where most pictures quietly cheat. Also: what even gets counted as one? As far as I can tell, the cheaper the fluctuation the more often it happens, so if all you need is the minimum physical thing that realises my current conscious moment, then whole brains with bodies are a rounding error and the numbers get astronomically worse. Does the requirement have to be stated so that it doesn't depend on where you draw that line, or is picking the reference class just a separate choice the physics can't hand you?
- Something about records, not just experiences. Trusting my own memory isn't enough — I want my phone photos, my dads handwriting on a birthday card, the fossil record, all of it mostly accurate. Is that a different requirement from "your memories are reliable", and does a theory have to earn it or can it just stipulate a real past?
- There's also this case I can't get out of my head: a fluctuation whose memories happen to be 90% accurate about a past that really did happen. Does the theory owe me more than 'most of your memories are true', and if so what?
- The self-undermining thing. My understanding of it: if a theory says observers like me are almost all fluctuations, then accepting it destroys the reasons I had for accepting it, and you can't coherently sit in that position. Is that a real desideratum or is it a bit of a trick — it feels like it would let you dismiss any theory you dislike by calling it self-undermining.
- The low-entropy-starting-point thing, whatever the right version of that is, plus why having it doesn't automatically make the problem go away.

Then the bit I care most about: is some subset of these jointly unsatisfiable? My vague sense is that you can't have all of (a) the universe goes on more or less forever in something like its current accelerating state, (b) observers with a long real history are the typical kind of observer, and (c) no stipulation bolted on purely to kill the fluctuation problem. If that's the real impossibility, show me the argument; if some other pair or triple is, tell me which. And tell me which single requirement on your list does the most work in ruling out actual candidate pictures, and which requirement on my list above is really just one camp's prejudice.
