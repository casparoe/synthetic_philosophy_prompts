Im taking a seminar on territory and migration this term and a friend in it walked me into an argument I cant find the exit from. Her version, more or less:

(1) A rule backed by force is legitimate only if it can be justified to each person it coerces.
(2) Immigration law is backed by force -- fences, detention, deportation -- and the people kept out are the ones the force is actually used on, so the law coerces them too, not just citizens.
(3) So any immigration restriction has to be justifiable to the excluded foreigners, not only to the people already inside.
(4) Justifiable to someone doesnt mean tossing them a bone; it means counting their interests equally with everyone elses.
(5) Count everyones interests equally and the numbers do the rest: there are hundreds of millions of people who would move if they could, their gains from moving are big, and the losses to current members from letting them in are mostly small and diffuse. So exclusion loses nearly every time.
(6) So a state may exclude essentially no one, and current borders are basically unjust.
(7) But nothing in (1)-(5) mentions states. Any association whose membership is unchosen and shapes your life chances -- a household, a monastery with a common purse, a doctors patient list, a hiring hall, a commune on an island with a fixed food supply -- sits in exactly the same position.
(8) So I cant keep a stranger out of my spare bedroom, the island commune has to admit whoever rows up and split the food evenly, and my seminar has to let everyone in the city enroll.

When I pushed her on (8) she said maybe she bites the bullet on households, but the commune and the seminar case make that look pretty desperate, and it still leaves her saying something is wrong with almost every exclusion everywhere.

I'd rather keep (1), (2), and the thought that small associations really can exclude than swallow (8), so I know something in the middle has to go -- but (4) and (5) both read like plain common sense to me and I cant see the seam between them. Which step is actually broken, why did it look fine when it was doing its work, and what does rejecting it cost someone who still wants to say rich states borders are deeply unjust? If more than one step is doing something sneaky I'd like to know which one is the cheapest to give up and what the alternatives each have to pay.
