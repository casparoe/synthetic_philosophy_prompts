I host a once-a-month thing at a brewery near my job that we call Think Night -- usually 12 to 18 people, 90 minutes, almost nobody with any philosophy background: a couple nurses, an HVAC guy, two software people, my cousin who works dispatch. I'm a pharmacy tech and I've been reading about this stuff on my own after shifts, so I probably use the wrong words for things.

I want to do lying vs. misleading on them: whether theres a real moral difference between flat-out saying something false and getting somebody to believe something false without ever saying anything false yourself. I don't want to explain it first and I don't want anyone to hear those two words until after we're done -- I want them to walk into it, and to notice that they feel like they've got clean hands while doing exactly the same damage. Ideally they leave arguing about whether the difference lives in what the speaker said or in what the hearer had to figure out for themselves.

What I have in my head is a game in pairs: one person can see how things actually stand, the other person has to make a call, and they get paid off differently so the person who knows has a reason to want the chooser to get it wrong. Score in poker chips or beer tickets, no real money. Then some rule change partway through that makes plain lying impossible but leaves the steering available -- I was thinking a third player who can veto any message that isn't literally true, or a round where the chooser picks the question instead of the knower picking the statement, or even a round where the chooser is told to take everything completely literally and do no reading between the lines. Whatever you think actually makes it land. I also want a little gut-check slip at the end where the people who knew write down how clean they feel and the choosers write down how played they feel, and we put them side by side -- I bet those two numbers don't match and that mismatch is the whole evening.

Can you design the thing for me: materials, roles, rules, payoffs, the rule changes, a timeline for the 90 minutes, and a debrief that maps what actually happened back onto the question? Be honest about what will go wrong the first time I run it and what I should do about it, and about the part of this question the game just can't produce and I'll have to say out loud in my own words.

Give it to me as exactly five numbered items, a sentence or two each: one, setup and materials; two, rules and payoffs; three, the rule changes; four, the debrief; five, what breaks and what the game can't do. No intro and no wrap-up.
