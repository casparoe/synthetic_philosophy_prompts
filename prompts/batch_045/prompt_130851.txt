hey — I'm in a social epistemology seminar this term and we did the bubble / echo chamber distinction last week (bubble = you just never run into the other side, so exposure fixes it; echo chamber = the group hands you a standing reason to distrust *anyone* outside it, so throwing more facts at you does nothing). For fun I want to parody it.

Could you write me a flat-pack assembly manual for the "EchoChamber 3000", IKEA-style: deadpan sincere voice, numbered steps, plus a short troubleshooting / FAQ section at the end, ~600 words total. It should be sold as the upgrade to the cheaper "Bubble 100" model it replaces, and every joke should come from the actual machinery rather than from jokes bolted on:

- the Bubble 100 ships with nothing but a filter, and a single outside source punctures it completely — hence the upgrade;
- the 3000's main component is a discrediting module, which has to be installed *after* the in-house authority figure, or it eats the authority too;
- and the module has to apply to literally every outsider, including anyone reporting that the module exists — otherwise it recurses on itself.

The troubleshooting section is where I want it to bite: "members keep accepting evidence from outside" (you've only built a bubble, see upgrade kit); "a member still trusts a childhood friend who disagrees" (see the loyalty-test attachment, which reframes the friend as already compromised); "a fact-checker has debunked our claims" (pre-installed, see clause 4).

Two constraints: never use the words "echo chamber" or "epistemic bubble" anywhere in the manual, and never mention social media — it should read like it's for building any kind of community. And don't let the voice wink; a reader who knows the literature should get that the author has it right, and someone who doesn't should still work out for themselves that exposure isn't the cure. Maybe one typo usally helps it feel like a real manual.
