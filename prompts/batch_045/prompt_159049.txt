I host a small audio-only interview series — forty minutes an episode, and most of the listeners find it through LessWrong or through the alignment-adjacent corners of the rationalist blogosphere. Next week's guest is a machine-learning theorist with a forthcoming paper whose central claim I would summarise like this: the four-way split of Goodhart failures into regressional, extremal, causal and adversarial cases is misleading, because every case anyone actually cares about is causal. On her account, a proxy stops tracking a target when the act of optimising it intervenes on the causal system that generated the correlation, so the right object of study is not the conditional distribution of the target given the proxy but the distribution of the target under an intervention that sets the proxy. The practical moral she draws is that the standard mitigation menu — optimising less hard, stopping early, ensembling several proxies, penalties that keep the policy near the distribution the proxy was fitted on — consists of roundabout ways of limiting the size of that intervention, and that what we should really be doing is holding the causal parents of the target fixed while we optimise.

I have read her draft and my honest reaction is that the collapse is either trivial or false, and I want to find out which without being rude about it. She also has one stock move I would like the questions to make progressively harder for her: whenever an objection is raised, she answers that the case in question is just distribution shift, as if that were a reduction rather than a relabelling.

Please give me ten to twelve questions, ordered as I should ask them, phrased so that a listener who knows the four-way taxonomy but not the causal formalism can follow. Concretely, I want at least one question on each of the following, in each case with the follow-up keyed to the answer I expect her to give:

Pure selection with no intervention. Selecting fellowship applicants by test score or grant proposals by citation count does not change any candidate: the proxy and the target are both properties of the same item, and choosing the item is conditioning, not intervening. Yet the realised quality of the selected cohort falls short of what the proxy predicted. Ask her where that shortfall comes from on her account. I expect her to say that selection changes the composition of the pool, or that the correlation was estimated off the selected distribution. The follow-up should press on whether that concession hands back the entire territory: if the regressional case reduces to distribution shift, what did calling it causal buy? A second follow-up should ask whether the size of the shortfall is predicted by the tail behaviour of the noise — extreme selections being noise-inflated under heavy tails, mild under light tails — and whether that prediction is a causal one or a purely statistical one.

Saturating proxies. A proxy confined to a bounded range while the target is unbounded will stop tracking simply because it runs out of headroom, with nothing causal breaking. I expect her to reply that the ceiling is itself a structural feature of the measurement. The follow-up should ask what, on that reply, would count as a non-causal case at all, and whether the taxonomy is being collapsed by stipulation.

Tracking versus level. Distinguish the proxy failing to track the target from the proxy tracking it perfectly well but rising more slowly than we would like. Ask which of these the empirical reward-model curves from RLHF actually display, and what statistic would separate them. I expect her to say the aggregate overoptimisation curve cannot distinguish them; the follow-up should propose evaluating the target along the whole optimisation path rather than at the endpoint, and ask her what she would predict from that design.

The adversarial case and the level set. This is the one I care most about. Even if the expected target given a proxy value is strictly increasing in that value, an adversary that picks, within the set of items achieving a given proxy score, the one that minimises the true target can destroy the proxy with the causal graph fully intact and monotonicity fully preserved: ordinary extremal optimisation picks a roughly typical member of the level set, adversarial optimisation picks the worst one. Ask whether her collapse has the resources to treat this as a difference in kind rather than degree, and whether adversarial Goodhart is in fact the one category that resists reduction. I expect her to say the adversary is simply another causal pathway or another part of the system. The follow-up should ask whether, on her view, the severity of adversarial Goodhart is bounded by the worst case inside the level set, and whether that gives any grip on how much the adversary's capability matters.

Specification gaming versus selection. The boat-race agent that discovered it could accumulate reward by circling the same targets forever seems to need an explanation in terms of what the optimiser represents, while a selection process on a fixed pool does not. Ask whether her account has to model the optimiser's knowledge of the gap. I expect her to say that gaming requires a world model and so is causal after all; the follow-up should ask whether the finding that reward hacking grows with model scale is evidence about capability or about knowledge of the gap, and how she would design a study to tell them apart.

Mitigations. If the failure is always an intervention on the causal parents, then ensembling proxies, KL penalties and early stopping are all just throttling the intervention, and holding the parents fixed may be incoherent when the parents are the very variables being optimised. Ask her for a concrete case where her framework recommends something the standard menu does not — ideally a case where the menu says stop early and she says keep going — and if she cannot produce one, ask what the framework is for.

Finally, two closing questions. One asking her to name the single observation that would make her abandon the collapse, stated in enough detail that I can tell whether it is genuinely possible. And one about where she thinks this leaves the field: if she is right that no amount of proxy quality helps without constraining the intervention, does she think scalable oversight is the only real option, or does she think the whole framing of optimise a proxy and hope is a dead end.
