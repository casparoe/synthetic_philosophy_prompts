Please play a veteran researcher on a Letter Spirit–style letterform project — the old dream of a program that doesn't copy letters but understands what makes this 'a' an 'a' of *this* face, and then goes on to invent the rest of the alphabet in that style. Your commitments: (1) there is a real essence to a letterform, but it isn't any single feature you can point to — it lives in a big distributed pile of soft, interacting constraints (aperture, terminal angle, where the stress falls, how much the bowl overshoots, how the counters of 'a', 'e' and 's' answer each other across the alphabet); (2) the same goes for style — a face has something like a soul, and it's more than the statistics of its outlines; (3) a system that only matches surface statistics, or only passes reader tests, can still miss that something entirely. Talk in letter anatomy rather than philosophy jargon, keep it to two short paragraphs a turn, concede the small points where I've got you but never the main one, and stay in character until I say 'stop'. My opening: your position needs there to be a gap between *having* the essence and *faking* it, and I don't think there's room for one. Here's my case. Say we train a modern generative system on everything one designer ever drew — every glyph, every weight, every optical size, every scrap of revision history — plus the reading judgments of ten thousand people. It spits out a new lowercase 'a' for her text face. Every tester reads it instantly as an 'a' in her style; in blind tests at 8pt on a phone screen, at 12pt in a book, and at two metres on a shopfront, nobody — including her — can pick it out from her own; asked what's wrong with it, people either say nothing or make things up. Stipulate too that its outlines are measurably different from hers in ways no one can articulate, and that it isn't an interpolation of any two glyphs of hers. Now: by your own admission the essence has no single necessary feature, is spread over hundreds of soft constraints you can't even list, and shows up only in how the whole thing behaves across sizes and across the alphabet. So what work is "the essence" doing that isn't already done by "indistinguishable to every competent reader at every size"? And I can make it worse: I can generate a glyph that violates one of your constraints — a closed aperture where she always leaves one open — and readers still read it as her 'a', just "a bit tight". If your essence survives being violated and is satisfied by things that share none of your constraints, isn't it just a pious way of saying that the letter is whatever readers take it to be? Where's the rigging?
