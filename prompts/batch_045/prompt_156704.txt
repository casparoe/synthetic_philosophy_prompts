i keep flip-flopping on whether the switch to farming was actually a bad deal for the people who lived through it, and i want a number rather than another essay. So: what's your credence that the neolithic transition lowered the welfare of a typical person over the first two thousand years or so, relative to the counterfactual where those same populations kept foraging? I'm at about 0.6, a friend of mine is at 0.25, and I can't tell where we actually disagree.

Give me the decomposition — a number and a line of justification for each:

1. how good the affluent-forager picture is as a description of people who were actually alive at the time of transition (short working week, varied diet, low crowding) versus the revisionist picture that foraging was already harsh, violent and under demographic/resource pressure, so that farming was a ratchet they were pushed into rather than a fall from grace
2. whether the skeletal evidence really tracks welfare — shorter stature, enamel hypoplasias, porotic hyperostosis, infectious lesions on long bones — or is mostly confounded by who got buried where and by a different age structure
3. how much rides on which counterfactual: no farming ever, farming just arriving later or with a different crop package, or dense hierarchy-forming forager societies that got inequality without domestication
4. how much rides on aggregation. Farming feeds far more people, so under a total-person-years view the whole episode looks fine even if nearly every individual life got worse. Give me your weight on the total versus per-life view here, and then a separate number for the veil-of-ignorance version: you get dropped into a random person-year drawn from the whole ten thousand years after the transition, weighted by how many people were alive at each point, not knowing whether you land as an early farmer or a medieval peasant.

Then combine them, tell me which single term your headline number is most sensitive to, and say which of the two of us is most likely wrong about which one. Last thing: if you could look at one already-collected dataset — more paleopathology, finer paleoclimate around the transition, something else — which would move you most?
