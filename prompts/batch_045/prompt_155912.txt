Then branch on questions, not on names. The thing I most want the instrument to diagnose is what you're entitled to conclude from the fact that you have actually been handed the move at node 4, the node the induction says will never be reached. That on-path versus off-path distinction has to show up as its own explicit question, and I'd like at least two more questions to be concrete variants of the game rather than abstract claims. Ones I've been playing with: a version with 100 nodes instead of 6; a version where before play starts each player can send one cost-free message saying they'll pass at every node; a version where I've watched my opponent pass in an earlier centipede against someone else; a version where my opponent is an exact copy of me who reasons exactly the way I do. Use some of those or invent better ones.

Build the space before the names: your questions, then the answer combinations they allow, then the actual positions placed into the cells. The leaves I need covered at minimum: the one that bites the bullet and says the data just shows people aren't commonly known to be rational (or that once you're at a reached node you should have doubts the setup forbade you to have at the start); the one that grants the induction as a theorem but denies it tells you what to do when you're the one actually moving; the team or we-reasoning one; the bounded-rationality / level-k one; and the one that says the model is misspecified and a bit of incomplete info or a tremble dissolves the whole thing.
