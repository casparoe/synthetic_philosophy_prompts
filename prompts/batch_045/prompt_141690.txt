I've been going back and forth on the dispute about whether dreams are experiences, and I can't tell whether it is a genuine disagreement or a stalemate with nothing at stake. On Malcolm's side: the waking report is the criterion for what someone dreamt, so there is no determinate fact about what a sleeper undergoes, and nothing for a dream conceived as an experience during sleep to be; any neurophysiological story is, at best, a predictor of what the subject will later say. On Dennett's side: sleep contains neural activity, the dream as recalled gets composed in the process that produces the memory, so asking what it was like to dream at three in the morning is like asking what it is like to hear a recording before it has been played. The exchange is decades old and both sides keep making the same two moves.

I want a diagnosis, not a verdict. My hypothesis is that the dispute is really about which items are admissible as data: Malcolm's criterion rules neural evidence out in advance as irrelevant to the concept of experience, while Dennett treats neural goings-on as the real story and the report as its downstream effect. If that is right, no experiment could settle it and the question should be dissolved rather than answered.

Test that hypothesis against two stipulations. First: a real-time neural readout that during REM sleep reconstructs a content-stream matching the sleeper's later report about as well as waking subjects describe what is in front of them. Second: in subjects given a drug that abolishes all subsequent dream recall, the readout keeps producing such streams, and its output predicts their next-day priming and mood. For each side, do these count as evidence, or get absorbed — redescribed as mere prediction of a report, or as the loading rather than the playing of the cassette? Is such absorption a mark of a verbal dispute, or of a position insulated against refutation? Given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a distinction, a conceptual argument, an experiment, a different question — or should none be expected, and why? Please answer in no more than 500 words.
