I'm co-organizing a small closed workshop for people who work on psychiatric nosology, and my co-organizer (a philosopher of science) and I have finally persuaded two colleagues who have been circling each other for years to try an adversarial collaboration instead of another round of papers. One of them is a psychiatrist-neuroscientist who sits on a diagnostic-manual working group; call her side the discovery camp: it holds that at least some psychiatric categories name causally structured targets that classification can track progressively, that the instability of our categories is a measurement problem to be engineered away, and that looping — the way a classified population's awareness of its classification feeds back into the profile being classified — is one such nuisance among others. The other is a philosopher who works on interactive and human kinds; call his side the looping camp: it holds that for these kinds the feedback is partly constitutive rather than merely disruptive, that the target moves because the description moves and not only because our instruments are crude, and that when a category looks stable that stability is a local, institutionally maintained achievement rather than evidence of having found a joint. Both have agreed in advance to sign whatever they can genuinely sign, and to say plainly where they can't. What I need from you is the document they should come out with: a list of agreed claims, numbered, each one substantive enough that a naive version of either camp would have denied it before they started (I don't want agreements of the form that classification is hard or that words matter), followed by a disagreements table whose rows are cruxes and whose columns give the precise formulation each side would accept for its own position, what each side would count as evidence or argument that would move it, and — this is the part I care most about — for each crux a case or prediction both accept in advance as bearing on it, with each side's guess written down before the fact.

To keep them off their usual examples, I've asked them to argue over material we've stipulated rather than over the settled literature, and I'd like you to do the same: no citations, no bibliographies, no named authorities, no contested history, and if you want an empirical illustration, flag clearly whether it is stipulated or something neither party could currently check. The case is a hypothetical proposed category for the next revision of a major diagnostic manual — maladaptive anticipatory vigilance, a pattern of compulsive threat-monitoring and checking — together with three stipulated findings. First, a proposed quantitative measure, the Semantic Recoil Index, tracking how much a category's prevalence and symptom profile shift when its criteria text is made public and taught in schools: the working group's multi-site data show prevalence roughly doubling within five years of an awareness campaign, but also show a comparable rise in a holdout cohort drawn from archival interviews recorded before the category existed and re-coded blind, with no exposure to the campaign. Second, a laboratory marker present in about 60 percent of those meeting criteria after the campaign versus about 55 percent before it. Third, when the criteria were tightened, the clinical presentation shifted to the new vocabulary while marker prevalence among cases stayed flat.

Two further things I want handled explicitly, because I suspect most of the last decade of argument is hiding in them. The first is a deflationary move the discovery camp likes: that looping is just a species of measurement reactivity or observer effect of the sort any science has to correct for — with the corollary that classifier-responsive targets also show up outside psychiatry, wherever classified systems adapt to being classified, as with tumor subtypes under treatment or machine-learning classifiers facing adaptive adversaries — so that nothing about human kinds is special except degree. I want the joint statement to say whether the looping camp can sign any of that, and if it can't, exactly which clause it has to reject and what that clause was doing. The second is the obvious peace treaty, that these categories are both discovered and made; I want to know whether the statement treats that as a substantive position with content the two sides could disagree about, or as a verbal truce that dissolves the moment anyone asks what a nosology committee should do. So please also include a short verbal-versus-genuine section naming which parts of the dispute you take to be about words and why, and a closing section addressed to the committee that has to vote on whether to adopt maladaptive anticipatory vigilance: what, if anything, follows for that decision from the disagreement as the two sides have now stated it, and what either side would want measured before the vote. Define any technical term you use, and keep both sides' formulations ones they would recognise as their own rather than as caricatures.
