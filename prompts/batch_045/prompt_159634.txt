I'm a molecular biologist by training — about a decade spent on one metabolic pathway in a model organism — and I now read philosophy of biology in the evenings, mainly to work out what my former colleagues mean when they use the word "function." I keep hitting a configuration that I cannot fit to any of the distinctions I know, and I would like to know whether it has a name of its own. I am not after references or reading suggestions; what I want is the carving: what exactly the thing is, what it is not, and where its edges are.

Two instances.

(1) The pathway I worked on carries one of its steps with two unrelated enzymes. Delete either one and the cells are indistinguishable from wild type — same growth rate, same fluxes, same transcriptome, in every condition anyone has thought to test. Delete both and the cells die. Both enzymes are present throughout the clade and have been for a very long time, which is the part I find hard: something is maintaining each of them, but it cannot be selection for that step, since a broken copy of either one would have been just as well off as long as the other was intact. Yet everybody writes that each of them does that step, and I do not see how either of the two accounts I keep reading about delivers that verdict. On the "what it was selected for" side, there is no selection for the step at the level of the individual enzyme; on the "what it currently contributes to the wider capacity" side, the enzyme makes no difference to the capacity, because the capacity is unchanged when it is gone.

(2) A variant I run into in the whole-organism literature. There is a main route that does all the work, and a second route that is silent: it carries nothing, does nothing observable, and is not developing into anything. Block the main route and the second route takes over the entire job immediately, in the same animal, with no evolution involved. So the second route has a real, specific, well-matched capacity for the job, but in the conditions that actually obtain it contributes nothing, and, as far as anyone can tell, the conditions in which it does the job have never arisen in the lineage's history. Again, everyone describes it as being for that job the moment it is needed.

Why the neighbours I know about seem not to fit. Co-option seems wrong because nothing has been taken over for a new use; in (2) the part is not doing any job at all until it is called on. By-product seems wrong because the fit between the part and the job is too specific and too expensive to maintain to be incidental. Vestigiality is wrong in both directions: in (1) the part is doing the job right now, and in (2) it is not a remnant of anything. And "redundant" or "robust" — the words people actually use — seem to me to describe what the *system* has, namely insensitivity to perturbation, not what the *part* is for; saying that two parts are functionally redundant just presupposes that each of them already has the function, which is the thing I am asking about.

So: is there an established concept for this exact configuration — parts with a genuine, specifiable, sometimes costly and long-maintained capacity for a job, where the job is neither something the part was plausibly selected for nor something the part currently makes a difference to? Are my two instances really one phenomenon or two? How should the carving go at the edges: the part whose contribution is small but measurable, the part that is needed only under heat or starvation, the duplicate that is never expressed at all, the stand-by that turns out to have a second unrelated job? And if there is no established concept, what would you call it, and how would you define it so that it does not swallow the neighbours above?
