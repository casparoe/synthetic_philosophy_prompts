The chapter: for choices that are both epistemically and personally transformative, the standard Paul-style move — you can't do expected-utility reasoning, so decide by asking whether you want to discover who you'll become — gives the wrong verdict in the cases I care about, where the change would destroy something you currently care about non-instrumentally. I call those cares "anchors." My positive proposal is the anchoring principle: some current cares function as side-constraints rather than as tradeable preferences, and you may take a transformative option only if it's compatible with keeping them.
