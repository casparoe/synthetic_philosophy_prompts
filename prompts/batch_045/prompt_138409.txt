hey so i run this philosophy club at school with two friends and a teacher who supervises us but mostly lets us do whatever (last term we did trolley problems for six weeks straight, dont ask). last meeting of the year is tuesday and i wanted to close it with a poem instead of us just arguing at each other again. our teacher is the one who got us onto eliminative materialism about beliefs and desires and he actually holds the thing, my friend thinks its the dumbest idea anyone has ever had, so i need something both of them can sit through without one of them cringing.

i was thinking a villanelle — five tercets and the final quatrain, 19 lines, no padding — in the voice of one single belief with some boring everyday content, like that its raining or that theres milk in the fridge, nothing poetic.

here's what i want the form to do. i know a villanelle's refrains are supposed to come back identical every time, but I want them to come back *wrong*. each time a refrain returns it should have degraded a little, and i want what frays first to be the content clause — the "that its raining" bit, the part that carries what the belief is about and that supposedly won't map onto anything in the brain — and only after that the attitude verb itself. by the fifth tercet, whatever comes back should read like something a finished neuroscience could actually assert: a state, a disposition, some machinery, with no content left in it at all. so the shape of the poem is the eliminativist's real charge, not "we dont know yet what beliefs are" but that the framework has been around forever, predicted nothing new, can't get a grip on sleep or learning or mental illness, and is a research program going nowhere — and the vocabulary should follow the theory out the door.

then the part i care most about, the final quatrain. both refrains come back there side by side, and I want at least one of them back in its original intact wording while the other stays wrecked. which one gets the last line is the poem's position, and I want the reader to be able to tell what you decided from that alone, with no explaining afterwards. the thing i keep getting stuck on is this: the guy stating the view has to say "I think" or "I believe" in order to say there are no thoughts or beliefs, and the person defending beliefs against him wants to say, fine, but whatever it is that actually plays the role we hand to beliefs, that's what beliefs are — you can throw out the theory and keep the things. whichever way you go, don't let the poem slide between "the theory is false" and "the things aren't there" as if those were the same move; if it slides, make the slide be the point.

rules: no proper names anywhere in the poem — dont name the view, dont name anybody who holds it, and no "folk psychology" either, only the teacher knows that phrase and itll sound like a textbook. no brain-scan technobabble filling in the gaps where the content used to be. it should still work as a poem for my friend who hasnt read any of this, she should get the argument from the way the lines fall apart.

just the poem when you're done, maybe a one line title, no preface and no notes after it explaining what it means — i want to hand it out as is.
