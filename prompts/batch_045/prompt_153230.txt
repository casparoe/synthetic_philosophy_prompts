So I'm a retired physiologist — ion channels, mostly, and a lot of grant paperwork — and a couple years ago I drifted into a philosophy reading group at the public library after my wife's book club lost half its members and they let me in to make up the numbers. We're currently on that four-level classification of minds that gets used in the philosophy of mind / evolution literature (I'm fairly sure it's Dennett's, though I can never remember which book): Darwinian creatures, hard-wired, who can only get smarter across generations; Skinnerian creatures, who learn within a single lifetime by trial and error and reinforcement, so individuals rather than genomes pay for the mistakes; Popperian creatures, who can preselect among candidate actions internally, so a doomed hypothesis dies in the animal's stead instead of the animal; and Gregorian creatures, who get fitted out with mental tools — words above all, but also diagrams, tally marks, maps, whatever — that upgrade the machinery which generates the hypotheses in the first place. What struck me, and I may be misremebering this, is that the levels seem to be ordered by *what is allowed to die in your place*: bodies, then behaviors, then hypotheses, then the tools that make hypotheses.

That got me arguing with a friend of mine, a retired electrical engineer, about whether there's a level above the Gregorian one. Here's what I keep circling back to. A Popperian creature can sacrifice this or that particular hypothesis. But the space of hypotheses it can generate is itself fixed — by selection at the Darwinian level, by its conditioning history at the Skinnerian one, and by whatever scaffolding it happened to inherit from its culture at the Gregorian one. So imagine a creature that can put the generator itself at risk: it notices that its way of carving up the problem space has stopped paying off, and it junks the whole framework — the tacit thing that decides which moves even show up as candidates, and which recalcitrant observations count as anomalies worth bothering about — and switches to a different one. My friend says to call that a Kuhnian creature, because on his reading you don't do this by calm deliberation; you get a build-up of anomalies, a crisis, and then a lurch you couldn't have justified in advance. I want to call it a Lakatosian creature, borrowing from the philosophy of science: the thing that dies is not a single hypothesis but a research programme — a hard core of commitments wrapped in a protective belt of auxiliary fixes — and the real capacity is knowing when to stop patching the belt and abandon the core.

What I'd like from you is an actual argument, not a tour. Please argue that the Lakatosian level is a genuine additional level above the Gregorian one — that no amount of internal hypothesis-testing plus borrowed mental tools adds up to the ability to sacrifice the framework itself. Don't just walk me through the four levels and say my example is a nice illustration; I've read that chapter. I want premises I can push on. Specifically: (1) name exactly which capacity is doing the work, and say why the tower of generate-and-test doesn't already contain it — because if all I mean is testing bigger things, I'll happily concede it's not new; (2) tell me whether the name should be Lakatosian or Kuhnian or something else entirely, and what difference the choice makes to what the level is supposed to be, since I suspect the label smuggles in a claim about whether the switch is deliberative or not; and (3) deal with the objection I can't shake, the Duhem-Quine-flavored one: if no hypothesis is ever tested in isolation but only as part of a package with its auxiliaries, then maybe the Popperian level was never really a level at all, and what I've stumbled on isn't a fifth rung but the discovery that the third rung is empty and needs to be rebuilt as my Lakatosian one. Make the strongest case you can rather than a balanced survey — six or eight tight paragraphs is fine — and only after you've done that, if you think the whole idea is confused, say so and say what exactly is confused about it.
