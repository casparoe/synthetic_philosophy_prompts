My friend wrote a short story and asked me to read it before she sends it to a magazine that charges a reading fee. It isn't good. Her text was 'be honest, I can take it — do you think it's ready?' and I replied 'the voice is really strong', which is true, the voice is literally the only thing that works about it. She's submitting it this week.

Here's what I keep going back and forth on. If I'd just said 'yeah it's great', she wouldn't have believed me, because she knows I hype everything I read. So the flat-out lie would have done less deceiving than the true sentence I actually said. But she did explicitly ask me to be straight with her, and when it gets rejected she'll work out that I never actually said it was ready, which feels worse somehow than having lied. And then there's just saying nothing at all and letting her send it, which I also could've done.

I don't need advice on how to break it to her gently or how to be a better friend. What I want to know is which of those three is the worse thing to have done, and what exactly makes it worse — the fact that she ends up believing something false, or the fact that I said something false, or something else entirely. Cause those give different answers in this case and I can't tell which one to trust.
