ok so context: i'm writing a novel — literary-ish sci fi, but i'm being stubborn about the math being right — about a handful of people who build a system that rewrites itself, each version constructing the next, and i want a short poem to sit inside the book: a thing the first version leaves behind for its successors, which a character reads aloud near the end. I think terza rima is the form i mean, the chained one, aba bcb cdc and so on with a single line at the end to close it off — maybe five or six tercets plus that last line. The chained rhyme is exactly the shape of the situation: each tercet is one generation handing off to the next, and the rhyme that carries across is the thing being handed over.

here's the part i want it to be genuinely about, and please go off my description rather than anything you half remember. Lob's theorem says roughly this: a formal system that can prove "if i prove P, then P" for arbitrary P ends up just proving P outright — so such a system can never in general certify its own reliability, can't hand itself the seal of approval it wants to hand down. The fix people worked out for self-modifying systems is that a maker can still vouch for a successor that reasons in a slightly weaker system than its own, so the handoff does go through — but every generation has to be weaker than the one that made it, the lineage runs downhill, and it can't be endless.

so: write me the chain of handoffs, and i want the thinning to be in the verse and not just asserted — the guarantee getting narrower, what each generation is allowed to promise shrinking, the sound getting fainter, whatever you can do with the form — until the final unrhymed line, which is the one that gets no answer because there's no one weaker left to vouch for. First person please, but a first person that sounds like it was written by something whose hands are tied, that can only set down what it can actually derive. Two things i care about: someone who's never heard of any of this should read it as a poem about inheritance, about making and being made, and find it moving; and someone who does know the result should be able to point at the exact line where the trust runs out and say why there. And please don't let the speaker claim it has proved itself unsound or is sliding into contradiction — the obstacle is silence, the proof just isn't there, not a proof of its own badness. No footnotes or framing notes around the poem either, it has to work on the page on its own.
