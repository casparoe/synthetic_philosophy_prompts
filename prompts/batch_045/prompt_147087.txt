My roommate and I got into an argument the other night about whether suffering-focused ethics is even a real research program or just utilitarianism with the sign flipped, and while I think she's wrong about that, it did make me realize I have no idea where the actual work in it is. Context: I'm helping a small funder put together a call for proposals — something like six philosophers for two years, conceptual work only, no data collection, no new empirical stuff about wild-animal welfare or anything like that — and the point isn't to win converts to negative utilitarianism, it's to figure out which questions, once answered, would most change what a suffering-focused community should actually do with its time and money. Also most of the people who'd apply come out of the longtermist end of things, so I want to know which crux, if it went the other way, should push the whole portfolio toward near-term work instead. So: a defended agenda, not a survey of the literature, and definitely not a reading list.

Here's my own stab at the candidate questions, but please add ones I'm missing and tell me if I've drawn the boundaries wrong. (a) Whether suffering takes lexical priority over happiness at every intensity or only above some threshold — and whether a thresholds version even deserves the name. (b) Whether the standard population-ethics asymmetry that negative views lean on — reason not to create suffering lives, no symmetric reason to create happy ones — survives once you drop person-affecting restrictions, or quietly collapses into something you can't use. (c) How to count suffering in minds whose sentience we're only uncertain about: is that just expected value with a credence multiplier, or does being unsure whether there's a subject there at all do something structurally different? (d) Whether a view that treats the painless removal of a sufferer as a way of reducing suffering has a genuine reductio available against it, or whether critics have been over-reading that implication for decades. (e) Whether suffering-focused axiology should be specified over quantities we could in principle measure or over something mind-independent, given that most of the world's suffering by headcount is in animals we can barely observe. My roommate also floated a homebrew view she calls "threshold lexicality with credence-discounted uncertain suffering" — suffering above some intensity counts lexically, and suffering in possibly-sentient minds enters discounted by credence rather than as its own expectation term — and I want to know where something like that lands.

What I actually want is a ranking. Tell me which questions are upstream of which, in the sense that answering one changes what the others even ask; which are realistically tractable in two years by people with logic and decision theory and no lab; which are neglected relative to how much hangs on them; and which you'd defund outright, either because the answer wouldn't change anyone's practice or because they're really empirical questions wearing a philosophical costume. For each, say what kind of result over the next two years would move it up or down your list. Give me exactly six numbered items, each no more than three sentences, no intro and no conclusion: five ranked priorities, plus a sixth on what you'd cut and why.
