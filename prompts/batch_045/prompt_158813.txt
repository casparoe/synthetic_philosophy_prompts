I'm a science-and-ideas journalist working on a long piece and I keep running into this three-way scheme people use for moral cultures: the honor setup, where an insult gets answered through self-help, your standing rests on your reputation for retaliating, and what you advertise is your willingness to get physical; the dignity setup, where you're supposed to let slights slide, disputes go to formal rules and authorities, and the prestige move is showing restraint; and the victimhood setup, where you publicize the harm, recruit an audience and third parties to side with you, and moral standing flows from having been wronged. My editor wants one clean sentence about whether any of these is morally superior to the others, and every time I try to write it I feel like the question is rigged in advance — dignity culture sounds like the answer only if you already accept dignity culture's vocabulary for what counts as maturity, restraint and harm.
