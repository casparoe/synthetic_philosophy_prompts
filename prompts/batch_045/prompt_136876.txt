so I've been reading up on interpretability in my spare time and I keep running into this argument about whether neural networks *really* have internal representations or whether we're just projecting human concepts onto a pile of matrix multiplications. I found this longread article (one of those "we're doing neuroscience on AI" pieces, from a lab that obviously likes this stuff) that summarizes the pro-representation side roughly like this:

"Neural networks turn out to store concepts as directions in their internal activation space: 'cat', or 'legal language', or 'deception' is a direction you can point at, and a neuron firing means the model is currently representing that concept — basically the same kind of thing neuroscientists found with grandmother cells in the brain. Because a network has far fewer dimensions than it has things to keep track of, it packs features in at slight angles to each other, which is what people call superposition, and that's why single neurons often look like they respond to a random grab bag of unrelated stuff, and why the field had to build sparse autoencoders to unpack them into clean one-concept-per-unit features. And the unpacking clearly works: when you amplify one of those recovered features the model starts talking about exactly that thing — there was that demo where turning up some feature made the chatbot obsess about a bridge in San Francisco — and if you can push the model around by pushing on a feature, then the feature is real and it's doing the causal work. So the models do have genuine internal representations, and the philosophers and skeptics who said we were only ever imposing our own concepts on a black box have been refuted by the engineering: the concepts were in there all along, we just needed the right microscope."

I'm inclined toward the skeptical side here — like, sure, you can always find *some* projection of a high-dimensional space where a direction correlates with cat-stuff, that doesn't obviously mean the network "has a cat concept" — but the steering demos genuinely give me pause, and I can't tell whether I'm the one being unfair. A friend who does philosophy of mind says the crux is whether the decomposition is in any sense unique or privileged: if there are endless equally good ways to carve up the activation space, then finding *a* carving that makes sense to us doesn't show the network itself is working with those concepts.
