You are a formal epistemologist who has published on diachronic rationality and updating rules. What I want from you is an audit, not a survey: take the package of views below, test it for joint tenability, and tell me where it breaks. Here is where I have landed, and I can't tell which part of it is load-bearing in a bad way:

(1) I accept van Fraassen's Reflection principle in fully general form: for any proposition A, any later time t, and any value x, my current credence in A conditional on my t-credence in A being x ought to be x. I mean this for every kind of proposition — about the external world, about what time it is, about who I am. What justifies it, for me, is the expert-deference thought: my later self will have everything I have now and more, and will be no worse at evaluating it, so her credences are owed my deference.

(2) I am a thirder about Sleeping Beauty. On Sunday: one-half on heads. Awake: one-third. I think both are the rationally required credences, and that the shift between them is a pure response to self-locating information — nothing new has been learned about the coin or the setup, only about where and when I am.

(3) I take it as obvious that you should not defer to a future self who has lost evidence. If I know that an operation at t will erase my memory of a crucial experiment, I may now rationally leave myself instructions, arrange my affairs around my current (well-informed) credences, and in general refuse to let those credences be dragged toward the ones I will have afterward. Deference to a self who knows strictly less is not deference at all.

(4) I reject diachronic Dutch book arguments as evidence about what credences to have. I am drawn instead to accuracy-based justifications of update rules — the sort of argument that aims to show conditionalizing minimizes expected inaccuracy — and I would want Reflection to be justified, if at all, in that style rather than by the standard Dutch-strategy route.

(5) In ordinary inquiry my credence in a hypothesis can be 0.4 now while I know that after the data arrive it will be either 0.8 or 0.15, and I see nothing wrong with that. Yet Reflection looks like it says I should already be at the later value, which is absurd — so either Reflection or ordinary scientific practice has to go.
