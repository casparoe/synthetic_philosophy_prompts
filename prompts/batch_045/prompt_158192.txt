Write a parody job advertisement, 600–700 words, in the deadpan voice of the careers page of a large defense and autonomous-systems contractor. The posting is for a position titled "Certified Human in the Loop (Liability-Bearing) — Autonomous Engagement Systems, Rotational Shift." It should read as though written by an HR department that has thought about the problem with great care and is proud of its solutions; it should never break character, never hint that anything is amiss, and never pause to explain the joke.

Work the following into the ad's sections — "About the Role," "Key Responsibilities," "Candidate Profile," and "Compensation and Benefits" — with each item exaggerating a real structural feature of the responsibility-gap problem rather than an invented one. Use at least four:

- The operator's authorization window is fixed at a latency below the threshold at which deliberation is possible (roughly 300 milliseconds). The copy should praise this as a latency-optimized judgment environment and describe rapid, unreflective confirmation as a skill.
- Operators are rotated across platforms, theaters, and model versions on a schedule designed so that none accumulates the pattern knowledge that would make a given outcome foreseeable. The company should name this its Foresight-Suppression Protocol and present it as an employee-protective measure: it spares staff the burden of anticipatory knowledge.
- The system's inference is opaque to the operator by design, so she can authorize an engagement without being able to give reasons for it. An Attribution Retention Clause should specify that legal and moral responsibility nonetheless vests solely in the human node and may not be reassigned upward to the deployment order, the procurement chain, or the requirement set, nor downward to the model, its weights, or its training data.
- The benefits section should address the situation of victims and publics who want someone to blame and find no satisfactory object: offer a Public Moral Absorption Package, comprising a communications team, a pre-drafted statement of remorse, and a commitment that the company will characterize any incident as operator error in a register calibrated to the audience's appetite for a culpable person.
- Compensation should include no indemnification and no coverage of legal costs, but generous post-incident dignity counseling plus a modest pay premium — the moral hazard differential — which the ad cheerfully notes is calculated by the platform itself.
- The candidate profile should require that applicants be incapable of understanding the system: those with machine-learning backgrounds will be declined for excess epistemic parity with the platform, since knowing how it works would make outcomes foreseeable to them and thus, in the company's phrasing, genuinely their fault.

The humor has to come from getting the structure right: the ordinary conditions under which blame is taken to require control, foresight, and the ability to give reasons, and what happens when a system strips away each of those while a human is deliberately kept in place to absorb what is left over. Someone familiar with the literature on responsibility gaps in autonomous systems should recognize what is being satirized and find it fair; someone unfamiliar should still be able to tell what is being ironized without the ad winking. Do not name, quote, or cite any real author, paper, or report, and do not close with a straight paragraph drawing the moral. The ad should end in its own register — for instance, an equal-opportunity statement in which the company describes itself as an equal-opportunity absorber of blame.
