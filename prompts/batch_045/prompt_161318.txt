I was at a casual math-philosophy happy hour last week and two people I don't know well got into an argument about the continuum hypothesis. I definately didn't jump in, but I wrote down the gist. (I might be paraphrasing a bit, and sorry for any typos.) Here's how it went:

Riley: CH is independent of ZFC, but that doesn't mean there's no fact of the matter. We should adopt new axioms. Forcing axioms—like the Proper Forcing Axiom—are justified by a maximality idea: the universe should be rich in sets. PFA actually implies the continuum is aleph_2, so CH is false. That gives us a real reason to reject CH.

Jordan: That's too fast. PFA deciding the question doesn't mean PFA is true. You say it's justified by maximality, but there are competing maximality notions. Large cardinals are about maximizing height, and they're compatible with CH—Gödel's constructible universe satisfies CH, and small forcing preserves large cardinals, so large-cardinal axioms can't settle CH either. So appealing to maximality doesn't uniquely pick ¬CH.

Riley: Large cardinals being neutral just means they're the wrong tool. In a single universe view, we want the true V. Forcing axioms have a better track record on alot of questions about the reals, and they give a coherent picture where the continuum is aleph_2. Also, CH is a minimality claim—it says the continuum is as small as possible—so if we like maximality, we should actually expect CH to fail.

Jordan: I think you're conflating two things. CH says there's no cardinal between the integers and the reals; if the continuum is aleph_1, CH holds, and that's not obviously "small" in any absolute sense. More importantly, forcing axioms don't have universal agreement, and the multiverse view explains why: there are many legitimate set-theoretic universes, gotten by forcing, and CH is true in some and false in others. On that view, asking whether CH is "really" true is like asking whether the parallel postulate is really true.

Riley: The multiverse view doesn't make the question go away, it just changes the subject. Even if there's a multiverse, we can still ask which universe is the core one, or which axioms are preferable. And small forcing preserving large cardinals cuts against your height maximality only if you thought large cardinals settled CH; I'm saying forcing axioms do settle it. So we have a reason to think ¬CH is true in the universe we care about.

Jordan: But that reason is just brute intuition, not intrinsic justification. The multiverse view honestly accounts for the independence: CH isn't indeterminate because we're ignorant, it's settled differently in different legitimate concepts of set. Your forcing axiom picks one universe by fiat, not by showing the others are illegitimate.
