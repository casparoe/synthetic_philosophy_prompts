For a graduate seminar on AI alignment I am assembling a one-page diagnostic instrument, and I want its centerpiece to be a decision tree rather than a literature survey. The seminar question is whose values an aligned AI system should serve, and I want the tree to sort participants by what they commit to in cases, not by which label they happen to prefer.

Please build it as follows.

Start from a commitment everyone in the room already grants: there will be a system whose behavior predictably affects many parties with conflicting values, and someone has to specify a single target for it. Everything else is up for grabs.

Internal nodes should be eight to ten questions, each phrased as a concrete case or a plain claim that a student with no background in the debate could answer, and each doing real diagnostic work: two adjacent leaves must differ in what they say about that case. Branch labels should therefore be questions, not position names. Where the answer to one question only makes sense after another has been answered, say so explicitly rather than pretending the order is arbitrary.

The distinctions I most want the tree to carve, and which I do not think are handled together anywhere I have seen:

One, whose values enter the aggregation — only current users, everyone affected, future people, non-human animals, possible artificial minds. Two, which of their values count — what they actually want now versus what they would want under some specified idealization, and note that the idealization itself needs a node. Three, how they combine — total, average, priority to the worst off, an individual veto, a sufficiency threshold. Four, and this is the branch I care about most: what to do when the set of value-bearers, or the content of their values, is partly produced by the system whose target we are choosing, as with a recommender that shapes the preferences it later measures. Five, whether intrapersonal aggregation across one person's changing values is part of the same question or a separate one. Six, whether the tree is meant to output a single target or a rule for hedging among candidate targets under moral uncertainty.

Locate the actual positions in the space the questions generate rather than starting from a list of names, and include at least these as leaves: an aggregative view, a prioritarian or maximin view, a contractualist view with something like an individual complaint or veto, a public-reason or neutrality view that declines to aggregate contested comprehensive doctrines, and a view on which the question dissolves because the candidate targets converge under idealization. Mark at least two coherent answer-combinations as unoccupied and say briefly why — incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate with the questions you have chosen, and specify the additional case that would separate them.

Two constraints. Each leaf gets one sentence naming its most awkward commitment. And do not attach leaves to particular papers, authors, or schools as such: I want the positions and their joints, not a reading list.
