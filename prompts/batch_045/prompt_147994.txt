I do anti-corruption training at a mid-sized manufacturing company and we've just expanded into two countries where basically every deal involves some gift, favour, or job-for-a-cousin going to somebody official. Our global policy says nothing above about 25 dollars and everyone ignores it, and to be honest I've stopped believing the policy describes anything real. The definition we're supposed to work with — roughly, abusing a position of trust for private gain — doesn't seperate the cases I actually care about, so on my own I've come up with three tests and I want to try them on something. Test one, the refusal test: a bribe is supposed to be an exchange both sides can decline, but if nothing in a given system can be refused without cost, then either every exchange there is coercion rather than corruption, or the concept has nothing to grip on. Test two, rent versus buy: cash buys a single decision, whereas a gift or a favour buys the person — it makes them your guy for the next ten years — so maybe the thing we treat as the mild case is actually the worse one. Test three, the substitute test: where the state doesn't deliver protection or credit, a patronage network isn't corrupting an order, it IS the order, and calling it corruption may just be what people say when they already have a state that works.
