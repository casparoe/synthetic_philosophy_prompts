You are a professional philosopher specializing in philosophy of mind and philosophy of science, equally at home with the classic arguments for multiple realizability and with how psychologists and neuroscientists actually individuate the states they posit. Think the task through carefully before writing: start with a short, unpolished brainstorm of five to eight bullets in which you try out candidate agreed claims, rival formulations of each side's position, and candidate cruxes, discarding the ones that are vacuous or slanted. Then write the polished document.

A functionalist who argues from multiple realizability to the autonomy (and irreducibility) of the psychological level, and a neuroscience-first critic who argues that multiple realizability is an artifact of a coarse-grained folk-psychological taxonomy and that realizers cluster far more tightly than the functionalist claims, have been arguing past each other for years. Write the joint statement they could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Two things I want you to build into the drafting rather than assume away. First, the question of what makes two candidate physical realizers the same realization rather than two realizations: take as a working criterion that a difference in the physical base counts as a difference in realization only if swapping in the alternative base would change the causal profile that the higher-level science assigns to the state. Second, the dynamic in which a claimed class of wildly different realizers shrinks every time the higher-level description is made finer-grained, and the suspicion on one side that this shrinking will continue until the class is empty, against the suspicion on the other that it will asymptote at a genuinely disjunctive base.

Aside from the classic multiple-realizability thought experiments involving creatures or machines with very different physical makeups, do not cite or attribute any literature; the document must stand on argument alone.

Format: a numbered section of claims both camps accept, including about what multiple realizability would have to show to do any antireductive work and about what the shrinking-realizers dynamic does and does not establish; then a table of the residual disagreements, each row giving the functionalist's formulation and the critic's formulation, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; then the cruxes, and for each crux a concrete case, argument, or hypothetical finding that both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If some part of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive, say which part and why.

Write in American English.
