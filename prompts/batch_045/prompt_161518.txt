I'm in the lower sixth, and nearly all of my time outside lessons goes on improv and scripted theatre, so I'm comfortable working in terms of given circumstances, objectives and through-lines, cold reading, staying in and breaking character, the fourth wall, typecasting, ensemble, and 'yes, and'. I've been reading about whether a large language model is best understood as an agent — something that acts in the world for reasons of its own — or as a 'simulator' that, handed a prompt, improvises whichever character that prompt suggests rather than speaking for itself. I'd like that disagreement restated in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can and without quietly assuming either side is right: for each of the main claims each side wants to make, give me the nearest thing an actor would say, what has to be stretched to make it fit, and what cannot be said in my vocabulary at all.

Two points where I expect the translation to strain, and which I want handled explicitly. First: an actor can break character, refuse a note, or play a part badly on purpose, because there is someone behind the role who took it on and could have taken a different one; with a model there seems to be no rehearsal room and no one standing off-stage, the role appears to be all there is. Does 'staying in character' survive without an actor, or does it turn into something else entirely? Second: in improv the audience, the director and the scene partner are normally different people, whereas the user of a model seems to be all three at once — writing the set-up, playing opposite it, and judging the result. Does anything in the disagreement turn on that, and does the vocabulary of theatre have a word for it?
