Write me a parody of a government endangered-species recovery plan — the actual genre, so: status review, taxonomy, critical habitat, threats, recovery actions, delisting criteria, a public-comment notice at the end — except the species is a meme. Use "You're welcome" as the reply to "thank you", which is losing ground to "no problem" and "mm-hm". About 600-700 words, deadpan bureaucratic voice, from some invented Office of Cultural Replicator Conservation.

The jokes all have to come from real memetics rather than from invented silliness: fitness broken into fidelity, fecundity and longevity; the species problem (is ironic "you're welcome" the same meme, is the one with the stress on "welcome" a separate strain, does the British variant count); the fact that cultural transmission is Lamarckian, so the captive-breeding programme can simply instruct hosts to improve the meme and the improvements will be inherited; and the point that an archive recording doesn't save anything — a meme that exists only on tape is a museum specimen, extant but no longer replicating. Then take it somewhere a reader flinches: host populations treated as habitat to be managed for the replicator's sake.

It should be funny to someone who knows why memetics fell out of fashion and still readable to someone who doesn't, and it shouldn't ever step out of character to explain the objections it's landing. Couple of typos is fine, just don't let the voice slip into winking.
