Part one — a case with no vagueness anywhere in it, so your favourite move about blurry boundaries and borderline cases isn't available. Imagine a man with a damaged dental nerve that fires in a clean on/off cycle: twelve milliseconds of real pain, twenty milliseconds of nothing, repeating for hours, sharp edges, no fading in or out. At any instant he is determinately in pain or determinately not, and there is nothing for a sorites argument to grip. But introspection isn't instantaneous: what's available to him at any moment is the output of a window maybe a hundred milliseconds wide, and averaged across that window the question is he in pain right Now has no stable answer. So take an instant inside an on-phase. He is in pain, and he is not in a position to know that he is. Luminosity is false, and the usual suspect isn't even in the room.

Part two — the bit I actually care about, because it targets KK rather than the mental state. Grant the margin condition: to know p you must not easily have believed p in nearby cases where p is false. Now run it on KK itself. To know that I know p I need a margin around that fact too, and a margin around the fact that I know p is going to have to be a wider margin around p than merely knowing p required — and then another one for the next level up. The margins stack. If each level costs one unit of margin and I've only got finitely many units to spend, there is some n where I know p but lack the n-fold margin that iterating KK demands. So something gives: either KK fails at some level, or higher-level self-knowledge is somehow free because the belief is introspective and cheap, or luminosity was already false at the bottom and the ladder never leaves the ground.
