THE NOTES

Working title: "Debate is a truth-amplifier, not a truth-generator."

Setup as I'll describe it: question goes in, two debaters argue for competing answers and cross-examine each other, a weaker judge picks a winner. The optimistic pitch is that the judge never has to evaluate the whole answer, only the smallest disputed step — so a weak judge can extract the abilities of much stronger debaters.

My thesis: that pitch quietly assumes the question has what I'll call a locally checkable answer, and a large and important class of questions don't.

- Call a question locally checkable if there's a decomposition of the answer into steps such that (a) the judge can check each step with better-than-chance reliability, and (b) the judge can tell when a decomposition is complete. Call it holistically checkable if the only thing the judge could in principle assess is the whole answer at once, and their reliability on the whole is roughly chance. Proofs in a formal system and arithmetic look locally checkable. Long-run policy forecasting, most questions about what a complex system will do under an intervention, and a lot of "was X really the cause of Y" questions look holistic.

- CLAIM 1 (the main one): debate amplifies a seed. If per-step check reliability is p > 1/2 and errors across steps are independent, recursive sub-debate drives the error down. But if p = 1/2 — the judge has no signal at all on the atomic step — there is nothing to amplify, and no amount of tree depth or debater intelligence helps. And on holistic questions, descending to the bottom of the tree is exactly where the judge's signal is thinnest. So the guarantee is conditional on a seed-reliability assumption that is usually the very thing in dispute.

- CLAIM 2: depth can actively hurt. Each extra layer is another place to make a claim the judge can't evaluate, and judges resolve unevaluable claims by deferring to style, length, confidence, or whoever conceded more gracefully. So on a holistic question a debater's optimal move is to manufacture small legible sub-disputes they can win, and spend the winnings on the judge's trust. Call this credibility farming. If trust is a single global quantity, six small legible wins can be worth more than being right about the one holistic thing. Obvious fix: make trust per-claim and non-transferable across levels. I think that fix is unavailable, because on a holistic question the judge has no other ground for believing anything.

- CLAIM 3 (my constructive bit): randomized adjudication budget. Give the judge a number of verification moves drawn from a distribution the debaters can't see, and don't reveal how it was spent. A fixed known budget invites what I'm calling a spend-down: concede cheap early points so that the decisive move arrives after the judge is out of moves. If the budget is unknown you can't plan a spend-down, since the strategy that wins under a large budget loses under a small one. (Loosely: make the exam a random length so nobody can time-box their bluffing.)

- Side point on the liar's-burden intuition. Debate is often sold on the idea that the liar must maintain a consistent web of lies while the truth-teller gets to lean on reality. But that only bites if the web has to interface with something the judge can check somewhere. A liar who can generate arbitrarily many mutually consistent but unchecked statements pays no such cost. So "lying is more expensive" holds only where there's a seed.

- Objection I want to preempt: fine, don't use debate on holistic questions, use something else. Reply: partly conceded, but the consequence worth drawing out is that debate looks best on exactly the questions we least need it for (where we already have verifiers) and worst on the ones where oversight is genuinely hard.

- Second objection: isn't this just "debate needs the judge to be able to tell truth from falsehood at least sometimes"? Reply: yes and no — the requirement isn't global, it's about atomic steps, and on holistic questions the atomic steps are the worst place to look.

- Where I'm shakiest: whether "holistically checkable" is a stable category or just means "questions we don't yet know how to decompose." If it's the latter, Claim 1 collapses into a claim about the current state of decomposition tools and the right response is "build better decomposers," not "be skeptical of debate."
