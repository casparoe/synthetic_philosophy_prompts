so I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about voting, and I've got this pile of gut reactions that don't obviously add up to anything. Let me just list them and you tell me if there's a principle underneath, or whether some of them have to go.

1. Skipped the last national election. I live in a district that's completely locked in, my vote couldn't have changed anything, and I had no real preference between the two people who could win. Fine, I think. Not even mildly guilty.

2. A friend skipped the same election because he never got around to registering. Also zero effect on the outcome. But I think that's not fine — sloppy and kind of disrespectful, like bailing on something you'd agreed to be part of. Why does 2 feel different from 1 when the consequences are exactly the same? That's what got me going on this.

3. Voted for the boring establishment candidate I mildly dislike over the candidate I think is genuinely dangerous. Fine. Obviously the right call, no guilt, no dirtiness.

4. Voted for a small party I actually like, in a local race where they had no chance and the two real contenders were near-identical. Fine, felt good, like I'd said something.

5. Same kind of vote, but national, in a region that actually decides, where the two real contenders were very different and one was dangerous. Not fine. Feels like indulgence — like making a statement out of other people's lives.

Are 4 and 5 different just because the stakes are different, or is it that in 4 the statement was free and in 5 it cost something? I go back and forth.

6. Vote pairing: I signed up to one of those matching services and got paired with a stranger in a swing district — I voted for their preferred candidate in my safe district, they voted for mine in theirs, neither of us could have got what we wanted alone. I think that's fine, maybe even a bit public-spirited.

7. Selling my vote — someone offers me cash to vote the way I was going to vote anyway. Clearly not fine. But I can't say why 6 is fine and 7 isn't. In both cases I'm treating my vote as something to trade for what I want, and in both cases the outcome is roughly what my sincere vote would have produced anyway. And 7 feels like a different kind of wrong, not a worse-outcome kind of wrong.

8. I'm a dual citizen and haven't lived in the other country for twenty years, but I still vote there — I pay a bit of tax there, my parents live there, the result affects them. Mixed feelings: it seems fine and also a bit like meddling, since I don't have to live with the consequences. I'd of felt different if I'd never lived there at all.

9. My housing co-op voted on whether to sell the building. I voted to sell, which was better for me and worse for most of the other members. Not fine — selfish in a way that voting my own interests in a national election isn't. Or maybe I'm just inconsistent.

10. Helped my grandmother with her postal ballot; she asked who to pick, I picked the one I thought was best, she signed it. Not fine, and I felt bad straight away. Whereas if I'd just laid out the options neutrally and she'd chosen, that would have been fine.

11. And the one I genuinely don't know: my city is piloting a ballot with a none-of-the-above box, and if it gets a plurality they scrap all the candidates and run a fresh election with new ones within two months. I'm inclined to vote none-of-the-above, because none of the current lot is acceptable to me. But I notice that if everyone reasoned like that the city would never settle anything. Is that kind of vote ever a real option, or is it always a way of feeling clean while doing nothing?

The tentative rule I keep coming back to is something like: vote unless your vote can't matter and abstaining doesn't express anything bad about you. But that seems to give the wrong answer on 7 (no effect, nothing expressed, still wrong) and it doesn't touch 10 at all.

So: is there one principle, or a short list of them, that gets most of these? Which of my verdicts does it force me to drop — I'd guess 2 or 1, but I don't want to give either up. And what does whatever principle you land on say about a case I haven't got a verdict on: my employer runs a staff vote on a policy change, one vote each, non-binding, and management has said they'll disregard the result if turnout is under 60%.
