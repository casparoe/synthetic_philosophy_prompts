Our reading group went over the bank cases last week and I want to check I've got the dialectic right before I write my response paper. The way I've reconstructed it: contextualism says 'knows' is like 'tall' — the standards that fix whether the sentence is true shift with the attributor's context — and pragmatic encroachment says exactly the same thing, except it's the subject's practical stakes that move the standards instead of the speaker's. So aren't they really one thesis with a different variable plugged in, and the whole fight just about which variable the intuitions happen to favor?

If so, I think there's a clean way to settle it. Take a bystander who is in a totally relaxed, low-standards conversation, but tell her that enormous stakes are riding on whether Hannah is right about the bank being open Saturday, and then ask her whether Hannah knows. If she cheerfully says yes, is that a point for contextualism and against encroachment — and if enough cases like that come out the same way, should I just be a contextualist?
