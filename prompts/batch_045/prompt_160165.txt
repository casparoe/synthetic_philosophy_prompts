I've been reading through the decisions of our country's Commission on Public Funding and Worldview Pluralism — we've got a historically established Protestant church whose bishops still hold seats in the upper house, a large Muslim minority, and a nonreligious plurality — and I cant work out what rule they're actually running. Six rulings so far:

1. Approved a restoration grant for a thirteenth-century cathedral the state doesn't own, under a scheme open to any listed building of any or no religion; conditions: free public opening several days a week, and none of the money spent on anything used in worship.
2. Rejected a grant toward a new mosque in a city with a big Muslim population — the stated reason was that the state does not fund places of worship.
3. Approved state salaries for teachers of a compulsory non-confessional Religion and Worldviews course: learning about religions, no worship, no doctrinal teaching.
4. Rejected by one vote a proposal letting parents swap that course for confessional instruction at state expense, taught by catechists the churches pick; the worries on record were that kids from nonreligious or minority backgrounds get no equivalent publicly funded option, and that the state would end up certifying who counts as a proper teacher of doctrine.
5. Approved, with conditions, contracts with a Catholic charity to run homeless shelters on state money: no religious test for staff in the funded posts, no prayer or attendance requirement for residents, no restrictions on what the charity does with its own money.
6. Rejected a proposal to collect through payroll withholding a levy for whatever registered religious body a taxpayer names, with an opt-out declaration for people who don't want to pay; the deciding worry was the state keeping a register of who belongs to what and deciding which bodies qualify.

Now there's a proposal in front of them: a "worldview levy." Every taxpayer designates one registered worldview body — religious, humanist, anything — and half a percent of there income tax goes to it. Any body qualifies once 0.1% of adults self-declare for it and it signs a charter accepting the democratic order. The money can be spent on anything at all, worship and proselytizing included. The state is only a collection agent, the money never passes through the budget. People who don't tick a box have their half-percent split proportionally across all qualifying bodies. It's more or less the checkbox arrangement a couple of European countries run.

What do you think they do with it?
