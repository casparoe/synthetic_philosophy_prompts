my family is in a fight about an ancestor and I got picked to break the tie, which I regret agreeing to.

His money endows a scholarship at a small college — one full ride a year, for a first-generation student from his home county. The original fortune came from a merchant firm he was a partner in during the 1840s and 50s that financed and insured voyages carrying enslaved people. In his circle that was completely ordinary. What was not ordinary is that he also paid for a school for freed children a few streets away from his office, and there's one surviving letter where a friend writes to him saying the trade is wicked, and his answer is roughly that the reformers are right in principle, but the whole port runs on it, nobody would thank them for wrecking it, and conditions on the plantations across the ocean are anyway much worse than anything he's responsible for. So he'd heard the argument and turned it down.

The name is on the scholarship, on a plaque, and gets read out every year. A woman whose family was enslaved through that firm has asked the college to take the name down. The college is sympathetic but says another relative will pull a much larger gift if the name goes, and the fund would shrink to about a quarter of a scholarship a year. Cousin A says he was a man of his time, everybody did it, if you judge him by now you have to condemn every ancestor who owned anything, and in a hundred years people will think we were monsters for things we don't even notice and we'd all want the same excuse. Cousin B says the letter settles it — he knew, he picked money, and you don't get to launder the money into doing good while his name stays on the wall. Cousin A also keeps bringing up the school for freed children as proof he was one of the good ones, which feels like it cuts both ways to me somehow but I can't say how.

I have to deliver two things: a vote on three options (leave it, leave it with a new plaque that says where the money came from and quotes the letter, or drop the name and take the hit to the fund), and a paragraph I read out at the ceremony in front of both cousins and her, whichever way it goes. Their probably both going to hate whatever I say. What should I do, and what is the actual thing I'm missing here? I feel like the two of them aren't disagreeing about the same question but I can't name the difference.
