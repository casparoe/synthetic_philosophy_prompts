My philosophy of medicine reading group has been grinding on the naturalism versus normativism dispute for about eight weeks and it is completely stuck. The pattern every single time: someone offers a necessary-and-sufficient style account of disease (a Boorse-ish one where disease is statistically subnormal part-function, a Wakefield-ish one where it is dysfunction plus harm-as-judged-value-laden, or a normativist one where disease is basically disvalue plus a claim to medical attention), someone else produces a counterexample everyone already knows, and both sides keep their view anyway. Nobody has moved an inch and I am starting to think the format of the debate is the problem rather than anybodys arguments.

I want a diagnosis, not a verdict or a survey, and please dont give me a reading list. Assume Ive read the standard moves.

Heres why I think something structural is going on. I made up three cases that I dont think the usual taxonomies handle, and I want to know whether theyre genuinely revealing or just more counterexamples to be absorbed:

1. Two patients with identical physiology, identical symptoms, identical prognosis. In the first, the cluster was picked up pre-symptomatically by a new multi-biomarker early-detection panel that clinics are rolling out; in the second it was found incidentally during a scan for something else. In practice the first gets a diagnosis, treatment, and a follow-up pathway; the second is told it is an incidental finding and sent home. If both theories say disease status supervenes on the condition, then the actual classificatory practice is sensitive to the route of discovery, which is a fact about our epistemic situation, not about the patient.

2. Once such a pre-disease category exists and gets treated, some of the harm that later justifies calling it a disease is caused by the treatment and by being labelled.

3. Whether a given cluster counts as a disease in practice often gets settled by things nobody would call conceptual: billing codes, trial eligibility, disability entitlements.

My hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the word is doing at least three different jobs at once (causal-explanatory, clinical-action-guiding, administrative/entitlement-conferring), no single account fits all three, and each side has quietly picked a different job as the one that counts. So the disagreement is partly verbal, partly a hidden empirical question about how often the jobs come apart, and partly a value question about which job matters, which neither side can argue for.

Tell me whether thats right, what kind of disagreement each layer actually is, what would count as progress given your diagnosis, and whether cases like mine could be data for it or are just noise.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give me the reasoning. No preamble and dont restate the question back at me.
