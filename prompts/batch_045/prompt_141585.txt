Draft the joint statement that an endurantist and a perdurantist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Assume both parties are eternalists: past, present and future times, and their occupants, are all equally real, so nothing below should turn on presentism. Take the endurantist to hold that a persisting object is *wholly present* at each time at which it exists, that its being located at a time is primitive and not to be analyzed as its having a part there, and that the object is strictly identical across the times it occupies. Take the perdurantist to hold that a persisting object is a four-dimensional whole with a distinct temporal part at each time it occupies, and that *x* is *F* at *t* in virtue of *x*'s *t*-part being *F* simpliciter.

Produce:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims — about change, temporary properties, coincidence, fission, and about what an account of persistence is supposed to do. Each should be substantive enough that an outsider could imagine denying it; no platitudes.
2. Each side's own position restated in wording that side accepts.
3. A disagreements table: one row per residual disagreement, with columns for the endurantist's formulation, the perdurantist's formulation, the crux (the sub-question such that answering it would move at least one side), the case or argument both would accept as bearing on that crux, and each side's prediction about it, recorded in advance.
4. A note on which parts of their old dispute were merely verbal, and what makes them verbal.

Cruxes worth considering, though add or replace them as you see fit: whether *x* is *F* at *t* can be treated as primitive or must be analyzed; how much work the argument from temporary intrinsics can still do once the endurantist is allowed time-relative instantiation of properties; whether unrestricted composition over temporal parts overpopulates the world for the perdurantist; whether the analogy between being located at a time and being located at a place is load-bearing for the endurantist or merely heuristic.

Two cases both parties have agreed in advance to treat as bearing on the cruxes:

(A) Splicing. O exists from t1 to t5. A process removes O's t2–t3 segment and substitutes the corresponding segment of a qualitatively identical O*, transferring O's old segment into O*'s career; both careers then continue uninterrupted to t5, with no later evidence of the swap available to either object. Each side should say whether O survives, whether what continues is O or something new, and what would have to be true for that question to have a determinate answer.

(B) A hypothetical successor physics. Suppose a future theory, as well confirmed as current relativity, restores a physically privileged global simultaneity — a distinguished cosmic-time slicing — while retaining relativity's other predictions. Each side should say in advance whether this helps, hurts, or leaves untouched its own case, and why; and if both say it makes no difference, say what that reveals about whether either view was ever drawing support from the structure of time.

Do not cite, quote, or appeal to the literature or to what most philosophers think: the document has to argue everything from scratch, so that it stands up for a reader with no library. Aim for roughly a page and a half. Finish with a single last line consisting of Bottom line: followed by one sentence stating the disagreement that neither side would concede.
