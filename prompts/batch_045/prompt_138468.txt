I've followed the extended mind debate on and off for years, and it looks frozen. Everyone grants that a subject can be coupled to an external resource in the way the classic notebook case describes, and everyone now grants that mere causal coupling settles nothing. Where it splits: one side argues that reliable availability, automatic endorsement, and functional similarity to ordinary recall are enough to make the resource literally part of the subject's cognitive system, and charges the other side with bare biological chauvinism; the other side argues that coupling is not constitution, that neural traces and inscriptions differ in the kind of content and processing involved (roughly, non-derived versus derived content), and that crying chauvinism just relocates the question rather than answering it. Both moves are decades old and nobody has moved.

Two probes I haven't seen run cleanly. Probe one, seamless Otto: the notebook is replaced by an implanted store whose retrieval latency, interference and confabulation profile, and accessibility match biological memory closely enough that the subject cannot tell by introspection whether a retrieved fact came from the implant. Probe two, Wanda: a store that sits inside the skull but that she must deliberately query and update by hand, the way you query a phone — spatially internal, functionally external.

My own hypothesis is that this is two questions under one label: a metaphysical question about what realizes or constitutes a mental state, and a methodological question about which coupled system is the right unit of explanation for cognitive science, with the vocabulary of constitution letting each side hear the other as answering the question it doesn't care about.
