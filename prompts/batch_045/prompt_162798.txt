For most of my working life I was an experimental physical chemist, and the instrument I knew best was a calorimeter. My habits of thought are accordingly the habits of closing an energy balance, of separating statistical from systematic uncertainty, of calibration against a standard, of control experiments and blind analysis, and of treating a reversible process as an unattainable limit that a slow quasic-static protocol approaches but never reaches. I am retired now and I sit in on a philosophy of physics reading group at the university, where the current stretch of the syllabus concerns Maxwell's demon. I can follow the argument as far as the Szilard engine, the kT ln 2 per bit that Landauer's principle charges for the erasure of a bit, Bennett's claim that the measurement itself can in principle be carried out reversibly so that the compensating cost falls on resetting the demon's memory rather than on acquiring the information, and the formulation now standard in stochastic thermodynamics, in which the bound on work extractable in a single feedback run carries a term proportional to the mutual information between the outcome of the measurement and the state of the system. I can follow the algebra, and I can follow the philosophy written about it, but I do not feel that I have either of them in the idiom I actually think in, and I should like that remedied.

So here is my request. Take the position my group keeps calling information-is-physical, in the specific form it takes in the demon literature: that a controller who acquires I bits about a system can extract at most on the order of kT ln 2 per bit more work than one who acquires none, and that this is the same coefficient that appears when a bit is returned to a standard state. Restate that claim, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary of the laboratory: error budgets, systematic as against statistical terms, calibration, closure of the ledger, what a perfect calorimeter would register on a single run, the distinction between a quantity you measure and a quantity you infer from a model of the apparatus. I am fluent in that vocabulary and in the thermodynamics that goes with it; my probability is only at an undergraduate level, so please define any information-theoretic quantity you use in physical terms rather than assuming it.

Then give me the residue report, which is what I actually want. Two things in particular bother me. First, the mutual information is a functional of a joint distribution whose partition into outcomes is chosen by me when I design the apparatus and decide how finely to read the pointer; heat and work, on my understanding, are supposed to be facts about a run that do not depend on how I describe it. I have started privately calling the kT I term an epistemic discount, by analogy with a trials factor or a look-elsewhere correction, which tightens a bound because of something about the experimenter's procedure rather than because of something in the ledger. Is that analogy right, and if it is, is the term then doing the work the literature claims it does? Second, I want to know whether the kT ln 2 is, in your translation, a number a calorimeter could in principle register, or only a statement about the boundary conditions of a protocol that must be returned to its starting state.

Say plainly which parts translate cleanly, which translate only if distorted, and which have no expression at all in my vocabulary: the ideal non-disturbing measurement, the demon's knowledge, the costless reversible acquisition, erasure as an operation defined on a register rather than on a physical system. And tell me, finally, whether the residue points to something my vocabulary cannot say or to something in the original claim that is less clear than it looks. Please do not send me to the literature or hand me a reading list; I have more papers than I can read, and what I lack is the translation.
