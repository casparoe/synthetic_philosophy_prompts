I have been going over the absent-minded driver problem, and I have come to think that the celebrated paradox in it is an artifact of a mistake; I would like to know whether my diagnosis is right.

The setup: two indistinguishable intersections, where exiting at the first pays 0, exiting at the second pays 4, and continuing past both pays 1. Since the driver cannot condition his action on which intersection he is at, a strategy is just a single exit probability p, and maximizing 4p(1−p) + (1−p)² gives p = 1/3 for an expected payoff of 4/3. The paradox is then supposed to be that on arrival at an intersection it is better to exit: taking the probability of being at the first intersection to be 1/2, as indifference seems to require for two subjectively identical intersections, exiting gives 2, whereas continuing and reverting afterwards to p = 1/3 gives only 1.5.

But a driver who adopts p = 1/3 and then exits at every intersection he reaches gets 0, so that mid-journey reasoning cannot be sound. My suspicion is that the error enters earlier: the standard treatment lets the driver's self-locating belief depend on the strategy he has adopted (the frequency-based value 1/(2−p), which is 3/5 at p = 1/3), and a belief obtained by inspecting one's own plan is no belief at all. On that view the right conclusion is simply that the driver should exit at the first intersection he comes to, and what remains is only the familiar gap between planning and execution.

Is that the right way to dissolve the puzzle? Two points in particular I would like checked: whether 1/2 is genuinely mandated here, or whether it comes from treating I am at an intersection as a fresh datum the driver may conditionalize on when, under imperfect recall, he already knew in advance that he would be; and whether, if the strategy-dependent belief really is illegitimate, the planning calculation is best read as a causal one and the mid-journey calculation as an evidential one, so that the whole episode is just another instance of the divergence between the two. Please reply in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, and tell me plainly if I have the matter backwards.
