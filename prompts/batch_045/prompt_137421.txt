I spent a career in sensory neurophysiology — latencies, receptive fields, the tedium of getting a timing measurement to mean anything — and since retiring I have been reading philosophy of mind. I keep tripping over an assumption in the literature, and I would like to know whether it has been isolated and named. More than that, I would like to know whether what bothers me is one assumption or three, because I cannot settle that on my own.

It shows up in three places, which may or may not be the same place.

First: the standard way of describing the blind spot, or the fact that the world does not appear to lurch during a fast eye movement, is that the brain fills in what is missing. Fills in where? The phrasing seems to require a surface with a gap in it, and then something that inspects the surface once the gap has been papered over. I do not think the researchers who write this way mean to commit themselves to anything; that is exactly what interests me.

Second: work on the timing of experience. A subject judges when they became aware of a touch or of an intention, and the judged time can be shifted by events that occur afterwards — a later stimulus can make an earlier one seem to have occurred later than it did. This is then described as an empirical question about whether the experience itself was referred backwards in time, or whether the subject's memory of an on-time experience was subsequently revised. I do not see what could settle that, since everything we learn in such experiments arrives through a report made afterwards. Yet the two descriptions are treated as rivals in good standing.

Third: talk of a stimulus reaching consciousness at some latency after onset, or of a neural signal failing to reach it, as though consciousness were a compartment with a doorway rather than a matter of what the system goes on to do.

My own attempt at a taxonomy gives three separable commitments: that there is a place where the results of distinct processes are assembled for something; that there is a time by which they must be assembled, so that later changes are changes in memory or report rather than in experience; and that at any moment there is exactly one official version of what is going on, the rest being mere machinery. My suspicion is that one of these is load-bearing and the other two collapse without it, but I cannot tell which, and I may be cutting at a joint that is not there.

So: is there an established concept, or an established family of concepts, for this cluster? Does the literature treat my three commitments as one thing wearing three faces, or as genuinely detachable? And what is the thing *not* — which nearby notions does it get confused with? I can think of the old worry about an inner observer, and of the general complaint that explaining vision by postulating someone who watches a screen only relocates the problem, and of the fashionable phrase about the hard problem, but none seems to be my target, since my worry is about a structural assumption embedded in perfectly ordinary, non-sceptical, empirically serious description. If there is no established concept, I would rather have one coined and defined, with its boundary cases stated, than a name I cannot use.

Two more things, if you will oblige me. I have been using a rough diagnostic: take a claim of the form that some event became conscious, or never became conscious, at a given time; substitute the claim that it was available then for spontaneous verbal report or for voluntary response; and ask whether anything of substance is left over. Where the substitution loses nothing, I take the original to have been leaning on the assumption. Is that test sound? Does it over-generate — flagging innocent claims — or under-generate? And has anything like it been proposed before?

And the obvious retort to abandoning the assumption: a subject asked what they saw gives one answer, not several. If there are many parallel processes and no canonical one, the singleness of the answer needs its own explanation. What is the strongest reply available, and does it leave anything intuitively unpaid?

I am after the carving, not the label.
