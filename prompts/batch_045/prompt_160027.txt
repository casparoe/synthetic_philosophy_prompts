I've been reading LessWrong and the forums that grew out of it for about fifteen years, so I've watched the part of that world concerned with doing good efficiently go through several self-corrections. There is a pattern I keep seeing in it that I can describe in three pieces but cannot name, and I am not confident the three pieces are the same thing.

One. A grantmaker compares two programs: a bednet-style distribution with clean randomised evidence and a dollars-per-life-saved figure, and a multi-year effort to change how a national research-funding agency sets its priorities. The second has no usable control group, diffuse and delayed effects, and no unit cost. It loses. It loses again five years later, and this time the reason recorded is that its evidence base is thin. But the thinness is not independent of the earlier decisions: nobody paid for the measurement infrastructure, and the people who would know how to judge that kind of work went elsewhere, so the file gets thinner as a consequence of never having been funded. Measurability here looks like a property of the intervention, but it is partly a product of the funding history.

Two. The same shape appears in career choice. Someone picks a career by expected lifetime donation. That expectation is computable for software and finance and essentially incalculable for "go build the infrastructure for evaluating the diffuse thing." So the pipeline fills the legible careers, and the advice ("earn to give") treats the existence of an evidence base as a fixed background condition when it is in part a function of who took which job. The evaluators are selected the same way, and are themselves ranked by the same style of cost-effectiveness reasoning, so the people whose job is to notice the exclusion are under the same pressure.

Three. The corrections I've seen proposed do not seem to escape it. Apply a multiplier to options with weak evidence; ring-fence a fraction of the budget for unmeasurable work; fund hits-based. But to size the correction you need a number for how much you are missing, and producing that number requires exactly the kind of evidence you do not have. Any estimate of the bias is generated by the biased process. And no one inside the system is rewarded for saying "I cannot produce a number for this." The filter appears to operate again on the attempted repair of the filter.

I don't think this is just Goodhart's law: nothing is being gamed, and the problem is not that a measure degrades once it becomes a target but that a whole class of options never enters the comparison. It isn't quite Campbell's law, which seems to be about deliberate distortion under pressure. The streetlight effect gets closer, except that in the streetlight case the key is wherever it is and only the light is misplaced, whereas here the light is being constructed as we go, so where it is now depends on where we looked before. It is more than path dependence or a Matthew effect, since what compounds is not advantage but apparent certainty. It has a family resemblance to Scott-style legibility, except that no simplifier is imposing a grid; it falls out of many individually sensible choices. And I can't tell whether some of it is just exploitation crowding out exploration, which might be fine.

So: is there an established concept for this configuration, or for the three pieces separately? Is "measurability bias," which gets thrown around loosely in these discussions, a term of art with a definition, or just a gesture? Does the recursive part — the correction being subject to what it corrects for — have its own literature? If nothing established fits, coin a term and define it, and give me boundary cases: when is this same structure benign, and when is it the thing I think I'm seeing?

Please don't give me a bibliography or citations; I want the carving — what the phenomenon is, what it isn't, where its edges are — and I'll consider the naming question closed if there is nothing established. Finally, say what would change your mind: which consideration would convince you that I have conflated three unrelated things, or that the whole pattern dissolves into ordinary value-of-information reasoning, and where in your analysis you think the reasoning is most likely to break.
