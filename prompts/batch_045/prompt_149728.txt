Write a dialogue of roughly 900–1,200 words between two equally competent consciousness researchers, Mara Vos and Idris Kale, who are finalizing the preregistration of a single experiment and disagreeing about how the first-person side of it should be handled.

The experiment: on each trial participants are shown an irregular polygon for 40 ms, then immediately masked, and they (a) select which of four named shapes it was, scoring barely above chance; (b) trace its outline with a stylus on a blank tablet, where the tracings correlate with the actual polygon at about r = 0.6; and (c) say whatever they like into a microphone. In open-ended debriefing most participants report something like a grey flicker with no shape to it. Three weeks later, in a follow-up session, participants who are shown their own tracings often say that they did see that shape and simply had no word for it at the time.

Vos is a heterophenomenologist. She holds that every first-person utterance is a text to be interpreted from the third-person stance and carries no authority about the experience it purports to describe, and that the discordance among the button press, the tracing and the debriefing report is a discovery about report-generating mechanisms rather than about consciousness. Kale defends disciplined first-person methods: he holds that untrained participants do not describe their experience but narrate it using their own folk psychology, so that the debriefing utterance Vos treats as canonical is the most contaminated measurement in the study, while the tracing is evidence of a channel that bypasses the subject's concepts and vocabulary.

Have them work through the concrete decisions they must preregister: whether to include a training phase in which participants learn to withhold interpretation; whether the tracing counts as a report of experience, as mere behavior, or as something that undermines that distinction; what to do with the three-weeks-later utterance, given that Kale's own method relies on retrospective elicitation; and, since the funder permits only one primary outcome measure, how to state in advance an operational criterion for the participant's being conscious of the shape without that criterion either presupposing what counts as a report of experience or silently settling the dispute between them.

Each of them should get the better of the exchange at least once and should concede at least one point that genuinely costs them: Vos something specific about what untrained reports are contaminated by, Kale something specific about what the later utterance could be a judgment about rather than a memory of. Neither may diagnose the other as confused, and neither may retreat to the claim that their disagreement is merely verbal.
