Two friends of mine got into it after a talk on anthropic reasoning last night, and I was asked to settle it and honestly couldnt tell who was winning. The case they were fighting over is a variant of the presumptuous philosopher thing, with a twist they made up themselves.

Setup: two cosmological theories, Big and Small, tie on all the ordinary astronomical evidence, so call it 50/50 before any anthropic reasoning. Big says the universe holds something like 10^24 observers, Small says around 10^12. As I understand it, the self-indication assumption (SIA) says to treat your own existence as evidence — reason as if you were a random draw from the observers there could have been, so worlds with more observers get proportionally more weight, and Big ends up a trillion-to-one favourite, which is why the presumptuous philosopher figures she can skip running the experiment. The self-sampling assumption (SSA) says instead to treat yourself as a random draw from the observers who actually exist, which doesn't give you that boost (and is what drives the doomsday argument). Fine so far.

The twist: compare two different ways Big could be true. Big-Varied: the observers are wildly different from each other and just one of them has your exact evidence. Big-Dup: the physics is such that the local configuration constituting you recurs over and over — a tiled or periodic universe, say — so essentially all the extra observers are exact subjective duplicates of you, same memories, same experiences, never interacting. (They agreed to set aside the extra headache that in a literally infinite universe both sides have infinitely many observers.) Question: does SIA give the same trillion-to-one push in Big-Dup as in Big-Varied, or does duplication make a difference?

[1] Nadia: same push in both. SIA counts observer-slots, not kinds of observer, so it literally cannot see the difference between duplicates and aliens; a trillion times as many slots means a trillion times the weight, full stop. And that's the right answer by analogy: suppose God flips a coin, heads creates one observer with experience E, tails creates a thousand exact duplicates with experience E, and you find yourself having E. You should say tails, a thousand to one — it's the same structure that gives you thirding in Sleeping Beauty. If you want thirding there you have to count tokens here, and if you count tokens for a thousand copies you count them for a trillion.

[2] Chris: the analogy supports me. Run the anthropic update on the evidence that some observer with my evidence exists, not on the claim that I'm a random pick from all possible observers. Then what matters is how many draws the world gets from mind-space. Small gets 10^12 draws; Big-Varied gets 10^24, so a trillion times the chance that one of them lands on you. But Big-Dup doesn't get a trillion times more draws — it gets one draw, repeated. The chance that a mind like yours gets instantiated at all is the same as under Small, so the trillion-to-one verdict collapses to 50/50 in the duplicate case while surviving in the varied one. Duplicates fill the universe with copies of a fact you already had; they don't create new opportunities for you to exist.

[3] Nadia: that isn't a reading of SIA, it's a rejection of it. And it gets God's coin wrong: on your instantiate-the-type view both hypotheses instantiate E, so you'd say 50/50, but thirders say tails. Plus your view makes the update hostage to how I describe my evidence — describe it finely enough and the type is instantiated once under any theory, describe it loosely and every theory instantiates it. You can't count tokens in the small cases and types in cosmology.

[4] Chris: on the first bit, 50/50 in God's coin isn't crazy, and Beauty is special because the set-up stipulates the two wakings — she's told she'll be woken twice, so she knows wakings are the things being sampled. Nothing stipulates duplication in cosmology; it's inferred, which is exactly the case where I don't trust the intuition. On the second bit: sure, my view inherits the reference-class problem, but everybody in this game does, SSA included, so that's not an objection to me in particular. And on the deeper point: a thousand copies is a thousand chances for you only if there's a fact about which possible observer you are. If possible people are just qualitative profiles with no built-in identity, then a thousand identical copies isn't a thousand chances, it's one chance with multiplicity, and the multiplicity does no work.

[5] Nadia: then your objection was never about duplication. Your no-built-in-identities view would kill token-counting everywhere, Big-Varied included, since there's no fact there either about wich of the 10^24 possible observers you are. So your claim that duplicates are different in kind has collapsed into a global attack on SIA.

[6] Chris: maybe it has. But look where that leaves the presumptuous philosopher: her trillion-to-one isn't a finding about the cosmos, it's a bet on a modal-metaphysics dispute nobody has settled, plus a choice of reference class nobody can justify. She should go do the experiment.

Can you go through this move by move and tell me who's right about what — rate each of the six from clearly wrong to clearly right, with a sentence or two of justification each — and then say who's more right overall and whether both of them are missing something important?
