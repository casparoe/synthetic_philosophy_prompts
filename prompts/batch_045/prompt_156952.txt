I run a small monthly reading group — mostly lettering and type people, plus two friends who just like arguing about concepts — and last week we spent the whole evening on the Letter Spirit project (Hofstadter and McGraw's attempt to get a program to render the 26 lowercase letters of the roman alphabet in a coherent style, starting from a few seed letters and filling in the rest by analogy). The thing we couldn't stop arguing about wasn't whether the program worked but something prior: when you say of some blob of ink that it's an 'a', what are you actually claiming? The project treats being-a-particular-letter and being-in-a-particular-style as two orthogonal aspects of a letterform, and everyone at the table signed on to that, and then immediately disagreed about what the first aspect consists in. So I want to build a quiz for next month's session: ten or so questions, each one a concrete glyph or case, two or three answer options each, plus a scoring key that sorts people into a position about what a letterform is. The point is that it should sort them by what they say about cases rather than by which -ism they like, so no question may just come out and ask whether categories have essences or whether you're a Platonist about letters. Phrase everything as something a person could answer in the room without knowing the labels, and describe the glyphs in words or crude ASCII, since half the group will be squinting at it on a phone.

Cases I defintely want in: the double-storey 'a' of a high-contrast Didone versus the single-storey 'a' of a geometric sans (same letter, wildly different shape — so what carries the identity?); a glyph halfway between 'a' and 'o' that people read differently depending on what it's sitting next to; a scratchy six-year-old's 'a'; the Fraktur or Cyrillic one; an invented alphabet in which the glyph doing the work of our 'a' looks like our 'q' (role in a system versus shape on the page); a glyph the program spat out that no human ever drew or intended as anything; the fact that you can spot an 'a' almost instantly while being unable to state a single rule you used; and a font so stylized that one glyph alone would baffle you but the same glyph in a string is plainly the 'a'. I'd also like one case that probes whether historical descent from aleph matters at all. Reorder or swap things as you see fit, but keep it around ten.

The leaves I want at the end: a feature-list or definitional view (there are necessary and sufficient conditions even if we can't state them); a prototype view with graded membership and typicality effects; an exemplar view built on similarity to stored instances; a family-resemblance view where nothing is strictly necessary and the boundary is one we draw; a role or relational view on which the 'a' is a position in a system of contrasts and the shape is nearly incidental; a skill or know-how view on which having the concept is a perceptual and manual capacity rather than anything statable; a historical or causal-chain view; and a flat conventionalist one. I also want the option I suspect half the room secretly holds — that there is something like an essence but it isn't a checklist of features, it's a small set of abstract roles or relations that survive every style. Please build the space first from the questions and the answer combinations they allow, then locate the actual positions in it, tell me which cells are empty and why (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored), and flag any pair of leaves my quiz can't really seperate. Finish with one question I can put to the whole group out loud that will reveal whether the quiz is sorting people by their first-order classifications of glyphs — where I suspect we'll all answer the same way — or by their second-order claims about what classifying is, where the real disagreement seems to be.
