I am revising a paper draft that I intend to circulate at a graduate workshop in philosophy of language, and I would be grateful for detailed comments on it. The paper responds to Kripkenstein's rule-following paradox; the draft follows, in six short sections. I have deliberately kept it without references, because I want the argument judged on its own terms rather than on its pedigree.

**Robustness, not determinacy**

**1. The demand I want to refuse.** Kripke's sceptic asks for a fact about me that determines which rule I meant by "plus" — a fact that singles out addition from quus, the rule that agrees with addition for arguments below 57 and delivers 5 otherwise. Straight answers try to produce such a fact: a disposition, an introspectable state, a Platonic item. The sceptical answer denies that there is one and reconstrues meaning talk in terms of what a community is entitled to assert. I want to argue that both sides concede too much in accepting that the required fact must *determine* a unique function over all arguments. What our practices actually need is not determination but *discrimination*: enough to underwrite attributions, corrections and predictions, and enough to distinguish a correction from a change of subject.

**2. The robustness test.** Proposal: a candidate rule R is the rule I mean only if my applications of it are robust across perturbations of the elicitation context — different notations (Arabic numerals, Roman numerals, tally marks, a different base), different orders of presentation, different modalities (written sums versus combining piles of blocks), distraction, unfamiliar magnitudes. The test is not a claim about how I represent the rule; it is a constraint on the pattern of my performances.

**3. Why notation-independence does real work.** The familiar gerrymanders are definable only by exploiting features of a particular notation: that a number is written with fewer than three characters, say, or that it falls below a bound we happen not to have crossed. A rule that survives translation across notations and modalities has to be stated in terms of the numbers themselves rather than in terms of how the numbers are written. This is a substantive constraint, and it is not merely a restatement of the requirement that a rule be general, because the generality demanded is cross-representational rather than merely counterfactual.

**4. The residue, and why I think it is benign.** The test does not eliminate every gerrymander. Let bplus be the rule that agrees with addition for sums below some astronomically large bound N and delivers 5 above it. Bplus is notation-independent, and no perturbation we could actually run discriminates it from addition. I concede this. My claim is that the residue is harmless: the residual rules agree with addition on every case we could confront, transmit or dispute; they generate no difference in what anyone ought to say; and where nothing in the practice could discriminate, there is no norm to violate. Indeterminacy at the vanishing point is not meaning-scepticism; it is the ordinary underdetermination of a practice by its finite exercise.

**5. Where the normativity comes from.** The standing objection to any dispositional approach is that it yields only what I will do, never what I ought to do. My reply is that robustness does not generate the "ought" on its own; it *constrains* the practice of correction. A correction that conflicts with a robust pattern is not a correction but a change of topic; a correction that fits the pattern while conflicting with my answer binds me. That asymmetry between the two directions is what makes the pattern normative for me rather than merely predictive of me. Note that the account needs no community: the pattern lies in my own cross-context performances, so the sceptic cannot simply rerun the paradox one level up.

**6. The objection I dread.** Finiteness reappears. My counterfactual performances under perturbation are themselves finite, so a gerrymander can be fitted to them too. My reply is that each fitted gerrymander must itself survive the same test at the next level up, and that the rules surviving at every level — call them *recursively robust* — form the space within which meaning is located. I conjecture that addition belongs to that space and quus does not.

What I would like from you, in roughly 1,200 words and organised under my section numbers:

1. Whether the contrast between determination and discrimination in §1 is stable, or whether the sceptic can simply reissue the demand at the level of discrimination: a fact that discriminates addition from quus is still meant to be a fact about me, so what have I achieved?
2. Whether cross-representational robustness in §3 is doing genuine work, or whether it is a trivial consequence of expressing any rule in terms of numbers rather than numerals. In particular, whether a gerrymander stated purely in terms of the numbers escapes §3 untouched, which would make that section a detour.
3. Whether the "benign residue" manoeuvre in §4 is coherent, or whether it quietly collapses into either a sceptical solution on which meaning talk is reconstrued as assertibility, or into a "no fact of the matter" view that I have not acknowledged. Is the claim that nothing in the practice could discriminate a claim about our epistemic limits or a metaphysical one, and can I have it both ways?
4. Whether the normative asymmetry in §5 is circular. To classify a proposed correction as changing the subject rather than as correcting me, must I not already be credited with meaning addition — in which case robustness arrives only after the normative work has been done?
5. Whether the recursion in §6 has any content at all, given that a gerrymander can be fitted at every finite level, or whether it merely relocates the finitude objection inside a hierarchy.
6. Editorial advice: which of §§3–5 should lead the paper; which section is weakest and should be cut or demoted to a footnote; and whether the whole thing is more interesting if reframed, not as a straight solution, but as an error theory about the demand for determinacy itself.
7. The objection I most want tested: whether this view is genuinely distinct from a dispositionalism that simply helps itself to counterfactual dispositions across contexts. If you think robustness adds nothing to a generous dispositional base, say so and explain why.

Please be blunt about where the argument fails, and where you think a claim is false give the counterexample you would use if you were refereeing the paper. Do not suggest further reading, and take the dialectic as I sketch it: I am asking about the argument, not about how best to read Wittgenstein.
