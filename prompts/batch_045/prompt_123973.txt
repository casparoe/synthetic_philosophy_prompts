i run a university debate society and we're doing a session on the motion that "western values" doesn't name anything real. rather than the usual two speakers a side, i want a handout that sorts people by what they actually believe when they use the phrase.

so: build me a taxonomy as a decision tree. six or seven branch nodes, every one phrased as a concrete case somebody with no theory could answer — would an athenian democrat count, a 19th-century ottoman reformer who wanted constitutional government, a japanese liberal today, an elected but illiberal european government, somebody who likes the whole list of norms but thinks the history is a crime scene? label each node with the claim that splits it, not with a name.

leaves: 7 to 9 of them, and these must all be in there — the list/content view, the genealogical/descent view, the contrastive view (defined against some other), the honorific view (a word of praise that tracks no property), the self-ascriptive view (uttering it just signals which team you're on), and one steelman i'm fond of: the tradition-of-self-criticism view, where what's distinctive isn't a doctrine but a canon of internal critics. add whatever leaf you think i'm missing. each leaf gets one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

then three or four combinations of answers the tree allows that nobody occupies, with a line each on whether it's empty because incoherent, unattractive, or just unmapped. and flag any pair of leaves your questions can't actually pull apart.

last thing: give each leaf the one cross-examination question that would hurt it most in the room.
