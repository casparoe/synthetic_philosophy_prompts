I keep ants as a hobby — a large wood-ant colony in an old aquarium, maybe forty thousand workers, with a second tank I split off it last year — and I have got myself into an argument I cannot settle. The queen in the original tank died a few weeks ago, the workers are still foraging and tending brood, and the colony is plainly fading. My brother-in-law says the humane thing is to freeze the tank now, on the grounds that what I have is not forty thousand patients but one, and that it is already dying. My daughter says that is killing forty thousand individuals to spare them a slower death, which she thinks is not mine to decide. The ant-keeping forum I use is firmly on his side, and it argues that way because it treats the colony as a superorganism: workers are the body, the queen was the reproductive organ, pheromone trails are the hormones and nerves, the nest is the skin, foraging is digestion. A second analogy gets used alongside it, sometimes by the same people: each ant is a neuron, the trail network is the wiring and the memory, the colony is the brain, and therefore the colony has a mind — beliefs about where the food is, something like preferences, possibly something like distress. That second one is the one I keep reaching for myself, because it is what makes the superorganism talk feel more than poetic. But I do not trust either one, and I would like an audit rather than a verdict.

A few things in my own setup keep breaking the pictures for me, and I would rather you worked them in than treated them as distractions. First, speed: whatever the colony is doing when it reroutes a foraging trail takes minutes to hours, so if it is thinking it is thinking on a timescale perhaps a thousand times slower than mine, and any conversation with it would take months per sentence. Second, I merged two unrelated colonies of the same species last spring, gradually, and after a few weeks of fighting they settled into one; if each was a mind, did one die, did they fuse, or are there now two minds sharing a body? Third, the boundary is not clean: workers drift between my tank and a neighbour's mound down the road and get adopted, so the parts are not exclusively mine in the way neurons are. Fourth, the split tank now runs on its own with no queen, and by the forum's own lights I am not sure whether that makes it a brain-damaged continuation of the original animal, a new one, or a body with no one at home.

So what I would like is this: lay out explicitly what maps onto what for each analogy — what plays the role of a memory, a belief, a decision, a sensation, a boundary, a birth, a death, a disease — and then say where each mapping fails, and which of those failures actually matter for the conclusion my brother-in-law is drawing. I am especially interested in the disanalogy that nags at me: a neuron is not itself an organism with its own nervous system, its own preferences and possibly its own experiences, whereas an ant is, so the colony is made of something more like minds than like cells. If that is right, does either analogy survive as anything more than a metaphor for coordination? Does the slowness matter, and if not, why not? What does the merger and the fission case do to the identity claim — is there a fact of the matter about how many minds are in my tanks, or does the analogy simply run out? And if both analogies fail, is there a better one that carries the real insight: a market with prices standing in for pheromone concentrations, a distributed computing cluster, a coral, a firm, an ecosystem? I would like to know which one earns the right to guide what I actually do on Sunday, and how much of the euthanasia conclusion was being carried by the analogy rather than by the biology.
