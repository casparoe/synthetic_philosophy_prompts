"Hofstadter's claim is that creativity is basically knob-twiddling. Any domain comes with a fixed set of stylistic knobs — tempo, key, rhyme scheme, brushstroke thickness, whatever — and being creative is just wandering around the space those knobs define until you land on a combination nobody has happened to hit yet. Boden calls stepping outside the space transformational creativity, but on Hofstadter's picture that never really happens; what looks like a leap into a new space is just a long enough chain of small twiddles that eventually crosses the boundary of the old one. So all creativity is exploratory search inside a space that's given in advance. Which is why a language model trained on everything in a genre should be the most creative thing in that genre — it's covered more of the space than any human has, and the one thing it supposedly can't do, leaving the space, isn't by Hofstadter's own lights a real thing anyway."
