so I'm in a reading group on psychopathology classification, and one of the grad students keeps leaning on this analogy: a psychiatric category is like a natural kind — gold, water, tiger — there's a real structure out there, the diagnostic manual is a rough first pass at carving it, and revising the manual is just getting closer to the joints, the way chemistry got closer with the periodic table. He uses it to say the whole Hacking "looping effects" line does no real work: looping just means our *descriptions* lag behind the kind, or that clinicians misapply criteria. On his view it definitely doesn't show the kind is made up, so the manual can be straightforwardly true or false of someone, and most criticism of it is really just a complaint about measurement error.

I dont buy it, but I'm having trouble saying exactly where it goes wrong, partly because I think several different things get lumped together under "looping" and they don't all break the analogy in the same way. Here's the split I've been using: (1) the criteria and the official description get revised as the people classified push back, complain, and organise; (2) the people themselves change — once you've been told you're a case, you experience yourself, present yourself, and regulate yourself differently, and clinicians start seeing you as a clearer instance than they did before; (3) the category leaks into ordinary language and changes who shows up at the clinic in the first place, so the referral stream is endogenous. Maybe there's a fourth: prevalence numbers feed back into funding and research attention, which feeds into the criteria again.

To make it concrete, take a made-up category — call it Selby syndrome: onset in late adolescence, core symptom is a persistent sense that your own voluntary actions aren't really yours, diagnosed almost entirely from interview, and a diagnosis unlocks housing support and gets you out of jury duty. It's Selby syndrome that he says is a natural kind waiting to be found.

Can you audit the analogy for me? Be concrete about what maps onto what rather than just telling me "human kinds are interactive" — I want to know which disanalogy does which work for which of my three (or four) loops, and whether any of them actually touch the conclusion he's using the analogy for. Two more things I can't sort out: is the looping point even special to psychiatric kinds, or does it apply just as much to "gifted child", "terrorist", "unemployed"? Because if it applies everywhere, he can say it isn't an objection to psychiatric classification at all, just a fact about any kind whose members can hear about it, and I'm not sure what to say back. And: the analogy quietly imports that the classifier isn't one of the classified — chemists aren't part of the nature of gold — but psychiatrists can themselves be cases. Is that the crux, or a red herring?

- Where it breaks (about 450 words): the disanalogies, organised by which of the loops each one attaches to
