so I got into a long, stupid argument last night with my friend about the causal exclusion argument and I definately lost it on points, so now I want to win it by other means. Her line was that exclusion only bites if you treat mental properties as extra causes sitting alongside physical ones and competing for the same work, and that once you think of them as higher-level patterns or program explanations there is nothing left to exclude and mental causation is totally untouched. I said that move just buys causal relevance by re-describing it, and that if you actually took the exclusion worry seriously it would reorganize ordinary institutional life and not only metaphysics seminar. She said fine, show me. So now I have to show her.

What I want from you: a parody document. Specifically, a section of a faculty handbook or HR policy appendix — the merit review and personnel procedures — from the philosophy department at a mid-sized state university that has fully and officially accepted the exclusion argument and rebuilt its committee procedures around it. About 750 to 950 words, numbered clauses, written in American English, in a deadpan policy voice (shall, pursuant to, the Committee notes that).

Some requirements, because I can feel this going wrong:

- The premises have to be doing the work without ever being stated as premises: the causal closure of the physical, the supervenience of the mental on the physical, and a ban on systematic overdetermination. Nobody in the document says "we are excluding the mental." It's just that the forms only have room for one causal story.
- It has to grind through the concrete cases a real handbook grinds through: annual merit raises, letters of recommendation, hiring, and academic-integrity cases where intent is the whole question (he submitted it deliberately / she knew the source was there). In each case the procedure it lands on has to be the one the exclusion worry actually forces: the credit, the blame, and the responsibility get attached to the physical event-sequence, while the mental description is retained purely as decor.
- Invent some committee jargon so this can be administered. A form that separates the sufficient physical history from the redundant mental overstory; a scoring rule that deducts points for counting the same outcome twice; a filing deadline for the one and a "courtesy notation" for the other. Do not explain the jargon. That it exists and is stamped at the bottom is the joke.
- At least two places where the handbook has to cope with a problem it cannot actually solve, and must cope with it bureaucratically rather than honestly. First: if the candidate's beliefs and desires didn't do any causal work, then the letter-writer's didn't either, so a letter is not evidence of anything — patch that with a certification someone signs. Second: the committee's own verdict. If it's the physical story all the way down, the deliberation isn't doing anything, and yet there is a deliberation scheduled for the 14th. Patch it too. I want the patch to visibly smuggle reasons back in while the document insists nothing was smuggled.
- One clause about self-knowledge: how the committee is supposed to establish what anyone intended, given that intentions leave no causal trace on the available evidence. Handle it the way an actual bureaucracy handles an impossible reporting requirement — a checkbox, a field too small for the answer, a signature line.
- Include one dissenting sub-clause from a small faculty minority that accepts systematic overdetermination and thinks the credit can simply be counted twice. Same deadpan voice, no winking at the reader, no aside to the effect that this is all a reductio. Anywhere the position collapses, it should collapse on the page and not in a footnote.

The bar I care about: the humor has to come from getting the metaphysics right, so that someone who works on mental causation laughs instead of winces, and so that my friend reads it and feels vaguely called out without being able to put her finger on where the unfairness is. No citations, no bibliographies, no named philosophers, no quotations from anybody, no concluding moral. Just the document.
