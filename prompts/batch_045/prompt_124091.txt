I've been going in circles on that old question of whether you could have come from a different sperm and egg, and I've given up trying to argue my way to an answer and started just writing down my gut reaction case by case. Here's the list, with my verdicts:

- My parents conceive a child nine months to the day after I was actually born, different sperm, different egg. That's my brother. I would never have existed.
- The same sperm from my dad, a different egg from my mum. Also not me, just some other kid.
- My actual egg and my actual sperm, but before the zygote divides someone edits one single base pair. Still me, obviously.
- My mum's nucleus transferred into a donor egg with somebody else's mitochondria — the three-parent baby setup. Still me. I don't feel like the mitochondria are doing any identity work.
- A numerically different sperm cell and a numerically different egg cell — different cells, different meiosis, different parents even — that happen to carry qualitatively identical sets of chromosomes, so the resulting genome is base-pair for base-pair the same as mine. My gut says that's my identical twin, not me. Which sits very badly with the previous two.
- My zygote, implanted in somebody else's uterus and carried by her. Me, no question at all.
- A clone: my genome dropped into an emptied-out egg and gestated now. Not me. That's my son or my very late twin, not me.
- The one I keep flip-flopping on: if my zygote had split at the two-cell stage and both halves had developed, I genuinely can't tell whether to say neither of the resulting people is me, or that both of them are me in some degenerate way, or that I just get to pick whichever one I like — which feels like cheating.

Also one wobble that I think contradicts my first two verdicts: if my parents had conceived two weeks earlier than they did — so, again, different gametes — I feel like that person is me, or at least me-ish, in a way that "a different sperm and a different egg" in the abstract doesn't feel at all.

The rule I've been trying out on myself is: I'm whoever develops from this very zygote, the one made out of these two particular cells, with small differences in the genome tolerated, but if the zygote splits then I don't exist. Can you test that against my cases and repair it where it breaks? Which of my verdicts does a decent rule force me to give up, and which one is the weak one?

And then tell me what your repaired rule says about a case I have no feel for whatsoever: the sequence is the same, but the genome is synthesised nucleotide by nucleotide in a lab from a read-out of my zygote's DNA, popped into an enucleated egg, and implanted. Me, or a very good counterfeit? And be honest if the whole exercise is a mess and my guts are just noise.
