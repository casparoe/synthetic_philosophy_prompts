hey — I'm putting together a short humour thing for our dept's end-of-term newsletter and I want it to be a parody of an official guidance document. Setup: a fictional agency, the Bureau of Admissible Precisifications, which administers the Heap Levy (a tax on any heap of sand on private land). It has two rival divisions — an Epistemicist Directorate and a Supervaluationist Directorate — and the piece is their joint guidance note to taxpayers, in numbered clauses, deadpan civil-service voice, roughly 600-700 words.

The jokes have to come from getting the two views right rather than from caricaturing them. Things I'd like in there:

- The epistemicist lot: there is an exact grain count that marks the boundary, it's recorded somewhere in the Bureau, and no citizen can ever be in a position to know it — their ignorance is supposed to fall out of the margin-for-error constraint on knowledge, not from any fuzziness in the sand. So give me a clause refusing Freedom-of-Information requests for the cutoff, another warning taxpayers off private grain-counting firms (any count near the boundary has a margin of error, so no certificate can be relied on), and, if you can make it land, a tax notice addressed to the single grain that made the difference.
- The supervaluationist lot: three stamps — 'heap', 'not a heap', 'no determinate status' — where heap means true under every admissible precisification. Their troubles should show up too: the enabling statute allows collection only where the levy is determinately owed, so in the penumbra the Bureau's own authorising statute comes out neither true nor false; and their mission statement (that the levy has no sharp cutoff) doesn't come out straightforwardly true on their own semantics. Whatever they say about definiteness should have a fuzzy edge as well — the line between 'definitely a heap' and 'borderline' is itself borderline — so let the footnotes be hedged, and then the footnotes to the footnotes.
- Ending I'd love: for any actual pile both divisions issue the same verdict for incompatible reasons, and an Inspector-General's appendix notes that no taxpayer has ever detected the difference, and proposes funding a research programme to find one.

Keep it readable for someone who's never done any philosophy of language, and don't let any clause wink at the reader.
