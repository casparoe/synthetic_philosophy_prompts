Is there a name for this? I've been reading about metaphysical grounding and fundamentality for a couple of years now (one seminar, mostly me rereading things I didn't get), and I keep noticing the same move, and I can't tell whether it's a known thing with a label or somthing I'm half making up.

The pattern: when someone lays out a grounding chain — F holds because H, H holds because G — the middle link never survives. Either they say H was only ever a stepping stone and the real explanation runs straight from G to F, or they say H adds nothing and fold it back into G. And it doesn't seem to matter what H is; the collapse happens every time. If you try the same thing with causes you get the opposite reaction: nobody explains the fire by citing the Big Bang, and if you skip from the Big Bang straight to the fire people say you left out the actual cause. Causal explanation seems to want its middles; grounding explanation seems to loose them.

Places I've seen it: (1) arguments about whether a composite thing is "nothing over and above" its parts — somebody says the arrangement or the structure does the work in between, and it gets either absorbed into the parts or demoted to a mere upshot of them; (2) talk about "levels," where every level between the fundamental one and the one actually under discussion gets treated as bookkeeping. And it seems to matter to fundamentality itself: if middles can't carry any weight, then either there's exactly one level doing the work, or the word "fundamental" is doing something sneaky.

What I think it isn't: it isn't just the distinction between an immediate ground and a mediate one — I know that distinction, and my point is about why the mediate one never gets to do any work. I don't think it's just contrastivity either, though I'm shakier there, since you can ask a contrastive question at every level and the middles still seem to get eaten. And it isn't the general "grounding isn't causation" point, because I'm after something narrower: depth being a virtue in one case and a vice in the other.

The case that makes me doubt myself: in ethics-ish contexts people seem perfectly happy with a proximate ground — the wrongness is grounded in the pain, not in the microphysics — and nobody says "no, go deeper." So maybe the thing is real but local, and working out where it stops is part of what I want.

So: is there an established concept here? If something's close, name it and tell me exactly where my cases stop matching it. If there isn't, coin something, define it tightly enough that I could tell a real instance from a fake one, and give me at least one boundary case you'd want to think harder about. Please don't just say "that's how grounding works," and don't point me at papers or reading lists — I can't get to a library right now, and anyway I want the carving, not a label and not a bibliography.

Last thing: tell me where you're shakiest. If you're saying nothing established fits, how confident are you, and what would convince you otherwise? If you're coining, which part of your definition gives out first under pressure?
