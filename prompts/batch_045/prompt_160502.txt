I'm in a seminar on the Scientific Revolution and my paper argues that your astrology was continuous with your astronomy rather than a leftover superstition. I want to test my central objection against a Kepler who actually holds the doctrine.

Please play Johannes Kepler: sharp, impatient with the vulgar astrologers, and willing to concede a great deal — the twelve houses, the signs of the zodiac, most of the received aspects, any claim that the planets compel events by pushing on things. What you will not concede is that the harmonic doctrine is empty. Here is the version of it I am working from: the planets act on sublunary things the way light acts on the eye or a consonance on the ear, by being perceived rather than by force; there is an animate faculty, in the earth and in the human soul, with an inborn sense of geometry, and it answers the angular configurations the planets form as seen from the earth, but only those that fall on the ratios you derive from constructible figures. A soul is stamped at birth with the configuration then sounding, and later aspects resonate with that stamp, so astrology yields inclinations rather than destinies. Correct me in character if I have this wrong.

Use only what was available to you in 1610 and what I tell you: no gravitation, no inertia, nothing from after your lifetime. Two or three paragraphs per reply, no breaking character until I say stop, and ask for any fact you need as yourself.

My objection: we can now compute planetary positions to the second for any date, past or future, so your complaint that astrologers worked from corrupt tables is answered — the data excuse is gone, and the predictions still fail. Worse for you, precision is what breaks the doctrine. With exact computation, some near-harmonic configuration among the planets is almost always within tolerance of one of your ratios, and we now know of numberless further bodies, including the satellites of Jupiter, that form those same angles as seen from here. The sky is almost never silent, and a theory of sounding harmonies needs a principle of selectivity it does not appear to have. Tell me which configurations really sound, how exact they must be, and why that is not a rule invented afterwards to fit.
