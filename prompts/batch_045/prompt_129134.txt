I'm a TA for an undergraduate game theory course, and I have one fifty-minute section to teach iterated elimination of dominated strategies when the eliminating strategy is allowed to be a mixed one. The students have already done IESDS with pure-strategy dominators and have computed mixed-strategy Nash equilibria, but have never seen the two ideas put together; rationalizability comes next week. Don't explain the material to me — plan how I should teach it. What has to be in place first, and in what order (in particular, where does the convex-hull reading of mixed dominance — the payoff vector lying below the upper envelope of the others — belong, and does anything have to precede it)? What single game should carry the session, and what must it contain and lack: I have in mind one where pure-strategy IESDS is uninformative, where the mixture doing the eliminating is not the uniform one, and where deleting the first strategy opens up a second round — tell me if asking for all three at once overloads the example, and what to drop if it does. Which misconceptions should I expect, and at exactly which point in the sequence do I defuse each? The ones I anticipate are: that the dominating mixture is something the player chooses or announces rather than a hypothetical benchmark; that a mixture of surviving pure strategies automatically survives itself; that a strategy strictly dominated by a mixture could still receive positive probability in some Nash equilibrium; and that order-independence carries over from strict to weak dominance. Finally, give me one question on a game the students haven't seen that would distinguish understanding the procedure from having learned the recipe, and say explicitly what I should refuse to cover at this level.
