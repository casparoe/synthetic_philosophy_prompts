I'm in an upper-level seminar on philosophy of mind and animal cognition, and we've been working through Dennett's ladder of kinds of minds: Darwinian creatures, whose behavioural repertoire is fixed by selection and who cannot learn within a single lifetime; Skinnerian creatures, who can learn by operant reinforcement but only by actually trying things and suffering the consequences; Popperian creatures, who can prescreen candidate actions against an internal model, so that bad hypotheses die in their stead instead of the creature dying for its hypotheses; and Gregorian creatures, which are Popperian creatures whose brains have been redesigned from the inside by mind-tools, above all words. My seminar presentation is on Thursday and I've been asked to circulate a short statement of my own position on this material in advance so the others can attack it. I've written it out below, but I have a nagging sense that it doesn't hang together and I genuinely can't see where the fault is, so I'd like an audit before it goes out. Here is the profile:

(1) I read the ladder as fully continuous. Each rung is a mechanical improvement in how a creature's behavioural design gets fixed, and being Gregorian is just a matter of having better equipment. Nothing spooky is added at any point, and there is no leap that a mechanism story can't in principle explain.
(2) I also think Gregorian status is a historical rather than a merely synchronic property. What makes a creature Gregorian is that its own cognitive machinery was reshaped during its lifetime by tools it acquired, not just that it currently behaves as if it had such tools.
(3) Relatedly, I don't think you have to invent the tools yourself. Using words you didn't coin is enough to count as Gregorian.
(4) I want to say that a lab-raised African grey parrot trained to use spoken labels for colours, shapes and numbers, or a bonobo trained on a lexigram keyboard, is Gregorian to some degree: those symbols are genuinely doing cognitive work for the animal, not just mediating a trick.
(5) I want to deny that a large language model is a Gregorian creature. Its parameters were shaped by a variation-and-selection process over a corpus, which strikes me as Darwinian, or at best Skinnerian, at the level of the system itself; its fluent word-use looks like a simulacrum of a Gregorian mind rather than the real thing, since nothing dies in its stead and it never prescreens anything against a world for its own sake.
(6) I think the Gregorian/Popperian line is where a difference in kind finally shows up: above it a creature acts for reasons, below it talk of reasons is really just talk of causes. And I mean that as a categorical difference, not as a difference of degree.
(7) I think the taxonomy as a whole is silent about consciousness. Plenty of Skinnerian creatures, and probably some merely Darwinian ones, are conscious; becoming Gregorian doesn't add phenomenology.

What I'd like is an audit, not a lecture or a summary of Dennett. Go through the set and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it — I'd rather you discriminated than flagged everything as problematic. For the real tensions, please order them by severity rather than listing them as equally bad, and for each one say what exactly would have to give, what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the set, and which of my other commitments would then be doing the work I was illegitimately getting out of the dropped one. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Please don't assume I'd rather give up any particular item: I haven't marked one, because I don't know which one it is, so don't read the hedging in my wording above as a hint about where the answer lies or about which way I want to go. One more thing: my flatmate in computer science insists the whole thing collapses as soon as you take LLMs seriously one way or the other, and I'd like to know whether that's right or whether it's a red herring. Finally, for each tension you identify, say how confident you are that it is a real tension rather than an artefact of how I've worded things, and single out the claim in your reply that you are least confident about, with a note on what would change your mind about it.
