Take the replication crisis in psychology — Bem's precognition paper in 2011, the Reproducibility Project in the mid-2010s reporting that a big share of textbook effects came out null the second time, ego depletion and power posing going from settled findings to probably nothing — and run it through the pessimistic meta-induction. Skip the methodology lessons (p-hacking, underpowered designs, publication bias) except where they matter for the mapping: what I want is disciplined mapping. Who or what is the target of the induction here — the field's unobservable posits, its causal claims, its statistical practices? And is this the same shape of problem as the usual historical list, or a different one?

The bit I keep getting stuck on: the standard realist rescues — structural realism, preservationism, the idea that the posits doing the explanatory work get carried into the successor theory — all seem to presuppose a successor theory that explains why the old one worked. Phlogiston got oxygen chemistry; the aether got special relativity. Ego depletion got nothing, the effect just evaporated. So does the crisis hurt realism about psychology more than the classic cases did, precisely because there is no successor theory to do the preserving? Or does it miss realism altogether and land only on statistical practice?

And the other direction: what does the episode say about the pessimistic meta-induction itself — that it needs a successor-theory clause, that it needs to distinguish an induction over posits from one over methods, or that it was never a metaphysical argument in the first place? If the honest verdict is local pessimism about psychology, tell me why that doesn't spread to the rest of science; if it does spread, tell me where it stops. Be explicit about the places where no mapping is available and the analogy just breaks.
