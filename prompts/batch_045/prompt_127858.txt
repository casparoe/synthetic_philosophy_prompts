I have to write the framing memo for a graduate seminar on the social construction of kinds, and before I assign anything I want the standard of assessment written down. What I need is the specification: what an account of the claim that kind K is socially constructed has to be able to do in order to be any good, with the case or consideration that motivates each requirement.

Fix the purpose as follows. The account is supposed to be good enough to settle the so-what question: for a given kind, does the fact that it is constructed give anyone a reason to do anything, what kind of reason, and to whom. It is not supposed to be a theory of reference or a general metaphysics of social facts, though it will presumably need enough of both to get off the ground. One further thing I want the list to take an explicit stand on: what the construction is out of — practices, concepts, institutions, material infrastructure such as ledgers or housing stock — and whether allowing non-intentional or material elements into the base is required, permitted, or forbidden.

Three things the list has to handle, and the third is my own, so treat it as a candidate requirement and tell me whether it is a genuine desideratum or a hobbyhorse.

First, the stock cases should not be treated as one case. Race, gender and money are where the seminar opens, and they pull apart: for some of them there seems to be a gap between the kind and our operative concept of the kind, so that the construction claim can be a claim about our concepts rather than about the people or things in question, while for others the concept and the kind look co-constituted; some are the sort of thing people can be straightforwardly wrong about and some not; some have members who know about the classification and adjust to it, and some do not. Say whether an account is required to have separate machinery for these differences, or whether the demand for a single univocal account of construction is itself the prejudice of one camp.

Second, there is a contrast set that must not be collapsed. Constructed gets run together with caused, chosen, conventional, merely nominal, subjective, fictional and unreal, and most of the bad arguments in this area are inferences from construction to one of those. State this as a requirement: which contrasts must the account be able to mark, and give the case that shows each collapse is a mistake.

Third, the case I actually care about. A bank's fraud model sorts its customers into a cluster that nobody named, nobody intended, and nobody accepted — a purely statistical artifact. The cluster tracks something real; customers begin to alter their behaviour because of it; some of them eventually come to describe themselves using the bank's own label; a decade later the category is doing real work both in credit decisions and in people's self-understanding. Is that kind socially constructed? Whatever the answer, the account has to give it for a reason that also applies to race, gender and money, or explain why it does not. I want to know which of the familiar styles of account this case forces a revision in, and whether the requirement I am implicitly imposing — that construction not presuppose anything as strong as collective acceptance, or as strong as an intention to create a kind — is a real desideratum or my politics in a trench coat.

Flag two things besides. Which requirement does the most work in excluding candidates, in the sense that it knocks out the largest share of the views people actually hold. And whether any pair of requirements on your list is jointly unsatisfiable: my suspicion is that one cannot simultaneously demand that the account stay neutral about whether the constructed kind is real and demand that it deliver the revisionary so-what conclusion, and I want to know whether that is the real fault line or whether I have located it in the wrong place.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of my question. Number the requirements, give each a short name and one motivating case, and mark each as non-negotiable, negotiable, or the preference of a particular camp. A table is fine for the contrast set in the second item. Keep the scorecard — how the familiar causal-historical, constitutive/ameliorative and collectively-accepted-status-function styles of account fare against the list — to a few lines at the end, since that part I can do myself.
