I did forty years in X-ray crystallography, a fair bit of it on clay minerals as it happens, and since retiring I've been sitting in on the philosophy department's reading group, so I'm making up for lost time and probably using the wrong jargon. We did the statue and the clay last week and I wrote down afterwords where I'd landed. I'd like to know whether the set hangs together, because I can't find the seam:

1. The statue and the lump are two things, not one thing under two descriptions — both of them are really there, coincident, made of the very same matter.
2. They don't differ in any intrinsic physical respect at any moment: same particles, same bonds, same shape. All the differences are modal and historical, which is why the lump can survive being rolled into a ball and the statue can't.
3. Modal facts ride on physical facts. Nothing can differ in what it can survive, or in how it will behave, unless it differs in its physical make-up — and I'm counting its history as part of that make-up. I brought this out of the lab with me and I'd rather not loose it.
4. Whether the thing in front of me now is the same thing as the thing that was here yesterday is never settled by what anyone thinks, wants or happens to have a word for. It's a fact about the world or it's nothing.
5. A case I made up, because the examples in the literature all seemed a bit rigged: a clay figurine lies in groundwater and over ten thousand years, petrified-wood style, every clay mineral is dissolved and replaced by silica grain for grain, so no original particle is left but the shape survives down to the tool marks. I want to say the museum still has the same figurine. But if someone dissolves the clay on Monday and casts a chemically and structurally identical silica figurine from the same mould on Tuesday, I want to say that's a replica, not the same object.
6. And for what it's worth, really there's only the clay. The statue is the clay under a description, an extra thing only in a loose sense of thing.

Give me exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each: the genuine tensions ranked worst first with what would have to give in each, then which of the six is only apparently in tension and why, then the single cheapest revision overall. No preamble and no summing-up at the end — I'll work out for myself which one I'd miss least.
