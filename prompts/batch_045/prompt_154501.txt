hey, I need a joke for the last slide of a short talk I'm giving on Nozick's utility monster — you know, the creature that converts resources into utility so efficiently that straight total-utilitarianism seems committed to feeding it everything and starving everyone else, and which is meant as a reductio of that kind of utilitarianism rather than as an actual policy proposal.

I don't want a generic "a utilitarian walks into a bar" bit. The laugh should depend on the actual machinery: the conversion efficiency, the fact that the monster's appetite is supposed to be embarrassing for the theory rather than a practical problem to manage, the way interpersonal utility comparison is doing all the work, or the total-vs-average distinction. If someone who has never heard of the thing would still find it funny as a joke about greed or about a big hungry animal, I've failed.

Here's the wrinkle I want in there, because my talk isn't about the original monster. I've been playing with a variant I'm calling the treadmill monster. Its utility doesn't come from how much it consumes in absolute terms — it comes only from increases in its consumption relative to its own recent baseline. So a steady diet of ten units a day does nothing for it; what produces utility is going from ten to eleven. Every transfer that pleases it resets the bar, so it's never finally satisfied at any finite allocation.

My suspicion is that this variant has some properly ridiculous consequence for what a utilitarian is supposed to do — something about the baseline itself becoming a resource, or about the theory owing the creature something weird before it can owe it anything. If you spot that consequence, that's probably where the joke should live, and I'd rather get one joke built on it than three safe ones.

Two constraints:

- It has to be tellable out loud in about twenty or thirty seconds. No setup that needs a whiteboard.
- If the treadmill thing turns out to not actually add anything — say it collapses back into the ordinary monster, or it's incoherent, or the consequence I'm gesturing at doesn't really follow — tell me that in a sentence or two before or after the joke, and give me the ordinary-monster joke instead. I'd rather know the variant is a dud than get a good joke resting on it.
